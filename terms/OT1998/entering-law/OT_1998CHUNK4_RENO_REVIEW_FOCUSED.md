#### Lincoln v. Vigil, 508 U.S. 182 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1833.
**Decided:** May 24, 1993.

##### Allocation of this unearmarked lump sum is committed to agency discretion

**Holding:** When Congress supplies a lump-sum appropriation without a binding program earmark or other meaningful allocation standard, an agency’s distribution among authorized statutory purposes is committed to its discretion under §701(a)(2). Legislative-history references and the general federal trust relationship do not require continued funding of this particular program; enforceable statutory boundaries and the separately presented constitutional claim remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Heckler v. Chaney, 470 U.S. 821: discretionary resource-allocation rationale applied beyond the enforcement setting within the statutory limits stated.
- ICC v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers, 482 U.S. 270: recognized discretionary administrative categories applied.
- Citizens to Preserve Overton Park v. Volpe, 401 U.S. 402: distinguished; an actual statutory standard remains enforceable.
- Webster v. Doe, 486 U.S. 592: separate constitutional review preserved.

##### This organizational and policy change does not require notice and comment

**Holding:** Even assuming the program’s termination is a rule, this reallocation and organizational policy change falls within §553(b)(A)’s exceptions for agency organization and general policy statements and does not require notice and comment. The ruling concerns a change in service availability, not a new binding eligibility rule, and leaves the separate §552 publication question unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Enacted program limits remain enforceable. The independent constitutional claim, §552 publication question and unreviewed remedial issue are reserved. The decision relies on §553(b)(A), not the benefits exception in §553(a)(2), which the Department had undertaken not to invoke.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand; continued funding cannot be compelled on the rejected APA grounds. No entitlement to a particular hearing or authority to spend outside the statutory mission.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morton v. Ruiz, 415 U.S. 199: distinguished; compliance with applicable agency procedures does not create a universal notice-and-comment duty.

#### Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, 510 U.S. 200

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-896.

**Decided:** 1994-01-19.

##### This Mine Act dispute must follow the statutory review channel

**Holding:** The Mine Act precludes original district-court review of this anticipatory challenge to miners' representation and posting requirements because Congress's intent to channel the dispute through the independent Commission and then a court of appeals is fairly discernible from the Act's language, structure, and purpose, and these claims can receive meaningful review there. The claim's relation to the statutory enforcement process, relevant agency expertise, and actual availability of judicial review inform that inquiry; they are not three newly mandatory elements governing every administrative statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, statutory-channel portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine participants adopt this proposition at the stated level of generality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Block v. Community Nutrition Institute, 467 U.S. 340 (1984): applied to discern congressional review allocation from the statutory scheme.
- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967): distinguished because that scheme preserved supplemental judicial review; the presumption favoring review does not require every forum.
- McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc., 498 U.S. 479 (1991): distinguished because its collateral systemic challenge lacked meaningful review through individual proceedings; that protection remains.
- Board of Governors v. MCorp Financial, Inc., December 3, 1991: its scheme-specific channel rule supports the analysis, without extending its express bar to the Mine Act by assumption.
- Darby v. Cisneros, June 21, 1993: preserved; limits on judicially added APA exhaustion do not displace Congress's allocation of original review.

##### The available route does not impose an intolerable choice on this record

**Holding:** The operator has not shown that compliance with these representation and posting requirements, or their contest through the Mine Act's review process, imposes a constitutionally intolerable obstacle to meaningful judicial review. This conclusion depends on the actual absence of demonstrated irreversible injury, review before contested penalties become payable, expedited proceedings, and the statute's bounded opportunities for temporary relief; it creates neither immunity from lawful penalties nor a rule that delayed payment alone always makes review adequate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, meaningful-review and due-process portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; seven participants adopt this ground. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment on a distinct constitutional formulation and are not counted as joining this explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Temporary relief is discretionary, not automatic. Under §815(b)(2), Commission relief from covered orders requires a hearing with an opportunity for every party to be heard, a substantial likelihood of favorable Commission findings, and no adverse effect on miners' health and safety; relief is forbidden for citations under §814(a) or (f). Section 816(a)(2) permits appellate temporary relief only after notice and an opportunity to be heard, a substantial likelihood of success, and protection of miners' health and safety; it excludes orders concerning §817(a) imminent-danger withdrawals and §814(a) or (f) citations. Section 816(c) makes clear that commencing review does not itself stay an order. Thus this decision promises no stay of a posting citation and no suspension of every accruing penalty. The separate availability of contest, expedition, review before collection, and the unproved irreparable-compliance injury supports the present result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908): distinguished on practical access; its protection against a penalty structure effectively foreclosing judicial review remains intact.
- McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc., 498 U.S. 479 (1991): its requirement of meaningful practical review is preserved and satisfied on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final agency action, cause of action, ripeness and exhaustion are unnecessary to the preclusion disposition. Commission inability finally to decide a constitutional question does not eliminate appellate review. The representation, posting and labor-law merits remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit's jurisdictional judgment is affirmed; the district-court injunction remains unavailable. The representation/posting challenge must use the authorized Commission and appellate process. No labor-law merits determination, penalty assessment, guaranteed stay or new administrative outcome is ordered.

#### Reno v. Catholic Social Services, Inc., 509 U.S. 43 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1826.
**Decided:** June 18, 1993.

##### The individual-determination channel does not foreclose all rule challenges

**Holding:** Section 1255a(f) channels review of individual legalization determinations but does not categorically eliminate district-court review of a challenge to generally applicable regulations where no individual determination supplies an effective review route. A regulatory label alone establishes neither ripeness nor a right to district-court reconsideration of an individual denial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, The individual-determination channel does not foreclose all rule challenges portion; Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McNary, 498 U.S. 479 — systemic-review distinction applied; individual denials remain distinct.
- Bowen v. Michigan Academy, 476 U.S. 667 — presumption of review applied, not converted into a factual burden.
- Section 1255a(f)(2) — late-filing denial bar preserved; no general permission to evade it by never filing.

##### A concrete application barrier must be established

**Holding:** An actual refusal to accept a timely application under the challenged rule supplies concrete application sufficient for a ripe district-court challenge where front-desking blocks the individual-review route. Mere awareness of an adverse rule is insufficient on this record; further facts may establish that the front-desking policy substantially caused a comparable inability to apply even without a personal front-desk rejection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, A concrete application barrier must be established portion; Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An individual denial ordinarily follows the statutory administrative route and judicial review connected to deportation; §1255a(f)(2)’s late-filing bar is not evaded by a regulatory label. Eligibility, substantive rule validity and general equitable tolling remain unresolved. Processing a timely tender is distinct from extending the deadline.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate undifferentiated relief and remand through the appellate court to identify actual front-desking, adequate class representation and any comparably concrete policy-caused inability to apply. No automatic extension or lawful-status award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Abbott Laboratories, 387 U.S. 136 — fitness and hardship applied to concrete effects.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 — concrete application and immediately operative burdens distinguished.
- McNary, 498 U.S. 479 — effective-review concern applied to refused filings.

### Mine Act channeling and meaningful judicial review

**Governing question:** Whether anticipatory Mine Act representation-and-posting disputes may bypass the Commission and appellate review.

**Current rule:** Where Congress’s intent is fairly discernible from the Mine Act’s language, structure and purpose and the claims can receive meaningful review, an anticipatory challenge to representation and posting requirements must proceed through the independent Commission and then the court of appeals. Connection to enforcement, relevant agency expertise and actual review availability inform that scheme-specific inquiry, not three mandatory elements for every administrative statute. Review adequacy must reflect actual injury and access; collection only after review, expedition and bounded temporary relief supported the conclusion on the absence of demonstrated irreversible compliance injury. Delayed payment alone does not establish adequate review.

**Authority by component:**

Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment on a distinct constitutional formulation and are not counted as joining this explanation.

**Present operation effective:** Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, 1994-01-19.

**Exceptions and limits:** Commission temporary relief under §815(b)(2) requires a hearing opportunity for each party, substantial likelihood of favorable findings and no adverse health-and-safety effect; relief is forbidden for §814(a) or (f) citations. Appellate relief under §816(a)(2) requires notice and hearing opportunity, substantial likelihood of success and protection of miners’ health and safety; it excludes §817(a) imminent-danger withdrawal orders and §814(a) or (f) citations. Section 816(c) makes review nonstaying. No stay of every posting citation, immunity from penalties or exclusion of every collateral constitutional claim is established.

### IIRIRA commencement and preserved review channels

**Governing question:** Which enacted commencement and transition provisions select the immigration review regime.

**Current rule:** IIRIRA §309(a) generally commences Title III-A and its amendments on April 1, 1997, with express exceptions for §309 and §§303(b)(2), 306(c), 308(d)(2)(D) and 308(d)(5). Corrected §306(c)(1) makes new §242(g) reach earlier, pending and future proceedings when §242(g) commences; it does not advance that provision’s commencement to September 30, 1996. For an alien in exclusion or deportation proceedings before April 1, 1997, corrected §309(c)(1) preserves the former procedure and judicial review, subject to its succeeding provisions. If the evidentiary hearing under former §§236 or 242 and 242B had not begun by that date, §309(c)(2) permits the Attorney General to elect amended chapter 4 procedure, giving notice at least thirty days before the evidentiary hearing; the earlier §235 or §242(a) hearing notice then counts as a §239 notice to confer immigration-judge jurisdiction. Under §309(c)(3), the Attorney General may terminate a qualifying proceeding lacking a final administrative decision and reinitiate under amended chapter 4; determinations in the terminated proceeding do not bind the new one. Section 309(c)(4) applies its transitional changes to qualifying final exclusion or deportation orders entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996. Its paragraph (E) lists discretionary decisions under §§212(c), 212(h), 212(i), 244 and 245; permanent §242(a)(2)(B)(i) instead lists §§212(h), 212(i), 240A, 240B and 245, and clause (ii) separately addresses other statutorily discretionary authority except §208(a) grants. These lists are not interchangeable. Section 306(c)(2) protects a judicial judgment or order actually entered under former INA §106, applying that section as amended by AEDPA §440, against an asserted effect of §306(c)(1) that would invalidate it or require reconsideration. It supplies no independent jurisdiction, reopens no merits review and does not bar ordinary appellate review of the judgment's correctness under governing law. The October 11, 1996 correction, effective September 30, left this protection unchanged.

### Immigration transition, completed adjudication and AEDPA review limits

**Governing question:** Ordinary judicial review and §212(c) eligibility during the AEDPA/IIRIRA transition.

**Current rule:** From April 1, 1997, corrected §306(c)(1) gives §242(g) cross-temporal reach. Subject to review provided in §242, and notwithstanding other law, the withdrawal concerns claims arising from the Attorney General’s identified decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders. It does not automatically encompass every legal challenge to a completed adjudication. Former §106 review is not textually provided in new §242; §309 preserves it only for claims outside §242(g)’s specified withdrawal. AEDPA §440(a) independently withdraws the listed criminal-alien ordinary appellate channel even for pending petitions, unless application attaches a supported new legal disability to a protected completed event. Filing, conviction, hardship and review expectations alone do not preserve that channel. Where an alien surrendered a colorable deportability defense before enactment to use the then-available waiver-and-review route, neither §440(d) nor §440(a) clearly withdraws original-claim waiver eligibility or ordinary review on account of that completed choice. That surrendered defense is one protected-event route, not a universal reliance requirement; pleas and eligibility interests are not categorically excluded. Section 440(a) does not clearly repeal independent §2241 jurisdiction. Subject to custody, proper respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable predicates, §2241 permits substantial legal or constitutional objections to executive authority to detain or deport, including an unlawful eligibility rule foreclosing discretionary consideration, and effective relief if warranted. Mere reweighing of discretionary equities is distinct, and §1651 supplies no independent jurisdiction.

### I. A live federal claim survives the second referendum

**Holding and operative rule:** A later referendum does not moot a substantial federal claim seeking recognition of an asserted entitlement created by the earlier vote when that relief remains capable of addressing a continuing injury. These plaintiffs maintain that claim; their ultimate failure to establish it does not eliminate jurisdiction.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls this threshold holding and Part II. Part III has the different coalition stated there.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the November 1998 vote does not dispose of the maintained demand to give legal effect to the asserted 1996 approval. Act 131 submitted a new question without expressly extinguishing that asserted entitlement. *Church of Scientology* separates the possibility of meaningful relief from ultimate entitlement to it; *Spencer* requires an actual surviving interest, which the maintained certification demand supplies here. *Steel Co.*, *NAACP v. Jones* and *Wisconsin Right to Life v. Paradise* require jurisdiction before contested merits and distinguish a substantial but unsuccessful claim from an absent controversy. Fees alone, hypothetical damages and an unproved collateral ruling supply no jurisdictional premise.

The federal plaintiffs were not parties to the state action, and the two organizations' participation as friends of the court did not make them parties. The bar against lower federal appellate review of a state-court loser's judgment therefore does not foreclose their own federal claim. The authoritative state construction remains binding as state law. Under *Ex parte Young*, a claim for future implementation of a federal right against responsible officials is not a demand for retrospective damages; under *Pennhurst*, it cannot become an instruction to follow a different federal construction of state law. This permits consideration of the constitutional claim without deciding the precise certification order available had a violation been proved. No mootness-based vacatur follows.
