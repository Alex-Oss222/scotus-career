## Event

INS v. Aguirre-Aguirre, No. 97-1754. Argued March 3, 1999; decided May 3, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 121 F.3d 521. The questions concern review of the Board of Immigration Appeals' interpretation and application of the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception to withholding under former §1253(h). The existing agency motion concerning alleged translation and transcription errors remains distinct from the legal questions presented.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed and the case remanded for proceedings under the correct legal framework.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Court rejects the Ninth Circuit's mandatory additional conditions but makes no new final withholding or asylum determination. The late translation/transcription contention is not decided; the existing BIA motion remains for lawful consideration, without an ordered new hearing, stay or removal instruction.

## Holdings

### Courts must respect the Board's permissible construction within actual delegated authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §§1103(a)(1) and former 1253(h), together with the Board's adjudicative assignment in 8 C.F.R. §3.1(d)(1), courts must respect a permissible Board construction of the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception after independently enforcing enacted limits. Interpretation, application and factual review retain their distinct requirements; neither agency status nor a general appeal to foreign-affairs expertise supplies automatic correctness.

**Authority:** Breyer's Parts I–II are joined by all nine Justices and control this holding and the next two merits holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Haggar requires authority before deference and distinguishes a lawful interpretive choice from particular validity and application. The present statute and Board assignment establish authority independently of Haggar's tariff rulemaking setting. NationsBank permits reasoned classification within genuine ambiguity; MCI and NCUA prohibit erasing statutory conditions. The exception concerns serious reasons for considering that the applicant committed a serious nonpolitical crime outside the United States before arriving here. No criminal conviction is required, but the territorial, temporal and substantive conditions remain operative. The humanitarian importance of withholding supports careful enforcement of those limits, not an exclusively judicial factor code. Cardoza-Fonseca and Stevic illustrate independent enforcement of statutory meaning; Elias-Zacarias preserves the separate standard for reviewing supported findings. The Board must apply its announced lawful construction to the actual record and explain its decision. The Court adopts neither unconditional deference nor an automatic new agency hearing whenever judicial review used a mistaken premise.

### A properly established crime exception requires no additional persecution-risk balance

**Holding and operative rule:** Once the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception is properly established, former §1253(h)(2) makes the paragraph (1) withholding protection inapplicable without an additional balancing of feared persecution against offense seriousness. The Board may address that exception while assuming the applicant could show that life or freedom would be threatened because of race, religion, nationality, membership in a particular social group or political opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute separately states the protection and its exceptions. A greater threatened harm does not dissolve a properly established crime exclusion through an additional condition Congress did not supply. The Handbook paragraph 156 argument relied on below presents a serious humanitarian objection, but does not change those distinct statutory inquiries. The Court finds no absence of persecution risk and creates no general discretion to deny withholding to an otherwise eligible applicant. Relevant political circumstances remain material under the lawful crime analysis. Discretionary asylum is a different question and receives no automatic disposition from this withholding rule.

### The political-criminal comparison does not require every judicially preferred subsidiary finding

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board may compare an offense's political character with its ordinary criminal character and consider gross disproportionality or atrocious conduct in determining which predominates. The statute requires neither a separate atrocity finding in every case nor findings that the violence was necessary to or successful in achieving its political objective; the Board must still assess actual political purpose, context and conduct under its announced standard.

**Controlling explanation:** Political motive alone does not establish a political offense, just as unlawfulness alone does not make one nonpolitical. The Board's explanation considered protest objectives alongside property destruction and civilian assaults and found the criminal character disproportionate. The Court reviews that actual explanation rather than adopting new factual findings from the contested testimony. The Ninth Circuit’s comparison with more severe conduct is a consistency objection, not necessarily a universal atrocity prerequisite. The Board’s actual disproportionate-conduct explanation answers that objection; the greater atrocity of another case is no minimum threshold for this alternative ground. Handbook paragraph 152 and the suggested McMullen comparison reinforce the importance of political context and disproportion, but the absence of a separate comparison with McMullen's atrocious acts does not defeat a determination resting on the supported political-criminal comparison. Atrocious means may defeat political characterization; atrocity is not necessary in every case. A clear causal relation to a political objective would not necessarily overcome a supported disproportion finding, and the Court reserves any independent causal-link requirement in a different case. Relevant comparisons and reasoned treatment of genuine inconsistency remain possible. No categorical rule about bus burnings, assault severity or unsuccessful protest is announced.

### The late record-error contention remains for its existing agency procedure

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court declines to decide the translation/transcription contention first raised here rather than before the Board or Court of Appeals; the applicant's existing BIA motion for a new hearing remains for determination through that lawful channel. If the BIA determines that modification of the record is necessary, it may determine whether the modified record warrants further consideration of withholding; no error, new hearing or outcome is presumed.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part III is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, eight rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins this component's disposition only and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged errors may be material if established, but this Court is not the first forum for resolving a contention not presented below. The existing motion supplies an actual avenue for that inquiry; its filing proves neither entitlement nor futility. The 1995 regulation's burden applies only if evidence indicates a withholding-denial ground; then the applicant must prove by a preponderance that the indicated ground does not apply. On the existing unrevised agency account, repeated gasoline-fueled bus burnings and civilian assaults, assessed against the political objective, indicate this exclusion and trigger that allocation. This uses conduct evidence, not silence, and makes no new credibility determination. The allocation is distinct from statutory serious reasons and judicial factual review. Failure to file a Board brief despite an extension is procedural history, not default, a concession, an adverse factual inference or proof that the conditional burden was unmet. The merits holdings rest independently on the lawful framework and stated Board explanation. A corrected record may affect their application without changing the statutory rule. The appellate remand itself commands no new Board hearing, stay or removal.

## Precedent Treatment

- Haggar preserves actual delegation, statutory boundaries and separate validity/application review; its tariff remand is not a universal agency-remand command.
- NationsBank permits reasoned classification within genuine statutory discretion.
- MCI and NCUA retain every enacted condition before deference.
- INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca and INS v. Stevic illustrate independent enforcement of statutory meaning; they do not assign every evaluative choice exclusively to courts.
- INS v. Elias-Zacarias distinguishes evidence permitting a finding from evidence compelling displacement under the relevant review standard.
- INS v. Yang preserves reasoned administration and the separation of eligibility from discretion; it does not require a new hearing merely because a court corrects a legal premise.
- AT&T and NFFE preserve the distinction between authority and the agency's actual lawful choice; the Board already supplied the pertinent comparison here.

## Law After Decision

The serious-nonpolitical-crime exception remains bounded by its actual statutory conditions and a permissible, reasoned Board construction. Courts may not impose a mandatory persecution-risk balance or additional political-necessity/success findings, nor treat atrocity as indispensable to the alternative disproportionate-conduct route. Actual record review and relevant political context retain their requirements; the existing motion remains for lawful agency determination. The Board’s separate discretionary asylum denial based on conduct against innocent civilians is not converted into an automatic statutory bar. No precedent is overruled.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit under the corrected statutory framework. The existing BIA motion concerning alleged translation and transcription errors remains for lawful consideration. If the BIA determines that record modification is necessary, it may determine whether the modified record warrants further consideration of withholding. No immediate removal, grant of withholding or asylum, automatic stay, new hearing, motion disposition or new deadline is ordered. The late contention is not decided on its factual merits.
