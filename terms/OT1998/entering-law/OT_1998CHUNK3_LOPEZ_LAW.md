## Reading selection: Holdings: Lopez

#### Lopez v. Monterey County

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1201.

**Decided:** November 6, 1996.

##### State compulsion does not exempt a covered County's administration

**Holding:** A covered political subdivision administers a voting change when its officials give the change operative effect within the subdivision. Section 5 therefore requires Monterey County to obtain preclearance before administering a postbaseline judicial-election change even if noncovered California compels the change and leaves the County no discretion; the duty is the County's and does not require California to preclear its laws in the abstract.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, Parts I-IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The duty belongs to the covered County. No general state-delegation test, statewide submission obligation, state-law supersession ruling, discriminatory-purpose finding or retrogression finding is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Presley is applied to the direct electoral operation; its internal-governance exclusion remains. Morse's bounded functional and statutory-sequencing principles are applied without enlarging its party-convention holding.

##### Electorate changes and the limits of prior submission

**Holding:** Replacing nine district electorates with one countywide electorate for municipal judges is a change affecting voting under §§5 and 14(c)(1). A later related submission does not preclear earlier changes that it failed to identify; the district court must determine the precise reach of the 1983 submission as to the final ordinance while treating the earlier unidentified consolidations as unprecleared.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, Parts I-IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The later submission did not identify the earlier ordinances. Whether it precleared the final 1983 ordinance depends on its actual terms; no blanket approval of the entire consolidation follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Presley, Allen and Perkins retain coverage for direct changes in electoral choice. McCain v. Lybrand and Clark v. Roemer retain their requirements of specific submission and narrow treatment of claimed administrative approval.

##### Effective designation and prospective enforcement

**Holding:** Section 4(b)'s administrative coverage determination may not be collaterally retried in this §5 enforcement action; statutory bailout under §4(a) is a distinct route. Delay cannot authorize prospective administration of an unprecleared change, although reliance, completed acts and practical timing may bear on lawfully available retrospective relief or the form of a lawful transition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, Parts I-IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide bailout eligibility or retrospective relief involving completed elections, judicial tenure, damages or judicial acts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Clark and NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission are applied to prospective enforcement; their remedial attention to actual circumstances is preserved. No new general retrospective equitable rule is announced.

##### Enforcement and a genuinely judicial temporary remedy

**Holding:** A §5 enforcement court may determine coverage and submission status and enforce nonuse, but may neither grant substantive preclearance nor authorize an unprecleared County or State electoral plan by calling it court ordered. A necessary, temporary plan wholly formulated by the court may operate without separate §5 preclearance only when independent lawful remedial authority supports it; it must comply with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, displace state law no further than necessary, and yield to a lawful precleared arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, Parts I-IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ordinary six-year at-large elections do not satisfy Clark's reserved extreme-circumstance possibility. The prior precleared four-division arrangement is an option only: neither preclearance nor Johnson's rule against a design-only racial injury resolves state-law linkage, population, municipality or residence objections, or establishes remedial authority. No replacement plan is selected.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Connor and McDaniel preserve the genuinely judicial/governmental-plan distinction; Clark supplies the nonuse rule and bounded emergency reservation; Morse and Shaw preserve the designated channels; Johnson governs the design-only constitutional objection without approving this particular plan.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand unanimously. Vacate authorization of the at-large election, enjoin prospective use until all operative post-November 1, 1968 changes are precleared, determine the actual 1983 submission and require expeditious submission of remaining changes through the Attorney General or D.D.C. No completed election, judicial office or judicial act is undone.

## Reading selection: Holdings: Young

#### Young v. Fordice

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2031.
**Decided:** March 31, 1997.

##### Whether discretionary implementation of the NVRA escapes preclearance

**Holding:** A covered jurisdiction must preclear voter-registration changes that embody state policy choices even when undertaken to comply with federal legislation. The NVRA preserves Voting Rights Act duties; wholly federally compelled conduct leaving no state discretion is not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** Mississippi retained choices over forms, instructions, agency designations, assistance, reporting, and communication of federal-only registration.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Voting Rights Coalition* preserves federal-registration obligations and distinct state-election questions without automatic approval. *Presley* places registration machinery inside the direct-voting boundary. *Lopez*'s state-compulsion holding does not decide the reserved wholly federal hypothetical.

##### Whether the Provisional Plan became the operative comparison baseline

**Holding:** This Provisional Plan never became the practice in force or effect for §5 comparison because its forty-one days of registration use, implementation by about one-third of officials, absence of an election held or imminent, and prompt abandonment when anticipated legislation failed establish its tentative character together. The Old System is the benchmark; no one factor creates a categorical threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** State-law illegality alone does not exclude an operative practice. Actual registrations and possible reliance are not erased; the Court determines neither every registration's validity nor a fixed duration, uptake, election-use, or intent test.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Perkins* and *Lockhart* preserve the possibility that actual practice controls despite state-law defects.

##### Whether preserving state-election qualifications avoids coverage of new registration machinery

**Holding:** The New System's altered instructions, agencies, manuals, reporting, assistance, and communication of federal-only and combined status are covered changes from the Old System despite retention of the old state-election qualifications. Minor and major changes alike require submission without proof of minority harm in the coverage suit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous; an independent coverage ground notwithstanding the Provisional Plan's failure to become the benchmark.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Allen* and *Hampton County* require submission of voting changes and leave discriminatory purpose and effect for the designated preclearance process.

##### Whether earlier approval reaches materially different later arrangements

**Holding:** Preclearance extends to sufficiently identified submitted changes, not unpresented materially different later policy choices. Ambiguity does not oblige the Attorney General to infer and approve every future contingency.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The former submission contemplated NVRA registration serving federal and state elections; it did not submit the later dual arrangement's altered communications and administration. Federal enactment and known legislative uncertainty do not supply blanket approval.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual submission questions require examination of their terms. The Court neither rescinds the earlier approval nor authorizes review of its merits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *McCain* and *Lopez* supply the specific-submission rule.

##### What relief may an enforcement court provide before substantive preclearance

**Holding:** The enforcement court decides coverage and submission status and prevents use of covered unprecleared practices; substantive preclearance belongs to the Attorney General or District Court for the District of Columbia. Relief must preserve lawful NVRA obligations and stay within independent remedial authority.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse summary judgment and remand to identify the components and submission status and enforce nonuse through fitted prospective relief. A State-selected plan does not become exempt merely by being called court ordered. A necessary temporary plan wholly formulated by a court may operate without separate approval only under independent lawful authority, consistently with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, displacing state law no further than necessary and yielding to a lawful precleared arrangement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular interim plan, emergency exception, merged rolls, registration cancellation, damages, discrimination or retrogression finding, or completed-election consequence is ordered. No new private-cause ruling is necessary given the United States' parallel suit.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Clark*, *Morse*, *Shaw*, and *Lopez* preserve nonuse and designated preclearance channels. *Connor* and *McDaniel* distinguish wholly judicial temporary relief from judicial adoption of governmental policy.

#### Foreman v. Dallas County

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-987 and 96-1389.

**Decided:** June 27, 1997.

##### Whether county discretion exempts changed methods for appointing polling officials

**Holding:** A covered county's change in the formula for selecting precinct officials who supervise election-day voting is subject to §5 when it differs from the operative coverage-date practice. Authority to exercise discretion under a precleared state statute does not exempt the new local method from preclearance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II–IV of the per curiam opinion, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The required comparison remains the County's actual November 1, 1972 practice; no covered change is found on this incomplete baseline record.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Presley's direct-voting boundary distinguishes polling administration from internal governing allocation. Allen and Hampton County cover practical and informal electoral changes. Monterey and Young reject exemptions based on administration or delegated discretion.

##### Whether approval of the 1985 state recodification precleared undisclosed county formulas

**Holding:** Preclearance of the state recodification and its identified changes to appointment timing and duration did not approve the County's partisan-selection formulas, which neither the code nor the explanatory submission identified. A distinct local method is approved only if the submission fairly identifies that method for review; a separate sufficiently specific submission or approval, if established, remains relevant.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II–IV of the per curiam opinion, unanimous.

**Material application:** The submission identified appointment beginning dates and duration, not the County's partisan-selection formulas.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** McCain and Clark require identification of the actual submitted change; Monterey and Young retain submission-specific approval rather than blanket approval of implementation.

##### What remand and nonuse follow from the missing 1972 baseline

**Holding:** The final dismissal must be vacated because the erroneous discretion and submission grounds do not resolve whether the County changed its November 1, 1972 appointment practice. On remand the District Court must identify that practice, compare each challenged later method and determine actual submission status; it must enjoin prospective use of any legally relevant covered change that lacks preclearance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II–IV of the per curiam opinion, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantive discriminatory purpose and effect remain for the Attorney General or D.D.C. The Court determines no completed-election consequence, appointment cancellation, damages or substitute partisan formula.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the final dismissal in No. 96-1389 and remand for the baseline, change and submission determinations. Nonuse is mandatory for an established covered change lacking preclearance; no universal injunction precedes the required findings and no automatic evidentiary hearing is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Morse and Young require a supported baseline. Clark supplies prospective nonuse; Monterey and Young require relief fitted to lawful remedial authority.

## Reading selection: Published 1996 Lopez

### Lopez v. Monterey County, No. 95-1201

Merits decision, November 6, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 8, 1996. On appeal from the three-judge United States District Court for the Northern District of California. The court authorized March 1996 municipal-court elections under a countywide, at-large system without preclearance of all operative changes and joined California for further litigation about the source of the system. Review concerns whether a covered County must preclear state-mandated changes despite having no policy discretion; the direct voting change, designation and prospective-laches defenses; and the limits on an enforcement court's use of an unprecleared governmental plan and a wholly court-developed temporary remedy. The plaintiffs seek reversal, prospective nonuse and lawful preclearance or interim proceedings.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The order authorizing the March 1996 countywide, at-large election is vacated. The district court must enjoin prospective administration of that system unless and until every operative post-November 1, 1968 voting change necessary to it has received §5 preclearance. It must determine the exact scope of the 1983 submission, identify changes still unprecleared, and require their expeditious submission through the Attorney General or the United States District Court for the District of Columbia. It must consider any necessary interim arrangement only within independent lawful remedial authority and the limits stated below. The Court orders no completed election undone, judicial office vacated, judicial act invalidated or particular replacement plan adopted.

#### Opinions and Joins

Justice Kennedy delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Its Parts I–IV correspond to the four controlling holdings below. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela files a concurrence, joined by no other Justice, adding his constitutional response to the designation objection and specifying his boundary for a wholly judicial plan.

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Parts I–IV of Kennedy's unanimous opinion control the four holdings below. The additional grounds in the concurrence do not form part of those holdings.

##### I. State compulsion does not exempt a covered County's administration

**Rule:** A covered political subdivision administers a voting change when its officials give the change operative effect within the subdivision. Section 5 therefore requires Monterey County to obtain preclearance before administering a postbaseline judicial-election change even if noncovered California compels the change and leaves the County no discretion; the duty is the County's and does not require California to preclear its laws in the abstract.

The Court holds that execution of an electoral change is distinct from enactment of it. Section 5 separately reaches a covered jurisdiction that enacts or seeks to administer a change. A condition of local policy discretion would erase the second route whenever a noncovered State required its covered subdivision to operate the new system. The statute contains no such condition. Monterey County conducts these public judicial elections and gives the countywide electorate operative effect; the voters experience the same change regardless of its legislative origin.

Presley supplies the distinction between a change in voting and a transfer of internal governing duties. Morse confirms the importance of the practice's actual electoral operation while preserving the designated substantive-preclearance process. Neither already decided this local-discretion question, and the Court adopts no general state-delegation test. The holding answers the legal question even assuming California law entirely displaced the County ordinances; it does not decide that disputed state-law characterization. Nor does it impose a statewide submission obligation, determine discriminatory purpose or retrogression, or confer general federal power to redesign California's judiciary.

##### II. Electorate changes and the limits of prior submission

**Rule:** Replacing nine district electorates with one countywide electorate for municipal judges is a change affecting voting under §§5 and 14(c)(1). A later related submission does not preclear earlier changes that it failed to identify; the district court must determine the precise reach of the 1983 submission as to the final ordinance while treating the earlier unidentified consolidations as unprecleared.

The Court holds that this consolidation changes who elects each judge, rather than merely the work performed after election. At the November 1, 1968 baseline, voters elected the judges of their separate districts. The six ordinances adopted between 1972 and 1983 ultimately placed the judges before one countywide electorate. Presley preserves coverage for that direct alteration of electoral choice. Allen and Perkins supply the same distinction through their treatment of electoral mechanisms and boundaries.

McCain and Clark require an actual, identifiable submission of the change for which preclearance is claimed; ambiguity is not blanket approval of earlier practices. The record establishes that California's 1983 submission did not identify or describe the earlier ordinances. Whether it reached the final 1983 ordinance must be determined from the submission's actual terms. The County cannot obtain approval of the whole consolidation sequence merely by pointing to that later submission. Coverage and lack of preclearance establish the nonuse obligation, not a finding that the system has discriminatory purpose or retrogressive effect.

##### III. Effective designation and prospective enforcement

**Rule:** Section 4(b)'s administrative coverage determination may not be collaterally retried in this §5 enforcement action; statutory bailout under §4(a) is a distinct route. Delay cannot authorize prospective administration of an unprecleared change, although reliance, completed acts and practical timing may bear on lawfully available retrospective relief or the form of a lawful transition.

The Court holds that the County's published designation remains operative in this case. Congress separately restricted review of the coverage determination and provided a judicial bailout process. The County has not obtained release from coverage through that process. Its objection cannot convert this enforcement suit into a collateral retrial of the administrative findings.

Nor does the age of the ordinances excuse their future use without submission. Section 5 conditions administration on preclearance; the passage of time supplies no substitute. Clark enforces that statutory sequence, and Hampton County confirms that elapsed time and electoral implementation do not themselves remove the review obligation. The practical cost of delay warrants a fitted transition, not continued use of the same uncleared governmental plan as an ordinary electoral system. The Court makes no factual bailout determination, awards no damages, and decides no backward-looking relief concerning a completed election, judicial tenure or completed judicial acts.

##### IV. Enforcement and a genuinely judicial temporary remedy

**Rule:** A §5 enforcement court may determine coverage and submission status and enforce nonuse, but may neither grant substantive preclearance nor authorize an unprecleared County or State electoral plan by calling it court ordered. A necessary, temporary plan wholly formulated by the court may operate without separate §5 preclearance only when independent lawful remedial authority supports it; it must comply with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, displace state law no further than necessary, and yield to a lawful precleared arrangement.

The Court holds that judicial approval does not change the governmental origin of an electoral plan. Connor distinguishes a wholly judicial remedy; McDaniel prevents a court order from insulating governmental policy choices from submission. Here the district court reinstated substantially the County's at-large system and authorized ordinary six-year terms. It did not formulate a wholly judicial temporary remedy. Clark's reservation for an extreme circumstance supplies no basis on this record: prolonged notice of the problem, abandonment of the County's D.C. preclearance action and ordinary terms do not establish a last-minute necessity. This decision recognizes no broader emergency exception.

Morse and Shaw preserve Congress's separate preclearance channels. The enforcement court cannot decide purpose or effect itself, even in light of the County's stipulation concerning its inability to prove nonretrogression. Administrative difficulty does not enlarge its authority.

The earlier four-division arrangement is a material option for consideration. It retained a single municipal court and received preclearance on March 6, 1995; its terms reportedly end in January 1997. Johnson v. Miller prevents racial predominance in a shared territorial electorate, standing alone, from supplying a constitutional defect. But neither that rule nor preclearance answers the separate objections concerning electoral and jurisdictional linkage, minimum population, divided municipalities and candidate residence, or establishes independent remedial authority. The Court selects no interim plan. Those matters, the exact 1983 submission and any actual officeholder circumstances require lawful determination below.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Presley v. Etowah County Commission; Mack v. Russell County Commission:** Distinguishes internal allocations of governing authority from the direct electorate change here; the former exclusion remains intact.
- **Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia:** Applies its bounded concern with actual electoral operation and its separate preclearance channels without enlarging its party-convention holding; no general delegation test or new private-action rule is adopted.
- **Allen v. State Board of Elections:** Applies protection of electoral choice to the changed means of electing judges.
- **Perkins v. Matthews:** Applies coverage of changes in electoral boundaries and the electorate, without equating coverage with discrimination.
- **McCain v. Lybrand:** Requires identification of the submitted change and rejects blanket approval of unidentified earlier practices.
- **Clark v. Roemer:** Enforces nonuse pending preclearance and preserves, without applying, its narrow extreme-circumstance reservation; the present ordinary six-year elections do not qualify.
- **NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission:** Passage of time and implementation do not eliminate the submission requirement; actual circumstances remain relevant to lawful relief.
- **Connor v. Johnson:** Retains the distinction for a wholly judicial remedial plan.
- **McDaniel v. Sanchez:** A court's adoption of governmental policy does not transform it into an exempt judicial plan.
- **Shaw v. Reno, designated-channel holding:** Substantive preclearance remains with the Attorney General or D.D.C.; no enforcement-court substitute is authorized.
- **Johnson v. Miller:** Racial predominance in a shared territorial electorate alone does not establish constitutional injury or trigger strict scrutiny; separate statutory and constitutional objections remain open.

#### Law After the Decision

Section 5 now expressly reaches a covered County's nondiscretionary administration of state-mandated voting changes. The election must not proceed under the unprecleared governmental system merely because the State requires it, delay has occurred or a federal court has ordered it used. Existing direct-voting coverage, specific-submission, designated-channel and genuinely judicial-remedy rules remain. The new construction neither places all California law under §5 nor changes the substantive purpose/effect burdens. The temporary divisions remain an option for lawful consideration, with their independent defects and authority unresolved. No new general rule governing retrospective equitable relief is announced.

#### Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the Court's entire opinion and judgment. He also concludes that South Carolina v. Katzenbach, which sustains the Act's coverage and preclearance machinery, and City of Rome, which sustains its operation against covered governmental units, foreclose the facial constitutional objection to the County's operative designation. This is distinct from the Court's statutory bar on collateral retrial of the coverage findings. He further states that the wholly judicial exception does not apply when a court adopts material electoral policy choices proposed, required or previously selected by the County or the State. Necessity and independent remedial authority must support any genuinely court-formulated temporary plan; he selects no plan and decides no purpose or effect question. No other Justice joins these additional formulations.

No deadline, permanent electoral plan, completed-election consequence or specific judicial tenure outcome is fixed here.
