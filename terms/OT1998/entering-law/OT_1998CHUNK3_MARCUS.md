## Standards and Tests selection: Editorial selection in public-broadcaster candidate debates

### Editorial selection in public-broadcaster candidate debates

**Governing question:** What forum rules govern a public broadcaster’s selectively composed candidate debate?

**Current rule:** A debate retaining actual editorial selection rather than general access for every qualified candidate is a nonpublic forum. Exclusions must reasonably serve the program’s journalistic purpose and be viewpoint neutral. Genuine assessments of public support and journalistic significance may govern limited participation; reasonableness does not demand the best possible format. Ballot qualification alone does not establish a right to participate, and no additional universal requirement of numerical criteria, a written policy or advance publication applies.

**Authority by component:**

- Selective-access classification and reasonable viewpoint-neutral editorial choice: Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes, No. 96-779 — Holdings 1–2, 1998-05-18.

**Present operation effective:** Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes, 1998-05-18.

**Exceptions and limits:** Public ownership or the label journalism supplies no immunity. Courts examine actual operation and pretext; sham criteria, incumbent favoritism, selective administration and exclusion for an unpopular viewpoint are not authorized. A program genuinely opened to qualifying candidates presents a different access question.

**Holdings navigation:** Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes — Selective public-broadcast candidate debates.

#### Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-779.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### 1. This selectively composed candidate debate is a nonpublic forum

**Holding:** A public broadcaster's candidate debate is a nonpublic forum when its actual program retains editorial selection and has not been opened generally to every qualified candidate. Exclusions must be reasonable in light of the program's journalistic purpose and viewpoint neutral; neither public ownership nor the label journalism supplies immunity from those requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. That six-Justice coalition controls both holdings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The candidates express private views, but the actual program retains individual editorial selection. A generally opened debate presents a different question. Neither public identity nor the broadcaster’s own label establishes immunity from viewpoint review.

##### 2. The supported exclusion is reasonable and viewpoint neutral

**Holding:** A genuine assessment of public support and journalistic significance may justify exclusion from this selective debate when it reasonably serves the program's purpose and is not a pretext for viewpoint discrimination. The established criteria and application satisfy that rule here; the Constitution imposes no additional prerequisite that future criteria be published.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The supported public-support and journalistic criteria bear on the program, and the verdict rejects political pressure and viewpoint disagreement. Ballot qualification alone does not require admission. Sham criteria, incumbent favoritism, viewpoint pretext and inconsistent denial under actual open-access terms remain prohibited; no numerical formula or universal written policy is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed, 6–3, with directions to enter judgment for the Commission on the reviewed forum claim. No future criteria-publication condition, damages, immunity ruling or compulsory broadcast is ordered; the abandoned statutory-access claim is not revived.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Cornelius and Perry Education:** Applied to actual selective access and reasonable, viewpoint-neutral restrictions.
- **Rosenberger:** Preserves private viewpoints within the actual program rather than compelling universal access.
- **Good News/Good Sports and Bronx Household:** Preserve actual comparators and genuine forum boundaries; the planned selective debate differs from broad rental access.
- **Denver Area:** No universal public-broadcast classification or government editorial immunity is drawn from its separate components or incompatible rationales.

#### Norman v. Reed, 502 U.S. 279 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1126 and 90-1435.
**Decided:** January 14, 1992.

##### Whether completion of the election moots the ballot-access dispute

**Holding:** Completion of an election does not moot a ballot-access dispute where election results carry continuing party-qualification consequences and the short election cycle presents a reasonably recurring controversy that otherwise evades review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, and Kennedy; these seven Justices supply a majority of the eight participants.

**Material application:** Several candidates exceeded the five-percent threshold that could alter later party qualification. The challenged restrictions operated on a timetable too short for complete review before voting, and the dispute retained concrete consequences.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not revive the unappealed water-district candidates or order a new election.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Moore v. Ogilvie is applied to election restrictions with continuing legal consequences after ballots are counted.

For the party-name and signature holdings in Norman v. Reed, decided January 14, 1992, Nos. 90-1126 and 90-1435, see Election Law, “Ballot access and political parties.”

#### Spencer v. Kemna

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-7171.

**Decided:** 1998-03-03.

##### Custody at filing and a continuing controversy are separate requirements

**Holding:** Section 2254’s custody requirement is satisfied when the petitioner is held under the challenged revocation at filing; later expiration does not retroactively defeat that threshold. Article III nonetheless requires a concrete continuing injury after the challenged sentence expires.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part I: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela agrees with the custody disposition without this additional ground; Stevens also accepts custody at filing.

**Material application:** Spencer was held under the challenged revocation when he filed. His unrelated later incarceration is not an unexpired consecutive-sentence aggregate; it does not preserve this controversy.

##### The asserted collateral consequences and exceptions do not keep this petition live

**Holding:** After this parole-revocation sentence expires, collateral injury is not presumed merely from the revocation; the petitioner must show an actual continuing consequence redressable in this action. Speculative future parole, sentencing or evidentiary uses, the possibility of recurrence and litigation delay do not supply that showing on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part II: Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the continuing-injury rule and mootness result. The seven Associates also adopt the exception and litigation-delay applications; Stone-Zsela agrees in those dispositions without their additional rationales.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision distinguishes conviction challenges and adopts no categorical exclusion of reputational injury. The capable-of-repetition exception requires both duration too short for review and a reasonable expectation that this claimant will again face the action. Delay, possible injury to others and fault or estoppel do not create jurisdiction; expiration by the sentence’s terms establishes no strategic cessation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Dismissal is affirmed as moot alone, without constitutional-merits preclusion. No writ, new hearing, expungement, damages or exoneration is ordered.

##### An unresolved future civil remedy does not create this habeas controversy

**Holding:** Heck and Edwards do not establish an unavoidable favorable-invalidation bar for every former prisoner: their controlling rules expressly reserve that category and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity. The unresolved scope of a possible later civil action does not create a damages claim in this habeas case or dispense with continuing Article III injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part III: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela agrees in the ancillary disposition without this rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reservations for former prisoners and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity remain open. No affirmative §1983 exception or universal favorable-invalidation bar is adopted, and no damages claim is pleaded in this habeas case.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lane is applied for the continuing-interest inquiry while its unchallenged-revocation posture is distinguished; no automatic consequence is inferred for this revocation.
- Lujan and Arizonans preserve an actual personal controversy throughout review. Arizonans’ vacatur remedy does not require clearing a nonexistent intervening merits judgment here.
- Garlotte’s continuous consecutive-sentence aggregate is distinguished from Spencer’s unrelated later custody; custody at filing remains sufficient for the statutory threshold.
- MacArthur preserves supported actual personal information interests without creating a universal reputation-based habeas rule.
- Heck and Edwards retain their bounded invalidation prerequisite and express former-prisoner/realistic-opportunity reservations; no affirmative section 1983 exception or universal bar is added.

### Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes, No. 96-779

Decision, May 18, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 8, 1997; decided May 18, 1998. On review of the Eighth Circuit's judgment, 93 F.3d 497, reversing judgment for the Commission on Forbes's First Amendment challenge to exclusion from a televised candidate debate.

The state broadcaster planned a selective, moderated debate program. Its spring 1992 selection category covered major-party candidates and other candidates with strong popular support. Forbes later qualified for the ballot, requested inclusion and was denied. Staff cited lack of campaign organization, appreciable voter support and press recognition as a serious candidate. The jury found that political pressure and disagreement with his views did not cause the exclusion. The question is whether this actual program created a forum requiring his admission and whether the exclusion was constitutionally permissible.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

The case returns for implementation of judgment for the Commission on this claim. No prospective publication requirement, fixed admission formula or compelled future broadcast is imposed.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. Justice Stevens dissents, joined by Justices Souter and Ginsburg; they would sustain the appellate judgment because the government's candidate-selection standards were insufficiently definite, without requiring universal ballot access.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. This selectively composed candidate debate is a nonpublic forum

**Holding and operative rule:** A public broadcaster's candidate debate is a nonpublic forum when its actual program retains editorial selection and has not been opened generally to every qualified candidate. Exclusions must be reasonable in light of the program's journalistic purpose and viewpoint neutral; neither public ownership nor the label journalism supplies immunity from those requirements.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. That six-Justice coalition controls both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The candidates express their own political views in this debate, so the broadcaster cannot treat their speech simply as the State's message. Yet a selective invitation program is not a general opening of airtime. Cornelius and Perry Education distinguish selective access from a designated public forum and preserve reasonable, viewpoint-neutral management.

The Commission planned a limited, moderated exchange and selected individual participants. Ballot eligibility did not itself open the program to every candidate. Rosenberger protects viewpoints within the opportunity actually created; it does not enlarge every opportunity into general access. Bronx likewise protects comparable private expression in a broad community forum while preserving genuine program boundaries. Its rental setting does not remove the broadcaster's supported selective function here. Denver Area supplies no categorical broadcasting classification or government editorial immunity.

The holding concerns this program, not every public broadcast or every candidate debate. A debate genuinely opened to all qualifying candidates would present a different access question. The State may neither disguise viewpoint exclusion as program definition nor rely on its own classification without examination of actual operation.

**Precedent treatment:** Cornelius and Perry Education supply the selective-access rule; Rosenberger and Bronx preserve viewpoint equality within genuine boundaries; Denver Area is not expanded beyond its distinct components.

##### 2. The supported exclusion is reasonable and viewpoint neutral

**Holding and operative rule:** A genuine assessment of public support and journalistic significance may justify exclusion from this selective debate when it reasonably serves the program's purpose and is not a pretext for viewpoint discrimination. The established criteria and application satisfy that rule here; the Constitution imposes no additional prerequisite that future criteria be published.

**Controlling explanation:** Limited discussion time supports selecting a manageable group that will provide a useful exchange. Reasonableness does not require the best or only conceivable debate design. The advance selection category and staff's supported assessment of organization, voter support and press recognition bear on the program's purpose. The jury's express rejection of political pressure and viewpoint disagreement confirms the absence of those asserted improper causes; it does not by itself substitute for examining the criteria's reasonable relationship to the program.

The contrary standards argument rightly identifies the danger of officials controlling political access through vague judgments of seriousness. It does not establish an additional constitutional requirement of a numerical formula, universal written policy or prospective publication for this otherwise reasonable, neutral selection. Courts retain authority to test genuine viewpoint neutrality and pretext.

Ballot qualification alone neither establishes strong public support nor defeats a legitimate selective format. This holding does not authorize incumbent favoritism, sham criteria, selective administration, exclusion because a viewpoint is unpopular or denial from a program actually opened on terms the candidate satisfies. It decides no damages amount, official immunity, election remedy or future broadcast obligation.

**Precedent treatment:** The nonpublic-forum reasonableness and viewpoint rules are applied to the established record without adding a new publication or formula requirement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Cornelius and Perry Education:** Applied to actual selective access and reasonable, viewpoint-neutral restrictions.
- **Rosenberger:** Preserves private viewpoints within the actual program rather than compelling universal access.
- **Good News/Good Sports and Bronx Household:** Preserve actual comparators and genuine forum boundaries; the planned selective debate differs from broad rental access.
- **Denver Area:** No universal public-broadcast classification or government editorial immunity is drawn from its separate components or incompatible rationales.

#### Law After Decision

This selective debate may use reasonable, genuinely viewpoint-neutral judgments about public support and journalistic significance. Forbes's exclusion satisfies that standard on the established record. Judicial review for pretext and discrimination remains; no universal candidate-access, published-criteria or numerical-threshold rule is created.

#### Separate Writings

Stevens, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, would sustain the appellate reversal on a narrower ground than an automatic right of every ballot-qualified candidate to participate. The State has created a platform for candidates' competing private political speech, and sufficiently definite, reviewable selection standards are necessary to constrain its discretion. Cornelius's access principles, Shuttlesworth's objection to uncontrolled permit discretion and Rosenberger's neutral-allocation requirement inform that conclusion. The spring category existed, but major-party status and strong popular support leave too much room for officials' judgments about seriousness. The jury's rejection of improper motive does not answer this distinct objection to the governing standards. The dissent permits reasonable selection, prescribes no particular polling number, finds no viewpoint bias contrary to the verdict, and would order no automatic damages or future broadcast.

#### Source Notes

The reported record at 523 U.S. 666 and the underlying Eighth Circuit judgment identify the advance selection category, invitations, ballot qualification, staff testimony and jury findings. The original trial papers are not reproduced. The verdict rejects improper motive; it is not a finding about every conceivable access standard. No broader past selection practice or later election fact is supplied.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower courts must implement judgment for the Commission on Forbes's reviewed First Amendment forum claim. No additional criterion-publication requirement applies. The abandoned statutory-access claim is not revived, and no separate damages, immunity, costs or future-election determination is made.
