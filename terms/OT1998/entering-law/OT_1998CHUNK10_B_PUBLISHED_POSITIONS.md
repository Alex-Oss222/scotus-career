### Bragdon v. Abbott, No. 97-156

Merits decision, June 25, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 30, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the First Circuit, 107 F.3d 934, affirming summary judgment for Abbott. Bragdon refused routine office dental treatment after Abbott disclosed HIV on September 16, 1994 and offered hospital treatment carrying an additional facility charge.

The granted questions concern Abbott's disability under the ADA and the legal treatment of a provider's judgment under the direct-threat exception. The separate independent-review and whole-record trial questions were not granted. State-law claims are outside the federal grant.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abbott's actual disability | Affirmed, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas | Abbott is treated as disabled on this record |
| Direct threat | Vacated and remanded, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Reconsider under the objective method and controlling source distinctions; ultimate risk and whole-record triability are not decided |

The lower court may reach the same summary-judgment outcome. No compulsory trial, expert appointment, hearing, new-evidence entitlement, treatment order, damages or hospital obligation is imposed. State-law claims receive no new substantive ruling.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Opinion of the Court, common objective-method part | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Governs direct-threat inquiry | Individualized, conduct-date objective evidence and precautions; all nine Justices |
| Opinion of the Court, public-health/source-remand part | Kennedy | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Supports bounded risk vacatur | Founded special but nonconclusive public-health weight and reconsideration of CDC/Association reliance; five Justices |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | Author alone | Supports both component judgments | Disability and objective-method joins; risk remand on that method, without adopting the special-weight/source rationale |
| Concurrence | Stevens | Breyer | Joins the full Court opinion and risk remand | Would prefer outright affirmance, but agrees with the bounded reconsideration ordered |
| Concurrence | Ginsburg | Author alone | Joins the full Court opinion | Broader life-choice observations and cautious remand |

O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas accept or assume the undisputed impairment premise; they do not join the affirmative general impairment rationale. Stevens and Breyer's affirmance preference is not an opposing remand vote.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. HIV can impair before visible symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity

**Holding and operative rule:** HIV disease is a physical impairment within the ADA before its most serious symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity. A claimant under the actual-disability branch must still show that the impairment substantially limits that individual's activity; diagnosis or reproductive-system impairment alone does not establish every element.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the disability part. The other three accept or assume impairment for this case without joining its general rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 12102(2)(A) separates impairment, major activity and individual substantial limitation. The Title III regulation, §36.104, expressly includes symptomatic and asymptomatic HIV and recognizes physiological disorders of relevant systems. Section 12201(a), except where the ADA otherwise provides, preserves at least Rehabilitation Act Title V standards and regulations. Arline's contagious-disease analysis does not turn its reservation about asymptomatic carriers into an exclusion from this statute.

The illustrative activity list is not confined to publicly performed, economic or daily activities. Reproduction's significance is not diminished because it is not continuously performed. The contrary reading would convert examples into a restriction the text does not impose. At the same time, a reproductive-system impairment and a substantial limitation of reproduction are separate inquiries. Yeskey resolves institutional coverage at its own scope and supplies no automatic individual entitlement here. No record-of or regarded-as holding is made.

##### 2. Abbott's own reproductive activity is substantially limited on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** Abbott's specific unchallenged testimony that HIV controlled her decision not to bear a child, considered with the supported perinatal transmission danger, establishes substantial limitation of her reproduction. Complete inability to conceive, a pre-infection plan, an attempted pregnancy or willingness to accept the danger is not an additional condition.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice disability coalition directly adopts this application.

**Controlling explanation:** The reported risks were approximately 25 percent without treatment and 8 percent with treatment. Even the treated figure represents a substantial danger to the child on this historical record. These figures are evidence here, not a legal threshold or a conclusion about current treatment. The Court therefore need not resolve whether mitigating measures generally must be disregarded.

The general answer denying impaired life functions describes daily functioning and does not retract the specific childbearing account. The opposing argument correctly demands a limitation in this person's life, rather than an abstract medical risk. Abbott supplies that connection. Unknown prior plans do not defeat her specific unchallenged testimony or justify resolving credibility against her. Ordinary Rule 56 summary-judgment burdens remain; no automatic rule covers every person with HIV, every activity or every future medical condition.

##### 3. Direct threat requires objective individualized assessment, not professional sincerity

**Holding and operative rule:** A covered provider may exclude a person only for a significant risk to others' health or safety that cannot be eliminated by modification of policies, practices or procedures or auxiliary aids or services. The provider must make an individualized reasonable judgment based on current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence, addressing the risk's nature, duration, severity, probability of injury and reasonable mitigation; sincere belief or professional status supplies no special legal deference.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly support this common objective method.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 12182(b)(3) and 36.208 distinguish significant risk from any imaginable residual risk. They require neither absolute safety nor confirmed past transmission as a universal prerequisite. The relevant standpoint is information reasonably available to the provider and profession when treatment was refused, September 16, 1994. Arline supports individual medical assessment but gives no private provider a subjective immunity. Daubert and Joiner require reliable foundation and fit; they do not decide the substantive dental risk or impose numerical quantification in every case.

The hospital offer creates no independent defense. The lower court reports that no comparative-hospital-safety theory was pursued or established, and the offer alone proves no privileges or safeguards. Section 12201(d) does not require Abbott to accept that accommodation or charge. The Court does not require service outside professional competence or bar precautions justified by objective evidence in another case. No later breakthrough overwhelmingly vindicating the concern is established; the possible relevance of such a development to prospective relief is not categorically foreclosed.

##### 4. Public-health authority has founded, nonconclusive weight; the cited sources require bounded reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule:** Reasonable medical judgments of public-health authorities receive special but nonconclusive weight, subject to a credible scientific challenge. That weight attaches only to a supported judgment within the source's actual role, date and scope; a private professional policy is not automatically an official public-health assessment. The direct-threat judgment is vacated for reconsideration of the CDC and Association materials on those terms, without deciding whole-record triability.

**Authority:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join the public-health/source-remand part. Stone-Zsela joins the objective method and remand judgment, not this distinct ground; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas support the risk judgment on their contrary official-weight approach.

**Controlling explanation:** Arline supports respect for reasonable public-health judgment while allowing supported scientific disagreement. The First Circuit largely used the correct objective method, rejected conclusive official consensus, permitted qualified contrary evidence and considered all eight proffers together. Its continuing rebuttable presumption was not an irrebuttable command. The Court does not invalidate every such presumption or find that cumulative review was omitted.

The CDC's 1993 recommendation says universal precautions reduce risk; it does not expressly assess the remaining risk's level or say further measures are unnecessary. The circuit drew those inferences. Their foundation must be considered before official authority is treated as resolving the defense. The Association's 1991 policy affirmatively describes safe office care, but it is a private professional policy whose scientific and professional-duty bases must be distinguished. The record does not establish that it was merely ethical, scientifically worthless or incapable of an implicit medical judgment. These source questions can affect the granted deference issue without deciding whether all evidence already requires trial.

The remand requires ordinary reconsideration of the existing sources and other evidence, without requiring reconstruction of a policy history, automatic supplementation or a new expert proceeding. It establishes neither zero risk nor significant risk. Personal sharp-injury observations and a scientific risk inference are distinct; the Court makes no finding that competent firsthand testimony was actually excluded. The lower court may again find summary judgment appropriate.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Arline:** Supplies individualized assessment and founded respect for public-health judgment; neither its carrier reservation nor private-physician reservation creates an exclusion or subjective defense.
- **Yeskey:** Institutional coverage does not resolve this individual's disability; its reserved question remains distinct.
- **Daubert and Joiner:** Reliable foundation, fit and supported inference remain separate from substantive risk and whole-record triability. No automatic exclusion, expert appointment or numerical threshold follows.
- **Ordinary summary-judgment and question-scope rules:** Supported inferences and actual individual proof remain required; the granted method question does not itself enlarge review to the separately ungranted trial issue.

#### Law After Decision

Abbott is disabled on the established reproduction record. The general rule recognizes impairment before visible symptoms without treating every HIV diagnosis as complete statutory disability. The direct-threat inquiry remains individualized and objective, with significant risk and reasonable mitigation assessed at the relevant time. Founded public-health judgments receive special but rebuttable weight; a private policy and an inferred official conclusion require their actual foundation. Ultimate risk, whole-record triability, other disability routes and state-law merits remain unresolved.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the disability and common objective-method holdings and supports vacatur for assessment of contemporaneous objective evidence and reasonable precautions. Professional sincerity cannot replace that support, but the ADA permits refusal when a significant risk that reasonable measures cannot eliminate is proved. He does not adopt the separate public-health special-weight or specific source-foundation rationale, decide ultimate risk, require treatment outside professional competence or declare every person with HIV disabled.

**Stevens, joined by Breyer, concurring.** They would prefer affirmance because the lower court applied the objective approach and thoroughly considered the evidence, including its cumulative force. They nevertheless join the entire Court opinion and the bounded source reconsideration, which leaves the lower court free to reach the same result. This is agreement with the remand entered, not an opposing vote or a trial mandate.

**Ginsburg, concurring.** She joins the cautious, informed remand and the complete disability analysis. HIV can affect family relations, employment potential, self-care and access to care before visible symptoms; invisibility does not justify discrimination. Those broader observations establish no additional individual finding, alternative regarded-as holding or automatic diagnosis-based rule.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissenting on disability.** They assume undisputed impairment but reject reproduction as the presented statutory activity: the illustrative functions concern repeated ordinary functioning, and naming reproductive-system disorders does not itself answer the separate activity question. Independently, even assuming classification, Abbott's post-diagnosis choice, unknown prior plans and general negative answer do not establish a present individual limitation to their satisfaction. They distinguish a risk affecting choice from demonstrated present loss and do not adopt utter inability as the standard. They would reject this actual-disability determination, without deciding every alternative claim or ordering a trial under the classification they reject.

**O'Connor, separately dissenting on disability.** Childbirth is not generally comparable to the representative recurring activities. She leaves open substantial limitation if reproduction were recognized and other intimate or family activities. Accepting impairment for this case is not an affirmative general impairment rationale or a finding that HIV is harmless.

**Scalia's risk-method part, joined by O'Connor and Thomas.** All three agree with objective, contemporaneous evidence and risk vacatur but reject special scientific weight from official endorsement in litigation between private parties. Credentials, foundation, fit and reasoning should stand on their own; review of an agency's own entrusted action differs. They view the circuit's continuing official presumption as an error requiring reconsideration, not merely a source-identification problem. Possible dental transmissions may have probative value without confirmation, and other occupational evidence cannot be rejected solely because its occupational name differs; actual exposure conditions and the link to this procedure still matter. The seven dental cases are possible, not confirmed, and the forty-two occupational cases are not dental cases. Their more favorable assessment of this evidence supplies no independent whole-record trial holding, proved significant-risk finding or mandated trial.

#### Source Notes

The complete lower opinion, 107 F.3d 934, supplies the cross-motion posture, specific and general testimony, actual continuing presumption, eight proffers and cumulative review. The relevant federal provisions are 42 U.S.C. §§12102(2), 12181(7)(F), 12182(b)(3), 12201(a), (d), and the contemporaneous Title III regulations, 28 C.F.R. §§36.104 and 36.208. The CDC recommendation, Association policy and court’s inference are distinct. Later categorical statements or later study descriptions do not become September 1994 medical knowledge. The historical risk percentages are evidence in this record, not contemporary medical advice or universal legal thresholds.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The disability determination is affirmed. The direct-threat portion is vacated and returned through the First Circuit for reconsideration under the objective method and controlling source distinctions. The lower court determines the consequence for the judgment without a commanded trial, new evidence, hearing or treatment. State-law claims remain subject to ordinary lower proceedings without a new substantive state-law ruling.

**End of entry: Bragdon v. Abbott, Merits decision, June 25, 1998.**

### Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County, Nos. 97-1115 and 97-1160

Plenary merits decision on submission, September 21, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

The petitions of Metropolitan Dade County and Allied Minority Contractors Association concern the Eleventh Circuit's September 2, 1997 judgment, amended October 8, 1997, 122 F.3d 895, affirming declarations and permanent injunctions against three contracting programs. No exact Supreme Court oral-argument date is established.

The questions concern the evidence and fit required for local Black-owned (BBE), Hispanic-owned (HBE) and women-owned (WBE) construction preferences. The programs cover County-funded construction contracts over $25,000 in general building (SIC 15), heavy construction (SIC 16) and specialty trades (SIC 17), with respective 15, 19 and 11 percent goals. Eligibility requires actual local operation and covered ownership and control, normally at small-business size; a larger firm may retain certification on showing it continues to experience the addressed discrimination.

The measures include set-asides ordinarily requiring three available covered firms; with two, competitive bidding may be waived only if neither obtained a like County contract within three years and price analysis establishes a competitive price. Contract-specific subcontract goals permit waiver for unavailability at a competitive price, but not inability to obtain bonding. Project goals draw on a covered subcontractor pool. Bid preferences lower the comparison price by up to 10 percent without lowering the amount paid; selection factors may raise nonprice evaluation by up to 10 percent. Annual efficacy and five-year continuation reviews apply.

The County offered 1989–91 and 1993 contract studies, broader marketplace, subcontract, business-entry and income studies, and anecdotal accounts. Hurricane Andrew's effect led to omission of 1992. Most firm-size-adjusted award disparities disappeared; residual results differed by group, sector and year. The existing BBE program could affect observed results, but no counterfactual dataset establishes its exact effect. Anecdotal accounts were chiefly BBE, some WBE and none HBE. They are evidence offered, not findings that every incident occurred.

All nine Justices participate.

| Hispanic-owned business program (HBE) | Affirm invalidation and permanent injunction for insufficient narrow fit, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Stone-Zsela | Program-specific permanent injunction remains; Stone-Zsela would vacate and remand under the same proposal, maintaining interim restraint. |
| Women-owned business program (WBE) | Affirm invalidation and injunction for inadequate evidentiary justification under intermediate scrutiny, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Program-specific injunction remains; the dissenters would vacate and remand for independent intermediate review. |

Each injunction remains limited to the challenged program. Lawful neutral outreach and procurement measures remain available. No unexamined future program, individual liability, contract award or damages claim is decided.

The five-Justice coalition adopts its application and the WBE affirmance; the four other Associates would vacate and remand. |

Scalia joins the controlling strict-scrutiny requirement and actual racial tailoring holding, but not the full affirmative remedial proposition beyond the rejection needed here. Every controlling proposition has direct majority support; no combined-rationale rule is needed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Unequal competitive opportunity supports this review

**Holding and operative rule:** Regular bidders facing a supported likelihood of exclusion from equal competition have an injury even without proving that they would win a contract. The associations may seek common prospective relief where their members possess standing, the interest is germane, and the claims and relief do not require individual participation; facts may be stipulated, but jurisdiction may not.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion has all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela joins the equal-competition injury proposition and the distinction between subsidiary factual review and constitutional legal review; he does not add a distinct associational-standing rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Northeastern Florida Chapter, Associated General Contractors v. Jacksonville identifies the denial of equal bidding opportunity as the injury. United Food & Commercial Workers Union Local 751 v. Brown Group preserves the association requirements and their distinct constitutional and prudential roles. The associations' regularly bidding members and stipulated likely future exclusion establish the necessary injury for all three programs, and the common justification challenge permits common prospective relief. No lost award or injury from every hypothetical contract mechanism is required. This does not confer standing to litigate every distinct operational challenge. Under §1254(1), the Court reviews the final appellate judgment. Subsidiary historical findings receive clear-error review; constitutional sufficiency, the level of scrutiny and remedial fit require independent legal assessment.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Northeastern Florida applies unequal-competition injury; its standing rule does not decide the preferences' validity.
- Brown Group applies the association requirements at their stated scope; its standing rule does not decide the preferences' validity.

##### 2. The local racial classifications remain subject to strict scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule:** Local racial and ethnic contracting classifications must rest on a supported compelling justification and narrowly fitted means. General social inequality, numerical imbalance and good intentions alone do not establish that justification; a supported local system of exclusion in which the County participates can permit fitted repair without invariably requiring a prior formal judgment of liability.

**Authority:** Part II has all eight Associates for strict scrutiny and the federal/local distinction. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the affirmative remedial proposition; Scalia reserves the permissible extent of racial remediation beyond the grounds needed to reject these programs. Stone-Zsela does not join this local rule.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co. governs local contracting preferences, and Hopwood v. Texas preserves supported justification, individual equality and narrow fit for state racial classifications. Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña sustained its distinct federal arrangement under Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC's important-objective and substantial-relationship standard. Adarand expressly preserved Croson; its federal rule cannot be transferred to Dade County merely because the County describes its purpose as corrective. The Court declines the proposed replacement of local strict scrutiny. This decision creates neither a right to proportionate contracts nor a prohibition on every properly supported local repair program.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Croson remains controlling for local racial classifications.
- Hopwood remains controlling for state racial classifications and fitted repair.
- Adarand retains its bounded federal application; it is neither overruled nor enlarged.
- Metro Broadcasting retains the different federal standard; it is neither overruled nor enlarged.

##### 3. The BBE and HBE programs fail the independent narrow-fit requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** Before using these racial and ethnic contracting preferences, the County must seriously consider reasonably available race-neutral measures responsive to the exclusion it identifies and provide a supported explanation of their inadequacy. This record does not satisfy that requirement for either BBE or HBE; it requires considered alternatives, not trial and exhaustion of every conceivable measure.

**Authority:** Part III has all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela would vacate and remand under a different proposed local rule. Four Associates also find the racial evidence insufficient, but that additional ground does not control.

**Controlling explanation:** Croson's narrow-fit requirement and Hopwood's fitted-repair principle require attention to measures addressing the actual problem. The record identifies procurement information, financing, bonding, payment and discriminatory-treatment obstacles. The County's limited technical and financial assistance and conclusory treatment of alternatives do not show serious assessment of responsive neutral measures; HBE lacks such consideration. Flexibility, waivers, annual review and five-year continuation review cannot supply the missing inquiry. The possibility that an existing BBE program reduced observed disparities matters to evidence, but cannot cure this distinct tailoring defect. The Court therefore need not decide whether the combined racial evidence would otherwise establish a sufficient remedial predicate. It does not constitutionalize a statistical-significance threshold, numerical parity, a requirement of a prior discrimination judgment or a duty to exhaust every possible neutral course.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Croson applies to the unperformed alternatives inquiry without making corrective contracting preferences categorically invalid.
- Hopwood applies its fitted-repair principle to that inquiry without categorical invalidation of corrective preferences.

##### 4. The WBE program fails the distinct intermediate-scrutiny inquiry on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** A sex-conscious contracting measure requires an actual important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the classification, with a demanding governmental burden. On independent assessment of the accepted findings and combined evidence, the County has not adequately substantiated the identified gender exclusion needed to justify this three-sector WBE preference; its invalidation is affirmed.

**Authority:** All nine accept the stated intermediate standard. Part IV's application and affirmance have five direct votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate and remand for a different independent assessment of the combined record.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley v. Board of Trustees, Klinger v. Department of Corrections and United States v. Virginia require actual important purposes and a substantial relationship, without importing racial strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. The Court independently examines the constitutional sufficiency of the evidence while retaining subsidiary findings not shown clearly erroneous. Firm-size adjustments largely explain award-dollar differences; the surviving WBE SIC 15 disparity, women's entry disparity and accounts of refusing to deal with female owners matter but do not adequately justify this program's allocation across all three construction sectors. The subcontract denominator and broader marketplace pool do not reliably identify the relevant procurement comparison. The conclusion does not rest merely on calling the trial judge's ultimate constitutional view permissible. Nor does it impose a numerical minimum or rule out sufficient nonstatistical proof. The court of appeals rejected strict racial tailoring for WBE and found its flexibility capable of satisfying substantial relationship if the evidentiary predicate existed. The present failure is that predicate, not the racial alternatives defect, an excessive 11 percent goal, or an obligation to exhaust neutral measures. A later supported and differently designed women's program is not decided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Kelley retains demanding intermediate scrutiny and rejects stereotyped or merely numerical justification; it supplies no strict-scrutiny or statistical-only prerequisite.
- Klinger retains that distinct intermediate standard and actual-justification requirement, without a statistical-only prerequisite.
- Virginia retains demanding intermediate scrutiny and rejection of stereotyped justification, without importing racial strict scrutiny or least restrictive means.

##### 5. The existing injunctions remain confined to these programs

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgments and permanent injunctions against the challenged BBE, HBE and WBE programs are affirmed on their respective grounds. The relief addresses the operative programs and established likely future unequal exclusion; it neither prohibits unexamined future corrective measures nor awards a contract, damages or an individual discrimination judgment.

**Authority:** Part V has the eight-Associate coalition for BBE/HBE and the five-Justice WBE coalition identified above. Stone-Zsela would maintain the racial restraints temporarily during his proposed remand, rather than affirm their present permanent disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Concrete future exclusion, not past injury alone, supplies a preventive basis for relief. Hopwood's independent prospective-relief requirements and Federal Election Commission v. Williams's insistence on an actual preventive foundation are satisfied by the still-operative programs and stipulated future exposure. Williams supplies no campaign-finance limitation rule here. Lawful race-neutral outreach and procurement measures remain available. No court is directed to adopt a new preference program, and the separate evidentiary bases are not merged.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Hopwood supplies its independent prospective-relief limits without deciding a future unexamined scheme.
- Williams supplies the actual preventive-basis requirement without deciding a future unexamined scheme.

#### Law After Decision

Local racial and ethnic contracting preferences remain subject to strict scrutiny, including serious assessment of responsive neutral alternatives without exhaustion of every imaginable measure. Sex classifications retain the separate important-objective/substantial-relationship test. These programs' injunctions stand on different grounds; no numerical constitutional minimum, exact-parity requirement or categorical ban on future supported repair is adopted. The four-Justice additional racial evidence ground and Stone-Zsela's replacement framework are noncontrolling.

#### Separate Writings

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the racial judgment on the additional ground that the evidence is insufficient after independent constitutional review. Size controls largely explain contract-dollar disparities; residual differences vary by sector and year, the subcontract and marketplace samples have comparability defects, and broad entry or income disparities do not by themselves establish the required local procurement link. The existing BBE program may mask discrimination, but that possibility supplies no missing adjusted data or finding. They assess the anecdotal proof together with the statistics, without a statistical-only rule or an assumption that every reported incident is false. This four-Justice ground adds no controlling holding; the common narrow-fit ground independently supports the racial judgment. Scalia also reserves the full extent of permissible racial remediation stated in Part II, without conditioning his join in the actual rejection on categorical invalidity of all repair.

Stevens, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, concurs in the BBE/HBE judgment on tailoring alone and dissents from the WBE affirmance. The significant women's entry disparity, surviving general-building disparity and reports of refusing to deal with women require an independent intermediate-scrutiny assessment of their combined force. Firm size may itself reflect barriers to obtaining capacity; controlling for size answers a question different from whether such barriers existed. They preserve supported subsidiary findings and invent neither a corrected regression nor a finding that the program is valid. They would vacate the WBE declaration and injunction for prompt reconsideration, with any necessary interim restraint separately assessed below. They do not direct immediate operation or a right to the entire program and reserve the additional racial evidence question because tailoring suffices.

Stone-Zsela concurs in the WBE judgment and its intermediate-scrutiny ground but dissents from the BBE/HBE affirmances. He would expressly replace Croson's local rule for supported corrective contracting measures with a requirement of lawful local authority, objective evidence of identified exclusion or its continuing effects in the relevant procurement market, a substantial relationship to actual remedy, reasonable eligibility and duration limits, and meaningful opportunity for nonbeneficiaries. The County would bear the burden. Neutral alternatives would inform fit without automatically invalidating a supported program. This is a proposed change, not an extension already accomplished by Adarand.

He would vacate the racial invalidations and remand for program-specific legal assessment of the existing record, preserving ordinary clear-error treatment of subsidiary findings and permitting further findings only as ordinary procedure and the record allow. Quantitative studies, earlier Black-firm receipt and owner-income disparities, and possible effects of an existing program merit reassessment; they do not establish that either BBE or HBE succeeds. He would keep the racial injunctions in force pending adequate findings, permitting narrowing or dissolution only as a supported program is shown to fit. He would leave the WBE injunction in force and preserve lawful neutral measures. His proposed local framework receives no other join and changes no governing law.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The existing permanent injunctions remain effective within their respective programs. No new factual hearing, revised program, contract award, deadline or automatic return is ordered. A later proposed measure must be assessed on its own lawful record.

### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, No. 92-757

Merits decision, April 26, 1994

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 13, 1993; decided April 26, 1994, before Rivers. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 968 F.2d 427. The question is whether Civil Rights Act of 1991 §102 supplies new damages and its associated jury option for conduct completed before enactment in a case then pending on appeal. After the February 1991 bench trial, the District Court found a hostile environment but no constructive discharge and no available equitable relief. The Act became law November 21, 1991. This review does not reopen the settled constructive-discharge findings.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms, 8–1, the refusal to apply §102’s new remedies to this preenactment conduct. Five Justices adopt the general temporal framework stated below.

**Affirmed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.
**Opposing:** Blackmun.

No new damages trial. Blackmun would reverse for availability of compensation and a consequent jury election, leaving remaining predicates and proper trial scope below.

The Court’s discussion of punitive liability forms part of its §102 temporal analysis. No separate punitive award or denial judgment is entered. Blackmun expressly reserves that question and is not counted as supporting a punitive proposition.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Exact scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg in full; Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas only in the background and statutory-direction discussion | Affirm | The five full joiners adopt the general retroactive-effect framework and its damages/jury application. The three partial joiners agree that the effective-date text and separate exceptions do not supply the necessary retrospective direction; they do not join the general framework or its controlling explanation. |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Scalia | Kennedy, Thomas | Affirm | Identify the activity regulated and require a clear textual statement or unavoidable necessary implication for application to earlier activity. |
| Dissent | Blackmun | No other Justice | Would reverse for compensation and the consequent conditional jury election | Pending-case compensation under the enacted temporal provisions and, alternatively, remedial principles; punitive damages expressly reserved. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences

**Controlling proposition:** When Congress has prescribed a civil statute’s temporal reach, courts apply that direction subject to constitutional limits; absent sufficient direction, they ask whether applying the provision would impair rights possessed when a party acted, increase liability for past conduct, or impose new duties concerning completed transactions. If it would, the provision does not govern those events without clear congressional intent; a pending case or reference to earlier facts alone does not establish such an effect, and prospective relief, jurisdiction, procedure and collateral matters require examination of what the particular provision actually changes rather than an automatic reopening of completed acts.

**Authority:** Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court begins with Congress’s direction, not a slogan that every pending case receives either old law or new law. Kaiser makes the enacted temporal instruction the first inquiry. Bowen supplies the presumption against attaching new obligations to completed conduct, and Bennett preserves substantive obligations fixed under earlier law. Bradley remains effective in its litigation-fee setting, where applying the later provision did not impose the new primary liability presented here. A statute is not retrospective merely because its application involves earlier facts or disappoints an expectation. The court must identify the legal consequence being changed. Rules governing future conduct or the court’s present power may operate in pending proceedings; procedural changes likewise do not necessarily alter past legal consequences, although they do not automatically undo a completed trial. Harper governs judicial decisions applied to litigants and does not command backward application of later legislation. The Court leaves constitutional limits on expressly retrospective legislation for a case presenting them.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Kaiser Aluminum & Chemical Corp. v. Bonjorno, 494 U.S. 827 (1990): applied to make statutory temporal direction the starting inquiry.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988): applied to the presumption against retrospective legal burdens; no automatic civil constitutional prohibition is inferred.
- Bennett v. New Jersey, 470 U.S. 632 (1985): applied to protect substantive obligations fixed under earlier law.
- Bradley v. School Board of Richmond, 416 U.S. 696 (1974): retained in its collateral litigation-fee setting and reconciled with the new-consequence inquiry, not overruled.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): distinguished as judicial retroactivity; its equal-application rule remains unchanged.

##### Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct

**Controlling proposition:** Section 102’s new compensatory and punitive liability does not attach to discriminatory conduct completed before November 21, 1991, because it adds legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. Its Title VII compensation supplements, rather than replaces, §706(g) relief and excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by that provision; punitive damages have the distinct statutory predicates of malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and exclude government, government-agency and political-subdivision respondents. No such damages award or predicate finding is made here.

**Authority:** Stevens’s §102 application, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas independently reach the same unavailability of new damages through their concurrence. Blackmun disagrees about compensation and reserves punitive damages.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that compensation can increase liability even when the underlying discrimination was already forbidden. Section 102 permits recovery for injuries that the earlier Title VII remedial scheme did not compensate; it is not merely a different method of enforcing an unchanged monetary obligation. Punishment presents a still stronger new consequence, although no constitutional question about an expressly retrospective punitive law is decided. Section 402(a) makes the Act effective upon enactment except as otherwise specifically provided. That date does not itself identify earlier conduct covered. Section 109(c) separately confines its extraterritorial amendments to later conduct. Section 402(b) excludes disparate-impact cases whose complaints preceded March 1, 1975 and whose initial decisions followed October 30, 1983. Those distinct, limited provisions do not compel a general retrospective command for §102. Remedial purpose cannot supply it. No employer has a protected entitlement to discriminate, but notice of the forms and magnitude of liability remains relevant. The Court neither approves the harassment nor eliminates relief previously available under Title VII.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bennett and Bowen: applied to the new monetary burden attached to completed conduct.
- Bradley: distinguished; the collateral fee award did not create this new compensation or punishment for the underlying private conduct.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992): distinguished; appropriate relief in an implied Title IX action does not enlarge Title VII’s earlier express remedial scheme.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished; its express substitution of law governing identified pending disputes does not supply the temporal direction missing here.

##### The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim

**Controlling proposition:** Under §102(c), if the complaining party seeks compensatory or punitive damages available under that section, any party may demand a jury trial, and the court must not inform the jury of the statutory monetary limits. Because §102 damages are unavailable for this preenactment conduct, its conditional jury provision supplies no independent right to a new trial; this conclusion does not prohibit applying a genuinely procedural change to an appropriate future procedural act in another case.

**Authority:** Stevens’s jury portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies the controlling five-Justice explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same conditional conclusion in their concurrence; Blackmun would permit a jury election because he would make compensation available.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reads the jury provision together with its express damages condition. Congress made the election available when a party seeks compensation or punishment under §102; it did not create a freestanding jury right for every pending Title VII action. Here neither category may be imposed for the earlier conduct. Calling a jury a procedural device therefore cannot supply the missing statutory claim. Moreover, a procedural change applicable to future proceedings does not necessarily require undoing a trial already completed under the governing law. The distinction preserves appropriate current-law treatment of procedure without allowing a procedural label to evade the Act’s remedial reach. Bradley does not sever the jury election from the condition Congress enacted, and Kaiser’s statutory-first inquiry requires respecting that condition. The Court orders no new damages trial and does not decide how a different, unconditional jury statute would govern an uncompleted trial. Existing findings are not vacated merely because the appeal was pending when this Act became effective.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Kaiser: applied to the enacted condition linking the jury option to §102 damages.
- Bradley: distinguished; current-law treatment of collateral matters does not erase an express condition on a new remedy or automatically reopen a completed trial.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

No cited decision is overruled. The distinction between later legislation and judicial construction preserves Harper; the distinctions for collateral fees, future procedure and prospective relief preserve the stated domains of Bradley and the other temporal authorities.

Effective April 26, 1994, courts apply the temporal-direction and new-legal-consequences framework stated above. New §102 compensation and punitive liability do not attach to the completed preenactment conduct here, and the conditional jury option supplies no independent retrial. The holding is provision-specific, not a ruling that every part of the 1991 Act is inapplicable in every pending case. Congress may prescribe retrospective reach within constitutional limits. Harper’s rule for judicial decisions remains distinct. Rivers applies this ruling later the same day in its review of §101.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas, agrees with the statutory-direction discussion and the judgment but uses a different general method. A court should identify the activity the statute regulates and require a clear textual statement or unavoidable necessary implication before applying the new law to earlier activity. Section 102 regulates the discriminatory conduct by attaching compensation and punishment; the later date of judgment does not transform that liability into regulation of future judicial procedure. His Kaiser concurrence supplies this approach, while his Bowen concurrence distinguishes new consequences for past events from regulation of future activity affected by old facts. A testimony rule ordinarily regulates the taking of testimony, a jurisdictional rule the exercise of judicial power, and prospective relief future conduct. Those examples require identifying the relevant event, not assigning dispositive labels such as substantive, procedural or vested. He does not adopt an open-ended fairness exception or treat legislative history as a substitute for the necessary textual command. The conditional jury provision fails with the unavailable damages claim. The concurrence does not require overruling Bradley’s actual fee holding or deciding every fee statute’s relevant event.

Justice Blackmun would make compensatory damages available in this pending action and permit the consequent statutory jury election. He reads §402(a)’s immediate effectiveness together with the separate exceptions in §§109(c) and 402(b): Congress expressly confined the extraterritorial amendments and excluded the defined older disparate-impact cases, supporting application of compensation in other pending cases. Alternatively, Bradley’s current-law principle and his Kaiser dissent join support remedial compensation for conduct already prohibited. Bennett’s protection of substantive obligations is the strongest contrary precedent, but he distinguishes changing the prohibition from supplying relief for its violation. An employer has no vested entitlement to discriminate. He does not treat the legislative history as reliable evidence defeating his reading. Punitive damages present a harder question not squarely addressed here, on which he expresses no opinion. His proposed remand would determine the remaining compensation predicates and proper trial scope; it would not award damages, find malice, order every issue retried or erase every earlier finding.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed. No new damages trial, damages award or remand is ordered. The judgment on the earlier equitable-remedy record remains intact, including the unreviewed constructive-discharge findings. The Court neither declares the harassment lawful nor decides the merits of a different claim for relief available under prior law.

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 14, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, which applied amended chapter 153 to Lindh's pending habeas application and denied relief. The question is whether amended §2254(d) and the related chapter 153 provisions govern a noncapital application filed before AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment. Ginsburg delivered the opinion of the Court. Scalia filed a dissent, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, that would affirm the temporal determination without independently adjudicating the Confrontation Clause claim.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application

**Holding and operative rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments, including amended §2254(d), do not govern Lindh's noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The application-date construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case rule and the successive-petition provisions governed by Felker; it does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA provision.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the statutory rule, its limits and reversal with remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf makes Congress's temporal choice the first inquiry. Ordinary statutory construction may establish that choice without a prescribed formula. Congress enacted the neighboring ordinary-habeas and capital-habeas chapters together and expressly directed chapter 154 to pending cases in §107(c), while omitting that direction for chapter 153. That deliberate contrast supplies the application-date inference here. The contrary reading stresses the capital chapter's distinct State-qualification system. Those differences do not deprive Congress's adjacent and express treatment of pending applications of significance. Applying the same pending-case reach to chapter 153 would erase that contrast. The changed standards governing an already filed request for relief reinforce the need to honor the enacted distinction; the Court need not classify every amendment as substantive or procedural to decide the case. Felker applied a particular authorization-review restriction to proceedings begun after enactment and expressly reserved other Title I temporal questions. It neither fixes chapter 153's present reach nor displaces the structural inquiry. Lindh filed on July 9, 1992, almost four years before enactment. His appeal must therefore be considered under the habeas law governing that application before AEDPA. The Court expresses no view on the psychiatrist cross-examination claim, bias, harmlessness, exhaustion, chapter 154 State qualification or ultimate entitlement to a writ. No constitutional right to permanent retention of every filing-date judicial rule is announced.

- Rivers preserves the provision-specific method; the decision establishes no timing rule for every AEDPA amendment.
- Felker is distinguished because it applied its own authorization-review provisions to proceedings begun after enactment and reserved other Title I questions. Its successive-petition and retained-original-authority holdings remain in force.

Pre-enactment noncapital applications such as Lindh's remain governed by pre-AEDPA chapter 153 law. Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, dissents. Chapter 154 addresses a distinct capital scheme dependent on State qualification, so its express pending-case clause does not reliably prescribe the contrary operation for chapter 153. Amended §2254(d) regulates the court's present authority to grant relief; the dissent would apply it to this still-pending adjudication. Hughes Aircraft's expansion of private enforcement exposure differs from this restriction on current judicial relief. ### Pennsylvania v. Labron, No. 95-1691; Pennsylvania v. Kilgore, No. 95-1738

Certiorari grants and summary merits decisions, July 1, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Certiorari granted and summary merits decisions issued July 1, 1996, without plenary argument. On petitions under 28 U.S.C. §1257(a) from the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania.

Labron, 543 Pa. 86, 669 A.2d 917, concerns a pretrial suppression judgment: its opinion was dated December 29, 1995, and issued with entry of judgment January 12, 1996. Kilgore, 544 Pa. 439, 677 A.2d 311, concerns a suppression ruling overturning the adverse ruling after conviction and requiring further proceedings: its opinion was dated December 26, 1995, and issued with entry of judgment January 24, 1996.

The question is whether the Fourth Amendment automobile exception requires an additional case-specific exigency when police have probable cause to search an automobile, and how that rule applies to these vehicle searches. Finality, independent state grounds and the limits of federal review are considered separately for each petition.

In Labron, officers observed drug transactions on a Philadelphia street, with drugs retrieved from the trunk of a parked 1977 Lincoln. The trial court suppressed; the Superior Court reversed; the Pennsylvania Supreme Court restored suppression because the Commonwealth had not shown the additional exigency that court required. The parties disputed the significance of prior investigation and time to seek a warrant. When officers attempted to impound the Lincoln, it would not start. Testimony described four tires, glass, a tag and an apparently highway-legal exterior; the trial judge discussed its apparent potential mobility. The timing of the failed start relative to the trunk search and the officers' prior knowledge of its condition are not established.

In Kilgore, a controlled purchase led officers to a pickup at a private farm. Observation of the approach to the truck was interrupted; the former wife then delivered cocaine, but the additional cocaine and purchase money were not found on her. An officer approached the farmhouse's back door, saw a person run from the kitchen, announced police presence, entered and detained Kilgore in the living room. The homeowner consented to a premises search; direct testimony specifically describes house consent. Police searched Kilgore's truck behind the residence without a warrant or his consent and found cocaine in a cup beneath other contents on the passenger floor. Money was later recovered under a carpet after its location was disclosed. The state court accepted probable cause but found no additional exigency because officers could guard the truck while obtaining a warrant.

All nine Justices participated. There was no plenary argument.

#### Judgment

The petitions for certiorari are granted. The Court summarily reverses both judgments in a reasoned opinion and remands for proceedings consistent with its federal decision. The petition-stage tally is not disclosed.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pennsylvania v. Labron, No. 95-1691 | Reversed and remanded, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Stevens, Ginsburg | Return to the Pennsylvania Supreme Court for proceedings consistent with the federal automobile and jurisdictional holdings. |
| Pennsylvania v. Kilgore, No. 95-1738 | Reversed and remanded, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Stevens, Ginsburg | Return to the Pennsylvania Supreme Court for proceedings consistent with the federal automobile and jurisdictional holdings. |

The dissenters would decline review of Labron because they read its judgment as resting on an adequate independent state ground, and would deny discretionary review of Kilgore despite its express federal conclusion. Their dissent does not affirmatively adopt a federal rule requiring an additional automobile exigency.

The Court applies the automobile exception to the reviewed searches and rejects the separate federal urgency requirement used to suppress the evidence. The remand does not leave that application wholly undecided. It permits lawful consideration of genuinely remaining matters, including any properly available independent state constitutional ground and search-scope issue, without deciding that an additional claim is preserved, waived or meritorious. It orders no automatic evidence admission, conviction, new trial, new hearing or particular state-law disposition.

#### Opinion Topology

O'Connor writes the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Seven Justices join its finality and independent-state-ground analysis, reasoned summary treatment, automobile-exception rule and application, and limited remand.

Ginsburg dissents, joined in full by Stevens. Their shared writing opposes federal review for different reasons in the two cases. Neither adds a conditional merits opinion endorsing an additional federal exigency requirement. There are no other separate writings.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

The following holdings and explanations are those of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, with the seven-Justice coalition identified above joining throughout. The dissenters' review objections are not additional merits votes.

##### 1. These suppression judgments satisfy the applicable finality requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1257 permits review under Cox's third practical-finality category where the highest state court has finally resolved the federal issue, further criminal proceedings remain, and later effective review for the State will be unavailable whether it wins or loses. These binding suppression determinations satisfy that route; the Court does not create a general exception for every interlocutory suppression order.

**Controlling explanation:** Cox Broadcasting distinguishes completion of all proceedings from final resolution of a federal issue in circumstances where later review cannot effectively occur. Labron's case remains at the pretrial suppression stage; Kilgore's suppression ruling follows conviction and requires further proceedings. In each, Pennsylvania's highest court has finally imposed the disputed federal condition. A later conviction would remove the Commonwealth's complaint about suppression, while an acquittal could not be appealed. The federal question therefore cannot simply await an ordinary final prosecution judgment. This is Cox's third category, not its different fourth category invoked merely because an evidentiary issue is important. The petitions' finality labels and the presence of a remand decide nothing by themselves. Finality also does not answer whether an adequate independent state ground defeats jurisdiction or whether discretionary review is warranted.

##### 2. Federal review is available under Long in each case

**Holding and operative rule:** When a state judgment fairly appears primarily to rest on federal law or to be interwoven with it and an adequate independent state ground is not clear, Long permits review absent a plain statement of an independently sufficient state basis. Labron's federal/state automobile analysis is interwoven without that clear independent basis; Kilgore expressly concludes that the search violated the Fourth Amendment. Both federal premises are reviewable, without forbidding Pennsylvania from applying a properly available independent state rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Long, retained in Arizona v. Evans, requires attention to the judgment's actual sources rather than a count of state citations. Labron invokes both constitutions and speaks of the Commonwealth's jurisprudence, but derives its exigency analysis through Carroll, Chambers and state automobile decisions treating federal search principles. Its reasoning does not clearly identify an additional state constitutional condition sufficient regardless of federal law. Kilgore repeatedly uses Fourth Amendment terms and expressly rests suppression on a Fourth Amendment violation; its federal ground is clearer still. The dissent's account of Pennsylvania's capacity and history of giving greater protection does not establish that these judgments already rest on an independent basis that forecloses correction. Sochor concerned an express sufficient state preservation ground; Middlebrooks involved actual independent state constitutional reasoning confirmed by state clarification. Neither makes every reference to state law independent. The Court corrects the federal rule and leaves the State free to use an actually independent, adequate ground through its proper procedures. It predicts no such result and resolves no unpresented preservation question.

##### 3. Probable cause and ready mobility do not require a fresh emergency

**Holding and operative rule:** Police may search a readily mobile automobile without a warrant when probable cause supports belief that it contains evidence or contraband; the Fourth Amendment requires no additional showing of unforeseen circumstances, imminent departure or insufficient time to obtain a warrant. Police custody, the ability to guard the vehicle or an earlier opportunity to seek a warrant does not itself impose that extra condition. The object sought limits the search to places and containers capable of containing it.

**Controlling explanation:** Carroll and Chambers recognize automobile authority resting on mobility rather than a warrant-impracticability inquiry renewed for every search. Carney adds the reduced privacy associated with vehicles' regulated, public character. Johns confirms that police custody does not by itself extinguish authority to search on probable cause; its separate protection against unreasonable delay remains. The Commonwealth need not prove that officers lacked every earlier opportunity to seek a warrant or that guarded vehicles were about to escape. Those demands would replace the exception's established predicates with an additional urgency test. Probable cause remains indispensable, and Ross ties the permissible search to its object and the places where that object may be found. This vehicle rule supplies no independent authority to enter a home or protected curtilage, treat an immobile structure as an automobile, dispense with probable cause or exceed object-based scope. Carney's reservation concerning different residential uses and the independent requirements for access to protected premises remain intact.

##### 4. The automobile rule applies to the reviewed searches

**Holding and operative rule:** The accepted probable cause and adjudicated automobile circumstances bring these searches within the exception for purposes of the federal additional-exigency ground on which the judgments rest. The Court reverses that ground in each case and remands; its application is neither an unconditional approval of every entry and search act nor an instruction merely to reconsider whether the rejected urgency requirement should remain.

**Controlling explanation:** In Labron, observed drug transactions and retrievals from the Lincoln's trunk supplied the accepted probable cause. The car was an ordinary street-parked automobile with the described apparently highway-legal exterior; the police's attempted impoundment revealed a failed start. That evidence does not establish when the failure was learned or a finding that the car had ceased to be an automobile within the exception. The Court applies the vehicle rule to the adjudicated setting without finding that the engine worked or treating appearance as a universal answer to known mechanical incapacity. The dispute over advance information does not justify the additional warrant-opportunity requirement. In Kilgore, the controlled-purchase circumstances and connection to the truck supplied probable cause accepted below despite the interrupted observation. Private parking, custody and available guards do not themselves supply the extra federal urgency condition. Lack of vehicle consent or visible contraband does not defeat an independently applicable automobile exception. This conclusion does not establish lawful crossing of a protected boundary, classify the farm location as outside curtilage, or turn house consent into truck consent. Those are distinct inquiries, not facts supplied by the automobile rule. The decision applies the exception to the reviewed searches while leaving genuinely different, properly available issues to lawful state procedure; it does not prescribe a new mechanical-condition or property-access hearing.

##### 5. Reversal permits ordinary state proceedings within the federal holding

**Holding and operative rule:** Each judgment is reversed, and each case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the Court's automobile and jurisdictional holdings. Pennsylvania may apply an actually adequate and independent state ground if properly available, and may address genuinely remaining scope or other distinct issues under its procedures; this mandate establishes neither their availability nor their merits and directs no automatic admission or prosecution outcome.

**Controlling explanation:** Correcting the federal error does not make the federal Constitution the ceiling of state protection. Long and Evans preserve genuine state independence without assuming it in the judgment under review. The remand also respects the distinction between a vehicle search's authority and a separate question about access, consent, execution or scope. Whren decided the authority for its ordinary initial stop, not every ensuing search; Wilson addressed dwelling-entry reasonableness, not an automatic justification for Kilgore's entry. Ornelas separates independent legal review from historical factfinding and supplies no missing finding here. Evans required further application because its own error-attribution predicate was unresolved; it does not compel a rule-only vacatur in every search case with an unanswered question. The present opinion applies the automobile exception to the reviewed extra-exigency issue, then leaves only legitimately remaining matters to the state courts. It does not restore a conviction, compel admission, reopen a forfeited claim or promise a particular independent state result.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Cox Broadcasting v. Cohn:** The Court applies the third practical-finality category to these binding suppression determinations and the State's inability to obtain later effective review. No general interlocutory-review rule is added.
- **Michigan v. Long:** Its federal/interwoven-ground inquiry is applied separately to Labron and Kilgore. A State's power to afford greater protection remains, but possible state authority is not proof of an actual independent judgment.
- **Arizona v. Evans:** Its Long holding remains controlling. Its separate exclusionary-rule exception and unresolved clerical-error attribution do not authorize these searches or require the same factual remand in a different case.
- **Sochor and Middlebrooks:** Their actually sufficient independent preservation and state constitutional grounds remain respected; the Court distinguishes them from the grounds under review here.
- **Carroll v. United States and Chambers v. Maroney:** The established automobile exception is applied without a new case-specific warrant-impracticability requirement. Police control does not by itself require a different federal rule.
- **California v. Carney:** Mobility and reduced automobile privacy support the rule; its actual setting and reservation concerning differently situated residential uses remain. It creates no general authority to enter protected property.
- **United States v. Johns:** Custody alone does not eliminate vehicle-search authority. The Court does not remove Johns's separate protection against unreasonable delay or decide a delay claim here.
- **United States v. Ross:** Probable cause's object determines the permitted places and containers to search; it is not a license for a search beyond that object.
- **Ornelas v. United States:** Independent review of ultimate constitutional probability questions, clear-error protection for historical facts and due weight to grounded inferences remain. The case supplies no new fact, waiver or probable-cause presumption.
- **Whren v. United States:** Objective authority for the initial stop remains separate from search, manner, scope and duration. Its unanimity does not supply an additional vote or factual premise here.
- **Wilson v. Arkansas and Pinson:** The distinct protections governing dwelling entry and the particular technological examination of domestic information remain. Neither is a general automobile rule or establishes Kilgore's property boundary or lawful access.

No prior decision is overruled. The Court applies the inherited automobile exception to reject the added federal condition; it does not adopt a new general mechanical-defect, curtilage or premises-consent rule.

#### Current Law

Ready mobility and probable cause support an automobile search without a separate showing that obtaining a warrant was impracticable. Custody, available guards and an earlier opportunity to seek a warrant do not alone create such a condition. The object sought continues to limit scope. The rule does not dispense with lawful authority to enter protected premises or convert an immobile structure into an automobile.

These two judgments cannot stand on the additional federal exigency requirement they applied. The Court's case-specific application does not establish actual operation of the Lincoln's engine, a universal rule resolving known mechanical incapacity, truck-owner consent, the outdoor extent of house consent or a curtilage finding. It also does not resurrect the Commonwealth's separate Labron privacy-interest objection as an established alternative basis; that objection's treatment below is not displaced by this holding.

Federal review remains bounded by §1257 finality and genuinely adequate independent state grounds. Cox's third category and Long/Evans govern for their stated reasons, not simply because a suppression order is important or cites federal law. Properly available independent state protection remains possible. No independent state outcome, evidentiary admission, conviction or further hearing is predetermined.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, dissenting from review and summary reversal:** Labron's opinion, read in the setting of Pennsylvania's decisions and Article I, §8, rests on an adequate independent state rule in their view. Its reliance on the Commonwealth's jurisprudence and White is substantive, not an incidental state citation accompanying an exclusively federal judgment. Federal search cases may describe the constitutional floor while the State imposes its own greater protection. They regard the state discussion, including the distinctions in White between automobile exigency and its separate Belton issue, and the state opinions' treatment of Pennsylvania protection as evidence that the judgment would remain independent of the federal answer. A disagreement within the state court over the wisdom or foundation of that rule does not itself make the ground federal.

Long's presumption begins with a judgment that fairly appears primarily federal or interwoven; these Justices would not extend it to a judgment they read as primarily state law merely because it lacks a particular verbal formula. Their actual Evans jurisdictional position reinforces the need for an affirmative basis for federal intervention. Cox finality cannot cure an adequate independent state ground. They therefore would deny Labron's petition and would not reach its federal automobile merits.

Kilgore is different: its express Fourth Amendment conclusion makes the federal ground much clearer. They nevertheless would deny discretionary review. The surrounding state jurisprudence makes renewed suppression on an independent Pennsylvania basis the expected result in their judgment, so a federal correction and remand would consume both courts' resources without a useful change in outcome. That is their reason for restraint, not an entered future state decision or a jurisdictional finding that Kilgore already rests exclusively on state law. Stevens's institutional caution in Carney supports examining whether a vehicle actually warrants intervention, rather than treating every asserted automobile error as requiring immediate review.

They do not turn the Lincoln's failed start into a new mechanical-incapacity holding or the farm's private location into an adjudicated curtilage claim. Nor do they adopt an extra federal exigency requirement as an affirmative merits position. Johns's distinction between custody and continuing vehicle-search authority remains consistent with their review objections. Their dissent concerns the authority and utility of deciding these cases, not a separately stated federal rule sustaining every suppression rationale below.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Both petitions are granted and the federal merits are resolved by summary reversal. Both cases return to the Pennsylvania Supreme Court for proceedings consistent with the Court's decision. The federal added-exigency ground has been rejected and the automobile exception applied at the stated scope; it is not left open for reconsideration under the same additional requirement.

Any genuinely remaining issue must be addressed through proper state procedures and within the mandate. The Court does not require a new hearing, create preservation for a previously unavailable claim, decide a distinct access theory's merits, prescribe a state constitutional result or direct automatic admission or conviction.
