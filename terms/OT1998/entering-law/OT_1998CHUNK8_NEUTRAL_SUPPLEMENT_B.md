## Maine Green Party — May 20

The March 2, 1999 First Circuit opinion, 173 F.3d 1, has been read in full, including Campbell's concurrence and every footnote. The affirmed judgment rests on preservation, not a majority determination of the exclusively-local associational theory's constitutionality. The majority regards the asserted philosophical refusal of national activity as a materially different factual premise from the original resource/newness argument; it also notes the affidavit about steps toward national organization. Campbell regards the initial right-to-organize argument and Eu/Tashjian citations as sufficient preservation, disputes inference of waiver from the district court's silence, and would affirm on the merits. Neither account is adopted as an independent finding of what the party actually intends. The State did not develop the waiver objection; the district judge's adoption does not expressly decide it. These competing procedural paths require actual assessment before constitutional adjudication.

The August 1, 1997 Maine Supreme Judicial Court certified-answer decision, 698 A.2d 516, establishes the state-law construction: the five-percent test applies at each biennial general election, including the intervening presidential election, rather than allowing the previous gubernatorial showing alone to preserve recognition. It describes the parties' agreed injunction pending resolution of the federal action; that historical procedural recital does not establish a present Supreme Court injunction or continued protection after the later judgment.

The statutory routes are distinct. Section 301 governs retention of qualified-party primary status, together with municipal caucus, convention and certification conditions. Section 302 permits formation around a consenting, petition-nominated nonparty gubernatorial or presidential candidate who received at least five percent; voter enrollment, caucuses in each of the sixteen counties and a convention are separate conditions. Section 303 offers the petition route. The official 1997 public law, chapter 581, section 1, effective June 30, 1998, requires approved petition form; signatures verified and certified under the referenced primary-petition provisions; filing before 5 p.m. on the 180th day preceding a primary; and signatures and legal addresses equal to at least five percent of the last gubernatorial vote. This is not a second presidential-vote requirement. The 1997 chapter 436 amendments separately alter declaration contact information and the section 302 caucus-notice deadline to March 20; do not import the older April 15 recital as the current deadline.

The 1993 First Circuit opinion in Libertarian Party v. Diamond, 992 F.2d 365, describes primary and independent nomination routes, including nonparty nomination petitions under sections 351–354 and a permitted political designation. Its account is useful statutory background, not a controlling Supreme Court holding or proof that every 1993 deadline remained unchanged in 1999. The 1999 opinion confirms alternative candidate routes exist but does not adjudicate every cumulative burden of a hypothetical requalification attempt. Treat the concrete availability and burden of an alternative as an evidentiary question if that premise is needed for a broad merits disposition; neither a nominal alternative nor a five-percent number establishes a constitutional safe harbor.

## Cleveland — May 24

The actual Fifth Circuit rule is more specific than automatic estoppel. The full opinion at 120 F.3d 513 expressly rejects a per se bar, creates a rebuttable presumption of judicial estoppel from application for or receipt of SSDI, and permits credible admissible evidence showing compatibility in the particular circumstances. It recognizes listed impairments, the absence of a reasonable-accommodation inquiry at the SSA and trial-work provisions, but describes compatible cases as limited and highly unusual. It concludes Cleveland's repeated sworn statements did not create a genuine issue rebutting the presumption. The question therefore includes the validity and application of that presumption, not merely a universally conclusive bar the lower court did not adopt.

The lower opinion supplies the actual sequence. Cleveland suffered a January 1994 stroke, applied for benefits, returned to work in April, requested accommodations and was terminated in July. She asserts that she notified the SSA and withdrew the application when she returned; the employer concedes notification of return but disputes withdrawal or abandonment of total-disability representations. Later reconsideration papers again asserted disability; the September 1995 administrative award treated disability as continuous from January 1994. Cleveland asserts worsening after termination and ability to work with accommodation during the intervening employment. These are the record's positions, not new Supreme Court factual findings. The district court dismissed the Texas claim without prejudice; review of the federal rule does not revive or decide it automatically.

The ADA's qualified-individual inquiry asks whether the person can perform the position's essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation. SSDI asks about substantial gainful activity under its distinct impairment, duration and vocational provisions. Statutory differences may explain a legal conclusion of disability without resolving every contradictory statement of historical fact. Supported paths include retaining the lower presumption, rejecting the presumption while requiring an adequate explanation under ordinary summary-judgment rules, or resolving a genuinely preserved narrower evidentiary question. None is current Court law merely because the original packet's Lawful Paths paragraph states a preferred rule. There is no adjudicated finding here that Cleveland was qualified, an accommodation reasonable, the employer liable or a trial inevitable.

## Davis — May 24

The exact Kinman and Gebser holdings and reusable rules are copied in LAW_09_davis. Kinman's educational-environment rule uses severe OR pervasive conduct, objective abuse actually experienced as abusive and full context; it imposes neither psychiatric injury nor measurable grade loss. Gebser's teacher-authority route is conjunctive and has the component-specific authority stated in the source. Peer conduct does not itself supply school-delegated teacher power.
