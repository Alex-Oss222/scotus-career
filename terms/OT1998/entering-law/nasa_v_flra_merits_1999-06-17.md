## Event

National Aeronautics and Space Administration v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, No. 98-369. Argued March 23, 1999; decided June 17. Certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, 120 F.3d 1208, enforcing the FLRA's order. The questions are whether this NASA Inspector General investigator is an agency representative for §7114(a)(2)(B), and whether NASA Headquarters must direct compliance with the resulting statutory representation duty.

The employee requested representation and reasonably feared discipline. His union representative attended but was restricted to observation and limited interruption, while refusal by the employee to answer risked dismissal. Before the interview OIG had determined that no criminal action would be taken against this employee. The interference finding was not separately appealed; its legal coverage predicate remains at issue. The Authority ordered OIG and Headquarters compliance, notices and Headquarters direction to OIG. The lower court reached the Headquarters objection under the case-specific extraordinary-circumstances provision of §7123(c), without creating a general exception to preservation.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the Eleventh Circuit's enforcement judgment.

## Judgment & Remedy

Both components are affirmed 6–3. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas. Coverage and the Headquarters compliance duty rest on distinct grounds. Stone-Zsela joins coverage in full and agrees only with the Headquarters component's judgment, adding no ground on that duty.

The compliance, notices and limited Headquarters direction remain enforceable. No damages, discipline, reinvestigation, universal interview protocol or new deadline is ordered. The Headquarters instruction requires statutory compliance and authorizes no control of investigative subjects, evidence, conclusions or termination.

## Holdings

### This OIG examination is covered by the statutory representation right

An investigator employed in NASA's Office of Inspector General is a representative of NASA when conducting the agency-related examination presented here. Section 7114(a)(2)(B) requires an opportunity for the exclusive representative to assist at the examination of a unit employee in connection with an investigation when the employee reasonably believes discipline may result and requests representation; OIG's independence from ordinary management does not eliminate that relationship.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this rule, explanation and limits.

The chapter defines agency broadly and does not exclude NASA. Its representation provision is not confined to the immediate component that negotiates with the union. Excluding specified investigative employees from membership in a bargaining unit under §7112(b)(7) does not exempt their examinations of covered employees under §7114. This investigator works within NASA on agency-related conduct capable of producing discipline. The Court does not treat every employee or outside examiner as an agency representative.

The IG Act must be independently respected. It creates an independent unit within the agency and protects investigations from managerial interference, while retaining an institutional relationship and general supervision. Those protections do not require elimination of a limited statutory right belonging to the examined employee. NFFE, Haggar and Aguirre-Aguirre require enacted authority and a permissible actual explanation, rather than automatic deference. The Authority's coverage explanation satisfies that inquiry here; its agency label is not the reason it prevails.

Weingarten's limited assistance function reconciles the right with orderly investigation. The representative may assist but may not obstruct questioning, compromise protected information, direct the investigation or require bargaining at the interview; the investigator may insist on the employee's own account. Those limits do not validate reducing representation to silent observation where interference is otherwise established. Reasonable fear and request remain statutory predicates. The Court decides no universal interview protocol, outside-agency or FBI examination, wider criminal inquiry, national-security question or negotiated extension of rights.

### Headquarters may be directed to secure this limited statutory compliance

Under the retained general-supervision relationship, NASA Headquarters may be required to direct OIG compliance with the limited representation right recognized here. That direction does not authorize Headquarters to determine investigative subjects, evidence, conclusions or termination or to bargain for expanded control over OIG procedures.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part II is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplying five direct votes for the complete rationale. Stone-Zsela's additional vote supports the judgment only.

Coverage and parent-agency responsibility are separate questions. The IG Act preserves general supervision by the head or next-ranking officer while forbidding specified interference with initiation, conduct and completion. Requiring compliance with an independently applicable federal protection respects both provisions. The Authority's order calls for that compliance, not management direction of investigative outcomes. Headquarters' relationship to OIG therefore supports this limited instruction even though it could not lawfully take over the investigation.

NFFE distinguishes available authority from its lawful exercise; the actual compliance ground here answers the distinct remedial question. Haggar and Aguirre-Aguirre require neither automatic enforcement nor an unnecessary new agency proceeding when the statutory analysis and actual explanation support the order. The Court sustains the existing compliance and notice relief without a general rule of derivative liability, a new governance power or an enlarged representational scheme. The case-specific preservation history permits review of this statutory objection; it creates no general exception for objections withheld from an agency.

## Precedent Treatment

- NLRB v. J. Weingarten, Inc.: applies the limited assistance function of representation, preserving orderly questioning, the employee's own account and the prohibition on obstruction or required bargaining.
- NFFE/FLRA v. Department of the Interior: applies the requirement of actual statutory authority and a reasoned agency choice; distinguishes its remand for an unexercised choice under a mistaken legal premise.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: preserves separate inquiries into delegation, permissible construction and application; supplies no automatic validation or remand rule.
- INS v. Aguirre-Aguirre: applies review of the actual permissible agency explanation without importing an automatic new-hearing requirement.
- Dickinson v. Zurko: preserves its separate factual-review holding; it does not resolve this statutory interpretation or turn it into deferential factfinding.

## Law After Decision

OIG independence does not remove this agency-related, discipline-capable examination from §7114(a)(2)(B). Request and reasonable fear remain necessary, and representation remains assistance within legitimate investigative limits. A separately justified Headquarters instruction to observe that statutory protection is permissible under retained general supervision. Neither holding confers control over investigative results or resolves every outside-agency, criminal or negotiated-right setting.

## Separate Writings

Thomas, joined by O'Connor and Scalia, dissents. Representative is a relational term in a labor-management statute, not a synonym for agency member. The Inspector General's independent investigative mandate, reporting responsibilities and insulation from ordinary managerial control distinguish this examiner from a management representative. Possible disciplinary use of answers does not supply the missing relationship, and the Authority's actual explanation does not overcome the statutory structure. The dissent would reverse coverage and both dependent enforcement components. It leaves open exceptional circumstances in which an OIG investigator actually represents management and does not exempt OIG from every employment law.

Independently, those three Justices would remove the disputed Headquarters direction even if coverage governs. They read retained general supervision together with the protections for independent investigation and find insufficient authority for the imposed managerial instruction. That conditional position preserves direct OIG compliance with a coverage rule established by the Court; it does not convert disagreement about Headquarters into a new exemption for OIG itself.

## Procedure After Action

The Eleventh Circuit judgment remains in force, sustaining the existing compliance, notice and limited Headquarters-direction order. Further compliance occurs through the existing statutory process. The Court orders no new hearing, investigation, disciplinary decision or representation regulation.
