## Current-term law

### National Federation of Federal Employees, Local 1309 v. Department of the Interior / Federal Labor Relations Authority v. Department of the Interior; Nos. 97-1184 and 97-1243. — 1999-03-03

#### Holdings

##### 1. The statute leaves the extent of midterm bargaining to bounded Authority construction

**Operative rule.** The federal labor statute does not categorically forbid union-initiated bargaining during an existing agreement, nor does it unambiguously require one comprehensive midterm bargaining regime. The Authority may determine the duty's timing and extent through a reasonable, explained construction, subject to contract coverage, lawful waiver, negotiable-subject restrictions, management rights and federal-law consistency.

**Authority.** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies a direct six-Justice majority for this holding and the holding below.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court must identify the authority Congress granted before deferring to an agency. *MCI* preserves statutory boundaries; *NationsBank* permits reasoned administrative choice where the enacted scheme leaves genuine discretion. Here the definition of a collective bargaining agreement is not expressly limited to the principal term contract. An agreement reached during its term can also fit that definition. Conversely, §7114's direction to negotiate toward an agreement and its reasonable-times and frequency requirements do not unmistakably prescribe a comprehensive continuing duty.

Section 7106 principally allocates authority by subject. Its management-right exceptions do not establish a prohibition on every other midterm proposal. The agency-election condition in §7106(b)(1) is not an election condition in (b)(2) or (3). Section 7105 assigns the Authority responsibility for administering bargaining obligations within those limits. The efficiency instruction in §7101 and the conditional impasse powers in §7119 inform that administration; they do not prove perpetual negotiations or inevitable imposition of this clause. A prediction of disruption cannot replace the statutory inquiry, just as the desirability of responding to new workplace needs cannot establish an unlimited duty.

The Fourth Circuit's categorical prohibition therefore rests on an erroneous legal premise. The Authority must explain the lawful regime; the Court does not select a universal duty or universal exemption, create a new factor checklist, or authorize reopening matters an agreement actually settles. The Court does not determine the treatment of a hypothetical future proposal under the Authority's covered-matter or waiver rules.

##### 2. Contractual negotiability requires reconsideration under the corrected legal framework

**Operative rule.** The absence of an independently compelled statutory entitlement does not itself make bargaining over a contractual midterm obligation unlawful. The Authority must reconsider the actual advance clause under the permissible statutory framework; its existing alternative rationale does not warrant an immediate direction to enforce this order because it remains tied to the premise that the general midterm regime was statutorily required.

**Controlling explanation.** The Authority did give an independent reason: contractual proposals can be negotiable even when their substance is not a statutory entitlement. It also distinguished contract coverage from a categorical denial of midterm bargaining. Those are actual reasons, not an appellate lawyer's invention. But the current order continued to treat the clause as restating a statutory right, and its alternative explanation chiefly answered the circuit's contrary precedent. It did not fully exercise the distinct choice over the relationship among a general duty, an advance clause, contractual repose, waiver and the public impasse system once statutory compulsion was removed.

*NationsBank* requires reasoned exercise of genuine discretion, not an assumption that legal necessity supplies the agency's choice. *AT&T v. Iowa Utilities Board* separates administrative implementing power from the validity of particular duties and permits return under the correct legal allocation. *Boston & Maine* requires a discernible agency ground; that requirement is satisfied here, but it does not make every stated alternative sufficient after a governing premise changes. *Good Samaritan* permits considered interpretive change; the Court does not remand merely because the Authority changed its view. This is legal-framework reconsideration, not an evidentiary remand or punishment for inconsistency.

The Authority may adopt the same negotiability result if it explains a permissible construction. Sections 7103(a)(14)(C), excluding matters specifically provided for by federal statute, and 7117(a), preserving federal-law consistency, remain distinct limitations; neither is satisfied merely by statutory silence. The Court does not impose a clause, require agreement or concession, or adjudicate a Panel award. Agreed waiver, a duty to negotiate a waiver clause and compulsory acceptance at impasse remain separate questions; an employer's power to impose a comprehensive waiver is not presumed.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Chevron* remains the framework for determining statutory meaning and the scope of permissible agency construction; no automatic deference follows from the agency label.
- *MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T Co.* supplies the requirement that operative statutory limits be identified and enforced before administrative discretion is recognized; its tariff holding creates no general rule restricting agencies to minor choices.
- *NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.* supplies the distinction between genuine statutory discretion and the agency's reasoned exercise of it. Establishing authority alone does not validate the existing order.
- *ICC v. Boston & Maine Corp.* supplies the discernible-agency-ground requirement. The Authority's contractual explanation is acknowledged rather than replaced with counsel's reasoning.
- *Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala* preserves permissible considered changes of interpretation. Change alone does not disqualify the Authority's construction or compel this remand.
- *AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board* supplies the distinction between implementation authority, particular statutory restrictions and rules requiring agency reconsideration under the correct allocation. Its telecommunications rules are not transferred to federal labor relations.
- *Your Home Visiting Nurse Services, Inc. v. Shalala* preserves statutory construction as distinct from additional regulatory deference; an administrative label cannot settle statutory power.
- *Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.* permits consideration of a fairly included legal argument within a presented claim. The general-duty question here is expressly presented and supplies an actual agency premise; no new question or relief is added.

#### Law After Decision

Federal labor law now rejects a judicial categorical prohibition on all union-initiated midterm bargaining and all advance contractual clauses requiring it. The Authority retains responsibility for a reasoned choice within Congress's actual limits. The Court establishes neither a universal duty to entertain every midterm proposal nor final mandatory negotiability of this particular clause.

The distinction between statutory entitlement and contractual negotiability remains legally meaningful, but the existing Survey order is returned for reconsideration rather than ordered enforced. Management rights, statutory consistency, lawful waiver and the agreement's actual coverage retain their independent force. The Authority's descriptions of express coverage, subjects inseparably bound up with covered subjects and matters fully explored during term negotiations are not findings about an unidentified proposal. No rule about compulsory negotiation or imposition of a comprehensive waiver clause is settled.

## Current-term law

### United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.; No. 97-2044; judgment below, 127 F.3d 1460. — 1999-04-21

#### Holdings

##### Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation

**Holding and operative rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing Congress's actual delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation: courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and the delegation's boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The Court of International Trade's duty to decide on its own record does not repeal that legal framework; agency labels and notice and comment alone do not establish authority or validity.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Parts I–III are joined by all eight Associates and control this complete framework.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes governing law from judicial determination of facts. Sections 1500(b) and 1502(a) and the tariff delegation provisions authorize implementing rules within their scope. Section 2640(a)(1)'s own-record review does not assign all substantive tariff lawmaking exclusively to courts, and §10.11's preservation of judicial review does not require disregarding a lawful regulation. Chevron supplies the division of responsibility; NationsBank permits reasonable gap filling within actual authority. MCI and NCUA require courts to enforce enacted limits before deferring. The importer correctly insists that review remain independent on those limits and effective on the facts. That does not justify categorically withholding the weight due a sufficiently authoritative delegated rule. Conversely, recognizing that framework does not make every Customs interpretation lawful. Informal classification letters and later administrative doctrines are not decided.

##### This regulation's statutory fit and actual application require the corrected inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment must be vacated because categorical nondeference left unresolved whether the regulation reasonably respects the tariff's incidental-assembly allowance and lawfully applies to this process. On remand the courts must enforce the enacted conditions, examine the regulation's actual introductory terms and examples, and resolve relevant application questions under the proper review standards; neither regulatory validity nor the claimed exemption is established in advance.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part IV is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, seven votes. Stevens and Ginsburg do not join this holding or remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The tariff requires United States fabricated components exported ready for assembly without further fabrication, retention of physical identity, and no advancement in value or improvement abroad except through assembly and incidental operations, including cleaning, lubricating and painting. The allowance reduces duties by qualifying component cost or value, with proportional treatment for specific or compound rates; it does not exempt the whole article. Regulation §10.16(b) permits incidental operations before, during or after assembly, including ordinary final pressing and folding. Section 10.16(c) addresses a significant nonassembly process whose primary purpose is fabrication, completion or physical or chemical improvement, or a significant process unrelated to assembly. Its chemical-treatment examples, including permapressing, do not erase those introductory alternatives. Timing after sewing alone settles nothing, and ordinary pressing is not necessarily resin-activating permapressing. The significance, purpose, relationship to assembly and new characteristics of the actual process bear on the statutory-regulatory boundary, not merely on duty arithmetic. NationsBank's framework does not answer those unfinished questions. AT&T distinguishes general authority from particular validity; NFFE distinguishes available discretion from its actual lawful exercise without requiring a new agency proceeding here. Both questions were granted, but that scope does not make this intertwined inquiry complete. The lower courts must undertake it without the erroneous categorical premise. The Court commands no new evidentiary hearing, burden, deadline or finding that existing litigation papers are missing.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Chevron supplies review within actual delegated authority and statutory ambiguity.
- NationsBank of North Carolina v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co. permits reasonable administrative classification within statutory limits.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T and National Credit Union Administration v. First National Bank require enforcement of enacted boundaries before deference.
- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board separates general authority, particular rule validity and unresolved applications; it creates no automatic validation or universal remand rule.
- NFFE/FLRA v. Department of the Interior distinguishes legally available discretion from its lawful exercise; its distinct remedy does not compel a new Customs proceeding.

#### Law After Decision

The Federal Circuit may not categorically exclude this promulgated tariff regulation from Chevron review. Judicial enforcement of statutory limits and trade-court factual review remain intact. The regulation's ultimate reasonableness and its application to Haggar's processing remain for the corrected inquiry. No precedent is overruled.

### Brooks v. Barnes; No. 98-1521; judgment below, Brooks v. Miller, 158 F.3d 1230. — 1999-04-22

#### Holdings

##### A primary barrier must be tested against actual nomination opportunity

**Holding and operative rule:** A Section 2 challenge to a primary-runoff barrier turns on protected voters' equal opportunity in the affected nomination or election process, supported political cohesion and majority bloc voting in the affected electoral settings, and the totality of circumstances. It does not require proof that every additional minority-preferred nominee would win the general election, and neither candidate race, the rarity of runoffs among all primaries nor a conclusive statewide offset of winners can replace the inquiry into the affected voters' actual opportunity.

**Authority:** Souter's Parts I–II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer, control the complete statutory rule, application and remand, six votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Gingles requires proof of political cohesion and majority bloc voting in the affected electoral settings, with the necessary causal connection to the challenged barrier and a separate totality inquiry; Voinovich keeps that burden with the challengers. The inquiry must fit the barrier challenged. De Grandy rejects automatic liability and numerical safe harbors alike; Holder requires a legally and practically grounded comparison. The lower court used the small proportion of primaries reaching runoffs and a statewide net of Black and white winners to reject the relevant bloc-voting condition. It also required proof that additional plurality nominees would win general elections. Those premises constrain the very findings invoked as independent reasons to affirm. The Court therefore cannot detach the failure-of-proof conclusion from the legal errors. Candidate race is not necessarily voter preference, and gains elsewhere do not conclusively answer a local opportunity loss. The 56 adverse and 29 favorable candidate-race reversals establish neither a number of unlawfully lost offices nor negligible harm. SCLC preserves supported subsidiary facts but permits correction when a mistaken legal standard compromises the determination. The State's interest in broadly supported nominees and resistance to vote-splitting deserves concrete consideration. It does not make an extreme hypothetical plurality winner a conclusive answer. Conversely, polarization alone does not establish a violation or require plurality. The lower courts must reconsider on the existing record and any supplementation independently allowed by ordinary procedure; no new trial or particular study is automatically required.

##### The supported purpose findings sustain constitutional rejection

**Holding and operative rule:** A constitutional challenge requires proof of discriminatory purpose attributable to the challenged enactment, assessed from the whole relevant record rather than electoral outcomes alone. The supported findings distinguishing the failed 1963 proposal from the enacted 1964 measure are not clearly erroneous; the historical racial climate and earlier motives do not compel a contrary purpose finding for the later enactment.

**Authority:** Souter's Part III is unanimous and controls this ground and the independent causation ground below.

**Controlling explanation:** Arlington Heights makes historical sequence, contemporaneous evidence and decisionmaking context relevant. The trial court considered those matters, including the committees and the roles of the proponents, and credited witnesses with firsthand involvement. Hunter does not make every related measure inherit the purpose of an earlier failed bill. The Court adopts no requirement of a direct admission and no categorical preference for retrospective testimony over contemporaneous evidence. The stated findings nonetheless permit the constitutional judgment. This inquiry remains separate from the statutory results errors; the Court need not resolve the full reach of Fifteenth Amendment dilution coverage because the claims fail even assuming that coverage.

##### The independent same-enactment finding also defeats causation

**Holding and operative rule:** If discriminatory purpose was a substantial or motivating factor, the defenders bear the burden of showing that the same measure would have been enacted without that factor. The supported independent finding that the 1964 measure would have passed for the credited anti-manipulation and election-administration reasons satisfies that burden here and independently sustains constitutional affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Hunter and Mt. Healthy distinguish a motivating factor from a measure that would have been adopted without it. The trial court made the latter finding separately; it does not depend on the statutory denominators or proof of later general-election success. The challengers' historical evidence does not establish clear error in that supported alternative. This ground does not make mixed motives invariably lawful, transfer the same-decision burden to plaintiffs or permit benign present effects to cure a purpose violation.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Gingles supplies causal predicates, totality and deference to supported findings under the correct legal standard.
- Voinovich preserves the claimant's burden and separates lawful state choices from a compelled federal remedy.
- Holder requires a grounded comparison; it supplies no ideal electoral system or quota.
- De Grandy rejects maximization and conclusive numerical offsets while preserving actual local opportunity.
- Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions requires correct opportunity and remedy standards while retaining supported subsidiary findings.
- Arlington Heights makes the full historical and decisionmaking context relevant to purpose.
- Hunter v. Underwood and Mt. Healthy supply the distinct motivating-factor and same-enactment sequence, including the defenders' burden.
- Bossier Parish keeps statutory results and purpose separate; its preclearance posture does not transfer a different burden into this suit.

#### Law After Decision

The statutory claim remains unresolved under corrected nomination-opportunity standards. Runoff systems have neither categorical immunity nor automatic invalidity. Supported constitutional purpose and independent same-enactment findings sustain the constitutional judgment. No plurality arrangement or racial vote guarantee is established and no precedent is overruled.

### INS v. Aguirre-Aguirre; No. 97-1754; judgment below, 121 F.3d 521. — 1999-05-03

#### Holdings

##### Courts must respect the Board's permissible construction within actual delegated authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §§1103(a)(1) and former 1253(h), together with the Board's adjudicative assignment in 8 C.F.R. §3.1(d)(1), courts must respect a permissible Board construction of the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception after independently enforcing enacted limits. Interpretation, application and factual review retain their distinct requirements; neither agency status nor a general appeal to foreign-affairs expertise supplies automatic correctness.

**Authority:** Breyer's Parts I–II are joined by all nine Justices and control this holding and the next two merits holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Haggar requires authority before deference and distinguishes a lawful interpretive choice from particular validity and application. The present statute and Board assignment establish authority independently of Haggar's tariff rulemaking setting. NationsBank permits reasoned classification within genuine ambiguity; MCI and NCUA prohibit erasing statutory conditions. The exception concerns serious reasons for considering that the applicant committed a serious nonpolitical crime outside the United States before arriving here. No criminal conviction is required, but the territorial, temporal and substantive conditions remain operative. The humanitarian importance of withholding supports careful enforcement of those limits, not an exclusively judicial factor code. Cardoza-Fonseca and Stevic illustrate independent enforcement of statutory meaning; Elias-Zacarias preserves the separate standard for reviewing supported findings. The Board must apply its announced lawful construction to the actual record and explain its decision. The Court adopts neither unconditional deference nor an automatic new agency hearing whenever judicial review used a mistaken premise.

##### A properly established crime exception requires no additional persecution-risk balance

**Holding and operative rule:** Once the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception is properly established, former §1253(h)(2) makes the paragraph (1) withholding protection inapplicable without an additional balancing of feared persecution against offense seriousness. The Board may address that exception while assuming the applicant could show that life or freedom would be threatened because of race, religion, nationality, membership in a particular social group or political opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute separately states the protection and its exceptions. A greater threatened harm does not dissolve a properly established crime exclusion through an additional condition Congress did not supply. The Handbook paragraph 156 argument relied on below presents a serious humanitarian objection, but does not change those distinct statutory inquiries. The Court finds no absence of persecution risk and creates no general discretion to deny withholding to an otherwise eligible applicant. Relevant political circumstances remain material under the lawful crime analysis. Discretionary asylum is a different question and receives no automatic disposition from this withholding rule.

##### The political-criminal comparison does not require every judicially preferred subsidiary finding

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board may compare an offense's political character with its ordinary criminal character and consider gross disproportionality or atrocious conduct in determining which predominates. The statute requires neither a separate atrocity finding in every case nor findings that the violence was necessary to or successful in achieving its political objective; the Board must still assess actual political purpose, context and conduct under its announced standard.

**Controlling explanation:** Political motive alone does not establish a political offense, just as unlawfulness alone does not make one nonpolitical. The Board's explanation considered protest objectives alongside property destruction and civilian assaults and found the criminal character disproportionate. The Court reviews that actual explanation rather than adopting new factual findings from the contested testimony. The Ninth Circuit’s comparison with more severe conduct is a consistency objection, not necessarily a universal atrocity prerequisite. The Board’s actual disproportionate-conduct explanation answers that objection; the greater atrocity of another case is no minimum threshold for this alternative ground. Handbook paragraph 152 and the suggested McMullen comparison reinforce the importance of political context and disproportion, but the absence of a separate comparison with McMullen's atrocious acts does not defeat a determination resting on the supported political-criminal comparison. Atrocious means may defeat political characterization; atrocity is not necessary in every case. A clear causal relation to a political objective would not necessarily overcome a supported disproportion finding, and the Court reserves any independent causal-link requirement in a different case. Relevant comparisons and reasoned treatment of genuine inconsistency remain possible. No categorical rule about bus burnings, assault severity or unsuccessful protest is announced.

##### The late record-error contention remains for its existing agency procedure

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court declines to decide the translation/transcription contention first raised here rather than before the Board or Court of Appeals; the applicant's existing BIA motion for a new hearing remains for determination through that lawful channel. If the BIA determines that modification of the record is necessary, it may determine whether the modified record warrants further consideration of withholding; no error, new hearing or outcome is presumed.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part III is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, eight rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins this component's disposition only and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged errors may be material if established, but this Court is not the first forum for resolving a contention not presented below. The existing motion supplies an actual avenue for that inquiry; its filing proves neither entitlement nor futility. The 1995 regulation's burden applies only if evidence indicates a withholding-denial ground; then the applicant must prove by a preponderance that the indicated ground does not apply. On the existing unrevised agency account, repeated gasoline-fueled bus burnings and civilian assaults, assessed against the political objective, indicate this exclusion and trigger that allocation. This uses conduct evidence, not silence, and makes no new credibility determination. The allocation is distinct from statutory serious reasons and judicial factual review. Failure to file a Board brief despite an extension is procedural history, not default, a concession, an adverse factual inference or proof that the conditional burden was unmet. The merits holdings rest independently on the lawful framework and stated Board explanation. A corrected record may affect their application without changing the statutory rule. The appellate remand itself commands no new Board hearing, stay or removal.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Haggar preserves actual delegation, statutory boundaries and separate validity/application review; its tariff remand is not a universal agency-remand command.
- NationsBank permits reasoned classification within genuine statutory discretion.
- MCI and NCUA retain every enacted condition before deference.
- INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca and INS v. Stevic illustrate independent enforcement of statutory meaning; they do not assign every evaluative choice exclusively to courts.
- INS v. Elias-Zacarias distinguishes evidence permitting a finding from evidence compelling displacement under the relevant review standard.
- INS v. Yang preserves reasoned administration and the separation of eligibility from discretion; it does not require a new hearing merely because a court corrects a legal premise.
- AT&T and NFFE preserve the distinction between authority and the agency's actual lawful choice; the Board already supplied the pertinent comparison here.

#### Law After Decision

The serious-nonpolitical-crime exception remains bounded by its actual statutory conditions and a permissible, reasoned Board construction. Courts may not impose a mandatory persecution-risk balance or additional political-necessity/success findings, nor treat atrocity as indispensable to the alternative disproportionate-conduct route. Actual record review and relevant political context retain their requirements; the existing motion remains for lawful agency determination. The Board’s separate discretionary asylum denial based on conduct against innocent civilians is not converted into an automatic statutory bar. No precedent is overruled.

### Saenz v. Roe; No. 98-97; judgment below, Roe v. Anderson, 134 F.3d 1400. — 1999-05-17

#### Holdings

##### Bona fide new citizens receive equal ordinary welfare treatment

**Holding and operative rule:** A United States citizen who establishes bona fide residence in a State becomes its citizen on equal terms; the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship and Privileges or Immunities provisions forbid lowering that otherwise eligible citizen's ordinary welfare benefits solely because of recent arrival or the former State's benefit amount. California's first-year differential violates that guarantee even if the person never received benefits before moving or moved for reasons unrelated to assistance.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls both holdings, eight votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court compares current citizens within their present State. Slaughter-House specifically recognizes a citizen's ability to settle in another State and become its citizen; the Court applies that limited proposition without reopening the decision's whole account of the Clause. Shapiro rejects durational welfare penalties, Dunn distinguishes genuine residence from added duration, and Zobel rejects allocation of ordinary resident benefits by arrival history. The State cannot answer that inequality by observing that a newcomer receives no less than in the former State. Prior benefits do not define present citizenship, and eventual equality does not cure twelve months of inferior treatment. Saving money is a legitimate general objective, but selecting new citizens for the saving merely restates the challenged disadvantage. This is equality within a program California has chosen, not a constitutional entitlement to a program or a national benefit level. Bona fide residence verification and genuinely equal prospective eligibility rules remain available. The decision does not govern distinct services or contributions earned elsewhere or supply a general catalogue of privileges. The implementing instructions' exclusion of families newly arriving from another country does not cure the discrimination among current citizens who moved from another State.

##### Federal permission cannot lower the constitutional floor

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may structure and condition federal welfare assistance, but may not authorize a State to violate the Fourteenth Amendment's equal-citizenship guarantee. Section 604(c)'s permission to use prior-State benefit levels therefore cannot sustain California's unconstitutional differential; permission is not a federal command.

**Controlling explanation:** Boerne distinguishes enforcement of a constitutional guarantee from replacement of its content. The federal provision does not change the fact that California administers unequal treatment among bona fide current citizens. Statutory permission cannot remove the protection that makes the classification invalid. The Court does not invalidate every federal welfare provision, require a nationwide program design or decide a constitutionally distinct nationwide rule. Thomas accepts the general inability to authorize a proved violation but disputes the underlying guarantee's application; that disagreement does not make the permission defense sufficient once this classification is held unconstitutional.

#### Precedent Treatment

- The Slaughter-House Cases supply the specific recognition of becoming another State's citizen through bona fide residence; their broader construction is not reopened.
- Shapiro v. Thompson supplies the prohibition on durational welfare penalties; a reduced payment still burdens current equality.
- Dunn v. Blumstein distinguishes actual residence from additional duration.
- Zobel v. Williams rejects ordinary resident-benefit allocation by arrival history.
- City of Boerne v. Flores prevents statutory replacement of constitutional content.
- Lunding and Romer retain their source-specific equality rules; neither is converted into a general judicial guarantee of welfare amounts.

#### Law After Decision

California must treat otherwise eligible bona fide current citizens equally in ordinary welfare benefits without the first-year prior-State ceiling. Legitimate residence verification and equal eligibility conditions remain enforceable. The ruling rests on narrow equal state citizenship, creates no national welfare amount and cannot be displaced by federal permission. No precedent is overruled.

### O'Sullivan v. Boerckel; No. 97-2048; Seventh Circuit judgment, 135 F.3d 1194. — 1999-06-07

#### Holdings

###### I. An available ordinary discretionary petition is part of one complete round

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the pre-AEDPA law governing this application, a prisoner must fairly present each federal claim through one complete round of the State's established ordinary appellate process, including a petition for discretionary review to its highest court when that petition is an ordinary available step. Former section 2254(b) preserves exceptions for absence of a state corrective process or circumstances making that process ineffective to protect the applicant's rights; actual state-law unavailability must be respected, and the rule does not require every extraordinary remedy or repetitive collateral presentation after a complete ordinary round.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, commands six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh makes former chapter 153 law govern Boerckel's pre-enactment application. Former section 2254(c) turns on a state-law right to raise the question through an available procedure, not a right to obtain a merits decision. Illinois Rule 315 supplies a regular petition for leave to appeal. Its selective criteria—importance, conflict, supervisory need and finality—allow the Illinois Supreme Court to choose cases; they do not make the ordinary filing unavailable. The State's highest court must have its own opportunity to consider each federal claim. The contrary argument stresses the burden of repeating rejected claims in a selective system. But selection belongs to the state court, and the ordinary petition gives it the choice. Picard supplies fair presentation; it does not permit omission of an otherwise required ordinary step. Castille involved a claim first raised through discretionary review and therefore does not mechanically answer whether an already presented claim must pass through the next ordinary stage. Lonchar requires the governing procedural sources, not a generalized finality preference. Here Illinois supplied the ordinary leave procedure and no established directive excluded it from the process. Mere discretion and unlikely acceptance do not establish unavailability. Petitioning this Court is not a substitute for giving the State's own highest court the opportunity, and no equivalent federal-certiorari exhaustion requirement follows.

###### II. Expiration of the omitted opportunity produces default, with existing gateways preserved

**Holding and operative rule:** When a prisoner omits a claim from an otherwise required ordinary state-review step and the opportunity to use that step has expired, the claim is procedurally defaulted rather than awaiting exhaustion through a still-available remedy. Boerckel's three omitted claims fall within that rule, subject to recognized cause-and-prejudice and actual-innocence gateways at their existing scope; further proceedings must respect the actual prior gateway rulings and the governing pre-AEDPA law.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; the same six-Justice coalition directly adopts the classification and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Coleman distinguishes failure to exhaust an available remedy from default when a required remedy has closed. The three claims were omitted from an ordinary petition and their opportunity has expired. The Seventh Circuit's contrary premise therefore cannot support further consideration without addressing default. Gray and Lambrix preserve claim-specific procedural treatment; a ruling on these claims does not decide all claims in the application. Nor does default eliminate the recognized gateways. The district court invited a cause showing, and Boerckel instead pursued actual innocence. It heard evidence in July 1996, incorrectly invoked AEDPA to exclude it, and alternatively concluded that evidence of other participants did not establish Boerckel's absence. Because the court of appeals found no default, it did not resolve any properly preserved challenge to that gateway treatment. Such review must apply pre-AEDPA law and consider the significance of the alternative ground. The Court makes no new finding of innocence, credibility, cause, prejudice or forfeiture, changes no gateway burden and orders no second hearing. The remand permits only further proceedings consistent with these existing procedural conditions; the constitutional merits remain undecided.

###### III. The effect of an express state opt-out retaining occasional review remains open

**Reservation and operative limit:** The Court does not decide whether a State may expressly identify an occasionally available discretionary procedure as outside its standard review process and state that prisoners need not invoke it before federal habeas. No such Illinois directive is established here. The decision neither requires a State to eliminate every possibility of occasional discretionary relief nor conclusively defines what an effective express opt-out must contain.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. It is a five-Justice reservation, not an adopted rule resolving the hypothetical procedure's availability. Stone-Zsela does not join this reservation.

**Explanation:** Ordinary selective review and a State's express definition of its standard process present different questions. The former is established here; the latter is not. Treating actual state-law unavailability as controlling therefore does not require a decision about a procedure that a State retains for occasional use while expressly excluding it from the standard exhaustion route. The Court leaves that question open without displacing the complete-round rule for Illinois's existing ordinary procedure.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lindh supplies pre-AEDPA chapter 153 for this application; the decision does not import amended exhaustion or evidentiary provisions merely because it is delivered after AEDPA.
- Picard's fair-presentation requirement is applied to each claim through the ordinary state-review round.
- Castille is distinguished: first presentation through a discretionary mechanism differs from omission at the next ordinary stage after proper presentation below. Its proper-presentation rule remains intact.
- Coleman supplies the distinction between an available unexhausted remedy and procedural default after an otherwise required route closes; it does not independently identify Illinois's required sequence.
- Lonchar preserves source-governed habeas procedure and does not itself decide which Illinois steps constitute exhaustion.
- Gray preserves separate treatment of distinct claims and defaults; it does not convert this exhaustion ruling into a decision of the constitutional merits.
- Lambrix preserves default as an independent procedural issue with its recognized gateway requirements; it does not convert this exhaustion ruling into a decision of the constitutional merits.

#### Law After Decision

Illinois's ordinary petition for leave to appeal must include each federal claim sought to be preserved through a complete ordinary review round. Selective acceptance alone does not excuse omission. Expiration of that required opportunity produces default, subject to the existing gateways and actual procedural posture. Actual state-law unavailability and the former statute's absent-process and ineffective-process exceptions remain distinct from mere discretion. The effect of an express opt-out retaining occasional review is unresolved. No precedent is overruled, no universal duty to pursue extraordinary remedies is adopted and no new innocence standard is imposed.

## Current-term law

### City of Chicago v. Morales, No. 97-1121. — 1999-06-10

#### Holdings

**Meaningful standards for dispersal.** A criminal ordinance is facially vague when every prosecution depends on an officer's conclusion that people remain in public with no apparent purpose, and the law supplies no meaningful objective criterion governing that conclusion. A later refusal to disperse cannot cure the standardless condition authorizing the order.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven directly adopt the enforcement-guidance ground and the remedy tied to its common trigger; no aggregation of distinct notice or liberty theories supplies their majority.

**Controlling explanation:** Kolender requires minimum legal guidance for enforcement independently of whether an individual knows that an officer demands compliance. Peoples Rights Organization distinguishes difficult factual proof from the absence of a central legal criterion; fault and apparent clear examples cannot automatically supply a missing criterion. Chicago's ordinance provides gang-identification, personnel and geographic limits, but none answers why a group's otherwise lawful presence may be ended. An innocent purpose need not be apparent. The ordinance requires no obstruction, threat, trespass, intimidation, criminal conduct or minimum duration before the officer must order everyone away. General Order 92-4 addresses officers, areas and gang identification, not this missing substantive condition. Illinois rejected the proposed narrowing constructions; the Court cannot insert an intimidation element. A warning makes departure avoid arrest but leaves the original choice of whom to displace uncontrolled.

The defect governs the common legal trigger of every dispersal order. Facial relief therefore rests on that central defect, not uncertain margins of an otherwise intelligible rule, nor a claim that every gang gathering is immune from regulation. Hoffman Estates preserves contextual limits on facial review; it does not transform an undefined predicate into a definite offense. The Court does not rely on an independently sufficient fair-notice ground or recognize an unlimited substantive right to remain anywhere. Objectively bounded public-order laws remain available. No severable independent offense survives within this single mechanism.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Kolender supplies an independent enforcement-guidance requirement; an individual warning does not replace a legal standard for initiating the command.
- Peoples Rights Organization applies the distinction between difficult proof and a missing central legal criterion; its defect-specific approach supports relief here without a universal criminal facial-review rule.
- Hoffman Estates retains contextual facial-review and clear-core principles; it is distinguished because the Court finds a defect in the common legal trigger rather than merely uncertain margins.
- Grayned preserves intelligible notice and enforcement standards.
- Papachristou illustrates the danger of discretionary criminalization of ordinary public activity.
- Coates rejects criminal boundaries depending on variable personal reactions.

The decision applies those principles without establishing a new substantive liberty immunity.

#### Law After Decision

Chicago cannot predicate punishment on this standardless dispersal mechanism. Defined rules addressing obstruction, trespass, intimidation, threats, criminal conduct or emergency dispersal remain available subject to their own constitutional requirements. The decision establishes neither an unlimited right to remain in every place nor a rule invalidating every law involving discretionary factual judgments.

## Current-term law

### Dickinson v. Zurko, No. 98-377. — 1999-06-10

#### Holdings

**APA review of PTO factual findings.** Direct Federal Circuit review of PTO factual findings is governed by the applicable APA court-agency standard, not the clearly-erroneous standard used to review district-court findings. Section 559 preserves additional requirements imposed by statute or otherwise recognized by law, but the asserted pre-APA direct-review requirement has not been sufficiently established; the Federal Circuit must identify and apply the proper §706 category on the whole record.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The PTO is an agency subject to the APA's review structure. Section 559 does preserve antecedent legal requirements; its expressness rule for later statutes is not a command that every pre-APA rule must have appeared in an express statute. The asserted exception nevertheless needs an identifiable legal foundation. The earlier decisions use varied standards in different settings, including interference suits, equity proceedings and direct appeals. Morgan v. Daniels addressed priority between contestants in a subsequent equity proceeding. Its requirement of a thorough conviction does not establish a general direct-appeal relationship equivalent to review of district-court facts. Neither the later specialized court's practice nor patent uniformity supplies the missing preserved requirement.

Universal Camera demonstrates that agency review remains meaningful on the whole record, including contrary evidence, while preserving agency choice between fairly conflicting views. Allentown Mack requires application of the actual governing standard; it does not authorize substitution of judicial factfinding. Haggar concerns statutory interpretation and does not determine the standard for this factual appeal. The Federal Circuit expressly regarded the standard as outcome-determinative, so the difference cannot be dismissed as wording. The Court remands without choosing between §706(2)(A) and (E), deciding the prior-art fact or ultimate obviousness, ordering a new agency hearing, or governing district-court findings on a new evidentiary record. Section 706's whole-record and prejudicial-error instructions remain applicable on their terms.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Morgan v. Daniels retains its priority-dispute rule in the later equity-suit setting; it does not establish the claimed general direct-review clear-error standard.
- Universal Camera supplies meaningful whole-record scrutiny and preservation of agency choices among fairly conflicting views.
- Allentown Mack requires scrutiny under the actual announced governing standard; it supplies no patent exception or general de novo review.
- Greenwich Collieries requires identification of the statutory departure actually invoked; its persuasion-burden holding is not itself an appellate-review standard.
- Haggar preserves its distinction between lawful delegated legal interpretation and factual adjudication; it does not select the standard for direct PTO factual review.

#### Law After Decision

The Federal Circuit must use the APA framework in this direct PTO appeal. Section 559 continues to preserve additional requirements actually established by prior law; the asserted general patent-review exception fails. Section 706(2)(E) applies on its statutory hearing-record predicates, while (A) and authorized trial-de-novo review under (F) retain their separate terms. The Court does not collapse those categories or govern district-court findings on a new evidentiary record.

## Current-term law

### Neder v. United States, No. 97-1985. — 1999-06-10

#### Holdings

##### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding and operative rule:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutes use fraud as a legal term with settled content. Morissette supplies the principle that Congress ordinarily carries that established meaning into the statute; the absence of a separate materiality adjective does not strip fraud of its significance requirement. Durland includes fraudulent future promises, but does not make an inconsequential falsehood a fraudulent scheme or import every element of a completed civil tort. The statutory focus on schemes, and §1344's inclusion of attempts, explains why actual reliance and completed injury are unnecessary. Mail or wire use remains a distinct requirement, and the communication need not itself contain the falsehood if it executes the scheme.

Section 1344 separately covers executing or attempting a scheme to defraud a covered institution and executing or attempting a scheme to obtain its property by false or fraudulent pretenses. Both routes retain materiality for the deceptive schemes presented here; their other possible differences, including mental-state requirements in other applications, remain open. Wells construes the different wording of §1014, which does not require objective materiality in addition to its false-statement or overvaluation and purpose-to-influence requirements. It does not turn fraud language into an unrestricted false-statement prohibition. A purpose to influence under §1014 does not automatically establish the separate fraud element.

The natural-tendency formulation, reflected in Kungys, concerns the targeted decision in context. The Court does not exhaust every common-law formulation, unusual recipient preference, omission duty, or nonrepresentational scheme. Nor does it decide materiality under every differently worded offense. The judgment rejecting materiality under the three fraud provisions is reversed.

##### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding and operative rule:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

**Controlling explanation:** Gaudin assigns the application of an actual element to the jury. Section 7206(1) independently supplies materiality through its express prohibition of willfully subscribing a return declared under penalties of perjury that the signer does not believe true and correct as to every material matter. Part I establishes the element under the fraud statutes. The express tax and bank directions withheld that application, while the mail and wire charges omitted it. The judge's separate materiality ruling is not a jury finding.

Chapman places the burden on the Government for these preserved constitutional errors. Failure to offer a separately labeled factual materiality defense supplies neither waiver nor forfeiture and does not require Neder to prove innocence. Johnson's refusal to correct forfeited error under the fourth condition of plain-error review does not govern these preserved objections. Genuine findings elsewhere in an actual verdict can establish an equivalent determination; whether they do so here, and whether other supported inferences suffice, are distinct questions addressed in the following parts. Neither the Court's recognition of error nor Gaudin alone decides those remedial questions.

##### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding and operative rule:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

**Controlling explanation:** Roy permits examination of a particular omitted element without converting every omission into a structural defect requiring automatic reversal. Its qualified method concerns the error's effect on the jury that returned this verdict. Yates supplies the instructional-focus requirement: a reviewing court must examine whether the instructions directed the jury to evidence supporting the proposed inference, rather than assume the jury decided a question removed from its task. Necessary equivalent findings are sufficient, but not invariably indispensable to a reliable noncontribution conclusion.

Sullivan addresses a different defect, an invalid reasonable-doubt standard vitiating all factual findings. These instructions left an otherwise valid reasonable-doubt standard governing the submitted elements. Their express removal of materiality heightens the need to justify the connection; it does not itself destroy every remaining finding or forbid the inquiry Roy allows. Gaudin's allocation rule remains fully operative at trial.

Powerful and unchallenged evidence may support an inference, but neither label creates an automatic safe harbor. A reviewing court must assess defense disputes for what they concern and determine whether the actual findings resolve them. Sufficient evidence for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction is not the required showing. Mitchell likewise distinguishes actual contribution from enough other evidence to sustain a result. This holding does not classify every omitted element, constructive amendment, or total denial of jury trial. Richardson's reserved harmlessness questions concerning its distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect remain unresolved.

##### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The instruction required knowing, purposeful falsity of the reported total income: $53,625 for 1985 and minus $980,377 for 1986. It did not permit conviction for an unidentified collateral entry. The jury considered the defenses that the receipts were repayable loans and that professional advice or good faith negated culpability. Its findings reject those defenses insofar as they would make the specified totals truthful or their falsity innocent. The verdict does not resolve every subsidiary advice dispute, but its particular subject and culpability findings furnish the connection required by Roy's actual-contribution inquiry. Annual income is information used to determine income-tax liability and administer the returns.

The objection is substantial: the jury was expressly forbidden to decide materiality, so a court must not supply that missing decision. The Court does not suppose that jurors disobeyed the instruction, treat the judge's ruling as theirs, or declare that they necessarily made the omitted evaluation. It instead asks what effect that withdrawal had on an otherwise valid verdict about these identified income statements. The charge's specific focus and findings establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt under Roy's permitted method.

The conclusion requires no unanimous numerical finding of the alleged additional income, acceptance of every transaction or witness, unpaid-tax calculation, actual IRS response, or successful deception. It does not excuse future courts from submitting materiality. The Government retains the burden in each case; this record-specific conclusion neither creates a tax exception to Gaudin nor decides the fraud counts.

##### V. Initial harmlessness review of the affected fraud convictions

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. Scalia and Ginsburg join V-A only and would limit review to genuine equivalent findings. Stone-Zsela joins the statutory reversal but dissents from the remand for another harmlessness inquiry; he would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now.

**Controlling explanation:** The court of appeals rejected the antecedent statutory element and therefore did not undertake the required fraud harmlessness analysis. Unlike the tax charge's specified annual-income findings, the fraud instructions and theories require a distinct inquiry into what each verdict resolved. Inflated appraisals, concealed relationships and down payments, presales, and advance requests may bear strongly on lending decisions. Yet the charge did not require proof of every alleged detail, expressly removed bank materiality, and used cross-references for mail and wire fraud. A generic intent-to-deceive finding does not automatically resolve capacity to influence, and Wells prevents treating a §1014 purpose-to-influence finding as an automatic equivalent. The lending-purpose and transaction-significance disputes must be considered in context.

Part V-B applies Roy's qualified method and Chapman's burden to that unfinished appellate task. The tax affirmance proves neither harmlessness nor harmfulness for any fraud count. No present determination requires a new trial on every affected fraud count or sustains any one of them automatically. If the Government fails the governing showing on an affected conviction, instructional-error relief permits retrial; it does not establish evidentiary insufficiency or require acquittal.

The mandate does not independently dispose of §1014, conspiracy, or racketeering claims, reopen acquittals, or calculate a sentence, restitution, or release date. Any properly available dependent consequence must follow the actual predicate and ordinary mandate proceedings. Those boundaries are part of the limited remand, not an advance resolution of unpresented claims.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Morissette v. United States:** Applies the borrowed-term principle to fraud's established materiality requirement; statutory silence alone does not discard that content.
- **Durland v. United States:** Preserves coverage of fraudulent future promises while distinguishing that breadth from elimination of materiality or importation of every completed-tort requirement.
- **Kungys v. United States:** Uses its natural-tendency or capacity formulation for the financial deceptions presented here; its civil allocation and burden do not govern the criminal jury question.
- **United States v. Wells:** Preserves the different §1014 construction without an added objective-materiality element; it neither removes materiality from fraud statutes nor automatically cures their instructional error through a purpose-to-influence finding.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies jury determination of each actual materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt; harmlessness review does not authorize withdrawal of that element at trial.
- **Chapman v. California:** Applies the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden to establish that the preserved error did not contribute to the actual verdict.
- **California v. Roy:** Extends its qualified actual-verdict method to the materiality errors presented here; necessary equivalence is sufficient but not exclusive, and its instructional-focus limitation remains essential.
- **Yates v. Evatt:** Applies attention to the actual charge, findings, and evidence considered under the instructions; proof sufficient for a different verdict cannot replace contribution analysis.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Distinguishes removal of a particular element from invalidation of the reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings; its structural-error rule remains intact.
- **Johnson v. United States:** Distinguishes its discretionary plain-error disposition after forfeiture; it neither supplies a waiver here nor reduces Chapman's burden.
- **Jones v. United States:** Preserves its protection for charged statutory elements without treating it as a classification or harmlessness decision for this error.
- **Mitchell v. United States:** Applies its distinction between an error's actual contribution and sufficient other evidence without transferring its separate privilege holding.
- **Richardson v. United States:** Leaves intact its reservation of availability and application of harmless-error review for the distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect.

#### Law After Decision

Effective June 10, 1999, materiality must be submitted for the charged mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud schemes; §7206(1)'s express materiality element and §1014's different Wells rule retain their respective force. For the materiality errors presented here, the Government may establish harmlessness through Roy's qualified actual-verdict inquiry, but uncontested or overwhelming evidence is not an automatic substitute for that showing. The tax verdicts meet the standard on their specific charge and record; the affected fraud verdicts require initial application below.

The six-Justice tax ground is noncontribution to the actual verdict, not a rule that the jury necessarily decided every omitted materiality question. Stevens's supplementary view and Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive equivalent-findings method do not control. The Chief accepts the governing broader method but disagrees with its application and the further fraud inquiry. The decision establishes no universal omitted-element classification, generic tax shortcut, new no-contest waiver, or automatic effect on a differently worded offense. Richardson's separate harmlessness reservation remains open.

## Current-term law

### Cunningham v. Hamilton County, No. 98-727. — 1999-06-14

#### Holdings

**Complete separation from the merits.** A monetary Rule 37 discovery sanction against counsel during ongoing litigation is not immediately appealable under §1291's collateral-order doctrine merely because counsel's representation has ended. The category does not satisfy the requirement of an important question completely separate from the merits when review of discovery relevance, responses and compliance remains intertwined with the case's substantive issues and litigation record.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices. This ground independently requires dismissal of the interlocutory appeal.

**Controlling explanation:** Cohen and Coopers & Lybrand require every collateral-order condition, not conclusiveness alone. Assuming the monetary determination is conclusive, evaluating whether discovery was relevant, responses sufficient or orders obeyed may require review of the underlying dispute. Counsel's personal financial interest differs from the client's but does not detach those questions from the litigation. Digital Equipment requires category-level assessment rather than an exception whenever one order looks especially separate, and Swint rejects convenience as a substitute for appellate jurisdiction. Cunningham's disqualification ended her representation; it did not end the civil-rights action or alter the connection between the discovery sanction and that action. The Court decides appealability of the $1,494 order, not whether the sanction, its amount or the disqualification was correct.

**Effective review after final judgment.** Independently, this monetary discovery sanction remains effectively reviewable after final judgment: the attorney personally subject to it may challenge her own sanction even though she no longer represents the client. Ordinary payment, delay and professional or reputational burdens do not establish an important right that will be irretrievably lost without immediate review.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices. This ground independently defeats the collateral-order appeal.

**Controlling explanation:** Digital Equipment distinguishes the costs of delayed review from the loss of a right sufficiently important to justify immediate appeal. Later reversal can vacate the monetary obligation and direct repayment or other correction. Cunningham's own right to seek review does not depend entirely on her former client's decision to appeal. Behrens preserves qualifying legal-immunity appeals, but this sanction establishes no immunity or right to avoid the trial itself. The Sixth Circuit reserved effective unreviewability; the Court resolves it as a legal question here. Ending representation alone does not make later relief ineffective. The holding does not resolve a materially different final-withdrawal situation establishing genuine independent nonparty finality, another sanctions regime, or a contempt adjudication carrying its own finality. Section 1292(b), extraordinary mandamus and genuine independent contempt routes retain their own requirements; none is satisfied or granted by this decision.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Cohen retains the narrow collateral-order exception and its cumulative conditions.
- Coopers & Lybrand preserves those cumulative requirements; conclusiveness does not excuse a failure of complete separation or effective unreviewability.
- Digital Equipment supplies category-level discipline and preserves adequate final review despite irretrievable ordinary litigation costs; those principles apply to this monetary discovery sanction.
- Swint rejects convenience-based appellate jurisdiction and distinguishes ordinary liability from legal immunity.
- Behrens retains otherwise qualifying legal-immunity appeals, not immediate review of every personal monetary or professional burden.

#### Law After Decision

The final-judgment rule governs this monetary Rule 37 sanction even after representation ends through disqualification. The attorney retains review of her own sanction after final judgment. Different statutory appeal provisions, extraordinary remedies and genuinely distinct finality categories retain their existing prerequisites. There is no general attorney-status exception and no holding that every order against an attorney is unappealable.

## Current-term law

### Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association, Inc. v. United States, No. 98-387. — 1999-06-14

#### Holdings

**Direct and material advancement.** Section 1304 cannot be enforced against these Louisiana broadcasters' truthful, nonmisleading advertisements for lawful private-casino gambling because the federal scheme's actual exceptions and operation do not directly and materially advance its asserted gambling-harm reduction and state-policy accommodation interests. Potential reception in a State prohibiting private casinos does not itself make the advertised lawful transactions unlawful.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens joins this application conditionally under the existing framework; Scalia joins the application while retaining broader doctrinal reservations. Thomas supports the judgment on a different ground and adds no balancing vote.

**Controlling explanation:** Central Hudson requires a substantial interest and direct material advancement; Edenfield requires real harm and material alleviation without invariably demanding studies. Even accepting the stated interests as substantial, the Government must explain how this restriction serves them in its actual setting. The conditioned state, tribal, nonprofit, governmental and occasional promotional exceptions allow materially comparable gambling promotion that undercuts the claimed demand-reduction mechanism. Different policies supporting those exceptions do not explain why suppression of these lawful private-casino advertisements materially serves that mechanism. Rubin requires attention to those contradictions rather than the prohibition viewed alone.

Edge upheld a licensing-State restriction that meaningfully reduced promotion and accommodated lottery and nonlottery policies on its record. This differently structured private-casino scheme does not establish that mechanism merely because some signals may cross a border. The Court does not require regulation of every source at once, regard leakage or any exception as automatically fatal, or demand numerical causal proof. It does not decide whether deficiencies in advertising/social-cost evidence independently fail the Government's evidentiary burden or find that advertising never increases demand. Stronger evidence of the assumed causal chain would not cure the structural defects identified here; this is not a remand to rescue this scheme with additional causal studies.

**Reasonable fit.** Independently, suppressing these truthful lawful-casino advertisements lacks a reasonable proportional fit with the asserted interests, given the scheme's extensive comparable permissions and available direct regulation of gambling. The Government may pursue genuine incremental policies, but the actual informational burden requires justification; the First Amendment does not require the least restrictive imaginable policy.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Court-opinion coalition directly joins this independently sufficient ground, with the same stated reservations.

**Controlling explanation:** Fox requires a reasonable fit, not the least restrictive means. Rubin and 44 Liquormart treat the burden on truthful information as an inquiry distinct from material advancement. Here the restriction broadly suppresses lawful gaming information while permitting the described comparable promotion. The ability to address gambling conduct directly bears on whether that informational burden is proportionate. Different interstate reach or administrative convenience does not itself explain the actual imbalance. The Court orders no tax, casino limit or replacement program and does not assume every alternative would achieve identical effects. It holds only that the restriction before it lacks justified proportionality on this record. Ordinary Central Hudson review remains governing; the decision creates no heightened tier, vice-speech immunity or general ban on incremental economic regulation. Relief is as applied to the advertisements and stations before the Court, not facial invalidation of §1304 or its implementing rule.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Central Hudson supplies ordinary lawful/nonmisleading commercial-speech review, substantial interest, material advancement and reasonable fit.
- Fox preserves the distinction between reasonable fit and least-restrictive means.
- Edenfield requires justification responsive to real harms and material alleviation without invariably requiring empirical studies.
- Ibanez tests the actual truthful-information restriction and its responsive justification.
- Rubin applies independent advancement and fit inquiries to a materially contradictory scheme and requires an actual state-accommodation mechanism.
- 44 Liquormart preserves those separate inquiries under ordinary scrutiny; its alcohol-specific rule does not create a gambling exception or a new tier.
- Edge retains its record-specific meaningful-reduction and state-accommodation holding; the different statutory permissions and mechanism here do not reproduce it.
- Posadas is not overruled through this as-applied decision, and no categorical constitutional rule against all vice regulation is adopted.

#### Law After Decision

The described lawful private-casino advertising is protected against this federal prohibition because each of two ordinary commercial-speech requirements fails independently. False or misleading advertisements, promotion of unlawful transactions, targeting minors and materially narrower evidence-supported restrictions remain outside the holding. Direct regulation of gambling survives. Other asserted applications require their own justification; neither §1304 nor the FCC restriction is erased in all settings.

### Saenz v. Roe; No. 98-97; judgment below, Roe v. Anderson, 134 F.3d 1400. — 1999-05-17

The preliminary injunction continues, and ordinary proceedings resume consistent with the resolved constitutional rule. ### Peoples Rights Organization, Inc. v. City of Columbus — 1998-10-15

**Justice Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part, unjoined.** He joins the Court's threshold, fault, definition and noncity-exception analyses and its common severability principles. He would affirm invalidity of both former-city-registration selectors. His Nordlinger objection to entrenched ownership and family preferences and his Heller and Romer positions require a meaningful relation to an impartial public purpose. Lawful pre-existing owners who registered and those unable to identify the invalid old duty are alike in the ownership reliance invoked here. Owners first covered by the replacement ordinance had no meaningful opportunity to enter the only registry. For him those defects disconnect the closed registry from the claimed protection, even though actual registration occurred. **Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part, unjoined.** He would affirm the Sixth Circuit's relief against all five challenged definition categories and both former-city-registration distinctions, while sustaining the remaining NFA magazine exception. Smolak and Walker's actual possession, closed registration window and threatened prosecution justify present review; P.R.O. seeks common relief for similarly situated members. No advisory judgment about a wholly hypothetical weapon is warranted.

In his view, the rifle definition does not choose among actual magazine possession, commercial availability and merely possible manufacture; the shotgun definition does not select a measuring shell length. Undefined modification and inaccessible original handgun-design history compound the handgun defect. Restoration and parts provisions inherit those uncertainties and add uncertain assembly conditions. Criminal exposure without a clear culpability safeguard makes those defects consequential for ordinary owners and grants enforcement discretion the text does not constrain. The City's conflicting suggested knowledge standard cannot rewrite the ordinance. He therefore does not adopt the Court's default-fault and definition constructions. Love reinforces intelligible notice for protected keeping but establishes neither a right to every listed weapon nor a judicially chosen capacity limit.

He rejects grandfathering based on success in guessing an invalid 1989 registration duty. That objection reaches section 2323.31(B)(3) and the matching city clause in section 2323.32(B)(2). NFA registration instead supplies an independent, intelligible reliance basis. Its broader coverage than magazines individually used with the registered firearm survives ordinary rationality under Romer without exact tailoring. He also agrees with the judgment sustaining the separate related inert-object magazine exception, adding no ground on that component. He would implement the declaratory and injunctive relief for the invalid provisions, preserve separable valid exceptions, and enact neither damages nor a fresh registry. He decides no unpresented substantive arms claim and joins no part of the Court's opinion.

## Current-term published noncontrolling positions

### Neder v. United States, No. 97-1985. — 1999-06-10

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I, II, and III. Fraud's settled meaning supplies materiality, Wells concerns another enactment, and Gaudin requires submission of actual elements. Roy makes these omissions reviewable trial error and permits reliable whole-record inferences beyond necessary equivalent findings. He does not adopt a universal structural-error rule or Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive method.

The Government's case must be weighed: the alleged omissions exceeding $1 million and $4 million plainly concern tax computation, the alleged inflated appraisals and nonexistent down payments concern lending decisions, and Neder did not separately contest materiality as a factual matter. Those considerations are substantial, not disqualified evidence. Nevertheless, the instructions removed tax and bank materiality and never asked the jury to assess that dimension of mail or wire fraud. The findings of knowing falsity and fraudulent intent do not reliably show the omission's lack of contribution to the actual verdict on each affected count. The annual-income findings identify the statements and culpability, but do not close that gap. The Government has shown what a properly instructed jury would almost certainly find, not harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt under these instructions and verdicts.

He would reverse the conviction-sustaining judgment, vacate every conviction affected by removal or omission of materiality, and remand for new trials if the United States elects to retry. He therefore dissents both from tax affirmance and from another fraud harmlessness inquiry. Sullivan forbids inventing a verdict, while Roy supplies the governing review; Johnson's forfeited-error disposition does not shift the burden. He would leave unaffected counts to their own preserved issues, disturb no §1014 conviction merely because of this holding, and order no insufficiency acquittal. A valid admission or a record establishing actual-verdict harmlessness could produce a different result.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Ginsburg.** Both join Parts I, II, and V-A. They agree on fraud materiality, the jury's role, the preserved claim and Government's burden, and a first appellate inquiry into the affected fraud convictions. Their Roy position permits review for actual findings that genuinely embrace the omitted element; it does not permit appellate certainty about an unresolved element to replace its jury determination. Gaudin and Sullivan protect that division of responsibility. They withhold assent from Part III's broader method and its classification of the express materiality withdrawal, while retaining Roy's classification within that decision's own personal-purpose category.

The tax verdict establishes knowing, purposeful false annual income. It does not establish the jury's own application of the omitted materiality requirement, which the instruction forbade it to consider. A judge's conclusion about the significance of annual income supplies the missing evaluation rather than identifies a genuine equivalent finding. They accordingly reject both Part IV's application and Stevens's additional rationale. Their ground permits new tax trials under correct instructions; it is not evidentiary insufficiency and does not require acquittal or unconditional release.

For fraud, they join the limited remand because the court of appeals' no-element ruling left a real initial inquiry unfinished. They would confine it to genuine equivalent findings on the affected counts, and do not join Part V-B's wider inference instruction. A valid equivalent determination can establish harmlessness, so their position is not automatic reversal for every omitted element. Ginsburg joins this writing throughout.

**Justice Stevens, concurring.** He joins the entire Court opinion, including its qualified actual-verdict method, tax noncontribution conclusion, and limited fraud remand. He adds that the required finding of knowing falsity of these particular annual-income totals embraces their material relationship to determining income-tax liability. His additional ground rests on the actual findings and their specific subject, not on appellate prediction about what another jury would decide. It requires no finding that every alleged dollar was income, that an unpaid-tax amount was established, or that the IRS was successfully deceived. His view supplies no additional holding beyond the Court's sufficient common ground. He continues to reject hypothetical-verdict substitution and does not use his disagreement with Wells to reopen the different §1014 offenses.
