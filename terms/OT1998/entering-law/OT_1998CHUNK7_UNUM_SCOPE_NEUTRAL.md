# UNUM — neutral scope confirmation

The contemporaneous United States merits amicus brief in No. 97-1868 expressly identifies two questions: California notice-prejudice insurance-saving, and whether the Elfstrom agency rule, under which an employer may in some circumstances be deemed an insurer's agent, relates to an ERISA plan under §514(a) when applied in a participant's §502 benefits action. This primary filing independently supports consideration of the second issue; the packet's short Question Granted sentence omits that issue. No contrary grant limitation has been identified. This is evidence of litigated scope, not an assertion that the amicus's wording is the Court's grant order.

Ward's merits position, as quoted in the published opinion from Respondent's Brief 35, did not invoke insurance-saving for the agency rule. Instead he characterized the lower court's reasoning as general agency principles and denied that it applied a categorical rule making all employers administering insured plans insurer agents as a matter of law. The contours of the lower doctrine were disputed. The scope of the issue is the lower court's no-relation rationale and its effect on benefit-plan administration; these source facts do not establish that all voluntary or fact-specific agency relationships are preempted.

Sources: [United States merits amicus, questions presented 1–2 and agency discussion](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/unum-life-insurance-v-ward-amicus-merits); the published recitation of Ward's position, 526 U.S. 358, Part IV. No historical Supreme Court outcome, reasoning or lineup is supplied here.
