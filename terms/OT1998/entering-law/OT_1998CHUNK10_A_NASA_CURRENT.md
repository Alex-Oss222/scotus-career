### National Federation of Federal Employees, Local 1309 v. Department of the Interior / Federal Labor Relations Authority v. Department of the Interior; Nos. 97-1184 and 97-1243. — 1999-03-03

#### Holdings

##### 1. The statute leaves the extent of midterm bargaining to bounded Authority construction

**Operative rule.** The federal labor statute does not categorically forbid union-initiated bargaining during an existing agreement, nor does it unambiguously require one comprehensive midterm bargaining regime. The Authority may determine the duty's timing and extent through a reasonable, explained construction, subject to contract coverage, lawful waiver, negotiable-subject restrictions, management rights and federal-law consistency.

**Authority.** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies a direct six-Justice majority for this holding and the holding below.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court must identify the authority Congress granted before deferring to an agency. *MCI* preserves statutory boundaries; *NationsBank* permits reasoned administrative choice where the enacted scheme leaves genuine discretion. Here the definition of a collective bargaining agreement is not expressly limited to the principal term contract. An agreement reached during its term can also fit that definition. Conversely, §7114's direction to negotiate toward an agreement and its reasonable-times and frequency requirements do not unmistakably prescribe a comprehensive continuing duty.

Section 7106 principally allocates authority by subject. Its management-right exceptions do not establish a prohibition on every other midterm proposal. The agency-election condition in §7106(b)(1) is not an election condition in (b)(2) or (3). Section 7105 assigns the Authority responsibility for administering bargaining obligations within those limits. The efficiency instruction in §7101 and the conditional impasse powers in §7119 inform that administration; they do not prove perpetual negotiations or inevitable imposition of this clause. A prediction of disruption cannot replace the statutory inquiry, just as the desirability of responding to new workplace needs cannot establish an unlimited duty.

The Fourth Circuit's categorical prohibition therefore rests on an erroneous legal premise. The Authority must explain the lawful regime; the Court does not select a universal duty or universal exemption, create a new factor checklist, or authorize reopening matters an agreement actually settles. The Court does not determine the treatment of a hypothetical future proposal under the Authority's covered-matter or waiver rules.

##### 2. Contractual negotiability requires reconsideration under the corrected legal framework

**Operative rule.** The absence of an independently compelled statutory entitlement does not itself make bargaining over a contractual midterm obligation unlawful. The Authority must reconsider the actual advance clause under the permissible statutory framework; its existing alternative rationale does not warrant an immediate direction to enforce this order because it remains tied to the premise that the general midterm regime was statutorily required.

**Controlling explanation.** The Authority did give an independent reason: contractual proposals can be negotiable even when their substance is not a statutory entitlement. It also distinguished contract coverage from a categorical denial of midterm bargaining. Those are actual reasons, not an appellate lawyer's invention. But the current order continued to treat the clause as restating a statutory right, and its alternative explanation chiefly answered the circuit's contrary precedent. It did not fully exercise the distinct choice over the relationship among a general duty, an advance clause, contractual repose, waiver and the public impasse system once statutory compulsion was removed.

*NationsBank* requires reasoned exercise of genuine discretion, not an assumption that legal necessity supplies the agency's choice. *AT&T v. Iowa Utilities Board* separates administrative implementing power from the validity of particular duties and permits return under the correct legal allocation. *Boston & Maine* requires a discernible agency ground; that requirement is satisfied here, but it does not make every stated alternative sufficient after a governing premise changes. *Good Samaritan* permits considered interpretive change; the Court does not remand merely because the Authority changed its view. This is legal-framework reconsideration, not an evidentiary remand or punishment for inconsistency.

The Authority may adopt the same negotiability result if it explains a permissible construction. Sections 7103(a)(14)(C), excluding matters specifically provided for by federal statute, and 7117(a), preserving federal-law consistency, remain distinct limitations; neither is satisfied merely by statutory silence. The Court does not impose a clause, require agreement or concession, or adjudicate a Panel award. Agreed waiver, a duty to negotiate a waiver clause and compulsory acceptance at impasse remain separate questions; an employer's power to impose a comprehensive waiver is not presumed.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Chevron* remains the framework for determining statutory meaning and the scope of permissible agency construction; no automatic deference follows from the agency label.
- *MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T Co.* supplies the requirement that operative statutory limits be identified and enforced before administrative discretion is recognized; its tariff holding creates no general rule restricting agencies to minor choices.
- *NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.* supplies the distinction between genuine statutory discretion and the agency's reasoned exercise of it. Establishing authority alone does not validate the existing order.
- *ICC v. Boston & Maine Corp.* supplies the discernible-agency-ground requirement. The Authority's contractual explanation is acknowledged rather than replaced with counsel's reasoning.
- *Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala* preserves permissible considered changes of interpretation. Change alone does not disqualify the Authority's construction or compel this remand.
- *AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board* supplies the distinction between implementation authority, particular statutory restrictions and rules requiring agency reconsideration under the correct allocation. Its telecommunications rules are not transferred to federal labor relations.
- *Your Home Visiting Nurse Services, Inc. v. Shalala* preserves statutory construction as distinct from additional regulatory deference; an administrative label cannot settle statutory power.
- *Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.* permits consideration of a fairly included legal argument within a presented claim. The general-duty question here is expressly presented and supplies an actual agency premise; no new question or relief is added.

#### Law After Decision

Federal labor law now rejects a judicial categorical prohibition on all union-initiated midterm bargaining and all advance contractual clauses requiring it. The Authority retains responsibility for a reasoned choice within Congress's actual limits. The Court establishes neither a universal duty to entertain every midterm proposal nor final mandatory negotiability of this particular clause.

The distinction between statutory entitlement and contractual negotiability remains legally meaningful, but the existing Survey order is returned for reconsideration rather than ordered enforced. Management rights, statutory consistency, lawful waiver and the agreement's actual coverage retain their independent force. The Authority's descriptions of express coverage, subjects inseparably bound up with covered subjects and matters fully explored during term negotiations are not findings about an unidentified proposal. No rule about compulsory negotiation or imposition of a comprehensive waiver clause is settled.

## Current-term law

### United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.; No. 97-2044; judgment below, 127 F.3d 1460. — 1999-04-21

#### Holdings

##### Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation

**Holding and operative rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing Congress's actual delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation: courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and the delegation's boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The Court of International Trade's duty to decide on its own record does not repeal that legal framework; agency labels and notice and comment alone do not establish authority or validity.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Parts I–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer and control this complete framework.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes governing law from judicial determination of facts. Sections 1500(b) and 1502(a) and the tariff delegation provisions authorize implementing rules within their scope. Section 2640(a)(1)'s own-record review does not assign all substantive tariff lawmaking exclusively to courts, and §10.11's preservation of judicial review does not require disregarding a lawful regulation. Chevron supplies the division of responsibility; NationsBank permits reasonable gap filling within actual authority. MCI and NCUA require courts to enforce enacted limits before deferring. The importer correctly insists that review remain independent on those limits and effective on the facts. That does not justify categorically withholding the weight due a sufficiently authoritative delegated rule. Conversely, recognizing that framework does not make every Customs interpretation lawful. Informal classification letters and later administrative doctrines are not decided.

##### This regulation's statutory fit and actual application require the corrected inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment must be vacated because categorical nondeference left unresolved whether the regulation reasonably respects the tariff's incidental-assembly allowance and lawfully applies to this process. On remand the courts must enforce the enacted conditions, examine the regulation's actual introductory terms and examples, and resolve relevant application questions under the proper review standards; neither regulatory validity nor the claimed exemption is established in advance.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part IV is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, seven votes. Stevens and Ginsburg do not join this holding or remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The tariff requires United States fabricated components exported ready for assembly without further fabrication, retention of physical identity, and no advancement in value or improvement abroad except through assembly and incidental operations, including cleaning, lubricating and painting. The allowance reduces duties by qualifying component cost or value, with proportional treatment for specific or compound rates; it does not exempt the whole article. Regulation §10.16(b) permits incidental operations before, during or after assembly, including ordinary final pressing and folding. Section 10.16(c) addresses a significant nonassembly process whose primary purpose is fabrication, completion or physical or chemical improvement, or a significant process unrelated to assembly. Its chemical-treatment examples, including permapressing, do not erase those introductory alternatives. Timing after sewing alone settles nothing, and ordinary pressing is not necessarily resin-activating permapressing. The significance, purpose, relationship to assembly and new characteristics of the actual process bear on the statutory-regulatory boundary, not merely on duty arithmetic. NationsBank's framework does not answer those unfinished questions. AT&T distinguishes general authority from particular validity; NFFE distinguishes available discretion from its actual lawful exercise without requiring a new agency proceeding here. Both questions were granted, but that scope does not make this intertwined inquiry complete. The lower courts must undertake it without the erroneous categorical premise. The Court commands no new evidentiary hearing, burden, deadline or finding that existing litigation papers are missing.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Chevron supplies review within actual delegated authority and statutory ambiguity.
- NationsBank of North Carolina v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co. permits reasonable administrative classification within statutory limits.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T and National Credit Union Administration v. First National Bank require enforcement of enacted boundaries before deference.
- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board separates general authority, particular rule validity and unresolved applications; it creates no automatic validation or universal remand rule.
- NFFE/FLRA v. Department of the Interior distinguishes legally available discretion from its lawful exercise; its distinct remedy does not compel a new Customs proceeding.

#### Law After Decision

The Federal Circuit may not categorically exclude this promulgated tariff regulation from Chevron review. Judicial enforcement of statutory limits and trade-court factual review remain intact. The regulation's ultimate reasonableness and its application to Haggar's processing remain for the corrected inquiry. No precedent is overruled.

### INS v. Aguirre-Aguirre; No. 97-1754; judgment below, 121 F.3d 521. — 1999-05-03

#### Holdings

##### Courts must respect the Board's permissible construction within actual delegated authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §§1103(a)(1) and former 1253(h), together with the Board's adjudicative assignment in 8 C.F.R. §3.1(d)(1), courts must respect a permissible Board construction of the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception after independently enforcing enacted limits. Interpretation, application and factual review retain their distinct requirements; neither agency status nor a general appeal to foreign-affairs expertise supplies automatic correctness.

**Authority:** Breyer's Parts I–II are joined by all nine Justices and control this holding and the next two merits holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Haggar requires authority before deference and distinguishes a lawful interpretive choice from particular validity and application. The present statute and Board assignment establish authority independently of Haggar's tariff rulemaking setting. NationsBank permits reasoned classification within genuine ambiguity; MCI and NCUA prohibit erasing statutory conditions. The exception concerns serious reasons for considering that the applicant committed a serious nonpolitical crime outside the United States before arriving here. No criminal conviction is required, but the territorial, temporal and substantive conditions remain operative. The humanitarian importance of withholding supports careful enforcement of those limits, not an exclusively judicial factor code. Cardoza-Fonseca and Stevic illustrate independent enforcement of statutory meaning; Elias-Zacarias preserves the separate standard for reviewing supported findings. The Board must apply its announced lawful construction to the actual record and explain its decision. The Court adopts neither unconditional deference nor an automatic new agency hearing whenever judicial review used a mistaken premise.

##### A properly established crime exception requires no additional persecution-risk balance

**Holding and operative rule:** Once the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception is properly established, former §1253(h)(2) makes the paragraph (1) withholding protection inapplicable without an additional balancing of feared persecution against offense seriousness. The Board may address that exception while assuming the applicant could show that life or freedom would be threatened because of race, religion, nationality, membership in a particular social group or political opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute separately states the protection and its exceptions. A greater threatened harm does not dissolve a properly established crime exclusion through an additional condition Congress did not supply. The Handbook paragraph 156 argument relied on below presents a serious humanitarian objection, but does not change those distinct statutory inquiries. The Court finds no absence of persecution risk and creates no general discretion to deny withholding to an otherwise eligible applicant. Relevant political circumstances remain material under the lawful crime analysis. Discretionary asylum is a different question and receives no automatic disposition from this withholding rule.

##### The political-criminal comparison does not require every judicially preferred subsidiary finding

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board may compare an offense's political character with its ordinary criminal character and consider gross disproportionality or atrocious conduct in determining which predominates. The statute requires neither a separate atrocity finding in every case nor findings that the violence was necessary to or successful in achieving its political objective; the Board must still assess actual political purpose, context and conduct under its announced standard.

**Controlling explanation:** Political motive alone does not establish a political offense, just as unlawfulness alone does not make one nonpolitical. The Board's explanation considered protest objectives alongside property destruction and civilian assaults and found the criminal character disproportionate. The Court reviews that actual explanation rather than adopting new factual findings from the contested testimony. The Ninth Circuit’s comparison with more severe conduct is a consistency objection, not necessarily a universal atrocity prerequisite. The Board’s actual disproportionate-conduct explanation answers that objection; the greater atrocity of another case is no minimum threshold for this alternative ground. Handbook paragraph 152 and the suggested McMullen comparison reinforce the importance of political context and disproportion, but the absence of a separate comparison with McMullen's atrocious acts does not defeat a determination resting on the supported political-criminal comparison. Atrocious means may defeat political characterization; atrocity is not necessary in every case. A clear causal relation to a political objective would not necessarily overcome a supported disproportion finding, and the Court reserves any independent causal-link requirement in a different case. Relevant comparisons and reasoned treatment of genuine inconsistency remain possible. No categorical rule about bus burnings, assault severity or unsuccessful protest is announced.

##### The late record-error contention remains for its existing agency procedure

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court declines to decide the translation/transcription contention first raised here rather than before the Board or Court of Appeals; the applicant's existing BIA motion for a new hearing remains for determination through that lawful channel. If the BIA determines that modification of the record is necessary, it may determine whether the modified record warrants further consideration of withholding; no error, new hearing or outcome is presumed.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part III is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, eight rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins this component's disposition only and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged errors may be material if established, but this Court is not the first forum for resolving a contention not presented below. The existing motion supplies an actual avenue for that inquiry; its filing proves neither entitlement nor futility. The 1995 regulation's burden applies only if evidence indicates a withholding-denial ground; then the applicant must prove by a preponderance that the indicated ground does not apply. On the existing unrevised agency account, repeated gasoline-fueled bus burnings and civilian assaults, assessed against the political objective, indicate this exclusion and trigger that allocation. This uses conduct evidence, not silence, and makes no new credibility determination. The allocation is distinct from statutory serious reasons and judicial factual review. Failure to file a Board brief despite an extension is procedural history, not default, a concession, an adverse factual inference or proof that the conditional burden was unmet. The merits holdings rest independently on the lawful framework and stated Board explanation. A corrected record may affect their application without changing the statutory rule. The appellate remand itself commands no new Board hearing, stay or removal.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Haggar preserves actual delegation, statutory boundaries and separate validity/application review; its tariff remand is not a universal agency-remand command.
- NationsBank permits reasoned classification within genuine statutory discretion.
- MCI and NCUA retain every enacted condition before deference.
- INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca and INS v. Stevic illustrate independent enforcement of statutory meaning; they do not assign every evaluative choice exclusively to courts.
- INS v. Elias-Zacarias distinguishes evidence permitting a finding from evidence compelling displacement under the relevant review standard.
- INS v. Yang preserves reasoned administration and the separation of eligibility from discretion; it does not require a new hearing merely because a court corrects a legal premise.
- AT&T and NFFE preserve the distinction between authority and the agency's actual lawful choice; the Board already supplied the pertinent comparison here.

#### Law After Decision

The serious-nonpolitical-crime exception remains bounded by its actual statutory conditions and a permissible, reasoned Board construction. Courts may not impose a mandatory persecution-risk balance or additional political-necessity/success findings, nor treat atrocity as indispensable to the alternative disproportionate-conduct route. Actual record review and relevant political context retain their requirements; the existing motion remains for lawful agency determination. The Board’s separate discretionary asylum denial based on conduct against innocent civilians is not converted into an automatic statutory bar. No precedent is overruled.

### Dickinson v. Zurko, No. 98-377. — 1999-06-10

#### Holdings

**APA review of PTO factual findings.** Direct Federal Circuit review of PTO factual findings is governed by the applicable APA court-agency standard, not the clearly-erroneous standard used to review district-court findings. Section 559 preserves additional requirements imposed by statute or otherwise recognized by law, but the asserted pre-APA direct-review requirement has not been sufficiently established; the Federal Circuit must identify and apply the proper §706 category on the whole record.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The PTO is an agency subject to the APA's review structure. Section 559 does preserve antecedent legal requirements; its expressness rule for later statutes is not a command that every pre-APA rule must have appeared in an express statute. The asserted exception nevertheless needs an identifiable legal foundation. The earlier decisions use varied standards in different settings, including interference suits, equity proceedings and direct appeals. Morgan v. Daniels addressed priority between contestants in a subsequent equity proceeding. Its requirement of a thorough conviction does not establish a general direct-appeal relationship equivalent to review of district-court facts. Neither the later specialized court's practice nor patent uniformity supplies the missing preserved requirement.

Universal Camera demonstrates that agency review remains meaningful on the whole record, including contrary evidence, while preserving agency choice between fairly conflicting views. Allentown Mack requires application of the actual governing standard; it does not authorize substitution of judicial factfinding. Haggar concerns statutory interpretation and does not determine the standard for this factual appeal. The Federal Circuit expressly regarded the standard as outcome-determinative, so the difference cannot be dismissed as wording. The Court remands without choosing between §706(2)(A) and (E), deciding the prior-art fact or ultimate obviousness, ordering a new agency hearing, or governing district-court findings on a new evidentiary record. Section 706's whole-record and prejudicial-error instructions remain applicable on their terms.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Morgan v. Daniels retains its priority-dispute rule in the later equity-suit setting; it does not establish the claimed general direct-review clear-error standard.
- Universal Camera supplies meaningful whole-record scrutiny and preservation of agency choices among fairly conflicting views.
- Allentown Mack requires scrutiny under the actual announced governing standard; it supplies no patent exception or general de novo review.
- Greenwich Collieries requires identification of the statutory departure actually invoked; its persuasion-burden holding is not itself an appellate-review standard.
- Haggar preserves its distinction between lawful delegated legal interpretation and factual adjudication; it does not select the standard for direct PTO factual review.

#### Law After Decision

The Federal Circuit must use the APA framework in this direct PTO appeal. Section 559 continues to preserve additional requirements actually established by prior law; the asserted general patent-review exception fails. Section 706(2)(E) applies on its statutory hearing-record predicates, while (A) and authorized trial-de-novo review under (F) retain their separate terms. The Court does not collapse those categories or govern district-court findings on a new evidentiary record.

## Current-term published noncontrolling positions

### National Federation of Federal Employees, Local 1309 v. Department of the Interior / Federal Labor Relations Authority v. Department of the Interior; Nos. 97-1184 and 97-1243. — 1999-03-03

**O'Connor, dissenting, Part I, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** The agreement-directed duty in §7114, the separately structured continuing obligations and §7101's efficiency instruction together supply a statutory boundary. In their view, the broad definition of bargaining and the required frequency of meetings regulate negotiations already required; they do not authorize a comprehensive continuing union-initiated regime. The statute's public impasse system matters because bargaining may lead, after the statutory conditions are satisfied, to a binding lawful resolution. They do not assume that this particular clause will inevitably be imposed.

They accept that the absence of an entitlement ordinarily differs from a prohibition on negotiation. This general advance clause nevertheless recreates the compulsory regime they find inconsistent with the statutory structure and §7117(a). Its reference to negotiable uncovered subjects does not cure that temporal objection. They would affirm the refusal of enforcement while reserving genuinely narrower clauses and voluntary negotiations on particular subjects. They do not treat every statutory omission as a matter specifically settled under §7103(a)(14)(C), and do not revive the dismissed allegations against Interior.

**O'Connor, Part II, alone.** She adds that the Authority's court-induced reversal, continued assumption of a statutory mandate and limited independent explanation do not establish a fully reasoned administrative choice deserving acceptance. *Good Samaritan* permits diminished weight for a changed interpretation but does not automatically disqualify a considered permissible one; her objection concerns the actual reasons and asserted legal setting, not change alone. She would examine the text and structure without assuming that compliance with an earlier judicial interpretation supplies the agency's independent justification. Her statutory ground remains sufficient, and she does not announce a general displacement of *Chevron*. Scalia and Thomas do not join this additional ground.

The earlier decisions' varying labels and procedural settings do not, in their view, erase the substantive continuity identified by the specialized court's historical survey.
