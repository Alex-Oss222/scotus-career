## Event
American Airlines, Inc. v. Tice, No. 98-1704. Merits decision, June 10, 1999. Submitted on full merits briefing. On writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, which reversed judgment on the pleadings dismissing twelve pilots' ADEA claims and separately affirmed denial of transfer to the Northern District of Texas. The question is whether earlier age-discrimination judgments bind these pilots through virtual representation based on shared employment, policy interests and later common counsel.

## Participation
Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action
The judgment of the Seventh Circuit is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy
**Affirmed, 9–0.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The preclusion dismissal remains reversed and the ADEA claims return for ordinary proceedings. The separately resolved venue ruling remains undisturbed and is outside the granted question. The Court awards no damages and decides no ADEA liability or BFOQ defense.

## Holdings
**Nonparty preclusion.** A judgment cannot extinguish an absent pilot's individual ADEA claim merely because earlier plaintiffs challenged the same employer policy, shared broadly similar interests and used counsel later retained by that pilot. Binding requires consent, control, genuine privity, adequate authorized representation or another recognized arrangement consistent with due process; both claim and issue preclusion require that authority.

**Authority:** The Court's constitutional ruling, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Richards requires a lawful basis for binding a person who did not appear; South Central Bell applies that requirement to both kinds of preclusion. Shared employment does not establish who controlled the earlier litigation or whom its plaintiffs were authorized adequately to represent. These pilots were excluded from the earlier Johnson plaintiff group, and their age-related employment interests could differ. The EEOC's hiring action represented 57 identified persons, not these plaintiffs. The lower record's findings concerning participation and relationships do not establish the authority American invokes, and later common counsel cannot create it retrospectively. Efficiency and repeated challenges to one policy do not replace that prerequisite. The Court does not find every conceivable relationship affirmatively disproved, disturb judgments against actual parties, or diminish the ordinary precedential force of earlier decisions. The asserted preclusion ground fails; statutory liability remains for further proceedings.

**ADEA collective-action participation.** Section 626(b)'s incorporation of §216(b) requires an employee's written consent before that employee becomes a party plaintiff to an ADEA collective action. An ordinary opt-out class analogy cannot treat these pilots as earlier party plaintiffs without that consent.

**Authority:** The Court's statutory ruling, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela does not join this distinct ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress made affirmative written participation a condition of party-plaintiff status. American does not claim that these pilots consented in writing to an earlier ADEA group action. Similar policy interests cannot do what the statute requires the employee to do. This defeats the proposed collective-action party analogy independently of its constitutional representation defect. It does not make written consent necessary for every recognized form of nonparty binding or decide the employer's substantive ADEA defenses.

## Precedent Treatment
- Richards requires constitutionally sufficient authority to bind an absent litigant; shared interests alone do not establish it. Its retained adequate-representation rule remains fully operative.
- South Central Bell applies that same prerequisite to claim and issue preclusion; changing the label cannot cure the missing relationship.
- Dodd preserves necessary, fairly litigated findings against the same actual parties; it supplies no absent-party exception.
- Hansberry preserves adequate representation as a condition of representative binding.
- Martin v. Wilks preserves a stranger's opportunity to be heard rather than an automatic duty to intervene.
- Phillips Petroleum v. Shutts preserves its distinct class-procedure protections.

None makes identical interests sufficient representation.

## Law After Decision
Earlier judgments retain their lawful force against persons actually bound and their ordinary precedential influence. They do not extinguish these individual statutory claims merely because the same employer policy has previously survived a challenge. An ADEA employee becomes a party plaintiff to a collective action only through the written consent required by the incorporated statute; ordinary opt-out class analogies do not replace that requirement. Recognized nonparty relationships remain subject to their own requirements.

## Procedure After Action
The Seventh Circuit's reversal of dismissal stands. Proceedings continue on the pilots' claims and legally available defenses, including any sufficient BFOQ defense. The Court neither resolves those defenses nor requires a new exploratory hearing to create a different preclusion relationship. It does not invalidate the FAA age rule, certify a class, compel transfer, reopen actual-party judgments or guarantee trial.
