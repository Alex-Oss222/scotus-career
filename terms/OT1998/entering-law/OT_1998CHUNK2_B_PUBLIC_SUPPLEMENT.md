# Authorized public law and published positions

### Field v. Mans, No. 94-967

Merits decision, November 28, 1995

#### Chronology and Posture

Reported at 516 U.S. 59. Argued October 2, 1995. On certiorari to the First Circuit, 36 F.3d 1089 (1994), affirming rejection of the Fields' bankruptcy nondischargeability claim. The question is whether the actual-fraud claim under 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(2)(A) requires actual reliance alone, justifiable reliance, or objectively reasonable reliance.

The debt arose from a $187,500 note secured by a second mortgage and Mans's personal guarantee; the mortgage required consent to a transfer and allowed acceleration for an unconsented conveyance. Mans concealed the accomplished October 1987 conveyance in subsequent consent letters; the bankruptcy court found that the Fields actually relied and effectively extended credit by forgoing acceleration, while the separate statutory obtained-by and debt-extent questions remain unresolved here.

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

#### Judgment

**First Circuit judgment vacated and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Breyer, who would affirm on this record while accepting the same reliance rule.

The case returns through the First Circuit to the bankruptcy court for the correct justifiability inquiry, further findings as needed, and independent resolution of properly remaining obtained-by, extent and other statutory issues. No immediate nondischargeability judgment on the whole debt is directed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Part I — Actual fraud requires actual and justifiable reliance

**Operative rule:** For the actual-fraud claim presented under 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(2)(A), the creditor must prove actual and justifiable reliance. Justifiability turns on this recipient's knowledge, capacities and circumstances: there is no general duty to investigate, but a creditor cannot rely on known or personally obvious falsity, and a warning actually discovered may require inquiry under that recipient-specific standard.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine adopt this rule and its limits. Scalia and Breyer do not join Part II or the judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress used established common-law fraud language. As the interpretive method recognized in Darden illustrates, such terms carry their established legal content unless the statute indicates otherwise. Common-law deceit requires reliance, and Restatement §§537 and 540–541 distinguish justifiability from an ordinary prudent person's duty to investigate. Carelessness alone does not excuse intentional fraud; known or obvious falsity does. The recipient's actual capacities and encountered warnings therefore matter without creating a universal title-search or professional-advice requirement.

Subsection (A) excludes statements respecting the debtor's or an insider's financial condition. Subsection (B) separately covers a materially false written statement of that kind, on which the creditor reasonably relied, that the debtor caused to be made or published with intent to deceive. Its express reasonable-reliance requirement does not import that broader inquiry into (A). Nor does the modifier's absence from (A) erase reliance from actual fraud. Objective unreasonableness may bear on whether claimed actual reliance is credible, but it is not an independent universal prudence requirement. This is general common-law content, not the tort law of a particular State.

Grogan's ordinary preponderance burden remains unchanged. Fraud, actual reliance, justifiability and the requirement that the debt be obtained by the specified conduct remain separate. The Court decides the actual-fraud claim before it; it does not independently classify a different claim involving only false pretenses or representation without actual fraud, or determine the amount of any nondischargeable debt.

**Precedent treatment:** Darden's common-law-term method is applied by analogy beyond employee classification; Grogan's proof burden is preserved, not heightened or reduced.

##### Part II — The lower courts must apply that standard before a final debt determination

**Operative rule:** An adverse actual-fraud discharge judgment resting on the broader objective prudent-person investigation requirement cannot stand merely because the record contains individualized facts relevant to justifiability. The judgment is vacated and the case remanded for application of the correct standard to the existing findings, further findings as needed, and independent resolution of properly remaining statutory debt-nexus and extent questions.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices directly adopt the application and remedy. Scalia and Breyer dissent from this part and would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The bankruptcy court expressly asked what a prudent person should have investigated, including whether to consult an attorney, even while accepting a lack of sophistication. Its discussion of visits and reported ownership does not establish a separate application of the recipient-specific standard. The Court cannot resolve that uncertainty by treating the two standards as interchangeable. The dissent's reading of the same findings is substantial, but the express use of the broader requirement makes proper lower-court application necessary.

The existing findings of actual reliance, detriment and effective extension of credit are not erased by this legal correction. They also do not establish, without the separate statutory inquiry, that every dollar of the original note was obtained by the later concealment. The First Circuit must return the matter to the bankruptcy court for application of justifiability and proper treatment of the obtained-by and other remaining elements, with any further findings needed. Ordinary preservation rules remain for issues not resolved here. The Fields' request for immediate judgment on the entire obligation is not granted; no new Supreme Court finding of justifiability, causation or amount is made.

**Precedent treatment:** The common-law standard and Grogan's unchanged burden govern further proceedings; neither authority supplies an automatic nondischargeability judgment.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Grogan v. Garner, 498 U.S. 279: the creditor's preponderance burden remains unchanged; a policy favoring a fresh start does not create a heightened fraud burden.
- Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318, March 24, 1992: its presumption favoring established common-law content is applied by analogy to actual fraud, without importing its employee-classification factors.
- Restatement (Second) of Torts §§537, 540–541: the common-law reliance distinctions inform the statutory term; no general prudent-creditor investigation requirement is adopted.

#### Law After Decision

For the actual-fraud claim presented under §523(a)(2)(A), creditors must prove actual and justifiable reliance under the recipient-specific standard, not universal objective prudence. The ordinary proof burden and obtained-by limitation remain. Subsection (A) excludes statements respecting the debtor's or an insider's financial condition; subsection (B) separately requires a materially false written statement of that kind, reasonable creditor reliance, and the debtor's causing it to be made or published with intent to deceive. Existing findings must be assessed under the correct rule; the Court does not declare the entire debt nondischargeable.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Breyer dissents from Part II and the judgment, joined by Scalia; both join Part I's actual-and-justifiable-reliance rule. The disagreement concerns what the bankruptcy judge actually did with the record. The judge considered Field's continuing project visits, discussions of the development, the report that De Felice claimed ownership, opportunities for inquiry and the creditor's own circumstances. In the dissent's view, those recipient-specific circumstances and discovered warnings establish unjustifiability despite the judge's broader reasonable-person language. A remand would replace terminology without changing the dispositive application.

The dissent does not deny the affirmative finding of actual reliance. A person may in fact rely while failing the distinct justifiability limitation. Nor does it impose a universal obligation to search land records or consult an attorney, declare every incorrect legal label harmless, or resolve the obtained-by question. It would affirm because this record already supplies the correct-standard result; the separate causal issue would remain unnecessary to that disposition. The majority instead concludes that the explicit prudent-person formulation prevents treating the individualized discussion as a completed justifiability inquiry.

#### Source Notes

The [filed record and briefs, No.94-967](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385013_0573), including JA 35–45 and petitioners' requested relief at page 23, support the facts and procedural positions. [11 U.S.C. §523](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title11/pdf/USCODE-1994-title11-chap5-subchapII-sec523.pdf) supplies the relevant statutory language. The finding of actual reliance is distinct from justifiability; the Court leaves the separate obtained-by and amount questions to proper further proceedings.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Remand through the First Circuit to the bankruptcy court for correct-standard application and any needed findings, preserving independent statutory and ordinary procedural requirements; no further Supreme Court event is set.

### Romer v. Evans, No. 94-1039

Merits decision, May 20, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 10, 1995, in October Term 1995; reported at 517 U.S. 620. On certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1257 to the Colorado Supreme Court, which affirmed a permanent injunction against Amendment 2 on October 11, 1994, 882 P.2d 1335, Nos. 94SA48/94SA128.

Colorado voters approved Amendment 2 on November 3, 1992, by 813,966 to 710,151; it was certified December 16. The suit began November 12. The amendment added article II, §30 to the Colorado Constitution:

> No Protected Status Based on Homosexual, Lesbian, or Bisexual Orientation. Neither the State of Colorado, through any of its branches or departments, nor any of its agencies, political subdivisions, municipalities or school districts, shall enact, adopt or enforce any statute, regulation, ordinance or policy whereby homosexual, lesbian or bisexual orientation, conduct, practices or relationships shall constitute or otherwise be the basis of or entitle any person or class of persons to have or claim any minority status quota preferences, protected status or claim of discrimination. This Section of the Constitution shall be in all respects self-executing.

The authoritative state construction establishes withdrawal of existing orientation-based nondiscrimination protection and foreclosure of similar or greater protection through ordinary state and local legislative, executive and judicial channels unless the state constitution changes. The prohibition reaches the State itself, its agencies and schools, as well as local regulation in public and private settings. It is not confined to quotas, one ordinance or local preemption leaving the state legislature free to act. The Court assumes, without deciding, that generally applicable criminal, tort, contract and public-service protections remain available.

Evans I, 854 P.2d 1270 (1993), applied political-process strict scrutiny at the preliminary stage. After trial, the courts sustained a permanent injunction on that ground. The trial court rejected suspect or quasi-suspect status; plaintiffs did not appeal that ruling and do not concede its correctness. Respondents also pressed ordinary rational-basis invalidity below and here. The trial court and state majority did not decide that alternative because they applied strict scrutiny.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7–2.** Amendment 2 violates the Equal Protection Clause under ordinary rational-basis review.

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Justices Scalia and Thomas.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Souter delivers the Opinion of the Court, Parts I–II, joined throughout by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. All seven directly adopt the same rational-basis ground, its complete explanation and qualifications, and the full injunction. Justice Scalia dissents, joined throughout by Justice Thomas.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Amendment 2's class-specific disability fails ordinary rational-basis review

**Controlling proposition:** The Fourteenth Amendment forbids this combined withdrawal of existing nondiscrimination protection and constitutional foreclosure of comparable protection through ordinary government for the identified class. The amendment's established operation bears no rational relationship to the asserted legitimate interests. This conclusion does not depend on suspect status, political-process strict scrutiny, disappearance of generally applicable protection or a finding about every voter's motive.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by the seven Justices identified in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** Ordinary rationality permits reasonable predictions, imperfect categories and conceivable legitimate grounds without formal findings or empirical certainty. It does not require the least restrictive means. But the justification must relate to the actual disadvantage. Heller's proof holding, Moreno and Cleburne preserve that inquiry; Heller's family-participation holding shows why a narrower alternative alone is insufficient.

Uniformity explains a common rule, not why this class alone becomes constitutionally ineligible for protection from state as well as local authorities. Regulatory restraint can explain rejecting particular private obligations; it does not explain prohibiting ordinary public policies and protection across nonintimate relationships. Scarcity permits priorities, but does not explain a blanket prohibition regardless of the authority's resources or the protection's nature. Denver and Wisconsin findings of no added cost or impaired enforcement reinforce that conclusion without requiring empirical refutation of every possible saving.

Religious and intimate-association burdens are real concerns. Hurley likewise protects actual private expression, not a general right to discriminate in ordinary services. Exemptions illustrate the particular settings these interests concern; their availability alone is not decisive, and no municipality's exemption is finally approved. Those concerns do not rationally explain the amendment's prohibition across public and impersonal commercial settings.

Parents remain free to teach their moral convictions; no demonstrated connection makes nondiscrimination protection prevent that teaching, and government need not endorse every private belief. Stopping one side's political objectives or avoiding its participation because the issue is divisive cannot independently justify this disability. Bowers's specified-conduct holding does not explain barring protection by orientation throughout ordinary public and commercial life. Morality was preserved, but this conclusion neither invalidates every conduct-based moral judgment nor overrules Bowers.

The amendment's structure and established operation, rather than a free-standing hostility finding, expose the missing connection. Its class-wide disadvantage therefore falls within Moreno/Cleburne's prohibition. The Court imposes no general duty to enact nondiscrimination legislation.

##### II. The preserved ordinary-review ground supports the complete existing injunction

**Controlling proposition:** The Court may affirm on the fully presented rational-basis alternative without adopting the lower court's strict-scrutiny rationale. The established constitutional defect extends throughout the inseparable amendment, warranting preservation of the complete injunction without a new merits remand or a federal rewriting of state law.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court; the same seven Justices join in full.

**Controlling explanation:** The amendment, authoritative construction, asserted interests, relevant findings and adversarial ordinary-review arguments permit legal resolution now. Failure of strict scrutiny below does not itself establish failure of rational-basis review; Part I independently decides that question. Nor does the Court rely on an unconditional State concession that it could not satisfy strict scrutiny.

The State sought severance of orientation from conduct, practices and relationships. Colorado's court applied autonomy and enacting intent, concluding that those terms identify the same class and are inseparable. Deleting one description leaves the same constitutional disability. Because the defect found here permeates that common operation, no independent lawful remainder is established. The injunction corresponds to the violation rather than disabling a separately lawful structure. No new federal severability rule or universal facial-challenge formula is announced.

States retain substantial authority over governmental organization and substantive policy. That authority does not immunize a state constitutional provision from the Fourteenth Amendment. The continued ability to seek a constitutional amendment does not cure the particular unequal barrier to ordinary protection. Yet a policy defeat, referendum, repeal or constitutional reservation is not unconstitutional merely because it affects an identifiable group.

The Court neither reopens the unappealed classification issue nor adopts a fundamental right of every group to every lawmaking level. Particular ordinances, religious exemptions and future expression conflicts remain for their own cases. Reported separate First Amendment claims are neither decided nor presumed abandoned; their existence requires no new hearing or remand in this disposition.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- FCC v. Beach Communications, 508 U.S. 307: preserves conceivable legitimate grounds, justified economic generalizations and the absence of a formal-findings requirement, together with its Moreno/Cleburne limit. It supplies no categorical immunity for every express group classification.
- Heller v. Doe, June 24, 1993: applies Souter's controlling invalidation of the unequal confinement-proof burden because the proposed differences did not justify the actual disadvantage. Kennedy's separate controlling family-participation holding preserves a relevant informational generalization with counsel, effective contest and independent adjudication; a narrower alternative alone does not defeat rationality. No new scrutiny tier follows from either holding.
- Department of Agriculture v. Moreno, 413 U.S. 528, and Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432: apply the limits on bare harm and irrational prejudice while preserving relevant distinctions. Neither requires a finding about each elector's private motives.
- Hurley, June 19, 1995: preserves the private parade's expressive composition and ordinary nondiscrimination applications. Customer identity is not automatically a message, and ordinary service is not categorically expressive association. Swanner and American Life League retain their application-specific religious-liberty inquiries and actual RFRA burdens; no automatic exemption or transfer of those burdens results here.
- Hunter v. Erickson, 393 U.S. 385, and Washington v. Seattle School District No. 1, 458 U.S. 457: retain their racial political-restructuring settings. James v. Valtierra, 402 U.S. 137, Gordon v. Lance, 403 U.S. 1, and Crawford v. Board of Education, 458 U.S. 527, preclude treating every referendum, neutral voting rule or policy revision as unconstitutional exclusion. The Court does not adopt a new fundamental-right extension of those cases.
- Bowers v. Hardwick, 478 U.S. 186: remains in force on its specified-conduct question. The Court rejects the proposed justification for this wider eligibility-for-protection disability without deciding an unpresented overruling or declaring every conduct-based moral judgment illegitimate.
- Adarand, June 12, 1995: retains its supported federal remedial-classification standard and distinction between meaningful opportunity and entitlement to the requested benefit. It creates no sexual-orientation tier. Klinger preserves comparison directed to the actual challenged opportunity and distinguishes express classifications from purposeful discrimination in genuinely neutral decisions; Armstrong's federal-charging discovery threshold does not govern this express amendment.
- Dalton, March 18, 1996: preserves relief commensurate with an established violation and independent lawful operation. No general facial-review formula displaces the present inseparability analysis. Miller's abortion-specific method and 44 Liquormart's commercial-speech advancement and fit requirements supply neither a scrutiny tier nor an empirical burden here.

No precedent is overruled. The Court does not adopt Scott's separate lower-court Privileges or Immunities theory or the dissent's conditional Salerno argument as an additional holding.

Effective May 20, 1996, Amendment 2 cannot be enforced. Its established combination of withdrawing existing protection and making the identified class ineligible for comparable protection through ordinary government fails rational-basis review. The full injunction remains because that defect extends throughout the inseparable measure.

Ordinary policy choices and reasonable generalizations remain permissible. The decision creates no suspect or quasi-suspect classification, universal political-access right, evidentiary checklist or entitlement to any particular ordinance. It assumes no loss of all generally applicable legal protection and decides no broader general-law effect. Specific religious, intimate-association and expressive claims retain their own governing rules. Bowers remains in force, and no nationwide nondiscrimination code, quota requirement or damages remedy is established.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The two Justices would reverse the reviewed equal-protection judgment and remove that foundation for the injunction, with remand bounded by any independently preserved unresolved claims. They reject an extension of Hunter and Seattle into a fundamental entitlement of every group to pursue legislation at every governmental level, and they would sustain the amendment under ordinary rationality.

They accept its full restriction on state as well as local institutions, including public and nonintimate settings. In their view, regulatory restraint, religious and associational choice, finite enforcement resources and statewide uniformity rationally support withholding the disputed antidiscrimination protection. Reasonable predictions and imperfect coverage suffice; narrower exemptions are not constitutionally mandatory. The breadth implements the substantive policy rather than defeating its connection to those purposes. Constitutional reservation does not immunize the policy from federal review, but neither makes an otherwise rational policy unconstitutional.

Bowers is a sufficient additional premise in their reasoning: constitutional power to criminalize the specified conduct supports the lesser choice of withholding the disputed protection. Conduct-based moral judgment is distinct from hatred of persons, and orientation may serve as a proxy in this noncriminal policy. They do not claim Bowers literally adjudicated Amendment 2 or that every moral dislike is legitimate. Moreno and Cleburne continue to forbid bare harm; the dissent finds legitimate policy grounds here. Heller's controlling proof holding is distinguished as addressing an inadequately justified confinement risk, not displaced by its opposing opinion.

Their conditional facial argument also respects the state construction. If orientation, conduct, practices and relationships identify the same class, the Bowers inference, on their view, reaches the common operation. If a distinct orientation-only application is instead posited, constitutionally permissible conduct applications would defeat wholesale invalidation under their Salerno argument. That contention depends on their rejection of the Court's defect throughout the actual class-wide operation; it does not independently answer that defect. They order no severance, find no conduct by every class member and rely on no unverified admission by a respondent.

Scalia's commercial-speech position supplies no general prohibition of conduct-based moral legislation. Thomas's rejection of suppressing independently protected truthful speech, and his separate rule against keeping lawful purchasers ignorant to manipulate their choices, remain confined to that speech setting; he does not extend them into a universal bar on the different Bowers inference. Neither Justice treats actual religious conflicts as automatic exemptions for every ordinance. Their proposed remand would not adjudicate surviving First Amendment claims, invent a new hearing or authorize enforcement despite any independent lawful restraint. Their grounds and disposition are noncontrolling.

#### Source Notes

The amendment's text, authoritative minimum construction, trial findings and severability ruling appear in the Colorado proceedings, including Evans I, 854 P.2d 1270, and the reviewed opinion, 882 P.2d 1335. The parties' submissions establish their competing arguments; advocacy does not itself establish a finding or concession. The reproduced state opinion lacks printed pages B15 and B19. No absent filing, litigant default or additional fact is inferred from that omission; the supported text, construction and findings suffice for the questions decided.

Morality was preserved below; child welfare was not reasserted on the merits appeal. The trial findings concerning Denver and Wisconsin do not erase the State's competing claims about limited Colorado resources, unmet enforcement targets and future demands. Those predictions remain distinct from historical findings and from the proof required by strict scrutiny. The Court decides no individual ordinance's exemption, broader general-law effect, unappealed suspect-status question or reported separate First Amendment claim. No unconditional concession of strict-scrutiny failure or universal voter-motive finding is assumed.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Colorado Supreme Court's judgment is affirmed, and the permanent injunction against enforcement of Amendment 2 remains effective in full. The constitutional defect extends throughout the amendment's common class-specific operation; the authoritative state severability determination supplies no independent remainder.

The Court's merits review is complete. No new merits hearing, rational-basis hearing, severance proceeding, factual inquiry, damages award, quota entitlement or enactment of a particular nondiscrimination ordinance is ordered. Any independently preserved unresolved claim remains subject to its existing procedural requirements. The Court decides no separate First Amendment claim and expresses no view on whether further proceedings are needed.

Decided November 10, 1998, on review of the Ninth Circuit's vacatur of a preliminary injunction against Proposition 209. The appellate opinion was amended August 21, 1997. Review proceeds on full merits briefing; no argument date is established. The rehearing writings concern this same proceeding.

## Holdings

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion controls Parts I, II and IV with the five votes of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part III has all nine Justices' support. The following four holdings carry those respective coalitions.

### I. Selective racial restructuring requires a political-process inquiry

**Rule:** A State cannot answer a supported claim that it selectively moved authority over otherwise lawful racial remedies to a more remote political process merely by calling the foreclosed measures preferences. On the district court's preliminary findings, California's removal of those measures to constitutional amendment, while ordinary preferences remain available through ordinary officials, supports confined protection against the resulting unequal political burden; it neither requires a preference nor validates an unconstitutional program.

**Controlling explanation:** Hunter addresses a special political hurdle for racial protection; Seattle applies that principle to selective relocation of decisionmaking over racial remedies even when the underlying measure exceeds federal requirements. Romer preserves that limited doctrine without promising favorable legislation at every governmental level. The district court found both lost opportunities and the special amendment hurdle. Equal words about all races do not dispose of how that allocation operates. Crawford permits withdrawal of a voluntary policy, but does not eliminate Seattle's distinct inquiry into selectively changing who may decide racial questions. The State can enforce the constitutional limits on each proposed program; its interest in preventing unlawful classifications does not alone justify excluding measures that satisfy those limits. Croson, Hopwood and Engineering Contractors continue to require justification and fit. The Court decides the basis for limited preliminary relief, not the permanent validity of every provision or any particular program, and finds no need to attribute animus to voters.

### II. The sex theory requires a distinct inquiry

**Rule:** The racial political-process rule does not by itself resolve a challenge to removing authority over sex-conscious measures. The lower court must examine the asserted sex-based barrier on its own terms, applying the important-objective and substantial-relationship standard to an actual sex classification without presuming that every statewide policy withdrawal creates such a classification.

**Controlling explanation:** Virginia and Engineering Contractors require a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and a sex classification; they do not treat a stereotype as justification. Hunter and Seattle concern racial political structure and do not automatically decide every sex-conscious policy. The appellate categorical treatment omitted the separate inquiry. Section 31(c) preserves bona fide sex qualifications reasonably necessary to normal governmental operations in the three covered fields; it is neither a general permission for sex preferences nor proof that the initiative rests on a stereotype. The Court remands the distinct theory without finding all sex-neutrality rules invalid or extending racial strict scrutiny to them.

### III. Federal permission does not establish blanket preemption

**Rule:** Title VII's permission for defined voluntary affirmative action does not alone preempt a State's withdrawal of that policy or guarantee every subordinate governmental entity power to adopt it. An actual conflict with a federal statutory duty or binding federal remedy remains governed by federal supremacy and the applicable savings provisions; no such conflict is decided categorically for every operation of this initiative.

**Controlling explanation:** Weber and Johnson recognize lawful voluntary measures; they do not make every such measure compulsory. Section 703(j) prevents treating numerical imbalance in employment, union membership or related categories, measured against community, geographic or available-workforce figures, as itself requiring preferential treatment. It does not excuse actual unlawful discrimination. Section 708 separately preserves state duties except where state law requires or permits an unlawful employment practice. Section 1104 disclaims occupation of the field and preserves state law unless inconsistent with a provision or purpose of the Civil Rights Act. Those provisions cannot be collapsed into an unlimited exception. Proposition 209 protects existing orders, necessary federal-program eligibility and maximum lawful implementation on its actual terms. The district court's broad statutory theory therefore cannot sustain its entire injunction. Concrete federal conflicts and any distinct question of deference to administrative guidelines remain undecided.

### IV. Preliminary relief must correspond to the supported injury

**Rule:** Interim relief must protect against the demonstrated threatened loss of access to otherwise available lawful racial remedies, while leaving independently lawful provisions operative to the extent severable under state law. The lower court must conform its order to that constitutional injury and the ordinary preliminary-relief requirements, separately resolve the sex theory and concrete federal conflicts, and avoid turning interim protection into a permanent entitlement to a program.

**Controlling explanation:** The district findings supply a present burden on ordinary political access for preliminary consideration, not merely disagreement with an election result. They do not resolve the lawfulness of every affected program, the exact threatened injury defining an injunction's scope, or all remaining equitable prerequisites. The original order extended to implementation insofar as the initiative prohibited or affected affirmative-action programs throughout employment, education and contracting. That formulation may reach beyond the supported injury and does not justify automatic restoration of every part of the order. Section 31(h) requires maximum lawful implementation and severance of an invalid portion. The distinct ban on discrimination, genuine operational qualifications and other independently valid applications must be respected. The Court vacates the appellate judgment that categorically foreclosed the constitutional claim and returns the matter for a fitted order and further proceedings. No injunction validates a program that fails Croson, Hopwood or Engineering Contractors.

**Operative statutory limits:** Section 31 bars governmental discrimination and preferential treatment based on race, sex, color, ethnicity or national origin in public employment, education and contracting. It applies only to action after its effective date. Its sex exception is limited to bona fide qualifications reasonably necessary to normal public employment, education or contracting. It does not invalidate an order or consent decree already in force on the effective date; this protection is not confined to federal decrees and does not automatically extend to every later decree. It preserves action necessary to establish or maintain federal-program eligibility when ineligibility would cost the State federal funds, rather than every federally permitted action. Its governmental coverage includes the State and its local governments, public university systems including the University of California, community-college and school districts, special districts and other political subdivisions or instrumentalities. Remedies are the same regardless of the injured person's race, sex, color, ethnicity or national origin and are those otherwise available under then-existing California antidiscrimination law. The section is self-executing, must operate to the maximum extent federal law and the Constitution permit, and severs an invalid provision from the remainder. These conditions do not themselves establish whether a particular program is lawful.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hunter v. Erickson is applied to the special political hurdle for racial protection; its force does not depend on a right to win a policy dispute.
- Washington v. Seattle School District is applied to selective relocation of racial remedial authority; a voluntary underlying policy is not outside its concern for that reason alone.
- Crawford v. Board of Education is distinguished at its policy-withdrawal scope, which remains effective without erasing Seattle's structural inquiry.
- Romer v. Evans is applied as preserving those racial precedents while rejecting a universal entitlement to favorable law at every governmental level.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co. remains the local racial-classification standard; the political-process ruling excuses no defective program.
- Hopwood v. Texas retains its supported educational-diversity justification and required fit; permission does not compel a program.
- Engineering Contractors retains local racial strict scrutiny and the distinct demanding intermediate scrutiny for sex classifications; its limits govern affected measures on remand.
- United States v. Virginia supplies the genuine-objective and substantial-relationship requirement for sex classifications, not an automatic racial political-process rule.
- Adarand Constructors v. Pena retains its distinct federal standard; that standard is not transferred to state or local programs.
- United Steelworkers v. Weber and Johnson v. Transportation Agency are applied as permission for defined voluntary measures, not a universal command or a complete preemption rule.

## Law After Decision

The racial political-process rule applies to a supported selective relocation of authority over otherwise lawful remedies despite a substantive preference label. It does not require a program, permanently invalidate every part of Proposition 209, or displace the existing constitutional standards for racial and sex classifications. The sex challenge remains distinct. Title VII permission alone supplies no blanket preemption; concrete federal duties retain their force. Preliminary relief must track the supported injury and preserve severable lawful operation.

## Separate Writings

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, agrees that voluntary-action permission does not establish blanket Title VII preemption, but would affirm vacatur of the preliminary injunction. The dissent treats Proposition 209 as a statewide substantive restriction on officials' classifications, leaving ordinary antidiscrimination protection available. Crawford preserves the State's choice to withdraw optional policies; lawful remedies under Hopwood and Engineering Contractors are not compulsory policies. Hunter concerned unequal access to ordinary racial protection, and Seattle concerned specially displaced desegregation authority. In the dissent's view, those decisions do not entrench every optional preference-making power at a subordinate governmental level. The reciprocal sex provision likewise does not establish the asserted facial denial of equal protection. This position preserves scrutiny of actual unequal administration, binding federal obligations and properly presented later applications, and does not declare every affirmative measure unconstitutional.

## Procedure After Action

The preliminary-injunction appeal returns for conforming relief and further proceedings. The lower courts must distinguish the supported racial structural injury from the unresolved sex theory and concrete statutory conflicts, respect the initiative's actual exceptions and severability, and apply existing standards to any affected program. No final liability judgment, damages award or mandatory preference issues.
