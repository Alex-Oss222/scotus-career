# McClellan v. Cablevision of Connecticut, Inc. — 1999-01-07

## Event

McClellan v. Cablevision of Connecticut, Inc.; Second Circuit No. 97-7156. Merits decision, January 7, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, reviewing 149 F.3d 161 and its reversal of a pleading-stage dismissal. The question is whether section 531(e) authorizes the asserted implied private damages action after a cable operator's exclusion of public-access programming. The complaint also requested injunctions and asserted Connecticut law.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court reverses in part and remands on the implied federal damages question.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed in part and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The reversal reaches the asserted implied damages action only. The lower courts shall test any properly preserved remaining relief under its actual source and ordinary procedural requirements. No finding about the lawfulness of the exclusion or an affirmative alternative entitlement is made.

## Holdings

### The asserted federal damages action

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 531(e) of the Cable Act does not, by itself and in the statutory setting presented, create the asserted implied private damages action against Cablevision. A prohibition protecting public-access programming does not establish congressional intent to authorize that judicial damages remedy; independently authorized forms of relief must be identified and tested under their own sources.

**Authority.** Souter's unanimous Court opinion controls this rule, its damages-only review scope and the confined remand. All nine Justices join the full explanation.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the statutory command and the authority to recover damages present different questions. Cort v. Ash, Touche Ross and Transamerica require an inquiry into Congress's intent to authorize the particular action. Cannon permits contextual implication, so the concrete protection of programmers matters and an express remedy elsewhere is not automatically conclusive. But the differentiated provisions here and the limited judicial background do not support this new damages action. Section 532(d) expressly provides a particular leased-capacity action; section 531(c) addresses enforcement of franchise requirements. Neither is a general public-access damages authorization.

The strongest contrary argument combines the protected speakers, the direct editorial-control prohibition, the possible gap in franchise enforcement and Congress's later amendments. Those amendments followed only a few district-court recognitions, not the settled received-action background on which Curran and the existing securities action rest. The subsequent abandonment of one lower recognition is not attributed to Congress as foreknowledge. A gap in compensation does not itself supply the intended remedy, and the Court does not assume local enforcement is adequate or exclusive.

Suter preserves the separate action-creation inquiry. Franklin's appropriate-relief principle applies after a cognizable action exists; it cannot create its own antecedent. Central Bank and Musick, Peeler concern incidents of the received securities action and do not authorize importing it into the Cable Act. The supplied question concerns damages. The Court therefore decides neither every prospective remedy nor the legality of Cablevision's actual exclusion.

**Application, remedy and limits.** The Second Circuit's judgment is reversed only insofar as it permits the asserted implied federal damages action, and the case is remanded. The complaint also requested injunctions and asserted Connecticut law. Any surviving relief requires an actual authorized source and the ordinary preservation, jurisdictional and remedial requirements; no equitable action is created by the request itself. Viewer-claim status remains unresolved, no unpleaded section 1983 claim is created, and no damages are awarded.

Section 531(e) remains subject to section 544(d). Its separate permission to refuse public-access programs or portions containing obscenity, indecency or nudity is permission, not an obligation, and does not automatically extend that public-access wording to every educational or governmental use. The constitutionality and application of that permission, channel forum status, operator state action and whether the actual blanket exclusion was lawful remain undecided.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Cort v. Ash; Touche Ross v. Redington; Transamerica Mortgage Advisors v. Lewis:** Applied to the intent necessary for this asserted action and remedy; no universal express-text prerequisite is created.
- **Cannon v. University of Chicago:** Its contextual inquiry is preserved; beneficiary language and contrast with express remedies are considered rather than made automatically decisive.
- **Merrill Lynch v. Curran:** Distinguished because the limited public-access decisions do not establish its settled judicial-remedy background.
- **Suter, March 25, 1992:** Applied to the independent question whether Congress authorized the asserted action, without deciding section 1983 here.
- **Franklin, February 26, 1992:** Its appropriate-relief rule remains operative within a cognizable action and does not supply the missing authority to recover damages here.
- **Central Bank, April 19, 1994, and Musick, Peeler, June 1, 1993:** Their received securities action remains intact; its development is distinguished from creation of this Cable Act damages action.
- **Denver Area, June 28, 1996:** Its limited PEG judgment and fractured rationales neither establish a universal public forum nor create a statutory damages action; the separate 1996 permission retains its own unresolved constitutional questions.

## Law After Decision

The asserted implied section 531(e) damages route is unavailable. The substantive statutory restrictions remain, while any other relief depends on its actual independent authority. The decision neither validates the exclusion nor establishes a general immunity from suit, and it changes no received securities or other existing private action.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the lower courts. The asserted implied federal damages route cannot support relief; any remaining claim must satisfy its own authorization and procedural requirements. Supplemental jurisdiction and the reserved viewer questions remain for lawful consideration without a directed outcome.
