#### Reyes-Hernandez v. INS

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 95-3677; Supreme Court docket unassigned.
**Decided:** 1997-05-12.

For discretionary denial and the new-evidence vehicle, see Immigration — Waiver discretion and supplementary evidence, Reyes-Hernandez, May 12, 1997, this docket.

##### Section 242(g) does not withdraw review of these completed-adjudication claims

**Holding:** After its April 1, 1997 commencement, §242(g) reaches past, pending and future proceedings but withdraws review of the identified executive decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders, rather than every legal challenge arising from completed adjudication. These challenges to temporal eligibility, the legality of the completed waiver denial and treatment of a supplemental brief remain outside that withdrawal and may use the otherwise saved former §106 review route.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by all seven other Associates; eight rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the threshold result.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Corrected §309(c)(1) saves earlier proceedings subject to specific transitions: paragraph (2) allows election only before the evidentiary hearing begins, on thirty days' notice; paragraph (3) allows termination/reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. Neither condition exists after the completed 1995 adjudication. The October 1995 order is not an order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996 under paragraph (4). Section 306(c)(2) protects existing §106 judgments but confers no jurisdiction. No election, later qualifying order, general constitutional-review entitlement or alternative habeas basis is found.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Yang's April 1 commencement rule is applied and its reserved postcommencement scope resolved at this boundary; the admitted corrected §§306 and 309 retain their different timing and transition commands. Felker requires identifying the channel actually withdrawn or retained; hypothetical §2241 or §1651 remedies supply no jurisdiction here.

##### The two AEDPA restrictions do not attach these new consequences to the completed concession

**Holding:** On the uncontested premise that Reyes surrendered a colorable defense to deportability before enactment in order to pursue the then-available waiver-and-review path, §440(d) does not remove eligibility for that original waiver claim, and §440(a) does not eliminate ordinary review of its lawful denial, because neither clearly prescribes those new consequences for that completed choice. A genuine defense is necessary to this lost-option rationale, not a universal element of retroactivity analysis or an exemption for every preenactment conviction, pending case or later reopening request.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Parts II–IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would dismiss under §440(a) and supply no merits votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 440(f) addresses subsection (e), not (a) or (d). A lost colorable defense is necessary for this surrendered-option rationale, not a universal retroactivity element or subjective-reliance requirement. No particular defense is invented; the Government accepted the premise. Other temporal predicates, later reopening requests, constitutional invalidation and habeas remain open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf requires separate examination of enacted direction and the consequences of each provision; a jurisdictional label or mere pendency does not decide retroactivity. Felker's post-enactment authorization proceedings differ.

#### Hincapie-Nieto v. INS

##### The saved procedural route remains subject to AEDPA's separate review restriction

**Holding:** IIRIRA §309(c)(1), as corrected, preserves the former procedure for proceedings begun before April 1, 1997, subject to its exceptions; corrected §306(c)(1) nevertheless gives §242(g) reach across past, pending and future proceedings after that commencement. The permanent scheme does not govern wholesale merely because the Court acts after April 1.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Yang and Reyes-Hernandez are applied to the distinct transition provisions and §242(g)'s limited subject matter.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the result on a distinct future-review rationale.

Section 440(f) addresses subsection (e), not a saving rule for (a). Section 306(d)'s correction operates as if part of AEDPA and preserves the dual-offense branch's requirement that both predicates are otherwise covered regardless of commission dates; that branch does not decide this case. Section 440(d)'s eligibility withdrawal is not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf and Rivers supply provision-specific temporal and completed-consequence analysis. Reyes-Hernandez is distinguished because its protected lost-defense predicate is absent; its reservation of other completed-event predicates remains.

#### Salazar-Haro v. INS

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 96-3007.
**Decided:** June 16, 1997.

##### The saved immigration proceeding remains subject to AEDPA’s separate bar and the actual-judgment protection

**Holding:** The saved former procedure governs this old deportation proceeding, subject to applicable transition exceptions, while §242(g)'s cross-temporal reach does not itself withdraw review of the completed-adjudication claims presented here. That conclusion preserves no exemption from AEDPA §440(a), whose separate temporal operation must be decided. Section 306(c)(2) preserves an actual judicial judgment or order entered under former INA §106 (8 U.S.C. §1105a), applying §106 as amended by AEDPA §440, against an asserted operation of §306(c)(1) that would invalidate that judgment or order or require its reconsideration. The Third Circuit's September 13, 1996 dismissal falls within that protection, which neither independently confers jurisdiction nor reopens merits review nor bars ordinary appellate review of the judgment's correctness under applicable law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion, adopted by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only with the transition disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Corrected §309(c)(1) saves proceedings begun before April 1, 1997, subject to its exceptions. Paragraph (2) permits election of new procedures only before the evidentiary hearing begins, with notice at least thirty days before it; paragraph (3) permits termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. No qualifying election is established. Section 309(c)(4) requires a final exclusion or deportation order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; this earlier order does not qualify. Section 242(g) retains its special cross-temporal operation over commencing proceedings, adjudicating cases and executing orders, but does not itself bar these completed-adjudication claims.

**Material application:** The September 13, 1996 judicial dismissal applied former INA §106 as amended by AEDPA §440(a). Section 306(c)(2) protects that actual judgment from invalidation or required reconsideration attributable to §306(c)(1), without insulating its correctness from ordinary appellate review. Public Law 104-302 made §306(c)(1) subject to §309 with September 30 effect and left paragraph (2) unchanged.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Yang supplies commencement; Reyes-Hernandez and Hincapie-Nieto supply the corrected transition construction and the distinction between specified executive action and completed legal adjudication. Their savings do not nullify AEDPA’s independent restriction.

##### Section 440(a) withdraws this pending direct-review channel without a new disability attached to a protected completed event

**Holding:** AEDPA §440(a) applies to this qualifying final deportation order despite the January 17, 1996 petition because withdrawal of future ordinary appellate review attaches no new legal disability to a protected completed event established by this record. Filing, prior conviction, family hardship and an expectation of favorable review alone establish no such disability; the agency had already considered discretionary waiver under the former law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg: six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A surrendered colorable defense is one possible protected completed-event predicate, not a universal prerequisite. Section 440(f) concerns subsection (e), not a saving clause for subsection (a). The agency considered waiver under the former law; the separate operation of §440(d) eligibility restrictions is not decided. Pendency alone gives no blanket exemption, and a jurisdictional label gives no categorical answer.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the jurisdictional dismissal, unanimously as to that disposition, without adopting the Third Circuit’s hypothetical merits footnote. No waiver, reopening, new hearing, transfer, stay or execution direction follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf and Rivers require a provision-specific completed-event inquiry. Hincapie-Nieto controls the absence of a protected choice based only on filing and expectation; Reyes-Hernandez is distinguished on its actual surrendered-defense predicate.

##### The discretionary-balance record does not decide the availability of an unfiled collateral action

**Holding:** This record does not require preservation of ordinary appellate review for another weighing of discretionary equities, and a constitutional label or §1651 alone does not restore that jurisdiction. Affirmance neither approves eliminating every meaningful judicial forum for an actually presented legal or constitutional defect nor decides the availability, scope or custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other predicates of immigration review under §2241.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, adopted in full by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela additionally joins the conclusion that this record presents no distinct legal defect requiring discretionary reweighing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No custody status, collateral filing, adequate substitute or satisfaction of statutory predicates is presumed. The holding neither recognizes affirmative immigration §2241 jurisdiction nor declares every meaningful forum closed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Felker concerned an actual retained original channel, not an assumed immigration substitute. Flores preserves its custody and facial-review safeguards without deciding this collateral issue. The Japanese Immigrant Case supplies antecedent due process, not a right to judicial reweighing of every waiver denial. McCardle, Yerger and Heikkila retain their channel-specific significance. Section 1651 assists jurisdiction already present; it does not create it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Kolster v. INS, June 23, 1997, First Circuit No. 96-1194, resolves the retained §2241 route for substantial legal and constitutional objections when its actual jurisdictional and procedural predicates are satisfied, including a developed legal-eligibility objection. It does not authorize reweighing discretionary equities.

#### Kolster v. INS

**Docket or dockets:** First Circuit No. 96-1194; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### I. The saved proceeding remains subject to AEDPA's independent restriction

**Holding:** Corrected IIRIRA §§306(c)(1) and 309(c)(1) preserve the former review scheme for this proceeding begun before April 1, 1997, subject to their particular exceptions; §242(g) reaches old proceedings but does not itself withdraw review of this legal objection to a completed adjudication. Section 306(c)(2) protects an actual judicial judgment or order entered under former INA §106, applying that section as amended by AEDPA §440, against an operation of §306(c)(1) that would invalidate it or require reconsideration; that protection neither supplies jurisdiction, reopens merits review nor bars ordinary appellate review of the judgment's correctness.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with this transition disposition without joining its reasoning.

**Material application:** Section 309(c)(2) permits election only before the evidentiary hearing starts and with at least thirty days' advance notice; (c)(3) allows termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. No qualifying election is established. Section 309(c)(4) requires a final exclusion or deportation order more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; the January 30, 1996 order does not qualify. The October correction made §306(c)(1) subject to §309, retained §242(g)'s cross-temporal reach and left (c)(2) unchanged. The First Circuit's December 4, 1996 dismissal applied former §106 as amended by §440(a), bringing that actual judgment within (c)(2)'s protection from paragraph (1) disruption.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Yang supplies the commencement rule; Reyes-Hernandez, Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro distinguish executive decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases and execute orders from review of a completed adjudication. Salazar-Haro supplies the full protection for qualifying §106 judgments without barring ordinary correctness review.

##### II. Section 440(a) governs this ordinary petition

**Holding:** AEDPA §440(a) withdraws ordinary appellate review of Kolster's final deportation order based on the admitted drug and aggravated-felony grounds, independently supported by the conviction documents, notwithstanding his February 28, 1996 petition. On the established facts, application of the channel restriction imposes no supported new legal disability on his plea, concession or another protected completed event; this conclusion establishes neither a universal actual-reliance requirement nor a rule that pleas or eligibility for discretionary relief can never support nonretroactivity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support the dismissal on a different temporal ground.

**Material application:** Deportability was admitted and independently documented; no colorable defense surrendered is identified. Even Kolster's preferred domicile calculation left his September 1992 plea three years short of seven-year eligibility. Those facts establish no protected lost option without findings about subjective motives.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A jurisdictional label does not alone determine temporal reach. Section 440(f) times subsection (e), not (a); §440(d)'s substantive eligibility amendment remains undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf and Rivers supply the provision-specific temporal inquiry. Reyes-Hernandez's actual lost-defense premise is absent, without making surrendered defenses the only protected completed events. Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro reject filing and expectations alone as a basis for preserving the channel. Hughes Aircraft's new private enforcement exposure is distinguished.

##### III. Independent habeas jurisdiction survives for substantial legal and constitutional objections

**Holding:** Section 440(a)'s replacement of a particular INA review channel does not clearly repeal the independent jurisdiction enacted in 28 U.S.C. §2241. When custody, proper respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable predicates are satisfied, that jurisdiction permits consideration of a substantial legal or constitutional claim that executive custody or deportation lacks lawful authority, including a claim that an unlawful eligibility rule prevented consideration of discretionary relief, and permits effective relief if the claim warrants it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join this construction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The retained route must be capable of reaching the substantial authority objection and effective relief when warranted; detention-only review required to assume the disputed eligibility rule valid is insufficient to answer that objection. The Court does not determine Kolster's habeas predicates, make every statutory error constitutional, reopen discretionary weighing or factual review generally, or define all constitutional minima. A demonstrated failure of the retained route remains reserved; §1651 supplies no independent jurisdiction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Felker's distinction between a withdrawn review channel and retained statutory habeas is extended to §2241 in this immigration setting. McCardle and Yerger support channel-specific repeal analysis; Heikkila distinguishes ordinary immigration review from habeas. The legal-eligibility objection resolves the reservation left in Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro at this conditional scope; Flores's custody safeguards do not themselves answer review of deportation authority.

##### IV. The direct petition ends without a decision on eligibility

**Holding:** The First Circuit's dismissal of the direct petition is affirmed. The Court neither decides the lawful-domicile calculation nor orders a waiver, agency reconsideration, conversion or transfer of the petition, collateral relief, a stay or execution of deportation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Souter's opinion is joined by all nine Justices.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The ordinary petition remains dismissed, unanimously. The sentencing recommendation does not bind the immigration decisionmaker. Any collateral challenge must establish its own jurisdictional and procedural predicates before legal eligibility or requested relief is decided.

### IIRIRA commencement and preserved review channels

**Governing question:** Which enacted commencement and transition provisions select the immigration review regime.

**Current rule:** IIRIRA §309(a) generally commences Title III-A and its amendments on April 1, 1997, with express exceptions for §309 and §§303(b)(2), 306(c), 308(d)(2)(D) and 308(d)(5). If the evidentiary hearing under former §§236 or 242 and 242B had not begun by that date, §309(c)(2) permits the Attorney General to elect amended chapter 4 procedure, giving notice at least thirty days before the evidentiary hearing; the earlier §235 or §242(a) hearing notice then counts as a §239 notice to confer immigration-judge jurisdiction. Its paragraph (E) lists discretionary decisions under §§212(c), 212(h), 212(i), 244 and 245; permanent §242(a)(2)(B)(i) instead lists §§212(h), 212(i), 240A, 240B and 245, and clause (ii) separately addresses other statutorily discretionary authority except §208(a) grants. ### Immigration transition, completed adjudication and AEDPA review limits

**Governing question:** Ordinary judicial review and §212(c) eligibility during the AEDPA/IIRIRA transition.

**Current rule:** From April 1, 1997, corrected §306(c)(1) gives §242(g) cross-temporal reach. Subject to review provided in §242, and notwithstanding other law, the withdrawal concerns claims arising from the Attorney General’s identified decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders. It does not automatically encompass every legal challenge to a completed adjudication. Former §106 review is not textually provided in new §242; §309 preserves it only for claims outside §242(g)’s specified withdrawal. AEDPA §440(a) independently withdraws the listed criminal-alien ordinary appellate channel even for pending petitions, unless application attaches a supported new legal disability to a protected completed event. Filing, conviction, hardship and review expectations alone do not preserve that channel. Where an alien surrendered a colorable deportability defense before enactment to use the then-available waiver-and-review route, neither §440(d) nor §440(a) clearly withdraws original-claim waiver eligibility or ordinary review on account of that completed choice. That surrendered defense is one protected-event route, not a universal reliance requirement; pleas and eligibility interests are not categorically excluded. Section 440(a) does not clearly repeal independent §2241 jurisdiction. Subject to custody, proper respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable predicates, §2241 permits substantial legal or constitutional objections to executive authority to detain or deport, including an unlawful eligibility rule foreclosing discretionary consideration, and effective relief if warranted. Mere reweighing of discretionary equities is distinct, and §1651 supplies no independent jurisdiction.

**Exceptions and limits:** An actual surrendered defense is necessary to the Reyes-Hernandez lost-option rationale, not to every possible retroactivity claim. #### United States v. Armstrong, 517 U.S. 456

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-157.
**Decided:** May 13, 1996.

##### Rule 16 authority for selective-prosecution discovery

**Holding:** Rule 16(a)(1)(C)'s defense-preparation materiality provision does not authorize discovery to prepare this selective-prosecution challenge to the decision to prosecute. The holding concerns that claim alone, rather than all affirmative defenses unrelated to guilt or every possible source of constitutionally warranted inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; six. Souter and Ginsburg join only the stated selective-prosecution scope. Breyer supplies a different source analysis; Stone-Zsela does not join Part I. Stevens's separate agreement that the Rule supplies no entitlement is not a join in the Court's reasoning.

**Material application:** The other disjunctive triggers—Government intent to use the material in its evidence in chief and acquisition from or ownership by the defendant—are not established. Protective and sanction powers regulate an existing duty rather than create it. No established entitlement under Rule 17(c), *Brady*, or compulsory process supplies the missing source on this showing. The Court makes no waiver finding and does not attribute its construction to the Government or lower courts.

##### Preliminary showing of discriminatory selection

**Holding:** Discovery on this indirect racial-selection theory requires some credible evidence tending to show discriminatory effect and purpose, including a reliable basis to infer that relevantly similarly situated persons of another race eligible for federal prosecution could have been prosecuted but were not. This is less than a prima facie merits case. Grounded statistics, circumstantial comparisons, and a specifically justified alternative evidentiary basis when comparator names cannot reasonably be obtained may establish the inference; neither perfect identity, universal naming, nor a fixed checklist is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight. The shared rule assumes discovery may be available on an appropriate showing without establishing another source of authority.

**Material application:** The selected sample of 24 Black federal defendants supplies a numerator, not an eligible charging population or sufficiently comparable offenders passed over. State-practice experience and relayed treatment observations, considered cumulatively with the developed Government response, fail to connect the comparison to relevant quantities, firearms, evidence, and criminal histories. Government proffers identify gaps without establishing neutrality or disproving the sample's bounded count. Insufficiency does not prove comparators nonexistent. Access difficulty is real; discovery may complete a grounded inference but cannot create its missing connection solely from hope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The request fails on discriminatory effect; no independent purpose deficiency or ultimate discrimination finding is decided. Whether direct discriminatory admissions dispense with similarly situated comparison remains open. No completed-merits burden, categorical direct-evidence rule, universally available comparator database, guaranteed state-record access, or enforceable subpoena access is presumed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Wayte* supplies effect-and-purpose limits but reserved discovery. *Wade* supplies meaningful preliminary screening by analogy from sentencing, not an already controlling charging rule. *Kimberlin* preserves statistical and circumstantial proof alongside lawful limits; *Klinger* ties similarity to the challenged treatment without requiring identity. *McCleskey* requires connection to the challenged decision without categorically excluding statistics or requiring merits proof for discovery. *Yick Wo*'s supported-pattern inference remains available; this record lacks its comparison connection.

##### Consequences for the order, dependent dismissal, and remand

**Holding:** The unsupported discovery order and dismissal for refusing it cannot stand. The case returns for ordinary pretrial proceedings without a directed renewed inquiry or automatic discovery; a later lawful, adequately supported request remains possible under its own source, threshold, and protective conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Setting aside the existing order and dismissal has the eight-Justice Part II coalition. Part III's formal reversal and ordinary remand has O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven. Stone-Zsela would expressly vacate and direct conditional staged inquiry; Stevens would affirm. Those positions do not alter the controlling mandate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit and remand, 7–2; the setting-aside component is 8–1. No new evidence-gathering opportunity, guilt determination, conviction, sentence, state-prosecution order, acquittal, or retrial after acquittal is directed. The Court assigns no dismissal-prejudice label.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Appropriateness of the sanction on the assumption of a valid discovery order and the remedy for proved selective prosecution are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Williams*'s grand-jury duty, prejudice requirements, and without-prejudice remedy do not automatically govern this distinct sanction. *Carlisle* preserves rule-consistent supervision without converting Rule 16 nonauthorization into universal immunity from constitutional review.

#### Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-5841.
**Decided:** June 10, 1996.

##### Investigative motive and customary enforcement in an ordinary traffic stop

**Holding:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause of a traffic violation does not become unreasonable under the Fourth Amendment because officers also investigate an unrelated offense. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce the violation absent that other aim. Independent constitutional limits on manner, scope, and duration remain; detention or search materially exceeding what the traffic violation reasonably justifies requires independent grounds.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous, including the application and qualifications.

**Material application:** The accepted observations of waiting unusually long at a stop sign, failing to signal, and unreasonable speed supply traffic probable cause. Petitioners' proposed objective “would-have” test receives the same rejection as a subjective-motive prerequisite: variable local priorities, uniforms, assignments, markings, and enforcement customs do not change constitutional authority for the known violation. A separate balance of ordinary traffic-safety gains against plainclothes-intervention anxiety supplies no additional requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Inventory and administrative-inspection authority retain their genuine-purpose and standardized or other source-specific constraints; motive is not irrelevant to every constitutional question. General Order 303.1 compliance, its immediate-safety-threat exception for otherwise unauthorized plainclothes or unmarked enforcement, and its extreme-circumstances oral-warning exception are not adjudicated. Neither speed nor missing execution details establish an exception or constitutional execution finding. The later search is not independently presented.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Scott* applies objective assessment of justified action; *Robinson*, *Gustafson*, and *Villamonte-Marquez* retain their distinct lawful-arrest, search, and customs-inspection predicates without creating new power. *Prouse* distinguishes suspicionless discretionary stops; *Martinez-Fuerte* and *Brignoni-Ponce* do not require a further balance for this ordinary probable-cause stop. *Bertine* and *Wells* preserve inventory constraints and forbid investigatory ruses; *Burger* preserves purpose-limited administrative inspection. *Abel* and *Bannister*'s regular-procedure and no-pretext observations do not establish the proposed customary-enforcement condition. *Wilson* preserves reasonable-execution review with its supported danger, futility, escape, and evidence-destruction qualifications, leaving suspicion quantum and automatic suppression open. *Garner*, *Welsh*, and *Winston* retain their distinct deadly-force, home-entry, and bodily-intrusion limits.

##### Review of the accepted predicate and separation of discriminatory selection

**Holding:** The accepted traffic observations permit resolution under *Ornelas*: historical facts, credibility, and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review, with genuine due weight to grounded contextual inferences; the legal standard and ultimate constitutional sufficiency properly at issue receive independent judgment. No new factual inquiry is required to decide this legal-rule challenge.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** Due weight means substantive consideration of supported trial and local-officer context, not a numerical weight, officer-credibility presumption, or intermediate standard insulating constitutional conclusions. Direct review imports no habeas presumption or clear-and-convincing burden and supplies no supposed antecedent drug probable cause.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purposeful racial selection remains independently forbidden by equal protection; traffic probable cause does not establish nondiscrimination. No discrimination finding, police-stop discovery entitlement, immunity rule, suppression consequence, or damages remedy is decided, and assumed adequacy of another remedy is not a ground for judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of suppression on the initial-stop ground, 9–0. The completed vacatur of lesser-included §841 convictions and §860 resentencing, including nonreimposition of Whren's fine, remain undisturbed. Later sentencing appeals are not resolved. No new trial, hearing, release, sentencing proceeding, or determination of every admissibility ground follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Ornelas*'s review allocation is applied unchanged. *Kimberlin* permits circumstantial proof when motive is an underlying element without adding one here. *Armstrong* remains confined to federal charging and discovery; its requirements and unresolved discrimination remedy do not become police-stop rules.

#### Dawson v. Delaware, 503 U.S. 159 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6704.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### When protected association may aggravate a capital sentence

**Holding:** The State may not use protected association to aggravate a capital sentence when the evidence establishes only abstract beliefs and supplies no supported connection to the crime, future danger, another legitimate sentencing issue, or the mitigation actually offered. The stipulated association evidence here lacks that connection and its admission violates the First and Fourteenth Amendments.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of White’s opinion; Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter join White at this level of generality.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal bar excludes association evidence tied to witness bias, demonstrated racial motive, future danger, another legitimate sentencing issue, or actual mitigation. A white victim does not categorically exclude racial motive in another case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining the death sentence is vacated and remanded, 8–1. Delaware may consider its harmless-error contention consistently with the constitutional relevance ruling; the Court neither finds harmlessness nor adopts a mandatory Chapman/new-sentencing sequence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- NAACP v. Alabama, 357 U.S. 449 — applied: freedom of association protects unpopular affiliations.
- Bates v. Little Rock, 361 U.S. 516 — applied: protected association cannot itself justify adverse governmental treatment.
- United States v. Abel, 469 U.S. 45 — distinguished on the facts: association established witness bias there; relevant association remains admissible.
- Barclay v. Florida, 463 U.S. 939 — distinguished: demonstrated racial motivation connected the association to the crime.
- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 — applied: protected beliefs cannot themselves supply an aggravating circumstance.
- Payne v. Tennessee, 501 U.S. 808 — applied with its relevance limit: rebuttal of mitigation does not make unrelated belief probative.
- Schware v. Board of Bar Examiners, 353 U.S. 232 — applied by analogy: an affiliation without a rational connection to the relevant character inquiry is insufficient.

##### Whether harmless-error review is available and satisfied for this association-evidence error

**Holding:** The state judgment sustaining this sentence cannot stand on the constitutional reasoning it adopted. On remand the state court may consider Delaware’s harmless-error contention; this Court decides neither whether harmless-error analysis is available for this violation nor whether the error was harmless.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of White’s opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the remand’s reserved scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither availability nor satisfaction of harmless-error review and does not command new sentencing automatically. The convictions remain intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining the death sentence is vacated and remanded, 8–1. Delaware may consider its harmless-error contention consistently with the constitutional relevance ruling; the Court neither finds harmlessness nor adopts a mandatory Chapman/new-sentencing sequence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — preserved: its constitutional harmless-error burden is not relaxed; applicability and satisfaction in this case remain undecided.

#### R. A. V. v. City of St. Paul, 505 U.S. 377 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-7675.
**Decided:** June 22, 1992.

##### Whether the authoritative state construction cures the ordinance's substantial protected coverage

**Holding:** A criminal prohibition is facially invalid when, even under its authoritative state-law construction, it reaches substantial protected expression relative to its legitimate sweep and cannot be cured by permissible limiting construction or partial invalidation. St. Paul's injury-and-resentment coverage extends beyond personally abusive provocation of an immediate breach of the peace and is therefore substantially overbroad.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this rule and application. Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support the same judgment on a distinct content-discrimination ground. Those paths are not combined into a broader nine-Justice rule, and no Marks synthesis enlarges the actual majority holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated; the decision creates no general rule governing distinctions wholly within unprotected speech categories.

**Material application:** Religious criticism and hostile debate about race or gender can cause resentment without personally provoking immediate violence. The authoritative state construction binds on state law, but its repeated injury branch still reaches protected speech; calling it fighting words, or adding an incitement branch, does not cure that coverage. The serious cross-burning allegation does not validate the ordinance's ordinary unconstitutional applications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The broader content-discrimination question within genuinely unprotected categories, vagueness, guilt on count one, threat mental state, sentencing enhancements and antidiscrimination laws not before the Court remain undecided. Independently punishable conduct is not immunized. The separate writings' proposed content-selection rule and exceptions remain noncontrolling.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Minnesota Supreme Court and remand for dismissal of count two under the facially invalid ordinance. Count one under the separate racially motivated assault provision remains unadjudicated; independently valid charges remain available. The remand creates no new offense through construction or severance.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chaplinsky v. New Hampshire, 315 U.S. 568, retains its narrow fighting-words scope rather than a general emotional-injury category. Cohen v. California, 403 U.S. 15, distinguishes offensive ideas from personally directed fighting words. Gooding v. Wilson, 405 U.S. 518, and Lewis v. City of New Orleans, 415 U.S. 130, require an authoritative construction actually to remove protected coverage. Broadrick v. Oklahoma, 413 U.S. 601, supplies real and substantial overbreadth and prior consideration of permissible narrowing. Brandenburg v. Ohio, 395 U.S. 444, retains advocacy directed and likely to produce imminent lawless action as the incitement boundary; that branch does not cure the separate injury coverage. New York v. Ferber, 458 U.S. 747, and Brockett v. Spokane Arcades, Inc., 472 U.S. 491, supply narrowing and partial-invalidation principles without authorizing judicial rewriting here. Simon & Schuster, December 10, 1991, Forsyth County, June 19, 1992, and Burson, May 26, 1992, retain their respective protected-speech, licensing and fractured electioneering holdings without resolving the reserved within-category question.

### Discovery for an indirect racial selective-prosecution claim

**Governing question:** Discovery of federal charging selection and the relevant Rule 16 scope.

**Current rule:** Rule 16(a)(1)(C)’s material-preparation-of-the-defense branch does not authorize discovery to prepare a selective-prosecution challenge to the decision to prosecute. Before obtaining discovery on an indirect racial-selection theory through any properly established authority, the defendant must offer some credible evidence tending to show discriminatory effect and purpose, including a reliable inference that persons of another race relevantly similarly situated for federal prosecution could have been prosecuted but were not. This is less than a prima facie merits case. Relevant similarity is not identity: grounded statistics, circumstantial comparison and a specifically justified alternative when comparator names cannot reasonably be obtained may support the inference. A selected numerator alone, demographic generalization or hope that discovery creates the missing link does not. A protective order or reduced request scope cannot supply the threshold or original authority.

**Authority by component:**

- Confined Rule 16 nonauthorization: I. Rule 16 does not authorize discovery to prepare a selective-prosecution claim, 1996-05-13; O’Connor Part I with Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (six).
- Preliminary effect/purpose showing and comparator flexibility: II. The existing showing does not support discovery of discriminatory federal selection, same date; O’Connor Part II with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Armstrong, 1996-05-13.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule neither decides every guilt-unrelated affirmative defense nor supplies an alternative discovery source. Rule 16’s intended-use and defendant-origin triggers remain separate; aggregate data, criteria and internal deliberations are not automatically identical categories. No universal naming requirement, fixed comparator checklist or transfer to police-stop discovery follows. Whether direct admissions of discriminatory purpose dispense with comparator proof remains open.

### Purposefully arbitrary group-based selective enforcement

**Governing question:** Whether probable cause defeats an equal-protection claim of deliberately arbitrary selection.

**Current rule:** Probable cause does not defeat a claim that officers deliberately selected an otherwise chargeable person solely to impose arbitrary group hostility while intentionally leaving an actually comparable person unenforced on that basis. The claimant must establish discriminatory purpose and effect, relevant comparison, personal participation and attributable injury; a supported legitimate enforcement distinction defeats the asserted arbitrary comparison.

**Authority by component:**

- Holding 3: narrow selective-enforcement rule: City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-01.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-01.

**Exceptions and limits:** No orientation-based scrutiny tier or general defense to otherwise lawful prosecution is created.
