## Standards and Tests selection: Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness

### Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness

**Governing question:** What instructions govern age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness?

**Current rule:** ADEA disparate treatment requires age actually to motivate and have a determinative influence on the employment decision. Service-based pension interference alone does not establish it; deliberate use of pension status as an age proxy or mixed age and pension motivation may. Age-triggered vesting and disparate impact remain unresolved. A disparate-treatment violation is willful if the employer knew or recklessly disregarded whether conduct violated the Act, for individual decisions as well as formal policies. Once shown, liquidated damages require no additional outrageousness, direct evidence or predominant-age motive. A good-faith nonreckless mistake about a statutory permission is not willful. Within the assumed McDonnell Douglas framework, the initial showing must support an inference of age discrimination; replacement by someone at least forty does not defeat it. A substantially younger replacement may support that inference, whereas an insignificant difference standing alone does not. No numerical threshold or indispensable replacement element is imposed when other evidence supplies the inference. Employer production of a lawful reason remains distinct from the employee’s ultimate persuasion burden; rejection of the explanation does not compel liability.

**Authority by component:**

- Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation: Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins — Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation, 1993-04-20. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies: Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins — Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies, 1993-04-20. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- O’Connor — Parts I–II, replacement status and relative-age inference, 1996-04-01; all nine support protected-group nonexclusion, the positive relative-age proposition and retained burdens; the eight Associates alone adopt insignificant-gap-alone insufficiency.

**Present operation effective:** O’Connor, April 1, 1996, for the replacement inference; Hazen Paper’s causation and willfulness rules survive.

**Holdings navigation:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, 507 U.S. 604 (1993); O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., 517 U.S. 308.

**Open question:** Wholesale application of McDonnell Douglas to every ADEA claim, alternative reduction-in-force formulations, particular small-gap records and the force of additional evidence remain unresolved.

## Standards and Tests selection: Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

### Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

**Governing question:** When discriminatory harassment alters employment conditions under Title VII.

**Current rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considering all circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and the employee actually perceives as abusive. Serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than merely offensive utterances, and unreasonable interference with work; none is indispensable. Psychological effects may inform subjective abuse. Mere offense without the objective and subjective predicates remains insufficient. The same-sex relationship of harasser and claimant does not exclude Title VII coverage. The claimant must show that the challenged conditions were imposed because of sex through competent direct or circumstantial evidence, without an invariant requirement of sexual desire, a particular orientation or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual language, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish that causal connection. An all-male workforce is part of the context rather than categorical immunity.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective and subjective requirements, totality and absence of an injury floor: Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc — Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury, 1993-11-09. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

- Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc. — Same-sex harassment remains subject to Title VII, 1997-03-19; Thomas for all nine supplies same-sex coverage
- Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc. — Sexual desire is possible evidence, not a necessary element, 1997-03-19; Thomas for all nine supplies evidentiary possibilities and the independent because-of-sex requirement

**Present operation effective:** Oncale, 1997-03-19, for same-sex coverage and causation boundaries; Harris's environment threshold retains 1993-11-09 authority.

**Open question:** A sex-specific reasonable-person formulation and constructive discharge remain unresolved by these holdings. Employer attribution is a separate inquiry governed where applicable by Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment.

**Holdings navigation:** Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury. Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc. (1997-03-19) — Labor and Employment / Hostile work environments.

## Public authority excerpt: O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., 517 U.S. 308

Source: `state/holdings/labor-and-employment.md`.

#### O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., 517 U.S. 308

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-354.
**Decided:** April 1, 1996.

##### Replacement within the ADEA's protected age group does not defeat the initial inference

**Holding:** An ADEA plaintiff need not show replacement by someone under forty to establish the initial inference of age discrimination. A replacement's membership in the protected group does not itself defeat that inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes McDonnell Douglas applies for this question and takes the case as a non-reduction-in-force matter, without settling every ADEA framework or alternative prima facie formulation. Claimant coverage at forty and prohibited age motivation remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hazen Paper supplies the actual-age-motivation inquiry; McDonnell Douglas, Furnco and Teamsters preserve the initial showing's evidentiary function without an under-forty replacement requirement.

##### Relative-age evidence must support an inference of age discrimination

**Holding:** Under the assumed framework, prima facie evidence and each required element must have a logical connection to an inference of age discrimination. A substantially younger replacement may support that inference even if at least forty; an insignificant difference, standing alone, does not supply it.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II has all eight Associates' complete joins. Stone-Zsela joins the inference requirement, positive relative-age proposition, unchanged burdens and reservations, but not the categorical insignificant-gap-alone proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No numerical minimum is prescribed. A substantially younger replacement is not indispensable when other evidence supplies the inference or no replacement sensibly can be identified. Every younger replacement does not suffice; materially different circumstances may defeat the comparison. The sixteen-year gap here establishes neither all initial elements, satisfactory performance nor actual motive. Additional age-related evidence and specific small-gap applications remain open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Burdine and Aikens preserve employer production and the plaintiff's ultimate persuasion burden. Hicks permits circumstantial proof without a mandatory additional category of evidence but does not compel liability merely from a prima facie case and disbelief of produced lawful reasons.

##### Reconsideration under the corrected rule leaves genuinely sufficient alternative grounds available

**Holding:** The reviewed ADEA merits affirmance must be reconsidered under the corrected replacement rule and ordinary proof and summary-judgment standards. The employer may defend the existing judgment on an appropriate alternative ground without a cross-petition, but intertwined lower performance and comparison analysis does not make this legal error immaterial on the disputed timely record.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand the ADEA merits affirmance. A genuinely sufficient remaining ground may still support summary judgment. The separate discovery and Rule 59/60 rulings remain intact; Dennis's postjudgment affidavit remains outside the timely record. No trial, hearing, liability, willfulness, damages, backpay or reinstatement is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Anderson and Celotex require a genuine material dispute and favorable reasonable inferences for the nonmovant, without accepting speculation or making credibility findings. McKennon distinguishes correction of a categorical ADEA bar from proof of liability and lawful relief; its after-acquired-evidence remedy is not extended.

## Public authority excerpt: Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.

Source: `state/holdings/labor-and-employment.md`.

#### Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 95-30510; 83 F.3d 118 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether Title VII excludes harassment by a person of the employee's own sex

**Holding:** Title VII's protection against discriminatory employment conditions includes same-sex harassment. The employee must still plead and ultimately prove discrimination because of sex and every other element of the asserted claim; common sex supplies neither immunity nor liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Meritor* supplies coverage of discriminatory abusive conditions, extended here to same-sex actors without eliminating any statutory element.

##### Whether sexual desire or an actual opposite-sex comparator is necessary to establish discrimination

**Holding:** Competent direct or circumstantial evidence can establish discrimination because of sex without proof of desire, a particular sexual orientation, or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual content, contact, or the gravity of conduct does not alone conclusively establish that causal element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** Sex-directed hostility, selective treatment, discriminatory statements, threats, and surrounding circumstances may support the inference. These examples create neither exclusive proof channels nor a compulsory checklist.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds no participant's sexual orientation or motive and decides no independent sexual-orientation coverage question. Personal hostility unrelated to sex is outside this cause of action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hicks* preserves ultimate persuasion and permissible circumstantial inference; it does not impose a compulsory desire showing or comparator. *Brown v. Polk County* and *Nabozny* retain their distinct statutory and constitutional scopes.

##### Whether a single-sex workplace changes the hostile-environment standard

**Holding:** Sex-based conduct must be severe or pervasive enough to produce an objectively hostile or abusive workplace in context, and the employee must actually perceive it as abusive. Workforce composition informs context but creates no exemption; psychiatric injury and measurable productivity loss are unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** Workplace custom cannot automatically recast forced physical humiliation or rape threats as harmless camaraderie. The allegations remain unproved, and their gravity does not establish unwelcome treatment, sex causation, or attribution.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No single circumstance, diagnosis, treatment, medical proof, or universal reasonable-woman formulation is required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Harris* supplies the contextual severe-or-pervasive inquiry and its separate objective and subjective requirements, without an economic or psychiatric injury prerequisite.

##### Which attribution and remedial issues survive removal of the categorical bar

**Holding:** Employer attribution, if reached, requires application of *Andrade*: actual delegated supervisory authority must materially aid creation or maintenance of the unlawful environment. A supervisory title alone does not establish that connection, and lack of higher-management notice alone does not defeat this agency route; negligence and a preserved quid-pro-quo theory remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agency route does not invariably require a completed employment action or economic loss. A quid-pro-quo claim separately requires a supported employment benefit or detriment conditioned on sexual participation. No strict employer liability, new affirmative defense, individual-coworker rule, or finding about Lyons's statutory status is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical ground as to affected Sundowner and Lyons claims and remand under ordinary procedure. Pippen and Johnson's separate not-an-employer dismissal remains outside review, without endorsement of a new immunity rule. Liability, factual sufficiency, constructive discharge, discovery, trial entitlement, and available relief for the 1991 conduct remain undecided; properly presented independent defenses survive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Andrade* supplies the authority-aided agency rule and preserves negligence as another route. The lower-court *Garcia* same-sex exclusion cannot govern after this decision.

## Public authority excerpt: O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., No. 95-354

Source: `terms/OT1995/output/OT_1995CHUNK5.md`.

### O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., No. 95-354

Merits decision, April 1, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

**O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp.**, No. 95-354; 517 U.S. 308. October Term 1995. Argued February 27, 1996; decided April 1, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, 56 F.3d 542.

The question is whether replacement by a person within the ADEA's protected class precludes a prima facie claim of age discrimination. O'Connor was 56; Finnell, treated below as his effective replacement, was 40. The Fourth Circuit affirmed summary judgment under a formulation requiring replacement outside the protected group and also gave performance, reduction-in-force and ordinary-proof grounds. This review addresses the protected-class replacement condition; it does not separately review discovery or postjudgment relief.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0, as to the reviewed ADEA merits affirmance.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The discovery and Rule 59/60 postjudgment rulings remain undisturbed. The remand directs no trial, evidentiary hearing, affidavit admission, liability finding or particular relief. A sufficient remaining ground may support summary judgment after consideration under the corrected rule and ordinary evidentiary standards.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III | O'Connor | Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Reversal of the reviewed merits affirmance and bounded remand | Entire opinion. |
| Partial join in the Court opinion | — | Stone-Zsela | Same judgment and remedy | Parts I and III; Part II except its categorical proposition that an insignificant replacement-age difference, standing alone, does not support the necessary inference. His assent preserves that a minor difference or materially different circumstances may fail to support an inference. |

The general insignificance-alone proposition has eight direct votes. The other holdings and bounded judgment have nine at their stated scope. No separate opinion is filed, and no fractured-opinion synthesis is needed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Replacement within the protected group does not preclude the claim

**Holding:** An ADEA plaintiff need not show replacement by a person under 40 to establish the initial inference of age discrimination. The replacement's membership in the protected group does not itself defeat that inference.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 631(a) identifies the individuals protected by the Act: those at least 40. Section 623(a)(1) identifies the prohibited reason for adverse treatment: the individual's age. The statute protects a 56-year-old against discrimination because of age even when the worker replacing him is also protected. Crossing the age-40 boundary has no independent significance that makes it a necessary element of the initial inference.

Hazen Paper requires attention to age as the actual cause, rather than an analytically distinct characteristic. The prima facie framework must serve that causal inquiry. An administratively simple cutoff is not a sound evidentiary screen when it disqualifies a 40-year-old replacement but accepts an otherwise comparable 39-year-old. The availability of ordinary direct or circumstantial proof does not justify an irrelevant restriction on the indirect route.

The parties proceed under McDonnell Douglas, and the Court assumes its applicability here without deciding its wholesale application to every ADEA claim. Review likewise takes the case as a non-reduction-in-force matter; the Court does not settle every alternative prima facie formulation. Finnell was exactly 40, so he was protected, but that fact cannot defeat O'Connor's claim.

##### II. Relative age must support an inference of the prohibited discrimination

**Holding:** Within the assumed framework, the prima facie evidence must adequately support an inference of age discrimination, and each required element must have a logical connection to that inquiry. A substantially younger replacement may support the inference even if at least 40; an insignificant age difference, standing alone, does not supply it.

**Authority:** Part II has the eight Associates' complete joins. Stone-Zsela joins the inference requirement, positive relative-age proposition, unchanged burdens and reservations, but not the categorical insignificant-gap-alone proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** McDonnell Douglas and Teamsters give the initial showing an evidentiary function: facts that support the inference call for an explanation. The replacement's relative age serves that function more reliably than membership in the protected group. An insignificant difference alone does not supply the required inference. This is not a ten-year threshold or any other numerical rule; nor does it resolve the force of an entire record containing additional age-related evidence.

A substantially younger replacement is not indispensable where other evidence supplies the inference or no replacement can sensibly be identified. Conversely, neither every younger replacement nor protected status, qualification, discharge and continued need for the job necessarily suffices without circumstances supporting an age inference. Material differences in the circumstances may weaken or defeat the suggested comparison. The sixteen-year difference here cannot be excluded because Finnell was 40, but it does not establish every initial element, satisfactory performance or actual motive.

Burdine keeps employer production distinct from employee persuasion. The employer remains free to articulate a lawful reason; the plaintiff retains the ultimate burden of proving that age caused the action. Hicks permits circumstantial proof without a special additional category of direct or independent evidence, but, after nondiscriminatory reasons are produced, rejection of those reasons does not compel liability. The Court decides neither pretext nor ultimate discrimination here.

##### III. Corrected consideration is required; the remaining grounds and proof are not finally resolved

**Holding:** The reviewed ADEA merits affirmance is reversed, and the lower courts must reconsider the claim under the corrected replacement rule and ordinary proof and summary-judgment standards. This disposition neither invalidates every remaining ground nor requires trial or recovery; it leaves the unreviewed discovery and postjudgment rulings intact.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Respondent may defend the existing judgment on an appropriate alternative ground. A missing cross-petition does not itself bar its performance and ordinary-proof arguments. But the separately stated grounds do not make the replacement error immaterial: the lower court's performance and comparison analysis also informed its ordinary-evidence conclusion, while the timely record contains competing accounts of the territorial reduction, a contemporaneous volume memorandum, comparative material and disputed age remarks.

The earlier review and bonus do not conclusively answer later performance concerns, and the larger-district explanation remains available. Those considerations also do not authorize this Court to choose among disputed accounts. Anderson and Celotex require a genuine material dispute and favorable reasonable inferences for the nonmovant, not acceptance of speculation or judicial credibility findings. Reconsideration below permits those standards to operate with the corrected inference rule.

As McKennon demonstrates, reversal of an ADEA categorical bar does not establish liability or relief. A genuinely sufficient remaining ground may still support summary judgment. The Court does not adjudicate the alternative reduction-in-force formulation, find all timely evidence sufficient, or reopen the diligence and likely-effect rulings concerning Dennis's later affidavit. That affidavit remains outside the timely record. No new discovery, compulsory hearing, willfulness finding, damages, backpay or reinstatement is ordered.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green, 411 U.S. 792; Furnco Construction Corp. v. Waters, 438 U.S. 567; International Brotherhood of Teamsters v. United States, 431 U.S. 324:** Preserve the prima facie showing's function of supporting an inference; their factual formulations do not impose an under-40 replacement condition. General ADEA applicability remains assumed for this question.
- **Texas Department of Community Affairs v. Burdine, 450 U.S. 248, and Postal Service Board of Governors v. Aikens, 460 U.S. 711:** Preserve the distinction between an initial inference, employer production and ultimate employee persuasion concerning actual discrimination.
- **Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, 507 U.S. 604:** Applies its actual-age-motivation principle without requiring proof of the entire ultimate case at the initial stage. Its remaining causation and willfulness rules are unchanged.
- **St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks, 509 U.S. 502:** Preserves its rejection of compulsory liability after employer explanations are produced and disbelieved, and its rejection of a mandatory additional category of proof. No new persuasion allocation follows.
- **McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co., 513 U.S. 352:** Applies its separation of correction of an ADEA legal bar from proof of liability and lawful relief; its distinct after-acquired-evidence remedy is not extended here.
- **Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, and Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317:** Preserve ordinary summary-judgment requirements and constrain further consideration without deciding the present evidence's ultimate sufficiency.

#### Law After Decision

The replacement's protected status is no longer a permissible categorical condition of the assumed ADEA prima facie formulation. Relative age must be assessed for its connection to prohibited discrimination; a substantially younger replacement may support the inference, while an insignificant difference alone is insufficient under the eight-Justice general rule. No numerical minimum or universally indispensable replacement element is created.

The plaintiff's ultimate persuasion obligation and the employer's opportunity to produce a lawful explanation remain. Correction of the age-40 condition guarantees neither trial nor recovery and does not reopen distinct unreviewed rulings. Specific small-gap applications, the effect of additional evidence and alternative formulations remain unresolved within the stated limits.

#### Record and Statutory Context

The lower opinions and appendix, including 56 F.3d 542, distinguish the replacement condition, alternative merits grounds, discovery ruling and postjudgment affidavit ruling. Disputed accounts remain disputed. Sections 631(a) and 623(a)(1) supply the coverage and prohibited-reason rules. Review makes no complete new determination of the district evidentiary record and does not admit Dennis's affidavit.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Fourth Circuit for proceedings consistent with the corrected rule. The lower courts must consider the properly remaining merits grounds under ordinary evidentiary standards; the Court prescribes no trial or further factfinding procedure. The discovery and postjudgment dispositions remain intact, and any later relief requires its own liability and remedial predicates.

**End of entry: O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., Merits decision, April 1, 1996.**

## Public authority excerpt: Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc., Fifth Circuit No. 95-30510

### Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc., Fifth Circuit No. 95-30510

Merits hearing and decision, March 19, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

The Fifth Circuit decision is reported at 83 F.3d 118. On review of the Fifth Circuit's affirmance of dismissal on a categorical same-sex-harassment ground.

The questions are whether Title VII excludes harassment when offender and employee share a sex, whether sexual desire is necessary, and whether an all-male workplace precludes actionable sex discrimination.

#### Judgment

**Reverse in relevant part and remand, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The categorical ground is rejected for the affected claims against Sundowner and Lyons. The independently grounded dismissal of Pippen and Johnson as not statutory employers remains undisturbed outside the grant; the Court adopts no new rule of individual immunity. Lyons's statutory status remains unresolved. No final liability, trial entitlement or award follows.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The unanimous opinion controls every holding and qualification below.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Same-sex harassment remains subject to Title VII

**Rule:** Title VII does not exclude discriminatory harassment merely because the offender and employee share a sex. The claimant must plead and ultimately prove that the challenged employment conditions were imposed because of sex and must satisfy the remaining requirements of the asserted claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Meritor establishes that abusive working conditions can constitute statutory discrimination. The enacted protection concerns the individual employee's treatment, not an unstated requirement that employee and offender belong to different sex categories. A categorical exclusion therefore cannot support this dismissal. Rejecting that exclusion does not prove any allegation, create liability for all objectionable conduct, or adopt a free-standing dignity cause of action. The independent statutory-employer ruling outside the grant remains untouched.

##### Sexual desire is possible evidence, not a necessary element

**Rule:** Discrimination because of sex may be established through competent direct or circumstantial evidence without proving sexual desire, a particular sexual orientation, or the presence of an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual language, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish discriminatory causation.

**Controlling explanation:** Hicks preserves the claimant's ultimate persuasion burden without making one evidentiary route compulsory. Sex-directed hostility, selective conduct, discriminatory statements, threats and the circumstances can support a causal inference; this illustrative list is neither exclusive nor a checklist. Desire can be relevant, but making it indispensable would exclude sex-based mistreatment for reasons the statute does not recognize. The trier must evaluate the actual inference and competing explanations. Ordinary interpersonal hostility unconnected to sex remains outside this cause, and no orientation identity is found here.

##### Apply Harris in the actual workplace context

**Rule:** A hostile-environment claim requires sex-based conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively hostile or abusive environment in context, which the claimant actually perceived as abusive. An all-male workforce supplies context, not categorical immunity; neither psychiatric injury nor measurable productivity loss is indispensable.

**Controlling explanation:** Harris requires an objective and subjective inquiry into the circumstances and supplies the severe-or-pervasive threshold. Context helps distinguish actionable abuse from ordinary offensive banter; it cannot excuse forced physical humiliation or rape threats simply by labeling them rough camaraderie. The alleged conduct may satisfy the threshold, but allegations are not findings and gravity does not substitute for unwelcome treatment, discriminatory causation or employer attribution. No single factor, diagnosis, treatment, universal reasonable-woman standard or medical proof is made compulsory.

##### Attribution, other claims and relief remain distinct on remand

**Rule:** If employer attribution is reached, Andrade requires examination whether actual delegated supervisory authority materially aided the hostile environment; job title alone is insufficient, and absence of higher-management notice alone does not defeat that agency route. Negligence and any preserved quid-pro-quo claim remain distinct inquiries, and this reversal decides neither their factual predicates nor relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Andrade supplies the governing agency rule, including that employment action or economic loss is not invariably necessary for its authority-aided-environment route. A quid-pro-quo theory separately requires the alleged demand, use of employment power and employment-condition consequence to be identified; coverage does not collapse the two theories. The lower courts must apply the correct rules to the affected claims and any properly presented independent defenses in ordinary course. The Court does not reinstate an unchallenged coworker-employer theory, decide Lyons's status, find constructive discharge, order discovery or trial, or award reinstatement, backpay or punitive damages. Remedy questions for the 1991 conduct remain open.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson:** Applied the recognition of discriminatory abusive working conditions, extending its statutory application to employees and offenders who share a sex without eliminating any element.
- **Harris v. Forklift Systems:** Applied its contextual severe-or-pervasive, objective and subjective inquiry; no psychiatric injury or measurable economic harm is added.
- **St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks:** Preserved ultimate proof of discrimination and the availability of circumstantial inference; no compulsory comparator or desire showing is created.
- **Andrade v. Mayfair Management:** Applied the actual-authority/material-aid employer-attribution rule, keeping negligence and employment consequences distinct.
- **Brown v. Polk County and Nabozny v. Podlesny:** Their separate statutory and constitutional grounds do not replace Title VII's requirements or decide a sexual-orientation coverage question here.
- **Garcia's categorical same-sex exclusion:** The lower-court rule cannot govern Title VII after this decision.

#### Law After Decision

Title VII reaches discriminatory harassment without an opposite-sex requirement. Desire and workforce composition create no categorical gates or immunity. The employee still must establish sex causation and every element of the applicable harassment theory; Harris and Andrade retain their independent force. No sexual-orientation coverage holding or automatic liability rule is adopted.

#### Source Notes

The complete substantive Fifth Circuit opinion, 83 F.3d 118, supports the allegations, categorical dismissal and separate coworker-employer ground. Allegations remain unproved. [Fifth Circuit opinion](https://openjurist.org/83/f3d/118/oncale-v-sundowner-offshore-services-inc).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The affected claims return for adjudication under the correct statutory, Harris and Andrade standards, with preserved independent grounds and remedies addressed in ordinary order. Pippen and Johnson's independently based dismissal stands outside review. No finding of liability or automatic entitlement to further trial proceedings is made.

**End of entry: Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc., merits hearing and decision, March 19, 1997.**
