## John Crane, Inc. v. Abate — No. 98-873

Collection: [original petition-stage filings](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40386005_1459). Saved files are `abate_1._Petition_for_Writ_of_Certiorari_djvu.txt`, `abate_2._Opposition_Brief_djvu.txt`, and `abate_4._Reply_Brief_djvu.txt`.

The petition's opinions-below section identifies the Maryland Court of Special Appeals opinion at 121 Md. App. 590, 710 A.2d 944, reproduced at petition appendix 1a–109a. It identifies trial-court orders at appendix 110a–136a. The complete appendix is in the saved petition source. The source contains the actual lower opinion, rather than only the petition's characterization of it.

The petition's statement, original OCR lines 510–641, distinguishes the common-issue proceeding for an estimated 1,300 individual plaintiffs and related cross-claims from five individual plaintiffs whose causation and damages were tried to final judgment. It states that John Crane was not a defendant in the earlier Abate I trial. It describes the plaintiffs' selection of the five trial plaintiffs, inclusion of mesothelioma cases among those five, the estimated scope of the consolidation, different product categories, and the special-interrogatory forms. These are petitioner's assertions and record citations, including E. 3887, 3892–3895, 4020–4021, and 3704–3864; they are not newly found facts.

The opposition's statement, original OCR lines 191–303, describes the common questions as manufacture/distribution/installation, danger in product use, knowledge, and warning adequacy. It states that later mini-trials would decide each plaintiff's disease, exposure to a particular defendant's product, substantial contribution, injury, and damages. It describes five trial plaintiffs' verdicts against six defendants and a three-phase procedure involving common issues, earlier third-party claims, and punitive damages. This account is preserved separately from petitioner's contentions.

The clean extracts do not settle whether a particular category verdict conclusively determines a contested claim-specific fact, whether instructions cured particular risks, or whether an asserted trial error was preserved. Those propositions can be checked in the saved lower opinion, trial orders and the parties' cited record excerpts. Neither a Rule 23 certification nor final individual liability for every consolidated plaintiff should be inferred merely from the label “consolidated.”

## National Collegiate Athletic Association v. Smith — No. 98-84

Collection: [original filings](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0207). Saved: petition (`02`), joint appendix (`05`), petitioner merits brief (`06`), respondent merits brief (`07`), and merits reply (`08`).

The petition includes the Third Circuit opinion, 139 F.3d 180, at appendix 1a onward; the district opinion is 978 F. Supp. 213. Petitioner's merits brief lines 384–397 identify the March 16, 1998 appellate judgment and April 20 rehearing denial. The parties' statements distinguish dismissal of the original complaint from denial of leave to amend.

Respondent's statement, lines 427–506, describes her two seasons of undergraduate volleyball, completion of the undergraduate degree in two and one-half years, and later programs at Hofstra and Pittsburgh, which St. Bonaventure did not offer. The postbaccalaureate bylaw and remaining-eligibility conditions are copied in that statement. She alleges unequal treatment of waiver requests; the merits of that allegation are not established by a coverage argument.

The NCAA's statement, lines 526–540, describes approximately 1,200 members, approximately $200 million annual receipts from other activities, and approximately $900,000 membership dues. It asserts it receives no federal financial assistance. Respondent's statement disputes the legal consequence and points separately to the National Youth Sports Program and its fund, as well as NCAA governance of member athletics. Her statement, lines 514–540 and 625–660, identifies the agency letters and appellate arguments on which she relies for that alternative theory. The briefs dispute preservation and the significance of the affidavit concerning federal assistance. These are live source disputes, not resolved facts.

The direct/indirect NYSP route, member-dues route, and governance/control route have separate factual and legal premises. The saved briefs and joint appendix permit checking each without assuming that a dues allegation decides either alternative. The original briefs also contain references to historical Supreme Court procedural actions; such references are not entered in-world actions and are not copied into this fact map.

Official period texts are saved as `usc_1997_20_1681.html` and `usc_1997_20_1687.html`, with mechanically generated `.plain.txt` copies. These include the statute's exceptions and definition provisions, not merely a short paraphrase of the opening prohibition. The petitioner's merits brief contains a statutory/regulatory addendum beginning at original OCR line 2225, including Title IX regulations; verify exact regulation text there as needed.

## Your Home Visiting Nurse Services, Inc. v. Shalala — No. 97-1489

Collection: [original filings](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0169). Saved: petition (`02`), joint appendix (`05`), petitioner merits brief (`06`), respondent merits brief (`07`), and reply (`08`).

The petition appendix reproduces the Sixth Circuit opinion, 132 F.3d 1135, at pages 1–15, district-court proceedings at 16–33, agency correspondence/decisions, and statutory/regulatory provisions at 40–52. The government's merits brief identifies the appellate judgment date as December 22, 1997, the PRRB decision at J.A. 14–24, and the statutory-regulatory appendix locations at original OCR lines 580–611.

The provider's merits statement, lines 354–403, states that it requested reopening within three years based on newly discovered material concerning the 1989 cost reports, that the intermediary refused, and that the Board declined jurisdiction. It distinguishes its reopening request from an original reimbursement appeal.

The government's statement, lines 789–849, identifies four constituent agencies and four 1989 cost reports; it reports an attached schedule of protested owners' compensation, NPRs dated March 29, 1991, the absence of a timely appeal of those NPRs, and the March 28, 1994 request to reopen. It identifies the intermediary's April 21, 1994 refusal and PRRB's January 10, 1995 jurisdictional dismissal. The originals are in the saved J.A., notably pages 25–56. It also states that the provider pursued ordinary administrative appeals for other fiscal years. A timely reopening request must not be described as a timely original appeal.

The intermediary's stated reasons concern no clear error, no inconsistency with governing instructions, and no new material evidence sufficient to alter compensation. Those are the agency's stated determinations, not independent findings by this preflight. The parties dispute whether the statute and regulations require Board or court review of that refusal.

Official 1997 §1395oo is saved as `usc_1997_42_1395oo.html` (Part D, the period's correct designation) and a plain-text conversion. Section 405 is saved separately for §405(h). Official 1998 CFR §405.1885 XML and plain text include all of paragraphs (a)–(e): three-year reopening language; the specified HCFA-notification provision; exclusive administrative-body jurisdiction; fraud-or-similar-fault treatment; and the historic cost-period qualification. Sections 405.1887 and 405.1889 are saved as XML/plain text. The complete §405.1885 text was read from its official XML-derived plain copy; a one-page official PDF was also downloaded as a companion source. The initial §1395oo Part E endpoint and CFR HTML endpoints were invalid; no reliance is placed on those failed responses.

## Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee — No. 97-1252

Collection: [original filings](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0161). Saved: petition (`02`), separate petition appendix (`03`), joint appendix (`07`), government merits brief (`08`), respondents' merits brief (`09`), and reply (`10`).

The separate appendix contains the current Ninth Circuit opinion, 119 F.3d 1367, at pages 1a–21a; district orders at 22a–76a; earlier appellate and district opinions; and the rehearing material. The current case concerns injunctions before final deportation orders, but the claimants' statuses and underlying charges are not all alike.

Government statement lines 368–390 identifies eight individual respondents: permanent residents Khader Hamide and Michel Shehadeh, and six initially nonresident visa holders. Lines 446–492 describe the withdrawn advocacy-of-communism charges, remaining status charges against six, revised charges against the two permanent residents, and the second amended complaint's claims and requested injunction. The source supplies named people and distinct charges, avoiding a uniform description of all eight as charged only with routine visa violations.

Government statement lines 750–761 reports that Barakat and Sharif were subsequently granted legalization and were no longer subject to deportation for the original status violations. Respondents' statement lines 662–680 agrees about legalization but describes continued protection against new charges and disputes whether all threatened action has ended. These facts and the precise injunctions require separate mootness/scope validation; the source receipt draws no jurisdictional conclusion.

The parties' factual accounts differ materially. Respondents point to district findings about protected associations, comparators, lack of specific intent to further unlawful aims, and present First Amendment injury (respondent lines 546–798). Government points to fundraising/investigative material and challenges the legal standard and availability of district review. Both statements cite the actual lower findings and J.A. materials. The entire government and respondent statements, with exact source lines, are preserved in the clean extract file. Neither an allegation of terrorism nor a party's characterization of protected conduct is substituted for a finding.

Government statement lines 872–944 and respondents' lines 997–1080 separately describe the lower courts' jurisdictional reasoning and continued injunctions. The current Ninth Circuit opinion and district orders are available in the saved petition appendix for direct checking. The sources distinguish the injunction for the six from the subsequently extended protection for Hamide and Shehadeh.

Official 1997 8 U.S.C. §1252 is saved with its notes in `usc_1997_8_1252.html` and plain text. Official 1997 §1101 notes are saved separately and contain IIRIRA transition provisions. These period sources avoid importing later amendments. The text, applicability notes, and §309 transition provisions must be considered together; no construction is supplied in this receipt.
