### UNUM

The comparator's disposition and unanimous Ginsburg authorship are accurate. The full opinion separately affirms the notice-prejudice branch, reverses the Ninth Circuit's no-relation agency branch, and remands. Notice-prejudice is an insurance-specific mandatory term, not simply a discretionary general antiforfeiture principle. The McCarran-Ferguson factors are guideposts; the Court leaves the risk-spreading factor unresolved because the others securely support the common-sense conclusion. The insured/self-insured distinction and deemer clause remain material.

The agency reach must be stated carefully. The Court determines that the agency rule relied upon below **relates to** plan administration; Ward does not press insurance-saving for that rule and disputes precisely what the Ninth Circuit derived from Elfstrom. The decision does not adjudicate every fact-specific or voluntary agency relationship. The §502 discussion preserves an important limit: Ward brought a federal benefits suit, so whether a saved state law may supply a separate state cause of action or remedy is not decided (note 7). Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/97-1868.ZO.html.

It is saved as `unum-us-amicus.html`/`.txt`: https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/unum-life-insurance-v-ward-amicus-merits.
