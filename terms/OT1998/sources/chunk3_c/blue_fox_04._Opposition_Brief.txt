                                                            702, which waives the United States’ immunity to suits
                                                            challenging “agency action” except suits for “money dam-

                                                            as “equitable” rather than “legal” in nature. Because the
                                                            decision below answers that question in the affirmative, it
                                                            conflicts with decisions of the District of Columbia and
                                                            Third Cireuits construing Section 702, and with numerous
                                                            other decisions holding that a subcontractor cannot re-
                                                            cover directly from the government. Given the substantial
                                                            federal and fiscal interests implicated by those conflicts,
                                                            this Court should grant certiorari to resolve them.

    It is no answer to assert (Br. in Opp. 16) that the United
States can avoid liability by requiring Miller Act bonds
 when the law so demands. If it had been Congress’s desire
that the government avoid “money damages” awards only
through perfect legal compliance, Congress would have ab-

 rogated sovereign immunity entirely. Section 702, of
course, does no such thing. Moreover, it is far from clear

that the Ninth Circuit’s decision can be limitedto the

 Miller Act context. See Tucson Airport Auth. v. General

Dynamic Corp., 136 F.3d 641, 645 (9th Cir. 1998) (following
decision below in a case not involving the Miller Act).
 Indeed, to the extent that the decision below permits suits
to be brought to remedy tort-type injuries whenever the
reloireacftion can be characterized as “equitable,” it
exposes the United States to liability that previouslhyad
been limited or nonexistent. Pet. 27-28. Because the impo-
sition of such an unprecane d ud npre edin ctat bleefd inan-

that burden conflicts with the decisions of other courts of
appeals—review by this Court is warranted.
