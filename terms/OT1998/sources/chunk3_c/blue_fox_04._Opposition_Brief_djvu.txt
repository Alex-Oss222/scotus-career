REPLY BRIEF FOR THE PETITIONER 


The question presented in this case is whether 5 U.S.C. 
702, which waives the United States’ immunity to suits 
challenging “agency action” except suits for “money dam- 
ages,” permits a party to seek a monetary award from the 
government simply because the relief can be characterized 
as “equitable” rather than “legal” in nature. Because the 
decision below answers that question in the affirmative, it 
conflicts with decisions of the District of Columbia and 
Third Cireuits construing Section 702, and with numerous 
other decisions holding that a subcontractor cannot re- 
cover directly from the government. Given the substantial 
federal and fiscal interests implicated by those conflicts, 
this Court should grant certiorari to resolve them. 

A. Respondent does not dispute that the Ninth Circuit 
below read 5 U.S.C. 702 and this Court’s decision in 
Bowen v. Massachusetts, 487 U.S. 879 (1988), as declaring 
that the United States has waived its immunity to suits 
seeking monetary awards whenever the cause of action or 
remedy can be characterized as “equitable” in nature. 
Pet. App. 8a-9a (“Since the APA waives immunity for 
equitable actions, the district court had jurisdiction under 
the APA.”); id. at 10a (“[SJuch equitable liens are properly

damages.”); see Pet. 8-9. Nor does respondent deny that 
the D.C. Cireuit, in Hubbard v. Administrator, EPA, 982 


F 2d 531 (1992), rejected precisely that position. Id. at 537 
(“What may qualify as an ‘equitable remedy’ * is not 
synonymous with specific relief” permitted by 5 U.S.C. 
702.); id. at 589 (Randolph, J., joined by R. B. Ginsburg, J. 
concurring) (IT e line section 702 draws is not between

Likewise, respondent does not deny that the Ninth Cir- 
cuit in this case—following Aetna Casualty and Surety 
Co. v. United States, 71 F.3d 475, 479 (2d Cir. 1995)—held 
that 5 U.S.C. 702 permits monetary awards against the 
government in the absence of any statutory right to pay- 
ment. Pet. App. 7a-8a (“there is no requirement in Bowen 
or the APA” that the right to the money “be statutorily

Dia Navigation Co. v. Pomeroy, 34 F.3d 1255, 1267 (3d Cir. 
1994), and Hubbard, 982 F.2d at 536).

Likewise, the question before the Court in this case has

decision below thus conflicts not only with the decisions of
