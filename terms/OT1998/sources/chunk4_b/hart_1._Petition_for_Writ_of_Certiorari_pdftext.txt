           STATEMENT OF THE CASE
       Petitioners Stephanie Hart and Mainers For
Medical Rights ask the Court to strike down two
state requirements that unconstitutionally limit the
methods by which a Maine registered voter and
Maine resident such as Ms. Hart may proinote
political speech and collect signatures in support of a
citizen’s initiative petition. Although the state may
have a legitimate interest in allowing only Maine
residents to sponsor legislation through the citizen’s
initiative process, the state cannot justify laws that
prohibit a Maine voter from enlisting non-residents
or non-voters to promote political discourse
regarding proposed legislation through the
                                5
circulation of an initiative petition. This Court has
struck down similar restrictions on political speech
after applying strict scrutiny to the justifications
offered by the different states. Two United States
courts of appeals have struck down as
unconstitutional voter registration requirements
identical to those challenged by Petitioners.

       The Secretary of State’s arguments justifying
the restrictions at issue ring particularly hollow in
light of the absence of similar restrictions in
analogous areas of the state legislative and electoral
process. Maine does not prohibit non-residents or
non-registered voters from working as lobbyists or
from circulating nominating petitions for aspiring
political candidates. Ifthe state truly has a
compelling interest in preventing non-residents or
non-voters from participation in the legislative
                                6
process, then restrictions on non-residents and non-
voters must be enforced across the board. Because
they are not, the requirements that persons who
circulate citizen’s initiative petitions be both
registered voters and residents cannot pass
constitutional muster.

       Petitioners do not argue that non-residents
should be permitted to sponsor legislation that cou!d
become the law of the state. A resident and

registered voter such as Ms. Hart, however, must be

permitted to exercise her First Amendment right to
spread her political message and garner support for
her proposed legislation without unreasonable
restrictions. The laws challenged by Petitioners
must be struck down to protect the constitutional
rights of all Maine voters who desire to sponsor
legislation through the citizen’s initiative process.

ballot in the next general election for approval by the
electorate.

         The reasoning discussed in Meyer, Bernbeck,
  and ACLF applies with equal force to a residency
  requirement for circulators and renders such a
  requirement unconstitutional. As with the voter-
  registration requirement, the state’s need to limit the
   ability to sponsor legislation to its own residents
   cannot justify a requirement that persons who
   circulate a citizen’s initiative petition be Maine

   residents. In no other aspect of the legislative
                                28
process does the state enforce similar restrictions on
political speech. The same arguments that
demonstrate the unconstitutionality of the voter-
registration intial also demonstrate the
unconstitutionality of the residency requirement.

       Although this specific residency requirement
has not been reviewed by the Court, a United States
district court implicitly rejected circulator residency
under the strict scrutiny standard of review. In
Term Limits Leadership, Inc. v. Clark, 984 F. Supp.
470, 475 (S.D. Miss. 1997), the court struck down a
registered voter requirement for circulators in part
because the state could not demonstrate “actual
fraud or threat to citizen’s confidence in government
posed by out-of-state circulators.” (emphasis added)
As with the voter registration requirement, the
Secretary has made no showing that non-resident
                               29
circulators present a greater threat to the integrity of
the legislative process than do resident circulators,
much less a showing that the requirement furthers a

compelling state interest.
        The residency requirement is intimately related
