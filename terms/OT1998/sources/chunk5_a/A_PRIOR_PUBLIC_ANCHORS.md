### Aktepe v. United States, Eleventh Circuit No. 96-2167

Plenary decision on justiciability, March 10, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Judgment below: 105 F.3d 1400. The Court reviews under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) the February 20, 1997 affirmance of summary judgment for the United States. The question is whether the pleaded maritime negligence controversy is nonjusticiable, including whether a separable operational claim can be adjudicated without choosing military policy. No separate Supreme Court docket or oral-argument date is established. The sailors seek adjudication of two wrongful-death and 299 personal-injury claims arising when the Saratoga fired live missiles at the Muavenet during an intended mock NATO attack. All nine Justices participate at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

The political-question summary judgment remains effective. No negligence, damages, statutory-coverage, sovereign-immunity or Feres ruling, discovery order or new trial follows.

#### Opinions and Joins

Kennedy delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer. Stevens dissented, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg. The Court adopts the complete claim-specific ground for nonjusticiability and its limits; the dissent would vacate and remand for separate examination of execution allegations and policy choices, without guaranteeing a surviving claim.

#### Holdings

##### The pleaded duties cannot be adjudicated without choosing military training policy

**Holding and operative rule:** A military setting alone does not make a damages action nonjusticiable. These claims nevertheless cannot proceed because determining the alleged duties concerning drill notification, command terminology, live arming and questioning orders would require choosing among military training, obedience and safety policies without an independently manageable legal standard on the record before the Court.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer; five of nine participating Justices directly adopt the rule, application and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker requires examination of the particular claim, not rejection because its subject is military. Nixon preserves that issue-specific inquiry. Here ordinary negligence terminology does not identify a duty independent of the disputed training choices. Deciding which personnel should know that an attack is a mock exercise, what live-weapons commands should mean, and when operators should question an apparent firing instruction would select the balance between realistic preparation, command obedience and safety that the pleaded allegations challenge.

The contrary argument has force: this was an unintended live discharge during an intended mock exercise, and compensation after an accident differs from continuing supervision. Gilligan expressly left specific-injury controversies distinct from the ongoing supervision it rejected. But that reservation neither makes every damages action justiciable nor supplies a manageable standard for these proposed duties. Retrospective relief can still depend on judicial selection of training policy. No independently fixed operational command is established in the record before the Court that separates the alleged breach from that selection; this is not a finding that no such command exists or that plaintiffs withheld evidence.

Weiss and Loving preserve enforcement of actual constitutional and enacted limits. Dalton concerns a particular statutory review scheme and supplies no general executive immunity. Richenberg likewise combines substantial military weight with an enforceable legal standard. None supplies the independent duty missing from this claim-specific inquiry. NATO participation, possible embarrassment and military status alone would be insufficient. The Court affirms the political-question judgment without finding that the firing was lawful, deciding negligence or damages, or resolving statutory coverage, sovereign immunity or Feres. A materially different claim enforcing an independent legal duty remains outside this holding.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current Law

- Baker v. Carr supplies the claim-specific political-question inquiry; military subject matter alone remains insufficient.
- Nixon v. United States preserves review of independently fixed legal limits while recognizing the confined nonjusticiability of an actually committed procedural choice; its impeachment holding is not military immunity.
- Gilligan v. Morgan, 413 U.S. 1, is applied to the policy-selection concern, while its reservation of specific-injury controversies remains intact and supplies no categorical damages exception.
- Weiss v. United States and Loving v. United States preserve constitutional and statutory review in military matters; their appointment and delegation holdings do not establish the maritime duty proposed here.
- Dalton v. Specter retains scheme-specific statutory preclusion and is not extended to these maritime statutes.
- Richenberg v. Cohen preserves substantial military weight within actual legal constraints, without creating a military-tort immunity. No precedent is overruled.

**Current law:** The issue-specific political-question rule applies to these pleaded training and communication duties. Military status does not alone bar judicial review, and the decision establishes no categorical military-tort immunity. Independent statutory defenses and any materially different claim resting on an independently manageable duty retain their own requirements.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, dissenting.** The dissent would vacate and remand for separate examination of the claims. The intended mock exercise and accidental live firing supply a concrete reason to distinguish execution from design; listing training and command purposes does not establish that every allegation requires choosing policy. Gilligan's reservation of specific-injury cases and Nixon's claim-specific limitation oppose the blanket effect of the judgment. Weiss and Loving preserve enforceable legal constraints despite military subject matter, and Dalton does not supply general executive insulation.

The dissent accepts that a court cannot invent a safer training rule, call it operational, and thereby evade the political-question doctrine. The remand would require the Eleventh Circuit to identify which allegations actually require selection of constitutionally committed military policy or lack manageable standards, without guaranteeing a surviving negligence claim, discovery, liability or compensation. No particular written protocol is found or presumed violated. Statutory coverage, sovereign immunity, Feres, causation, damages and the justiciability of any specifically identified high-level policy challenge remain unresolved. The dissent's proposed examination is not the Court's mandate.

#### Source Notes

The [official Eleventh Circuit opinion, 105 F.3d 1400](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/19962167.OPA.pdf), supplies the summary-judgment posture, uncontested drill and communication facts and the unadjudicated Feres alternative. [Gilligan, 413 U.S. 1](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-413/pdf/USREPORTS-413-1.pdf), distinguishes continuing training supervision from reserved specific-injury controversies. The original maritime pleadings and evidence are not separately reproduced; no particular written operational protocol is established.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

This Supreme Court review is complete. Summary judgment on the political-question ground remains in force; the Court directs no further merits proceedings.

**End of entry: Aktepe v. United States, Plenary decision on justiciability, March 10, 1998.**

### Bousley v. United States, No. 96-8516

Decision, May 18, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 3, 1998; decided May 18, 1998. On certiorari to the Eighth Circuit's denial of relief in a first §2255 proceeding.

Bousley pleaded guilty to a drug offense and using a firearm during and in relation to drug trafficking. He appealed his sentence without challenging the plea's validity. After Bailey explained that use requires active employment, he challenged the understanding of the firearm charge underlying his plea. The questions are whether that claim is available on collateral review, how procedural default affects it, and what factual innocence requires. The indictment charged use, not carrying.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices or different remedy | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Set aside the Eighth Circuit's rejection | Reversed, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | Set aside the rejection; the mandate is stated separately below. |
| Conditional gateway remand | Six adopt the controlling mandate | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stevens would immediately vacate the firearm conviction and permit a new plea; Scalia and Thomas would affirm | Opportunity to establish factual innocence, followed by adjudication of the plea-intelligence claim only if that gateway is satisfied. |

The firearm conviction is not immediately vacated. Neither factual innocence nor constitutional invalidity is found. The drug conviction is unaffected. The remand does not automatically require an oral evidentiary hearing, release or dismissal of the firearm charge.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer throughout; Stone-Zsela in I–III and IV.B; Stevens in I | Supports reversal and the conditional remand; Stevens seeks a different remedy | I: informed plea and substantive meaning; II: default; III: factual gateway; IV.A: actually forgone more-serious charges; IV.B: use-only indictment and conditional remand. |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stevens | No other Justice | Would immediately vacate the firearm conviction and permit a new plea | Joins I; rejects the default and innocence prerequisites. |
| Dissent | Scalia | Thomas | Would affirm | Cause is absent; the trial-conviction innocence gateway should not extend to this plea. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Correct knowledge of the offense is necessary to an intelligent plea

**Holding and operative rule:** A guilty plea requires real notice of the charge's essential nature. A materially mistaken description of a statutory element can render the plea unintelligent; Bailey's explanation of what the firearm statute prohibits is substantive law, not a new constitutional procedure barred by Teague.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Parts II–III and IV.B are joined by the same coalition except Stevens. Part IV.A has O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each stated rule has direct majority support.

**Controlling explanation:** Bailey requires active employment of a firearm; proximity, accessibility or possession alone is not use. Henderson and Smith require an understanding of the actual charge. A statutory construction states the substantive reach of the crime, as Rivers explains, rather than imposing a new trial procedure. Teague's procedural restriction therefore supplies no answer to this claim.

Broce distinguishes claims waived by an otherwise valid guilty plea from a challenge to the plea's own validity. Brady's concern with later disappointed expectations does not excuse a materially mistaken account of the offense's essential elements. Godinez likewise separates capacity to choose from the informed choice itself.

**Precedent treatment:** Bailey supplies the offense's meaning; Henderson and Smith supply notice; Rivers explains substantive interpretation; Teague does not bar this substantive claim; Broce, Brady and Godinez retain their distinct limits.

##### 2. Direct-review default requires cause and prejudice or factual innocence

**Holding and operative rule:** Failure to challenge plea intelligence on direct review defaults this claim. Neither anticipated defeat nor adverse precedent establishes cause where the legal argument was available; absent cause, the claim may proceed only through a sufficient factual-innocence showing, not a showing of legal error alone.

**Controlling explanation:** The distinction between using and possessing a firearm was available before Bailey. Reed's unavailable-claim concern does not encompass an argument thought unlikely to prevail, and Engle rejects expected futility as cause. Prejudice cannot replace missing cause.

Schlup nevertheless permits review where, considering the whole probative record under the correct offense, it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. That exceptional inquiry applies here despite the plea. The absence of a trial record does not justify treating an unsupported conviction as conclusive, but neither does it shift the inquiry to whether the existing plea record was formally sufficient. Carriger preserves the difference between passing this gateway and proving the underlying constitutional violation. The district court has not adjudicated innocence, and the Government does not contend that omission of that issue below independently waived this opportunity. The Court does not now decide constitutional invalidity; the gateway and underlying claim must be resolved in that order.

**Precedent treatment:** Reed and Engle delimit cause; Schlup supplies the demanding whole-record gateway; Carriger keeps the gateway separate from the constitutional merits.

##### 3. The evidence and actual plea bargain define the innocence inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government may offer admissible evidence outside the plea record to rebut factual innocence. Where it actually forgave an identified more-serious charge in the plea bargain, the innocence showing must also cover that charge; hypothetical charges, unproved bargains and alternative statutory verbs create no added burden.

**Controlling explanation:** Factual innocence concerns what occurred, not merely what the plea papers recite. Limiting the Government to those papers would convert the gateway into a legal-sufficiency test. The five Justices joining IV.A also require attention to an actual bargain relinquishing more-serious exposure, so that the inquiry does not disregard the exchange that produced the plea.

This indictment alleged use, not carrying. No bargain relinquishing carrying or another identified more-serious offense is established. Bousley therefore need establish absence of use under Bailey on this record. The Government cannot preserve the conviction simply by relabeling it an uncharged carrying conviction. A proved gateway permits adjudication of the plea-intelligence claim; it does not itself require vacatur or acquittal.

**Precedent treatment:** Schlup's factual inquiry is applied to a guilty plea with evidence beyond the plea record; Bailey continues to define the charged use offense.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Bailey:** Active employment remains necessary for the charged use offense; possession alone is insufficient.
- **Henderson and Smith:** Applied to real notice of the charge's essential nature.
- **Rivers:** Supplies the substantive effect of an authoritative statutory construction.
- **Teague:** Its new-procedure limitation does not bar this substantive statutory claim.
- **Broce and Brady:** Distinguished from an attack on the plea's informed character; a disappointed expectation and misinformation about an element remain different.
- **Godinez:** Competence does not itself establish informed assent.
- **Reed and Engle:** An available argument's expected failure does not establish cause.
- **Schlup:** Extended to this defaulted plea claim with its demanding factual test intact.
- **Carriger:** Passing the gateway remains distinct from proving constitutional error and entitlement to relief.
- **Hyde:** Its presentence withdrawal setting does not decide this collateral claim.

#### Law After Decision

A defaulted challenge to a materially misdescribed guilty plea may receive collateral review upon the required factual-innocence showing. Admissible proof beyond the plea record is available. Actual relinquishment of a more-serious charge matters; an imagined charge does not. Passing the gateway opens consideration of plea validity and supplies no automatic merits relief.

#### Separate Writings

Stevens joins the substantive and constitutional principles in Part I but would vacate the firearm conviction and permit Bousley to plead anew. Timmreck and Hill distinguish formal plea-taking defects from a constitutionally invalid plea. In his view, the erroneous understanding of the statutory element establishes the constitutional defect here, and failure to appeal does not require Bousley to prove innocence before obtaining correction. The Government would retain its ordinary burden if prosecution proceeds. His position does not require acquittal or dismissal with prejudice and does not affect the drug conviction.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, would affirm. He agrees that no cause excuses the default but rejects extending Schlup's trial-conviction gateway to guilty pleas. A plea leaves no comparable trial record of facts that participants may then have thought immaterial, may exchange admission for relief from greater charges, and may leave stale evidence insufficient to reconstruct either side of that exchange. Those features make the proposed exception materially different from Schlup's setting. The dissent does not treat possession as active use, find actual carrying, or decide Bousley's innocence.

#### Source Notes

The published record at 523 U.S. 614 supplies the charged offense, plea and review history, and the Government's position on the separate omission of innocence below. The original plea transcript and bargain agreement are not included in the available source record. No relinquished carrying or more-serious charge is established by the available record, and no such bargain is presumed.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The district court must permit the bounded factual-innocence inquiry and resolve any factual development needed under ordinary procedures. If Bousley satisfies that gateway, it must consider whether his plea was voluntary and intelligent and determine any lawful relief. No automatic live hearing, release or acquittal is ordered.

**End of entry: Bousley v. United States, decision, May 18, 1998.**

### Hohn v. United States, No. 96-8986

Decision, June 15, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 3, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit. The question is whether 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) permits review of the court of appeals’ denial of a certificate of appealability.

Hohn sought §2255 relief from a firearm-use conviction under Bailey. His district-court motion was pending when AEDPA became law on April 24, 1996. After the motion was denied, he filed a notice of appeal on July 29, 1996. A circuit panel treated it as a certificate request and denied it because it regarded the Bailey claim as statutory rather than constitutional. The Government now characterizes the claim as constitutional and supports reconsideration. An appointed advocate contests this Court’s jurisdiction.

Hohn separately argues that Lindh removes the certificate requirement from his pre-enactment motion. The Government contests preservation, scope and that temporal construction; Hohn disputes those objections. The Court resolves the statutory review question without deciding that alternative or its preservation.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, who would dismiss for want of statutory jurisdiction.

The Eighth Circuit must reconsider the certificate question in light of the Government’s changed constitutional characterization and applicable law. No certificate, automatic merits appeal, collateral relief, trial-conviction vacatur, hearing, release or acquittal is ordered.

#### Opinions and Joins

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer | Vacatur of the certificate denial and remand | Complete statutory-jurisdiction rule, limited overruling of House, transition reservation and certificate remand. |
| Dissent | Scalia | Thomas throughout; O'Connor in Part I only | Would dismiss for want of statutory jurisdiction | Part I preserves House and rejects §1254 jurisdiction. Part II applies the certificate condition to the post-enactment appellate step. |
| Separate dissent | O'Connor | No other Justice | Would dismiss for want of statutory jurisdiction | Joins Part I of Scalia's dissent; gives a distinct, provision-specific temporal reason for rejecting the no-certificate alternative. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. A judicial certificate-denial proceeding is reviewable under §1254(1)

**Controlling proposition:** An appellate court’s adjudication denying a certificate of appealability is a case in the court of appeals reviewable by certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), even though the denial prevents the underlying merits appeal from proceeding. House v. Mayo is overruled only insofar as it denies that statutory certiorari jurisdiction; its distinct extraordinary/common-law certiorari discussion under §1651 and other holdings are not enlarged or displaced.

**Authority:** The six-Justice Opinion of the Court directly supports both holdings, their explanations, the reservations and the mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The certificate request asks a judicial body to decide an asserted legal entitlement adverse to the applicant. Denial of permission does not erase the proceeding that decided permission. Section 1254’s provision for review before or after judgment fits that threshold judicial decision. Section 2253(c)’s condition on taking the merits appeal does not say that the certificate proceeding itself is beyond review. Allocating the determination to circuit judges does not change the judicial character of the actual appellate adjudication.

House identified the case only with an underlying appeal already permitted to proceed. That premise fails to account for the separately adjudicated certificate request. Statutory precedent warrants substantial respect, but the Court must correct that mistaken jurisdictional construction expressly. Its statutory character does not cure weak reasoning about the judicial proceeding Congress made reviewable. Quirin and ordinary review of threshold jurisdictional decisions support treating that judicial business as a case even before the merits appeal proceeds. Lower courts were not free to treat House as silently overruled before this decision.

Felker enforces Congress’s express bar on review of successive-application authorization decisions. Triestman and Dorsainvil apply that bar; none transfers it to a different certificate ruling. Stewart likewise recognizes the importance of identifying the actual threshold act rather than extending a review prohibition by label. Those distinctions support statutory review here, not review of a true authorization refusal. Agreement of the parties creates no jurisdiction, and no extraordinary-writ substitute is used.

This ground establishes review of the actual denial whether the circuit correctly or incorrectly required a certificate. The Court therefore reserves Hohn’s competing Lindh transition argument and the disputed preservation and scope objections. It holds neither that a certificate was required nor that the underlying appeal was automatically perfected.

##### II. Reconsideration of the certificate question does not grant collateral relief

**Controlling proposition:** The certificate denial is vacated and the matter returned for reconsideration in light of the Government’s changed constitutional characterization and governing law. If a certificate is required and is later issued, §2253(c) still requires a substantial showing of the denial of a constitutional right and identification of the qualifying issues; this judgment itself establishes neither condition.

**Controlling explanation:** Bailey defines the substantive reach of firearm use. Bousley distinguishes that statutory meaning from a possible constitutional claim and from satisfaction of the requirements for collateral relief. The statutory origin of an argument therefore does not alone dispose of every constitutional claim it may support. The Government’s changed position warrants reconsideration of the actual claim but proves no constitutional violation. The lower court must apply the proper law to the claim presented, including the unresolved certificate question. The Court finds no default exception, factual innocence, invalid conviction or entitlement to a certificate, and supplies no trial or plea facts not established in this proceeding.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **House v. Mayo:** Its contrary statutory certificate-review holding is expressly overruled; its extraordinary/common-law certiorari discussion under §1651 is neither expanded nor a substitute ground for today’s action.
- **Ex parte Quirin:** Its treatment of judicial business before completion of the ordinary appellate path supports statutory review of the threshold proceeding.
- **Felker v. Turpin:** Its actual authorization-review bar and separately retained original-habeas jurisdiction remain intact, including their independent conditions.
- **Triestman v. United States and In re Dorsainvil:** Their dismissals of ordinary authorization review are distinguished from review of a certificate denial.
- **Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal:** Its identification of an independently reviewable threshold classification supports the act-specific channel inquiry; it did not previously decide certificate review.
- **Lindh v. Murphy, Landgraf v. USI Film Products and Rivers v. Roadway Express:** Their provision-specific temporal inquiry remains governing. This decision reserves the certificate amendment’s application to this pre-enactment motion and later notice.
- **Bailey v. United States and Bousley v. United States:** Their distinction between substantive statutory meaning, a constitutional claim and ultimate relief informs reconsideration without deciding Hohn’s claim.
- **Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.:** The Court establishes its statutory review authority before granting the bounded vacatur; it does not assume jurisdiction to decide the merits.

#### Law After Decision

A certificate denial is subject to ordinary statutory certiorari review. That rule removes House’s contrary jurisdictional obstacle without weakening the express bar on review of successive-application authorization decisions. It changes neither certificate eligibility nor the requirements for collateral relief, and resolves no general AEDPA transition rule.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Scalia, dissenting, with Justice Thomas throughout and Justice O’Connor in Part I.** Part I would preserve House’s statutory construction. Section 2253 conditions the taking of an appeal on a certificate; until a circuit justice or judge gives that permission, the underlying case has not entered the court of appeals in the sense §1254 requires. The decision of a judge on permission is not a separate appellate case merely because the request involves legal judgment. Congress retained and extended the certificate structure against House’s settled construction, strengthening the statutory-stare-decisis objection. Felker’s express bar concerns permission for initial district consideration of a successive application, a different act for which House supplied no controlling construction; Stewart concerns a different circuit classification. Neither warrants discarding the certificate-specific precedent.

Part II, joined only by Thomas, rejects the no-certificate alternative because the provision regulates the appellate step initiated after AEDPA’s enactment. Applying it to the July 29, 1996 notice is prospective at the relevant activity; it does not impose a new merits rule on the earlier district motion. They make no forfeiture finding. Without statutory review authority they would dismiss, not vacate the denial. They grant no extraordinary writ and do not treat §1651 as an independent source of jurisdiction.

**Justice O’Connor, separately dissenting.** She joins the House and statutory-jurisdiction analysis in Part I but states a narrower temporal ground. Landgraf requires attention first to statutory direction and the particular provision, not a generic procedural label. Lindh’s application-date construction for the amendments governing the pending district application does not settle the separate appellate condition. Section 2253(c) regulates the later appeal initiated on July 29, 1996; applying it there is consistent with Felker’s separately examined post-enactment authorization and review proceedings. She therefore rejects Hohn’s alternative on that provision-specific basis, without resolving every chapter-153 amendment or finding his argument forfeited. She would dismiss for want of statutory jurisdiction.

#### Source Notes

The reported proceeding at [524 U.S. 236](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-236.pdf) supplies the certificate-denial posture and Government’s changed characterization. The original briefs in [the record for No. 96-8986](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0098) contain the competing temporal and preservation arguments; those submissions are not findings of preservation or waiver. Sections 1254(1) and 2253(c) supply the distinct statutory inquiries.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Eighth Circuit’s certificate denial is vacated. It must reconsider under applicable law and the Government’s changed position. The disputed transition argument remains unresolved; the Court neither directs issuance of a certificate nor orders consideration of the underlying appeal as automatically authorized. No collateral merits relief follows from this mandate.

**End of entry: Hohn v. United States, Decision, June 15, 1998.**
