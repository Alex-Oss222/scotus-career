B. Even Under the District’s Multifactor Test, the ALJ s 
Decision Must be Affirmed. 


Under the District's own proposed test, Petitioner's Brief at p. 
34-35, on the present record, the District must provide the nursing 
services requested. The District's proposed test is as follows: 


. . . [CJourts should consider several factors in 
determining whether the services are excluded 
medical services or included school health services. 
These factors include the nature and complexity of 
the care, whether the care is continuous or 
intermittent, whether existing school health 
personnel may provide the services, the cost of the 


41 


services, and the potential repercussions if the services 
are not properly performed. 


Petitioner’s Brief at p. 34-35. In order to prevail, the District would 
have to persuade this Court to reweigh the evidence, which is 
inconsistent with the deference accorded state boards of education 
under the IDEA as interpreted by this Court in Rowley, 458 U.S. 
at 206-07. That evidence is as follows. 


1. Nature and complexity of the care 


As the ALJ found, many of the services required by Garret 
can be and are provided by nonskilled personnel, including family 
members and unlicensed personal attendants. The District relies 
on its own IEP team’s finding and the original declaratory ruling 
of the Nursing Board. The District neglects the fact that it was this 
plan that the ALJ rejected upon appeal by Garret’s mother. The 
District further neglects the fact that the Board of Nursing has now 
specifically ruled that an LPN can provide the services. 


2. Whether the care is continuous or intermittent 


Despite the District's assertion to the contrary, the only medical 
evidence, given by Dr. Reinertson, was that Garret did not require 
continuous monitoring and assessment. Tr. at 627-28. Someone 
simply needs to be within “earshot” to respond to his needs. Tr. at 
628. According to the District, no child, regardless of physical or 
mental disability, should be left unattended during the school day. 
Tr. at 410-12. The care he needs is intermittent and it can and has 
been provided by a lay person. App. 10-11; 19; Tr. at 68-69; 640- 
41; 653-55. 


3. Whether existing school health personnel may provide the 
services 


There has been much said about who can provide the services 
to Garret F. Curiously, although the District claims that the services 
must be provided by an RN, it has permitted Garret’s family to 
furnish these services throughout the years first with an eighteen- 
year-old non-professional aunt and then with an LPN. Further, the 


42 


District now has an opinion from the Nursing Board that the services 
may be performed by an LPN. 


4. The cost of the service 


As argued above, Garret F. strenuously denies that costs should 
be a factor and asserts that there is no controlling precedent that 
would permit cost to be taken into account once the threshold 
question of whether the services requested are “supportive services” 
within the meaning of the IDEA is answered. However, even if 
cost is a factor, the actual costs of the services rendered in this case 
have been exaggerated by the District. Finally, the District has 
access to additional state money to finance these services. Pet. App. 
37a; Tr. at 588. 


5. The potential repercussions 


Even if this were to be a relevant factor, which Garret F. denies, 
there is no evidence that the potential repercussions if the services 
were performed improperly would be greater if performed by a 
school nurse as opposed to the LPN hired by the parents. Further, 
the record in this case is that emergency breathing can be provided 
for a lengthy period of time through the use of ambu-bagging 
until emergency medical care arrives. Tr. at 473-74. For example, 
the Frey family sustained Garret’s breath by use of the ambu-bag 
on a trip from Cedar Rapids, lowa, to Denver, Colorado, and back. 
Tr. at 36. Also, the history of Garret at school shows that there has 
never been an emergency situation. Tr. at 88. 


Thus, the District’s argument under a multifactor analysis must 
fail and even if this approach were adopted, the judgment must be 
affirmed.

AS A FURTHER ALTERNATIVE GROUND, THE 
JUDGMENT MUST BE AFFIRMED ON STATE LAW 
GROUNDS. 


This Court as a general rule will not affirm on state law 
grounds. City of Mesquite v. Aladdin's Castle, Inc., 455 U.S. 283, 
292 (1982). However, in IDEA cases, state law provisions for 
special education that create a higher duty for school districts than 
recognized by federal law have been held to be automatically 
incorporated into, and enforceable under, the IDEA. See David D. 
v. Dartmouth Sch. Comm., 775 F.2d at 420. Thus, affirmance is 
proper under state law grounds. In the alternative, the Court could 
remand for a determination on state law grounds. City of Mesquite, 
455 U.S. at 292. 


The ALJ held that Garret F. was entitled to the services as 
“support services” under lowa law. The Department of Education 
held that the District had the responsibility under lowa Code 
Sections 256B.2 and .3 and lowa Code Section 280.8 to provide 
the services. It stated: 


Under [the] lowa statute Garret is a child requiring 
special education, and a six-year history of successful 
functioning in the regular classroom, with aids and 
services provided by his family and the school prove 
beyond a doubt that [education in regular classes] can 
be accomplished satisfactorily. Under state law, Garret 
must be provided an education program “within the 
regular school environment” and under the same state 
law; it is the District’s responsibility to do so. 


Pet. App. 55a. 


The ALJ found that the alternative of a homebound program 
would “certainly not be an education with nondisabled children to 
the ‘maximum extent appropriate’ ” within the meaning of federal 
regulations. Pet. App. 59a. The ALJ also properly noted that under 


44 


lowa law, “only when a child ‘cannot sufficiently profit from the 
work in the regular classroom’ may he be removed.” Pet. App. 
59a: lowa Code § 256B.8 (1993). The ALJ further found that the 
lowa Board of Nursing’s declaratory ruling that an RN was required 
to render the care did not relieve the District of responsibility to 
provide health care services to Garret at school. Pet App. 60a. 
Accordingly, the Court should affirm on this ground.
