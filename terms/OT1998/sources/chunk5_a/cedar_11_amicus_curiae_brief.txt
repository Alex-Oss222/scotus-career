The American Academy of Pediatrics is a non-profit 
charitable and educational organization of 53,000 primary care 
pediatricians, pediatric medical subspecialists, and pediatric 
surgical specialists dedicated to the health, safety, and well- 
being of infants, children, adolescents, and young adults.

by providing support and counsel to others concerned with the 
well-being of children, their growth and development, and by 
serving as an advocate for children and their families within the 
community at large.

Garret F. is an intelligent and motivated boy who wants to 
continue to join other children his age in enjoying free access to 
education in America's public school system. See Pet. App. 
19a. Garret is a special education student, Pet. App. 10a, who 
is able to attend school in the classroom, and has been attending 
school since 1988 when he began kindergarten. Pet. App. 2a. 
But because he is paralyzed from the neck down and ventilator- 
dependent, he needs certain caretaking tasks performed dunng 
school hours in order to attend school. /d. These tasks are 
supportive in nature and permit Garret and his mechanical 
equipment to function in the school setting and to participate in 
the educational program with other children. This support ts 
also needed outside the school setting, twenty-four hours a day 
to permit Garret to live. With it, he is able to be part of his 
community -- to visit friends and neighbors, take vacations with 
his family, go on shopping trips, and take part in other activities 
Garret’s parents, and sometimes his friends, provide these 
services at home, on weekdays after school until 11:00 p.m. and 
on weekends without the assistance of health care personnel. 
Pet. App. 21a; J.A. 3-4, 19-20. 


The maintenance procedures Garret requires during the 
school day are clean intermittent catheterization (approximately 
once a day), periodic suctioning (cleaning) of his tracheostomy, 
ventilation by means of an Ambu bag during suctioning, and 
monitoring his breathing and ventilator in case of malfunction. 
Pet. App. 24a. It is undisputed that none of these tasks needs 
to be performed by a physician. Pet. App. 6a, 12a, 15a. It is

SUMMARY OF ARGUMENT 


The services at issue here are not treatment. They are not 
provided by a doctor or hospital. They are simply services 
necessary to operate and monitor the equipment that substitutes 
for the functions Garret has lost in order to keep him alive. 
With that equipment, he is able to be with family and friends and 
to acquire, from the public school system, the educational and 
social skills he will need to participate in society initially as a 
child and later as an adult. 


It is undisputed that Garret is capable of attending public 
school and benefiting from the educational and other 
opportunities provided by the public school system, he has been 
doing so for years. The only dispute is whether the lowa 
Department of Education’s specific finding -- that the 
monitoring and maintenance services Garret requires while he 
attends school must be provided, see Pet. App. 63a -- is 
consistent with the statute Congress enacted to encourage states 
to provide children with disabilities access to public education 
and consistent with the United States Department of 
Education’s regulations implementing that statute. Because the 
state agency’s decision is consistent with both, amici urge this 
Court to affirm. 


1. Congress enacted the Individuals with Disabilities 
Education Act in order to encourage states to provide public 
school educations for children with disabilities. Congress 
predicated federal assistance for educating such children on the 
states’ providing not only special education when needed but 
also related support services to enable the children to benefit 
from the education, excepting only medical services other than 
diagnosis and evaluation. Pt. L.A. 


9 


2. The Secretary of Education’s implementing 
regulations -- requiring states to provide nursing services but 
exempting them from providing services performed by a 
physician -- are permissible under the statute and therefore due 
deference under Chevron. Pt. 1.B. 


3. Petitioner's proposed balancing test for differently 
determining which services are exempt is merely an alternative 
policy choice. It has no basis in the statute, and is, indeed, 
inconsistent with the statute, as Congress made clear when it 
prohibited the agency from decreasing required support services 
below 1982 levels, without unequivocal support from 
subsequent legislation. In addition, the test petitioner proposes 
has the practical infirmity of being difficult to apply and to 
police. Pt. 1.C & Pt. 1.D. 


4 The lowa Department of Education properly 
applied the statute and regulations in finding that Garret must 
be provided the maintenance and monitoring services he 
requires to attend school. Pt. II. 


5. Contrary to petitioner’s complaints, the federal 
government is not forcing high costs on school districts) The 
federal statute and regulations offer states federal assistance if 
they agree to meet set standards, including providing support 
services to children with disabilities. States are required to 
comply only if they choose to avail themselves of the assistance 
Congress delegated decision: as to the sources of additional 
funding to the states, so long as there is no charge to the family. 
Thus, it is the State of lowa’s decision whether the school 
district, a state agency, the family’s insurance, or some 
combination funds the services Garret requires. Pt. III.

found that one million of these children were being excluded
