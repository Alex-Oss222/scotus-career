VI. THIS CASE SHOULD NOT BE AFFIRMED ON 
STATE LAW GROUNDS. 


Since neither of the lower federal courts addressed the 
state law issues, they should be considered on remand. 
Moreover, for all practical purposes, the relevant Iowa 
law is the same as the IDEA. If Petitioner prevails on the 
IDEA issues, it is entirely likely that the lower courts 
will find for Petitioner on the state-law issues. 


The ALJ relied upon language in the Iowa statute 
which defines “special education” as including “corrective 
and supporting services.” This language mirrors the defi- 
nition of “related services” in the IDEA. Compare 
§ 256B.2(3) of the Iowa Code with 20 U.S.C. § 1401 
(a)(17). The Iowa Department of Education has also 
adopted the federal definition of “related services,” in- 
cluding the specific exclusion of medical services. 281 
Iowa Admin. Code § 41.94(2)(d) (1995). Furthermore, 
the Iowa Supreme Court has already ruled that variations 
in language between the federal and state laws do not 
grant greater rights of entitlement to special education 
services under state law. Buchholtz v. lowa Dept. of 
Public Instruc., 315 N.W. 2d 789 (Iowa 1982). 


CONCLUSION 


The decision of the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals 
should be reversed.
