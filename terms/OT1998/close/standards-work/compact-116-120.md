## Holdings

### The first condition includes a proved functional ability-to-elect route

For a claim by one protected group, the first Gingles condition may be met either through the retained district-majority route or through rigorous proof that a lawful, geographically compact proposed district would provide the cohesive group dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its candidates of choice. Speculation, an isolated favorable election, near-majority population arithmetic or mere influence does not suffice; the relevant majority-bloc showing, causal connection and independent totality remain necessary.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this express modification and its limits.

The first condition links the challenged electoral arrangement to an achievable opportunity to elect. Numerical majority provides a predictable demonstration of that capacity, but an exclusive number can reject the same actual opportunity when dependable voting evidence proves it. Section 2 protects equal participation and opportunity, without guaranteeing proportional outcomes. The Court therefore expressly modifies the numerical exclusivity reflected in Gingles and Growe; it does not declare that earlier reservations already adopted this alternative.

De Grandy rejects numerical maximization and conclusive safe harbors while preserving actual local opportunity. Brooks fits proof to a different nomination barrier. Those decisions support the inquiry's purpose but do not themselves decide this new district-capacity rule. Dependability matters because a contingent alliance or favorable candidate alone cannot establish lost opportunity attributable to the system. Plaintiffs must prove cohesion and actual voting behavior within the proposed district and the relevant majority-bloc defeat in the challenged arrangement; successful crossover in one setting does not silently negate or establish bloc defeat in another. The ordinary special-circumstances qualification, including unopposed candidacy, remains.

The majority route survives. So do lawful district population and geography, constitutional limits and the separate totality inquiry. The Court prescribes no mathematical crossover formula, racial floor, proportional-election right or preferred political tradeoff, and decides no combination of distinct minority groups or influence-only entitlement. States retain lawful choice among effective arrangements; a court-compelled remedy still requires an established violation.

### The population figures do not resolve functional opportunity

The accepted 47.9% and 48.3% Hispanic citizen voting-age shares neither prove nor foreclose dependable ability to elect. The judgment resting exclusively on their being below fifty percent must be vacated for application of the changed rule, with all remaining statutory conditions and totality to be established before relief.

**Authority:** The same five Justices join Stone-Zsela's Part I and this application and remedy.

Population is not evidence by itself of the electorate's actual crossover behavior. The lower courts did not make the findings necessary under the functional route, and this Court cannot supply them. The proposed district must be lawful in population and geography; an 8.1% underpopulation is not approved merely because it appears in the calculation. If admissible proof does not establish dependable opportunity, the claim fails. If capacity is established, the remaining conditions and unequal opportunity under totality still require determination. Voinovich preserves the difference between state choice and judicially compelled remedy; De Grandy permits neither automatic liability nor a numerical safe harbor. The remand commands no map or voting system and guarantees no favorable result.

### The independent demographic and disclosure rulings stand

The credited proof of concrete post-census housing, occupancy and growth changes supports the present demographic findings under clear-error review. Excluding the survey disclosed on the eve of trial for surprise under the applicable disclosure rule was within the trial court's discretion; changing the capacity rule does not itself make either independent ruling erroneous.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia and Thomas do not join it unconditionally.

The 1990 census's presumptive accuracy does not require treating later population as unchanged. The April 1997 report supplied documented local changes under the burden the lower court applied; the excluded August report does not become the basis for affirmance. The Court establishes neither a universal heightened demographic burden nor automatic approval of local estimates. Commerce's national-enumeration holding supplies no contrary rule about these later local facts.

The plaintiffs disclosed the survey's existence late Friday and its data late Saturday before Monday trial. The earlier deposition reference did not disclose its basis. Local Rule CV-16(e) permitted good-cause supplementation, but the trial court could enforce disclosure and avoid surprise, particularly after excluding the defense update said to occasion the survey. The Court does not decide the survey's accuracy, whether it was proper rebuttal or unestablished factors in a separate exclusion framework. Preservation of the exclusion is not a substantive rejection of the survey's results.

Evidence previously immaterial under an exclusive numerical threshold may bear on the changed inquiry. The trial court may determine whether fair supplementation is needed under ordinary rules, with adequate notice and response; reopening is neither automatic nor forbidden. No new trial, mandatory study or automatic admission of the excluded survey is ordered.

## Law After Decision

A single protected group below a numerical majority may prove the first condition through dependable actual ability to elect in a lawful compact district. A majority-population route remains available, but neither route establishes every other condition or liability. Cohesion, relevant bloc-voting causation with applicable special circumstances, totality, independent constitutional limits and ordinary evidence procedures retain their force. Mere influence and speculative near-majority calculations do not create a right to districting. The present record establishes no functional success or completed violation.

## Holdings

### Local racial and sex classifications retain distinct constitutional standards

Municipal racial and ethnic promotion preferences require a supported compelling justification and narrow fit; general inequality, imbalance and good intentions alone are insufficient. Sex preferences separately require an actual important objective and a substantial relationship supported by an exceedingly persuasive justification, without importing racial strict scrutiny or a statistical-only proof rule.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I has all nine Justices for these distinct standards and for rejecting an invariably required prior liability judgment or egregious resistance. Eight Justices other than Scalia also accept the bounded affirmative proposition that supported local exclusion may permit fitted repair; Scalia reserves its full reach beyond the rejection necessary here.

Croson and Engineering Contractors govern local racial measures. Adarand preserved a distinct federal arrangement under Metro Broadcasting and did not transfer that latitude to municipalities. A government must connect the exclusion it identifies to the opportunity and remedy at issue. It must seriously consider reasonably available responsive neutral measures and explain their inadequacy, but need not try and exhaust every conceivable alternative. A prior adjudication or resistance to litigation is not an invariable condition of constitutional repair.

Kelley, Klinger, Virginia and Engineering Contractors require a separate inquiry into the government's actual sex-based justification. Racial evidence cannot be relabeled as a sex finding, and underrepresentation alone supplies no automatic justification. Neither a numerical minimum nor statistics as the exclusive form of proof is required. Subsidiary factual support remains distinct from independent constitutional assessment; summary judgment permits no adverse resolution of a genuine material factual dispute. The Court decides the challenged promotions under their actual standards, not the validity of every employment measure or future remedial program.

### The racial/ethnic preferences lack the required connected justification

The combined identified history and workforce evidence do not sufficiently connect the asserted exclusion to bypassing higher-ranked eligible candidates in the challenged promotion categories. The racial/ethnic invalidation is affirmed on this independent failure of supported justification.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt this application and the independent fit ground below.

The DOJ letter identifies serious entry barriers, and the internal ladder provides a possible route for continuing effects. The Court considers those facts together rather than demanding an admission or prior judgment. But a possible relationship and general departmental imbalance do not establish the needed connection to each present promotion opportunity and its relevant qualified feeder pool. The 1976 agreement's commitments likewise do not adjudicate every current remedial predicate. The City's account supplies no constitutional entitlement to parity in each rank.

Engineering Contractors requires independent assessment of the actual record; its separate four-Justice evidence conclusion did not settle this case. Here the five-Justice coalition finds the combined showing insufficient for the particular preferences. That conclusion does not deny the reality of earlier exclusion, deem all comparisons conclusive, or impose a statistical-only standard. It does not make the absence of a reproduced dataset a failure by the City or resolve a genuine material factual dispute against it. The legal connection required for these actual departures remains inadequately established on the record presented.

### The racial/ethnic preferences also fail independent narrow fit

Even assuming an adequate remedial objective, the record does not sufficiently explain why the actual rank departures, beneficiaries and allocation limits repair the identified exclusion. Qualification and score bands, duration and percentage limits do not independently establish the required fit.

**Authority:** The same five Justices join Kennedy's Part II and this independently sufficient racial ground.

Dallas used real neutral measures: validated testing, recruitment, tutoring, removed seniority points and a further paramedic-course opportunity. The Court does not treat those efforts as nonexistent or demand exhaustion of every imaginable alternative. Continued imbalance nevertheless does not by itself show that responsive neutral efforts are inadequate or that these preferences are properly calibrated to each rank's need.

A qualified candidate within a score band is eligible, but qualification does not establish equivalence with every higher-ranked candidate or justify racial allocation within that band. The fifty-percent annual cap, continuation periods and cessation at rank balance limit operation without supplying the missing relationship between remedy and supported injury. Croson and Engineering Contractors require that relationship, including attention to third-party burdens. Promotion delay is neither automatically trivial nor identical to a layoff. The Court imposes no fixed numerical ceiling or new formula; it holds that the actual limits here do not cure the independent fit deficiency. The conclusion leaves lawful neutral measures and future adequately supported, fitted remedies for their own review.

### The sex preferences lack an independently supported actual justification

The identified record does not adequately establish a connected sex-specific remedial justification for the challenged out-of-rank promotions. The sex component is affirmed under demanding intermediate scrutiny, independently of the racial evidence and narrow-fit conclusions.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III is joined by all eight other Justices. The Court does not independently decide sex fit after this predicate fails; Stone-Zsela's additional fit position is separate.

The 1972 letter's stated findings concern racial and ethnic entry barriers. The agreement's sex commitments and women's underrepresentation are relevant but do not themselves establish the actual sex-specific exclusion and promotion connection necessary to justify these preferences. Kelley and Klinger require an important objective genuinely supporting the classification; Virginia makes the government's justification demanding. None allows racial proof or parity goals to substitute for that showing.

Engineering Contractors applied a distinct sex inquiry and preserved the possibility of sufficient nonstatistical proof. Its competing assessment of combined women's-business evidence does not supply missing affirmative evidence in this employment record. The Court does not require strict scrutiny, every-alternative exhaustion, a numerical minimum or statistics alone. It affirms because the actual connected justification has not been sufficiently established, leaving any independently fitted future measure undecided.

## Holdings

### Federal-officer removal is proper for these collection actions

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal judge may remove a county collection action under the event-applicable §1442(a)(3) when the action has the required connection to official duties and the judge asserts a colorable federal defense. The work-linked levy and official-duty immunity defense establish those requirements here; the defense's eventual failure does not defeat removal.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer, has six direct votes for this rule, explanation and scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Mesa requires a federal defense in addition to federal office, and Willingham preserves a federal forum for a colorable official-duty defense without requiring the officer first to prevail on it. The County's demand arises from the compensation attributable to these judges' performance of federal judicial duties, and the ordinance makes engaging in the work the asserted basis of liability. Their claim that this exaction impermissibly burdens those duties supplies the necessary connection and a nonfrivolous federal defense. The United States' contrary nexus argument correctly separates personal tax liability from an official act, but gives insufficient weight to the particular work-linked ordinance and defense presented here. Federal employment and a compensation measure alone would not authorize removal of every personal obligation. This holding neither dispenses with the office nexus nor imports a later expansion of the removal statute. Characterizing the defense as colorable decides the forum, not whether federal law ultimately exempts the judges from this levy.

### The Tax Injunction Act does not bar the defensive adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1341 does not prevent a federal court with removal jurisdiction from adjudicating the federal defenses in a taxing authority's own collection suit. These collection actions seek payment, rather than anticipatory federal restraint of assessment, levy or collection by taxpayers.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II has all nine Justices' joins, with Scalia, Souter and Thomas accepting the Court's removal ruling as the predicate for this independent question.

**Controlling explanation:** The Act limits federal injunctions, suspensions and restraints of state-tax administration; a tax collector's suit to obtain payment is a different proceeding. Removal changes its tribunal without transforming the defendants' defenses into an affirmative taxpayer action. Rejecting the County's asserted liability would resolve its own claim, not grant a separately requested anticipatory injunction. Farm Credit distinguished substantive tax immunity from access to a federal forum and involved private associations seeking affirmative injunctive and declaratory relief with an adequate state remedy conceded. Its entity-specific rule does not decide these removed judicial-officer collection actions. The Court therefore need not find a universal federal-officer exemption from §1341, treat the United States' amicus appearance as party joinder, or supply a new finding about the adequacy of Alabama remedies. Taxpayer-initiated federal restraint remains subject to its own statutory limits.

### The levy operates as compensation taxation within §111

**Holding and operative rule:** The levy asserted in these collection actions operates as a tax on compensation for federal personal services within 4 U.S.C. §111, notwithstanding its occupational-license label. Section 111's consent does not authorize discrimination because of the compensation's federal source or a local requirement of permission to exercise federal office.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven directly adopt this compensation-tax rule on the jurisdictional predicate stated above.

**Controlling explanation:** Graves and §111 require attention to what the exaction does, rather than a constitutional exemption created by its state-law title. The County seeks a percentage of compensation attributable to services performed within its boundaries, with apportionment for work elsewhere. It has not established or exercised a local veto over the judges' performance of their duties. The unlawful-work language and exemptions for occupations paying other license charges are relevant to the ordinance's operation, but together do not convert the revenue demand presented here into an exercise of licensing control over federal adjudication. A compensation measure alone would not save every direct federal-function license; the actual demand and operation are decisive. Blaze preserves the distinction between legal incidence and indirect economic effect, while reserving direct federal burdens and actual instrumentalities; federal judges are not equated with independent contractors. The Court decides no hypothetical restriction preventing a judge from sitting and need not decide the County's alternative Buck Act theory. The separate Article III Compensation Clause question is not resolved by this intergovernmental-immunity holding.

### The renewed discrimination defense may be considered on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** Respondents may defend the existing favorable judgment on the renewed, record-supported source-discrimination ground without seeking greater relief, and the Court elects to consider that alternative here. Its earlier rejection and reported intermediate nonpursuit do not establish an automatic cross-appeal defect, jurisdictional bar or intentional waiver.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part IV has the same seven Justices' direct joins for this confined decision to reach the defense. The permission to consider an alternative is distinct from an obligation to consider every omitted ground.

**Controlling explanation:** X-Citement Video applies American Railway Express's rule allowing a prevailing party to support the same judgment on a ground in the record, including one rejected below, without enlarging its rights or reducing its opponent's rights under that judgment. Here the district court adjudicated discrimination and respondents expressly renewed it in their merits brief. They seek the same exemption judgment, not additional relief. The intermediate nonpursuit remains relevant to review discretion, but the established tax and comparison record permits decision without a new factual theory or findings. Zafiro's prudential treatment of an individual petitioner's unpursued claim does not erase this favorable-judgment posture. AT&T declined to construct an unadvanced independent theory; this one is actually presented. Lebron preserves bounded discretion over included legal arguments, not a duty to revive every abandoned ground. Dandridge's remand option and West's distinct alternative-ground remand likewise create no mandatory postponement of this established and renewed legal defense. The Court chooses present consideration without finding an intentional waiver or assuming that the lower rejection establishes the defense's substantive failure.

### The presented source-discrimination defense fails

**Holding and operative rule:** On this ordinance and record, taxing the respondent judges' compensation while exempting occupations subject to other specified state licensing charges does not discriminate because their compensation comes from a federal source. The intergovernmental-immunity judgment must be reversed, with remand consistent with that determination and the reservation of the separate Compensation Clause question.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies seven direct votes for the source-discrimination determination and limited remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Davis forbids unfavorable treatment attributable to the federal source of compensation and requires attention to the relevant comparison. This levy includes federal, state and local officeholders; the record establishes that state judges pay it. The exemption for occupations paying other state or county licensing charges does not, on this record, distinguish state compensation from federal compensation. Federal judges' inability personally to fall within that alternative-license category does not by itself establish the forbidden source classification. The Court independently rejects the presented comparison rather than presuming the district court correct or treating all possible exemptions as necessarily valid. No unadmitted workforce percentages, individual licensing facts or withholding practices supply the conclusion. An exemption favoring comparable state officials, an actual federal-source distinction or a genuine local restraint of judicial performance would present a different question. The separate Compensation Clause ground was accepted below but not reached by the en banc court and is not decided here. Remand leaves its proper treatment below without reopening discrimination, quantifying a tax obligation or directing collection regardless of that remaining ground.

## Holdings

### Actual sentencing deadlock requires a lawful nondeath sentence

When completed penalty deliberations end without a unanimous recommendation, §3594's otherwise clause requires the judge to impose a lawful nondeath sentence. For this death-resulting kidnapping the alternative is life without release. Section 3593(b)(2)(C) does not authorize another attempt to obtain death solely because the jury deadlocked.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I-A has eight direct votes from all Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition without joining that rationale.

Section 3593(e) requires a unanimous jury recommendation. Section 3594 directs the judge to impose recommended death or life without release and otherwise to impose an authorized lesser sentence. The Government's construction would confine otherwise to a unanimous lesser recommendation and use good-cause discharge to repeat the penalty hearing. That reading denies operative effect to the nondeath command when the jury cannot agree. Good-cause discharge has independent work, including disqualification, without treating failure of agreement itself as authorization for another death effort. Section 1201's offense-specific penalty fixes the available nondeath sentence; §3593's general reference to other lesser sentences creates none for this offense.

The Government's concession that this offense permits only death or life does not itself concede the deadlock question; the Court rejects the contrary new-jury construction on the statutes. No actual deadlock occurred. Reconsideration after reversible sentencing error under §3593(b)(2)(D) remains distinct and supports the present new-hearing mandate.

### There is no universal deadlock script, but a false sentencing choice must be corrected here

Neither the Eighth Amendment nor a supervisory rule requires a fixed deadlock-consequence instruction in every capital case. In this case, however, the preserved request for an accurate account of the lawful consequence required correction of the charge's materially misleading below-life alternative; refusing that correction requires a new penalty proceeding.

**Authority:** Part I-B's rejection of a universal script is unanimous. Part II's case-specific corrective duty and remedy are directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The Court does not adopt every constitutional or plain-error reason stated in the separate concurrence.

Jurors ordinarily should deliberate and make their own punishment choice. Congress prescribed no mandatory deadlock script, and Simmons's parole-ineligibility protection does not dictate one. But this jury was choosing punishment, unlike Shannon's ordinary guilt jury, and it had to receive an accurate description of the lawful alternatives. Section 1201 allowed only death or life without release. Form C expressly required unanimity for life, while the direction toward C or D on nonunanimity and D's lesser option could communicate that failure to agree on life would permit the judge to impose a punishment below life.

The initial unanimity direction, optional death, conscience protection and instruction not to consider the judge's later sentence are considered together. They do not correct the specific false statutory choice created by that combination. Instruction 5 and repeated references to its accurate core identified the need for correction, notwithstanding counsel's imperfect lesser-sentence wording. The Court does not require the request verbatim, a nonunanimous jury life verdict or a reasonable-doubt burden for the ultimate moral choice. The shared ruling addresses the preserved refusal and the actual charge. A new accurately instructed penalty proceeding is necessary if death remains sought; the ruling rests on objective directions and findings, not juror affidavits or an invented deliberation inquiry.

### Independent aggravator validity and cure are not resolved for the Court

The instruction disposition removes this death sentence. No majority resolves whether factors 3(B) and 3(C) were independently invalid or harmless: O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas find contextual validity; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg find the specific formulations defective; Scalia, Stone-Zsela and Breyer reserve validity. O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would find narrowly assumed drafting error harmless; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg reject that cure, while Stone-Zsela and Breyer reserve it.

**Authority:** These accounts identify the limits of the Court's decision, not additional factor holdings. No Marks synthesis supplies a validity or cure rule from the divided positions.

Any new proceeding remains governed by Tuilaepa's understandable relevant selection subjects and the requirement of a genuine lawful cure for an actually established invalid factor under Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa and §3595. Overlap is not automatically unconstitutional, relevant victim-impact evidence is not categorically forbidden, and remaining valid eligibility does not by itself cure an independent selection error. The Court creates no new factor test, appellate moral-reweighing power or permission to repeat misleading instructions.

## Holdings

### 1. The enacted fee language supplies no command repricing completed services

**Holding and operative rule:** The general action-and-award language in original 42 U.S.C. §1997e(d) does not clearly direct application of its new hourly ceiling to legal services completed before April 26, 1996. The separate express transition for prospective relief in PLRA §802 does not itself establish the fee amendment's treatment of every pending action; each provision's operation must be examined.

**Authority:** Thomas's Part I, directly joined by all nine Justices. The statement does not resolve whether the undertaking of representation or each service controls later work; that disagreement is addressed separately.

**Controlling explanation:** Kaiser and Landgraf make congressional temporal direction the starting question. Section 1997e(d) defines covered actions and limits awards, but its reference to an award does not alone identify the activity whose legal treatment changes. Section 802 expressly addresses prospective relief granted before, on or after enactment; §803's fee amendment contains no equivalent instruction. Lindh permits ordinary structural inference without a prescribed verbal formula, yet does not make every adjacent express transition an automatic direction for a provision with a different function. Neither the text nor that comparison commands a reduced hourly return for services already performed. The remaining temporal application therefore requires attention to the concrete services and the change the cap would make. This conclusion rejects neither all fee changes in pending cases nor Congress's authority to prescribe a different temporal rule within constitutional limits.

### 2. The new ceiling does not govern pre-enactment services

**Holding and operative rule:** The hourly ceiling in original §1997e(d)(3) does not govern legal services completed before the PLRA's April 26, 1996 enactment, even if the fee request or award follows enactment. Those services remain subject to the compensation law governing when they were performed.

**Authority:** Thomas's Part II, directly joined by all nine Justices at this concrete level of application; broader methodological agreement is not inferred.

**Controlling explanation:** The hourly compensation provision addresses the legal work for which reimbursement is sought. Reducing the permissible rate for completed work would change the legal compensation consequences of services already supplied. The later act of issuing an award does not transform those completed services into future conduct. Landgraf protects completed events absent sufficient temporal direction; its application here turns on actual legal work, not the old prison conduct or the mere pendency of litigation. Bradley remains valid in its actual litigation-fee setting, but does not establish that every later fee statute may reprice completed services. The statutory incentive and the earlier fee arrangements support this treatment without establishing an immutable contract for future rates. Requests spanning January 1 through June 30, 1996 must therefore separate earlier services from later ones. The Court does not determine compensable hours, prevailing-party status, an amount, or the treatment of a vested final fee judgment.

### 3. Later services are subject to the ceiling despite the older action

**Holding and operative rule:** Original §1997e(d)(3)'s hourly ceiling governs services performed after enactment in these already-pending prisoner actions. On remand, the district court must allocate compensation by when the legal work was actually performed, retaining the prior regime for completed pre-enactment work and applying the enacted ceiling to later work.

**Authority:** Thomas's Part III, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Stevens and Ginsburg dissent from this component. No Marks reconstruction supplies the rule.

**Controlling explanation:** A fee statute regulating compensation for later services operates on that later work even though counsel entered the litigation before enactment. The original representation, decree and monitoring-fee orders do not establish an unalterable statutory rate for all work yet to be performed. Counsel's continuing professional obligations deserve consideration, but do not convert every later service into a completed pre-enactment act. This conclusion assumes neither that counsel could withdraw as of right nor that any withdrawal occurred. The new hourly ceiling supplies the compensation rule for work after its enactment; the earlier filing date alone does not remove that work from the statute. Landgraf requires examining the provision's actual operation, and Lindh's provision-specific habeas construction supplies no universal exemption for pending proceedings. The Court accordingly reverses the Sixth Circuit's exemption of later services. It neither changes the underlying decrees nor decides the unaddressed constitutional objections, privately contracted fee entitlements, other distinct fee restrictions, or a statute carrying materially different express temporal direction.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Landgraf v. USI Film Products:** Applies the concrete inquiry into temporal direction and changed consequences for completed services. The existing general-framework holding retains its prior force and original coalition; this decision does not turn service-date agreement into new assent to every methodological proposition.
- **Rivers v. Roadway Express:** Preserves provision-specific construction; a statute's general remedial purpose does not supply retrospective reach for a particular amendment.
- **Kaiser Aluminum & Chemical Corp. v. Bonjorno:** Applies the priority of enacted temporal direction; its fee discussion does not establish an automatic rule choosing either action filing or fee-award date.
- **Bradley v. School Board of Richmond:** Retains its actual fee-legislation result; distinguishes it from using a new hourly ceiling to reduce compensation for already completed work. It is not overruled.
- **Lindh v. Murphy:** Preserves ordinary structural construction and its particular AEDPA application-date rule. The PLRA provisions' different functions prevent automatic transfer of that result to all fees in old prisoner actions.
- **Hughes Aircraft Co. v. United States ex rel. Schumer:** Preserves examination of the real legal change rather than a jurisdictional, procedural or collateral label. No new rule about its distinct private-enforcement facts is announced.

## Law After Decision

Before this decision the disputed pending-case application of the original PLRA hourly ceiling remained open. Effective June 21, 1999, the hourly-ceiling temporal rule requires service-date allocation in this setting: prior law for completed pre-enactment services and the new ceiling for later services, even in an older action. The original text's independent fee-entitlement and monetary-judgment provisions retain their own predicates and are not newly construed. No cited precedent is overruled; no final fee award is reopened.
