## Holdings

### This interruption does not establish deprivation of occupational liberty

**Holding and operative rule:** Liberty to pursue a profession does not guarantee freedom from government interruption on every occasion for providing professional services. This search-related interruption of consultation, with no prohibition on practice, impairment of professional qualifications or supported consequential interference with Gabbert's legal practice, does not establish deprivation of his Fourteenth Amendment occupational liberty, even assuming the prosecutors deliberately timed the search to obstruct consultation.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this occupational-merits portion of Stone-Zsela's Court opinion and its record-limited application. It is the sufficient ground for reversal; no combination of separate rationales is required.

**Controlling explanation:** Roth recognizes liberty to pursue a vocation, not a personal guarantee of every opportunity to perform a professional service. Collins requires an identified constitutional deprivation; its separate workplace-omission holding does not itself answer affirmative interference with a lawyer. The reported record supplies no evidence of adverse effects on Gabbert's income, reputation, clientele or professional qualifications. It separately supplies no real evidence or allegation of substantial prejudice to the client. Those descriptions do not erase the interrupted consultations or establish lawful search execution. They identify the limited professional interference shown here. Gabbert's strongest argument is that deliberately obstructing consultation at the moment counsel is needed can injure professional activity without lasting financial harm. The Court does not reject that possibility categorically, but the asserted vocation guarantee and this record do not establish the professional deprivation claimed. Duration alone is not decisive, and permanent exclusion, lost fees, reputational harm and client prejudice do not become mandatory elements for every occupational-liberty case. Soldal and James Daniel Good preserve genuinely independent constitutional protections; Albright supplies no controlling broader displacement rule or palpable-consequence threshold. Lewis's emergency-pursuit standard does not govern this planned investigation, and Haddle's statutory employment-injury rule does not create a constitutional guarantee of uninterrupted practice. The Court decides Gabbert's own occupational claim, not his client's constitutional rights.

**Confined source explanation and authority:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer also join the explanation that, to the extent Gabbert objects that the search itself was unreasonable because of its execution or timing, the Fourth Amendment supplies the governing standard under Graham. Lanier preserves that actual-coverage rule without excluding every independent due-process injury. This is a distinction of constitutional sources, not a second sufficient ground that absorbs the entire occupational allegation; the occupational interest is resolved by the preceding holding. Stevens does not join this additional case-specific explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not hold the search lawful, extinguish a genuinely independent protected interest, foreclose preserved privilege or attorney-client objections, or decide the separately pleaded Fourth Amendment claim. It creates no immunity based on prosecutorial office or possession of a warrant. Because the asserted occupational deprivation is not established, the Court does not decide whether that asserted right was clearly established when the prosecutors acted. No mandatory merits-first sequence, absolute-immunity ruling or new client-right holding is adopted.

## Precedent Treatment

- Board of Regents v. Roth supplies the recognition of vocational liberty; it does not establish an entitlement to every occasion for professional service.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights, decided February 26, 1992, supplies the need for an actual constitutional deprivation. Its workplace-omission rules are not extended automatically to affirmative investigative interference.
- Graham v. Connor supplies the specific-protection inquiry for the search objection. United States v. Lanier, decided March 31, 1997, preserves that inquiry while rejecting categorical exclusion of independent due-process injuries.
- Albright v. Oliver, decided January 24, 1994, retains its fractured judgment without a controlling combined rationale. No broader displacement rule or palpable-consequence requirement is extracted from it.
- Soldal v. Cook County and United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property preserve genuinely independent protections despite possible constitutional overlap. City of West Covina v. Perkins, decided January 13, 1999, preserves that distinction in the criminal-warrant setting without deciding occupational liberty.
- County of Sacramento v. Lewis, decided May 26, 1998, remains confined to its supported emergency-pursuit setting; its culpability rule does not decide this planned investigation.
- Haddle v. Garrison, decided December 14, 1998, remains a statutory witness-conspiracy injury decision, not a constitutional entitlement to uninterrupted employment or professional practice.

## Law After Decision

The described interruption does not establish Gabbert's claimed occupational deprivation. The ruling creates neither a categorical rule that every temporary interference is harmless nor a requirement of permanent exclusion or financial loss. An objection to this search as unreasonable belongs under its specific constitutional standard, while a genuinely independent professional deprivation requires its own analysis. The search claim and immunity law are otherwise unchanged; no precedent is overruled.

## Holdings

### I. The plea did not waive privilege concerning reserved quantity

**Holding and operative rule:** A guilty plea does not itself waive the privilege against compelled self-incrimination concerning disputed sentencing facts while testimony creates a genuine risk of additional criminal punishment. This accepted plea, its limited admissions and its trial-silence warning did not waive protection concerning expressly reserved quantity.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Estelle preserves protection against sentencing incrimination. Brown prevents a voluntary witness from disclosing a subject selectively while refusing relevant cross-examination; it does not make a plea's limited factual inquiry consent to compelled testimony on unresolved quantity. Rule 11 permits inquiry into a plea's factual basis, and refusal to supply necessary information may leave that basis inadequate. Here the court accepted the plea and reserved quantity. Neither awareness of possible punishment nor admission of the offense establishes the missing waiver. The plea and actual voluntary admissions remain usable. When sentence is fixed, conviction final and further testimony presents no remaining adverse criminal consequence, the privilege has no continuing incriminating risk to protect; finality alone does not extinguish another genuine criminal exposure. Immunity-backed testimony is not decided.

**Precedent treatment:** Estelle's sentencing protection and Brown's scope-based testimonial-waiver rule are applied without making every plea statement inadmissible or establishing a permanent privilege.

### II. Protected silence cannot prove the crime's circumstances at sentencing

**Holding and operative rule:** A sentencing court may not draw an adverse factual inference from a defendant's protected silence in determining the circumstances and details of the crime. The Government must establish disputed quantity through lawful affirmative evidence; permission for judicial sentencing findings does not authorize this source of proof.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: six Justices including Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Griffin prevents using protected silence to supply criminal proof, and Estelle preserves the privilege when punishment remains unresolved. Their protection cannot depend on whether admitted guilt precedes determination of the crime's remaining punitive facts. Baxter's civil inference and Woodard's discretionary-clemency setting differ from this initial criminal sentencing determination. Witte and Watts permit certain sentencing information within lawful authority but retain independent constitutional limits; Dunnigan permits consequences for established willful material perjury, not an inference from giving no testimony. This holding concerns the source of proof, not whether every sentencing fact is an element or must go to a jury. It does not bar voluntary statements. Remorse and actual acceptance-of-responsibility claims remain undecided. The ruling does not prevent denial of an affirmative acceptance reduction for failure to carry a lawful burden; it creates no new burden or entitlement and decides no particular reduction ruling.

**Precedent treatment:** Griffin's no-inference protection is applied to proof of crime facts at criminal sentencing; Estelle supplies continuing protection. Baxter and Woodard are distinguished by their civil settings. Witte, Watts and Dunnigan retain their stated lawful operations.

### III. Other potentially sufficient evidence does not establish harmlessness here

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that this constitutional error did not contribute to the affected sentencing determination. Because the judge expressly relied on silence in crediting disputed quantity testimony, the finding cannot be preserved merely because the remaining evidence could support it; the sentence must be vacated and quantity reconsidered without that inference.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same six Justices as Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** Chapman asks whether error contributed, not whether an appellate court could independently assemble sufficient evidence. The judge used silence to support the disputed testimony and indicated that a contrary privilege ruling could warrant closer examination. No independent conclusive quantity determination unaffected by that use is established. Wright independently requires a lawful sentencing basis established in the sentencing findings rather than a substituted appellate ground; its Guidelines holding is not converted into a universal constitutional rule. The District Court must reassess quantity from affirmative evidence admissible under the governing sentencing standards. The Court neither declares the witnesses unreliable nor guarantees a lower sentence. The pleas and admitted offenses remain undisturbed, and any lawful consequential sentencing adjustments remain for remand.

**Precedent treatment:** Chapman supplies the contribution-based constitutional harmlessness standard. Wright's existing lawful-basis requirement is preserved and applied at its actual sentencing scope.

## Precedent Treatment

Estelle and Brown govern continuing privilege and actual waiver. Griffin now forbids adverse use of protected silence to determine crime facts at sentencing; its established trial operation remains. Baxter and Woodard do not authorize the present criminal inference. Witte, Watts and Dunnigan preserve lawful sentencing findings and consequences without removing independent constitutional safeguards. Chapman governs harmlessness; Wright retains its separate sentencing-basis requirement. Jones and Monge supply no quantity classification or universal mandatory-minimum rule here. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

The privilege survives this plea concerning reserved punitive facts, and protected silence cannot supply adverse proof of the crime's circumstances at sentencing. Express reliance requires relief unless the Government establishes constitutional harmlessness. Lawful sentencing allocation, proof requirements and voluntary-statement use otherwise remain; no universal jury rule, mandatory-minimum classification or right to an acceptance reduction is adopted.

## Holdings

### Formal process and the statutory pleading events govern the original removal period

**Holding and operative rule:** Informal receipt of a courtesy copy before formal service, waiver or legally equivalent process has subjected the defendant to the court's authority does not by itself begin §1446(b)'s original-pleading removal period. Under its first branch, thirty days run when that process requirement and receipt, through service or otherwise, of the initial pleading setting forth the claim for relief have both occurred; the separate branch runs thirty days after service of summons if the initial pleading has then been filed in court and is not required to be served on the defendant, whichever period is shorter.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court has the complete seven-Justice coalition identified in Opinion Topology. All seven directly adopt both statutory branches, their limits and the application to Murphy's removal.

**Controlling explanation:** Receipt of useful information and an obligation imposed by court process are different events. Section 1446(b) operates within the system in which formal service, waiver or a legally equivalent process subjects the defendant to judicial authority. The phrase permitting receipt otherwise than through service has work to do: pleading information may arrive separately from formal process. If the complaint arrives first, later process starts the first branch; if process precedes the required pleading receipt, that branch awaits receipt. The separate summons branch applies on its own stated conditions and does not invariably require actual receipt or service of the complaint. The dissent correctly observes that Congress expressly referred to receipt in the first branch and summons service in the second. Reading the paragraph as a whole, however, those different information events operate within the formal-process framework, rather than making an unsolicited advance copy a compulsory forum-election deadline. Henderson supports attention to service's actual procedural role without answering this statute or erasing its commands. Caterpillar preserves prospective removal requirements; no completed-trial cure is invoked here. Murphy had no obligation to appear or face default when the fax arrived, and its removal was within thirty days after the subsequent formal service. The notice was therefore timely under the statutory rule, not an equitable extension. This construction does not hold that Congress could never enact an earlier deadline or impose a universal clear-statement requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both original thirty-day periods and the shorter-period direction remain operative. A validly begun period is not extended, and service-evasion gamesmanship is not excused. The Court decides no unpresented waiver's precise timing or other process sequence, does not prohibit removal before service, and does not apply the separate later-removability paragraph. Timeliness supplies no subject-matter jurisdiction. Other preserved remand grounds, contract merits and fees are not decided.

## Precedent Treatment

- Henderson v. United States, decided May 20, 1996, supplies source-specific attention to service's procedural function. Its Suits in Admiralty Act holding does not itself define this removal trigger, repeal other service statutes or authorize a discretionary extension here.
- Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis, decided December 10, 1996, preserves the prospective duty to comply with removal requirements. Its bounded protection of an actually jurisdictionally sound completed judgment is distinguished; this case has no such judgment and applies no cure.
- Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca, decided December 5, 1995, and Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., decided June 3, 1996, retain their covered-remand-review boundaries. Review here arose from the certified denial of remand, not appellate reconsideration of a district-court remand order protected by §1447(d).
- Omni Capital International, Ltd. v. Rudolf Wolff & Co. preserves the distinct role of authorized service in establishing personal jurisdiction. It does not independently fix the statutory time to elect removal.

## Law After Decision

A courtesy complaint copy received before process does not itself start the original removal clock. The first branch requires both the legally relevant process status and pleading receipt; the summons branch operates independently when its filed-pleading and no-service-required conditions are met, with the shorter statutory period controlling. The rule preserves timely statutory compliance without confusing the removal opportunity, duty to appear and subject-matter jurisdiction. No precedent is overruled.

## Holdings

### Vehicle probable cause reaches this detached passenger-owned purse

**Holding and operative rule.** When police have probable cause to search an automobile for drugs, the automobile exception permits searching a detached passenger-owned purse found inside the car if it can conceal the drugs. Known passenger ownership does not alone remove that container from the object-based search; this holding does not authorize searching the passenger's person or decide the treatment of an effect worn or carried on the person.

**Authority.** O'Connor's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, counting the author within that five-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation.** Labron applies the automobile exception without a separate exigency requirement and measures its container scope by the object sought. Ross and Acevedo likewise permit a properly supported search of places and containers capable of concealing that object. Wellons recognizes a personal privacy interest in luggage but preserves otherwise valid search exceptions. Those propositions coexist: a protected interest does not defeat an established justification for searching it. Houghton's purse was a container within the searched vehicle and could conceal the drugs. Its acknowledged ownership does not change that capacity or establish an exception to the rule. Di Re's protection of the passenger's person remains distinct; the Court does not equate a detached purse with the passenger's body. Nor does it presume Houghton guilty or engaged in a common criminal enterprise. Young's syringe and drug-use admission supplied the conceded vehicle probable cause; that existing authority justifies the initial purse intrusion. The false-name discrepancy and later discoveries play no role in justifying the initial intrusion. Searches of clothing or attached personal effects remain outside the decision.

## Precedent Treatment

- Labron/Kilgore is applied without modification: actual vehicle probable cause supplies object-based container authority without an additional exigency requirement.
- Ross and Acevedo are applied: a capable container within a justified automobile search is not exempt solely because a passenger owns it.
- Wellons is preserved: separate personal privacy and an applicable search exception are distinct questions.
- Di Re is distinguished: authority over a vehicle does not by itself authorize a bodily search.
- Knowles retains its rule that a citation alone supplies no general search authority; the independent probable cause conceded here presents a different ground.
- Carter/Johns retains its personal-right, justification and causal limits; the Court makes no new independent-source finding.

## Law After Decision

The existing automobile exception reaches this detached passenger purse. The four dissenting Justices support the same proposed ownership qualification and suppression judgment; that qualification is not adopted. Known ownership neither eliminates personal privacy nor, by itself, defeats the applicable exception. Person searches and attached effects remain separate questions.

## Holdings

### The proposed monitoring class does not satisfy the applicable classwide-relief requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 23(b)(2), together with Rule 23(a), requires a supported showing that generally applicable defendant conduct makes final injunctive or corresponding declaratory relief appropriate for the proposed class; common allegations of tobacco conduct and a shared request for monitoring do not establish that showing by themselves. This proposal fails because the materially different exposure, addiction-related, causation, monitoring-entitlement and limitations issues cannot be resolved by the proposed classwide ruling while preserving meaningful individual proof and substantive defenses; the Court imports neither Rule 23(b)(3)'s predominance and superiority requirements nor a categorical prohibition based on individual differences.

**Authority:** Breyer's unanimous Opinion of the Court controls the complete rule, application, limits and remedy. All nine participating Justices directly adopt it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the requirements of the class device actually requested. Falcon requires examination of the applicable prerequisites. Castano requires an actual account of governing law and proof, and Amchem confirms that the attractiveness of aggregate relief cannot replace Rule 23's conditions. Their Rule 23(b)(3) applications do not create a predominance or superiority test under (b)(2). Here a common product or marketing campaign does not establish that every smoker needs the same screening, presents the same risk or faces the same limitations response. The stated differences concern entitlement to monitoring and preservation of substantive defenses, not merely convenient administration of an already established common remedy. Calling differential personal medical treatment one injunction cannot itself answer those questions. The proposal therefore lacks the cohesion necessary for the classwide relief it seeks on this record. Proponents correctly maintain that individual differences do not invariably defeat a common monitoring program. That proposition does not cure these demonstrated entitlement problems. Nor would an unexplained appeal to predominance or management difficulty suffice to deny a different supported (b)(2) proposal. The Court adopts no additional universal indivisibility requirement beyond the actual rule, decides no new medical fact or Pennsylvania claim element, and does not design a replacement monitoring program.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Medical-monitoring classes are not categorically barred, and tobacco deception receives no immunity. Rule 23(a)'s requirements remain applicable, but the Court makes no independent adequacy-conflict holding drawn from Amchem's different settlement facts. It decides no separate named claim, individual standing question, new limitations rule or medical-necessity rule. The class ruling creates no assumed jurisdiction over individual substantive merits. A different proposal would require an available procedural route, appropriate actual representatives and satisfaction of every applicable prerequisite; neither its availability nor its result is decided.

## Holdings

### Rule 501 does not recognize the proposed protective-function privilege

**Holding and operative rule:** Information obtained by Secret Service personnel while protecting the President is not, on this record, protected from federal grand-jury testimony by the asserted protective-function privilege. Because the Court rejects recognition, it establishes neither an absolute or qualified privilege nor a special test for overcoming one; other lawful objections and concrete security accommodations retain their own requirements.

**Authority:** Stevens's unanimous Opinion of the Court controls the complete rule, reasoning, reservations and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court recognizes the exceptional importance of presidential protection. Rule 501 nevertheless requires a reason-and-experience basis for withholding a class of relevant evidence. Trammel supplies that inquiry; Jaffee shows that principled common-law development remains possible, while requiring attention to the confidential relationship and its practical justification. The proposed protection extends beyond confidential communications to ordinary observations acquired through proximity. In a federal investigation or prosecution, its exception permits disclosure when an observation or statement, at the time made, gave reasonable grounds to believe a felony had been, was being, or would be committed. That contemporaneous understanding may exclude observations whose significance appears only later; it does not establish a predictable protected relationship. Director Merletti's declaration and former President Bush's letter support the prediction that compelled testimony could impair confidence and access. The Court considers that concern seriously, but the supported prediction and proposed scope do not justify this evidentiary class. No adjudicated impairment is established, and the Court does not make an actual security failure a prerequisite for every new privilege. University of Pennsylvania counsels against creating institutional confidentiality from insufficiently supported chilling predictions. Swidler protects an established attorney-client relationship; it does not automatically extend to operational observations. Neither it nor Jaffee is subjected to routine investigative-need balancing. Once recognition fails, an extraordinary-need test would supply an advisory rule for a nonexistent privilege. Clinton's accommodation of concrete official duties supports preserving independently lawful security measures, not categorical evidentiary immunity.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision concerns this federal grand-jury claim and record. It decides no classified-information, state-secrets, executive-privilege or distinct constitutional-security claim; no finding about a particular agent's knowledge, criminal conduct, actual impaired access, waiver or future protective arrangement is made.

**Holding and operative rule:** California's insurance-specific rule prevents an insurer from forfeiting coverage for late notice unless it proves actual prejudice and is saved by ERISA §514(b)(2)(A) as applied to this insured policy. The rule informs Ward's federal benefits action under §502(a)(1)(B); it creates no independent state enforcement remedy and establishes neither absence of prejudice nor entitlement to benefits.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's unanimous Opinion of the Court controls this holding and the agency holding below, including their qualifications and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the insurance-saving clause to the rule's operation. Metropolitan Life preserves regulation of the insurer-insured bargain. Notice-prejudice restricts an insurer's forfeiture defense and thus directly governs that bargain. The common-sense insurance inquiry and the McCarran-Ferguson considerations support that conclusion: the rule is directed to insurance and integral to the policy relationship. Those considerations are guideposts, not three indispensable conjunctive elements; the Court need not decide whether this rule independently transfers or spreads risk. Pilot Life rejects a competing state enforcement remedy, whereas Ward invokes ERISA's own benefits action. The written deadline cannot nullify a substantive insurance rule that Congress saved. Travelers and De Buono preserve federal control of plan administration but do not erase that express reservation. John Hancock likewise requires respect for compatible insurance obligations while preserving genuinely conflicting federal commands; no command requiring absolute forfeiture is shown here. UNUM's uniformity argument therefore does not justify preemption of this policy term. The record still requires the actual-prejudice inquiry. Self-funded plans, the deemer clause's distinct protection and other timing rules are not decided.

### Mandatory administrative agency cannot rest on a no-relation premise

**Holding and operative rule:** A state rule assigning the employer an insurer-agency role because it performs benefit-plan administration directly connects with that administration under ERISA §514(a); the Ninth Circuit's contrary no-relation premise and contingent instruction cannot stand. The categorical administrative reassignment may not be used here, but the decision does not preempt every ordinary agency principle or resolve a properly presented voluntary or fact-specific agency undertaking.

**Controlling explanation:** Travelers and De Buono distinguish remote economic effects from direct commands to administration. A mandatory rule making an employer the insurer's agent for notice and claims processing reallocates administrative responsibility itself. That is materially different from the saved content of an insurance contract. Ward disputes the breadth of Elfstrom and invokes ordinary agency principles; the Court accordingly does not declare that every application of California agency law is an irrebuttable employer-agency rule. Nor does it find actual authorization, apparent authority or their absence. Ward did not press insurance saving for this agency theory, so the notice-prejudice holding cannot silently supply that distinct argument. Reversal corrects the legal premise, not the disputed agency facts. If actual prejudice makes agency material, the lower courts must assess any properly presented theory under the corrected rule, giving the policy's written-authorization provision and the legal source of the asserted agency their proper effect. No general federal agency code or automatic ineffectiveness of notice to the employer follows.

- Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Massachusetts preserves substantive insurance-contract regulation; the saving clause covers this forfeiture limitation.
- Pilot Life Insurance Co. v. Dedeaux excludes a competing state enforcement scheme; Ward's §502 benefits action presents a distinct question.
- Travelers and De Buono distinguish indirect economic effects from direct regulation of benefits or administration; that distinction separates the two state rules here.
- John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust preserves compatible insurance obligations while requiring attention to actual federal conflict.
- Hughes Aircraft Co. v. Jacobson distinguishes entitlement, administrative function and remedy; it supplies neither automatic benefits nor an additional state cause of action.

## Holdings

### Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation

**Holding and operative rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing Congress's actual delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation: courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and the delegation's boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The Court of International Trade's duty to decide on its own record does not repeal that legal framework; agency labels and notice and comment alone do not establish authority or validity.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Parts I–III are joined by all eight Associates and control this complete framework.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes governing law from judicial determination of facts. Sections 1500(b) and 1502(a) and the tariff delegation provisions authorize implementing rules within their scope. Section 2640(a)(1)'s own-record review does not assign all substantive tariff lawmaking exclusively to courts, and §10.11's preservation of judicial review does not require disregarding a lawful regulation. Chevron supplies the division of responsibility; NationsBank permits reasonable gap filling within actual authority. MCI and NCUA require courts to enforce enacted limits before deferring. The importer correctly insists that review remain independent on those limits and effective on the facts. That does not justify categorically withholding the weight due a sufficiently authoritative delegated rule. Conversely, recognizing that framework does not make every Customs interpretation lawful. Informal classification letters and later administrative doctrines are not decided.

### This regulation's statutory fit and actual application require the corrected inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment must be vacated because categorical nondeference left unresolved whether the regulation reasonably respects the tariff's incidental-assembly allowance and lawfully applies to this process. On remand the courts must enforce the enacted conditions, examine the regulation's actual introductory terms and examples, and resolve relevant application questions under the proper review standards; neither regulatory validity nor the claimed exemption is established in advance.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part IV is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, seven votes. Stevens and Ginsburg do not join this holding or remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The tariff requires United States fabricated components exported ready for assembly without further fabrication, retention of physical identity, and no advancement in value or improvement abroad except through assembly and incidental operations, including cleaning, lubricating and painting. The allowance reduces duties by qualifying component cost or value, with proportional treatment for specific or compound rates; it does not exempt the whole article. Regulation §10.16(b) permits incidental operations before, during or after assembly, including ordinary final pressing and folding. Section 10.16(c) addresses a significant nonassembly process whose primary purpose is fabrication, completion or physical or chemical improvement, or a significant process unrelated to assembly. Its chemical-treatment examples, including permapressing, do not erase those introductory alternatives. Timing after sewing alone settles nothing, and ordinary pressing is not necessarily resin-activating permapressing. The significance, purpose, relationship to assembly and new characteristics of the actual process bear on the statutory-regulatory boundary, not merely on duty arithmetic. NationsBank's framework does not answer those unfinished questions. AT&T distinguishes general authority from particular validity; NFFE distinguishes available discretion from its actual lawful exercise without requiring a new agency proceeding here. Both questions were granted, but that scope does not make this intertwined inquiry complete. The lower courts must undertake it without the erroneous categorical premise. The Court commands no new evidentiary hearing, burden, deadline or finding that existing litigation papers are missing.

- NationsBank of North Carolina v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co. permits reasonable administrative classification within statutory limits.

## Holdings

### A primary barrier must be tested against actual nomination opportunity

**Holding and operative rule:** A Section 2 challenge to a primary-runoff barrier turns on protected voters' equal opportunity in the affected nomination or election process, supported political cohesion and majority bloc voting in the affected electoral settings, and the totality of circumstances. It does not require proof that every additional minority-preferred nominee would win the general election, and neither candidate race, the rarity of runoffs among all primaries nor a conclusive statewide offset of winners can replace the inquiry into the affected voters' actual opportunity.

**Authority:** Souter's Parts I–II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer, control the complete statutory rule, application and remand, six votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Gingles requires proof of political cohesion and majority bloc voting in the affected electoral settings, with the necessary causal connection to the challenged barrier and a separate totality inquiry; Voinovich keeps that burden with the challengers. The inquiry must fit the barrier challenged. De Grandy rejects automatic liability and numerical safe harbors alike; Holder requires a legally and practically grounded comparison. The lower court used the small proportion of primaries reaching runoffs and a statewide net of Black and white winners to reject the relevant bloc-voting condition. It also required proof that additional plurality nominees would win general elections. Those premises constrain the very findings invoked as independent reasons to affirm. The Court therefore cannot detach the failure-of-proof conclusion from the legal errors. Candidate race is not necessarily voter preference, and gains elsewhere do not conclusively answer a local opportunity loss. The 56 adverse and 29 favorable candidate-race reversals establish neither a number of unlawfully lost offices nor negligible harm. SCLC preserves supported subsidiary facts but permits correction when a mistaken legal standard compromises the determination. The State's interest in broadly supported nominees and resistance to vote-splitting deserves concrete consideration. It does not make an extreme hypothetical plurality winner a conclusive answer. Conversely, polarization alone does not establish a violation or require plurality. The lower courts must reconsider on the existing record and any supplementation independently allowed by ordinary procedure; no new trial or particular study is automatically required.

### The supported purpose findings sustain constitutional rejection

**Holding and operative rule:** A constitutional challenge requires proof of discriminatory purpose attributable to the challenged enactment, assessed from the whole relevant record rather than electoral outcomes alone. The supported findings distinguishing the failed 1963 proposal from the enacted 1964 measure are not clearly erroneous; the historical racial climate and earlier motives do not compel a contrary purpose finding for the later enactment.

**Authority:** Souter's Part III is unanimous and controls this ground and the independent causation ground below.

**Controlling explanation:** Arlington Heights makes historical sequence, contemporaneous evidence and decisionmaking context relevant. The trial court considered those matters, including the committees and the roles of the proponents, and credited witnesses with firsthand involvement. Hunter does not make every related measure inherit the purpose of an earlier failed bill. The Court adopts no requirement of a direct admission and no categorical preference for retrospective testimony over contemporaneous evidence. The stated findings nonetheless permit the constitutional judgment. This inquiry remains separate from the statutory results errors; the Court need not resolve the full reach of Fifteenth Amendment dilution coverage because the claims fail even assuming that coverage.

### The independent same-enactment finding also defeats causation

**Holding and operative rule:** If discriminatory purpose was a substantial or motivating factor, the defenders bear the burden of showing that the same measure would have been enacted without that factor. The supported independent finding that the 1964 measure would have passed for the credited anti-manipulation and election-administration reasons satisfies that burden here and independently sustains constitutional affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Hunter and Mt. Healthy distinguish a motivating factor from a measure that would have been adopted without it. The trial court made the latter finding separately; it does not depend on the statutory denominators or proof of later general-election success. The challengers' historical evidence does not establish clear error in that supported alternative. This ground does not make mixed motives invariably lawful, transfer the same-decision burden to plaintiffs or permit benign present effects to cure a purpose violation.

## Holdings

### An illegal gratuity requires an identifiable official-act link

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 201(c)(1)(A) requires proof that a thing of value was given for or because of an identifiable official act performed or to be performed by the official. Officeholding, general goodwill and the donor's interests before the agency do not alone satisfy that connection; the Government need not prove bribery's corrupt exchange, an express agreement or direct evidence of the link.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court controls the complete construction, error and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court gives the statute's relational language its ordinary work. Its object is an official act within §201(a)(3), not the recipient's status. A reward for an act already performed can qualify without purchasing that act; a future act can also qualify, but future timing does not eliminate the need for an act. The Government invokes anticorruption purposes and the distinction from bribery. Those points explain why gratuity need not include a corrupt bargain, but do not convert it into a general ban on gifts to officials. Aguilar illustrates the need to preserve each offense's operative object, without importing its separate obstruction elements. Holloway likewise requires offense-specific analysis. Here the jury was expressly allowed to convict without identifying an act connected to the gifts. Reading the statutory words elsewhere in the charge did not cure that contrary permission. Gaudin preserves the jury's responsibility to find the enacted elements. Circumstantial proof and multiple act-linked gratuities remain possible. The Court gives no comprehensive definition of every official act and does not legalize gifts prohibited by ethics rules, campaign-finance law or another statute.

### The supported harmless-error argument does not sustain this conviction

**Holding and operative rule:** The error is not harmless on the Government's offered ground that pending regulatory interests and repetition of statutory words necessarily supplied the missing act-linked jury determination. The Count One reversal and lawful new-trial remand therefore stand; this is no rule that every error concerning an element is structural or that the Government lacks all sufficient evidence.

**Controlling explanation:** The explicit instruction allowed a conviction for cultivating goodwill or honoring official status. The vice-president's business role, company reimbursement and two regulatory interests may support act-linked proof, but do not make that inference unavoidable. His friendship with the Secretary confirms that motive was a real issue without creating a friendship defense. The jury's general verdict cannot be treated as a finding of an element it was told it need not find. Gaudin protects that actual allocation of decisionmaking while leaving other harmless-error settings unresolved. The Court conducts no new exhaustive sufficiency review. Indictment sufficiency was not granted and is not decided; any district-court power to consider a properly available indictment question remains intact.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Aguilar preserves the offense's required object; its obstruction knowledge and nexus rules are not imported into gratuity.
- United States v. Gaudin preserves jury determination of the enacted elements; it does not make all errors concerning elements structural.
- Holloway v. United States supplies offense-specific construction; its conditional-intent holding is not an additional gratuity requirement.
- Jones v. United States retains its distinct statutory and jury safeguards; this case creates no new sentencing classification rule.

## Law After Decision

The gratuity statute reaches act-linked gifts, including rewards for past acts, without requiring a bribery bargain. Status-only or goodwill-only instructions cannot support this conviction. Other gift restrictions, lawful circumstantial proof and ordinary proceedings on remand remain available. No precedent is overruled.
