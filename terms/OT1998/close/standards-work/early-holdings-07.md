## Holdings
#### 1. The Natural Law Party committee's policy challenge is capable of repetition yet evading review

**Holding and operative rule:** A challenge to completed pre-election broadcasts remains justiciable for the same continuing party committee when its established relationship to several excluded candidates, general electoral function, the broadcaster's general selection practice and its recurring program support a reasonable expectation of another short candidate-access controversy involving that committee. Both the period too short for complete review and the same claimant's reasonable prospect of recurrence are required; neither election timing nor an abstract organizational purpose alone suffices.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees that the Court reaches the merits but does not join this threshold explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Natural Law Party committee's common selection-policy challenge survives the 1996 broadcasts. The stipulated background distinguishes that general party organization from the separate committee formed for Marcus's 1996 campaign. Its participation with excluded candidates across congressional districts connects its electoral function to this broadcaster's actual selection practice. That concrete pattern, the general policy and the established recurring Iowa Press program support a reasonable expectation that this same committee will again confront the disputed practice. The opportunity for pre-election appearances is too short for complete ordinary appellate review. Spencer v. Kemna requires both facts and forbids a merely possible injury to others from supplying recurrence. Norman v. Reed supplies the comparison to continuing party interests, although its distinct party-qualification consequences are absent here. Chandler v. Miller established recurrence through an individual's stated intention to run again; it does not make that particular proof indispensable for a continuing organizational claimant. Honig v. Doe permits a grounded assessment of likelihood rather than requiring certainty of repetition. The unknown next candidate and unscheduled next debate make recurrence less certain, but do not erase the established relationship between this committee and the general practice. The conclusion is an inference from those facts, not a finding of a new candidacy, later exclusion, unchanged policy in 1999 or later campaign activity. The absence of contrary evidence does not shift the jurisdictional burden.

**Limits:** The surviving common claim does not separately preserve every individual's expired demand, establish future standing for every named plaintiff or supply an independent current injury from program-guide language. There is no general election exception, relaxed jurisdiction because review was granted, presumed reputational consequence or order to recreate a past broadcast.

#### 2. The actually selective debate is a nonpublic forum

**Holding and operative rule:** A state broadcaster's candidate debate is a nonpublic forum when the broadcaster actually retains editorial selection rather than opening the program generally to all qualified candidates. Restrictions must reasonably serve the program's journalistic purpose and remain viewpoint neutral; public ownership, a journalism label and ballot qualification alone decide neither access nor validity.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion is joined by all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Agreement on classification and the governing requirements does not imply agreement that Iowa's standards satisfy them.

**Controlling explanation:** Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes supplies the governing rule because Iowa Press retained selection by invitation. The District Court independently adopted the finding that the broadcaster intended admission only for invited candidates, and the actual format paired selected candidates with a host and political reporters. Calling the programs debates, as the District Court did, does not transform them into an open microphone; calling them bona fide news interviews does not immunize them. Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educational Fund and Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators' Association direct attention to actual access and permitted purpose. Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia preserves the speakers' private viewpoints within the actual opportunity. The question is therefore whether this selective platform was administered reasonably and without viewpoint discrimination. It is not whether every ballot-qualified candidate must be admitted or whether the broadcaster's public status makes all candidate speech its own message.

**Limits:** A program genuinely opened to qualifying candidates presents a different access question. Reasonable selection is permitted; sham criteria, incumbent favoritism, inconsistent administration and unpopular-viewpoint exclusions remain subject to review.

#### 3. Iowa's adjudicated selection satisfies Forbes; the forum claim and its derivative relief fail

**Holding and operative rule:** On this record, the selective format, legitimate informing purpose, editorial-integrity policies, evidence of electoral engagement and public interest, and independently credited findings support reasonable and viewpoint-neutral selection. Forbes imposes no additional universal numerical formula, written-policy, advance-publication or prescribed investigative prerequisite; the affirmed absence of a forum violation defeats declaratory, injunctive and guide-correction relief sought as remedies for that violation.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer: six Justices. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg dissent from the sufficiency of Iowa's standards and would vacate the rejection of the common policy claim and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Forbes permits the editorial judgment established here. Instruction 15 described newsworthiness through sufficient audience and public interest. It operated alongside policies of fairness, objectivity, balance, independence from pressure and an informed citizenry, and testimony about campaign activity, organization, prior performance, fundraising breadth, media recognition and public engagement. Those considerations bear on journalistic purpose; they are not a binding checklist or findings that every candidate lacked each characteristic. The judge independently assessed credibility and adopted findings of genuine selective editorial judgment, rather than exclusion because of disagreement with political views. That finding matters, but does not alone settle constitutional reasonableness. Petitioners also asserted insufficient individual inquiry, missing candidate information, substantial publicity, invitations from opponents and fundraising comparisons. Those contentions do not establish clear error in the credited findings or compel a different constitutional judgment. The reported figures prove neither an adopted dollars rule nor materially equal electoral significance. The Court does not declare every allegation false or reduce the challenge to ballot qualification. Regan v. Time, Inc., which concerns governmental restrictions on another publisher's content, does not invalidate all content-sensitive selection in a nonpublic candidate forum. Nor does Forbes make professional sincerity conclusive. The actual record supplies the reasonable connection here without judicial substitution of a different editorial ranking.

**Limits and remedy:** The Court does not approve categorical exclusion of idea-centered campaigns, manipulation favoring incumbent parties, or rejection of a genuinely viable minor-party candidate simply because of party status. Offering another program does not cure viewpoint discrimination. The program-guide correction request fails only as derivative relief for the rejected forum claim; no independent cause of action, current guide dissemination, reputational injury or correction entitlement is decided. The judgment orders no new broadcast, future inclusion, damages or statutory access remedy.



SOURCE 32: roberts_v_galen_of_virginia_merits_1999-01-13.md
## Holdings
#### 1. Stabilization has no additional improper-motive element

**Holding and operative rule:** A claim under 42 U.S.C. §1395dd(b) does not require proof that a participating hospital acted with an improper financial or other invidious motive. Liability nevertheless requires proof of the enacted stabilization or transfer duty and the applicable civil-action predicates; removing the extra motive requirement establishes neither breach nor injury.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt both holdings and their stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the stabilization provision means what its conditions say. When an individual comes to a hospital and the hospital determines that the individual has an emergency medical condition, subsection (b)(1) requires further examination and treatment within the available staff and facilities to stabilize the condition, or transfer in accordance with subsection (c). The text does not make that obligation depend on why the hospital acts. Subsection (a)'s separate requirement of an appropriate screening examination cannot insert an additional condition into subsection (b). National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler supplies the analogous principle that a court may not add a motive requirement while Congress's actual liability conditions remain intact. Concern that EMTALA should not become general federal malpractice law does not permit rewriting those conditions. Its determination, stabilization, transfer, injury and remedial requirements continue to distinguish a statutory violation from negligent care. Cleland's screening analysis therefore cannot sustain the additional motive element imposed on this stabilization claim. The Court does not decide whether subsection (a) contains a motive requirement, how long this hospital's duty continued, or whether the patient proves its violation.

**Operative qualifications retained:** Subsection (b)(2) deems the treatment requirement satisfied when the hospital offers the required examination and treatment, informs the patient or representative of their risks and benefits, and that person refuses; the hospital must take all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. Subsection (b)(3) separately addresses refusal of a transfer offered in accordance with subsection (c), after risk-and-benefit information, with the same reasonable-efforts duty concerning written informed refusal. Neither provision is a finding about this patient.

For an unstabilized emergency condition, subsection (c)(1) separately requires either a written transfer request made by the individual or legally responsible representative after receiving information about the hospital's EMTALA duties and the risks of transfer, or a physician's signed certification, based on the information available when transfer occurs, that the expected medical benefits of appropriate treatment elsewhere outweigh the increased transfer risks, including risks to the unborn child in labor; it also requires an appropriate transfer. If no physician is physically present in the emergency department, a medical person qualified under the Secretary's regulations may sign after consultation with a physician who makes the required determination and later countersigns. The certification must summarize its supporting risks and benefits. Appropriate transfer requires risk-minimizing treatment within the transferring hospital's capacity; available receiving space, qualified personnel and acceptance with appropriate treatment; transmission of all emergency-condition records available at transfer, including the condition, observed signs or symptoms, preliminary diagnosis, treatment, test results and written request or certification, together with the name and address of an on-call physician who refused or did not appear within a reasonable time to provide necessary stabilization; qualified personnel and transportation equipment, including necessary and medically appropriate life support during transfer; and any further applicable regulatory conditions. None is declared satisfied or violated here.

Subsection (d)(2)(A) permits the person directly harmed by a participating hospital's violation to sue that hospital for personal-injury damages available under the law of its State and appropriate equitable relief. The separate limitation is two years after the violation. This decision creates no personal action against an individual physician and no automatic liability for later deterioration.

#### 2. The motive-based summary judgment must be reversed; other predicates remain open

**Holding and operative rule:** Summary judgment rejecting the stabilization claim because the guardian failed to establish improper motive rests on an element the statute does not contain and must be reversed. The hospital's actual-knowledge and completed-screening defenses remain open for lawful consideration on remand; neither is accepted or rejected on its merits by this decision.

**Authority:** The same unanimous per curiam opinion controls this confined reversal and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court of Appeals recognized a genuine dispute about stabilization but nevertheless sustained judgment because the evidence did not sufficiently connect financial pressure to the physician's discharge decision. Eliminating that erroneous element removes its ground for judgment. Evidence concerning the patient's infection, the safety of transfer before the antibiotic response and culture results could be assessed, and the possible role of later care does not establish the remaining statutory elements in either party's favor. The hospital argues that it lacked actual knowledge of an emergency condition and that proper screening ended its duty. Those arguments must be assessed on their own legal and factual predicates. The Court neither converts the stabilization dispute into an undisputed absence of knowledge nor holds that screening necessarily ends every stabilization obligation. The preserved ability of a respondent to defend a favorable judgment on a supported alternative does not supply missing findings. The separate state-law ostensible-agency ruling and other claims are not reopened. No liability judgment, damages award or mandatory trial is ordered.

**Questions not reached:** Whether an emergency medical condition existed, what the hospital determined or knew, duration of any duty, stabilization, authorized transfer, causal injury, damages and ultimate liability remain undecided. The treatment-refusal, transfer-refusal and transfer-certification provisions remain distinct; no exception is presumed.



SOURCE 33: state_street_bank_v_signature_financial_group_merits_1999-01-14.md
## Holdings
#### Part I-A — Business use supplies no separate exclusion

**Operative rule.** An otherwise eligible invention is not excluded from §101 merely because its use is commercial or concerns financial administration. Rejecting that categorical exclusion does not establish eligibility, which remains subject to the mathematical-principle limitation and every other statutory requirement.

**Authority.** Part I-A of Stevens's Opinion of the Court has all nine Justices' direct joins.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 101 identifies statutory categories without excluding an industry. Chakrabarty applies those broad categories while preserving the exclusion of abstract principles. A business label therefore supplies no independent ground for invalidity, just as commercial usefulness supplies no independent ground for eligibility. The District Court's categorical business-method reasoning cannot sustain its judgment. The Court must examine the subject actually claimed.

#### Part I-B — Useful financial output is insufficient; the actual whole claim controls

**Operative rule.** A mathematical calculation does not become eligible solely because it yields a useful financial result, is confined to a named business arrangement, or is expressed as a programmed machine; the whole claimed combination must supply a patentable application rather than the calculation with its ordinary implementation. Conversely, mathematics or computer use does not itself make a claim ineligible, and means-plus-function limitations must be construed to include the corresponding disclosed structure and equivalents.

**Authority.** Part I-B has all nine Justices' direct joins at this level of generality; agreement on the standard does not establish agreement on its final application.

**Controlling explanation.** Benson involved useful computation, and Flook involved a useful numerical update limited to a particular setting. Those decisions prevent utility from deciding eligibility. Diehr requires considering the entire claimed application and forbids removing limitations merely because they were familiar; its curing process also shows the distinction between using mathematics in an application and claiming the calculation itself.

Section 112, paragraph 6, limits functional means to corresponding disclosed structure and equivalents. The issued combinations therefore cannot be treated simply as the cancelled methods or as every possible means of obtaining a number. Markman and Vitronics require actual claim meaning to be determined from the public instrument; Warner-Jenkinson preserves each material boundary. None makes a machine label dispositive. The Court adopts no universal physical-transformation requirement, independent noncomputer-contribution requirement, or rule that every program is abstract. The distinct requirements of novelty, nonobviousness, disclosure and infringement remain intact.

#### Part II — Each issued combination remains the financial calculation and its bookkeeping implementation

**Operative rule.** With each disclosed means and incorporated limitation retained, issued claims 1–6 cover the calculation, verification and recording of financial allocations rather than a qualifying application of their result, and are ineligible under §101. This conclusion sustains their invalidity without deciding the separate claims and counterclaims on their merits.

**Authority.** Part II has the eight Associates' direct joins identified in Opinion Topology; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Controlling explanation.** The programmed computer and storage are real limitations and are considered together with every routine. The following table identifies each complete combination and why its added work remains accounting calculation:

| Claim | Whole combination considered and application |
|---|---|
| 1 | Processor/storage, initialization, fund-change and percentage processing, daily income/expenses/realized allocations, unrealized allocations and year-end data calculate and record the financial values. |
| 2 | Adds pricing-date/fund-asset storage, medium identification and investment, ratio, incremental, daily and breakage files: these organize inputs, intermediate values and results. |
| 3 | Adds correct-medium verification, date retrieval/correction, fund-change input/storage and prior-investment retrieval with adjusted-investment/ratio calculation/storage: these protect the calculation's intended input record. |
| 4 | Begins "A method as claimed in claim 3" and retains that dependency; adds medium/date checks/correction, incremental income/expense/realized retrieval and storage, investment/ratio retrieval and prepricing-asset computation: these refine the accounting values. |
| 5 | Incorporates claim 4; adds medium/date checks, unrealized input, investment/ratio/incremental/breakage retrieval, end-of-day calculation/storage and breakage/daily updates: these handle fractional discrepancies and maintain accurate allocations. |
| 6 | Incorporates claim 5; adds medium verification, daily-activity retrieval and year-end income/expense/capital-gain-or-loss aggregation and storage: these produce and retain the annual accounting result. |

The useful values affect ownership records, tax reporting and valuation, but the claims require no further operation applying their result comparable to Diehr's curing process. Flook's useful restricted calculation remains the relevant distinction. This conclusion assumes novelty and nonobviousness; no limitation is subtracted as prior art, and no disputed external technical fact is resolved against Signature. The accepted specification and scope reveal no material unperformed task requiring an eligibility remand. Claim 4's wording supplies no independent drafting-defect holding. A different computer operation or commercial application requires its own analysis.



SOURCE 34: california_public_employees_retirement_system_v_felzen_merits_1999-01-20.md
## Holdings
#### 1. Rule 23.1 objection does not itself confer the party status needed for this appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** A shareholder who is otherwise not a party does not acquire appellate party status merely by appearing under Rule 23.1 notice and presenting objections that the district court hears and rejects when approving a derivative settlement. To appeal that settlement judgment, the shareholder must properly become a party through intervention; shareholder interest, notice and the recognized opportunity to object do not themselves supply the necessary status.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court is joined by the other four members of the coalition comprising Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. All five adopt both holdings and their stated qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an opportunity to assist the settlement court by objecting is distinct from authority to continue the corporate litigation on appeal. Marino v. Ortiz supplies both the general party-status rule and its application to persons actually heard in opposition to a settlement who had not intervened. Rule 23.1 recognizes shareholder participation in the district court's supervision of the corporate claim; that function does not itself confer the separate appellate role. Tryforos v. Icarian Development Co. supplies the strongest contrary rule. Its distinction between objecting to disposition and assuming prosecution expressly included an appeal by a shareholder who appeared to oppose a proposed settlement. The Court rejects that settlement-appeal proposition insofar as it permits this appeal without intervention: limiting an objector's appellate objectives does not remove the status prerequisite Marino applies. Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp. reinforces the distinction between interest and status, although its undisturbed intervention denial and separately unpresented intervention question differ from this case's direct status challenge. Johnson v. Manhattan Railway Co. does not establish an independent route here. Its discussion of invitation, appearance and appeal at page 495 is followed at page 496 by an explanation why the applicable equity rule would not obstruct intervention, in a distinct receivership setting. Neither passage decides modern derivative-objector status by itself. These institutions were heard but sought no intervention. Their reliance on earlier circuit practice does not constitute admission as parties, and no denial of an application is before the Court.

**Limits:** This is a rule for the presented derivative-settlement appeal, not a categorical prohibition of every nonparty appeal under every procedural regime. Section 1291 supplies the final-decision framework, Appellate Rule 3(c) concerns identification of appellants, Rule 23.1 governs the recognized objection role, and Rule 24 supplies intervention; neither §1291 nor Rule 3(c) is treated as expressly deciding every party-status category. The decision rests on actual participation and the absence of intervention, not on a finding that the institutions violated a notice or service condition.

#### 2. Intervention review and nonparty due-process protection remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Requiring intervention for this settlement appeal does not foreclose review by an actual party or an appropriate appeal from an actual denial of intervention. Nor does it impose a general duty to intervene in another person's suit to preserve an otherwise independent claim: constitutional limits on binding genuinely unrepresented persons remain, without establishing an appellate entitlement or a collateral remedy in this case.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice opinion of the Court controls this boundary and the confined affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Izumi preserves a lawful route for reviewing an intervention denial while distinguishing it from review of the underlying judgment by a party. No such denial exists here, so the Court decides neither entitlement to intervention nor the timing or result of an application never made. Richards v. Jefferson County protects a person's own rights against preclusion without constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate representative litigation or another recognized basis consistent with due process. Its rule does not make every affected person an appellate party. Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein preserves the independent requirement of adequate representation; notice, opportunities to object or opt out do not cure its constitutional absence. Those protections remain effective, but neither adequate nor inadequate representation is established by the derivative label or by these institutions' appearance. The claim settled below belongs to the corporation, while Richards involved direct personal claims. Keeping that distinction does not decide whether any particular independent shareholder claim exists or may proceed. The status ruling accordingly leaves the settlement's fairness, compensation terms and any properly presented intervention or due-process question undecided.

**Questions not reached and remedial limits:** The Court orders no reopening, tolling, late intervention, new hearing, discovery or review of a fictional denied motion. It does not independently decide the petition's requested pure-prospectivity question or adopt the Court of Appeals' broader assertion that every possible operation of Chevron Oil was eliminated. Harper's rule against selective prospectivity after application of a federal rule to the Court's litigants remains at its established scope; it creates no unsupported transition entitlement here. No settlement-fairness, fee-merits, collusion, representation-adequacy, personal-claim or collateral-preclusion judgment follows.



SOURCE 35: department_of_the_army_v_blue_fox_merits_1999-01-20.md
## Holdings
#### The requested lien supplies substitute compensation without an applicable waiver

**Operative rule.** Section 702 does not waive federal immunity for an equitable lien whose function is substitute payment from Government property for a private debtor's default when no identified retained property belonging to the claimant or specific statutory payment entitlement supports the requested recovery. The equitable form and identification of a project account do not establish consent; specific enforcement of an actual entitlement remains a different question under its own governing law.

**Authority.** Scalia's Opinion of the Court has all nine Justices' direct joins and controls the rule, application and limits.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 702's waiver excludes money damages. Bowen distinguishes substitute compensation from specific relief enforcing an actual entitlement, including a statutory entitlement to payment; it does not classify all monetary relief as damages. Nordic Village and Lane separately require consent to the remedy actually sought. A substantive duty, general jurisdiction or equitable description cannot supply missing consent.

Blue Fox expressly seeks contract funds and argues that its lien attached at notice, making later disbursements ineffective against it. But that attachment is the legal proposition to be established, not an accepted finding that the Army retained Blue Fox's property. No earned Verdan payment remains withheld, and the distinct completion balance is not shown to belong to Blue Fox. The requested recovery would replace Verdan's unpaid debt from Government property. Calling the Army a stakeholder cannot establish both the underlying entitlement and consent to that substitute obligation.

Henningsen and Pearlman concern surety or subrogation rights; Munsey Trust likewise supplies no general direct subcontractor right to recover against the Army free of immunity. The Miller Act's bond protection does not make the Government a guarantor when it fails to obtain the required bond. No bond exists here, and the Government's administration does not itself enact a payment mandate or waiver. That failure is not approved; it simply does not supply consent.

Immunity resolves this claim before independent equitable merits. The Court does not declare every equitable lien money damages or decide a genuine proceeding for claimant property under applicable authority. It also leaves the disputed agency-action, reviewability and adequate-remedy arguments under §§702 and 704, and the related preservation objection, unanswered. The money-damages limitation is sufficient.
