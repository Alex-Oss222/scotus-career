**Event and date:** merits decision, 1999-04-28; October Term 1998.
**Result:** Affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded; unanimous invalidation of $300 list and two appellate-office $100 limits; 5–4 on residual $100/PAC receipts, 5–3–1 on small-donor allowance.

## Holdings

### Contribution limits require actual anticorruption fit; two candidate categories fail here

**Holding and operative rule:** Candidate contribution limits must be closely drawn to a sufficiently important interest in preventing corruption or its appearance while preserving meaningful political association. On this record the $300 per-person/per-election ceilings for Governor, Lieutenant Governor, Secretary of State, Treasurer of State, Auditor of State, Attorney General and Commissioner of State Lands, and the $100 ceilings for Arkansas Supreme Court Justice and Court of Appeals Judge, fail that requirement.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I is unanimous. The three holdings that follow are controlled by her Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, five votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Buckley distinguishes contributions from expenditures and allows prophylaxis without a completed bribe; it does not permit equality of political influence as an independent justification. Neither a dollar figure nor an inflation comparison establishes a permanent constitutional boundary. The statewide campaign scale and fundraising burden make the legislative examples inadequate to justify the enumerated $300 limits. The two appellate judicial offices face a distinct combined burden: large electoral areas, no personal solicitation or acceptance, and committee fundraising confined to 180 days before the primary through 45 days after the last contested election. The supported findings establish inadequate resources for effective advocacy under that combination. Suster recognizes judicial impartiality but does not ignore the practical restriction or decide a contribution case through expenditure rules. The Court invalidates neither the solicitation canon nor every judicial limit. Arkansas Supreme Court races belong to the statutory $100 category despite their statewide geography. Successful campaigns, self-financing and a small gift's relative share are relevant circumstances, not automatic answers.

### The residual $100 restriction survives the tried categorical challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** The record does not establish the asserted categorical invalidity of the $100 per-person/per-election ceiling for covered offices outside the seven-office $300 list and the two named appellate judicial offices. This residual class includes other covered judicial offices; rejection of the blanket challenge does not decide a genuinely different office-specific application without its own presented grounds and record.

**Controlling explanation:** The trial evidence included perceived influence from concentrated contributions, the Maddox fundraiser and tobacco-related giving, even though actual improper access or undue influence was not found. Candidate agreement with donors is not itself corruption. The actual special-election experience, reported $24,000 and $40,000 Senate fundraising and resetting of limits for each election support the State's assessment in the demonstrated legislative setting. Against that stand professional fundraisers' contrary testimony, sharp fundraising changes and effects on up to eighty percent of earlier legislative contributions. The Court finds the practical evidence sufficient for this tried challenge without requiring campaign impossibility or equating hypothetical donor counts with actual recruitment. Kentucky Right to Life requires analysis of the actual contribution channel, not automatic invalidation from the amount. The two appellate offices' cumulative burden cannot be imputed to every judge merely because solicitation restrictions are shared. Nor are legislative successes findings about every other office. The decision rejects the grounds offered for blanket invalidation; it orders no universal office survey and invents no waiver or absent distinct claim.

### The ordinary-PAC receipt limit survives on this operational record

**Holding and operative rule:** The $200 annual limit from one person to any one ordinary PAC survives the presented challenge where the tried operational record preserves effective collective advocacy and the restriction is fitted to the actual candidate-conduit channel. It is a per-recipient limit, not one aggregate pool across all committees, and supplies no general approval for materially different recipient functions or demonstrated pooling burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Day protects meaningful political pooling and requires a real fit inquiry. Here years of operation and robust candidate-directed PAC activity support the lower findings of no appreciable impairment in the tried case. Ordinary Arkansas PACs lack the federal multicandidate qualifications of at least fifty contributors and contributions to at least five candidates, so the candidate-transfer channel gives the circumvention concern concrete work. Kentucky Right to Life's aggregate-pool problem is distinct. Annual duration, recipient function, actual operation and amount must be considered together. Supporting other committees does not alone cure a burden on the chosen association, and aggregate success is not an automatic safe harbor. The combination established here nevertheless supports the restriction. California Medical permits genuine conduit regulation without making its fractured lowest-protection rationale a universal rule. A different operational burden or independent recipient function remains open.

### The exact small-donor qualification supports a different candidate allowance

**Holding and operative rule:** Arkansas may permit a qualifying registered small-donor PAC to give $2,500 per candidate per election while ordinary sources face lower ceilings, where that committee receives contributions exclusively from individuals and no more than $25 cumulatively from any individual during a calendar year. The financial distinction is justified by the anticorruption interest and fit of the dispersed-donor structure; it is neither a viewpoint preference nor a rule that any small-donor label suffices.

**Controlling explanation:** Annual individual aggregation disperses financing and attenuates concentrated donor control; ordinary PACs may receive larger individual amounts and entity contributions. Those differences support differentiated treatment under the asserted compelling anticorruption interest and the required fit of this exact classification; the ruling does not rest on rational-basis review. A committee's $2,500 transfer can still create recipient-end influence, the principal contrary argument. The Court acknowledges that risk but finds this precise qualification sufficient to distinguish the financing channels without requiring identical caps. It does not find small-donor committees incapable of corruption, authorize unlimited candidate giving or rely merely on the possibility of forming a different association. California Medical recognizes that financing structures can matter; Day preserves actual association rather than nominal permission. The $25 condition is annual cumulative intake, not a repeatable allowance per gift. The $2,500 figure governs outgoing candidate contributions, not committee receipts. Every qualification remains operative; sham or materially different structures gain no adjudicated protection.

## Holdings

### Old equity cannot reserve this valuable investment opportunity because of its former ownership

**Holding and operative rule:** When an impaired senior unsecured class objects and is not paid in full, §1129(b)(2)(B)(ii) forbids a plan from reserving to old equity alone the opportunity to contribute new capital and acquire reorganized ownership where old ownership supplies that exclusive eligibility. The opportunity is property received under the plan on account of the old interest, even when the proposed investment purchases new ownership and the underlying valuations are unchallenged.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, eight votes for the complete rule, application, reservations and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court identifies the right the plan distributes. New money explains payment for new ownership; it does not explain why only former owners receive the chance to acquire it. Ahlers recognizes economic ownership rights and confines equitable treatment to the Code. The promised $6.125 million over five years, worth about $4.1 million presently, does not erase the separate eligibility preference. The Court finds no numerical valuation error and presumes no higher outside bid. Section 1121 governs proposal exclusivity, while §1129 governs a resulting plan's substantive acceptability. The lawful, unappealed proposal period does not independently make an ownership preference confirmable. Noland and Reorganized CF&I preserve actual statutory priorities and exceptions; Nobelman illustrates why valuation and the legal treatment of rights are different inquiries, without deciding this Chapter 11 question itself. Stevens's contrary construction gives decisive weight to the new consideration and accepted confirmation findings. Those facts do not answer why old ownership alone confers the opportunity. The preserved priority objection therefore succeeds without reopening the unappealed procedural order or claim classification. The Court reserves whether a general new-value corollary survives and its complete conditions. It prescribes no auction, bidder, automatic termination of exclusivity or universal valuation method, and approves no future plan. The same exclusive allocation cannot sustain this confirmation; a changed proposal or process must satisfy the Code through lawful further proceedings.

- United States v. Noland and United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators preserve statutory ranking and genuine exceptions rather than judicially preferred priorities.

## Holdings

### Federal courts determine Price-Anderson coverage before imposing tribal exhaustion

**Holding and operative rule:** When the invoked Price-Anderson federal proceeding requires deciding whether pleaded claims are covered public-liability actions, the federal court must decide that predicate itself before imposing prudential tribal exhaustion. Covered claims follow the Act's specific federal channel; uncovered claims retain their own jurisdictional, tribal-authority and exhaustion analysis.

**Authority:** Thomas's unanimous Opinion of the Court controls this holding and the cross-appeal holding below.

**Controlling explanation:** National Farmers Union and Iowa Mutual ordinarily allow tribal courts to consider their authority first as a matter of judicial comity. Price-Anderson creates a specific federal cause and jurisdiction, together with removal and consolidation mechanisms for covered public-liability claims. That arrangement cannot perform its assigned work if a court-created exhaustion rule postpones the federal coverage decision. The removal provision expressly names state courts; the Court does not rewrite it to say tribal courts or invent a tribal removal filing. The relevant question is the federal court's already invoked coverage task, which the Act's jurisdiction and consolidation design place before ordinary comity. Quackenbush keeps jurisdiction distinct from discretionary surrender. Strate likewise requires an actual source of tribal governing authority; exhaustion itself supplies none. The Court does not decide whether these allegations establish coverage, whether every mining injury is a covered nuclear incident, or whether any uncovered claim may proceed in a tribal forum. Mille Lacs requires attention to actual instruments and rights, not a presumed extinguishment of unexamined treaty protections.

### Nonappealing appellees may defend a decree but may not enlarge it

**Holding and operative rule:** An appellee may defend the existing decree on an alternative preserved ground but may not, without a cross-appeal, obtain an enlargement of its own rights or reduction of the appellant's rights under that decree. The Ninth Circuit could not dissolve the partial injunctions for plaintiffs who did not appeal them; that enlargement is vacated.

**Controlling explanation:** American Railway Express draws the distinction between a ground supporting the existing judgment and a request for a better judgment. Respondents' proposed dissolution was the latter. Comity does not convert their defense into an appeal, and no independent jurisdictional defect supplies a basis requiring that dissolution here. The Court need not classify every cross-appeal omission as jurisdictional or decide every possible exception. Restoring the prior decrees also does not decide their substantive merits or authorize broader relief. Any additional coercive relief must have an actual jurisdictional and equitable basis, respect proper-party and immunity limits, and fit the coverage determination.

## Precedent Treatment

- National Farmers Union Insurance Cos. v. Crow Tribe and Iowa Mutual Insurance Co. v. LaPlante retain their ordinary prudential exhaustion rule; it yields to this specific federal coverage task.
- American Railway Express Co. v. Levee preserves alternative grounds for defending a decree but prohibits an unappealed enlargement.
- Strate v. A-1 Contractors requires a substantive source of tribal authority and preserves proper-party, immunity and equitable limits; it does not decide these mining claims by label.
- Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co. keeps jurisdiction and prudential abstention distinct.
- Minnesota v. Mille Lacs Band preserves instrument-specific rights; no unexamined treaty right is extinguished here.

Price-Anderson coverage is a federal threshold task in these proceedings. Covered claims cannot be delayed by ordinary prudential tribal exhaustion, while uncovered claims remain governed by their actual jurisdictional rules. Appellees still need a cross-appeal to obtain the favorable decree enlargement at issue. No precedent is overruled.

### Courts must respect the Board's permissible construction within actual delegated authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §§1103(a)(1) and former 1253(h), together with the Board's adjudicative assignment in 8 C.F.R. §3.1(d)(1), courts must respect a permissible Board construction of the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception after independently enforcing enacted limits. The present statute and Board assignment establish authority independently of Haggar's tariff rulemaking setting. NationsBank permits reasoned classification within genuine ambiguity; MCI and NCUA prohibit erasing statutory conditions. The exception concerns serious reasons for considering that the applicant committed a serious nonpolitical crime outside the United States before arriving here. No criminal conviction is required, but the territorial, temporal and substantive conditions remain operative. The humanitarian importance of withholding supports careful enforcement of those limits, not an exclusively judicial factor code. ### A properly established crime exception requires no additional persecution-risk balance

**Holding and operative rule:** Once the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception is properly established, former §1253(h)(2) makes the paragraph (1) withholding protection inapplicable without an additional balancing of feared persecution against offense seriousness. The Board may address that exception while assuming the applicant could show that life or freedom would be threatened because of race, religion, nationality, membership in a particular social group or political opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute separately states the protection and its exceptions. A greater threatened harm does not dissolve a properly established crime exclusion through an additional condition Congress did not supply. The Handbook paragraph 156 argument relied on below presents a serious humanitarian objection, but does not change those distinct statutory inquiries. The Court finds no absence of persecution risk and creates no general discretion to deny withholding to an otherwise eligible applicant. Relevant political circumstances remain material under the lawful crime analysis. Discretionary asylum is a different question and receives no automatic disposition from this withholding rule.

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board may compare an offense's political character with its ordinary criminal character and consider gross disproportionality or atrocious conduct in determining which predominates. The statute requires neither a separate atrocity finding in every case nor findings that the violence was necessary to or successful in achieving its political objective; the Board must still assess actual political purpose, context and conduct under its announced standard.

**Controlling explanation:** Political motive alone does not establish a political offense, just as unlawfulness alone does not make one nonpolitical. The Board's explanation considered protest objectives alongside property destruction and civilian assaults and found the criminal character disproportionate. The Court reviews that actual explanation rather than adopting new factual findings from the contested testimony. The Ninth Circuit’s comparison with more severe conduct is a consistency objection, not necessarily a universal atrocity prerequisite. The Board’s actual disproportionate-conduct explanation answers that objection; the greater atrocity of another case is no minimum threshold for this alternative ground. Handbook paragraph 152 and the suggested McMullen comparison reinforce the importance of political context and disproportion, but the absence of a separate comparison with McMullen's atrocious acts does not defeat a determination resting on the supported political-criminal comparison. Atrocious means may defeat political characterization; atrocity is not necessary in every case. A clear causal relation to a political objective would not necessarily overcome a supported disproportion finding, and the Court reserves any independent causal-link requirement in a different case. Relevant comparisons and reasoned treatment of genuine inconsistency remain possible. No categorical rule about bus burnings, assault severity or unsuccessful protest is announced.

### Future discard and ordinary approach access do not themselves authorize inspection in protected residential space

**Holding and operative rule:** Greenwood does not authorize warrantless inspection of closed refuse containers merely because their owner intends later collection or private persons can approach them; courts must determine whether police entered or exploited constitutionally protected residential space and assess the actual location, exposure and intrusion. The lower determination must be set aside because collection-point equivalence and ordinary passage access cannot substitute for that legal inquiry into this home-side physical inspection; the Court leaves its ultimate search classification open.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, controls the complete bounded rule and vacatur/remand. The three concurring Justices' additional search conclusion is not a majority holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenwood concerns refuse exposed for collection outside the home's protected surrounding area, or curtilage. Dunn examines proximity to the home, enclosure, the area's uses and measures shielding it from passersby; no one consideration controls. The layout here is known: the open common driveway supplies the approach to both front doors, and the containers stood near the connected garages about twenty-four feet from the sidewalk for routine collection. The lower court discussed Dunn and announced a fact-specific result. The legal objection is not that it ignored every circumstance. Ordinary passage and future transfer to a collector do not, without the proper residential-space inquiry, establish authority to remove concealed contents. Fields and Carter distinguish actual lawful access and natural observation from access to protected information or effects. The shared approach is substantial contrary evidence, and collection placement also matters; neither becomes a universal investigative license. The three concurring Justices find these facts already establish a search, but five agree that the adverse determination cannot stand on the legal equivalence used below. Reconsideration preserves supported facts and requires no finding that every driveway is curtilage. If no protected space was entered or exploited and the exposure fits Greenwood, the judgment may be reinstated. If a constitutional search occurred, ordinary warrant-fruit, independent-source, attenuation and suppression rules and the actual conditional-plea consequences apply on their established predicates. No exception, exclusion or outcome is presumed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision creates no general privacy right in curbside garbage and does not decide a collector's private inspection, a private search later reported to police, or containers outside protected residential space freely accessible from a public route. It orders no new facts, automatic hearing or sentence and does not treat limited private access as general police permission.

- United States v. Dunn supplies the four contextual curtilage considerations without making proximity or enclosure alone decisive.
- Fields/Crawley and Carter/Johns preserve distinctions among actual access, natural observation, protected space and concealed information, with their actual separate joins intact.
- Ornelas preserves independent review of legal application with appropriate deference to supported facts and reasonable inferences.
- Knowles and Wyoming v. Houghton retain their separate roadside and automobile rules; neither supplies residential investigative authority here.

The Court has not decided that this inspection was a search. Greenwood remains applicable to its actual exposure setting, and lawful findings and ordinary conditional remedies remain necessary. It must determine the incorporated treatment's scope and satisfy the specified lien conditions and applicable pre-1994 §507(a)(7) requirements before granting tax priority; the Court grants none here.

**Authority:** Souter's Parts I and III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, control the tax and valuation holdings, eight votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 412(n) addresses covered unpaid required contributions exceeding $1 million; its pertinent rule makes the lien arise on the sixtieth day after the installment's due date. The tax direction concerns an amount with respect to which that lien is imposed and imports rules similar to §4068(c), (d) and (e). Section 4068(c), codified at §1368(c), expressly addresses Title 11 or insolvency and treatment as a tax for Title 11 purposes. Congress's instruction does not disappear because of its location. Reorganized CF&I examined a separate ten-percent assessment whose label did not determine its operation; it established neither a universal checklist nor one-way incorporation. This actual connector must be construed before functional private-plan purpose decides the matter. Noland preserves enacted priorities and genuine exceptions. The strongest affirmance alternative remains open: the bankruptcy and district courts found the installment due fewer than sixty days before filing and concluded no lien arose, with the stay preventing later attachment. The circuit did not decide that ground. The Court returns it without finding attachment, declaring the lien unnecessary, deciding every stay exception or disturbing the reported timing. A lawful resolution may still defeat priority. Remand requires neither new evidence nor a lien proceeding and selects no payment.

### Prepetition-service compensation does not become a new administrative debt merely because payment is later due

**Holding and operative rule:** The additional pension-contribution claim, attributable to prepetition employee services, does not obtain §503(b)(1)(A) administrative priority merely from a later due date, continuing funding duty or later plan termination. The already allowed component for actual postpetition services remains, and Reading's distinct postpetition-liability route without an estate-benefit requirement remains intact.

**Authority:** Souter's Part II is joined by all nine Justices and controls this complete debt-origin ground and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Noland requires the actual statutory category, not a preferred ranking based on creditor hardship. Hughes Aircraft and UNUM distinguish an enforceable obligation from the particular relief it supports. Reading supplies a real alternative: postpetition negligence liability received administrative treatment without a measurable estate benefit. Even assuming a qualifying statutory liability can receive comparable treatment, these additional amounts remain compensation earned by earlier labor, not a newly incurred postpetition injury or debt. Operating the business and retaining a statutory funding duty do not excuse nonpayment, but neither changes the origin of every dollar owed. The Court adopts no universal benefit prerequisite, no rule that all pension duties are merely contractual and no categorical exclusion of postpetition statutory wrongs. The Coal Act analogy has a different statutory trigger; §1114's retiree-medical category is also distinct. Neither makes these earlier-service contributions newly incurred. This independent ground survives the separate tax remand and preserves the existing postpetition-service allowance.

### The statutory liability measure must be reconciled with bankruptcy allowance

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 1362(b)(1)(A) and 1301(a)(18), including the latter's incorporation of PBGC-prescribed assumptions, define the underlying unfunded-benefit liability and cannot be displaced categorically merely because the claim is in bankruptcy. Courts must apply that lawful measure together with §502(b)'s petition-date allowance command, enforce actual statutory limits and conflicts, and consider properly presented validity or application objections; the Court validates no universal methodology and fixes no amount.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1362 measures liability at termination; §1301 measures benefit liabilities with assumptions prescribed for §1344 purposes, less current plan assets. That is actual delegated authority, not deference from agency identity. Haggar requires enforcement of clear limits and then respect for a reasonable choice within genuine delegated ambiguity. The introductory ERISA-purpose language does not erase the measure of the ERISA-created debt. Nor does §1123(a)(4)'s equal treatment within a class require every legally different claim to originate under identical assumptions. Section 1144(d) preserves other federal law; an actual incompatibility must be identified. The estate's petition-date and distribution concerns are substantial, but §502 must be reconciled with the liability measure rather than used to discard it categorically. Conversely, the statutory reference does not establish PBGC's proposed figure without the allowance inquiry. Rash follows the valuation provision governing its transaction, and LaSalle distinguishes legal rights from numerical value; neither supplies a universal prudent-investor rate or market auction here. The resulting $124,441,000 valuation is vacated. Remand permits ordinary consideration of properly raised statutory-fit, reasonableness and application challenges without inventing one, ordering new rules or requiring a hearing. Different statutory dates do not themselves authorize duplicate discounting.

## Holdings

#### Seizure of a forfeitable automobile in a public place

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers with probable cause that a readily movable automobile itself is forfeitable may seize it from a public place without a warrant or an additional case-specific emergency. The rule rests on the automobile's characteristics and the public-place seizure, not merely on a legislature's decision to label property contraband.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; these six Justices control the rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the established automobile rationale reaches custody of the automobile that probable cause identifies as the forfeitable object. Pennsylvania v. Labron supplies mobility and the reduced privacy associated with regulated vehicles without requiring a further showing that process was impracticable. Wyoming v. Houghton makes the object supported by probable cause important to the exception's scope; here that object is White's own car. The seizure occurred in public and required no additional invasion of a home. United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property requires prior process for the real-property deprivation it decided while expressly distinguishing movable property; its land-specific rule does not decide this case. G. M. Leasing Corp. v. United States supports distinguishing public seizures from intrusions into protected premises. The dissent correctly identifies a possessory interest and the retrospective purpose of forfeiture, but those differences do not remove this vehicle's mobility or the direct probable-cause connection. Earlier opportunity to obtain a warrant therefore does not itself defeat the exception.

The Court does not decide whether excessive delay can make probable cause stale. The holding authorizes no entry into a home or protected curtilage, and does not adjudicate inventory policy, prolonged retention, notice, ultimate forfeiture or a distinct state-law protection.

## Precedent Treatment

- Pennsylvania v. Labron is extended from a vehicle evidence search to seizure of the readily movable vehicle itself when probable cause identifies it as forfeitable and it is taken in public.
- Wyoming v. Houghton is applied for attention to the object and scope supported by probable cause; it did not itself decide forfeiture seizure.
- United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property is distinguished: its real-property process requirement and movable-property reservation retain their force.
- G. M. Leasing Corp. v. United States supports the public-place/protected-premises distinction; its revenue-enforcement holding is not converted into unlimited forfeiture authority.
- Knowles v. Iowa remains intact: an inventory or adjacent enforcement power cannot itself furnish authority for the antecedent seizure.

## Law After Decision

The automobile exception permits this public seizure of the forfeitable vehicle without a separate urgency showing. It supplies no general warrant exception for all property designated contraband, and leaves distinct seizure, search, retention, hearing and final-forfeiture questions separate.

Reversal rejects the federal warrant/additional-emergency ground for suppressing the fruits of this seizure. ## Holdings

#### I. Predominance alone cannot support this injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** A districting injunction cannot rest solely on a finding that race predominated in forming a common territorial electorate, even if ordinary districting criteria were subordinated. A plaintiff must establish a separately cognizable denial of equal participation or treatment; remand evaluates any such actually presented claim under its own elements and ordinary procedural rules.

**Authority:** Part II and the governing remand in Part III are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five direct joins control.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that factual disagreement matters only when the governing law makes the fact material. Shaw v. Reno and Johnson v. Miller distinguish racial composition of a shared electorate from unequal civic status, racial eligibility or representation legally limited to one race. Predominance may exist without establishing any of those deprivations. The district court therefore could not invalidate the district merely by resolving the design's motivation against the State. A trial on the same legally insufficient theory would not cure that error. Coalition for Economic Equity v. Wilson illustrates a concrete burden on political access; it does not turn racial motivation alone into that burden. An independently cognizable injury may be noneconomic and need not arise from a second act or later electoral loss, but its own elements must be established. The Court neither invents such a claim nor rules it forfeited because its preservation is not established here. It leaves narrow tailoring, statutory vote dilution, the applicable scrutiny for an actually supported independent injury and the district's validity against other claims undecided.

#### II. Summary judgment cannot resolve a genuine material inference against the nonmovant

**Holding and operative rule:** When an actually cognizable claim makes motive material, a supported genuine dispute between racial and political explanations cannot be resolved against the nonmoving party on summary judgment. Circumstantial proof can support summary judgment in an appropriate case; neither district shape nor racial and partisan correlation is automatically conclusive.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I at this ordinary procedural level, without agreeing on which constitutional claim may proceed.

**Controlling explanation:** The State offered a supported political explanation for the boundaries. The court could not treat the contrary motive inference as a trial finding on this summary record. Johnson's evidentiary treatment separates proof from legal consequence. Under the Court's substantive holding, no motive trial follows on a design-only theory; under any actually preserved independent claim, ordinary materiality and evidentiary rules govern. The Court does not treat new reply-brief material as evidence that was before the district court or decide the denial of the State's cross-motion as a separate component.

## Precedent Treatment

- Shaw v. Reno is applied: territorial racial composition alone does not establish the asserted unequal-treatment injury.
- Johnson v. Miller controls both the substantive insufficiency of predominance alone and the distinction between evidentiary dispute and legal materiality; its genuine-injury protections and limits remain.
- Coalition for Economic Equity v. Wilson is distinguished as an actual political-access burden, not authority to infer such a burden from motive or constituency composition.

## Law After Decision

The Court applies the existing Shaw/Johnson rule and requires a conforming remand. Predominance alone does not sustain an injunction or require a trial. Ordinary summary-judgment rules remain available for facts material to a properly presented independent claim; the four-Justice proposed predominance trigger is not governing law.
