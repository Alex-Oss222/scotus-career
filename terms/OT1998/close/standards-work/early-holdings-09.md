

SOURCE 41: hughes_aircraft_co_v_jacobson_merits_1999-01-25.md
## Holdings

### I. Genuine payments within the continuing plan do not inure to the employer

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 403(c)(1) does not prohibit payment of genuine benefits to participants under the amended single plan merely because those benefits reduce future employer contributions or payroll costs. Surplus remains plan property, and the anti-inurement rule independently governs an actual diversion, unlawful reversion or payment outside the lawful plan.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion's Part I is joined by all eight Associate Justices and controls; Stone-Zsela agrees in dismissal of the anti-inurement claim without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that adding a benefit structure and eligible participants does not, on the acts alleged, create a second legal plan to which assets were transferred for Hughes's separate obligations. Both benefit structures draw from the continuing defined-benefit fund. A contribution holiday does not itself distribute cash to the employer. John Hancock separates asset status from a particular unlawful use, while Spink preserves anti-inurement as an independent claim even when adoption of the design was not fiduciary conduct. The retirees correctly insist that employee-derived surplus is protected plan property; that premise does not establish that genuine payments to the plan's participants are an employer diversion. The Court rejects the separate-plan and incidental-employer-benefit theories rather than creating an exception to §403 or declaring the surplus Hughes's property. A different actual recipient or unlawful transfer would require its own statutory analysis.

### II. Amendment design is distinct from fiduciary implementation

**Holding and operative rule:** Design and adoption of these pension amendments are sponsor functions, even though employees contributed to the plan; those acts alone create no fiduciary breach under §§404–405. Actual asset management or disposition and administration remain subject to §3(21)(A)'s functional requirements, including authority or control over disposition without an additional discretion requirement.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join the sponsor-capacity rule and its preservation of independent duties. The eight Associates additionally join the claim-specific conclusion that these original implementation and derivative cofiduciary theories identify no independent breach after their surplus-ownership and separate-plan predicates fail; Stone-Zsela agrees in that disposition without supplying an additional implementation ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that employee contributions do not make participants co-sponsors entitled to control plan design. Spink directly separates amendment adoption from implementation and requires independent claims to receive their own analysis. Varity preserves responsibility for actual administration and misleading benefit communications; it does not turn this design decision into administration. John Hancock keeps plan assets, fiduciary function and breach distinct. Assuming fiduciary implementation of the genuine payments, the original claims identify no independent imprudent management or disloyal diversion beyond the failed surplus-ownership and separate-plan theories. The cofiduciary label supplies no underlying breach. The holding neither adds a discretion condition to asset-control responsibility nor immunizes misrepresentation, investment misconduct, discriminatory administration or an actual diversion. Dismissal follows from the particular pleaded acts and failed legal predicates, not from treating everything Hughes did as sponsor conduct.

### III. Accrued benefits are protected without individual ownership of all surplus

**Holding and operative rule:** Participants in this defined-benefit plan have enforceable rights to promised and statutorily protected accrued benefits, including the employee-contribution floor, but no present individual ownership of all actuarial surplus. Because the original theory identifies no reduction of those protected benefits, use of pooled surplus for lawful plan benefits establishes neither forfeiture nor an anti-cutback violation.

**Authority:** The nine Justices join the no-surplus-ownership and no-reduction holding. The statutory distinction between required and voluntary contributions explains the eight Associates' rejection of the original employee-contribution theory; no benefit amount is calculated.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §§203(a)(1) and 204(c) protect accrued employee-derived benefits without converting all actual investment appreciation into a separate participant account. For mandatory contributions, the statutory measure uses accumulated contributions with the enacted interest treatment and expresses the result as an annual benefit beginning at normal retirement age; another benefit form requires actuarial equivalence. The contribution conditions are employment, plan participation or obtaining employer-derived benefits. Section 204(c)(4)'s separate treatment of voluntary contributions remains intact; the original complaint describes mandatory payroll contributions. The Court decides no disputed computation, applicable rate or amount under those provisions. John Hancock identifies protected plan assets and fiduciary functions; it does not give each retiree title to a share of an ongoing defined-benefit plan's overfunding. No promised benefit or statutory floor was reduced by the theories alleged here. Actual underpayment, a reduction below the statutory floor or violation of a greater benefit promised by the plan remains actionable under its own elements.

### IV. Continuing benefit obligations do not trigger termination distribution

**Holding and operative rule:** Closing the contributory structure to new entrants while continuing the plan and its benefit obligations is not a statutory termination triggering §4044 distribution. A common-law characterization as a wasting trust cannot replace §4041's standard-or-distress termination process or PBGC proceedings under §4042.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join the nontermination holding on these continuing-plan facts. The eight Associates join the separate statutory distribution analysis; no general rule for every partial termination is decided.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the 1991 amendment changed benefit structures within an ongoing plan; it did not terminate the plan merely because new employees could no longer enter its contributory structure. The plan continues to owe and pay benefits. Sections 4041–4044 govern termination and distribution. When their distribution trigger occurs, plan liabilities and the statutory allocation order must be respected, including protection of remaining assets attributable to employee contributions. Employer reversion also requires satisfaction of liabilities, consistency with law and authorization under the plan, and remains subject to the enacted timing and employee-residual protections. The Court applies none of those residual-distribution rules to authorize a present reversion or calculate a distribution here. Protecting employee contributions does not require a court to invent an earlier termination event. Genuine termination, mass exclusion and another event satisfying an enacted termination rule remain outside this holding. The deleted lower-court common-law discussion supplies no alternative trigger.

### V. Incidental employer advantage does not make these benefit payments prohibited transactions

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §406(a)(1)(D), genuine pension payments under an otherwise lawful plan do not become prohibited transfers merely because they save the employer money. Nor does §406(b)(2), which bars a fiduciary from acting in a transaction for an adverse party, establish a violation merely from the sponsor's adoption of a benefit design advantageous to itself.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join Part V, which controls both statutory grounds; Stone-Zsela agrees only in dismissal of the prohibited-transaction component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the alleged recipients are participants of the continuing plan, not Hughes or strangers renamed as beneficiaries. Spink's genuine-benefit-payment rule applies after the separate-plan, unlawful-benefit and premature-distribution predicates fail. Its rule assumes fiduciary implementation for that inquiry and preserves sham transfers, kickbacks, independently unlawful plans and other prohibited transactions. Souter's and Breyer's earlier reservation about a technical release-payment question does not establish that these genuine pension payments are prohibited; they apply the controlling rule to the narrower original allegations here. The distinct adverse-party prohibition of §406(b)(2) does not turn sponsor adoption itself into a fiduciary transaction for an adverse party. There is no blanket benefit-payment exemption, no net-benefit defense, and no reliance on §408 to excuse an otherwise established violation. A later merger transaction or separately pleaded implementation wrong is not decided.

### VI. Fair administration does not promise a later incentive to ineligible retirees

**Holding and operative rule:** The plan's fair-and-equitable administration, interpretation and application clause does not give every retiree a contractual right to an incentive later offered to specified active employees. The original sixth claim fails independently under §502(a)(1)(B) because no benefit due under the operative eligibility terms is withheld, and under §404(a)(1)(D) because the asserted difference is lawful eligibility design rather than unequal administration among eligible participants.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join Part VI and its distinct statutory and plan grounds. Stone-Zsela agrees only in dismissal of this separate plan-enforcement component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Article V, §5.2 has work in governing administration, interpretation and application of plan terms, but does not require identical benefit design for existing retirees and active employees whom the sponsor seeks to induce to retire. Spink defeats fiduciary liability resting solely on creation of the eligibility class. That classification alone would not answer a benefits-enforcement claim: the separate answer is that the administrative clause promises no incentive to persons outside the class and no due benefit is alleged to have been withheld. Curtiss-Wright preserves both actual amendment procedures and authorized adoption; these six original theories identify no separate unauthorized-adoption defect. Mertens does not bar an otherwise proper §502(a)(1)(B) claim and is not the basis for dismissal. Specific statutory benefit, allocation, anti-discrimination or amendment restrictions remain effective. A fairness clause gives no free-standing judicial power to redesign an eligibility scheme.



SOURCE 42: suster_v_marshall_merits_1999-01-28.md
## Holdings

### Judicial-campaign expenditures and the preliminary protection

**Holding and operative rule:** A mandatory ceiling on the total amount of judicial-campaign advocacy receives the demanding First Amendment review applicable to campaign expenditures. Ohio's interest in impartial adjudication and its appearance does not justify this source-independent $75,000 ceiling on the present record; the candidates' likelihood of success supports the existing preliminary expenditure injunction at its actual, consent-extended scope.

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion for the unanimous Court supplies the complete rule, application and remedial limits. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join every controlling proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the judicial office's distinctive function does not remove campaign advocacy from the expenditure rule. Buckley v. Valeo distinguishes the quantity of a candidate's expression from a donor's contribution. City of Cincinnati v. Kruse applies that distinction to a source-independent total ceiling while reserving materially different institutional restrictions. The Court now considers judicial impartiality directly: judges must decide cases impartially, and public confidence in that function is a weighty interest. The lower court's suggestion that corruption is the only interest worthy of consideration is not adopted.

The asserted interest nevertheless does not establish this ceiling's necessary fit. The record concerns contribution influence, adjudicative commitments and bias; the total limit also forbids further personally financed and qualifications-related advocacy without identifying the corresponding danger. That a candidate can communicate adequately below the ceiling does not explain why additional advocacy may be prohibited. A concern about interested donors does not make every expenditure a contribution restriction. Colorado Republican preserves that transaction-specific distinction rather than immunizing transfers or other financial arrangements.

Separate contribution, solicitation, disclosure and recusal measures address different dangers. Their relevance does not establish that every such measure is valid or invariably sufficient, and no proof of a completed bribe or compulsory empirical waiting period is required. The candidates' continuing exposure under the old canon gives the preliminary protection practical legal effect despite the completed election and amendment. The amended canon remains for consideration below on its own text and record. The Court affirms the confined preliminary protection; it neither enters a final statewide decree nor adjudicates the separate transfer ruling.

**Application and limits:** Protection continues for the plaintiffs, plaintiff-intervenors and their respective opponents under the October 22, 1996 consent extension, including Maloney's later preliminary protection. It does not extend to every Ohio judicial candidate. The removed $18,750 primary sublimit and its separate consent decree are outside this decision. Ordinary recusal and disqualification, distinct ethics restrictions and lawful donor-contribution controls are undisturbed. The state-law remand is preserved, and no new general preliminary-injunction test is adopted.



SOURCE 43: wright_v_ernst_and_young_merits_1999-02-04.md
## Holdings

All three holdings are adopted in the complete Opinion of the Court by the nine Justices identified in Opinion Topology. Each has direct majority authority; no combination of separate rationales is required.

### I. Private approval does not itself make the auditor the public speaker

**Holding and operative rule:** An auditor's private review and oral approval do not, on these allegations, make an issuer's expressly unaudited release the auditor's own public certification when no auditor representation was communicated or attributed to investors. A general market expectation about an outside auditor's involvement cannot supply that missing representation; the Court does not adopt a universal public-name requirement for every deceptive act, agency relationship, scheme, or indirect communication.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that responsibility for one's own statement and responsibility for helping another's fraud are distinct. Central Bank preserves that distinction in recognizing a confined assistance action. First Jersey imposes primary responsibility for proved hands-on deception, not for office, ownership, or assistance alone. BT's January 30 release did not identify Ernst & Young or convey its assurances and expressly described the figures as unaudited. The pleaded January 22 oral approval therefore does not turn BT's statement into the auditor's public certification. The contention that investors assumed an auditor's involvement does not identify an assurance the auditor actually communicated through this release. This conclusion addresses the primary-speaker theory presented; it neither immunizes a culpable assistant nor decides responsibility for an auditor's own report, independently attributed statement, or other direct deception.

### II. The preserved assistance allegations require application of Central Bank

**Holding and operative rule:** A preserved allegation of knowing or severely reckless affirmative substantial assistance to an underlying securities fraud cannot be dismissed merely because the assistant's conduct was undisclosed or the public statement was not attributed to it. The defendant must know or severely recklessly disregard both the fraud and its assisting role; severe recklessness requires an extreme departure from ordinary care presenting a specific danger of deception known or so obvious that the defendant must have appreciated it, and the plaintiff must establish the underlying fraud, qualifying assistance, legally sufficient reliance on that fraud, causation, and recoverable loss.

**Controlling explanation:** Central Bank requires no separate disclosure duty for affirmative assistance, but excludes negligence, professional status, valuation concerns alone, ordinary services without the necessary culpability and assisting effect, and mere duty-free silence. Investors need not rely on concealed assistance itself; that rule creates no automatic reliance presumption. Wright's amended complaint alleges affirmative approval after discovery of a specific accounts-payable under-accrual, and substantial participation was argued below. These are existing allegations to test, not a new claim supplied by the Court. The release's unaudited label bears on primary attribution but does not license knowing assistance to deception. Conversely, knowledge that figures will reach investors is not knowledge that they are fraudulent. Later discovery of accounting irregularities, alleged failures to follow auditing standards, correction of the reported profit, and a subsequent price fall do not themselves establish the required January mental state or the other elements. The strongest independent objection—that the alleged conduct shows only ordinary professional error—must be considered under the correct rule. The categorical rejection of assistance cannot decide it. The Court vacates that ground and remands for ordinary application of the elements and applicable pleading requirements, without finding that the complaint satisfies them or prescribing discovery, amendment, or recovery.

### III. The separate prospectus branch remains dismissed

**Holding and operative rule:** Correction of the categorical assistance rule for the pleaded January 1996 conduct does not revive the separately deficient 1995 prospectus theory or insert a later duty-to-correct claim into the amended complaint. That dismissal remains intact, and the remand creates no entitlement to amend.

**Controlling explanation:** The prospectus branch has its own pleading and preservation defects, including the absence of the duty-to-correct claim later pressed in opposition papers and deficient allegations concerning the earlier scienter, this class's reliance, and causation. Wright declined leave to replead before final judgment. Those circumstances differ from the affirmative January approval already alleged and argued. Central Bank corrects the governing law for the preserved assistance claim; it neither supplies missing allegations nor erases the distinct prospectus defects. The Court therefore leaves that dismissal in effect while returning only the January assistance branch for consideration under the proper rule.

Actual auditor reports, independently attributed statements, direct market deception, SEC enforcement, and nonfederal professional liability remain separate. The Court decides no settlement credit or ultimate recoverable amount.



SOURCE 44: hart_v_gwadosky_threshold_1999-02-11.md
## Holdings

### I. Qualification ends this federal controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** A challenge seeking relief from an initiative's failure to qualify becomes moot when the initiative qualifies and the claimant shows neither a surviving redressable injury nor a reasonable expectation of renewed exposure to the challenged restriction. The exception for a dispute capable of repetition yet evading review requires both duration too short for complete review and reasonably expected renewed exposure of the same claimant; the continuing existence of an election rule alone does not satisfy it.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. These nine directly support the claimant-specific mootness holding and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the December 23 qualification removed the ballot-access injury presented for review. The Secretary accepted 47,406 signatures against the new threshold of 42,101 and found the petition valid for legislative consideration. The determination also states that previously accepted signatures already exceeded the new threshold. Petitioners' asserted future harm concerned further collection to secure this same November 1999 effort; acceptance removes that need. The record supplies no further initiative plan, surviving individual-capacity damages claim or other collateral injury redressable by the requested federal ruling.

*Spencer v. Kemna* requires a concrete continuing injury and both elements of the recurrence exception. *Norman v. Reed*, *Chandler v. Miller* and *Libertarian Party of Illinois v. Rednour* preserved review because their records established continuing electoral consequences or actual future exposure. They establish no general exception for election disputes. The persistence of Maine's rules and petitioners' repeated work on this measure do not supply the missing post-qualification injury. Nor does possible legislative rejection revive a circulation obstacle the initiative has already overcome. *Arizonans for Official English* prevents treating a general declaratory request against an official-capacity defendant as an unpleaded surviving damages claim.

*DeFunis v. Odegaard* requires the Court to resolve federal mootness even when reviewing a state judgment. *Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation* reserved residency and supplies neither a residency holding nor jurisdiction here. The Court reaches no residence, registration or signature-validity merits. Qualification is not enactment, a completed election, repeal of the restrictions or a finding that petitioners will never circulate another initiative.

### II. Equitable vacatur leaves state proceedings to Maine

**Holding and operative rule:** When a changed qualification threshold removes this controversy while petitioners continue seeking qualification and review, section 2106 permits equitable vacatur of the reviewed constitutional judgment and remand for proceedings allowed by state law. Vacatur is justified here by those circumstances, rather than by an automatic entitlement whenever review becomes moot; Article III does not require an order directing dismissal of the state action.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; those eight Associates directly support its equitable rationale. Stone-Zsela agrees in the vacatur and bounded state remand without joining Part II's additional equitable explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that mootness and the proper treatment of the judgment require separate decisions. *U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership* preserves a just disposition under section 2106 while rejecting automatic vacatur; its settlement rule is not a bar to relief whenever a litigant continues trying to obtain the practical benefit sought. The gubernatorial election changed the qualifying threshold. Petitioners' resubmission pursued qualification while seeking review; it was neither a settlement, consent to the adverse constitutional judgment nor abandonment of review. *Kentucky Right to Life v. Stengel* supports clearing a constitutional merits determination that intervening circumstances prevent the reviewing loser from obtaining review of, subject to the actual equities.

Leaving the judgment intact is a substantial alternative because this is a state proceeding and petitioners voluntarily resubmitted. But the external threshold change and continued pursuit of review justify vacating the reviewed judgment here. *DeFunis* supplies the bounded state-remand form. *Arizonans* concerned lower federal judgments and does not require importing its federal-action dismissal direction into Maine's courts. Maine determines the status of the remaining pleadings under its own law.

The judgment is vacated and the cause remanded for such proceedings as state law provides. This neither declares the judgment void when entered nor reopens abandoned factual-residency challenges. The administrative qualification remains undisturbed. The Court orders no new signature count, validates no otherwise defective signature and determines no future legislative or electoral outcome.



SOURCE 45: boring_v_buncombe_county_board_of_education_merits_1999-02-17.md
## Holdings

### Assigned instructional production and the asserted personal speech right

**Holding and operative rule:** A public-school teacher's selection, production, and direction of material as part of the assigned school instructional program constitute institutional curricular expression subject to the school system's lawful control; the artistic discretion entrusted to the teacher does not, on this claim, create a personal First Amendment right against curricular and related employment control. This rule concerns the assigned school production, and does not authorize punishment of separate citizen speech or an independently unconstitutional classification or exclusion.

**Authority:** The complete Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas, supplies five direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the personal right asserted must be identified before deciding whether an employer's justification defeats it. Boring selected and directed *Independence* through her assigned advanced acting class and an interscholastic competition for which the school bore institutional responsibility. Artistic judgment and public controversy do not turn that instructional choice into a personal right to control the institution's expression. Hazelwood distinguishes personal student expression from school-sponsored activity and recognizes legitimate educational control; it does not give the employee administering that activity a parallel personal editorial entitlement. Settle protects a student's otherwise permissible perspective within an assignment against categorical religious exclusion disguised as pedagogy. Its constitutional limit remains, but the student's claim does not establish a teacher's personal right over the school's selected program. Jeffries protected an unaffiliated address expressly outside assigned work; Brewster addressed a particular attendance-error report, not selection of instructional content.

The strongest contrary argument is that Boring challenges retaliation after approval, not the school's final power to choose a play. The Court accepts the pleaded approval, compliance, and retaliatory motivation at their proper status. Those allegations do not create the missing personal curricular interest or make approval an irrevocable constitutional delegation. This conclusion does not establish that officials actually acted for legitimate reasons. Settle and the independently applicable constitutional prohibitions prevent treating every act labeled curricular as lawful by definition. But the granted claim identifies no separate citizen expression or independent forbidden classification supplying the personal right asserted here. Waters and Mt. Healthy regulate justification and cause once protected conduct is established; they do not supply that antecedent right. Dismissal therefore stands on this confined ground.

The conditional second question is not reached because the asserted personal interest in this assigned curricular act is absent. The Court decides no standard for a distinct protected interest outside assigned curricular responsibilities, no rule for every duty-related statement of every public employee, and no materially different university academic-freedom question. Statutory, contractual, tenure, and state-law limits remain separate. The Court finds neither insubordination nor lawful actual motive nor a same-decision defense.
