### The EEOC may administer the compensation Congress expressly authorized

When federal-sector Title VII intentional discrimination falls within 42 U.S.C. §1981a's damages authorization, §2000e-16(b) permits the EEOC to award that compensation as an appropriate remedy. The agency must preserve §1981a's substantive conditions and monetary limits; its power does not depend on an administrative jury, and a proper court action retains the statutory jury election.

Section 717(b) delegates enforcement through appropriate remedies and introduces the listed equitable forms as examples. Congress subsequently added compensation to the remedies for intentional discrimination under §717. Reading those enactments together gives effect to both the express monetary authorization and the administrative mechanism Congress retained. Section 1981a's reference to an action does not silently confine §717 as a whole to its judicial subsection. Its jury clause regulates trial in court; it neither requires a jury in an administrative proceeding nor creates a governmental appeal from an adverse EEOC award. The Government's inability to demand judicial review therefore does not negate the separately authorized administrative remedy. This conclusion satisfies Lane's requirement of unequivocal enacted consent and Blue Fox's demand for a genuine statutory entitlement; administrative convenience supplies no independent waiver.

The compensation category applies to unlawful intentional discrimination, not disparate impact, and requires that the complaining party cannot recover under §1981. It excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by §706(g). The statutory caps remain applicable, and punitive damages are unavailable against governments, government agencies and political subdivisions. When available §102 damages are sought in court, any party may demand a jury; the court must not tell that jury the statutory caps. No damages under another statute or free-standing appropriations authority follow.

### Statutory authority does not decide Gibson's preserved procedural alternatives

The Seventh Circuit's no-power premise cannot sustain its judgment, but identifying an available administrative remedy does not establish whether Gibson exhausted it or has a properly preserved alternative answer to the exhaustion defense. The reviewed judgment must be vacated and remanded for consideration of proper presentation and, if appropriate, resolution of those alternatives.

McCarthy requires attention to the governing statutory scheme and the actual administrative remedy; its result involving a compensation-incapable prison process cannot resolve a claim involving an EEOC that may compensate. Gibson's asserted cash-award request, completed complaint procedures and inadequate counseling do not become judicial findings merely because the Court rejects the appellate statutory premise. The Court decides neither preservation nor actual exhaustion, estoppel, compensable loss or amount. It creates no categorical exception to an applicable exhaustion requirement. No new damages award or trial is ordered by this decision itself.

### The presented facial vagueness challenge fails

A juvenile curfew that specifies age, hours, covered places and the prohibited act of remaining is not facially vague on this record merely because its exception for constitutionally protected First Amendment activity requires legal application in particular encounters. ### The preliminary-period authority controversy remains live

When a preliminary restraint is challenged as beyond the court's legal power during the period before judgment, later success on the underlying contract and conversion to a permanent injunction do not necessarily extinguish that distinct controversy. Here the asserted preliminary-period injury and existing bond preserve a consequential authority question, while actual bond liability and damages remain undecided.

Camenisch distinguishes a surviving case from a surviving preliminary-injunction issue. The Court applies that distinction rather than treating the mere presence of a bond as conclusive. The investors' success on their contract may justify relief after judgment without establishing authority to restrain an unsecured debtor beforehand. GMD's acceptance of the permanent conversion therefore does not resolve whether the earlier restraint was legally authorized. A ruling on that distinct question can matter to the asserted loss during the preliminary period. This is not review of the final money judgment or an adjudication of the separate turnover appeal. Whether a proper bond claim ultimately proves wrongful restraint, causal loss and an amount, and whether independent defenses defeat it, remains for the appropriate proceedings. No universal rule preserves every appeal involving an expired injunction and a bond.

### General equity does not supply this unsecured-creditor asset freeze

A federal court may not use general equitable authority, at the request of a general unsecured creditor suing solely for money damages, to freeze a defendant's unencumbered assets before judgment when the creditor has no lien, equitable interest, statutory authorization or other recognized source of prejudgment control. Likely success and threatened inability to collect do not supply the missing power.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices adopt the complete rule, explanation and remedy.

The historically conferred equity jurisdiction permits flexibility within its sources and objects. It does not authorize creation of a security interest for an unadjudicated general debt. The judgment and proprietary prerequisites protect the debtor-creditor relationship; merger of law and equity did not erase them. Rule 65 regulates injunction procedure, and Rule 64 preserves authorized prejudgment security routes. Neither makes the inadequacy of collection itself a new source of power. The bondholders sought to secure payment from assets that were not the subject of their contract claim. The strong findings favoring them therefore address the usefulness of a restraint, not its legal availability.

Deckert involved equitable rescission and preservation in that setting. First National City Bank involved federal tax collection and statutory authority. Their preservation reasoning does not create the omitted private-creditor predicate. De Beers confirms that preliminary relief must remain within recognized equitable authority related to the available final remedy. Expanding general equity here would supply security and affect other creditors outside enacted attachment and bankruptcy arrangements. The Court does not restrict injunctions protecting specific property in dispute, statutory freezes, independently authorized fraudulent-transfer remedies, bankruptcy orders, Rule 64 attachment or postjudgment enforcement. Nor does it decide bond recovery, create a lien, establish fraud or adjudicate the rights of absent Mexican creditors.

## Holdings

### This OIG examination is covered by the statutory representation right

An investigator employed in NASA's Office of Inspector General is a representative of NASA when conducting the agency-related examination presented here. Section 7114(a)(2)(B) requires an opportunity for the exclusive representative to assist at the examination of a unit employee in connection with an investigation when the employee reasonably believes discipline may result and requests representation; OIG's independence from ordinary management does not eliminate that relationship.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this rule, explanation and limits.

The chapter defines agency broadly and does not exclude NASA. Its representation provision is not confined to the immediate component that negotiates with the union. Excluding specified investigative employees from membership in a bargaining unit under §7112(b)(7) does not exempt their examinations of covered employees under §7114. This investigator works within NASA on agency-related conduct capable of producing discipline. The Court does not treat every employee or outside examiner as an agency representative.

The IG Act must be independently respected. It creates an independent unit within the agency and protects investigations from managerial interference, while retaining an institutional relationship and general supervision. Those protections do not require elimination of a limited statutory right belonging to the examined employee. NFFE, Haggar and Aguirre-Aguirre require enacted authority and a permissible actual explanation, rather than automatic deference. The Authority's coverage explanation satisfies that inquiry here; its agency label is not the reason it prevails.

Weingarten's limited assistance function reconciles the right with orderly investigation. The representative may assist but may not obstruct questioning, compromise protected information, direct the investigation or require bargaining at the interview; the investigator may insist on the employee's own account. Those limits do not validate reducing representation to silent observation where interference is otherwise established. Reasonable fear and request remain statutory predicates. The Court decides no universal interview protocol, outside-agency or FBI examination, wider criminal inquiry, national-security question or negotiated extension of rights.

### Headquarters may be directed to secure this limited statutory compliance

Under the retained general-supervision relationship, NASA Headquarters may be required to direct OIG compliance with the limited representation right recognized here. That direction does not authorize Headquarters to determine investigative subjects, evidence, conclusions or termination or to bargain for expanded control over OIG procedures.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part II is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplying five direct votes for the complete rationale. Stone-Zsela's additional vote supports the judgment only.

Coverage and parent-agency responsibility are separate questions. The IG Act preserves general supervision by the head or next-ranking officer while forbidding specified interference with initiation, conduct and completion. Requiring compliance with an independently applicable federal protection respects both provisions. The Authority's order calls for that compliance, not management direction of investigative outcomes. Headquarters' relationship to OIG therefore supports this limited instruction even though it could not lawfully take over the investigation.

NFFE distinguishes available authority from its lawful exercise; the actual compliance ground here answers the distinct remedial question. Haggar and Aguirre-Aguirre require neither automatic enforcement nor an unnecessary new agency proceeding when the statutory analysis and actual explanation support the order. The Court sustains the existing compliance and notice relief without a general rule of derivative liability, a new governance power or an enlarged representational scheme. The case-specific preservation history permits review of this statutory objection; it creates no general exception for objections withheld from an agency.

### Suppression and reasonable disclosure reliance establish cause here

Governmental suppression of the five documents, combined with counsel's reasonable reliance on the prosecution's open-file practice and the Commonwealth's later completeness representation during state collateral proceedings, supplies cause for this omitted claim. General awareness of interviews and changing recollection, and the existence of a discretionary discovery avenue, do not on these facts establish knowledge of the missing factual basis or a duty to distrust the asserted disclosure.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part I has eight direct votes; Thomas dissents from this ground.

Murray v. Carrier and Amadeo distinguish an external impediment and unavailable factual basis from ordinary attorney error. The case-team police possessed the missing material, and Kyles does not let the prosecution avoid its disclosure responsibility through personal ignorance. That responsibility is distinct from an entitlement to unrestricted open-file access. Here the actual open-file practice and later response asserting full Brady disclosure reasonably concealed the particular basis of this claim.

The Commonwealth's later response reinforced reliance while state proceedings continued; it is not moved to a time before the state petition was filed. References to several interviews and recollection changes gave general information, not the five documents' specific contents. Nor does a discretionary discovery mechanism prove that a hypothetical request would have produced them. The Court decides this combination, not automatic cause for every Brady allegation or a new right to collateral counsel. Coleman v. Thompson's default rule and recognized excuses remain. Cause permits the materiality inquiry; it does not establish prejudice, a completed Brady violation or entitlement to a writ.

### The cumulative suppression does not undermine confidence in capital guilt

Considering all lawful combined uses of the five established suppressed documents in the whole record, there is no reasonable probability of a different capital-guilt verdict sufficient to undermine confidence in that verdict. This conclusion assesses revised preparation and presentation as well as impeachment; it does not require proof of innocence or rest merely on legally sufficient remaining evidence.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part II is joined by all nine, with Thomas adopting it as an independent alternative to his default ground.

Kyles requires the documents to be considered together. Their identification uncertainty, reconstructed timing and photo assistance could support a materially different cross-examination and investigation of how a tentative account became confident. Disclosure could also alter the defense presentation; counsel's existing arguments are not binding concessions that erase those uses. The Court assumes substantial damage to Stoltzfus's confident portrayal and considers the Henderson-related defense evidence and challenges to Tudor.

The independent driving, property, bank-card, blood and forensic evidence and the supported account of the killing conversation nevertheless preserve confidence in Strickler's active joint participation. Stoltzfus did not witness the killing. The actual instruction permitted joint participation in the fatal acts and did not require identifying who alone dropped the rock; evidence of Henderson's participation therefore does not necessarily displace Strickler's active role. The Court does not convert possession into eyewitness proof or add later admissions and an unverified recantation to the jury's evidence.

The five-document assessment needs no suppression finding concerning Exhibits 2, 7 and 8. It adjudicates no freestanding challenge to the instruction's disputed predicate. No second harmlessness inquiry is imposed: the claim fails at Brady's initial confidence inquiry on the guilt record itself.

### The separate death-selection inquiry also retains confidence

After separately considering the five documents' combined lawful effects on the jury's individualized selection of death, the Court finds no reasonable probability of a different penalty recommendation sufficient to undermine confidence in that selection. Reliable capital guilt or a surviving aggravator does not by itself answer this question.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part III is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Thomas's join is an independent alternative. Five other Justices directly adopt the complete rationale without relying on Thomas's default position.

The witness's vivid portrayal of Strickler as the aggressive initiator matters beyond the bare fact of participation. The Court considers the potential for a substantially changed defense account, including greater emphasis on Henderson's role, and the actual jury-closing references to grabbing and abducting a stranger in the dangerousness argument. The longer leadership passage at 20R15 was addressed to the judge, not the penalty jury. The prior-record account describes no convictions involving actual violence; the Court does not invent such convictions or equate that qualification with absence of all dangerousness evidence.

Even with severe impeachment of Stoltzfus's certainty and relative-role portrayal, the independent evidence of active fatal conduct, the brutality of the rock killing and the supported surrounding conduct retain their force in the individualized death choice. The Court evaluates those facts with the revised defense uses; it does not treat argument brevity, lawful eligibility or the existence of an aggravator as conclusive. The complete jury mitigation presentation is not established by the sentencing judge's later account, and no assumed rejection of childhood-abuse testimony supplies this conclusion. The judge's separate leadership comments furnish neither a substitute jury finding nor an independent cure.

Tuggle preserves the distinction between eligibility and selection within its own assistance setting; it does not mandate this Brady result. Kyles governs the present initial materiality inquiry without a second duplicative harmlessness hurdle. No different burden from Coleman, Lilly or Neder is imposed. The claim fails because the collective disclosure counterfactual does not sufficiently change confidence in this actual selection, not because every eligible defendant may receive death.
