**Case and dockets:** Jackson v. Benson; No. 98-376; OT1998.
**Event and date:** Merits decision; 1999-03-16.
**Result:** Vacate the federal Establishment Clause disposition and its dependent blanket injunction-dissolution direction and remand, 5-4 on that disposition. Six join the funded-function holding; Stone would reverse and require more definite payment relief, while Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm.
**Version / lineage:** Original decision.

## Holdings
#### Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction

**Operative rule.** Wisconsin may not justify public payment for the demonstrated devotional instructional function merely by neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement of its provider-cost payment. Where the financed educational work integrates devotional instruction without reliable separation, those features do not cure the expenditure; religious identity alone does not establish that defect, and genuinely independent private benefits and reliably separated secular services retain their distinct protection.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six direct joins control the rule and its application; the remedy has the separate coalition stated below.

**Controlling explanation.** Agostini asks what governmental expenditure purchases. It does not require every act of a recipient to become government speech before the State's financing can be examined. Lemon governs through that updated inquiry: controlling specific prohibitions and established permissible relationships retain their force; unresolved legally sufficient claims require attribution and function analysis. Purpose, effect and institutional relationships supply evidence, not three independent fallback prongs.

The actual payment covers educational operating and debt-service costs and can exceed ordinary tuition. Supported school materials integrate devotional formation into that paid work. A parent's endorsement chooses the provider; it does not separate the purchased instruction. The written-request opt-out concerns required participation, not segregation of public expenditures. Academic standards and financial audits verify performance and spending without making the financed work secular.

The Court accepts the real parental choice and educational alternatives. Witters protects its genuine individual vocational benefit and Mueller its generally available tax deduction. Neither makes every restricted provider-cost payment the same relationship. Rosenberger permitted a real printing benefit for independently edited and attributed private expression under its actual payment controls; it did not approve the purchase of integrated devotional teaching. Bronx Household's genuinely private access likewise does not decide this expenditure.

Agostini permitted separately administered secular instruction with reliable public controls and safeguards against devotional use. Those functional distinctions answer the private-benefit argument here without condemning a school by its name or mission. The record establishes more than affiliation, but does not establish that every expense or service is devotional. The Court rejects categorical approval of the demonstrated arrangement; it neither imposes a universal voucher prohibition nor creates a right to public subsidy.

#### The corrective judgment must preserve the actual limits of the federal conflict

**Operative rule.** The federal approval and its dependent blanket injunction-dissolution direction are vacated, and the lower courts must determine appropriate provider/payment relief and state-law severability under the funded-function holding. Relief must prevent the established federal conflict while preserving lawful severable applications; the judgment neither rewrites state law nor commands an immediate ban on every payment or religious provider.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part II is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five directly support this disposition and remedy. Stone-Zsela seeks the different reversal and mandatory direction stated separately; the three affirming dissenters' conditional acceptance of remedial limits supplies no extra vacatur vote.

**Controlling explanation.** Dalton requires relief to follow the established federal conflict while preserving lawful applications. Leavitt distinguishes correcting a severability premise from authorizing automatic enforcement. Wisconsin's categorical approval cannot support its blanket dissolution direction, but that does not determine every provider's allowable payment or resolve the state severability question the state court left open.

The supported integration evidence must be used, not erased or replaced with a presumption from religious identity. An adequate existing record may support the necessary determinations without a new trial. The August 25, 1995 educational-status qualification for affected pupils remains at its lawful scope: it preserves that enrollment-status date, not an exemption from income limits, caps or other conditions and not a guarantee of unconstitutional payments.

Independent Wisconsin-law and racial-equality dispositions remain untouched. A secular-services remedy governs public expenditures; it does not require schools to restructure their private theology. The Court orders no school closure, pupil transfer, recoupment, damages or replacement program and decides no individual coercion claim.

**Case and dockets:** National Committee of the Reform Party of the United States of America v. Democratic National Committee; Ninth Circuit No. 98-15443; 168 F.3d 360.
**Event and date:** Merits decision, 1999-03-17; OT1998, chunk 5.
**Result:** Affirm the reviewed funding dismissals and the jurisdictional dismissal of the affiliation claim, 9-0. Eight join the antecedent statutory-channel explanation; separate damages and certification judgments remain unreviewed and undisturbed.
**Version / lineage:** Original decision.

## Holdings

### I. Demonstrated electoral support does not create a duty to equalize subsidies

**Operative rule.** The Constitution does not require equal presidential-campaign subsidies merely because parties compete or qualify for public financing. On the pleaded claim, a formula tied to prior electoral support and the alleged inability to match larger major-party resources do not establish the materially different practical suppression or discriminatory administration that remains open to challenge.

**Authority.** Parts I-III of Thomas's opinion are joined by all eight other Justices and control their respective holdings. Part IV has the distinct eight-Justice coalition in the opinion table.

**Controlling explanation.** Buckley v. Valeo, Part III, sustains support-sensitive public funding while preserving a properly supported different application. Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith applies that distinction to the actual burden: access to electoral competition does not itself entitle a party to equal government assistance, but the funding label supplies no constitutional immunity.

The reported comparison is about $29 million for the Reform campaign and $62 million for each major-party campaign. The statutory categories use prior vote shares: at least 25 percent for a major party and at least five but less than 25 percent for a minor party. The scheme also contains a returning-candidate route and post-election eligibility for minor or new candidates reaching five percent. Those routes are not eliminated by the present decision. Section 9003(c) permits a minor- or new-party candidate's total qualified expenses to reach the major-party entitlement, with private contributions covering the excess of those expenses over public receipts; the smaller subsidy is not itself a total-spending ceiling. The Court does not find that fundraising succeeded or decide a new payment entitlement.

The Court accepts the reported disparity and inability to close it. Those allegations assert parity, not a different pleaded practical disability from the one Buckley considered. A party need not disappear or stop fielding candidates before challenging a substantially suppressive application. Viewpoint favoritism, concrete exclusionary administration and a materially different cumulative burden remain open; none is adjudicated on unreported facts here.

### II. The actual contribution-limit argument requires its own narrow answer

**Operative rule.** The alleged inability to close this public-subsidy gap under the $1,000 individual-candidate contribution restriction does not, by itself, state a materially different constitutional impairment from the parity claim Buckley rejected. This holding neither establishes a permanent dollar safe harbor nor resolves a properly supported prohibitory or cumulative contribution burden.

**Controlling explanation.** A direct contribution to a candidate differs from the candidate's own expenditure, independent advocacy and an aggregate restriction on association through intermediaries. Buckley preserves those distinctions. Kentucky Right to Life's direct-limit holding applies contribution doctrine within its actual facial scope; its separate aggregate and sourcewide-receipt holdings do not make one amount valid for every time and circumstance. Day's intermediary holding protects a different recipient class and meaningful pooling. Colorado Republican protects actual independent expression while preserving scrutiny of genuine candidate channels.

The plaintiffs' contribution argument is considered separately, not dismissed for the affiliation claim's standing defect or because public funding was voluntary. Accepting their inability-to-close allegation, equality of total resources remains the asserted constitutional demand. No finding of actual corruption, successful solicitation or consent to every contribution restriction supplies the decision. Nonrecipients do not accept the program's spending ceiling, while generally applicable contribution restrictions remain a separate question. A genuinely different burden would require its own analysis and the proper statutory tribunal.

### III. Invalidating this affiliation maximum would not redress the asserted representation injury

**Operative rule.** These plaintiffs lack standing to challenge the FEC's maximum of three members affiliated with one party because they identify no concrete exclusion caused by that maximum that its removal would likely redress. The jurisdictional dismissal decides neither the cap's constitutional merits nor a different discriminatory appointment or enforcement claim.

**Controlling explanation.** Lujan requires a concrete injury, a connection to the challenged provision and likely redress. Northeastern Florida protects the opportunity to compete free of an actual discriminatory barrier without demanding proof that the claimant would ultimately succeed. Commerce and Glavin likewise preserve concrete injury and redress without making certainty a universal requirement.

The provision requires six presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed Commissioners and limits one party to three seats. It does not reserve seats to named major parties or prohibit Reform affiliates. The reported three-and-three practice is not the statutory command. Removing the maximum would permit the same appointees, and the supported account identifies no appointment opportunity or particular enforcement injury obstructed by this maximum. The defect is the missing cap-created barrier and remedial connection, not failure to guarantee an appointment. Financial injury from the separate allocation statute cannot supply standing against every provision governing its administrator. No Appointments Clause, separation-of-powers, equal-protection, due-process or associational merits ruling follows.

### IV. The granted claims supply no basis for bypassing or requiring a different statutory tribunal

**Operative rule.** A claim lacking Article III standing or presenting a constitutional theory plainly foreclosed by controlling law may be rejected before invoking the extraordinary statutory tribunal. A live substantial constitutional question cannot be bypassed merely because a court expects it ultimately to fail; a substantial FECA question follows section 437h, while a substantial Fund Act implementation or construction claim follows section 9011(b).

**Authority.** Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part IV. Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation.** California Medical Association v. FEC supplies the standing and wholly-insubstantial-question distinction. It does not equate a weak ultimate claim with a frivolous question. The Court's present conclusions concern the reported legal theories, accepting their allegations, rather than an adverse factual finding or failure to produce unavailable papers.

Section 437h directs qualifying FECA constitutional questions brought by an authorized plaintiff to the court of appeals sitting en banc. Statutory eligibility, including that of a national committee or presidential voter, does not establish standing on every claim. Section 9011(b) separately provides the three-judge district court and direct Supreme Court appeal for its Fund Act suits, without requiring exhaustion of administrative or other remedies. Section 9011(a)'s D.C. Circuit review of Commission action within thirty days is another route, not a substitute for either. The financing theories as presented are foreclosed and the affiliation claim lacks standing; no statutory-tribunal remand follows. This antecedent explanation enters no independent disposition of the separate certification judgments outside the grant.

## Holdings

### Faculty workload administration supplies a rational basis for this distinction

**Holding and operative rule.** Ohio may exclude public-university faculty workload standards from collective bargaining to promote classroom teaching without proving that bargaining caused each earlier reduction in teaching. An ordinary occupational classification must rationally serve a legitimate purpose; a challenger cannot establish irrationality merely by identifying a lack of empirical proof or a different possible means.

**Authority.** The eight Justices joining the per curiam adopt this rule, its application and the stated limits in full.

**Controlling explanation.** Beach supplies ordinary rational-basis review without an empirical-certainty requirement. Nordlinger permits reasonable generalization, while Heller's distinct holdings require a rational connection to the actual disadvantage and preserve legislative choice among rational arrangements. Faculty classroom workloads are the subject Ohio seeks to administer. Removing those workload standards from bargaining can enable universities to increase or standardize classroom teaching, whether or not bargaining caused every past decline. The fact that other public employees retain bargaining rights does not defeat that subject-specific connection. Equality Foundation requires examination of the law's actual reach; it does not equate this defined management measure with a sweeping exclusion from ordinary civic protection. The presented record supplies no forbidden-purpose or independently protected-right ground that defeats the rational connection. The Court decides neither independent Ohio constitutional limits nor other bargaining and association rights.

## Precedent Treatment

- Beach is applied: ordinary economic and occupational classifications do not require empirical proof of the legislature's proposed causal account.
- Nordlinger is applied: reasonable generalization may sustain a classification despite imperfect fit.
- Heller's separate holdings are preserved: review examines the actual disadvantage without converting legislative choice into a necessity or empirical-certainty test.
- Equality Foundation is distinguished: the workload exclusion has a defined management relationship and does not enact the sweeping civic disability addressed there.

## Law After Decision

Existing rational-basis doctrine governs this faculty-workload classification and sustains it against the presented federal challenge. No new scrutiny tier, general immunity for anti-union action or rule overriding independent state protections is adopted.

**Event and date:** Merits decision, 1999-03-23; argued 1998-12-07.
**Result:** Reversed and remanded: formal reversal 8–1; eight Justices support permissible exclusion, with Stevens reserving application; conforming summary-judgment remand 7–2; common Rule 702 and review rules unanimous.

## Holdings

### I. Rule 702 requires reliable specialized knowledge, whatever the field

**Holding and operative rule:** Original Rule 702 requires the trial judge to assess the reliability and fit of scientific, technical and other specialized expert testimony. Experience may supply relevant knowledge, but the expert's professional label does not excuse a reliable connection between the method used and the proposition offered.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt this rule; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Rule addresses knowledge that will assist the factfinder and expressly includes technical and other specialized knowledge. Its reliability requirement cannot turn on whether the witness describes the same practical inference as scientific or experiential. Daubert supplies the preliminary duty to examine foundation and fit; its scientific setting does not establish a categorical exemption for technical testimony. Joiner supplies the requirement that the offered conclusion have an adequately supported inferential connection. Experience can provide that connection, but credentials alone cannot replace it. The Eleventh Circuit therefore erred in excluding experience-based tire analysis from the reliability inquiry. Cross-examination remains available for admissible evidence; it does not eliminate the threshold foundation required by the Rule. This holding does not decide ultimate defect or causation.

**Precedent treatment:** Daubert's reliability-and-fit duty is extended to the other categories of knowledge expressly covered by original Rule 702; its flexible character remains. Joiner's inferential-connection rule applies to this technical proffer, without converting admissibility into a decision on the underlying claim.

### II. The reliability inquiry must fit the proffer and receives abuse-of-discretion review

**Holding and operative rule:** A trial judge may use Daubert's nonexclusive reliability considerations when they help evaluate the particular expertise, method and application, but need not demand every consideration in every field. Appellate abuse-of-discretion review governs both the reasonable choice of how to assess reliability and the ultimate admission or exclusion, while categorical legal error remains independently correctable.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by the same nine-Justice coalition identified for Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Daubert's considerations include testability and testing, peer review and publication, meaningful error information and operational standards, and acceptance. Their usefulness depends on the question and the proposed expertise. A technical practice may yield reliable knowledge without laboratory testing or publication; conversely, the assertion that a method rests on experience does not establish its reliability. The judge must select considerations that examine the method's actual work and explain its connection to the conclusion. Flexibility permits a suitable inquiry, not abandonment of screening. Joiner's allocation of appellate review preserves correction of legal error and deference to permissible evidentiary judgments; an exclusion's effect on summary judgment does not change that allocation. The inquiry concerns preliminary foundation, not proof of the ultimate tort claim. The Court requires no invariant checklist, identical independent study, expert appointment or new hearing, and permits no exclusion merely because the judge disagrees with a well-supported conclusion.

**Precedent treatment:** Daubert's considerations remain useful but nonmandatory tools. Joiner's uniform review rule is applied to technical evidence and to reasonable selection of the reliability inquiry; its protection against unsupported analytical gaps remains intact.

### III-A. The District Court was entitled to reject the unsupported defect inference

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 702 permitted the District Court to reject Carlson's defect inference because he did not reliably explain how his inspection distinguished defect from abuse or why the observations supported the asserted cause. This permissible-exclusion proposition does not itself settle the distinct instruction governing appellate review and summary judgment on remand.

**Authority:** Part III-A of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela joins this bounded application and its unsupported-inference ground; Justice Stevens reserves application for the Court of Appeals. Part III-B's fuller direct-disposition discussion and mandatory conforming remand have a separate coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Carlson's experience was relevant, but experience alone did not establish the connection between his observations and a defect rather than abuse. Assuming that separation caused the blowout did not supply that further inference. Joiner permits the judge to examine the analytical connection without deciding ultimate causation. On this proffer, the District Court was entitled to reject an unsupported bridge between inspection and conclusion. The Court holds rejection permissible, not compulsory for every reasonable judge, and does not discredit visual tire inspection as a category or authorize exclusion merely because a judge disagrees with a well-supported conclusion.

**Precedent treatment:** Joiner's permission to reject an unsupported inference is applied to this technical proffer. Daubert's flexible foundation-and-fit requirement remains distinct from a finding of ultimate causation.

### III-B. The remand must conform to the exclusion and resulting summary judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** On the reconsidered proffer, the case is remanded for a disposition sustaining the exclusion and resulting defense summary judgment, because the record supplies no remaining evidence of defect. The mandate does not require a new hearing, appointed expert or another appellate determination whether to sustain this exclusion.

**Authority:** Part III-B of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven Justices. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justice Stevens oppose this conforming mandate and retain their distinct appellate-review remedies.

**Controlling explanation:** Carlson proposed to exclude overdeflection unless at least two of four physical signs were significant, then infer a manufacturing or design defect. He acknowledged shoulder wear, a bead groove, discoloration, rim-flange marks and inadequately repaired punctures, and explained why he regarded the symptoms as insufficient. Those explanations did not require acceptance of his significance threshold or the resulting elimination of misuse. After reconsideration, the District Court recognized Daubert's flexibility and possible accepted uses of visual inspection; its remaining objection concerned the analysis connecting this inspection to defect. Thus the initial concern about an inflexible checklist does not describe the ruling under review. Joiner's deferential allocation permits the Court to resolve the supported exclusion now; the Court need not leave that application for another appellate determination. Because no other defect evidence remains on this record, the exclusion carries the stated summary-judgment consequence. This direct disposition does not establish the accident's actual cause.

**Precedent treatment:** Joiner's abuse-of-discretion allocation is applied to the reconsidered ruling and its supported procedural consequence. Daubert's adaptable inquiry is followed as reflected in reconsideration; neither decision requires rejection of every experience-based method.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Original Rule 702 governs. The Court adopts no later Rule amendment, universal technical checklist or substantive finding that misuse rather than defect caused the accident. The ruling establishes neither automatic admissibility of experiential evidence nor categorical unreliability of visual inspection.

## Precedent Treatment

Daubert supplies flexible foundation and fit and now reaches all knowledge covered by original Rule 702, while its nonexclusive considerations remain adaptable. Joiner supplies uniform abuse-of-discretion review and permission to examine an unsupported analytical connection; both apply to technical expertise. Neither decision becomes a mandatory-factor checklist or a merits causation rule. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Scientific, technical and other specialized testimony receives reliability-and-fit screening suited to the actual proffer. The judge's reasonable screening method and evidentiary ruling receive abuse-of-discretion review, subject to correction of legal error. This particular exclusion and the resulting summary judgment are sustained; experience-based tire analysis as a category remains available when adequately supported.

**Case and dockets:** South Central Bell Telephone Co. v. Alabama; No. 97-2045; OT1998.
**Event and date:** Merits decision, 1999-03-23; argued 1999-01-19.
**Result:** Eleventh Amendment objection rejected; preclusion and Commerce Clause judgments reversed; remanded for constitutionally adequate state relief, 9–0.
**Version / lineage:** Original decision; supersedes no adjudication. Written in the authorized chunk 6 Run; no Git operation or commit-hash verification performed.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt each following holding and its qualifications; no separate-rationale aggregation is required.

### I. The Eleventh Amendment does not bar review of Alabama's own judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Amendment does not prevent this Court from reviewing under 28 U.S.C. § 1257 the federal questions decided by Alabama's own courts. Such review is distinct from initiating a new federal damages action against the State.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous Opinion of the Court, under the common authority stated above.

**Controlling explanation:** Alabama's immunity objection concerns the character of this proceeding. The taxpayers seek review of the federal judgment entered in litigation in Alabama's courts. McKesson recognizes both this Court's authority to review such federal questions and the State's obligation to administer constitutionally adequate relief when required. Treating appellate review as a new action would insulate the state court's decision of federal law from the review Congress authorized. The Court rejects that objection without deciding an unpresented immunity question concerning a separate federal suit or awarding damages against the State.

**Precedent treatment:** McKesson supplies the distinction between review of a state judgment and initiation of a federal action; it remains effective on that ground.

### II. Shared taxpayer interests do not authorize binding these nonparties

**Holding and operative rule:** Claim or issue preclusion cannot bind these taxpayers to earlier litigation merely because they share the earlier taxpayers' interests or were aware of the public dispute. Binding requires constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or other legally accountable representation, or another recognized relationship consistent with due process; the asserted common-interest rationale supplies none of those prerequisites.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Richards requires lawful authority to bind the person before the State may extinguish that person's own claim by another litigant's judgment. The earlier litigation concerned different taxpayers; shared objectives, vigorous advocacy and the public importance of a tax question do not establish representative authority over these taxpayers' personal liabilities. Awareness of another action creates no general obligation to intervene. Describing the proposed bar as issue preclusion rather than claim preclusion does not cure the missing relationship. The Court rejects the relationship asserted below without inventing a factual finding that every conceivable ground of control or representation has been disproved. Ordinary party preclusion, actual control, adequate class representation, fiduciary representation and authorized public representation retain their lawful operation. A prior decision may also supply precedent without conclusively binding a stranger to its judgment. The tax merits therefore require independent consideration.

**Precedent treatment:** Richards's due-process prerequisite is applied to both proposed forms of preclusion; its protection of lawful representative adjudication and ordinary precedent remains intact.

### III. Alabama's franchise-tax bases discriminate against foreign corporations

**Holding and operative rule:** Alabama's franchise-tax structure discriminates against interstate commerce by taxing foreign corporations on actual capital employed in the State while allowing comparable domestic corporations to use a par-value base that may be set well below actual value. The favorable domestic base cannot be justified by comparing nominal rates or tax labels while disregarding the actual competitive burden.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Oregon Waste requires scrutiny of an express geographic tax preference, and West Lynn Creamery requires examination of linked economic operation. Fulton applies those principles to corporate tax classifications. Here, the ability of domestic corporations to employ the favorable par-value base places a materially heavier franchise-tax burden on comparable foreign corporations. Calling both charges franchise taxes does not equalize their bases. Nor does the State's collection of revenue from domestic corporations in other forms establish that the challenged distinction is evenhanded. Tracy's different regulated-service comparison supplies no exemption for this domestic/foreign classification on the comparable-business facts presented. The Court decides this discriminatory structure, not the validity of every regulation with differing economic effects or every tax imposed on corporations with some common product.

**Precedent treatment:** Oregon Waste's origin-discrimination rule, West Lynn Creamery's practical comparison and Fulton's treatment of corporate tax preferences are applied. Tracy's distinct actual-service comparison is distinguished, not displaced.

### IV. The domestic shares tax does not establish a compensatory defense

**Holding and operative rule:** A compensatory-tax defense requires an identified actual intrastate burden, an interstate charge that roughly approximates and does not exceed that burden, and substantially equivalent taxable events capable of functioning as mutually exclusive proxies. Alabama's domestic shares tax reaches a different event and does not establish the equivalence needed to compensate for the discriminatory foreign-corporation franchise tax.

**Authority:** Part IV of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Oregon Waste and Fulton make these requirements cumulative. A domestic tax does not compensate for a foreign-business disadvantage simply because the two taxes relate economically to corporate activity or raise revenue for the same government. The shares tax concerns a different taxable event from the challenged franchise burden, and Alabama has not established substantially equivalent events serving the claimed compensatory purpose. That failure independently defeats the defense. The Court does not hold that different nominal taxpayers always prevent compensation or prohibit genuinely complementary charges. It requires the substantive equivalence that the asserted defense has not demonstrated here.

**Precedent treatment:** Oregon Waste and Fulton supply the cumulative actual-burden, nonexcess and equivalent-event requirements. Their allowance for genuinely complementary taxes survives; fiscal correlation alone remains insufficient.

**Independently sufficient alternative holding:** Alabama also fails to establish an actual matching domestic burden and a roughly equivalent, nonexcessive interstate burden by invoking other domestic taxes or an aggregate fiscal balance. This separate deficiency defeats the compensatory defense even apart from the absence of equivalent taxable events.

**Authority for the alternative holding:** The same unanimous Part IV coalition.

**Controlling explanation for the alternative holding:** Fulton distinguishes a demonstrated matching burden from a general description of corporate taxation. The present justification does not establish that the domestic burden actually corresponds to the disadvantage imposed on comparable foreign corporations. An assertion that the overall system balances revenue cannot replace that showing. The Court supplies no numerical ratio, new formula or finding that every conceivable tax comparison necessarily fails; it holds the asserted comparison insufficient on this record.

**Precedent treatment for the alternative holding:** Fulton's independently sufficient actual-burden and approximate-equivalence ground is applied without creating a universal calculation.

### V. Alabama must provide adequate relief through lawful state remedial choices

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment is reversed and the case remanded for constitutionally adequate relief under state law. When payment was required before an adequate opportunity to contest the tax, McKesson requires meaningful backward-looking correction; independently, a State may not withdraw a clearly held-out postpayment remedy after payment and substitute an unpursued prepayment route.

**Authority:** Part V of the unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** McKesson permits a lawful refund, constitutionally permissible retrospective equalization, or a permissible combination when backward-looking relief is required. Reich separately protects the postpayment remedy the State clearly held out, including after voluntary payment; ordinary proof, reasonable previously announced conditions and prospective changes with fair notice remain possible. Fulton preserves those rules while leaving actual payment, preservation, state procedures and severability for lawful state administration. Alabama may choose a remedy within those constraints. Prospective correction may govern future taxation, but cannot by itself replace backward-looking relief when the stated conditions require that relief. A genuinely adequate prepayment system presents a different remedial setting. The Court does not select a replacement tax base, order assessments against particular domestic corporations, fix a refund amount or declare every taxpayer entitled to recovery. Those matters depend on the actual claims and lawful state choices, which must cure rather than preserve the unconstitutional disadvantage.

**Precedent treatment:** McKesson's conditional duty of backward-looking correction and Reich's independent no-withdrawal rule govern the remand. Fulton confirms the distinction between invalidating a discriminatory tax and choosing its precise lawful remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No exact refund, replacement assessment, severability ruling or particular taxpayer's satisfaction of payment and preservation conditions is decided. Ordinary valid preclusion and genuinely equivalent compensatory taxes remain possible under their own prerequisites. The Court does not reopen otherwise binding judgments or decide a new federal damages action.

## Precedent Treatment

McKesson supplies authority to review the federal questions in a State's own judgment and the conditional duty of backward-looking relief. Richards defeats the asserted nonparty bar while preserving genuine representative and party preclusion. Oregon Waste, West Lynn Creamery and Fulton govern the practical tax comparison; Oregon Waste and Fulton require all three compensatory predicates and preserve genuinely equivalent taxes. Tracy's regulated-service distinction does not control this classification. Reich supplies the independent prohibition against withdrawing a clearly held-out postpayment remedy. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

The discriminatory domestic/foreign franchise-tax structure cannot stand on the asserted preclusion or compensation grounds. Federal review and nonparty hearing protection remain separate from tax liability and remedy. Alabama retains lawful remedial choices, but must supply backward-looking relief when its governing conditions apply; prospective correction alone cannot discharge that duty. The Court establishes no automatic refund or replacement tax base.

### I. Serious bodily injury and death define aggravated carjacking offenses

**Holding and operative rule:** The version of 18 U.S.C. § 2119 governing the December 1992 offense creates a basic carjacking offense and aggravated offenses when serious bodily injury or death results. **Controlling explanation:** The Court reads the additional injury and death conditions as facts defining aggravated crimes. The Government emphasizes the complete opening description of firearm carjacking, the common punishment command, dependent clauses and penalty headings. Those features support its reading, but they do not establish that the additional facts merely guide sentencing: aggravated offenses may share the basic offense's elements and add the fact that permits greater punishment. Almendarez-Torres supplies a statutory analogue in which placement within a punishment provision did not dispense with an aggravated element. The fairly available graded-offense reading also avoids serious constitutional doubt about assigning to a judge a current-offense fact indispensable to punishment beyond the charged verdict's authority. ### II. The existing indictment and verdict authorize only the basic carjacking range

**Holding and operative rule:** The aggravated injury or death element must be alleged in the federal indictment; absent a valid admission as an element of the charged offense or a valid waiver of jury determination, the Government must submit it to the jury and prove it beyond a reasonable doubt. An ordinary factual admission does not itself cure an indictment that omits the aggravated element. Because injury was neither charged nor established by Jones's verdict, the judge's later injury finding cannot authorize the aggravated range; the aggravated sentence must be vacated, and sentencing on the existing carjacking indictment and verdict may not exceed the fifteen-year basic maximum.

**Authority:** Part II of the same six-Justice Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Hamling requires the federal charge to contain the offense's elements, and Gaudin places their determination beyond a reasonable doubt with the jury. Almendarez-Torres applies those safeguards to a statutory aggravator and holds that an admission to a fact cannot repair its omission from the charge. Here the indictment and general verdict establish only the basic carjacking offense. No valid admission or waiver supplies the missing aggravated charge and verdict. The judge's injury finding therefore cannot perform their work. Witte and Watts permit relevant-conduct findings to select punishment within authority supplied by the conviction; they do not supply additional statutory authority or remove an actual element from the jury. The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit's authorization of the injury-based aggravated sentence, vacates that sentence and remands for lawful proceedings. The fifteen-year ceiling concerns this carjacking count on the existing charge and verdict. It neither fixes the sentence at fifteen years nor determines an aggregate sentence. No acquittal, harmless-error finding or automatic entitlement to another aggravated prosecution is entered; any further prosecution must satisfy its own procedural and constitutional requirements. Monge's completed-insufficiency and renewed-proof question is absent.

**Precedent treatment:** Hamling and Gaudin are applied to the aggravated statutory elements. Almendarez-Torres supplies the distinction between admitting a fact and properly charging the aggravated offense. Witte and Watts retain their ordinary within-authority sentencing operation and provide no basis for this increased statutory range. Monge supplies no independent remedy here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court's ground is statutory construction and the protections of the resulting elements. McMillan's unchanged-maximum holding and Walton's capital rule are not overruled. The death grade is part of the statute's construction, not a finding that Jones caused death.

## Holdings

### I. The combined removal order did not terminate the reserved use rights

**Holding and operative rule.** An unauthorized compelled-removal order does not terminate reserved treaty use rights when its revocation and removal commands are inseparable parts of the same purpose and operation. The 1850 order therefore did not extinguish these rights; the Court does not decide the full extent of an independently exercised presidential power under the treaty's pleasure clause.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, counting the author within that six-Justice coalition. All six join the bounded combined-order rationale and its reservation of independent presidential revocation authority.

**Controlling explanation.** The Removal Act's consent structure did not authorize the compelled removal described by this record. That unlawful removal command could not cancel the separately reserved use rights. The order connected its revocation command to that same removal purpose and operation; treating revocation as a standalone measure would create a materially different executive action rather than preserve an independently chosen lawful termination. Minnesota's strongest argument is that the treaty separately permitted revocation during the President's pleasure. Even assuming an independently lawful revocation could occur, that possibility does not make this combined order severable or validate its unauthorized removal command. The Court decides this order's supported relationship and operation. It adopts neither a prohibition of every independent presidential revocation nor a rule that subsequent nonenforcement restores rights already lawfully extinguished.

### II. The 1855 land cession did not surrender these independent use rights

**Holding and operative rule.** A later land cession does not extinguish separately retained treaty use rights unless the agreement, read in its setting and as the tribal parties reasonably understood it, clearly relinquishes those rights. The 1855 agreement did not surrender the Band's retained hunting, fishing and gathering privileges.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part II has the six-Justice coalition of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation.** Dion requires clear evidence that the disputed right was considered and relinquished, without imposing an invariable formula of express words. Menominee and Fishing Vessel protect retained rights against casual implication. Here the supported negotiations concerned a land purchase and did not address surrender of the independent use privileges. The broad cession language must be read in that setting. Klamath's reservation-dependent exclusive rights do not make every land transfer a surrender of a separately retained use right. Bourland likewise distinguishes regulation based on a power to exclude from personal treaty uses that remain after the relevant land change. The Court therefore rejects Minnesota's claim that comprehensive conveyancing words alone settle this distinct right. No cession by a nonsignatory Band or surrender under another agreement is inferred.

### III. Statehood did not itself extinguish the treaty rights

**Holding and operative rule.** Minnesota's admission to the Union did not itself terminate otherwise surviving federal treaty use rights. Those rights can coexist with lawful, necessary nondiscriminatory conservation regulation; the treaty's presidential-pleasure condition does not make state admission the terminating event.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part III has the same six-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation.** The Supremacy Clause preserves enforceable federal treaty rights after state admission. Winans, Tulee and Fishing Vessel demonstrate the compatibility of reserved uses with lawful conservation authority. Race Horse addressed a differently conditioned right on unoccupied lands. Its treaty-specific construction does not establish that this pleasure-qualified reservation ended at statehood. To the extent Race Horse treats equal footing alone as incompatible with surviving treaty use rights, the Court rejects that proposition. Minnesota's resource-management interest remains substantial, but it can operate through the governing conservation standards rather than an inference of wholesale extinction. The Court does not decide every concrete regulation or displace Race Horse's separate construction of its own treaty condition.

### IV. The affirmance preserves the rights at their actual scope

**Holding and operative rule.** Recognition of the surviving rights within the ceded territory remains in force against the presented extinction claims, subject to the applicable treaty and conservation standards. Survival does not itself decide private access, unrelated criminal conduct, numerical allocations or rights beyond the treaty's territory.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part IV has the same six-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation.** None of the presented terminating acts extinguished the rights, and no supported later event supplies another termination premise. That resolves the reviewed judgment. It does not require reopening every resource-management ruling in the larger proceedings, granting an unrestricted harvest or conferring a veto over every state rule. The declaration and related injunctive framework therefore remain in force within their lawful scope, without a new allocation, title transfer or retrospective award.

## Holdings

### I. The reimbursement condition does not establish the claimed exclusion or transfer of electoral authority

**Holding and operative rule.** A legislature may refer this particular public-private proposal subject to the beneficiary's undertaking to reimburse public election costs when the arrangement preserves equal franchise, viewpoint neutrality and meaningful legal participation. On this record the condition neither charges citizens for voting or ordinary petition access nor transfers electoral administration or the public's approval power to the beneficiary.

**Authority.** Kennedy's unanimous Court opinion controls this and the following two holdings, including each stated limit.

**Controlling explanation.** Anderson and Burdick require examination of the actual injury, the State's precise interests and how those interests require the burden. Severe restrictions require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest; reasonable nondiscriminatory administration receives proportionate review. Here the legislature selected the proposal and its financial contingency. The affiliate has a real choice whether to assume the expense, but voters need not pay to vote and public officials administer the election. Requiring this proposal's beneficiary to bear its special-election costs serves the identified fiscal function without making wealth a citizen qualification. The strongest objection is that the State has conferred private gatekeeping over electoral machinery. That objection requires examining what the private consent actually controls: the undertaking to finance this particular arrangement, not the citizen franchise, tally or public approval. Ordinary petition routes also remain available. Their availability supports this access analysis but would not cure a proven discriminatory referral practice or actual exclusion. The Court establishes no general right to purchase an election, categorical immunity for private funding or presumption that fiscal savings justify any burden.

### II. The supported timing and resource-disparity claim establishes no right to delay or equalize the campaign

**Holding and operative rule.** The short campaign and opposition's inability to match the beneficiary's resources do not, on this record, establish the asserted abridgment of political expression or a right to postpone the election or equalize advocacy. A supported practical inability to organize or communicate, or a deliberately disabling calendar, would require analysis of that actual burden and the State's precise scheduling justification.

**Controlling explanation.** ACLF protects political conversation against inadequately justified direct restraints, while Reform Party rejects the presented entitlement to equal campaigning resources without excusing actual exclusion or suppression. The claim here establishes no separate restriction on the opposition's speech, fundraising, spending or organization. The lower court found no support for the alleged suppressive purpose. That record assessment does not make hostile intent indispensable: a practically prohibitive calendar can require scrutiny without it. Neither public control of counting nor the absence of a voter fee would answer such a burden. But duration and resource disparity alone do not establish that different record. The Court does not supply an emergency rationale for the calendar, find that opponents raised enough money, establish a minimum campaign period or declare every period shorter than sixty days adequate. The State's administration and financing interests support this arrangement at its demonstrated scope; they are not a sufficient answer to every possible restriction.

### III. Financial consent is not an additional governmental electoral vote

**Holding and operative rule.** The affiliate's agreement to reimburse costs is an antecedent financial condition of this legislative proposal, not an allocated vote in the public electorate's decision. Because it gives the affiliate no ballot and changes no citizen's voting weight or the public tally, it does not violate the asserted federal equal-vote rule.

**Controlling explanation.** Reynolds protects equality in governmental electoral choice. Brower argues that an entity able to prevent the election by withholding reimbursement has more consequential power than an ordinary voter. The condition indeed affects whether this arrangement proceeds, but legal consequence alone does not turn private consent to an expense obligation into an electoral ballot. The legislature controls the referral; citizens approve or reject it; the affiliate controls acceptance of its own financial undertaking. No citizen's vote is multiplied or diminished, and no private choice displaces the public result. White's district-dilution setting is different, but absence of districts is not itself an exemption from equal-vote review. The Court rejects this characterization after examining the actual allocation of authority. It leaves separate access and speech claims to their own analysis and does not authorize private control of counted votes or any different arrangement conferring unequal governmental electoral power.

## Holdings

### Continuing kidnapping supplies essential conduct for venue

**Holding and operative rule:** When the charged §924(c) firearm offense is committed during and in relation to a continuing kidnapping, venue lies in any district where that kidnapping continued, even if the firearm was physically used in another district, because commission of the ongoing kidnapping is essential conduct incorporated into the firearm offense. The Government must still prove the distinct firearm act and its required relation to the kidnapping; the predicate's status as a necessary element does not make every related circumstance a venue-conferring act.

**Authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court has the complete seven-Justice coalition identified in Opinion Topology. All seven adopt this rule, its New Jersey application and its limits, supplying direct majority authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court identifies the crime Congress enacted and the conduct constituting it. United States v. Anderson supplies that inquiry; Article III, the Sixth Amendment and Rule 18 tie trial to the charged offense, while §3237(a) permits venue where a continuing offense begins, continues or ends. Section 924(c) requires both firearm use or carriage and commission of the violent crime during and in relation to which that act occurs. Here the defendant continued confining the victim in New Jersey. That continuing kidnapping is incorporated conduct of this charged offense, not a geographical fact borrowed from an unrelated or completed crime. Cabrales forbids locating completed laundering transactions solely by the earlier criminal origin of their proceeds. Its distinction between conduct and circumstance supports, rather than defeats, examining what this different statute makes part of the offense. The contrary construction would require the firearm act and kidnapping to occur simultaneously in the venue district. The statute's incorporation of the continuing kidnapping and §3237(a) permit venue where that essential conduct occurred, although the firearm act occurred elsewhere. This does not dispense with proof of either requirement or make every statutory element a geographical act. It authorizes no venue based solely on remote effects, preparatory acts or evidentiary circumstances, or on a predicate completed before the firearm conduct began.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the Government's broader effects theory or establish a universal prohibition governing every effects-based venue statute. It decides no different conspiracy, aiding-and-abetting, transportation or possession charge, no number-of-violations question, no independent sentencing issue and no other conviction. Cabrales's separate charging reservations remain open.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Anderson, 328 U.S. 699, supplies the inquiry into the nature of the offense and the acts constituting it; counting statutory verbs alone is insufficient.
- United States v. Cabrales, decided June 1, 1998, is applied for its charged-conduct distinction and distinguished because these allegations incorporate the defendant's ongoing kidnapping rather than the earlier criminal origin of property. Its completed-laundering holding and reserved alternative charges remain intact.
- Smith, Bailey and Muscarello retain their distinct constructions of firearm conduct and its relation to the predicate. Those requirements remain independently necessary; none is converted into a prior holding on this venue question.

## Law After Decision

The §924(c) charge predicated on this continuing kidnapping may be tried in New Jersey because incorporated kidnapping conduct continued there. The decision adds this application of essential-conduct venue while preserving Cabrales's limit on using completed criminal provenance to relocate a separate offense. Every necessary element must still be proved; no independent effects-only venue rule is adopted and no precedent is overruled.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens and Scalia.

The §924(c) conviction and its sentence are restored subject to any genuinely unresolved, properly available claim. The case returns for proceedings consistent with that conditional restoration and lawful New Jersey venue. No such claim is resolved by this decision; no other conviction or sentence is adjudicated. Stevens and Scalia would affirm the venue vacatur, which would neither acquit the defendant nor command or prohibit a new prosecution.
