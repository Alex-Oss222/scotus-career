## Holdings

#### A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while the subject-matter issue is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first; the choice must respect applicable statutory remand commands and review bars and must decide no contested merits.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls the rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that declining authority over a defendant does not assume authority to decide liability. Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. forbids assumed Article III power for contested merits, and NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones applies that boundary to constitutional claims. Neither exhausts the order among nonmerits thresholds. Personal jurisdiction protects a defendant's interest in being subject to the court's authority; its waivability does not make it an optional prerequisite to a binding merits judgment. Here choosing that straightforward ground avoids an unnecessary difficult subject-matter determination without deciding the tort claims. The State's forum interest and the possible consequences of jurisdictional determinations remain relevant to the choice, but do not establish an absolute order. If subject matter is as readily resolved and personal jurisdiction raises difficult state law, comity ordinarily favors deciding remand first. Murphy Brothers keeps removal timing distinct from actual jurisdiction, and El Paso Natural Gas gives effect to a particular statutory priority rather than a universal hierarchy. An established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence; an unresolved difficult issue is not the same thing. The Court leaves the correctness of the actual personal-jurisdiction dismissal to the Court of Appeals and decides none of the three asserted removal grounds.

## Precedent Treatment

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. is applied within its express scope: jurisdiction must precede contested merits, but its nonmerits-sequencing reservation permits today's rule.
- NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones is applied on the same merits boundary; its prohibition on assumed standing is unchanged.
- Murphy Brothers, Inc. v. Michetti Pipe Stringing, Inc. is distinguished as removal timing and formal process, which establish no subject-matter jurisdiction or universal sequence.
- El Paso Natural Gas Co. v. Neztsosie is distinguished as a statute-specific priority over prudential exhaustion, not a general order between jurisdictional grounds.
- Insurance Corp. of Ireland v. Compagnie des Bauxites de Guinee supplies the distinction between the individual interest protected by personal jurisdiction and institutional subject-matter limits; the distinction does not establish an invariable order.

## Law After Decision

Federal courts may use the bounded personal-jurisdiction-first route in removed as well as originally filed actions. The rule adds discretion among nonmerits thresholds while preserving ordinary comity, actual statutory commands and the prohibition on deciding contested merits without jurisdiction. It establishes no universal preclusion rule or actual jurisdiction over these claims.

No removal ground or state-court remand is decided.

## Holdings

### Bona fide new citizens receive equal ordinary welfare treatment

**Holding and operative rule:** A United States citizen who establishes bona fide residence in a State becomes its citizen on equal terms; the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship and Privileges or Immunities provisions forbid lowering that otherwise eligible citizen's ordinary welfare benefits solely because of recent arrival or the former State's benefit amount. California's first-year differential violates that guarantee even if the person never received benefits before moving or moved for reasons unrelated to assistance.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls both holdings, eight votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court compares current citizens within their present State. Slaughter-House specifically recognizes a citizen's ability to settle in another State and become its citizen; the Court applies that limited proposition without reopening the decision's whole account of the Clause. Shapiro rejects durational welfare penalties, Dunn distinguishes genuine residence from added duration, and Zobel rejects allocation of ordinary resident benefits by arrival history. The State cannot answer that inequality by observing that a newcomer receives no less than in the former State. Prior benefits do not define present citizenship, and eventual equality does not cure twelve months of inferior treatment. Saving money is a legitimate general objective, but selecting new citizens for the saving merely restates the challenged disadvantage. This is equality within a program California has chosen, not a constitutional entitlement to a program or a national benefit level. Bona fide residence verification and genuinely equal prospective eligibility rules remain available. The decision does not govern distinct services or contributions earned elsewhere or supply a general catalogue of privileges. The implementing instructions' exclusion of families newly arriving from another country does not cure the discrimination among current citizens who moved from another State.

### Federal permission cannot lower the constitutional floor

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may structure and condition federal welfare assistance, but may not authorize a State to violate the Fourteenth Amendment's equal-citizenship guarantee. Section 604(c)'s permission to use prior-State benefit levels therefore cannot sustain California's unconstitutional differential; permission is not a federal command.

**Controlling explanation:** Boerne distinguishes enforcement of a constitutional guarantee from replacement of its content. The federal provision does not change the fact that California administers unequal treatment among bona fide current citizens. Statutory permission cannot remove the protection that makes the classification invalid. The Court does not invalidate every federal welfare provision, require a nationwide program design or decide a constitutionally distinct nationwide rule. Thomas accepts the general inability to authorize a proved violation but disputes the underlying guarantee's application; that disagreement does not make the permission defense sufficient once this classification is held unconstitutional.

## Precedent Treatment

- The Slaughter-House Cases supply the specific recognition of becoming another State's citizen through bona fide residence; their broader construction is not reopened.
- Shapiro v. Thompson supplies the prohibition on durational welfare penalties; a reduced payment still burdens current equality.
- Dunn v. Blumstein distinguishes actual residence from additional duration.
- Zobel v. Williams rejects ordinary resident-benefit allocation by arrival history.
- City of Boerne v. Flores prevents statutory replacement of constitutional content.
- Lunding and Romer retain their source-specific equality rules; neither is converted into a general judicial guarantee of welfare amounts.

## Law After Decision

California must treat otherwise eligible bona fide current citizens equally in ordinary welfare benefits without the first-year prior-State ceiling. Legitimate residence verification and equal eligibility conditions remain enforceable. The ruling rests on narrow equal state citizenship, creates no national welfare amount and cannot be displaced by federal permission. No precedent is overruled.

## Holdings

### A refined legal argument does not itself forfeit the original associational claim

**Holding and operative rule:** A party that initially challenged the presidential-vote condition and asserted its right to choose its organizational structure does not lose that legal organizational-autonomy objection merely by sharpening it later. That conclusion neither proves an exclusive local philosophy nor permits new summary-judgment facts to be assumed; the national-organizing affidavit and the factual basis of the as-applied theory retain their ordinary evidentiary significance.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's concurrence and Stevens's dissent, joined by Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, directly adopt this same limited proposition. Those five votes control the legal-argument distinction, without supplying a majority rationale for the judgment or a command to reopen this case.

**Controlling explanation:** Yee permits a different legal argument supporting a properly presented federal claim while preserving distinct limits on facts and the question presented. Here the original challenge identified the same presidential-vote condition and the asserted freedom to organize as the members chose. The later legal formulation therefore is not wholly new merely because it gives that associational objection greater precision. The contrary position correctly identifies a possible difference between inability to build a national party and a philosophical refusal to do so. That difference prevents a court from assuming the asserted philosophy; it does not itself erase the originally presented legal objection. Thomas v. Arn permits reasonable, nonjurisdictional presentation rules, but its failure-to-object setting does not automatically decide an argument raised in objections. The district court's silence establishes neither a new fact nor an express waiver ruling. This conclusion makes no factual finding about exclusive-local intent and resolves no constitutional merits.

### The affirmance has no controlling constitutional rationale

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** Five Justices affirm, but the four-Justice preservation ground and the Chief's contextual merits ground are not logical subsets of one another. Marks yields no common narrower rationale. The judgment binds the parties; it establishes no general five-percent safe harbor, no duty to participate nationally, and no Court holding approving every alternative ballot route or rejecting every concrete as-applied exclusion claim.

**Event and date:** Merits decision, 1999-05-24; October Term 1998.

## Holdings

### Coverage includes an association serving its members' commercial profit

**Holding and operative rule:** The FTC Act reaches a nonprofit professional association organized to carry on business for its members' profit when its activities confer proximate, substantial economic benefits on their for-profit operations. CDA satisfies that condition through its insurance, financing, commercial support and other member-business activities; nonprofit form, tax status and absence of distributions to the association do not eliminate the members'-profit branch of section 44.

**Authority:** Souter's coverage opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, directly controls this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory alternative concerning members' profit would do little work if only the association's own earnings counted. CDA materially advances its members' commercial practices, so the outside suppliers' role does not make those benefits merely incidental. This application does not require exclusive devotion to member profit, a substantial percentage of total activity devoted to it, or proof that each member makes a net profit from membership. The connection must be real and proximate; professional education's presumed or incidental benefit alone is not held sufficient. The Court does not decide coverage of every charity, educational organization, nonprofit with an annual surplus, or commercial competitor without for-profit members. Ordinary statutory interpretation suffices, without deciding deference to the Commission's jurisdictional view. Section 45's sector exceptions remain; none is established for CDA. Neither professional status nor an unverified guideline's description of state law supplies immunity. Coverage authorizes inquiry, not condemnation.

### The inquiry must test the restraint's actual competitive tendency and material explanation

**Holding and operative rule:** An abbreviated rule-of-reason inquiry may suffice when experience and the restraint's actual character make its likely anticompetitive tendency sufficiently clear to assess both the restraint and its material competing explanation. Where professional-information disparities make those effects insufficiently obvious, fuller analysis fitted to the restraint is required; neither a fixed sequence of labels nor a complete industry or market-definition study is invariably compulsory.

**Authority:** Souter's competitive-analysis opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, supplies five direct joins for this rule and both applications below.

**Controlling explanation:** NCAA permits inquiry calibrated to an established competitive mechanism. Professional Engineers rejects the claim that competition itself is undesirable; it does not dispense with examining a plausible claim that a practice improves reliable comparison. Indiana Federation of Dentists permits direct analysis of an agreement withholding a desired service, but does not make every professional-information restriction identical to that agreement. Khan and NYNEX require attention to the actual restraint and mechanism without converting this horizontal association into a vertical arrangement. The FTC's concrete enforcement findings are adverse evidence, not a substitute for the missing analysis of information quality. The Court corrects legal sufficiency and does not reweigh supported findings under section 45(c), independently affirm every economic conclusion, or decide whether substantial evidence supports ultimate liability. It finds neither restraint reasonable and creates no professional immunity. Legitimate restraints on materially false or misleading advertising remain possible under their actual conditions; no categorical per se issue is independently decided.

### Price restrictions require more examination on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** The practical restrictions on fee descriptions and broad discount advertisements cannot be sustained on the abbreviated analysis employed here without adequately examining their asserted effect on the reliability of price comparison. Further inquiry must test the actual enforcement and claimed information benefits; the mere reduction of advertising does not settle the net competitive tendency.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice competitive-analysis opinion directly adopts this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Price communication ordinarily helps consumers compare and dentists compete. Bates recognizes that value while preserving legitimate deception controls. But an across-the-board discount without an intelligible baseline or scope may be unreliable, and a disclosure condition might improve comparison, obstruct it or do both. The reported restrictions on truthful descriptions and practically prohibitive disclosures remain serious evidence against CDA. Their existence alone does not adequately test whether these particular conditions improve reliable information before demanding empirical justification on the assumption of an already established harmful net tendency. This is no finding that every advertisement misleads, that every condition helps, or that CDA's explanation ultimately succeeds. The lower proceedings must perform the missing analysis without automatically adopting the ALJ's no-market-power conclusion, a local-share threshold or mandatory comprehensive market definition.

### Quality advertising is not equivalent to service output

**Holding and operative rule:** Quality-advertising restrictions whose effects depend on the reliability and verifiability of professional information require fuller consideration than equating fewer advertisements with a restriction of dental-service output. The actual conditions and competitive explanation must be assessed; possible effects on demand do not themselves establish a producer-imposed service-output restriction.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice opinion directly controls this distinct application.

**Controlling explanation:** Restricting useful quality information can impede service rivalry, yet patients may have difficulty verifying professional claims. A restraint addressing unverifiable or misleading claims may improve information. Bates recognizes that quality-claim problem, while Indiana Federation's established desired-service restraint provides no automatic answer to this different mechanism. The court below could not settle the matter merely by translating fewer advertisements into reduced dental output. The Court requires neither measured output decline in every antitrust case nor acceptance of a blanket ban. Supported enforcement against truthful information remains material. Fuller examination may condemn either or both restrictions, but the present analysis does not resolve their competitive explanation. The discontinued fear-allaying policy is not treated as a continuing restraint.

## Precedent Treatment

- NCAA v. Board of Regents supplies flexible inquiry fitted to established competitive tendencies; it creates no compulsory full-market procedure.
- National Society of Professional Engineers v. United States remains a bar to defending restraints on the premise that competition itself is undesirable; an information-quality explanation must be tested on its actual competitive terms.
- FTC v. Indiana Federation of Dentists is distinguished at the level of the restraint and information mechanism; its direct-effects method and separation of supported findings from legal sufficiency remain.
- Bates v. State Bar of Arizona supplies the value of truthful price information and the distinct difficulty of unverifiable professional quality claims.
- State Oil Co. v. Khan retains its vertical maximum-price holding and horizontal distinctions; its attention to competitive mechanisms informs this inquiry without conferring immunity.
- NYNEX Corp. v. Discon retains its vertical supplier-substitution holding and distinct horizontal categories; labels or improper purpose do not replace analysis of the actual mechanism.

The Court establishes neither final liability nor professional immunity, and imposes no universal market-definition trial or required proof of measured service-output decline. ## Holdings

### SSDI does not create an automatic bar or special adverse presumption under the ADA

**Holding and operative rule:** Applying for or receiving SSDI neither automatically estops an ADA plaintiff from proving qualification nor creates a special strong presumption against that proof. The statutes ask different questions, and qualification under the ADA still depends on the ability to perform the position's essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls this holding, the explanation requirement below and the bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The ADA expressly includes reasonable accommodation in its qualification inquiry. SSDI's impairment, duration and vocational provisions do not conduct that same workplace-accommodation inquiry. Administrative listings and trial-work provisions, changes over time and properly alternative legal theories may also explain why the two claims coexist. Bragdon requires individual statutory analysis and founded proof; a label under one scheme cannot replace the elements of another. The Fifth Circuit recognized some of these distinctions but confined compatibility to a highly unusual class under its negative presumption. That presumption still treats the differing legal conclusions as ordinarily inconsistent and is unwarranted. The employer's concern about obtaining incompatible advantages is substantial, but ordinary evidentiary requirements address it without a label-based presumption. None of these possible explanations is a finding that Cleveland was qualified, that any accommodation was reasonable, or that discrimination occurred. The holding supplies no universal immunity from the consequences of contradictory historical facts and leaves general law governing purely factual contradictions and other statutes undisturbed.

### An apparent inconsistency still requires an adequate explanation

**Holding and operative rule:** An ADA plaintiff confronting an apparently inconsistent earlier total-disability representation must provide an explanation sufficient for a reasonable juror, accepting the earlier statement as true or as made in good faith, still to find that the plaintiff could perform the essential functions at the relevant time with or without reasonable accommodation. The plaintiff retains the ordinary qualification burden; good faith alone, a bare contradiction or a statutory label without an answer to the material factual inconsistency does not satisfy it.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this requirement and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 56 asks whether the evidence permits a reasonable finding on the actual ADA element. Prior sworn assertions remain evidence. Statutory differences may explain an assertion about disability without erasing a contradictory statement about what the person actually could do. The explanation must address the relevant time, job functions and accommodation to the extent they create the apparent conflict. Cleveland's return to work, requested accommodations, asserted later worsening and the disputed status of her first application may matter, but the Court does not resolve their truth. The continuous-disability award also remains part of the record and must be addressed rather than assumed compatible. On remand both sides may present and contest the explanation, in sworn form where appropriate. A sufficient explanation permits ordinary assessment; it guarantees neither trial nor success. Summary judgment may again follow if the explanation is inadequate or another essential element is absent. The decision changes no benefits award, compels no unreasonable accommodation and makes no credibility finding.

## Precedent Treatment

Bragdon v. Abbott is applied at the shared level of individual statutory elements and founded objective proof. Its disability-definition and direct-threat holdings remain distinct and unchanged. Ordinary summary-judgment requirements remain; the Court does not adopt every lower-court rule concerning a later affidavit that contradicts earlier factual testimony.

## Law After Decision

SSDI application or receipt carries no special estoppel presumption in the ADA qualification inquiry. Apparent contradictions remain material evidence and require an adequate explanation under ordinary proof rules. Qualification, reasonable accommodation, discrimination and defenses retain their independent elements. This decision does not settle general judicial-estoppel doctrine for purely factual contradictions or other statutory relationships.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Cleveland must have an opportunity to offer a legally and factually sufficient explanation, and the employer may contest it. The lower courts must decide qualification, accommodation and any remaining defenses under the ADA. No compulsory trial, liability finding, award, benefits revision or automatic revival of the Texas claim follows.

**Case and dockets:** Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education; No. 97-843.
**Event and date:** Merits decision, 1999-05-24; October Term 1998.
**Result:** Appealed recipient Title IX dismissal reversed and remanded, 6–3; five retain severe-or-pervasive educational-harm rule.
**Version / lineage:** Original adjudication; supersedes no completed event. Frozen handoffs identified below establish stage lineage. Repository verification and commitment remain with the operator.

## Holdings

### A school's own culpable response can support damages for controlled peer harassment

**Holding and operative rule:** A Title IX funding recipient may be liable for its own response to peer harassment because of sex when an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial danger has actual notice, the recipient substantially controls both the harasser and the relevant educational context, and deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that danger causally contributes to further harassment or vulnerability producing educational deprivation and compensable injury. A peer's attendance does not impute the peer's acts to the recipient; knowledge, culpability, control, sex-based discrimination and causal loss are independent requirements.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer; six direct joins establish this sufficient route.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin permits compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination but leaves recipient responsibility to be established. Kinman's actual-notice route identifies a recipient's own deliberate disregard of a known educational danger. The Court extends that sufficient route to peer conduct where the school has substantial remedial control; it does not claim Kinman already decided peers. The school's knowing, culpable continuation of educational deprivation supplies the institutional wrong, answering the objection that a student cannot personally bind the funding recipient. Mere discipline authority or an ineffective result does not suffice. Actual notice must reach an official empowered to correct the danger; generic constructive notice, the harasser's own knowledge and technical policy noncompliance do not establish this route. A reasonable response is lawful even if harassment continues, and courts prescribe neither a selected punishment nor a victim's preferred remedy. Constitutional and statutory limits on discipline inform control and response. Gebser's school-delegated teacher authority does not transfer to classmates; its conjunctive teacher route and the other teacher holdings remain intact. The Court neither adopts ordinary negligence or general agency law nor rejects every distinct theory of direct official discrimination. Receipt of assistance remains separately necessary under NCAA v. Smith. Causal injury cannot be inferred solely from harassment predating legally sufficient recipient notice.

### Educational deprivation follows the contextual severe-or-pervasive standard

**Holding and operative rule:** The harassment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive and impair educational access or benefit in its full context. Sexually charged conduct or offense alone does not establish discrimination because of sex, the required educational harm or the independent institutional elements.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer, directly controls with five votes. Stone-Zsela does not join this disjunctive threshold; no combination of separate rationales is needed for its authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Kinman adopted severe-or-pervasive, objectively abusive and actually experienced educational harassment without mandatory psychiatric injury, measurable grade loss or a rigid list of factors. Its reliance on Meritor and Harris preserves attention to welcome, objective and experienced abuse, and the actual setting. The peer relationship affects the contextual application and recipient-control inquiry; it does not silently replace the substantive standard with a requirement that conduct be both severe and pervasive. Age, relationship, educational setting and actual effect may matter without becoming a mandatory checklist. Neither physical exclusion nor total loss of schooling is invariably required, and no one proxy is sufficient by itself. Ordinary teasing or offense fails without the substantive and institutional requirements. No universal rule determines whether one incident always suffices or never can. These allegations involve repeated touching and propositions over months with educational interference, a materially different claimed environment. The Court decides no ultimate fact and does not treat every unwelcome interaction as actionable discrimination.

### The pleaded recipient claim warrants ordinary further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** Allegations of repeated school-setting harassment, reports including to the principal, continued conduct, deliberately inadequate protection and resulting educational interference state a claim for examination under the sufficient recipient-fault route. The categorical dismissal must be reversed, but an absent element, reasonable response, inadequate control or notice, missing causation or a supported defense may defeat recovery on the developed record.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six directly support this allegation-stage application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged response concerns the district's own use of authority after reports of a known danger, rather than automatic responsibility for the classmate. The asserted repeated abuse and educational disruption permit the necessary elements to be tested. Allegations that reports reached the principal do not finally establish every official's corrective power, knowledge or opportunity; the criminal plea supplies no earlier notice or complete causal showing. The plaintiff must prove the institutional and substantive conditions, including postnotice causal injury. ## Holdings

### Unanimous agreement on the constituent violations

**Operative rule:** In a prosecution under 21 U.S.C. section 848, each particular violation necessary to compose the continuing series is an element that the jury must find unanimously beyond a reasonable doubt. Jurors must agree on the same qualifying violations, although they need not agree on every evidentiary detail or means of committing an agreed violation; an instruction expressly allowing disagreement about the constituent violations is erroneous.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, supplies six votes for this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute makes a continuing series of independently criminal violations part of the greater offense. The additional requirements of management of five or more persons and substantial income or resources do not turn those violations into interchangeable evidence. Otherwise jurors could agree only that the defendant ran a criminal business while relying on wholly different crimes to supply the required series. Gaudin assigns actual offense elements to the jury; the statutory language here identifies the elements to which that protection attaches. Rutledge distinguishes the series requirement from the narcotics agreement, so the conspiracy verdict does not itself establish the required predicate transactions.

Schad permits genuine alternative means of committing an offense, but its traditional alternatives for a single homicide do not answer whether distinct criminal episodes constitute this statutory series. The breadth of the violations that could otherwise be combined reinforces the ordinary unanimity reading and avoids serious constitutional doubt without deciding a universal constitutional classification rule. The Government's focus on the continuing enterprise explains the offense's target; it does not erase the enacted violations requirement.

The charge expressly permitted the disagreement the statute forbids. The Court does not require unanimity about every item of proof, every overt act, the identity of each supervised person or every managerial relationship. Those distinct questions are not decided. Roy's treatment of a personal-intent omission and Sullivan's treatment of a defective reasonable-doubt standard retain their own scopes. ### 1. Independent constitutional claims remain available

**Operative rule:** Title IX does not displace a section 1983 claim enforcing an independently secured Equal Protection right merely because the same school harassment also supports a Title IX theory. The claimant must establish the constitutional violation, state action, defendant-specific responsibility or proper municipal attribution, causation and an available remedy; correcting displacement supplies none of those elements.

**Controlling explanation:** The Fourteenth Amendment imposes duties independent of a funding recipient's statutory obligations. Blessing requires attention to the particular right and actual congressional foreclosure, not an assumption that overlapping facts create identical rights. The funding-termination process and the implied Title IX action do not provide an exclusive personal remedial system incompatible with the independent constitutional claim. The differing duties and defendants make categorical extinction especially unwarranted.

Sea Clammers and Smith v. Robinson concerned actual incompatibility with the statutory enforcement designs before the Court; neither holds that every private federal action extinguishes constitutional remedies arising from the same events. Kinman kept Title IX and constitutional supervisory and municipal liability distinct. Gebser and Davis refine responsibility under Title IX; they do not turn every peer's misconduct into unconstitutional official action or silently repeal Equal Protection enforcement.

The District and Board claims were foreclosed categorically and must receive ordinary consideration. The individual defendants prevailed on an independent immunity ground, and the general Title IX verdict establishes no new, specified factual finding in this Court. Neither judgment is overturned by correcting displacement. Purposeful official discrimination, municipal policy or final-authority attribution, causation and any actual preclusive effect remain separate questions; institutional rank, negligence and receipt of funds do not alone prove them.

### 2. The identified statutory right is not categorically foreclosed under section 1983

**Operative rule:** Title IX's individual right against sex discrimination in a covered educational program is not categorically excluded from section 1983 enforcement by the administrative funding process or the established implied private action. A statutory section 1983 claim must prove the actual Title IX right and violation, including the statute's defendant coverage and recipient-responsibility conditions; the enforcement vehicle does not expand the underlying duty.

**Authority:** Thomas's statutory portion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Controlling explanation:** Blessing separates identification of an individual statutory right from the further inquiry whether Congress excluded section 1983 expressly or through an incompatible remedial scheme. This claim identifies a student's right against sex discrimination, rather than an aggregate demand for better program administration. Title IX contains no express exclusion. Its funding oversight does not give this student an exclusive adjudicatory forum, and the implied action recognized in Cannon and Franklin supplies no elaborate mandatory claim-processing route that section 1983 would evade.

The principal objection is that another enforcement label might undo the limits of the recipient's funding obligation. That objection is answered by enforcing the actual statutory right: section 1983 does not make a nonrecipient liable under Title IX, erase statutory attribution conditions or create a new harassment standard. The scheme therefore does not require categorical foreclosure of the identified right. The remand permits consideration of properly pleaded statutory claims against the District and Board subject to those limits, ordinary defenses and the existing verdict's demonstrated legal effect. No merits finding or new trial follows automatically.

- **Cannon v. University of Chicago:** Preserved as the source of Title IX's established implied private action; that action's existence does not itself demonstrate exclusivity.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools:** Preserved as recognizing compensatory relief for established intentional Title IX discrimination; this decision creates no new liability or damages finding.
- **Middlesex County Sewerage Authority v. National Sea Clammers Association:** Distinguished by the incompatible enforcement structure there; the actual foreclosure principle remains available when established.
- **Smith v. Robinson:** Distinguished by the remedial incompatibility there; factual overlap alone does not reproduce that holding.
- **Kinman v. Omaha Public School District:** Applied to the separation of statutory recipient liability, personal constitutional responsibility and municipal attribution, without treating its adjudication as an earlier decision of every preclusion question.
- **Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District:** Its controlling teacher-attribution route and unappealed-claim limits remain unchanged; no current rule is used to reopen the separate immunity judgment here.
- **Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education:** Its peer-harassment conditions govern actual statutory recipient liability, but do not extinguish an independent official-discrimination claim or establish liability on this record.

## Law After Decision

From June 3, 1999, Title IX supplies no categorical bar to the independently sourced Equal Protection claims or the identified statutory-right claims enforced through section 1983 here. The constitutional holding has nine votes; the statutory enforcement holding has eight. Different sources of rights retain their different substantive conditions and defendant coverage.

The statutory enforcement vehicle does not enlarge the Title IX duty. The independent constitutional claim requires its own purposeful discrimination and responsibility; it is not a means of treating all peer harassment or negligent supervision as unconstitutional conduct. Existing Title IX attribution law, conduct-date immunity rules, municipal-liability requirements and valid preclusion defenses remain in force.
