**Case and dockets:** Marcus v. Iowa Public Television, No. 98-710.
**Event and date:** Merits decision, 1999-01-13; October Term 1998, chunk 3.
**Result:** Affirm the rejection of the common selection-policy claim and derivative relief, 6–3; eight Associates adopt the threshold rationale.
**Version / lineage:** Original decision.

## Holdings
#### 1. The Natural Law Party committee's policy challenge is capable of repetition yet evading review

**Holding and operative rule:** A challenge to completed pre-election broadcasts remains justiciable for the same continuing party committee when its established relationship to several excluded candidates, general electoral function, the broadcaster's general selection practice and its recurring program support a reasonable expectation of another short candidate-access controversy involving that committee. Both the period too short for complete review and the same claimant's reasonable prospect of recurrence are required; neither election timing nor an abstract organizational purpose alone suffices.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees that the Court reaches the merits but does not join this threshold explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Natural Law Party committee's common selection-policy challenge survives the 1996 broadcasts. The stipulated background distinguishes that general party organization from the separate committee formed for Marcus's 1996 campaign. Its participation with excluded candidates across congressional districts connects its electoral function to this broadcaster's actual selection practice. That concrete pattern, the general policy and the established recurring Iowa Press program support a reasonable expectation that this same committee will again confront the disputed practice. The opportunity for pre-election appearances is too short for complete ordinary appellate review. Spencer v. Kemna requires both facts and forbids a merely possible injury to others from supplying recurrence. Norman v. Reed supplies the comparison to continuing party interests, although its distinct party-qualification consequences are absent here. Chandler v. Miller established recurrence through an individual's stated intention to run again; it does not make that particular proof indispensable for a continuing organizational claimant. Honig v. Doe permits a grounded assessment of likelihood rather than requiring certainty of repetition. The unknown next candidate and unscheduled next debate make recurrence less certain, but do not erase the established relationship between this committee and the general practice. The conclusion is an inference from those facts, not a finding of a new candidacy, later exclusion, unchanged policy in 1999 or later campaign activity. The absence of contrary evidence does not shift the jurisdictional burden.

**Limits:** The surviving common claim does not separately preserve every individual's expired demand, establish future standing for every named plaintiff or supply an independent current injury from program-guide language. There is no general election exception, relaxed jurisdiction because review was granted, presumed reputational consequence or order to recreate a past broadcast.

#### 2. The actually selective debate is a nonpublic forum

**Holding and operative rule:** A state broadcaster's candidate debate is a nonpublic forum when the broadcaster actually retains editorial selection rather than opening the program generally to all qualified candidates. Restrictions must reasonably serve the program's journalistic purpose and remain viewpoint neutral; public ownership, a journalism label and ballot qualification alone decide neither access nor validity.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion is joined by all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Agreement on classification and the governing requirements does not imply agreement that Iowa's standards satisfy them.

**Controlling explanation:** Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes supplies the governing rule because Iowa Press retained selection by invitation. The District Court independently adopted the finding that the broadcaster intended admission only for invited candidates, and the actual format paired selected candidates with a host and political reporters. Calling the programs debates, as the District Court did, does not transform them into an open microphone; calling them bona fide news interviews does not immunize them. Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educational Fund and Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators' Association direct attention to actual access and permitted purpose. Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia preserves the speakers' private viewpoints within the actual opportunity. The question is therefore whether this selective platform was administered reasonably and without viewpoint discrimination. It is not whether every ballot-qualified candidate must be admitted or whether the broadcaster's public status makes all candidate speech its own message.

**Limits:** A program genuinely opened to qualifying candidates presents a different access question. Reasonable selection is permitted; sham criteria, incumbent favoritism, inconsistent administration and unpopular-viewpoint exclusions remain subject to review.

#### 3. Iowa's adjudicated selection satisfies Forbes; the forum claim and its derivative relief fail

**Holding and operative rule:** On this record, the selective format, legitimate informing purpose, editorial-integrity policies, evidence of electoral engagement and public interest, and independently credited findings support reasonable and viewpoint-neutral selection. Forbes imposes no additional universal numerical formula, written-policy, advance-publication or prescribed investigative prerequisite; the affirmed absence of a forum violation defeats declaratory, injunctive and guide-correction relief sought as remedies for that violation.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer: six Justices. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg dissent from the sufficiency of Iowa's standards and would vacate the rejection of the common policy claim and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Forbes permits the editorial judgment established here. Instruction 15 described newsworthiness through sufficient audience and public interest. It operated alongside policies of fairness, objectivity, balance, independence from pressure and an informed citizenry, and testimony about campaign activity, organization, prior performance, fundraising breadth, media recognition and public engagement. Those considerations bear on journalistic purpose; they are not a binding checklist or findings that every candidate lacked each characteristic. The judge independently assessed credibility and adopted findings of genuine selective editorial judgment, rather than exclusion because of disagreement with political views. That finding matters, but does not alone settle constitutional reasonableness. Petitioners also asserted insufficient individual inquiry, missing candidate information, substantial publicity, invitations from opponents and fundraising comparisons. Those contentions do not establish clear error in the credited findings or compel a different constitutional judgment. The reported figures prove neither an adopted dollars rule nor materially equal electoral significance. The Court does not declare every allegation false or reduce the challenge to ballot qualification. Regan v. Time, Inc., which concerns governmental restrictions on another publisher's content, does not invalidate all content-sensitive selection in a nonpublic candidate forum. Nor does Forbes make professional sincerity conclusive. The actual record supplies the reasonable connection here without judicial substitution of a different editorial ranking.

**Limits and remedy:** The Court does not approve categorical exclusion of idea-centered campaigns, manipulation favoring incumbent parties, or rejection of a genuinely viable minor-party candidate simply because of party status. Offering another program does not cure viewpoint discrimination. The program-guide correction request fails only as derivative relief for the rejected forum claim; no independent cause of action, current guide dissemination, reputational injury or correction entitlement is decided. The judgment orders no new broadcast, future inclusion, damages or statutory access remedy.

## Precedent Treatment
- **Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes (May 18, 1998):** Applied to Iowa's actual selective format, governing reasonable and viewpoint-neutral choice, and supported adjudicated application. Its rejection of universal numerical, written or advance-publication requirements remains operative; its pretext, actual-comparator and open-access limits remain intact.
- **Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educational Fund, 473 U.S. 788, and Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators' Association, 460 U.S. 37:** Applied to actual selective access and restrictions reasonably related to the forum's purpose.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia (June 29, 1995):** Its protection of private viewpoints within an actual expressive opportunity remains applicable; it does not compel universal access to a genuinely selective debate.
- **Regan v. Time, Inc., 468 U.S. 641:** Distinguished because its private-publication restriction differs from the selective candidate-forum question governed by Forbes. It is not overruled or treated as banning every judgment of newsworthiness.
- **Spencer v. Kemna (March 3, 1998):** Its concrete-continuing-controversy and two-part recurrence requirements are applied. The committee's established organizational connection supplies the same-claimant basis absent from speculative injury to an unidentified future person.
- **Norman v. Reed (January 14, 1992):** Its continuing-party-interest analysis informs the recurrence inquiry. Its actual party-qualification consequences are not imported into this record.
- **Chandler v. Miller (April 15, 1997):** Its reliance on the candidate's stated future candidacy is distinguished from this continuing committee's established electoral activity; that individual proof is not made an exclusive method of demonstrating recurrence.
- **Honig v. Doe, 484 U.S. 305:** Applied for a supported likelihood assessment rather than certainty of repetition. No general post-grant relaxation of Article III is adopted.

## Law After Decision
Forbes continues to govern genuinely selective public-broadcast debates. Iowa's supported contextual editorial judgment satisfies that rule without a new universal formula or procedural prerequisite, while viewpoint, pretext, actual-comparator and forum-boundary review remain required. The committee-specific Article III holding permits review of this common policy challenge; it does not keep all former candidates' individual demands alive. The affirmed denial of the common forum claim defeats its derivative equitable relief, including the guide-correction request only in that capacity.

## Judgment & Remedy
**Affirmed, 6–3.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. Opposing: Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. The affirmance denies declaratory, injunctive and program-guide correction relief insofar as it depends on the rejected forum violation. It decides no independent guide claim, establishes no continuing guide injury, and orders no replacement broadcast, future admission or damages. The dissent would vacate rejection of the common policy claim and remand under adequately constrained standards, leaving the guide request's permissible remedial and jurisdictional basis for examination.

**Case and dockets:** Roberts v. Galen of Virginia, Inc., No. 97-53.
**Event and date:** Merits decision, 1999-01-13; October Term 1998, chunk 3.
**Result:** Reverse the motive-based judgment on the appealed EMTALA stabilization claim and remand, 9–0.
**Version / lineage:** Original decision.

## Holdings
#### 1. Stabilization has no additional improper-motive element

**Holding and operative rule:** A claim under 42 U.S.C. §1395dd(b) does not require proof that a participating hospital acted with an improper financial or other invidious motive. Liability nevertheless requires proof of the enacted stabilization or transfer duty and the applicable civil-action predicates; removing the extra motive requirement establishes neither breach nor injury.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt both holdings and their stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the stabilization provision means what its conditions say. When an individual comes to a hospital and the hospital determines that the individual has an emergency medical condition, subsection (b)(1) requires further examination and treatment within the available staff and facilities to stabilize the condition, or transfer in accordance with subsection (c). The text does not make that obligation depend on why the hospital acts. Subsection (a)'s separate requirement of an appropriate screening examination cannot insert an additional condition into subsection (b). National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler supplies the analogous principle that a court may not add a motive requirement while Congress's actual liability conditions remain intact. Concern that EMTALA should not become general federal malpractice law does not permit rewriting those conditions. Its determination, stabilization, transfer, injury and remedial requirements continue to distinguish a statutory violation from negligent care. Cleland's screening analysis therefore cannot sustain the additional motive element imposed on this stabilization claim. The Court does not decide whether subsection (a) contains a motive requirement, how long this hospital's duty continued, or whether the patient proves its violation.

**Operative qualifications retained:** Subsection (b)(2) deems the treatment requirement satisfied when the hospital offers the required examination and treatment, informs the patient or representative of their risks and benefits, and that person refuses; the hospital must take all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. Subsection (b)(3) separately addresses refusal of a transfer offered in accordance with subsection (c), after risk-and-benefit information, with the same reasonable-efforts duty concerning written informed refusal. Neither provision is a finding about this patient.

For an unstabilized emergency condition, subsection (c)(1) separately requires either a written transfer request made by the individual or legally responsible representative after receiving information about the hospital's EMTALA duties and the risks of transfer, or a physician's signed certification, based on the information available when transfer occurs, that the expected medical benefits of appropriate treatment elsewhere outweigh the increased transfer risks, including risks to the unborn child in labor; it also requires an appropriate transfer. If no physician is physically present in the emergency department, a medical person qualified under the Secretary's regulations may sign after consultation with a physician who makes the required determination and later countersigns. The certification must summarize its supporting risks and benefits. Appropriate transfer requires risk-minimizing treatment within the transferring hospital's capacity; available receiving space, qualified personnel and acceptance with appropriate treatment; transmission of all emergency-condition records available at transfer, including the condition, observed signs or symptoms, preliminary diagnosis, treatment, test results and written request or certification, together with the name and address of an on-call physician who refused or did not appear within a reasonable time to provide necessary stabilization; qualified personnel and transportation equipment, including necessary and medically appropriate life support during transfer; and any further applicable regulatory conditions. None is declared satisfied or violated here.

Subsection (d)(2)(A) permits the person directly harmed by a participating hospital's violation to sue that hospital for personal-injury damages available under the law of its State and appropriate equitable relief. The separate limitation is two years after the violation. This decision creates no personal action against an individual physician and no automatic liability for later deterioration.

#### 2. The motive-based summary judgment must be reversed; other predicates remain open

**Holding and operative rule:** Summary judgment rejecting the stabilization claim because the guardian failed to establish improper motive rests on an element the statute does not contain and must be reversed. The hospital's actual-knowledge and completed-screening defenses remain open for lawful consideration on remand; neither is accepted or rejected on its merits by this decision.

**Authority:** The same unanimous per curiam opinion controls this confined reversal and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court of Appeals recognized a genuine dispute about stabilization but nevertheless sustained judgment because the evidence did not sufficiently connect financial pressure to the physician's discharge decision. Eliminating that erroneous element removes its ground for judgment. Evidence concerning the patient's infection, the safety of transfer before the antibiotic response and culture results could be assessed, and the possible role of later care does not establish the remaining statutory elements in either party's favor. The hospital argues that it lacked actual knowledge of an emergency condition and that proper screening ended its duty. Those arguments must be assessed on their own legal and factual predicates. The Court neither converts the stabilization dispute into an undisputed absence of knowledge nor holds that screening necessarily ends every stabilization obligation. The preserved ability of a respondent to defend a favorable judgment on a supported alternative does not supply missing findings. The separate state-law ostensible-agency ruling and other claims are not reopened. No liability judgment, damages award or mandatory trial is ordered.

**Questions not reached:** Whether an emergency medical condition existed, what the hospital determined or knew, duration of any duty, stabilization, authorized transfer, causal injury, damages and ultimate liability remain undecided. The treatment-refusal, transfer-refusal and transfer-certification provisions remain distinct; no exception is presumed.

## Precedent Treatment
- **National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler (January 24, 1994):** Applied by analogy for refusal to add an omitted motive element while preserving every enacted liability condition. Its RICO construction remains distinct from EMTALA.
- **Cleland v. Bronson Health Care Group, 917 F.2d 266 (6th Cir. 1990):** The extension of its screening-motivation analysis to impose an extra element under subsection (b) is rejected. The Court does not decide subsection (a)'s screening-motive question.
- **United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc. (November 29, 1994):** Its favorable-judgment defense rule is respected without treating an unestablished alternative factual premise as proved. Neither alternative defense is conclusively adjudicated here.

## Law After Decision
EMTALA stabilization litigation proceeds without an additional improper-purpose element. The statutory determination, treatment or lawful-transfer conditions, direct personal harm, causation, permitted remedies and limitations period retain their independent force. Screening, stabilization and the separate state-law agency claim remain distinct. The judgment changes no general medical-negligence rule and awards no relief beyond reversal and remand of the appealed stabilization disposition.

## Judgment & Remedy
**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The reversal removes the improper-motive ground for summary judgment. The actual-knowledge and completed-screening defenses remain open, as do the statute's other predicates and available remedies. The separate state ostensible-agency ruling and other claims are not reopened; no liability judgment, damages award or mandatory trial is ordered.

Per curiam opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Case and dockets:** State Street Bank & Trust Co. v. Signature Financial Group, Inc.; Federal Circuit No. 96-1327; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; OT1998.
**Event and date:** Merits decision; 1999-01-14.
**Result:** Reverse the Federal Circuit's eligibility-based reversal, 8–1; sustain §101 invalidity of issued claims 1–6 on the mathematical-calculation ground and remand for consistent proceedings on remaining matters. Stone-Zsela would vacate and remand the eligibility question under the corrected standard.
**Version / lineage:** Original decision.

## Holdings
#### Part I-A — Business use supplies no separate exclusion

**Operative rule.** An otherwise eligible invention is not excluded from §101 merely because its use is commercial or concerns financial administration. Rejecting that categorical exclusion does not establish eligibility, which remains subject to the mathematical-principle limitation and every other statutory requirement.

**Authority.** Part I-A of Stevens's Opinion of the Court has all nine Justices' direct joins.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 101 identifies statutory categories without excluding an industry. Chakrabarty applies those broad categories while preserving the exclusion of abstract principles. A business label therefore supplies no independent ground for invalidity, just as commercial usefulness supplies no independent ground for eligibility. The District Court's categorical business-method reasoning cannot sustain its judgment. The Court must examine the subject actually claimed.

#### Part I-B — Useful financial output is insufficient; the actual whole claim controls

**Operative rule.** A mathematical calculation does not become eligible solely because it yields a useful financial result, is confined to a named business arrangement, or is expressed as a programmed machine; the whole claimed combination must supply a patentable application rather than the calculation with its ordinary implementation. Conversely, mathematics or computer use does not itself make a claim ineligible, and means-plus-function limitations must be construed to include the corresponding disclosed structure and equivalents.

**Authority.** Part I-B has all nine Justices' direct joins at this level of generality; agreement on the standard does not establish agreement on its final application.

**Controlling explanation.** Benson involved useful computation, and Flook involved a useful numerical update limited to a particular setting. Those decisions prevent utility from deciding eligibility. Diehr requires considering the entire claimed application and forbids removing limitations merely because they were familiar; its curing process also shows the distinction between using mathematics in an application and claiming the calculation itself.

Section 112, paragraph 6, limits functional means to corresponding disclosed structure and equivalents. The issued combinations therefore cannot be treated simply as the cancelled methods or as every possible means of obtaining a number. Markman and Vitronics require actual claim meaning to be determined from the public instrument; Warner-Jenkinson preserves each material boundary. None makes a machine label dispositive. The Court adopts no universal physical-transformation requirement, independent noncomputer-contribution requirement, or rule that every program is abstract. The distinct requirements of novelty, nonobviousness, disclosure and infringement remain intact.

#### Part II — Each issued combination remains the financial calculation and its bookkeeping implementation

**Operative rule.** With each disclosed means and incorporated limitation retained, issued claims 1–6 cover the calculation, verification and recording of financial allocations rather than a qualifying application of their result, and are ineligible under §101. This conclusion sustains their invalidity without deciding the separate claims and counterclaims on their merits.

**Authority.** Part II has the eight Associates' direct joins identified in Opinion Topology; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Controlling explanation.** The programmed computer and storage are real limitations and are considered together with every routine. The following table identifies each complete combination and why its added work remains accounting calculation:

| Claim | Whole combination considered and application |
|---|---|
| 1 | Processor/storage, initialization, fund-change and percentage processing, daily income/expenses/realized allocations, unrealized allocations and year-end data calculate and record the financial values. |
| 2 | Adds pricing-date/fund-asset storage, medium identification and investment, ratio, incremental, daily and breakage files: these organize inputs, intermediate values and results. |
| 3 | Adds correct-medium verification, date retrieval/correction, fund-change input/storage and prior-investment retrieval with adjusted-investment/ratio calculation/storage: these protect the calculation's intended input record. |
| 4 | Begins "A method as claimed in claim 3" and retains that dependency; adds medium/date checks/correction, incremental income/expense/realized retrieval and storage, investment/ratio retrieval and prepricing-asset computation: these refine the accounting values. |
| 5 | Incorporates claim 4; adds medium/date checks, unrealized input, investment/ratio/incremental/breakage retrieval, end-of-day calculation/storage and breakage/daily updates: these handle fractional discrepancies and maintain accurate allocations. |
| 6 | Incorporates claim 5; adds medium verification, daily-activity retrieval and year-end income/expense/capital-gain-or-loss aggregation and storage: these produce and retain the annual accounting result. |

The useful values affect ownership records, tax reporting and valuation, but the claims require no further operation applying their result comparable to Diehr's curing process. Flook's useful restricted calculation remains the relevant distinction. This conclusion assumes novelty and nonobviousness; no limitation is subtracted as prior art, and no disputed external technical fact is resolved against Signature. The accepted specification and scope reveal no material unperformed task requiring an eligibility remand. Claim 4's wording supplies no independent drafting-defect holding. A different computer operation or commercial application requires its own analysis.

## Precedent Treatment
- **Gottschalk v. Benson, 409 U.S. 63:** Applied to reject useful computation as sufficient eligibility; no general software prohibition follows.
- **Parker v. Flook, 437 U.S. 584:** Applied to the distinction between a restricted useful numerical calculation and its qualifying application; no element-by-element novelty subtraction is adopted.
- **Diamond v. Diehr, 450 U.S. 175:** Applied to the entire actual combination and distinguished in application: the claimed curing process used the calculation in another operation, while these combinations obtain and maintain the accounting calculation itself.
- **Diamond v. Chakrabarty, 447 U.S. 303:** Applied for broad statutory categories, with abstract principles still excluded; business use is no additional exclusion.
- **Markman v. Westview Instruments:** Applied to judicial claim meaning with external factual work retained; its allocation does not establish universal de novo review of subsidiary findings.
- **Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc.:** Applied to the public instrument's meaning and permissible technical assistance; absent prosecution history supplies no disclaimer or mandatory hearing.
- **Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co.:** Applied to preserve every material limitation; its infringement and prosecution-surrender rules are not replaced by this eligibility ruling.
- **Pfaff v. Wells Electronics, Inc.:** Preserved as governing the separate commercial-offer and readiness conditions of the on-sale bar; it supplies no mathematical-claim eligibility test.

## Law After Decision
Section 101 neither excludes inventions because they serve business nor establishes eligibility because they produce useful financial values. Courts must construe and assess the actual complete combination under the inherited calculation/application distinction. These six issued combinations are ineligible; that result establishes no categorical answer for all software, data structures or commercial inventions. Other patent requirements and the retained judicial/factual allocation continue to operate separately.

**Supporting:** Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela.

The Court does not affirm an unspecified disposition of the separate unenforceability, noninfringement or Massachusetts counterclaims; the lower courts must address their remaining procedural consequences consistently with this decision. No new §101 evidentiary inquiry, finding under §§102, 103 or an independent §112 ground, infringement determination or damages award is directed. Stone-Zsela would vacate the appellate eligibility determination and remand for the corrected claim-specific inquiry.

**Case and dockets:** California Public Employees' Retirement System and Florida State Board of Administration v. Paul Felzen et al., No. 97-1732.
**Event and date:** Merits decision, 1999-01-20; October Term 1998, chunk 3.
**Result:** Affirm dismissal of the attempted nonparty appeal, 5–4, without deciding settlement fairness or fees.
**Version / lineage:** Original decision.

## Holdings
#### 1. Rule 23.1 objection does not itself confer the party status needed for this appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** A shareholder who is otherwise not a party does not acquire appellate party status merely by appearing under Rule 23.1 notice and presenting objections that the district court hears and rejects when approving a derivative settlement. To appeal that settlement judgment, the shareholder must properly become a party through intervention; shareholder interest, notice and the recognized opportunity to object do not themselves supply the necessary status.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court is joined by the other four members of the coalition comprising Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. All five adopt both holdings and their stated qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an opportunity to assist the settlement court by objecting is distinct from authority to continue the corporate litigation on appeal. Marino v. Ortiz supplies both the general party-status rule and its application to persons actually heard in opposition to a settlement who had not intervened. Rule 23.1 recognizes shareholder participation in the district court's supervision of the corporate claim; that function does not itself confer the separate appellate role. Tryforos v. Icarian Development Co. supplies the strongest contrary rule. Its distinction between objecting to disposition and assuming prosecution expressly included an appeal by a shareholder who appeared to oppose a proposed settlement. The Court rejects that settlement-appeal proposition insofar as it permits this appeal without intervention: limiting an objector's appellate objectives does not remove the status prerequisite Marino applies. Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp. reinforces the distinction between interest and status, although its undisturbed intervention denial and separately unpresented intervention question differ from this case's direct status challenge. Johnson v. Manhattan Railway Co. does not establish an independent route here. Its discussion of invitation, appearance and appeal at page 495 is followed at page 496 by an explanation why the applicable equity rule would not obstruct intervention, in a distinct receivership setting. Neither passage decides modern derivative-objector status by itself. These institutions were heard but sought no intervention. Their reliance on earlier circuit practice does not constitute admission as parties, and no denial of an application is before the Court.

**Limits:** This is a rule for the presented derivative-settlement appeal, not a categorical prohibition of every nonparty appeal under every procedural regime. Section 1291 supplies the final-decision framework, Appellate Rule 3(c) concerns identification of appellants, Rule 23.1 governs the recognized objection role, and Rule 24 supplies intervention; neither §1291 nor Rule 3(c) is treated as expressly deciding every party-status category. The decision rests on actual participation and the absence of intervention, not on a finding that the institutions violated a notice or service condition.

#### 2. Intervention review and nonparty due-process protection remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Requiring intervention for this settlement appeal does not foreclose review by an actual party or an appropriate appeal from an actual denial of intervention. Nor does it impose a general duty to intervene in another person's suit to preserve an otherwise independent claim: constitutional limits on binding genuinely unrepresented persons remain, without establishing an appellate entitlement or a collateral remedy in this case.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice opinion of the Court controls this boundary and the confined affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Izumi preserves a lawful route for reviewing an intervention denial while distinguishing it from review of the underlying judgment by a party. No such denial exists here, so the Court decides neither entitlement to intervention nor the timing or result of an application never made. Richards v. Jefferson County protects a person's own rights against preclusion without constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate representative litigation or another recognized basis consistent with due process. Its rule does not make every affected person an appellate party. Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein preserves the independent requirement of adequate representation; notice, opportunities to object or opt out do not cure its constitutional absence. Those protections remain effective, but neither adequate nor inadequate representation is established by the derivative label or by these institutions' appearance. The claim settled below belongs to the corporation, while Richards involved direct personal claims. Keeping that distinction does not decide whether any particular independent shareholder claim exists or may proceed. The status ruling accordingly leaves the settlement's fairness, compensation terms and any properly presented intervention or due-process question undecided.

**Questions not reached and remedial limits:** The Court orders no reopening, tolling, late intervention, new hearing, discovery or review of a fictional denied motion. It does not independently decide the petition's requested pure-prospectivity question or adopt the Court of Appeals' broader assertion that every possible operation of Chevron Oil was eliminated. Harper's rule against selective prospectivity after application of a federal rule to the Court's litigants remains at its established scope; it creates no unsupported transition entitlement here. No settlement-fairness, fee-merits, collusion, representation-adequacy, personal-claim or collateral-preclusion judgment follows.

## Precedent Treatment
- **Marino v. Ortiz, 484 U.S. 301:** Applied to the distinction between being heard as a settlement objector and properly obtaining party status for appeal. Its separate equally divided collateral-action disposition supplies no general preclusion rule here.
- **Tryforos v. Icarian Development Co., 518 F.2d 1258, Part I and footnote 22:** Its express route for an appearing Rule 23.1 shareholder to appeal a proposed settlement without intervention is rejected to the extent inconsistent with today's rule. Its separate service, notice-of-appeal and fee rulings are not adjudicated or displaced merely by that conclusion.
- **Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp. (November 30, 1993):** Its party-status boundary and route to review an actual intervention denial are applied; its different §1254(1), denied-intervention and question-presentation posture is preserved. ## Holdings
#### The requested lien supplies substitute compensation without an applicable waiver

**Operative rule.** Section 702 does not waive federal immunity for an equitable lien whose function is substitute payment from Government property for a private debtor's default when no identified retained property belonging to the claimant or specific statutory payment entitlement supports the requested recovery. The equitable form and identification of a project account do not establish consent; specific enforcement of an actual entitlement remains a different question under its own governing law.

**Authority.** Scalia's Opinion of the Court has all nine Justices' direct joins and controls the rule, application and limits.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 702's waiver excludes money damages. Bowen distinguishes substitute compensation from specific relief enforcing an actual entitlement, including a statutory entitlement to payment; it does not classify all monetary relief as damages. Nordic Village and Lane separately require consent to the remedy actually sought. A substantive duty, general jurisdiction or equitable description cannot supply missing consent.

Blue Fox expressly seeks contract funds and argues that its lien attached at notice, making later disbursements ineffective against it. But that attachment is the legal proposition to be established, not an accepted finding that the Army retained Blue Fox's property. No earned Verdan payment remains withheld, and the distinct completion balance is not shown to belong to Blue Fox. The requested recovery would replace Verdan's unpaid debt from Government property. Calling the Army a stakeholder cannot establish both the underlying entitlement and consent to that substitute obligation.

Henningsen and Pearlman concern surety or subrogation rights; Munsey Trust likewise supplies no general direct subcontractor right to recover against the Army free of immunity. The Miller Act's bond protection does not make the Government a guarantor when it fails to obtain the required bond. No bond exists here, and the Government's administration does not itself enact a payment mandate or waiver. That failure is not approved; it simply does not supply consent.

Immunity resolves this claim before independent equitable merits. The Court does not declare every equitable lien money damages or decide a genuine proceeding for claimant property under applicable authority. It also leaves the disputed agency-action, reviewability and adequate-remedy arguments under §§702 and 704, and the related preservation objection, unanswered. The money-damages limitation is sufficient.

## Precedent Treatment
- **Bowen v. Massachusetts, 487 U.S. 879:** Preserved and distinguished. Its specific statutory payment entitlement differs from compensation for this private debt; money alone is not the classification rule.
- **United States v. Nordic Village, Inc.:** Applied to require consent for the particular affirmative recovery and to reject equitable or property labels as substitutes. Genuine identified-property proceedings retain their own facts and authority.
- **Lane v. Peña:** Applied to separate a substantive governmental duty from waiver of immunity for the actual monetary remedy.
- **Henningsen v. United States Fidelity & Guaranty Co., 208 U.S. 404:** Distinguished as a surety/subrogation setting; it supplies no direct Army waiver for this subcontractor.
- **Pearlman v. Reliance Insurance Co., 371 U.S. 132:** Distinguished for its surety/property relationship; it does not establish Blue Fox's entitlement or consent here.
- **United States v. Munsey Trust Co., 332 U.S. 234:** Preserved in its governmental/surety setting; it creates no general right for an unpaid subcontractor to attach Army funds free of immunity.

## Law After Decision
Courts must identify the claimant's actual entitlement and the operation of the requested remedy before treating a monetary demand as specific relief under §702. A private-debt substitute does not become authorized merely through an equitable lien, a named project account, notice or the Government's failure to obtain a required bond. A genuine identified-property claim, enacted payment mandate, properly founded Tucker Act claim, recovery against an actual Miller Act surety or another unequivocal waiver remains governed by its own requirements. None is awarded here.

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Army lien claim must be dismissed. The Court orders no payment, recreated project balance, replacement lien or tracing hearing. The SBA judgment is untouched. A properly founded alternative remedy remains subject to its own governing law; no existing surety recovery or direct Tucker Act entitlement is established here.

## Holdings
#### Compatible federal fraud remedies do not impair the actual state insurance scheme

**Operative rule.** A generally applicable federal law impairs protected state insurance law when its operation contradicts the state law or frustrates an actual state policy or the administration of the state scheme; literal contradiction is not the only possible impairment. An additional private action or greater federal remedy does not itself establish impairment, and the Co-Payors' RICO claim may proceed because it targets fraud Nevada forbids while leaving the State's scheme intact and displacing no demonstrated exclusive remedial arrangement.

**Authority.** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court has all nine Justices' direct joins and controls the rule, application and reservations.

**Controlling explanation.** The parties do not dispute here that Nevada enacted the relevant laws to regulate the business of insurance or that RICO does not specifically relate to that business. Those predicates frame the impairment question. Fabe protects policy performance and administration necessary to implement it, so the inquiry must examine the actual state policy that federal law would frustrate. Barnett Bank separately addresses an insurance-specific federal exception; RICO has no such subject matter and receives no exemption by that analogy.

The insurers correctly observe that enforcement machinery and remedial choices can form part of protected state policy. A common prohibition of fraud therefore does not by itself establish compatibility. Here, however, Nevada prohibits the alleged deception, and its regulatory authority and remedies are not established as an exclusive arrangement displaced by this Co-Payor action. RICO neither invalidates the state command nor replaces its regulator or defeats an identified policy forbidding this action or remedy. Larger federal damages alone do not establish the missing interference.

The allegations span 1985–1988. The 1987 addition of an express private remedy in Nevada Revised Statutes §686A.310 is not applied retrospectively, and the Court does not decide whether an earlier statutory action was implied. Even assuming no statutory private remedy for earlier conduct, that absence does not itself establish a policy making administrative sanctions exclusive or excluding independent federal relief. The result rests on the actual nonexclusive arrangement, not invented earlier private rights or a comprehensive state-remedy inventory.

A federal claim requiring conduct the State forbids, nullifying a state-approved practice or displacing an exclusive remedial structure established by enacted law would present impairment. No such operation, conflicting allocation of a finite insolvency estate or displacement of an administrative judgment is established here. The Court decides neither hypothetical exclusive schemes nor unrelated insurance conduct, criminal RICO or other federal laws. ERISA, antitrust, the rejected Premium Payor branch and ordinary RICO prerequisites remain unadjudicated in this review.

## Precedent Treatment
- **United States Department of Treasury v. Fabe:** Applied to preserve actual state policy-performance rules and necessary administration against conflicting nonspecific federal law. It does not insulate every insurer-related act or remedy from federal law.
- **Barnett Bank of Marion County, N.A. v. Nelson:** Preserved. Its insurance-specific federal exception remains separate from the interference question and does not extend to RICO by analogy; the actual relationship between the statutes must still be examined.

## Law After Decision
The McCarran-Ferguson inquiry compares the operation of the federal claim with the State's actual protected policy and administration. Neither overlap nor greater federal recovery alone bars the federal claim; actual conflict or frustration can do so even without a literal contradiction. On the relationship established here, the surviving Co-Payor RICO claim complements Nevada's fraud prohibition without displacing an exclusive state mechanism. No retrospective Nevada cause of action or broader RICO entitlement is created.

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The actual-overpayment injury limit remains; a lawful statutory multiplier is a separate remedial matter. The Court awards no liability, premium recovery or treble-damages amount and directs no automatic new trial.

**Case and dockets:** Lopez v. Monterey County; No. 97-1396; Northern District of California No. C-91-20559-RMW.
**Event and date:** Merits decision after argument, 1999-01-20; October Term 1998, chunk 3.
**Result:** Reverse dismissal and remand, 9–0; set aside the dependent tenure-order disposition to the stated extent, 9–0.
**Version / lineage:** Original decision.

## Holdings

**Authority and scope.** Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court. The eight Associate Justices join Parts I–V. Stone-Zsela joins Parts I, II and IV, and Part III only insofar as it rejects delay as authorization for prospective use and rejects the internal-administration characterization of a direct electorate change. He agrees in rejection of the collateral-designation defense and in the dependent tenure-order disposition without adding grounds to those parts. Each controlling part thus has at least eight direct joins. No separate constitutional justification for Section 5 is adopted.

### I. State compulsion does not exempt covered administration

**Holding and operative rule.** A covered political subdivision must obtain Section 5 preclearance before administering a postbaseline voting change within that subdivision, even when a noncovered State mandates the change and leaves no local discretion. The duty attaches to the County's administration; it does not require California to submit its laws in the abstract.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court applies its November 6, 1996 Lopez mandate. That decision expressly assumed complete state displacement of local discretion and nevertheless held that giving the change operative effect is administration. The lower court's renewed reliance on state compulsion cannot reopen the settled question. Neither the 1979 law nor the January 1995 elimination of justice courts supplies an exemption from the County's duty.

Young preserves the difference between the source of a policy and its actual administration, while reserving a different question about wholly federally compelled conduct. Foreman likewise rejects the proposition that an earlier authorization automatically clears subsequent local implementation. California's argument that the County cannot choose a different arrangement confuses authority to enact with authority to administer an unprecleared change. Practical difficulty affects lawful implementation, not whether the completed mandate governs. The Court decides no discriminatory purpose, retrogressive effect or entitlement to substantive preclearance.

### II. The actual submission controls the reach of approval

**Holding and operative rule.** Replacing district electorates with a countywide electorate for judges is a covered change in voting, and a submission does not preclear earlier operative changes it failed to identify. The District Court must determine the actual reach of the 1983 submission as to the final ordinance; its attachment and the Attorney General's nonobjection do not establish blanket approval of the preceding consolidations.

**Controlling explanation.** The prior Lopez decision fixes the direct electoral character of the change and the treatment of the earlier unidentified ordinances. Presley distinguishes internal allocation of governmental authority from changes in who votes for an official; this alteration concerns the electorate itself. Allen and Perkins preserve coverage of such practical electoral changes.

McCain and Clark require identification of what was submitted. Young and Foreman preserve that submission-specific boundary. The 1983 additional-judgeships submission, attached final ordinance and nonobjection are relevant evidence, but they do not establish the operative terms needed to decide conclusively whether the final ordinance itself was approved. That question remains for determination below. The Court does not invalidate an actual approval, review the merits of the Attorney General's decision, or treat an unavailable source as a party's failure to comply.

### III. Delay and a collateral designation challenge do not authorize prospective use

**Holding and operative rule.** Delay does not authorize prospective administration of an operative covered change that lacks preclearance, and the effective Section 4(b) coverage determination cannot be collaterally retried in this Section 5 enforcement action. Statutory bailout remains a distinct route; reliance, completed acts and practical timing may bear on legally available retrospective relief or a lawful transition, without supplying clearance.

**Controlling explanation.** California's remaining defenses must be separated rather than treated as another version of compulsion. Lopez already resolved prospective nonuse and the improper collateral challenge to designation. Clark and Hampton County preserve attention to remedial circumstances without making delay an authorization to use an uncleared practice. The direct change in judicial electorates likewise cannot be recast as merely internal administration under Presley. These applications enforce the existing mandate; they announce no new facial constitutional holding, decide no bailout request and create no general retrospective equitable rule.

### IV. Nonuse and the confined judicial-plan alternative

**Holding and operative rule.** The District Court must prevent prospective use of established operative changes lacking preclearance until approval is obtained through the Attorney General or the District Court for the District of Columbia. A necessary temporary plan wholly formulated by the federal court is distinct only if supported by independent lawful remedial authority, consistent with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, no more displacing of state law than necessity requires, and limited to the period before a lawful precleared arrangement becomes available.

**Controlling explanation.** Connor distinguishes a genuinely judicial plan from governmental policy, and McDaniel prevents a governmental plan from becoming exempt merely through judicial adoption. Lopez and Young apply that distinction while preserving nonuse. The lawful alternatives include the lawful precleared baseline, completed preclearance, or an independently justified judicial plan meeting the stated limits. The Court selects none here and does not authorize a court to adopt the County's policy as its own.

Clark left open whether extreme circumstances could justify allowing an election to proceed while preclearance remained pending. No such circumstance is established here, and ordinary administrative difficulty supplies no exception. The earlier ordinary six-year at-large elections did not qualify under the prior mandate. Shaw preserves substantive purpose-and-effect determinations for the designated preclearance forums. The enforcement court's task is coverage, actual submission and lawful implementation, not its own substantive approval.

### V. The dependent tenure order requires lawful reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule.** The disposition of the judicial-term-extension order must be set aside insofar as it depends on the rejected exemption and reconsidered under the corrected legal premise and actual circumstances. Reversal does not automatically revive an expired arrangement, extend any judge's tenure, install or remove an officeholder, cancel a completed election or invalidate judicial acts.

**Controlling explanation.** The District Court vacated its September 25, 1996 extension order together with dismissal of the enforcement action. Once the exemption premise fails, that dependent disposition cannot stand on the same ground. Its correction nevertheless requires lawful authority and the actual tenure circumstances; the mandate did not select officeholders or create indefinite holdovers.

Prior preclearance of a four-division option resolves only its preclearance status. It does not settle independent state-law, electorate-and-jurisdiction linkage, population, divided-municipality, residence or remedial-authority questions. Johnson's rejection of a design-only racial injury likewise does not approve all features of a proposed arrangement. The District Court must apply the existing mandate to the actual remaining questions without treating preclearance as complete legal approval. No new submission deadline, compulsory hearing or automatic return to this Court is ordered.

**Case and dockets:** Citizens for a Constitutional Convention v. Yoshina; No. 98-671; Ninth Circuit Nos. 97-16408, 97-16543, 97-16540 and 97-16596.

## Holdings

### I. A live federal claim survives the second referendum

**Holding and operative rule:** A later referendum does not moot a substantial federal claim seeking recognition of an asserted entitlement created by the earlier vote when that relief remains capable of addressing a continuing injury. These plaintiffs maintain that claim; their ultimate failure to establish it does not eliminate jurisdiction.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls this threshold holding and Part II. Part III has the different coalition stated there.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the November 1998 vote does not dispose of the maintained demand to give legal effect to the asserted 1996 approval. Act 131 submitted a new question without expressly extinguishing that asserted entitlement. *Church of Scientology* separates the possibility of meaningful relief from ultimate entitlement to it; *Spencer* requires an actual surviving interest, which the maintained certification demand supplies here. *Steel Co.*, *NAACP v. Jones* and *Wisconsin Right to Life v. Paradise* require jurisdiction before contested merits and distinguish a substantial but unsuccessful claim from an absent controversy. Fees alone, hypothetical damages and an unproved collateral ruling supply no jurisdictional premise.

The federal plaintiffs were not parties to the state action, and the two organizations' participation as friends of the court did not make them parties. The bar against lower federal appellate review of a state-court loser's judgment therefore does not foreclose their own federal claim. The authoritative state construction remains binding as state law. Under *Ex parte Young*, a claim for future implementation of a federal right against responsible officials is not a demand for retrospective damages; under *Pennhurst*, it cannot become an instruction to follow a different federal construction of state law. This permits consideration of the constitutional claim without deciding the precise certification order available had a violation been proved. No mootness-based vacatur follows.

### II. The record does not establish fundamental unfairness

**Holding and operative rule:** A state court's neutral interpretation of an ambiguous election provision does not violate due process merely because election officials earlier gave a contrary advisory interpretation. Federal relief remains available for fundamentally unfair administration, but the dissemination, reliance and voting circumstances here do not establish the pervasive deprivation needed to overturn the 1996 legal result or certify approval.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices; it states the Court's controlling due-process rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that official misinformation and authoritative construction of state law require distinct examination. A legally possible construction does not by itself answer a claim of actual official inducement, and informal guidance is not irrelevant merely because it lacked power to amend the Constitution. The polling-place sheet explained yes and no choices but said nothing about blanks. The contrary fact sheet had limited distribution. Statements to proponents and allegations of individual reliance matter, but campaigning reliance and a voter's inability to cast an effective choice are different injuries. Every submitted ballot was counted; voters had ready yes and no choices; and the record does not establish the election-wide frustration asserted.

*Burdick* requires consideration of the actual burden and the State's precise interests. Requiring affirmative support among those submitting ballots serves Hawaii's legitimate decision whether to convene a constitution-making body. Its highest court's distinction between convention and amendment-ratification language is not facially arbitrary. *Griffin v. Burns* and *Roe v. Alabama*, persuasive lower-court decisions, concern materially different changes affecting ballots cast through or withheld from officially described absentee procedures; neither makes every administrative mistake a federal violation. *Smiley* preserves the difference between interpretive authority and displacement of settled guidance; it supplies no universal immunity for retrospective change.

The Court does not find that no individual relied, that every abstainer was indifferent, or that deliberate misleading directions can never justify relief. It rejects the constitutional premise for the relief sought on this record without recounting the election under a different rule of state law.

### III. A denominator rule does not compel political speech

**Holding and operative rule:** A neutral referendum-approval threshold may include blank ballots in the denominator without compelling voters to espouse opposition when blanks remain accurately counted and reported as blanks and the rule does not discriminate among identifiable voters. The legal consequence of abstention does not itself supply the asserted First Amendment violation.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those eight Justices supply a controlling majority. Stone-Zsela agrees only with rejection of this claim and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that determining how much affirmative support a convention requires differs from attributing a political message to an unwilling voter. *Gordon v. Lance* permits nondiscriminatory heightened approval requirements; *Burdick* rejects a general entitlement to use the official ballot as an expressive tally. Here the State did not relabel blanks as affirmative no votes, alter recorded yes or no choices, require a public statement, or suppress advocacy outside the ballot. *Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation* concerns an actual requirement of personal identification during political solicitation and therefore does not govern this denominator rule. The claim that abstention makes approval harder does not establish compelled speech. The Court grants no general permission falsely to attribute political views to nonconsenting voters.

## Holdings

### I. National implementing rules coexist with state determinations

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 201(b) authorizes FCC rules implementing the local-competition obligations added to the Communications Act by §§251–252. That authority includes national standards governing the statutory tasks while state commissions retain the negotiations, arbitrations, individual rates and other determinations Congress assigned to them.

**Authority:** Scalia's Parts I–II are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices control the general-authority and associated remand propositions; O'Connor, Thomas and Breyer dissent on their distinct allocation grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's insertion of the local-competition provisions into the Communications Act brings those provisions within its implementing grant. Section 251(d)(1) directs implementation; §252(c) requires state commissions to apply §251 and the Commission's rules. National methodology and state determination of particular rates can coexist. Section 152(b) does not remove the new federal duties from the regulatory authority the amendments create. Louisiana Public Service Commission prevents a general grant from regulating matters Congress left wholly intrastate; it does not disable implementation of newly enacted federal obligations. MCI requires an actual delegation and preservation of operative limits, not a rule that every delegation permits only minor action. The contrary reading gives proper attention to the state role but treats methodology and particular determinations as mutually exclusive when the statute does not. The holding decides authority, not the substantive lawfulness of TELRIC, a confiscatory rate or every instruction the Commission might issue.

### II. The rural-exemption and agreement rules require review within the correct allocation

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical absence-of-federal-authority grounds for vacating Rules 51.405 and 51.303 and displacing otherwise lawful federal rules from state implementation cannot stand. Unresolved substantive objections return for review with the actual statutory rural-exemption, agreement-approval and state-law preservation limits intact.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice coalition controls Part II. The remand does not adopt a new rule resolving the returned substantive objections.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the general implementation rule does not adjudicate every asserted federal instruction. Section 251(f)'s exemption predicates remain for the actual assigned determinations; §§251(d)(3) and 252 retain their preservation and approval limits. MCI requires those specific statutory conditions to retain their force even when a general delegation exists. The lower court therefore must examine any unresolved objection to the rural burden-of-proof rule or preexisting-agreement requirements on its own substance. Rejection of exclusive state regulatory authority supplies no blanket validation of either rule and no permission for state commissions to disregard otherwise lawful federal requirements. The remedial disposition removes the categorical jurisdictional ground and returns the residual questions without selecting a new proof burden or deciding a particular agreement.

### III. A network element need not be a detachable physical object

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 153(29)'s network-element definition includes features, functions and capabilities supplied through facilities or equipment, as well as the physical facilities or equipment themselves. Eligibility under that definition does not establish that §251(d)(2) requires unbundled access to every eligible function.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part III is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Those eight supply a controlling majority; Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on the independent definition component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that operational-support systems, operator and directory services, and vertical-switching functions are not excluded merely because they are not detachable physical components or can also be described as services. The statutory definition itself supplies the answer. The physical-component-only construction would omit its express functional language. But definition and compulsory access are separate inquiries: the Commission must still apply the proprietary-necessity and general-impairment criteria to identify required elements. No general requirement to unbundle every technically available service follows.

### IV. Necessity and impairment must perform independent limiting work

**Holding and operative rule:** In selecting required network elements under §251(d)(2), the FCC must give independent limiting effect to necessity for proprietary elements and impairment for network elements generally, considering realistically available alternatives outside as well as inside the incumbent's network. Rule 51.319 is set aside because the adopted comparison and impairment standard did not perform that task.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, giving the complete rule seven votes. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the invalidity holding that the rule must make element-specific judgments giving necessity and impairment independent constraining force, and joins vacatur and agency remand; he does not supply a separate join in the more specific outside-alternatives requirement. Souter dissents from invalidation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that any increase in cost or decline in quality cannot by itself establish the required impairment while external alternatives are categorically ignored. MCI preserves operative statutory predicates before agency deference. The agency's competitive-entry expertise remains substantial, but it cannot make every purchasing disadvantage a sufficient statutory obstacle. The proprietary standard of significant impairment or thwarting is not invalid simply because it is less than absolute indispensability; its defect includes the cramped alternative comparison limited to the incumbent's nonproprietary elements. The Court does not require access only to an irreplaceable natural monopoly, invent a numerical threshold or choose a replacement list. On remand the agency may explain real entry impairment, actual availability, costs, quality, service requirements and workable selection criteria under the enacted standard. The strongest contrary argument invokes the competitive-entry purpose and danger that theoretical alternatives will require disabling duplication. The Court accepts that real commercial availability matters; that is why an actual inquiry is required rather than either automatic access or theoretical replication.

### V. The Act imposes no freestanding own-facilities prerequisite

**Holding and operative rule:** A requesting telecommunications carrier need not own some local facilities merely to qualify to seek unbundled access under §251(c)(3). Its entitlement to particular elements remains governed by §251(d)(2) and the lawful conditions of access and combination.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part V is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejection of the independent own-facilities prerequisite, without a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(3) reaches any requesting telecommunications carrier and contemplates combining elements into a service. The separate resale route in §251(c)(4) does not insert a minimum-property requirement into the unbundling route. Combining or buying elements entails obligations and commercial consequences distinct from buying a finished retail service for resale; the existence of both routes does not make one redundant. This holding promises no entrant the incumbent's whole network regardless of necessity and impairment and decides no assumed difference in particular rates.

### VI. Existing combinations need not be dismantled

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 51.315(b) lawfully prohibits an incumbent from separating requested network elements already combined in its network unless the requesting carrier asks for separation. Unbundled availability and valuation do not require physical destruction of an existing combination.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Scalia's Parts VI–VII; those parts control the existing-combination and individual-arrangement rules with their stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(3)'s direction to make elements available in a manner permitting the requester to combine them does not require the incumbent first to dismantle a configuration already joined. The Commission reasonably implements nondiscriminatory access by preserving that configuration subject to the requester's option to separate. The contrary reading confuses separately available elements with mandatory physical separation. This holding restores paragraph (b), not the distinct affirmative new-combination duties in paragraphs (c)–(f), and does not require an unestablished configuration to be built. Those duties receive their own disposition. Particular requests remain subject to the Act and valid technical and operational requirements.

### VII. An entrant may select an individual agreement arrangement with its pertinent conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 252(i) permits Rule 51.809's access to an individual interconnection, service or network-element arrangement in an approved agreement on the same pertinent rates, terms and conditions. The rule preserves proof of greater provision costs or technical infeasibility and limits availability to a reasonable period after public filing.

**Authority:** The same nine-Justice coalition joins Part VII and controls this rule and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory reference to any interconnection, service or network element supports selection of an individual arrangement, not a requirement to accept the entire agreement. The entrant may not take an isolated favorable price while discarding its relevant obligations. The incumbent may establish that provision to this requester costs more or is technically infeasible, and the reasonable availability period protects against indefinite use of stale arrangements. The challengers argue that selective access undermines negotiated tradeoffs; the agency reached a contrary practical judgment with nondiscrimination and these safeguards in view. That disagreement does not establish that Congress commanded whole-agreement adoption or that the agency's incentive assessment is irrational. No unrestricted cherry-picking right results.

### VIII. Dialing-parity authority and numbering-cost ripeness remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical absence-of-FCC-authority ground for vacating intraLATA toll-dialing-parity rules is rejected, with remaining objections returned for review. The separate Rule 52.17 numbering-cost challenge remains unripe because its asserted disadvantage depends on particular cost-recovery rate decisions not before the Court.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part VIII parity-authority ground is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. O'Connor agrees on the narrower §251(b)(3)/§251(d)(1) implementation ground stated in her separate opinion. Stone-Zsela agrees in the parity disposition without an additional ground; the five-Justice lead rationale independently controls. All eight Associates join Part VIII's separate numbering-cost ripeness holding; Stone-Zsela agrees only in that disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that federal implementation of §251(b)(3)'s substantive parity duty is authorized. The general implementing grant supports that conclusion, and the narrower substantive-duty ground also supports the parity disposition without resolving the separate state-pricing dispute. Section 271 and other actual statutory limits remain operative. The separate numbering issue concerns who will bear costs under future particular rate decisions. Reversal of the authority ground neither resolves those rates nor creates an accrued numbering injury. The California judgment therefore receives distinct dispositions rather than complete reversal by inference from the general-authority holding. No particular carrier's liability or cost share is fixed.

### IX. Technical feasibility cannot replace the required access criteria

**Holding and operative rule:** The partial vacatur of Rule 51.317's technical-feasibility presumption and Order paragraphs 278 and 281 is affirmed because the presumption and its actual exceptions fail to preserve the required necessity and impairment limits. The separately sustained definition of technical feasibility and other unaffected portions of the rule remain outside that invalidation.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IX is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven control the substantive presumption holding. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the partial invalidation; Souter would remand this component without a final validity ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that technical feasibility may inform element selection: §251(d)(2)'s minimum criteria do not prohibit consideration of additional relevant matters. For elements beyond Rule 51.319's list, once technical feasibility is found, Rule 51.317 permits the state commission to decline unbundling only if either (1) the element is proprietary or would reveal proprietary information, and the requester could offer the same proposed service through other nonproprietary unbundled elements within the incumbent's network; or (2) denial would neither reduce service quality nor increase its financial or administrative cost compared with providing that service through other unbundled elements in that network. Those restrictions reproduce the limiting-standard defect in the required-element list. They do not allow realistic outside alternatives to perform their proper role. The result rests on this independent substantive flaw, not on the mistaken premise that feasibility can never matter. A corrected-delegation remand cannot preserve the existing defective criteria without agency reconsideration. The Court does not forbid every lawful presumption or invalidate all of Rule 51.317.

### X. Superior-quality duties return for review under the correct framework

**Holding and operative rule:** The invalidation of Rules 51.305(a)(4) and 51.311(c) is vacated and remanded for reconsideration of the actual conditions and agency justification under the correct implementing authority. The Court does not decide their ultimate validity or order unconditional reinstatement.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join Part X's corrected-standard remand and its confined review directions. Thomas and Breyer do so under the Court's governing authority framework while retaining their dissent from Part I. Stone-Zsela agrees in the remand without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(2)'s requirement of at-least-equal quality establishes a floor; the absence of an express superior-quality command does not alone show that an implementing rule can never require more. The lower court's existing-network and substantive-conflict objections nevertheless remain for review. Rule 51.305(a)(4) concerns requested, technically feasible interconnection superior to that supplied to the incumbent, affiliates or other interconnection recipients. Rule 51.311(c) separately concerns the element and access superior to the incumbent's own, upon request and to the extent feasible, placing technical-infeasibility proof before the state commission on the incumbent. Lesser quality at the requesting carrier's sole request remains permitted. On remand the actual improvement, statutory relationship and agency explanation must be examined. Compensation alone supplies no missing authority, and nondiscrimination alone does not command every upgrade. The lower court's allowance for modifications necessary to accommodate lawful access is preserved; neither an unconditional superior-facilities right nor a categorical ban on necessary modifications is adopted.

### XI. Affirmative new-combination duties require separate reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule:** The invalidation of Rule 51.315(c)–(f) is vacated and remanded for review under the proper implementing-power standard. Section 251(c)(3)'s direction to enable requesting carriers to combine elements does not unambiguously assign every physical combination task exclusively to the requester; the particular affirmative duties and burdens remain undecided.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part XI is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Breyer's agreement is a remand under the governing authority framework, not a Part I join. Seven Justices control this remand; Stone-Zsela agrees only in its disposition, and Thomas dissents on his independent task-allocation ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the lower construction treated enabling access as an exclusive assignment of all combining labor without adequately addressing the actual delegation, network control, conditions and agency explanation. That differs from preserving existing combinations, which the Court separately upholds. Subsection (c) addresses requested configurations the incumbent does not ordinarily make, only when technically feasible and not impairing other carriers' access or interconnection. Subsection (d) addresses combination with the requester's own elements in a technically feasible manner. The other-carrier condition expressly belongs to (c), not automatically to (d). Subsections (e) and (f) place the relevant infeasibility or other-carrier-impairment showing before the state commission on the incumbent. MCI preserves specific statutory allocations, but the contested sentence does not alone establish the exclusive task allocation the lower court found. The remand neither approves every proof burden nor compels a particular configuration. Voluntary combination and necessary accommodation remain distinct from a mandated new combination.

### XII. The general section 208 announcement is not ripe for review

**Holding and operative rule:** A definite agency announcement of a possible enforcement forum is not ripe merely because the position is complete and the dispute predominantly legal. Because this announcement directs no present change in primary conduct, determines no carrier-specific liability and permits meaningful review of a concrete application, the general challenge must be dismissed without prejudice, without deciding enforcement authority.

**Authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Scalia's Part XII, supplying a controlling majority. Stone-Zsela agrees only in vacatur of the premature enforcement-merits adjudication and dismissal without prejudice as unripe; he adds no enforcement or ripeness ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that finality, standing, fitness and hardship are distinct; it decides ripeness, not independent standing or final-order questions. Ohio Forestry and Toilet Goods distinguish settled policy from presently consequential application. Paragraphs 126–128 definitively assert complaint authority even where a carrier complies with a state-approved agreement, and separately contemplate enforcing agreement terms; a later expedited-procedure rulemaking does not make that assertion tentative. Yet the announcement changes no substantive carrier duty, identifies no operative agreement conflict, revises no state determination and orders no act or liability. A concrete complaint would distinguish statutory enforcement, agreement enforcement and a carrier proceeding that effectively revisits state action.

Footnote 236 says the Commission might decline financial penalties in some state-authorized circumstances; it establishes neither immunity nor a required penalty. The statement that judicially decided issues are unlikely to be revisited likewise creates no absolute preclusion rule. The absence of an absolute safe harbor is not itself withdrawal of an established protection or present liability. Bennett's operative legal-protection change and Franklin's completed determination are therefore different.

Section 208(a) supplies complaint, notice, opportunity to satisfy or answer and investigation under its conditions; §208(b)(3) makes an order concluding a covered investigation appealable under §402(a). Concrete application permits the authority objection in its actual setting. The prospect of defending a future complaint and uncertainty about forum allocation do not alone establish present primary-conduct hardship. Lee preserves review of presently burdensome civil duties, and Pic-A-State concerns an existing business actually constrained by a law; neither condition is established by this instrument. NAACP and Paradise require examination of the actual claim, not an automatic further inquiry into hypothetical applications. The Court creates no universal exhaustion rule, promises no future filing's standing or timeliness, and does not defer every challenge until penalties are collected. A presently binding or burdensome statement remains a different case. The Court decides neither carrier statutory-violation authority, agreement enforcement, review of state determinations under §252(e)(6), nor state-failure authority under §252(e)(5); no carrier complaint, agreement, state order or penalty is adjudicated.

## Precedent Treatment

- *MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T* is applied to identify actual delegation and preserve enacted limiting conditions; it is distinguished from a categorical ban on consequential implementing rules. Its tariff-filing holding is not changed.
- *Louisiana Public Service Commission v. FCC* retains its protection of matters Congress left wholly intrastate; the Court distinguishes the newly enacted federal local-competition obligations from those matters.
- *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. NRDC* supplies review within actual delegation and statutory limits, not authority to remove necessity or impairment from the selection inquiry.
- *Allentown Mack Sales & Service v. NLRB* remains authority for administering the actual announced lawful standard; no judicial substitution of an economic rule is authorized.
- *Ohio Forestry Association v. Sierra Club* and *Toilet Goods Association v. Gardner* are applied to the difference between settled policy and presently consequential application, and the fitness/hardship inquiry.
- *Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner* retains its fitness-and-hardship framework; a presently burdensome rule remains distinct from this forum announcement.
- *Lee v. Harcleroad* is distinguished because present civil duties can create hardship without criminal penalties; the announcement here itself imposes no such duty.
- *Pic-A-State PA, Inc. v. Reno* is distinguished because its claimant's existing business was actually stopped by the challenged law and real enforcement risk.
- *Franklin v. Massachusetts* and *Bennett v. Spear* are distinguished as completed determinations carrying concrete legal consequences; finality alone supplies no section 208 ripeness holding.
- *NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones* and *Wisconsin Right to Life v. Paradise* require attention to the actual jurisdictional claim and remedy; neither commands another factual inquiry after this instrument's threshold status is ascertainable.

## Law After Decision

As of January 25, 1999, the FCC may issue national implementing rules for §§251–252 while respecting specifically assigned state functions. Required-element selection must give necessity and impairment independent effect and consider realistic alternatives; the existing Rule 51.319 list and identified feasibility presumption cannot supply the defective standard. Functional network elements and requests by carriers without their own facilities remain eligible subject to lawful access criteria. Existing combinations and qualifying individual agreement arrangements receive the protections and limits stated here. Superior-quality and affirmative new-combination requirements return for review without final merits approval. General rulemaking authority decides no particular rate or section 208 enforcement power. The general section 208 announcement and contingent California numbering-cost challenge receive their distinct unripe dispositions; no carrier proceeding, penalty or state order is decided.

**Case and dockets:** Department of Commerce v. United States House of Representatives; Clinton v. Glavin; Nos. 98-404 and 98-564.
**Event and date:** Merits decision, 1999-01-25; OT1998, chunk 4.

## Holdings

### I. A supported threat to personal representation permits Glavin review

**Holding and operative rule:** A voter may challenge an announced census method before the final apportionment when summary-judgment evidence sufficiently supports a concrete threat to that voter's representation, causally attributable to the method and likely redressable by relief against responsible officials. Hofmeister's Indiana representation claim meets that standard; neither statutory authorization alone nor an unsupported prediction would suffice.

**Authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices, controls.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Weber's comparison of Bureau projections and measured differential undercounts identifies a particular marginal Indiana seat at risk. Wetrogan's updated projections and Thompson's objections concerning redesigned methods, uncertainty and overseas personnel qualify the forecast but do not eliminate the supported threat attributable to sampling. *Lujan* requires evidence appropriate to summary judgment, not merely pleading a possible injury; it does not require certainty of the eventual allocation. *Franklin* connects representation injury to relief directed at census officials without requiring a completed census before prevention may be sought. Section 209 makes the plan reviewable and provides expedition, but does not manufacture injury. The Court adopts no finding of conceded or certain seat loss and need not decide county-funding, intrastate-districting or feared invalid-census/reversion theories. Each would require its own personal and causal showing.

### II. The House has not established its own institutional injury

**Holding and operative rule:** Statutory authorization for the House to sue does not establish Article III injury where the asserted harm is disagreement with the lawfulness of a future census methodology, without withheld information or a demonstrated present impairment of institutional power. This House action must be dismissed without prejudice, and its judgment and injunction vacated.

**Authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies six controlling rationale votes. Stone-Zsela supplies a seventh judgment vote only. Stevens and Breyer dissent from this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the authorized House is a different plaintiff from the individual legislators in *Raines*, which distinguishes authorization from the existence of a concrete constitutional injury. The House will receive an apportionment report; the dispute concerns the legality of its preparation, not the withholding of particular information for an established use. *Akins* protects actual deprivation of statutorily required electoral information and does not convert every methodology dispute into informational injury. The House retains 435 seats, while the composition theory concerns later electorates and membership without present nullification of its votes or demonstrated impairment of concrete work. The House's continuing identity and statutory authorization therefore do not cure the missing injury. The Court adopts no categorical bar on institutional suits and reserves actual information refusals, subpoena enforcement, property injury and complete vote nullification for their own records. Standing in Glavin cannot be transferred to this separate plaintiff.

### III. The two proposed sampling methods are statutorily permissible

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 141 and 195 permit the proposed nonresponse follow-up and Integrated Coverage Measurement methods, as components of the actual enumeration effort described in this record, to contribute to the population tabulation used for interstate House apportionment. The Act does not require the Secretary to select these methods, and the statutory injunction against their use cannot stand.

**Authority:** Souter's Part III-A has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The shared proposition concerns these two designs, not every possible sampling plan. Part III-B's general construction has only Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; Breyer supplies a distinct, narrower statutory explanation in his separate opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute allows the challenged methods in the described census. That conclusion has five votes, but the five do not adopt a common general construction of the apportionment exception. The plurality reads the exception as removing a duty while preserving discretion; Breyer permits the methods because they supplement the actual enumeration effort. Their distinct grounds are set out below. Neither the general discretion theory nor Breyer's proposed ban on replacing enumeration with a sample census becomes a majority holding. The common permission does not establish a fixed percentage safe harbor, compel sampling when feasible for apportionment, or dispose of a materially different design. Section 141(e)(2)'s separate mid-decade restriction is not the §195 exception and remains undisturbed.

### IV. The Constitution permits this explained use of statistical supplementation

**Holding and operative rule:** The Enumeration Clause requires a genuine population-counting effort reasonably related to enumeration and population-based representation, rather than an exclusively headcount method. The proposed follow-up and coverage-measurement design satisfies that requirement on its explained geographic and methodological record; materially different, unsupported, discriminatory or unauthorized implementation remains reviewable.

**Authority:** Souter's Part IV is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer and controls with five votes. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas resolve Glavin on statutory prohibition and do not decide constitutional validity.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an actual enumeration may combine direct collection with reasoned statistical correction of its limitations. *Franklin* rejects a literal physical-presence requirement without itself deciding sampling. *Wisconsin* supplies the reasonable-relationship standard, respect for an explained methodological choice and the need to examine geographic accuracy and uncertainty; its reservation left this question open. The plan combines initial collection, tract-based follow-up and coverage measurement directed at differential omissions. Its explanation distinguishes random block error from systematic bias, examines state and congressional-district accuracy, and addresses aggregation and uncertainty. The challengers correctly observe that better national totals alone would not establish better representation. The actual geographic explanation meets that objection sufficiently to establish the required reasonable relationship; the Court does not find that every State or district will improve or that error is impossible. Neither conventional enumeration nor statistical supplementation receives automatic superiority. The decision requires no particular census method, fixes no permissible error rate, and gives no advance approval to unexplained later changes. The presented constitutional objections therefore supply no basis to preserve the Glavin injunction or require a remand for those same objections.

## Precedent Treatment

- *Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife* is applied at the summary-judgment stage: a supported threatened personal injury is required; legislative authorization cannot replace it.
- *Franklin v. Massachusetts* is applied to representation injury, the official remedial chain and rejection of a literal physical-presence rule. Its actual-reasons review remains distinct from constitutional reasonableness, and no presidential APA or coercive-relief question is decided.
- *Raines v. Byrd* is applied by distinguishing the authorized institution from individual legislators while independently requiring concrete injury. Its reservation of institutional suits is preserved; this particular House claim fails.
- *Federal Election Commission v. Akins* is distinguished: actual withheld information for a concrete electoral use differs from disagreement over lawful preparation of a future report. Its informational-injury rule remains effective.
- *Wisconsin v. City of New York* is applied to explained census choice, geographic accuracy and uncertainty. Its reserved constitutional sampling question is answered for these methods; its reasonable-relationship standard is unchanged, and neither it nor this decision commands adjustment.
- *Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment*, *NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones* and *Wisconsin Right to Life v. Paradise* are applied to decide each plaintiff's actual threshold before contested merits. No jurisdiction is assumed to obtain either docket's result.

## Law After Decision

As of January 25, 1999, the two proposed apportionment methods are permitted by the Census Act and satisfy the Enumeration Clause on this design and explanation. Five Justices support that statutory permission and the constitutional rule, but only four adopt the general permission-versus-duty interpretation of §195. No controlling prohibition on every replacement sample census or general percentage safe harbor is established. The six-Justice House-standing rationale requires an institution's own concrete injury and leaves other institutional claims open. Review of actual implementation, geographic reliability and compliance with independent statutory limits survives. No sampling mandate or new exemption from judicial review results.
