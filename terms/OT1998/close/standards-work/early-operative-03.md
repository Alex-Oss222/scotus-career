### The lender's pecuniary stake permits appellate review

**Operative rule:** The credit union is sufficiently pecuniarily aggrieved to appeal refusal of this proposed reaffirmation because approval would make the proposed personal car-loan and $1,500 unsecured obligations enforceable. That appellate stake permits review but does not establish a right to approval or remove §524's conditions.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this threshold proposition and its limited consequence.

### Mandatory notice and performance do not create an exclusive retention election

**Operative rule:** For the scheduled consumer debts secured by estate property covered by the then-effective §521(2), the debtor must file the statement of retention or surrender within thirty days after filing or by the creditors' meeting, whichever is earlier, with a for-cause extension fixed within that period; the statement must specify exemption, redemption or reaffirmation when applicable, and the debtor must perform the lawful stated intention within forty-five days after that notice, with a for-cause extension fixed within those forty-five days. Read with (C)'s preservation of the debtor's and trustee's rights, those mandatory duties do not themselves make surrender, redemption or reaffirmation the exclusive substantive options: a debtor may maintain payments and retain collateral insofar as existing law and the enforceable agreement permit, without stripping the lien, excusing an actual lawful default or obtaining judicially reduced installment redemption.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the complete statutory rule, timing qualifications and retained-rights limitation.

The creditor argues that the list otherwise lacks force and that possession without personal liability imposes an involuntary new bargain. Notice and performance retain their stated work, while the creditor retains the lien and remedies for a legally established default. Personal discharge and survival of a property claim are distinct, as Johnson and Dewsnup recognize. Dewsnup's particular Chapter 7 lien rule does not add a compulsory election here. Nobelman's Chapter 13 principal-residence protection likewise turns on its own enacted rights clause, not a universal requirement of continued personal liability for every retained asset. Ron Pair requires attention to this provision's actual text; Butner preserves state-defined interests subject to the Code's specified consequences.

The Court does not construe an unseen bankruptcy-default, insurance or impairment clause or hold that every such clause is invalid. Continued possession remains subject to the actual lawful agreement, applicable default rules, the valid lien and the Code's independent stay and discharge provisions. Section 521(2) alone supplies no new acceleration or forfeiture command.

### This agreement remains subject to independent debtor-protection approval

**Operative rule:** For this unrepresented individual, §524(c)(6)(A) requires judicial approval that the reaffirmation both does not impose undue hardship on the debtor or a dependent of the debtor and is in the debtor's best interest; (6)(B)'s exception for consumer debt secured by real property does not cover this car and unsecured card debt. Signing the agreement does not satisfy those distinct conditions, and on the reviewed continued-payment-retention premise refusal of this agreement was within the bankruptcy court's discretion.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the approval rule and the agreement-specific affirmance.

The no-undue-hardship and best-interest inquiries are separate requirements. The lender's appellate standing does not displace either, and mutual assent does not substitute for the approval required for this unrepresented debtor. This decision concerns approval under §524(c)(6), not compliance with the other enforceability conditions in §524(c) and (d). Section 524(f) permits voluntary repayment, which does not restore enforceable discharged personal liability.

The affirmance concerns this agreement and this reviewed statutory premise. It does not presume every reaffirmation unwise, force the lender to extend credit, reduce the debt by judicial valuation or approve a replacement bargain. Ordinary lien enforcement remains subject to its actual legal prerequisites.

## Holdings

### I. Chapman governs this available capital-selection misinformation claim

**Holding and operative rule:** For a constitutional trial error in the commutation instruction affecting this jury's choice between death and life without parole, when the claim is properly available on federal habeas, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt from the whole penalty record that the error did not contribute to the actual death sentence. If an available constitutional violation fails that showing, conditional sentence relief must permit a reasonable opportunity for a lawful new penalty proceeding; a proper harmlessness showing permits denial of relief.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Ginsburg disagrees with the remand disposition but directly joins this rule and its conditions. No combination of different standards establishes its authority.

Collateral finality and respect for state judgments remain substantial, but they do not supply a sufficient reason to lower the constitutional burden for this category. Independent habeas requirements continue to operate, and Chapman permits a harmless sentence to stand. The inquiry concerns this jury's actual decision, not whether a properly instructed hypothetical jury could have imposed death. The reviewing court must consider the complete charge, mitigation, prosecution theory and supported jury requests. A direction to disregard commutation may matter; it does not itself prove that the earlier misinformation made no contribution.

The Court assumes the constitutional error found below to resolve the presented effect question. It neither newly decides the unpresented accuracy rule nor treats every capital instruction as structural. The rule is confined to this defined capital-selection error and does not settle every remaining constitutional-habeas category. A final writ requires an available established violation and failure of the State's showing, not merely an appellate method error.

**Precedent treatment:** Brecht, O'Neal and Roy are extended to this defined capital-selection category while their prior holdings and reservations otherwise remain. Chapman retains the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden; Kotteakos is not substituted for that burden here.

### II. The required effect inquiry is substantive, not a verbal formula

**Holding and operative rule:** Identifying this instructional error under Boyde does not by itself establish the effect necessary for relief under the applicable harmlessness rule. A court may perform that inquiry within its existing discussion without prescribed words, a particular citation or a separate heading, but its actual reasoning must assess the error's relationship to the sentence under the governing burden and treatment of uncertainty.

**Authority:** Part III of the per curiam opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices. That agreement concerns the governing distinction and record boundaries; it does not resolve their disagreement about the sufficiency of the analysis in this case.

The District Court examined the actual prosecution argument, aggravation, mitigation, conviction-document request and record as a whole. It also explained why the corrective direction did not cure the misinformation. The Ninth Circuit expressly received the State's effect objection. It would therefore be incorrect to describe these courts as having ignored the record or the issue. Conversely, discussing the record while identifying likely interference with mitigation does not automatically establish the separate causal conclusion the selected harmlessness rule requires.

The trial evidence must remain distinct from mitigation first developed during habeas. New's excluded account of deliberations supplies no finding of actual release discussion. The jury's request for conviction documents and its separate general instruction rereading do not establish a specific commutation inquiry. The trial judge's automatic-review findings are not jury findings on individual mitigators. Supported inferences remain available, but neither strong aggravation nor an imagined jury's preferred sentence can substitute for the required actual-effect determination. The Court orders no new admission ruling or factual hearing.

**Precedent treatment:** Boyde retains its error-identification function. Sochor's substantive-correction principle is applied without transferring its distinct invalid-aggravator facts. Roy's actual-verdict approach informs the inquiry without requiring factor-specific jury findings or supplying an appellate substitute sentence.

### III. The limited vacatur requires renewed review without deciding harmlessness

**Holding and operative rule:** The commutation-based appellate sentence disposition is vacated and returned for a genuine whole-record inquiry into the error's effect under the governing rule. The receiving court may reach the same sentence-relief result, but neither the constitutional defect alone nor a failure to use a particular formula decides the inquiry; guilt, genuinely independent habeas conditions and the actual appellate status of other penalty claims remain undisturbed.

**Authority:** Part IV and the judgment have Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. These seven adopt the confined return without a common join in one harmlessness standard. The five direct joins in Part II independently establish Chapman as the rule governing further proceedings; no fractured-opinion synthesis is used.

The Court returns that unfinished causal task rather than demanding another label. The receiving court must consider the entire appropriate trial record, including the State's substantial aggravation and prison-safety case, the broader danger language described below, the mitigation actually heard and the complete instructions. It must determine the error's effect without presuming subjective deliberations or treating the court's own sentencing preference as the jury's verdict. Neither the corrective direction nor the strength of aggravation is conclusive by itself.

No finding that the error was harmless, or that the State has already failed its governing burden, issues now. The court may retain sentence relief after the proper inquiry. No new evidentiary hearing, admission of excluded material, execution permission, release order or fixed sentencing deadline follows. Other penalty claims were addressed below but not reached on appeal; this disposition neither decides them nor revives an abandoned claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Roy's distinction between an actual method defect and an absent citation supports the limited return; it does not supply a result on this record. Brecht and O'Neal preserve the distinction between corrective remand and conditional relief after an available violation fails the applicable showing. Simmons's different express parole question is not imported.

### IV. Carriger supplies the former-law rule for this pending capital application

**Holding and operative rule:** Because this capital habeas application was pending before April 24, 1996, and chapter 154 qualification is not established, amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)'s replacement factual presumption do not govern. Former §2254(d) governs that factual inquiry: its presumption requires a historical fact determination after a merits hearing by a competent state court in a proceeding involving the applicant and the State or its officer or agent, evidenced by a written finding, opinion or other reliable and adequate written indicia; paragraph (8) requires examination of whether the pertinent produced state record as a whole fairly supports the finding.

**Authority:** Part V of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. This is application of Carriger's existing controlling rule; the Chief Justice supplies no additional temporal-law ground.

The legal harmlessness inquiry remains distinct from the proper treatment of historical state findings. Former §2254(d) protects findings satisfying its predicates while retaining paragraph (8)'s whole-record qualification; neither unrestricted appellate factfinding nor conclusive deference follows. Separately directed provisions retain their own operation. The Court does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA amendment, decide §2254(e)(2)'s temporal reach or establish an entitlement to new evidence or a hearing.

**Precedent treatment:** Carriger's April 16, 1998 pending-capital holding is applied without change. Lindh's noncapital holding and preserved statutory exceptions remain intact.



SOURCE 23: haddle_v_garrison_merits_1998-12-14.md
## Holdings

### Loss of at-will employment may be the injury caused by a forbidden federal-witness conspiracy

**Operative rule:** When two or more persons conspire to deter a party or witness by force, intimidation or threat from attending federal court or testifying there freely, fully and truthfully, or to injure a party or witness for having attended or testified, loss of at-will employment and wages caused by acts furthering that conspiracy may constitute injury to person or property under §1985's civil-remedy clause without a constitutional entitlement to continued employment. Haddle's allegations of concrete employment loss cannot be dismissed merely because the employment was terminable at will; the prohibited conspiracy and object, required federal-witness nexus, acts, causation, recoverable damages and properly presented defenses remain to be adjudicated.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this proposition, its application to the categorical ground of dismissal and the bounded remand.

At-will status defines the employer's ordinary authority to terminate the relationship. It does not make the employee's existing work and wages valueless against prohibited interference. Truax v. Raich recognizes that distinction. Roth's constitutional-property rule concerns an entitlement protected against deprivation without due process, a different inquiry from injury caused by this statutory conspiracy. Darden's attention to common-law context supports reading the employment interest in its actual legal setting, without importing its separate employee-classification factors.

Respondents' argument correctly denies guaranteed employment but draws the wrong consequence for the alleged economic harm. Bray requires attention to the particular §1985 clause and does not make its distinct private-right requirements govern this federal-witness injury branch. Robinson and Inter-Modal likewise distinguish protection against conduct forbidden by a statute from a permanent right to the underlying job or benefit. Haddle alleges lost employment and wages from the forbidden object, which defeats this categorical pleading objection.

The Court does not find that the alleged conspiracy occurred or that these corporate agents are legally independent third parties capable of conspiring. It decides neither every witness-definition question nor damages amount, reinstatement or punitive relief. The statutory object, causation and other applicable requirements prevent this holding from becoming a federal remedy for every discharge.



SOURCE 24: nynex_corp_v_discon_merits_1998-12-14.md
## Holdings

### This vertical supplier substitution is not a per se group boycott

**Operative rule:** A single buyer's agreement to purchase services from one supplier instead of another, without horizontal concerted exclusion or another established per se basis, is not a per se group boycott merely because it intentionally injures a rival, serves a fraudulent purpose or lacks an apparent efficiency justification. The pleaded vertical purchasing arrangement must therefore be assessed under the rule of reason on the surviving §1 competitive-effects theory; genuine horizontal restraints, actual price fixing and other established categories retain their distinct requirements.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this classification and its limitations.

MECo purchased removal services for its affiliated telephone company; it did not supply those services or compete in their overall market. Its separate incorporation and resale to the affiliate do not supply a horizontal agreement among removal-service competitors. The allegation that it preferred AT&T Technologies to Discon describes the vertical supplier choice at issue, not concerted exclusion among competing suppliers.

Discon's strongest argument is that choosing a more expensive supplier to destroy a rival has no plausible competitive benefit. The alleged purpose may matter in ordinary review, but reprehensible motive does not establish the class of restraint required for automatic condemnation. Otherwise any buyer's questionable supplier choice could become a per se boycott without proof that competition was reduced. The Court rejects that classification; it neither finds the purchasing arrangement reasonable nor immunizes independently actionable fraud or exclusion.

### Regulatory fraud and harm to one supplier do not replace competitive injury

**Operative rule:** Regulatory deception, consumer overcharges and elimination of one supplier do not by themselves establish that the challenged purchasing agreement injured the competitive process; Discon must establish the competitive injury and other elements of the theory actually pursued. The lower courts must reconsider the surviving §1 effects claim and the derivative rationale for the distinct §2 conspiracy claim without treating the rejected per se classification or fraud alone as sufficient, while leaving previously dismissed claims outside this remand.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the requirement, its application and the bounded remand for both affected components.

That distinction is not a finding that the arrangement was harmless. Kodak requires attention to actual competitive conditions rather than an unsupported categorical assumption, and Khan preserves liability for restraints shown to have harmful competitive effects outside a rejected per se category. The lower court had retained a §1 effects inquiry as well as possible per se treatment. Its error in allowing the latter supplies no automatic judgment against every version of the former.

The separately reinstated §2 conspiracy claim must likewise be reconsidered insofar as it relies on the rejected premises concerning the same purchasing practices. Spectrum Sports preserves the elements of the actual offense; an attempted-monopolization requirement cannot simply be transferred to conspiracy. The Court decides neither that conspiracy claim's ultimate sufficiency nor a final market-power or causation fact. The limited grant does not authorize wholesale dismissal of all remaining theories or reopening of the already dismissed vertical-price-fixing, monopoly, attempt and RICO components. No damages or injunction follows from this classification decision.

## Holdings

### Candidate expenditures and the asserted municipal interests

**Holding and operative rule.** Cincinnati may not impose this mandatory ceiling on the total campaign expenditures of a candidate or candidate committee. A direct limit on the quantity of candidate advocacy receives the demanding First Amendment review applicable to expenditures; the accepted corruption, campaign-cost, equalization, official-work and public-confidence grounds do not justify this salary-linked, source-independent restraint.

**Authority.** Kennedy's opinion for the unanimous Court supplies the complete rule, application and limits. Every participating Justice joins it in full.

Fundraising's interference with official work is a distinct concern. The ceiling nevertheless reaches challengers and personally financed advocacy as well as incumbent fundraising, and its link to salary does not supply the missing fit. General public confidence cannot cure that defect or revive the rejected cost and equalization grounds. That a meaningful campaign can be conducted below approximately $140,000 does not permit government to forbid additional advocacy. The 1995 spending evidence preceded the new contribution controls and does not establish their inadequacy; the Court imposes no mandatory empirical waiting period.

Colorado Republican distinguishes actual candidate-resource transactions from political expression; Kentucky Right to Life likewise requires attention to the particular receipt or intermediary restraint. Neither converts this total-spending ceiling into a contribution limit. The Court accepts the City's asserted facts for summary judgment and decides their legal sufficiency, without finding those submissions false. It leaves materially different measures addressing official duties, separate contribution and disclosure provisions, voluntary public-financing conditions and damages outside this decision.

**Application.** The invalid ceiling is three times annual council compensation and governs candidate or candidate-committee election spending. Its exclusions for independent expenditures, uncompensated volunteer services and the described party slate or sample-ballot publications identifying endorsed candidates do not cure the restriction on candidate expression. The Sixth Circuit's invalidation is affirmed; no further evidentiary proceeding or replacement ceiling is ordered.

## Holdings

### The asserted federal damages action

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 531(e) of the Cable Act does not, by itself and in the statutory setting presented, create the asserted implied private damages action against Cablevision. A prohibition protecting public-access programming does not establish congressional intent to authorize that judicial damages remedy; independently authorized forms of relief must be identified and tested under their own sources.

**Authority.** Souter's unanimous Court opinion controls this rule, its damages-only review scope and the confined remand. All nine Justices join the full explanation.

The strongest contrary argument combines the protected speakers, the direct editorial-control prohibition, the possible gap in franchise enforcement and Congress's later amendments. Those amendments followed only a few district-court recognitions, not the settled received-action background on which Curran and the existing securities action rest. The subsequent abandonment of one lower recognition is not attributed to Congress as foreknowledge. A gap in compensation does not itself supply the intended remedy, and the Court does not assume local enforcement is adequate or exclusive.

Suter preserves the separate action-creation inquiry. Franklin's appropriate-relief principle applies after a cognizable action exists; it cannot create its own antecedent. Central Bank and Musick, Peeler concern incidents of the received securities action and do not authorize importing it into the Cable Act. The supplied question concerns damages. The Court therefore decides neither every prospective remedy nor the legality of Cablevision's actual exclusion.

**Application, remedy and limits.** The Second Circuit's judgment is reversed only insofar as it permits the asserted implied federal damages action, and the case is remanded. The complaint also requested injunctions and asserted Connecticut law. Any surviving relief requires an actual authorized source and the ordinary preservation, jurisdictional and remedial requirements; no equitable action is created by the request itself. Viewer-claim status remains unresolved, no unpleaded section 1983 claim is created, and no damages are awarded.

Section 531(e) remains subject to section 544(d). Its separate permission to refuse public-access programs or portions containing obscenity, indecency or nudity is permission, not an obligation, and does not automatically extend that public-access wording to every educational or governmental use. The constitutionality and application of that permission, channel forum status, operator state action and whether the actual blanket exclusion was lawful remain undecided.

#### Part I — Local law cannot avoid the Convention's conditions within its covered field

**Operative rule.** When a passenger's personal-injury occurrence falls within Article 17's temporal and substantive field, Article 24 subjects an action against the carrier, whatever its legal foundation, to the Convention's conditions and limits. Failure to satisfy the accident or bodily-injury condition does not itself remove the occurrence from that field or permit an independent local damages action; occurrences outside that field remain outside this holding.

**Authority.** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court has the eight direct joins identified in Opinion Topology and controls this rule and its application.

Saks distinguishes the injury from the external event causing it. Floyd enforces Article 17's bodily-injury condition while leaving this exclusivity question open. Zicherman permits domestic law to identify compensable losses and beneficiaries subject to treaty conditions; it does not permit domestic law to create liability when those conditions fail. The absence of a uniform treaty damages code therefore does not establish a separate local source of recovery. Uniformity supports this reading but does not enlarge the treaty's field.

Tseng's search occurred in the operations of embarking for her international flight, not merely somewhere in an airport. The accepted absence of qualifying bodily injury defeats the reviewed personal-injury claim. The Court need not decide whether the search was an accident. It does not categorically exclude every physical manifestation of emotional harm, decide events outside embarking, carriage or disembarking, or alter the baggage award. Part I leaves Article 25's operation open.

#### Part II — The Article 25 argument does not change the result on this record

**Operative rule.** The asserted Article 25 route cannot alter the disposition where the trial's finding of no treaty willful misconduct remains undisturbed. Intentional touching alone does not establish that misconduct; this application decides neither contested preservation nor Article 25's full effect when its misconduct predicate is proved.

**Authority.** Part II has the seven direct Associate joins identified in Opinion Topology. Stone-Zsela does not join it.



SOURCE 30: city_of_west_covina_v_perkins_merits_1999-01-13.md
## Holdings
### 1. Notice of the seizure does not require an individualized guide to published return procedures

**Holding and operative rule:** Due process requires timely notice reasonably calculated to identify the seized property and its responsible custodian so that an owner may seek its return. When the owner receives that notice, due process does not additionally require personalized instructions describing adequate state return procedures reasonably ascertainable from generally available statutes and judicial decisions; publication alone does not establish meaningful access to an inadequate, concealed, misleading, obstructed or practically inaccessible remedy.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by the other members of the seven-Justice coalition of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. They adopt both holdings and the procedural-due-process explanation. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment and the negative rule rejecting the additional legal-instruction duty, but withhold assent to deriving notice duties for criminal warrant execution from civil procedural-due-process decisions. The seven complete joins independently establish controlling authority; no fractured-decision synthesis is necessary.

**Limits:** The holding does not validate an unlawful initial seizure, unreasonable retention, a failure to disclose the seizure or custodian, or an inaccessible return route. It adopts no comprehensive notice checklist, universal deadline or rule that a warrant satisfies every later process obligation. California Penal Code §1536's custody subject to court order, the nonstatutory return motion recognized in People v. Icenogle, and §1540's separate restoration conditions remain distinct; this case does not merge them or decide that every owner satisfies any one route.

### 2. The additional-notice judgment must be reversed without deciding an unproved obstruction claim

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's requirement of individualized instructions about published return procedures, and its direction to enter judgment for Perkins on that ground, must be reversed. The omitted warrant number does not sustain that judgment because the District Court found that respondents had not established the number was necessary to bring a return motion; the reported difficulties do not establish a different obstruction ground.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice opinion of the Court supplies the controlling explanation. Scalia and Thomas also support this application and its reservations through their concurrence in the judgment; all nine support reversal and remand.

**Remand limit:** The lower courts must reject the federal individualized-instruction theory. Any preserved adequacy, accessibility, obstruction or operation issue remains subject to ordinary procedural and substantive requirements; no new claim, fresh hearing or exploratory discovery is ordered.
