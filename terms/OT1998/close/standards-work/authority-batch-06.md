**Case and dockets:** Arizona Department of Revenue v. Blaze Construction Co.; No. 97-1536; OT1998.
**Event and date:** Merits decision; 1999-03-02.
**Result:** Reverse the state appellate tax-preemption judgment and remand, 9-0; unresolved credits remain for the state courts.
**Version / lineage:** Original decision.

## Holdings
#### Federal contracts on a reservation do not themselves exempt the contractor

**Operative rule.** A State may impose a nondiscriminatory gross-receipts tax whose legal incidence falls on an independent nonmember contractor performing ordinary federal construction contracts on a reservation, absent applicable constitutional immunity or a controlling federal exemption. Federal payment, supervision, reservation location and benefit to tribes do not themselves supply that exemption or require Bracker/Ramah interest balancing for the federal transaction.

**Authority.** Stevens's opinion has all nine Justices' direct joins in this rule, its application and the stated reservations.

**Controlling explanation.** New Mexico distinguishes the Federal Government from its independent contractors; economic reimbursement does not make the contractor the United States. California preserves that incidence distinction, and Chickasaw protects a different tax whose incidence actually falls on a tribe. Blaze's organization and ownership do not make it a member of the reservation tribes here.

Bracker and Ramah concern distinct federal and tribal arrangements, including tribal contracting and administration. They do not replace the independent-federal-contract rule merely because federally purchased work occurs on tribal land. Federal supervision and diminished purchasing power are not exemptions. Cotton Petroleum likewise rejects indirect economic burden and tax/service equivalence as substitutes for the governing legal inquiry. No finding about particular road miles or project-specific Arizona services is necessary.

The asserted transaction-period regulations do not change that result. Former 25 CFR 271.4(d) recognizes the tribe's option not to request a self-determination contract; subsection (e) restricts BIA sanctions for contracting or declining to contract. They regulate the Bureau's conduct, not all downstream state-tax incentives. The Buy Indian procurement preference likewise supplies neither a tax exemption nor universal tax equivalence between affiliated and nonaffiliated contractors.

An actual governing federal exemption remains effective; none of these asserted sources supplies one. Direct federal or tribal taxes, genuinely incorporated federal instrumentalities, discriminatory taxation and contracts with tribes remain distinct. The unresolved credit claims affect the amount payable, not the categorical exemption the state court adopted.

## Precedent Treatment
- **United States v. New Mexico, 455 U.S. 720:** Applied; ordinary independent federal contractors do not share federal tax immunity merely because federal money pays the tax's economic burden.
- **United States v. California:** Applied to distinguish legal incidence from reimbursement and economic effect.
- **Chickasaw Nation v. Oklahoma Tax Commission:** Preserved for actual tribal incidence; its reserved nonmember questions do not create an exemption here.
- **White Mountain Apache Tribe v. Bracker, 448 U.S. 136:** Distinguished from this ordinary federal contract; its particular tribal timber-operation holding remains.
- **Ramah Navajo School Board v. Bureau of Revenue, 458 U.S. 832:** Distinguished as a tribally controlled undertaking; its holding is not extended to every federal reservation supplier.
- **Cotton Petroleum Corp. v. New Mexico, 490 U.S. 163:** Applied to reject indirect economic effect or tax/service equivalence as sufficient grounds for immunity.

## Law After Decision
Ordinary federal construction contracts do not acquire a tax exemption from reservation location. The legal taxpayer and any actual federal exemption control this transaction. Tribal-incidence immunity and the distinct law governing tribal contracts remain intact. The amount of Blaze's liability awaits resolution of preserved credits.

## Judgment & Remedy
**Reversed and remanded, 9-0.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The State may apply the nondiscriminatory gross-receipts tax to these federal contracts. The state courts must address Blaze's unresolved claims for credits for taxes paid by subcontractors and suppliers. The Court fixes no net assessment and restores no excluded tribal-contract receipts.

**Case and dockets:** Holloway v. United States; No. 97-7164; OT1998.
**Event and date:** Merits decision; 1999-03-02.
**Result:** Affirm the Second Circuit's judgment against the conditional-intent challenge, 7-2.
**Version / lineage:** Original decision.

## Holdings
#### A settled intent to inflict serious harm if necessary for the taking satisfies section 2119

**Operative rule.** Section 2119 is satisfied when, at the moment the defendant demands or takes control of the vehicle, the defendant actually intends to kill or cause serious bodily harm if necessary to complete the taking. The Government must prove that defendant's purpose beyond reasonable doubt; intimidation alone, an empty threat, recklessness, an accomplice's purpose alone or an intent formed only afterward is insufficient.

**Authority.** Ginsburg's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Seven direct joins control.

**Controlling explanation.** A person may decide to inflict harm if a victim resists while hoping compliance will make that harm unnecessary. The condition does not erase the decision. Section 2119 requires actual intent concerning death or serious bodily harm, not a desire to harm a compliant victim regardless of whether violence advances the taking.

The separate force-or-intimidation element confirms the limit. A bluff may intimidate without establishing a real purpose to inflict the specified harm. Bryan's offense-specific treatment of culpability and Bailey's insistence on enacted elements reinforce the need to prove this mental element rather than replace it with dangerousness. Traditional conditional-purpose usage corroborates the reading; the Court adopts no general Model Penal Code formula.

Congress's addition of intent therefore does real work. The Court neither treats that amendment as a drafting mistake nor permits an automatic inference from possession of a gun. Holloway's actual charge and recharge required his own serious-harm purpose if resistance made violence necessary, separately on the relevant counts and beyond reasonable doubt. They satisfy the statute against the presented objection.

The statute adds no requirement that resistance be objectively probable or that the defendant hope, believe or know it will occur. Such evidence may bear on whether the asserted purpose was real, but it is not another element. That conclusion answers the statutory argument; the Court makes no ruling on a separate instruction request whose existence and preservation are not established here. Unrelated trial errors and broader congressional-power questions remain outside review.

## Precedent Treatment
- **Bryan v. United States:** Applied for offense-specific examination of the required mental state; no universal formula for every intent or willfulness provision is adopted.
- **Bailey v. United States / Robinson v. United States:** Applied to preserve the statutory element itself rather than substitute a broader practical or dangerousness connection.
- **Griffin / Langley:** Its distinction between actual enacted elements and proposed additional elements is preserved; this decision adds no probability-of-resistance element.
- **In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358:** Applied to require proof beyond reasonable doubt of this defendant's actual intent on the pertinent counts.

## Law After Decision
Actual present conditional intent satisfies section 2119's serious-harm element. The decision preserves the separate intimidation element, individual culpability, timing and burden of proof. It creates no universal rule about conditional intent in every crime, no gun-based presumption and no new probability element.

## Judgment & Remedy
**Affirmed, 7-2.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Justices Scalia and Thomas.

The reviewed carjacking judgments and dependent firearm judgments stand. The Court orders no retrial or other further proceeding and does not reopen the independent chop-shop convictions or unrelated challenges.

**Case and dockets:** Peguero v. United States; No. 97-9217; OT1998.
**Event and date:** Merits decision; 1999-03-02.
**Result:** Reach the issued-certificate appeal and affirm denial of section 2255 relief, 9-0; eight join the certificate rationale and all nine join the causal-loss holding.
**Version / lineage:** Original decision.

## Holdings
#### The issued certificate supplied the appellate authorization in this case

**Operative rule.** An authorized judge's actually issued, issue-specific certificate admits the identified claim to appeal; the asserted error in that judge's assessment of section 2253(c)(2)'s constitutional-showing condition does not nullify the authorization for appellate-jurisdiction purposes here. The statutory showing remains a condition for proper issuance, and this decision neither finds it satisfied nor authorizes future certificates for bare rules claims without it.

**Authority.** Scalia's Part I has all eight Associate Justices' direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees with reaching the appeal without joining this rationale.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 2253(c)(1) requires a judicial certificate before an appeal may be taken. A certificate was issued and identified this Rule 32 claim. Subsection (c)(2) directs the judge's eligibility assessment; an alleged mistake in that assessment does not mean the judicial permission never existed. Section 1254(1) therefore supplies review of the resulting appellate case.

Hohn preserves certificate conditions while distinguishing the judicial proceeding and the appeal. It does not turn every dispute over an issued certificate into an absence of appellate power. The Government's earlier concurrence cannot create jurisdiction, and the Court does not rely on waiver of a jurisdictional objection. It resolves the statutory distinction before reaching the merits.

The rejected ineffective-assistance claim is not recharacterized to supply a constitutional issue. The Court also leaves aside how a timely direct challenge should correct an issued certificate in a different procedural setting. This entire motion and certificate process followed AEDPA; Hohn's separate temporal reservation does no work here.

#### A mandatory warning error does not restore an appeal that the omission did not cause the defendant to lose

**Operative rule.** The sentencing judge must give Rule 32's required advice despite a defendant's independent knowledge. But where actual knowledge and an unchallenged voluntary decision not to appeal establish no lost appeal attributable to the omission, that violation alone does not require section 2255 relief; causal loss is distinct from whether an appeal would probably succeed.

**Authority.** Scalia's Part II has all nine Justices' direct joins.

**Controlling explanation.** Hill and Timmreck distinguish a consequential collateral defect from formal noncompliance with a criminal rule. They do not excuse the judge's duty. They require the requested collateral remedy to address the injury for which relief is sought.

Peguero already knew of the appeal right. The unappealed findings establish that he chose cooperation and possible sentence reduction rather than appeal. Repeating the required advice would not supply the opportunity whose loss he asserts. A per se restoration rule would enforce the warning duty symbolically even though this omission caused no appeal loss.

Rodriquez protects a person actually deprived of an appeal without demanding proof that the appeal would succeed. The Court preserves that distinction: causal deprivation is not a meritorious-issue requirement. It does not adopt a universal clear-and-convincing burden for disputed knowledge, a presumption that cooperation waives appeal, or a procedure for future contested cases. No appeal is restored here, so the possible scope of a restored appeal remains undecided.

## Precedent Treatment
- **Hohn v. United States:** Applied to distinguish the judicial certificate process and resulting appellate case while preserving statutory issuance conditions; its different temporal reservation is not extended here.
- **Hill v. United States, 368 U.S. 424; United States v. Timmreck, 441 U.S. 780:** Applied to distinguish a consequential collateral injury from formal rule error, without excusing the underlying judicial duty.
- **Rodriquez v. United States, 395 U.S. 327:** Preserved for actual deprivation of an appeal without a likely-success showing; it does not establish causal deprivation on these contrary findings.
- **Bousley v. United States:** Its separation of statutory meaning, constitutional defect and collateral remedy remains; this decision supplies no new constitutional claim or default exception.

## Law After Decision
Proper certificate issuance still requires the statutory constitutional showing, but the disputed eligibility assessment does not erase the actual authorization here. Rule 32's judicial warning duty remains mandatory. Section 2255 supplies no automatic restoration for an omission that caused no appeal loss; actual loss and appellate success remain separate inquiries.

**Affirmed, 9-0.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The sentence remains in force. No appeal is restored, no resentencing is ordered and no new factual hearing is required. The rejected counsel claim remains outside this review.

**Case and dockets:** American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan; No. 97-2000; OT1998.
**Event and date:** Merits decision; 1999-03-03.
**Result:** Reverse the private-carrier attribution ruling, 7-2 on reaching that ground; unanimously set aside the categorical advance-payment/severance requirement on divided grounds; remand with unchallenged procedures and distinct preserved claims intact.
**Version / lineage:** Original decision; the supplied brief's No. 97-1620 is clerically corrected to No. 97-2000 for this same case.

## Holdings
#### Statutory permission does not itself make the private carrier's payment choice governmental

**Operative rule.** These private carriers' independent decisions to dispute treatment and withhold payment pending utilization review are not state action merely because state law permits that course, requires compensation coverage and supplies review machinery. Attribution requires an actual governmental basis for responsibility for the challenged choice, such as compulsion, significant encouragement, joint participation or a function exclusively reserved to government; those predicates are not established for this initial withholding.

**Authority.** Kennedy's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Seven direct joins control.

**Controlling explanation.** Blum and Rendell-Baker distinguish regulation and public support from responsibility for a particular private decision. Lugar requires both the relevant state-created framework and sufficient state involvement; invoking the first does not dispense with the second. Bureau screening for proper invocation and assignment of a reviewer do not substantively approve the carrier's medical objection or order its initial suspension. Later adjudication is a different act.

Edmonson involved private participation in constituting a governmental jury; workers' compensation insurers do not perform that exclusively governmental function. Lebron prevents government from escaping its own constitutional obligations through an institution it creates and permanently controls, but these carriers do not satisfy that institutional-status ground. Mandatory coverage and a statutory source for compensation do not establish the required connection to their particular withholding decision.

This conclusion does not immunize the legislature's own acts or every decision in the public review process. Denver Area requires attention to the actual challenged governmental act, and Tulsa likewise does not dispense with significant governmental involvement merely because a statutory procedure is not self-executing. Actual public obligors, official review decisions or a distinct proved attribution basis require their own analysis. The Court decides only the private-carrier theory supported here.

#### General eligibility does not establish an unconditional right to advance payment of every disputed service

**Operative rule.** General eligibility for workers' compensation does not by itself establish an unconditional constitutional right to constant payment of every disputed medical service before its reasonable-and-necessary character is established. This rule neither extinguishes a protected claim to fair adjudication nor decides against a claimant an existing service-specific entitlement, operative prior award or legally sufficient individual expectation of continued payment merely because an insurer invokes review.

**Authority.** Kennedy's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight direct joins control. Stevens supports the payment disposition on a different ground.

**Controlling explanation.** Pennsylvania's substantive promise concerns reasonable and necessary care. General program eligibility therefore does not make every contested invoice presently due without regard to that condition. The facial demand for constant advance payment cannot rest on that inference alone.

Logan protects a statutory claim from arbitrary destruction; it does not equate a right to fair determination with unconditional payment before the substantive condition is established. Loudermill likewise prevents government from defining substantive property away through procedure. The Court preserves that distinction rather than making an insurer's objection conclusive or requiring a new adjudication before every payment.

The allegations concerning earlier orders and ongoing treatment are not resolved by assuming the orders concern different services or that no worker has an existing entitlement. Individual reliance on prior payments also remains open. Nor does reimbursement and interest necessarily replace medical care lost during an interruption. Those limitations prevent the confined negative rule from becoming a universal no-property holding.

The categorical advance-payment/severance requirement is set aside only to this extent. The Court does not decide every procedure adequate under Mathews, impose new exhaustion or proof requirements, or adjudicate unchallenged notice/input. Distinct preserved public-party and individual claims return for ordinary resolution under their own predicates.

## Precedent Treatment
- **Blum v. Yaretsky, 457 U.S. 991; Rendell-Baker v. Kohn, 457 U.S. 830:** Applied to distinguish regulation or public support from governmental responsibility for the particular private act.
- **Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co., 457 U.S. 922:** Applied; a state-created procedure and sufficient governmental participation remain separate requirements.
- **Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614:** Distinguished for actual participation in the governmental jury function; its attribution inquiry remains effective.
- **Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.:** Preserved for governmental institutional creation and permanent control and for the proper scope of an included question; it supplies no finding of such carrier status here.
- **Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium:** Preserved at its actual component holdings; it creates neither universal immunity for permissive legislation nor automatic attribution of every permitted private choice.
- **Tulsa Professional Collection Services v. Pope, 485 U.S. 478:** Preserved; a non-self-executing procedure does not eliminate the need to identify significant governmental involvement.
- **Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co., 455 U.S. 422; Cleveland Board of Education v. Loudermill, 470 U.S. 532:** Preserved for protected claims and the distinction between substantive entitlement and procedure; neither establishes unconditional payment of every disputed bill.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319:** Its procedural inquiry is not replaced or applied to decide every preserved individual or public-party claim.

## Law After Decision
Private initial withholding requires its own governmental attribution basis. General benefit eligibility alone supplies no unconditional advance-payment right. Actual governmental conduct, fair adjudication of protected claims, prior service entitlements and properly supported continuation interests remain distinct. The decision establishes no universal immunity or absence of property and designs no additional statewide procedure.

## Holdings
#### Necessary nonphysician school-health services are covered despite continuity and cost

**Operative rule.** IDEA requires qualified nonphysician school-health services necessary during the school day for an eligible child to attend and benefit from special education, even when the required availability is continuous and the service is costly. The medical-services exclusion covers services requiring a physician, except diagnosis and evaluation; educational necessity and lawful qualifications remain required, and care appropriately furnished outside school hours need not be supplied at school.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct joins control the service rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation.** Tatro reads the same medical-services term consistently in its inclusion of diagnosis and evaluation and its exclusion of other physician services. The regulations separately include school-health services furnished by qualified nurses or other qualified people. The district's objection that the regulation defines only included physician services does not undo Tatro's statutory construction or the express nursing category.

Supportive services need not themselves teach a lesson. Garret can attend and benefit from his educational program if the necessary routine and emergency assistance is available; none of the disputed tasks requires a physician. Constant availability is not constant administration of each procedure, but coverage does not depend on understating the actual work or cost.

Tatro's account of why Congress could exclude costly physician care does not enact an additional local affordability, complexity, continuity or existing-staff test for covered nursing. The Anderson letter reports conflicting judicial decisions and reserves individual determination to the IEP team; it does not adopt every court's multifactor exclusion. Auer requires the responsible agency's fair, considered interpretation consistent with the text, not deference based on an amicus signature alone. The actual agency treatment retains individual necessity and the outside-school-hours qualification.

The rule governs Garret's IEP while its qualifying conditions remain satisfied. It does not require experimental treatment, services unrelated to educational access or physician care outside diagnosis/evaluation. Nor does it select a provider, override licensing rules or fix additional reimbursement. The independent Iowa-law ground is unnecessary to this federal holding.

#### The existing statutory service obligation supplies clear funding notice

**Operative rule.** The express IDEA related-services duty, the qualified school-health regulation and Tatro's established construction provide clear notice of the covered nonphysician service obligation at issue here. Continuous or substantial need does not by itself make that specified condition an unannounced funding obligation.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six direct joins control this independently stated answer to the Spending Clause objection. Stone-Zsela agrees in rejecting the objection without joining its rationale.

**Controlling explanation.** Pennhurst and New York preserve the requirement that funding conditions be stated clearly. Tatro and the school-health regulation preceded the school-year expenses at issue. They identify the service category and retain educational necessity, rather than promising all medically useful care under a general educational purpose.

Suter, Franklin, Blessing and Gebser address distinct rights, enforcement or remedial questions; they neither abolish clear notice nor create categorical immunity from specific service conditions. This case uses IDEA's existing individual service and review framework. It does not imply a new section 1983 right, import Title IX attribution rules or create a general program-adequacy injunction. The district's contrary reading depends on rejecting Tatro's construction; it does not establish that continuity itself negates an otherwise stated condition.

- **Florence County School District Four v. Carter:** Preserved for actual IDEA obligations and lawful equitable reimbursement; it supplies no automatic new monetary award.
- **Auer v. Robbins:** Applied to the actual fair, considered regulatory interpretation within the statutory text; litigation format alone is neither disqualifying nor sufficient.

- **Suter; Franklin; Blessing; Gebser:** Preserved at their separate rights, enforcement and remedy scopes; none substitutes a different statutory scheme's prerequisites for IDEA's service duty.
- **Board of Education v. Rowley, 458 U.S. 176:** Its due-weight treatment of administrative findings remains. The Court does not select a new burden test or disregard the ALJ's alternative finding under one.

Necessary qualified nonphysician school-health services remain within IDEA even when continuous and costly. No independent affordability or existing-staff exclusion is added. Eligibility, educational necessity, lawful staffing and the physician exception with its diagnostic/evaluation qualification remain operative. The service duty governs the IEP; the amount of any unadjudicated later reimbursement is not decided.

## Holdings

### 1. The statute leaves the extent of midterm bargaining to bounded Authority construction

**Operative rule.** The federal labor statute does not categorically forbid union-initiated bargaining during an existing agreement, nor does it unambiguously require one comprehensive midterm bargaining regime. The Authority may determine the duty's timing and extent through a reasonable, explained construction, subject to contract coverage, lawful waiver, negotiable-subject restrictions, management rights and federal-law consistency.

**Authority.** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies a direct six-Justice majority for this holding and the holding below.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court must identify the authority Congress granted before deferring to an agency. *MCI* preserves statutory boundaries; *NationsBank* permits reasoned administrative choice where the enacted scheme leaves genuine discretion. Here the definition of a collective bargaining agreement is not expressly limited to the principal term contract. An agreement reached during its term can also fit that definition. Conversely, §7114's direction to negotiate toward an agreement and its reasonable-times and frequency requirements do not unmistakably prescribe a comprehensive continuing duty.

Section 7106 principally allocates authority by subject. Its management-right exceptions do not establish a prohibition on every other midterm proposal. The agency-election condition in §7106(b)(1) is not an election condition in (b)(2) or (3). Section 7105 assigns the Authority responsibility for administering bargaining obligations within those limits. The efficiency instruction in §7101 and the conditional impasse powers in §7119 inform that administration; they do not prove perpetual negotiations or inevitable imposition of this clause. A prediction of disruption cannot replace the statutory inquiry, just as the desirability of responding to new workplace needs cannot establish an unlimited duty.

The Fourth Circuit's categorical prohibition therefore rests on an erroneous legal premise. The Authority must explain the lawful regime; the Court does not select a universal duty or universal exemption, create a new factor checklist, or authorize reopening matters an agreement actually settles. The Court does not determine the treatment of a hypothetical future proposal under the Authority's covered-matter or waiver rules.

### 2. Contractual negotiability requires reconsideration under the corrected legal framework

**Operative rule.** The absence of an independently compelled statutory entitlement does not itself make bargaining over a contractual midterm obligation unlawful. The Authority must reconsider the actual advance clause under the permissible statutory framework; its existing alternative rationale does not warrant an immediate direction to enforce this order because it remains tied to the premise that the general midterm regime was statutorily required.

**Controlling explanation.** The Authority did give an independent reason: contractual proposals can be negotiable even when their substance is not a statutory entitlement. It also distinguished contract coverage from a categorical denial of midterm bargaining. Those are actual reasons, not an appellate lawyer's invention. But the current order continued to treat the clause as restating a statutory right, and its alternative explanation chiefly answered the circuit's contrary precedent. It did not fully exercise the distinct choice over the relationship among a general duty, an advance clause, contractual repose, waiver and the public impasse system once statutory compulsion was removed.

*NationsBank* requires reasoned exercise of genuine discretion, not an assumption that legal necessity supplies the agency's choice. *AT&T v. Iowa Utilities Board* separates administrative implementing power from the validity of particular duties and permits return under the correct legal allocation. *Boston & Maine* requires a discernible agency ground; that requirement is satisfied here, but it does not make every stated alternative sufficient after a governing premise changes. *Good Samaritan* permits considered interpretive change; the Court does not remand merely because the Authority changed its view. This is legal-framework reconsideration, not an evidentiary remand or punishment for inconsistency.

The Authority may adopt the same negotiability result if it explains a permissible construction. Sections 7103(a)(14)(C), excluding matters specifically provided for by federal statute, and 7117(a), preserving federal-law consistency, remain distinct limitations; neither is satisfied merely by statutory silence. The Court does not impose a clause, require agreement or concession, or adjudicate a Panel award. Agreed waiver, a duty to negotiate a waiver clause and compulsory acceptance at impasse remain separate questions; an employer's power to impose a comprehensive waiver is not presumed.

### I. The particular renewed method election defeats this gas objection

**Holding and operative rule:** A prisoner who affirmatively selects lethal gas when injection is available as the statutory alternative and default, and reaffirms that selection after an express opportunity to choose injection, cannot obtain relief on this gas-method objection when injection is not challenged in the present proceeding. ### II. General unavailability does not excuse the earlier omission of the gas claim

**Holding and operative rule:** A general claim that execution by gas causes unconstitutional pain and lingering death was reasonably available before Walter's direct review ended in 1987; neither a later favorable circuit ruling nor a later federal election prerequisite establishes cause for omitting that theory during state proceedings when gas was the only method. The injunction cannot rest on the appellate court's contrary general-unavailability rationale; this error independently warrants vacatur and lawful claim-specific reconsideration, but does not establish a final adequate, independent and unexcused bar to every gas-related claim or cause theory.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that unavailability of a claim is different from the prospect of losing it. *Reed v. Carrier* requires an objective external impediment in the ordinary case and distinguishes ordinary lawyer error from constitutionally ineffective assistance. The 1983 litigation in *Gray v. Thompson* requires the actual default predicates and exceptions. *Trest* keeps default nonjurisdictional; *Gray v. Netherland* requires separate treatment of distinct claims and their procedural histories. The March 2 state ruling is reported, but its citation does not establish all those predicates.

The possibility of another lawful ground does not sustain an injunction resting on this demonstrated error.
