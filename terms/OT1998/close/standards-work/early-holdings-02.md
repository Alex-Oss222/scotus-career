## Holdings

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Parts I-VIII, is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Those eight Justices directly adopt every holding and independently sufficient ground below. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgments without joining the opinion; no inference from his votes supplies additional rationale support. No fractured-decision analysis is necessary.

### I. Franklyn's retained machinegun falls within the transfer-and-stock mechanism

**Holding and operative rule:** The Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses permit section 922(o)'s possession restriction as a fitted means of preventing evasion or material impairment of the national post-1986 machinegun transfer-and-stock regime. Franklyn's possession of a completed automatic weapon within that regime does not require proof of an individual interstate journey or sale absent from the offense; his commerce challenge fails.

**Controlling explanation:** *Lopez* requires identification of the interstate mechanism before aggregate effects can do constitutional work. *Hale*, *Rybar*, *Kirk* and *Wright* supply that mechanism: exempting possession of prohibited stock would allow retention and circulation beyond transfer enforcement. The completed .45-caliber automatic weapon falls within the regulated class. The contrary objection correctly rejects general violence, productivity costs, dangerousness and remote manufacture as sufficient grounds, but does not answer this particular anti-evasion connection. The ammunition stipulation neither supplies constitutional power over the machinegun nor adds a jurisdictional element to section 922(o). No inference that every local firearm possession lies within federal power is adopted.

Section 922(o)(2) retains transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, a State, or a State's department, agency or political subdivision; it also retains lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986. Franklyn did not assert the preexisting-lawful-possession exception. The Court decides no new exception application or burden concerning it, and no Second Amendment issue.

### II. Gonzalez's ammunition conviction rests on a distinct supply-and-recipient connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(g)(1)'s restriction on a qualifying convicted recipient's acquisition and retention of firearms and ammunition is a fitted anti-evasion component of section 922(d)(1)'s convicted-person supply restriction, which forbids sale or other disposition of firearms or ammunition when the supplier knows or has reasonable cause to believe the recipient has been convicted of a crime punishable by imprisonment exceeding one year. That connection defeats Gonzalez's separate commerce objection; the stipulated interstate movement of the ammunition satisfies the enacted nexus here, but past movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism.

**Controlling explanation:** For the convicted-person branch at issue, section 922(d)(1) prohibits sale or other disposition when the supplier knows or has reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction. For the qualifying convicted-person class at issue, a corresponding possession restriction prevents the recipient from defeating that supply rule by acquiring and retaining the prohibited supply. *Hiley* establishes the method for the different domestic-violence-recipient structure; this is an application to the separately identified felon-supply provision, not a claim that Hiley already decided section 922(g)(1). The machinegun-stock rule of Part I does not automatically answer this count. The independent nexus remains part of the enacted offense, and no new conviction category, conviction-status finding or mental-state rule is supplied. Gonzalez's plea does not replace examination of his presented constitutional objection. No independent arms claim is decided.

### III. The actual ultimate discrimination finding sustains rejection of Franklyn's Batson claim

**Holding and operative rule:** After explanations are offered, the court must actually determine whether purposeful racial discrimination has been proved; facially neutral wording alone does not complete that inquiry. A supported ultimate credibility and purpose finding is reviewed for clear error, and the actual finding of no purposeful discrimination here supplies an independent ground to reject Franklyn's claim.

**Controlling explanation:** *Batson* preserves the initial inference, production of explanations and ultimate persuasion inquiry. *Hernandez* requires attention to the actual credibility finding and supports clear-error review. The Government used three of six peremptory strikes against three of four Black panel members. That pattern requires examination; it is not dismissed as irrelevant. The trial judge heard explanations and separately found no discriminatory intent, and the appellate court examined that determination. No identified clear error warrants displacement of that finding on the supported record. The individual explanations are not reproduced in the published account, so the Court supplies neither invented reasons nor a comparative-juror analysis. *Trevino* and *McCollum* retain the ordinary staged burdens; *J.E.B.* confirms that a neutral label cannot defeat supported pretext, without converting this racial inquiry into a cause challenge. If the transcript established only facial neutrality, the missing ultimate finding could not be replaced by deference. The reported actual finding supports this disposition. Counsel's objection came after voir dire, dismissal of the challenged jurors and lunch recess, impairing contemporaneous correction. The alternative merits ground makes a universal objection deadline unnecessary; no categorical forfeiture rule is announced.

### IV. The related counsel claim fails on prejudice, not on lateness alone

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming without deciding that counsel performed deficiently by objecting late, Franklyn has not established a reasonable probability of a different result from a timely objection because the supported ultimate Batson determination defeats the underlying challenge. Untimeliness itself neither proves absence of prejudice nor excuses an inadequate discrimination inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** *Strickland* requires both deficient performance and prejudice, and a court may resolve the latter without deciding the former. The decisive ground is the actual merits finding discussed in Part III. An earlier objection would not probably have produced relief from the challenged strikes on that supported determination. The argument that lost contemporaneous correction necessarily establishes prejudice would bypass the required inquiry into the underlying claim. Conversely, merely accepting facial neutrality could not support this conclusion. No general approval of counsel's delayed objection or new limitation on effective assistance is adopted.

### V. Supported assistance and joint activity permit the machinegun base level

**Holding and operative rule:** For conduct during the offense of conviction, in preparing for it, or in attempting to avoid detection or responsibility for it, section 1B1.3(a)(1)(A) permits attribution of supported acts aided or abetted by Gonzalez; subsection (B) separately requires another participant's conduct to fall within jointly undertaken criminal activity, further it and be reasonably foreseeable in connection with it. The supported findings satisfy both routes here, permitting level 20 under section 2K2.1(a)(4)(B) for this prohibited person's offense involving the machinegun described in 26 U.S.C. section 5845(a), rather than proposed level 14 under section 2K2.1(a)(6).

**Controlling explanation:** Gonzalez's matching loaded magazine and ammunition and the coordinated episode support the finding that he assisted Franklyn's machinegun possession. The court also found the relevant joint activity and its connection to the foreseeable weapon possession. The same coordinated episode supplies the required connection to the ammunition offense. Mere proximity or foreseeability divorced from agreed criminal scope and furtherance would not suffice. The findings were established by a preponderance and are not clearly erroneous. The base-level determination is application of the relevant-conduct guideline, distinct from the two later departures, although the lower opinion grouped them as three sentencing departures. Neither the prosecutor's recommendation, plea estimate nor presentence recommendation binds the judge to the lower level. *Koon* preserves independent legal review and deference to supported factual application; *Stinson* preserves consistent authoritative commentary without permitting it to amend the guideline. No new machinegun conviction for Gonzalez, automatic attribution to every ammunition offender or altered offense element follows.

### VI. Discharge supports the separate three-level departure

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 5K2.6 permits an increase where a weapon or dangerous instrumentality was used or possessed in committing the offense, with extent ordinarily depending on dangerousness, manner of use and danger to others; firearm discharge may warrant a substantial increase. Supported recent discharge in the attributed joint activity justifies three levels here, because the possession base level does not already account for that different conduct.

**Controlling explanation:** The gunshot report, bystanders' directions, spent shell jammed in the weapon and bore carbon support the discharge finding. Disputed testimony about the weapon's condition does not establish clear error. *Koon* requires a legally permissible factor, factual support and an independently reasonable extent; this encouraged factor addresses risk beyond the possession accounted for in the base level. The three-level increase is within that supported discretion. The double-counting argument fails because possession and discharge are distinct in this calculation, not because every weapon circumstance may always be counted twice. The judge also invoked a densely populated neighborhood's violent-crime history; the Court does not reach or approve that alternative. Acceptance credit then reduced level 23 by three to level 20. No acceptance-of-responsibility dispute is decided.

### VII. The presented criminal-history objections do not defeat category IV

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 4A1.3 permits departure when reliable information shows that the initial category inadequately reflects the seriousness of past criminal conduct or the likelihood of further crime; section 4A1.2 application note 8 permits consideration of stale convictions for similar or serious dissimilar conduct. The supported total-history assessment and explained selection of category IV defeat Gonzalez's claim that the move beyond category III mechanically counted only an ineligible old misdemeanor; no empty category-by-category recital is required when the reasons support the actual extent.

**Controlling explanation:** The two serious juvenile felonies, involving narcotics and a loaded weapon, could inform inadequacy despite their exclusion from baseline points and would support category III alongside the adult record. The judge's assessment of repeated offending and response to prior dispositions supplied the distinct total-history and recidivism basis for category IV. *Koon* permits supported contextual judgment but not an unexplained increase or invented replacement reasons. The principal objection misdescribes that assessment as arithmetic based solely on the third, stale misdemeanor. The Court rejects that objection and the demanded mechanical intermediate-category ritual.

The judge also mentioned education, employment and drug use. Those references are not erased or declared categorically permissible. Sections 5H1.2 and 5H1.5 make education, vocational skills and employment record ordinarily irrelevant to departure; their different potential uses in conditions of probation or supervised release do not authorize an upward prison-range departure. Section 5H1.4's distinct drug-dependence or abuse provisions are not an unconditional upward-departure authorization. The published appeal presents the mechanical-misdemeanor and extent objections, not a separate preserved challenge under those offender-characteristic provisions. This decision resolves the presented objections only. It makes no finding that an established invalid factor was harmless or that the same sentence necessarily would recur. A genuinely presented offender-characteristic challenge would require Koon's factor classification and, if invalid reliance were established, its separate whole-record and reasonable-extent remedy inquiry.

### VIII. Other-trial evidence and within-range selection remain limited sentencing decisions

**Holding and operative rule:** Reliable evidence from Franklyn's trial is not categorically barred at Gonzalez's sentencing merely because Gonzalez was neither a defendant nor represented there; the applicable preponderance burden, Rule 32 opportunity to address material factual assertions and independent evidentiary and Guidelines safeguards remain. The reported flight consideration explains selection of 63 months within the lawfully calculated 51–63-month range; it is not a newly imposed obstruction enhancement, and the presented objections supply no basis to disturb that sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** Gonzalez's categorical exclusion argument would make the source of otherwise reliable evidence dispositive. *Witte* and the limited sentencing principles preserved in *Watts* distinguish ordinary relevant-conduct consideration from a new conviction or separate disguised punishment, while retaining conviction-based authority and independent safeguards. No acquitted-conduct extension is necessary here. Neither decision makes another trial's assertions conclusively true, dispenses with reliable proof or eliminates the opportunity to contest material facts. No particular unreliability or denied opportunity requiring a new hearing is established on this record. The Court sustains the supported use of unacquitted joint-activity evidence. It also keeps the reported choice at the top of the range separate from the earlier base level and departures. It imposes no section 3C1.1 adjustment and announces no categorical flight rule or universal below-maximum sentencing safe harbor.



SOURCE 7: united_states_v_griffin_and_langley_merits_1998-10-29.md
## Holdings

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, is joined in full by all eight Associates. All nine Justices directly adopt each holding and its qualifications below; no judgment-only agreement or fractured-decision inference supplies authority.

### I. The supply restriction and recipient-possession rule supply the constitutional connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may regulate these qualifying convicted recipients' possession under section 922(g)(1) as a fitted counterpart to section 922(d)(1)'s convicted-person supply restriction, which forbids sale or other disposition of firearms or ammunition when the supplier knows or has reasonable cause to believe the recipient has been convicted of a crime punishable by imprisonment exceeding one year. Preventing the recipients' acquisition and retention from defeating that supply restriction supplies the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clause connection; a pistol's former interstate movement, burglary or generalized dangerousness alone does not supply it.

**Controlling explanation:** *Lopez* requires the actual interstate mechanism before aggregation. *Franklyn*, Part II, directly identifies the section 922(d)(1)/section 922(g)(1) relationship for firearms and ammunition: permitting the prohibited recipient class freely to acquire and retain the restricted supply would permit evasion or material impairment through redistribution and concealment. Each defendant's pistol possession falls within that relationship; no separate commercial transaction by either defendant is needed for this constitutional ground. *Hale*, *Rybar*, *Kirk* and *Wright* explain analogous necessary inclusion in the different machinegun-stock regime, but do not automatically decide every prohibited-person statute. *Hiley* likewise supplies its distinct recipient-class application, not universal approval of every status disability. The objection that remote manufacture cannot alone support federal power is correct but does not defeat the independent mechanism here. The restaurant burglary makes the prosecution concrete without creating a federal burglary power or an uncharged commercial element. The enacted nexus remains independently necessary. The supplier's knowledge or reasonable-cause condition is not imported as the complete recipient-offense culpability rule, and statutory conviction exclusions or relief provisions are not displaced. No arms claim is adjudicated.

### II. The requested direct-effect instruction would add an element

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(g)(1)'s retained nexus construction does not require contemporaneous interstate movement or an individually substantial or direct interstate effect from the defendant's possession. Because the regulated class has the independent constitutional connection stated in Part I, the court properly refused each defendant's requested additional direct-effect instruction; the prosecution still must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Controlling explanation:** *Bass* supplies the statutory requirement of a commerce connection, and *Scarborough* establishes that the retained nexus can be satisfied by the firearm's prior interstate movement. *Lopez* addresses congressional authority and does not silently add a per-defendant direct-impact requirement. Constitutional avoidance therefore supplies no reason to insert the requested condition: the actual supply-and-recipient mechanism resolves the constitutional objection without changing the statute. The jury decides the required offense facts, not whether Congress's national scheme is constitutionally sufficient. This holding rejects only the additional legal demand described in the lower opinion. It supplies no verbatim replacement charge, approves no unidentified wording in the instructions actually given and decides no different preserved instructional objection.

### III. The evidence independently supports the nexus for each pistol

**Holding and operative rule:** Viewed favorably to the verdict under *Jackson v. Virginia*, testimony that each pistol was manufactured in Connecticut, together with each defendant's possession of his pistol in North Carolina, permits a rational jury to find that firearm's prior interstate movement beyond a reasonable doubt. Neither a precise shipment date, carrier or seller nor a personal state-line crossing by the defendant is necessary to establish this nexus; both motions for acquittal were properly denied.

**Controlling explanation:** The expert's testimony concerned both weapons. Connecticut manufacture followed by North Carolina possession permits the interstate inference for Griffin's pistol and separately for Langley's. *Scarborough* identifies the statutory fact to be proved, while *Jackson* supplies the rational-jury sufficiency standard. Constitutional authority over the class does not itself prove that fact, and one pistol's history could not substitute for evidence about the other. The account supplies no disputed expert qualification, authentication failure or contrary-evidence defect; none is invented. Gonzalez's ammunition stipulation in *Franklyn* establishes no fact in these appeals. The absence of a detailed shipping record does not defeat this supported inference. The Court makes no additional credibility finding or ruling on possession knowledge, the prior-conviction element, conviction exclusions, weapon classification or sentence.



SOURCE 8: marquez_v_screen_actors_guild_merits_1998-11-03.md
## Holdings

### 1. Statutory membership wording does not alone breach fair representation

**Holding and operative rule:** A union does not breach its duty of fair representation merely by negotiating a union-security clause that uses section 8(a)(3)'s statutory membership terms without restating every judicial qualification. The wording remains subject to the governing limits on compulsory financial obligations and does not authorize compelled full membership, misleading notice, nonchargeable collections or unlawful enforcement.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court; all nine participating Justices adopt this rule, its explanation and its limits directly.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the clause must be read with the law governing it. General Motors limits enforceable membership to financial obligations rather than full formal membership. Beck limits an objecting nonmember's compulsory charges to collective bargaining, contract administration and grievance adjustment. Omitting those qualifications from the agreement does not enlarge the union's lawful authority.

Vaca supplies the separate grounds of arbitrariness, discrimination and bad faith; O'Neill applies them to negotiation as well as administration. Under the arbitrariness ground, a negotiated choice must fall outside the broad range of reasonableness assessed against the relevant facts and law. Using statutory terminology with an established legal meaning does not, by itself, cross that boundary or establish discriminatory treatment or dishonest purpose.

Marquez's strongest objection is that an employee may understand membership language to demand more than the law permits. That concern matters to actual notice and enforcement; it does not establish that using the statutory terms always breaches the duty. The Court does not presume that employees know every governing decision or hold that the clause itself adequately informed Marquez. Its wording may be relevant evidence of actual misconduct. A supported deception claim remains available, and both existing remand proceedings remain untouched.

### 2. A standalone statutory timing claim belongs initially to the Board

**Holding and operative rule:** A claim alleging only that a union's grace-period arrangement violates the NLRA belongs initially to the National Labor Relations Board; labeling the alleged statutory inconsistency a fair-representation breach does not create an independent judicial basis. A federal court may resolve a statutory issue necessary to a representation claim supported by operative facts independently showing arbitrariness, discrimination or bad faith.

**Authority:** The same Opinion of the Court; all nine participating Justices adopt this rule, its application and its limits directly.

**Controlling explanation:** Garmon assigns the independent unfair-labor-practice question to the Board in the first instance. Vaca preserves the judicial action for a breach of the union's duty of fair representation, and Beck permits a court deciding such a claim to resolve the labor-law question necessary to it. Those authorities distinguish a genuine representation claim from a statutory dispute carrying a different label.

Marquez's thirty-day theory, as presented here, contests compliance with the statute without an independently supported arbitrary, discriminatory or bad-faith feature of representation on that theory. The Court therefore affirms the refusal to decide that statutory question. The other enforcement and notice claims already pending below do not automatically transfer every collateral statutory issue to a federal court.

This conclusion neither makes intent indispensable to every representation breach nor bars judicial statutory construction whenever a labor-law issue appears. If a statutory question is necessary to an independently supported representation claim, Vaca and Beck continue to govern at that scope. The Court does not decide whether the thirty-day period restarts after a break, whether prior employment counts, or whether SAG's calculation was lawful.



SOURCE 9: naacp_los_angeles_branch_v_jones_merits_1998-11-05.md
## Holdings

### Actual Article III standing before contested constitutional merits

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III standing to decide these contested constitutional claims about judicial-election financing and optional candidate statements. It must determine, with support appropriate to the litigation stage, whether the plaintiffs have a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury fairly traceable to the challenged conduct and likely to be redressed by the prospective relief they seek; the Ninth Circuit's merits judgment is vacated so that determination can be made on the actual pleadings and appropriate record.

**Authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices adopt this rule, its explanation and the remand limits below; it controls directly without any fractured-opinion inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the disputed jurisdictional question must be answered before these constitutional merits. *Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.* forbids deciding contested merits on assumed Article III power. *Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife* supplies personal injury, fair traceability and likely redress, with support suited to the litigation stage; *Bennett v. Spear* applies those requirements at pleading without demanding proof appropriate only at a later stage. The District Court found no injury in fact as to the county defendants, but the Ninth Circuit expressly assumed standing to reject the constitutional theories. An unsuccessful claim to free publication or public financing does not itself establish an absence of factual injury, just as a claimed constitutional entitlement does not itself establish standing.

The apparent weakness of a subsidy claim supplies no jurisdictional shortcut. *Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith*, which examined the actual burden of a different election subsidy, does not settle these plaintiffs' standing or this optional-statement charge. *Norton v. Mathews* and *Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech* retain the confined settings, preserved in *Steel Co.*, in which other controlling decisions had already disposed of the merits; no such controlling resolution of this distinct dispute is established. The Court prescribes no exhaustive order among all nonmerits questions.

The available account identifies a former candidate and prospective relief but does not establish every material allegation in the complaint. The Court therefore neither invents a future candidacy nor treats an allegation omitted from that account as absent from the complaint. This uncertainty supports remand for an actual determination, not a finding that standing exists or a direction to dismiss every plaintiff. Both constitutional merits questions remain open if jurisdiction is established.

**Remand limits and questions not reached:** The lower courts must determine standing on the actual pleadings and appropriate record before any merits ruling. The Court orders no particular evidentiary hearing, standing discovery, document production or amendment entitlement. It neither approves the optional charge nor requires campaign financing or free publication. No indigent-candidate claim is decided, and the unpreserved state-law claims are not revived. No finding of racial intent or viewpoint discrimination, new future-candidacy allegation, ballot-access ruling or rule for a compulsory filing fee is supplied. Merits arguments about the wealth-primary theory and optional printing costs are not alternative holdings.



SOURCE 10: coalition_for_economic_equity_v_wilson_merits_1998-11-10.md
## Holdings

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion controls Parts I, II and IV with the five votes of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part III has all nine Justices' support. The following four holdings carry those respective coalitions.

### I. Selective racial restructuring requires a political-process inquiry

**Rule:** A State cannot answer a supported claim that it selectively moved authority over otherwise lawful racial remedies to a more remote political process merely by calling the foreclosed measures preferences. On the district court's preliminary findings, California's removal of those measures to constitutional amendment, while ordinary preferences remain available through ordinary officials, supports confined protection against the resulting unequal political burden; it neither requires a preference nor validates an unconstitutional program.

**Controlling explanation:** Hunter addresses a special political hurdle for racial protection; Seattle applies that principle to selective relocation of decisionmaking over racial remedies even when the underlying measure exceeds federal requirements. Romer preserves that limited doctrine without promising favorable legislation at every governmental level. The district court found both lost opportunities and the special amendment hurdle. Equal words about all races do not dispose of how that allocation operates. Crawford permits withdrawal of a voluntary policy, but does not eliminate Seattle's distinct inquiry into selectively changing who may decide racial questions. The State can enforce the constitutional limits on each proposed program; its interest in preventing unlawful classifications does not alone justify excluding measures that satisfy those limits. Croson, Hopwood and Engineering Contractors continue to require justification and fit. The Court decides the basis for limited preliminary relief, not the permanent validity of every provision or any particular program, and finds no need to attribute animus to voters.

### II. The sex theory requires a distinct inquiry

**Rule:** The racial political-process rule does not by itself resolve a challenge to removing authority over sex-conscious measures. The lower court must examine the asserted sex-based barrier on its own terms, applying the important-objective and substantial-relationship standard to an actual sex classification without presuming that every statewide policy withdrawal creates such a classification.

**Controlling explanation:** Virginia and Engineering Contractors require a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and a sex classification; they do not treat a stereotype as justification. Hunter and Seattle concern racial political structure and do not automatically decide every sex-conscious policy. The appellate categorical treatment omitted the separate inquiry. Section 31(c) preserves bona fide sex qualifications reasonably necessary to normal governmental operations in the three covered fields; it is neither a general permission for sex preferences nor proof that the initiative rests on a stereotype. The Court remands the distinct theory without finding all sex-neutrality rules invalid or extending racial strict scrutiny to them.

### III. Federal permission does not establish blanket preemption

**Rule:** Title VII's permission for defined voluntary affirmative action does not alone preempt a State's withdrawal of that policy or guarantee every subordinate governmental entity power to adopt it. An actual conflict with a federal statutory duty or binding federal remedy remains governed by federal supremacy and the applicable savings provisions; no such conflict is decided categorically for every operation of this initiative.

**Controlling explanation:** Weber and Johnson recognize lawful voluntary measures; they do not make every such measure compulsory. Section 703(j) prevents treating numerical imbalance in employment, union membership or related categories, measured against community, geographic or available-workforce figures, as itself requiring preferential treatment. It does not excuse actual unlawful discrimination. Section 708 separately preserves state duties except where state law requires or permits an unlawful employment practice. Section 1104 disclaims occupation of the field and preserves state law unless inconsistent with a provision or purpose of the Civil Rights Act. Those provisions cannot be collapsed into an unlimited exception. Proposition 209 protects existing orders, necessary federal-program eligibility and maximum lawful implementation on its actual terms. The district court's broad statutory theory therefore cannot sustain its entire injunction. Concrete federal conflicts and any distinct question of deference to administrative guidelines remain undecided.

### IV. Preliminary relief must correspond to the supported injury

**Rule:** Interim relief must protect against the demonstrated threatened loss of access to otherwise available lawful racial remedies, while leaving independently lawful provisions operative to the extent severable under state law. The lower court must conform its order to that constitutional injury and the ordinary preliminary-relief requirements, separately resolve the sex theory and concrete federal conflicts, and avoid turning interim protection into a permanent entitlement to a program.

**Controlling explanation:** The district findings supply a present burden on ordinary political access for preliminary consideration, not merely disagreement with an election result. They do not resolve the lawfulness of every affected program, the exact threatened injury defining an injunction's scope, or all remaining equitable prerequisites. The original order extended to implementation insofar as the initiative prohibited or affected affirmative-action programs throughout employment, education and contracting. That formulation may reach beyond the supported injury and does not justify automatic restoration of every part of the order. Section 31(h) requires maximum lawful implementation and severance of an invalid portion. The distinct ban on discrimination, genuine operational qualifications and other independently valid applications must be respected. The Court vacates the appellate judgment that categorically foreclosed the constitutional claim and returns the matter for a fitted order and further proceedings. No injunction validates a program that fails Croson, Hopwood or Engineering Contractors.

**Operative statutory limits:** Section 31 bars governmental discrimination and preferential treatment based on race, sex, color, ethnicity or national origin in public employment, education and contracting. It applies only to action after its effective date. Its sex exception is limited to bona fide qualifications reasonably necessary to normal public employment, education or contracting. It does not invalidate an order or consent decree already in force on the effective date; this protection is not confined to federal decrees and does not automatically extend to every later decree. It preserves action necessary to establish or maintain federal-program eligibility when ineligibility would cost the State federal funds, rather than every federally permitted action. Its governmental coverage includes the State and its local governments, public university systems including the University of California, community-college and school districts, special districts and other political subdivisions or instrumentalities. Remedies are the same regardless of the injured person's race, sex, color, ethnicity or national origin and are those otherwise available under then-existing California antidiscrimination law. The section is self-executing, must operate to the maximum extent federal law and the Constitution permit, and severs an invalid provision from the remainder. These conditions do not themselves establish whether a particular program is lawful.
