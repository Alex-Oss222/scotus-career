

SOURCE 11: pfaff_v_wells_electronics_merits_1998-11-10.md
## Holdings

### Commercial offer and readiness for patenting

**Operative rule.** The on-sale bar in then-effective 35 U.S.C. § 102(b) applies when, before the date one year before the patent application in the United States, the actual claimed invention is both the subject of a commercial offer for sale in the United States and ready for patenting. Readiness may be shown by reduction to practice or by drawings or descriptions sufficiently specific to enable a person skilled in the art to practice the invention; a working prototype is therefore sufficient but not indispensable.

**Authority.** The opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine Justices, controls both conditions, their application and the following limits.

Both conditions must be proved. A substantial commercial commitment cannot make an incomplete conception ready for patenting, and later successful development cannot retroactively establish readiness before the critical date. Requiring enabling drawings or descriptions of the claimed invention gives that requirement an objective content. An open-ended judgment that development was merely substantially complete is not a substitute.

Here, the Texas Instruments purchase order establishes the commercial commitment, and the engineering drawings were complete enough to permit production before the critical date. The later manufacture of a physical embodiment does not postpone the bar. Pfaff's proposed prototype prerequisite would let the manufacturing schedule extend the period for exploiting an already enabling design.

Mere preliminary negotiations, proposals for future inventive research and incomplete conceptions do not meet the two conditions. Elizabeth v. Pavement Co. preserves the distinction between genuine controlled experimentation and ordinary commercial exploitation. The Court decides no separate experimental-use dispute and invalidates no unidentified claims beyond those under review.



SOURCE 12: merrill_lynch_v_kravitz_merits_1998-11-12.md
## Holdings

The following two holdings are supported by the opinion of the Court and all nine Justices. They resolve the knowing-deception theory and the summary-judgment record. They do not establish a general primary-fraud recklessness rule.

### A knowingly false execution undertaking can be primary securities fraud

**Operative rule.** A broker-dealer that accepts a customer order without price instructions while knowingly intending to disregard the best-execution undertaking it thereby represents can commit primary deception under § 10(b) and Rule 10b-5 when the representation is materially false and the other requirements of a private securities-fraud action are proved. Breach of care or fiduciary duty, inferior execution or the broker's status alone does not establish such fraud; the customer must establish legally sufficient reliance, causation and actual loss as well as the required deception and culpability.

First Jersey recognizes a broker's own knowing deception through implied transaction representations. That principle reaches a knowingly false execution undertaking, but First Jersey's proved facts and SEC-enforcement posture do not establish these private customers' reliance, injury or damages. Santa Fe Industries v. Green excludes fiduciary unfairness without deception, and Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder excludes negligence. The present theory respects both limits because it requires a false undertaking made with knowledge of its falsity, rather than a later failure to exercise due care.

The securities firms contend that customary execution at the national best bid and offer defined their undertaking and defeated culpability. Such custom is relevant to the actual representation and state of mind. It cannot conclusively immunize an undertaking that the firms understood to be false when made. The customers must prove the representation actually made, including material instructions or disclosed limits, and every other private-action element. A less favorable price creates no presumption of reliance or automatic damage award.

The evidence permits the knowing-deception theory to proceed. The Court therefore does not decide a general primary-liability recklessness standard or transfer Central Bank's separate affirmative-assistance standard to this direct claim.

### The disputed contemporary execution evidence precludes the categorical summary judgment

**Operative rule.** Execution at the national best bid and offer, or through the Small Order Execution System, does not by itself defeat a primary-fraud claim where evidence supports a genuine dispute that better terms were reasonably available and the firm knowingly accepted the order on a false undertaking. Reasonable availability is assessed under contemporary conditions, including order size, the security's trading characteristics, speed, clearing costs and the expense and difficulty of market access, as well as quoted price; later adoption of a rule or development of technology does not itself establish earlier duties, access or culpability.

The relevant question is the best terms reasonably available to the customer. A nominally better quotation need not improve the customer’s execution after practical costs and timing are considered. Conversely, a customary published benchmark is not conclusive when a firm knowingly ignores a feasible improvement inconsistent with its undertaking. Small-order execution through the Small Order Execution System is part of that practical inquiry; the record establishes no universal safe harbor, and not all orders used that system.

This reasoning applies separately to the asserted electronic quotations, crossed market orders and in-house limit orders. It mandates no particular venue, guarantees no best possible price and imposes no universal search of every quotation for every order. Later execution regulations cannot be imposed retroactively on these transactions. A later study of these markets may supply reliable retrospective evidence of contemporary feasibility; adopting a later rule is not itself proof of earlier conditions. Particular-trade evidence supports a real damages controversy, but does not prove injury in every trade, determine class certification or establish reliance, causation or recoverable amounts. The ordinary rules for resolving the remaining issues continue to apply.



SOURCE 13: wright_v_universal_maritime_service_corp_merits_1998-11-16.md
## Holdings

### Neither instrument clearly waives the statutory judicial forum

**Operative rule:** A union-negotiated surrender of an employee's judicial forum for an independent federal discrimination claim must be clear and unmistakable: the agreement must identify that statutory claim or an equivalent class of federal rights rather than merely submit contractual disputes. Neither this CBA nor the separate Seniority Plan does so, and their grievance provisions cannot support dismissal of Wright's ADA action for nonexhaustion; whether a sufficiently explicit collective waiver would be enforceable remains undecided.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the clarity rule, both instrument-specific applications and the bounded remand.

The employers emphasize that qualification for employment concerns a subject comprehensively covered by the CBA and that its grievance decisions are final. Clause 15(B)'s machinery addresses disputes within the collective relationship. Clause 15(F)'s employment coverage, together with its exclusion of matters outside the agreement, does not expressly incorporate the ADA or submit independent federal claims. Clause 17 prevents the contract from violating law; a legality safeguard is not an agreement to arbitrate every statutory cause of action. Mastrobuono requires reading the actual instrument together, not presuming the broadest imaginable submission.

The Seniority Plan is a separate instrument. Its coverage of disputes about its own terms, interpretation, application and implementing rules likewise identifies no surrender of this ADA forum. The same employment event may give rise to both contractual and statutory questions without merging their sources.

The Court therefore need not decide whether an explicit union-negotiated waiver would be enforceable, resolve general FAA coverage or find the union's alleged advice to sue established. Wright's qualifications, benefits history, discrimination claim and independent defenses remain for the proper court. No ADA violation or entitlement to recovery follows from removal of the grievance-exhaustion bar.



SOURCE 14: dodd_v_hood_river_county_merits_1998-11-19.md
## Holdings

### I. Reservation of the federal cause does not erase a necessary, fairly litigated state finding

**Operative rule:** Section 1738 gives the Oregon judicial determination its lawful state preclusive effect: reserving a federal takings cause does not exempt an identical factual issue actually and necessarily litigated with a full and fair opportunity by parties bound in proceedings Oregon recognizes. Those requirements establish preclusion of profitable timber use here, but not of the entire federal claim, every federal element, a precise residual valuation or the legal assessment of partial deprivation.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support.

Each Oregon condition is satisfied at the proper level of specificity. The existence of profitable timber use is the same factual issue relevant to total federal deprivation. The competing appraisal and forester evidence were presented, and the use finding was necessary to the final state no-taking determination. Available evidentiary procedures, including a hearing available on request and witness examination, together with actual presentation and judicial review, supplied a full and fair opportunity. The Dodds themselves were parties. The LUBA adjudication and ensuing judicial review are proceedings Oregon recognizes for this purpose. The reported failure to request the available hearing does not eliminate the opportunity; Kremer distinguishes adequacy from use of every available procedure.

The federal cause remains distinct. The state finding establishes retained profitable use, not the federal significance of every restriction or a particular dollar value. The Court therefore rejects complete preclusion and separately addresses the federal grounds. No rule makes every administrative proceeding fair or every reserved federal issue precluded.

### II. Profitable timber use defeats only the complete-deprivation category

**Operative rule:** The binding finding that this parcel retains profitable timber use defeats a Lucas claim that the restriction eliminated all economically beneficial use. It does not itself decide partial deprivation under Penn Central, and no background-property limitation need be found when the necessary complete loss has not been established.

**Authority:** Part II has the eight Associate Justices' direct support; Stone-Zsela does not join this federal merits determination.

### III. The independent contextual federal inquiry supports this summary judgment

**Operative rule:** A partial-taking claim requires consideration together of economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations and the character of governmental action; retained value, acquisition under an existing restriction or a legitimate regulatory objective alone is not dispositive. On this undisputed record, continued profitable timber use, the existing necessary-and-accessory dwelling condition, nonassuring County communications and the forest-management character of the restriction support rejection of the claim; the restriction also bears the required relationship to the specified forestry purposes under the received Agins inquiry, without a demanded conveyance triggering Dolan.

**Authority:** Part III has the eight Associate Justices' direct support, including its distinct contextual and purpose-relationship applications; Stone-Zsela does not join those merits grounds.

The regulation restricts forest use to serve actual forestry, fire-protection and chemical-application objectives. It neither occupies the parcel physically nor demands a property conveyance. Its actual relationship to those purposes satisfies the received Agins inquiry at the bounded scope preserved in Del Monte Dunes. Dolan's special nexus-and-proportionality burden for a demanded conveyance therefore does not govern.

The owners correctly distinguish their federal cause from the narrower Oregon finding and insist that retained use alone cannot answer their claim. The Court addresses the remaining legal restriction, actual communications, continuing use and governmental character independently. It neither fixes a percentage diminution nor calls an unknown loss trivial. The reported undisputed record supplies no material factual conflict requiring another trial under the governing standards. Del Monte Dunes protects a properly supported factual taking claim while retaining judicial sufficiency review; it does not guarantee a trial on a precluded fact or unsupported dispute. Personal residential plans receive ordinary property protection, and the result does not depend on labeling them noncommercial.

### IV. Later permission limits the claimed duration without curing a prior taking

**Operative rule:** The July 1, 1996 building authorization ends treatment of this dispute as an ongoing prohibition but does not itself defeat compensation for an earlier proved taking. Because the independently considered total- and partial-taking grounds establish no antecedent violation here, the actual-period claim supplies no compensation or basis for new factfinding.

**Authority:** Part IV has the eight Associate Justices' direct support; Stone-Zsela would reserve and remand the federal issues rather than join this final resolution.



SOURCE 15: wisconsin_right_to_life_v_paradise_merits_1998-11-24.md
## Holdings

### Credible personal injury and effective relief remain necessary

**Operative rule:** A speaker seeking pre-enforcement relief must establish concrete actual or imminent personal injury, fairly traceable to a defendant and likely to be redressed by the requested relief, with support appropriate to the litigation stage; a completed prosecution is unnecessary when an objectively credible threat burdens the speaker's intended expression. Self-censorship, a facial label or the existence of private enforcement provisions alone does not establish those requirements, and a threat from independent private parties must be connected to relief lawfully available against the defendants before the court.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice Opinion of the Court identified in Opinion Topology directly adopts this rule, its qualifications and the application requiring reconsideration; no aggregation of separate writings is needed.

The parties agree that WRTL's ordinary comparative guides and flyers do not require registration. No official has threatened WRTL or required it to register. Those facts weigh against the asserted official threat. The Board's Travis proceedings and private suits concern other speakers and cannot be treated as enforcement against WRTL. They nevertheless require consideration in assessing the actual claimed exposure; they are not erased by broad agreement about the governing speech principles. WRTL must identify its intended communications and the threatened burden.

Bennett requires a supported connection to the defendant's conduct; Pic-A-State permits meaningful partial relief without requiring removal of every independent barrier. Lee v. Harcleroad requires inquiry into an actually applicable duty and present burden. Together they require a defendant-specific assessment rather than either automatic standing or automatic defeat from the existence of an additional private impediment. The Court determines neither whether WRTL has standing nor whether persuasive effects on absent litigants could suffice in a materially different case.

### The actual facial claim must receive the correct jurisdictional inquiry

**Operative rule:** A court evaluating this facial invalidity and nonenforcement request may not dispose of the entire action solely by treating the requested relief as a command prescribing future state-court construction, or by treating predicted failure of an otherwise substantial constitutional claim as lack of jurisdiction. The Seventh Circuit must reconsider the actual injuries, defendants and available relief; contested merits and the District Court's abstention ruling remain for proper consideration only after the necessary Article III inquiry.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court directly adopts the rule, its application and the confined remand.

Steel Co. distinguishes jurisdiction from success on a substantial federal claim, and NAACP requires an actual standing determination before contested election-law merits. A prediction that a narrowing construction would defeat the claim is not that determination. Conversely, the facial form of the claim supplies no missing threatened injury. Baker preserves the limits on a judgment's reach: a declaration against officials does not automatically bind absent candidates or state judges. The Court does not adopt the suggestion that invalidation would itself end all private suits.

The official assurances and independent private enforcement remain substantial objections on remand. They must be assessed against the actual communications, defendants and relief, rather than an assumed demand for universal judicial supervision. Ordinary further proceedings may resolve that inquiry; no new claim, waiver, discovery, amendment or hearing entitlement is created. The Court expresses no view on Wisconsin's constitutional validity, detailed statutory exceptions or the ultimate propriety of Pullman abstention.



SOURCE 16: att_universal_card_services_v_rembert_merits_1998-12-01.md
## Holdings

### Card use communicates an intent to repay, not a warranty of solvency

**Operative rule:** When a cardholder obtains an advance by using the card, she communicates a present intention to repay the resulting debt, not a warranty of current solvency or an objectively reasonable prospect of repayment; on the actual-fraud theory presented, the creditor must prove by a preponderance that the professed contemporaneous intention was false and deceptive. Financial condition, losses, borrowing patterns and other circumstances may establish that state of mind, but inability to pay or unreasonable optimism alone cannot substitute for it, and the debtor's denial is not conclusive.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified in Opinion Topology directly adopts the rule, its evidentiary qualifications and its application.

AT&T correctly observes that a dishonest intention can be inferred from conduct. Repeated advances, severe financial distress and mounting gambling losses can be persuasive circumstantial evidence. The distinction is between using those facts to find actual deceit and making financial improbability a substitute for that finding. A debtor may honestly intend repayment through an irrational plan, and an assertion of hope may also be disbelieved on a supported record. Neither conclusion follows categorically from gambling or insolvency.

No confession, mandatory factor count, heightened burden or additional plan to file bankruptcy is required. Nor does repayment create a safe harbor for a deliberately false promise. The Court decides the presented actual-fraud theory, not every false-pretense or representation-only theory. Field's independent actual-and-justifiable-reliance requirements and the statutory causal connection remain necessary when otherwise material; resolving intent does not eliminate them.

### The record supports affirmance without a new fraud inquiry

**Operative rule:** A finding of fraudulent repayment intent cannot stand on an objective inability-to-pay substitute for the subjective inquiry; factual findings remain subject to clear-error review and the governing legal standard to independent review. Here the reported bankruptcy explanation rests on that erroneous substitute, while substantial actual repayments corroborate the intent to repay and no independent contrary contemporaneous-intent finding is identified, so the Sixth Circuit's dischargeability judgment concerning AT&T is affirmed without further factfinding.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this record-specific application and disposition.

That reasoning does not become a valid subjective finding merely because it uses the word intent. Nor does recognizing its defect authorize this Court to reassess demeanor or discard a supported independent credibility finding. The actual stated premise and reported record support the intermediate courts' rejection of nondischargeability under the ordinary standards of review.

Field requires application of the proper standard where a material issue remains unresolved. It does not require a new hearing whenever a trial court has applied an incorrect rule. Here the intermediate courts have already corrected the objective substitute against the existing repayment evidence, and no unresolved independent contrary intent finding requires another trial-level determination. A remand to seek a new ground for the creditor's failed proof is unwarranted. The Court does not decide whether AT&T actually or justifiably relied, whether a debt was obtained by another proved fraud, or what amount would be excluded from discharge if fraud had been established.



SOURCE 17: minnesota_v_carter_and_johns_merits_1998-12-01.md
## Holdings

### I. Each respondent has a personal interest in the shared domestic privacy

**Operative rule:** Invited occupants who use a resident's deliberately screened home for a private activity possess their own Fourth Amendment interest against police acquisition of interior information; brief duration, commercial purpose and illegality, separately or together, do not automatically defeat that interest. Carter and Johns each have that interest on the actual host-authorized joint occupancy here; permission merely to enter, another person's rights or a desire to conceal crime alone does not establish it.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The proposition controls despite their disagreement over the ultimate disposition. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join it.

The resident deliberately admitted Carter and Johns to the screened living interior, joined their private undertaking and allowed that use for approximately two and one-half hours. Their interest rests on that actual sharing, not her title alone. The State stresses the brief compensated transaction and absence of an established prior relationship or continuing control. Those facts bear on the relationship, but do not erase the personally shared private occupancy actually shown. Payment informs the arrangement without turning the domestic interior into a public business area. Criminal activity neither supplies the interest nor forfeits one otherwise established.

Neither respondent needs an accepted key finding for this application. Carter's disputed proffer is not adjudicated and is not attributed to Johns. The Court does not protect every casual visitor, unauthorized occupant, person permitted merely to enter, or activity plainly exposed to ordinary public observation. Recognition of personal privacy leaves the distinct question whether the officer's particular observation invaded it.

**Precedent treatment:** Rakas retains the personal-right requirement and exclusion of mere presence. Jones remains relevant to protected use without ownership. Olson's shared-home principle is applied beyond its overnight facts through Fields. Fields's relationship inquiry extends beyond Crawley's recurring use to the particular host-authorized sharing here; its independent-control branch and rejection of automatic protection for every visitor remain intact.

### II. Conditional suppression rules remain distinct from the observation question

**Operative rule:** If an antecedent intrusion is held unlawful in properly available further proceedings, a later warrant is an independent source only when both the decision to seek it and its issuance are genuinely independent of the illegality. A warrant's arrival does not retroactively authorize the earlier intrusion, and consent, attenuation, inevitable discovery and other asserted grounds require their own factual and procedural predicates; neither automatic exclusion nor automatic admission follows.

**Authority:** Part III of Breyer's opinion has all eight Associate Justices' direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional remedial rationale. These conditional rules govern the remand if their triggering conditions arise; they do not find that this observation was unlawful or that any particular later item must be excluded.

The stop, door opening, handgun discovery, arrests, apartment and vehicle warrants, physical evidence and statements require their actual personal-right and causal inquiries. The prosecutor's concession that an invalid arrest would bring down statements and physical evidence retains that condition. It does not concede that every defect in the observation automatically excludes every later item for both respondents. The ninety-minute surveillance gap is not erased.

Consent requires actual authority, scope, voluntariness and any applicable taint analysis. Leon affords no protection for knowing or reckless deception, abandoned judicial neutrality, an affidavit wholly lacking reasonable probable-cause indicia or a facially deficient warrant; the conduct of obtaining and supplying officers and proper execution scope matter. Evans remains confined to qualifying court-clerical error with objectively reasonable reliance, not a general exception for deliberate police investigation. Attenuation and inevitable discovery need their actual predicates. Ordinary preservation rules remain operative, and no new statement, Miranda or Franks claim is created.

The Court orders neither an evidence-by-evidence exclusion nor unconditional admission or reinstatement. The remaining claims may be resolved only to the extent properly available under ordinary procedure.

**Precedent treatment:** Fields and Murray's genuine-independence rule is applied without expansion. Karo's supported untainted-warrant possibility remains available. Pinson and Myers retain the distinction between antecedent error and final suppression. Leon and Evans retain their stated institutional, factual and procedural limits; Franks's false-affidavit inquiry does not substitute for independent source.

### III. The judgment does not establish a controlling search application

**Judgment without controlling search rationale:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer vacate the reviewed judgments to the extent they dispose of the federal suppression claims through the observation determination and remand for lawful further proceedings. O'Connor would require renewed actual-vantage analysis. The other four accept the judgment only on that point and would conclusively find no search on their qualified actual-view ground. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would affirm because they find a search on the supported minimum.

The five-Justice personal-interest rule and eight-Associate conditional remedial rules control their subjects. No majority decides whether this observation was a search. O'Connor's reservation and the four-Justice conclusive no-search rule are not combined under Marks: leaving the legal application open does not supply a logical subset of a rule conclusively resolving it. The five votes for the operative judgment do not create a fifth vote for no search or for O'Connor's rationale.

The mandate removes the adverse federal suppression judgments and permits ordinary lawful further proceedings. It neither declares the record insufficient nor holds that further actual-vantage analysis is necessary. If a later disposition depends on rejecting an appellate factual characterization, the governing factual-review and preservation objections must be addressed. If a constitutional answer instead rests on an express assumption of the disputed additions, it must explain why the conclusion follows on that assumption. These conditional source safeguards create no new hearing, photographic reconstruction, discovery, burden shift or factual finding. Neither an actual search nor actual nonsearch application is imposed as Court law.



SOURCE 18: equality_foundation_v_cincinnati_merits_1998-12-03.md
## Holdings

### The municipal class-specific foreclosure fails ordinary rational-basis review

**Operative rule:** Article XII's combined withdrawal of existing sexual-orientation nondiscrimination protection and prospective prohibition of ordinary municipal protection for the named class violates equal protection because its actual breadth bears no rational relationship to the asserted fiscal, associational, regulatory and local-policy interests. Municipal scope, continued general-law remedies and access to higher government do not cure that unexplained selective disability; the rule does not make every repeal unlawful or require the City to enact a particular protection.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice Opinion of the Court identified in Opinion Topology directly adopts the ordinary rationality rule, application and limits.

Local fiscal management, regulatory restraint, private association and durable policy are legitimate subjects. Yet Article XII reaches the City's own employment and ordinary protection across housing, public accommodations and private employment, regardless of a particular protection's expense or operation. Avoiding some complaint and defense costs does not explain why this class alone becomes prospectively ineligible for all that ordinary protection. Charter authority explains who sets policy and its durability, not the missing rational connection to the full selective burden.

Hurley protects actual private expression; it does not make impersonal services or the City's own conduct categorically expressive association. Those distinct concerns cannot justify this entire public and private reach. Bowers's specified-conduct holding likewise does not explain the broader disability based on orientation or status. No particular expression or religious exemption is adjudicated and Bowers is not overruled.

The City emphasizes that Ohio institutions and general laws remain accessible. Romer already assumed generally applicable protection remained. The relevant breadth is the civic activity excluded from ordinary protection, not simply the number of governmental levels affected. Ordinary repeal remains permissible; the reported concession about repeal is not assent to prospective charter foreclosure. Coalition's distinct racial political-process inquiry supplies neither a heightened orientation standard nor a universal entitlement to favorable legislation. This decision applies ordinary rationality to Article XII's own operation.

### Equitable protection must address the discriminatory barrier without writing municipal law

**Operative rule:** The appellate direction to enter judgment for defendants cannot stand, and declaratory and injunctive protection must be restored against Article XII's unconstitutional ordinary-protection foreclosure. The lower courts must conform the injunction to that common operation and any genuinely independent lawful remainder under applicable severability law, without rewriting the charter federally, imposing a nondiscrimination code or reopening the resolved rational-basis merits.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court directly adopts this remedial holding.

The remand concerns fitting the injunction to the decided constitutional defect and respecting any genuinely independent lawful operation under the applicable state rule. It is not permission to continue the invalid common foreclosure or to require a new rationality showing from the plaintiffs. No damages, new ordinance, mandatory preference or adjudicated religious exemption is supplied.

### The dependent fee vacatur loses its merits foundation

**Operative rule:** Because the constitutional merits judgment is reversed and protection against the charter barrier is restored, the dependent vacatur of the plaintiffs' fee award cannot stand on loss of merits success. Their prevailing-party basis on this constitutional claim is restored, while remaining lawful §1988 issues and review of the calculation return to the lower courts; no particular amount is established here.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court directly adopts the fee disposition and its limits.



SOURCE 19: knowles_v_iowa_merits_1998-12-08.md
## Holdings

### I. A citation does not trigger the full custodial-arrest search exception

**Operative rule:** Issuing a traffic citation without a custodial arrest does not authorize the full search incident to arrest; unexercised authority to make an arrest cannot substitute for the custody that triggers that exception. A full search requires an independently applicable recognized basis, and the citation, general roadside risk and observed speeding supply none for this search; supported exit orders, protective measures, consent searches and searches based on independent probable cause retain their own conditions and scope.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support. Part II's ordinary-remedial-law return has the eight Associates' support; Stone-Zsela would require the specific suppression stated in his concurrence.

The officer chose to issue the speeding citation before searching. Transport and station-custody risks had not arisen, and the observed speeding offense supplied no further evidence ordinarily to be preserved by a full automobile search. The Court does not invent a later arrest to validate the intrusion retrospectively. State permission cannot by itself establish Fourth Amendment reasonableness.

Iowa correctly emphasizes that danger can remain at roadside and that the officer had authority to arrest. Those considerations may support recognized limited precautions, not transfer the full arrest-search category to an encounter lacking its predicate. Whren preserves independent limits on an intrusion beyond the initial stop; Robinette distinguishes lawful traffic business from later detention or consent. Maryland v. Wilson's limited exit-direction authority is not automatic frisk or search authority. Ricci likewise distinguishes enforcement power from justification for an added restraint without deciding a general prohibition on arrest for this offense.

A Terry frisk requires supported reasonable suspicion that the person is armed and dangerous. A Michigan v. Long protective vehicle search requires its own reasonable belief, based on specific facts, that the person is dangerous and may gain immediate control of weapons, and remains limited to areas where a weapon may be hidden. Voluntary consent and the automobile exception retain their independent predicates. None supplies an established justification for this search. The Court neither assumes every stop safe nor imposes least restrictive means, and it leaves Robinson's rule intact for actual qualifying custody.

### II. The dependent judgment returns under the corrected constitutional rule

**Operative rule:** The citation-search theory cannot sustain this search or the dependent judgment, which is reversed and remanded for proceedings under the corrected constitutional rule and ordinary, properly available remedial law. No alternative justification is established here, and the Court orders no acquittal, release, replacement sentence, civil relief or universal exclusion rule for traffic encounters.

**Authority:** Part II has the eight Associate Justices' direct support. Stone-Zsela joins reversal and remand but would add the express suppression direction stated in his separate opinion.
