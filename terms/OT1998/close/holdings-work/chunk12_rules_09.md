The lower courts must address the supported present burden on ordinary political access to otherwise lawful racial measures while applying the ordinary requirements for preliminary relief and respecting severability. The exact scope of any restraint must follow the actual threatened injury; the Court does not order a particular injunction or automatically reinstate the prior order in its entirety. The discrimination prohibition, unchallenged exceptions and independently lawful operation remain effective to the extent state severability law permits. No particular preference is ordered or permanently validated, and the initiative is not repealed wholesale. The sex theory and any concrete conflict with federal duties require their own adjudication.

## Holdings

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion controls Parts I, II and IV with the five votes of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part III has all nine Justices' support. The following four holdings carry those respective coalitions.

### I. Selective racial restructuring requires a political-process inquiry

**Rule:** A State cannot answer a supported claim that it selectively moved authority over otherwise lawful racial remedies to a more remote political process merely by calling the foreclosed measures preferences. On the district court's preliminary findings, California's removal of those measures to constitutional amendment, while ordinary preferences remain available through ordinary officials, supports confined protection against the resulting unequal political burden; it neither requires a preference nor validates an unconstitutional program.



### II. The sex theory requires a distinct inquiry

**Rule:** The racial political-process rule does not by itself resolve a challenge to removing authority over sex-conscious measures. The lower court must examine the asserted sex-based barrier on its own terms, applying the important-objective and substantial-relationship standard to an actual sex classification without presuming that every statewide policy withdrawal creates such a classification.



### III. Federal permission does not establish blanket preemption

**Rule:** Title VII's permission for defined voluntary affirmative action does not alone preempt a State's withdrawal of that policy or guarantee every subordinate governmental entity power to adopt it. An actual conflict with a federal statutory duty or binding federal remedy remains governed by federal supremacy and the applicable savings provisions; no such conflict is decided categorically for every operation of this initiative.



### IV. Preliminary relief must correspond to the supported injury

**Rule:** Interim relief must protect against the demonstrated threatened loss of access to otherwise available lawful racial remedies, while leaving independently lawful provisions operative to the extent severable under state law. The lower court must conform its order to that constitutional injury and the ordinary preliminary-relief requirements, separately resolve the sex theory and concrete federal conflicts, and avoid turning interim protection into a permanent entitlement to a program.



**Operative statutory limits:** Section 31 bars governmental discrimination and preferential treatment based on race, sex, color, ethnicity or national origin in public employment, education and contracting. It applies only to action after its effective date. Its sex exception is limited to bona fide qualifications reasonably necessary to normal public employment, education or contracting. It does not invalidate an order or consent decree already in force on the effective date; this protection is not confined to federal decrees and does not automatically extend to every later decree. It preserves action necessary to establish or maintain federal-program eligibility when ineligibility would cost the State federal funds, rather than every federally permitted action. Its governmental coverage includes the State and its local governments, public university systems including the University of California, community-college and school districts, special districts and other political subdivisions or instrumentalities. Remedies are the same regardless of the injured person's race, sex, color, ethnicity or national origin and are those otherwise available under then-existing California antidiscrimination law. The section is self-executing, must operate to the maximum extent federal law and the Constitution permit, and severs an invalid provision from the remainder. These conditions do not themselves establish whether a particular program is lawful.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hunter v. Erickson is applied to the special political hurdle for racial protection; its force does not depend on a right to win a policy dispute.
- Washington v. Seattle School District is applied to selective relocation of racial remedial authority; a voluntary underlying policy is not outside its concern for that reason alone.
- Crawford v. Board of Education is distinguished at its policy-withdrawal scope, which remains effective without erasing Seattle's structural inquiry.
- Romer v. Evans is applied as preserving those racial precedents while rejecting a universal entitlement to favorable law at every governmental level.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co. remains the local racial-classification standard; the political-process ruling excuses no defective program.
- Hopwood v. Texas retains its supported educational-diversity justification and required fit; permission does not compel a program.
- Engineering Contractors retains local racial strict scrutiny and the distinct demanding intermediate scrutiny for sex classifications; its limits govern affected measures on remand.
- United States v. Virginia supplies the genuine-objective and substantial-relationship requirement for sex classifications, not an automatic racial political-process rule.
- Adarand Constructors v. Pena retains its distinct federal standard; that standard is not transferred to state or local programs.
- United Steelworkers v. Weber and Johnson v. Transportation Agency are applied as permission for defined voluntary measures, not a universal command or a complete preemption rule.

## Law After Decision

The racial political-process rule applies to a supported selective relocation of authority over otherwise lawful remedies despite a substantive preference label. It does not require a program, permanently invalidate every part of Proposition 209, or displace the existing constitutional standards for racial and sex classifications. The sex challenge remains distinct. Title VII permission alone supplies no blanket preemption; concrete federal duties retain their force. Preliminary relief must track the supported injury and preserve severable lawful operation.
