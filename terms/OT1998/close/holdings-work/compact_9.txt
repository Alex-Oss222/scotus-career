**Case and dockets:** Richardson v. United States; No. 97-8629.
HOLDINGS
### Unanimous agreement on the constituent violations

**Operative rule:** In a prosecution under 21 U.S.C. section 848, each particular violation necessary to compose the continuing series is an element that the jury must find unanimously beyond a reasonable doubt. Jurors must agree on the same qualifying violations, although they need not agree on every evidentiary detail or means of committing an agreed violation; an instruction expressly allowing disagreement about the constituent violations is erroneous.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, supplies six votes for this rule and its limits.


TREATMENT
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applied to jury determination of identified offense elements; it does not itself resolve the antecedent elements-versus-means question or make every instructional defect structural.
- **Rutledge v. United States:** Applied to the distinction between an agreement and the continuing series. Its duplicate-conviction rule remains intact but supplies no unpresented Count One remedy here.
- **Schad v. Arizona:** Distinguished because accepted alternative means for a traditional single offense do not establish that separate episodes composing this federal series are mere evidence. Its result and bounded elements/means distinction remain intact.
- **Griffin v. United States:** Its treatment of a factually unsupported lawful conspiracy object does not resolve the independent unanimity defect expressly reserved by that decision.
- **California v. Roy:** Its rejection of automatic reversal for the personal-intent omission and its qualified actual-verdict inquiry remain effective within their stated category; neither is extended here to decide reviewability of express predicate nonunanimity.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Its structural rule for a vitiated reasonable-doubt standard remains intact; this decision does not decide whether the present defect has the same consequence.
- **Johnson v. United States:** Its forfeited-error disposition does not replace the governing rules for this preserved objection.
- **Jones v. United States:** Its statutory identification of offense-defining facts remains consistent with the present construction; no universal constitutional maximum-fact rule is added here.
LAW
From June 1, 1999, section 848 requires unanimous agreement on the particular violations necessary to constitute the continuing series. Series-wide agreement alone cannot satisfy that requirement when the jurors are expressly permitted to rely on different violations. The distinction between offense elements and evidentiary means survives. No separately contested minimum number of violations, supervisee-identification rule or universal constitutional element test is decided.

Harmless-error availability and application remain unresolved for this defect. Sufficient evidence to convict is not itself an adjudication that the actual instructional error was harmless. If constitutional harmless-error review is available, its applicable beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden remains the Government's; the Court does not decide that the inquiry applies or that its showing succeeds or fails.
JUDGMENT
**Supporting vacatur and remand:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. **Opposing:** O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg, who would affirm against this unanimity objection.

The Seventh Circuit must consider in the first instance whether the preserved instructional error is subject to harmless-error review and, if so, whether it is harmless under the governing standard. The CCE conviction must be set aside unless it may lawfully be sustained in proceedings consistent with the predicate-unanimity rule. The Court decides neither harmlessness question and orders no automatic new trial, acquittal, release or fixed sentence. Ordinary double-jeopardy and retrial principles remain applicable. The separate conspiracy conviction receives no independent disposition here.

The patent owner may mine coal for domestic use on the land before federal disposal of the deposit. #### I. An available ordinary discretionary petition is part of one complete round

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the pre-AEDPA law governing this application, a prisoner must fairly present each federal claim through one complete round of the State's established ordinary appellate process, including a petition for discretionary review to its highest court when that petition is an ordinary available step. **Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, commands six direct votes.

**Holding and operative rule:** When a prisoner omits a claim from an otherwise required ordinary state-review step and the opportunity to use that step has expired, the claim is procedurally defaulted rather than awaiting exhaustion through a still-available remedy. Boerckel's three omitted claims fall within that rule, subject to recognized cause-and-prejudice and actual-innocence gateways at their existing scope; further proceedings must respect the actual prior gateway rulings and the governing pre-AEDPA law.

**Reservation and operative limit:** The Court does not decide whether a State may expressly identify an occasionally available discretionary procedure as outside its standard review process and state that prisoners need not invoke it before federal habeas. **Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. It is a five-Justice reservation, not an adopted rule resolving the hypothetical procedure's availability. Stone-Zsela does not join this reservation.

**Explanation:** Ordinary selective review and a State's express definition of its standard process present different questions. The former is established here; the latter is not. Treating actual state-law unavailability as controlling therefore does not require a decision about a procedure that a State retains for occasional use while expressly excluding it from the standard exhaustion route. The Court leaves that question open without displacing the complete-round rule for Illinois's existing ordinary procedure.

**Holding and operative rule:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.


### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding and operative rule:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.


### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding and operative rule:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.


### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.


### V. Initial harmlessness review of the affected fraud convictions

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. Scalia and Ginsburg join V-A only and would limit review to genuine equivalent findings. Stone-Zsela joins the statutory reversal but dissents from the remand for another harmlessness inquiry; he would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now.


TREATMENT
- **Morissette v. United States:** Applies the borrowed-term principle to fraud's established materiality requirement; statutory silence alone does not discard that content.
- **Durland v. United States:** Preserves coverage of fraudulent future promises while distinguishing that breadth from elimination of materiality or importation of every completed-tort requirement.
- **Kungys v. United States:** Uses its natural-tendency or capacity formulation for the financial deceptions presented here; its civil allocation and burden do not govern the criminal jury question.
- **United States v. Wells:** Preserves the different §1014 construction without an added objective-materiality element; it neither removes materiality from fraud statutes nor automatically cures their instructional error through a purpose-to-influence finding.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies jury determination of each actual materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt; harmlessness review does not authorize withdrawal of that element at trial.
- **Chapman v. California:** Applies the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden to establish that the preserved error did not contribute to the actual verdict.
- **California v. Roy:** Extends its qualified actual-verdict method to the materiality errors presented here; necessary equivalence is sufficient but not exclusive, and its instructional-focus limitation remains essential.
- **Yates v. Evatt:** Applies attention to the actual charge, findings, and evidence considered under the instructions; proof sufficient for a different verdict cannot replace contribution analysis.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Distinguishes removal of a particular element from invalidation of the reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings; its structural-error rule remains intact.
- **Johnson v. United States:** Distinguishes its discretionary plain-error disposition after forfeiture; it neither supplies a waiver here nor reduces Chapman's burden.
- **Jones v. United States:** Preserves its protection for charged statutory elements without treating it as a classification or harmlessness decision for this error.
- **Mitchell v. United States:** Applies its distinction between an error's actual contribution and sufficient other evidence without transferring its separate privilege holding.
- **Richardson v. United States:** Leaves intact its reservation of availability and application of harmless-error review for the distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect.
LAW
Effective June 10, 1999, materiality must be submitted for the charged mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud schemes; §7206(1)'s express materiality element and §1014's different Wells rule retain their respective force. For the materiality errors presented here, the Government may establish harmlessness through Roy's qualified actual-verdict inquiry, but uncontested or overwhelming evidence is not an automatic substitute for that showing. The tax verdicts meet the standard on their specific charge and record; the affected fraud verdicts require initial application below.

The six-Justice tax ground is noncontribution to the actual verdict, not a rule that the jury necessarily decided every omitted materiality question. Stevens's supplementary view and Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive equivalent-findings method do not control. The Chief accepts the governing broader method but disagrees with its application and the further fraud inquiry. The decision establishes no universal omitted-element classification, generic tax shortcut, new no-contest waiver, or automatic effect on a differently worded offense. Richardson's separate harmlessness reservation remains open.

FILE cunningham_v_hamilton_county_merits_1999-06-14.md
**Case and dockets:** Cunningham v. Hamilton County, No. 98-727.
HOLDINGS
**Complete separation from the merits.** A monetary Rule 37 discovery sanction against counsel during ongoing litigation is not immediately appealable under §1291's collateral-order doctrine merely because counsel's representation has ended. The category does not satisfy the requirement of an important question completely separate from the merits when review of discovery relevance, responses and compliance remains intertwined with the case's substantive issues and litigation record.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices. This ground independently requires dismissal of the interlocutory appeal.


**Effective review after final judgment.** Independently, this monetary discovery sanction remains effectively reviewable after final judgment: the attorney personally subject to it may challenge her own sanction even though she no longer represents the client. Ordinary payment, delay and professional or reputational burdens do not establish an important right that will be irretrievably lost without immediate review.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices. This ground independently defeats the collateral-order appeal.


TREATMENT
- Cohen retains the narrow collateral-order exception and its cumulative conditions.
- Coopers & Lybrand preserves those cumulative requirements; conclusiveness does not excuse a failure of complete separation or effective unreviewability.
- Digital Equipment supplies category-level discipline and preserves adequate final review despite irretrievable ordinary litigation costs; those principles apply to this monetary discovery sanction.
- Swint rejects convenience-based appellate jurisdiction and distinguishes ordinary liability from legal immunity.
- Behrens retains otherwise qualifying legal-immunity appeals, not immediate review of every personal monetary or professional burden.
LAW
The final-judgment rule governs this monetary Rule 37 sanction even after representation ends through disqualification. The attorney retains review of her own sanction after final judgment. Different statutory appeal provisions, extraordinary remedies and genuinely distinct finality categories retain their existing prerequisites. There is no general attorney-status exception and no holding that every order against an attorney is unappealable.
JUDGMENT
**Affirmed, 9–0.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The appeal remains dismissed. The Court neither affirms the sanction on its merits nor vacates it, and decides neither the sanction amount nor the validity of disqualification. No payment stay or substitute appellate route is granted.

FILE greater_new_orleans_broadcasting_association_inc_v_united_states_merits_1999-06-14.md
**Case and dockets:** Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association, Inc. v. United States, No. 98-387.
HOLDINGS
**Direct and material advancement.** Section 1304 cannot be enforced against these Louisiana broadcasters' truthful, nonmisleading advertisements for lawful private-casino gambling because the federal scheme's actual exceptions and operation do not directly and materially advance its asserted gambling-harm reduction and state-policy accommodation interests. Potential reception in a State prohibiting private casinos does not itself make the advertised lawful transactions unlawful.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens joins this application conditionally under the existing framework; Scalia joins the application while retaining broader doctrinal reservations. Thomas supports the judgment on a different ground and adds no balancing vote.


**Reasonable fit.** Independently, suppressing these truthful lawful-casino advertisements lacks a reasonable proportional fit with the asserted interests, given the scheme's extensive comparable permissions and available direct regulation of gambling. The Government may pursue genuine incremental policies, but the actual informational burden requires justification; the First Amendment does not require the least restrictive imaginable policy.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Court-opinion coalition directly joins this independently sufficient ground, with the same stated reservations.


TREATMENT
- Central Hudson supplies ordinary lawful/nonmisleading commercial-speech review, substantial interest, material advancement and reasonable fit.
- Fox preserves the distinction between reasonable fit and least-restrictive means.
- Edenfield requires justification responsive to real harms and material alleviation without invariably requiring empirical studies.
- Ibanez tests the actual truthful-information restriction and its responsive justification.
- Rubin applies independent advancement and fit inquiries to a materially contradictory scheme and requires an actual state-accommodation mechanism.
- 44 Liquormart preserves those separate inquiries under ordinary scrutiny; its alcohol-specific rule does not create a gambling exception or a new tier.
- Edge retains its record-specific meaningful-reduction and state-accommodation holding; the different statutory permissions and mechanism here do not reproduce it.
- Posadas is not overruled through this as-applied decision, and no categorical constitutional rule against all vice regulation is adopted.
LAW
The described lawful private-casino advertising is protected against this federal prohibition because each of two ordinary commercial-speech requirements fails independently. False or misleading advertisements, promotion of unlawful transactions, targeting minors and materially narrower evidence-supported restrictions remain outside the holding. Direct regulation of gambling survives. Other asserted applications require their own justification; neither §1304 nor the FCC restriction is erased in all settings.
JUDGMENT
**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The Government may not enforce §1304 against these truthful, nonmisleading advertisements for lawful private-casino gambling. Relief concerning the companion FCC restriction conforms to that same as-applied prohibition. Section 1304 and the rule are not facially invalidated; no damages or replacement gambling policy is ordered.
