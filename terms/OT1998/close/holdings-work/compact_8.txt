**Case and dockets:** Florida v. White; No. 98-223; judgment below, 710 So. 2d 949.
HOLDINGS
#### Seizure of a forfeitable automobile in a public place

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers with probable cause that a readily movable automobile itself is forfeitable may seize it from a public place without a warrant or an additional case-specific emergency. The rule rests on the automobile's characteristics and the public-place seizure, not merely on a legislature's decision to label property contraband.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; these six Justices control the rule, application and remedy.


TREATMENT
- Pennsylvania v. Labron is extended from a vehicle evidence search to seizure of the readily movable vehicle itself when probable cause identifies it as forfeitable and it is taken in public.
- Wyoming v. Houghton is applied for attention to the object and scope supported by probable cause; it did not itself decide forfeiture seizure.
- United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property is distinguished: its real-property process requirement and movable-property reservation retain their force.
- G. M. Leasing Corp. v. United States supports the public-place/protected-premises distinction; its revenue-enforcement holding is not converted into unlimited forfeiture authority.
- Knowles v. Iowa remains intact: an inventory or adjacent enforcement power cannot itself furnish authority for the antecedent seizure.
LAW
The automobile exception permits this public seizure of the forfeitable vehicle without a separate urgency showing. It supplies no general warrant exception for all property designated contraband, and leaves distinct seizure, search, retention, hearing and final-forfeiture questions separate.
JUDGMENT
Reversed and remanded, 6–3. Supporting: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Opposing: Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg. Reversal rejects the federal warrant/additional-emergency ground for suppressing the fruits of this seizure. It does not unconditionally reinstate the conviction, order admission of every item, or enter a forfeiture award. Ordinary properly available proceedings and independent grounds remain for the state courts.

The Court applies the existing Shaw/Johnson rule and requires a conforming remand. Predominance alone does not sustain an injunction or require a trial. FILE ruhrgas_ag_v_marathon_oil_co_merits_1999-05-17.md
**Case and dockets:** Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co.; No. 98-470; judgment below, 145 F.3d 211.
HOLDINGS
#### A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while the subject-matter issue is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first; the choice must respect applicable statutory remand commands and review bars and must decide no contested merits.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls the rule, application and remedy.


TREATMENT
- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. is applied within its express scope: jurisdiction must precede contested merits, but its nonmerits-sequencing reservation permits today's rule.
- NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones is applied on the same merits boundary; its prohibition on assumed standing is unchanged.
- Murphy Brothers, Inc. v. Michetti Pipe Stringing, Inc. is distinguished as removal timing and formal process, which establish no subject-matter jurisdiction or universal sequence.
- El Paso Natural Gas Co. v. Neztsosie is distinguished as a statute-specific priority over prudential exhaustion, not a general order between jurisdictional grounds.
- Insurance Corp. of Ireland v. Compagnie des Bauxites de Guinee supplies the distinction between the individual interest protected by personal jurisdiction and institutional subject-matter limits; the distinction does not establish an invariable order.
LAW
Federal courts may use the bounded personal-jurisdiction-first route in removed as well as originally filed actions. The rule adds discretion among nonmerits thresholds while preserving ordinary comity, actual statutory commands and the prohibition on deciding contested merits without jurisdiction. It establishes no universal preclusion rule or actual jurisdiction over these claims.
JUDGMENT
Reversed and remanded, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The District Court was permitted to choose the personal-jurisdiction inquiry. The Fifth Circuit must review the dismissal under the correct sequencing rule; the Court does not affirm the correctness of the disputed contacts or the ultimate personal-jurisdiction conclusion. No removal ground or state-court remand is decided.

**Holding and operative rule:** The FTC Act reaches a nonprofit professional association organized to carry on business for its members' profit when its activities confer proximate, substantial economic benefits on their for-profit operations. **Holding and operative rule:** An abbreviated rule-of-reason inquiry may suffice when experience and the restraint's actual character make its likely anticompetitive tendency sufficiently clear to assess both the restraint and its material competing explanation. **Holding and operative rule:** The practical restrictions on fee descriptions and broad discount advertisements cannot be sustained on the abbreviated analysis employed here without adequately examining their asserted effect on the reliability of price comparison. FILE davis_v_monroe_county_board_of_education_merits_1999-05-24.md
**Case and dockets:** Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education; No. 97-843.
HOLDINGS
### A school's own culpable response can support damages for controlled peer harassment

**Holding and operative rule:** A Title IX funding recipient may be liable for its own response to peer harassment because of sex when an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial danger has actual notice, the recipient substantially controls both the harasser and the relevant educational context, and deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that danger causally contributes to further harassment or vulnerability producing educational deprivation and compensable injury. A peer's attendance does not impute the peer's acts to the recipient; knowledge, culpability, control, sex-based discrimination and causal loss are independent requirements.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer; six direct joins establish this sufficient route.


### Educational deprivation follows the contextual severe-or-pervasive standard

**Holding and operative rule:** The harassment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive and impair educational access or benefit in its full context. Sexually charged conduct or offense alone does not establish discrimination because of sex, the required educational harm or the independent institutional elements.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer, directly controls with five votes. Stone-Zsela does not join this disjunctive threshold; no combination of separate rationales is needed for its authority.


### The pleaded recipient claim warrants ordinary further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** Allegations of repeated school-setting harassment, reports including to the principal, continued conduct, deliberately inadequate protection and resulting educational interference state a claim for examination under the sufficient recipient-fault route. The categorical dismissal must be reversed, but an absent element, reasonable response, inadequate control or notice, missing causation or a supported defense may defeat recovery on the developed record.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six directly support this allegation-stage application and remedy.


TREATMENT
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools is applied to available compensation for proved intentional discrimination, with recipient responsibility, causation and loss still distinct.
- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District is extended at its sufficient actual-notice/culpable-disregard route to substantially controlled peer conduct. Its severe-or-pervasive contextual educational-harm standard remains; the peer extension is stated expressly.
- Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District retains the limited teacher-authority-plus-recipient-fault conjunction and its rejection of exclusivity in teacher cases. Neither delegated teacher power nor that complete route is imputed to peers.
- NCAA v. Smith preserves the distinct federal-assistance recipient requirement; economic connection or control alone does not establish receipt.
- Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson informs the distinction between participation and welcome through Kinman without importing employment attribution wholesale.
- Harris v. Forklift Systems informs the contextual objective-and-experienced abuse inquiry through Kinman, without mandatory psychiatric injury or any indispensable factor.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman retains its funding-notice principle; the controlling recipient-own-wrong route concerns intentional educational discrimination, not automatic liability for a third party.
LAW
A funding recipient's own actually informed, deliberately unreasonable response to peer harassment within substantial control can support damages when all independent discrimination, educational-harm and causal-injury elements are established. The controlling environment threshold remains severe or pervasive, objectively abusive and actually experienced as abusive, with impaired educational access or benefit. Teacher-attribution rules remain separately bounded. Ordinary negligence, policy breach, unsuccessful reasonable correction and peer attendance alone do not establish liability. The Chief's separate reporting-failure extension does not become a controlling peer rule, and no complete alternative-attribution or defense code is decided.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The recipient claim may proceed under the stated conditions. LAW
Officials may not bring nonassisting private media into a home merely under the authority of an execution warrant. Consent, separate lawful authority and genuine reasonable execution assistance remain distinct. These officers nevertheless receive personal immunity for this April 1992 application. The judgment creates no future media-access privilege and changes neither ordinary immunity procedure nor the requirement of individual responsibility and injury in a properly available damages claim.
JUDGMENT
**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela and Stevens.

All nine agree that the nonassisting media's added entry violated the homeowners' Fourth Amendment rights. Seven grant personal qualified immunity under April 1992 law. Stevens and the Chief would reverse immunity and remand for ordinary personal-participation, causation, injury, damages and preserved-defense questions, without an automatic trial or award. The affirmance ends the reviewed federal personal-damages theory against these respondents; separate probable-cause and force rulings are not reopened.
