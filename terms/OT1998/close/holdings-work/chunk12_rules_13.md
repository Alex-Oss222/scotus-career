## Holdings

### Neither instrument clearly waives the statutory judicial forum

**Operative rule:** A union-negotiated surrender of an employee's judicial forum for an independent federal discrimination claim must be clear and unmistakable: the agreement must identify that statutory claim or an equivalent class of federal rights rather than merely submit contractual disputes. Neither this CBA nor the separate Seniority Plan does so, and their grievance provisions cannot support dismissal of Wright's ADA action for nonexhaustion; whether a sufficiently explicit collective waiver would be enforceable remains undecided.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the clarity rule, both instrument-specific applications and the bounded remand.



## Precedent Treatment

- Alexander v. Gardner-Denver Co.: applies the distinction between collective contractual rights and the individual's statutory discrimination claim; no categorical decision on explicit waiver enforceability is added.
- Metropolitan Edison Co. v. NLRB: applies the requirement of a clear waiver of protected statutory rights to the forum question presented.
- Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp.: distinguishes its actual individual statutory-claim agreement; enforceability there does not create assent here.
- AT&T Technologies v. Communications Workers: preserves judicial determination of the submission actually made before arbitration may be compelled.
- First Options of Chicago v. Kaplan (May 22, 1995): applies antecedent assent and independent judicial determination; its arbitrability-delegation rule is not expanded into an already-decided universal collective-waiver rule.
- Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton: applies integrated, instrument-specific construction; no contractual presumption supplies missing statutory-forum language.

## Law After Decision

Broad collective grievance language and a contractual legality clause do not alone surrender an employee's forum for an independent federal discrimination claim. Both instruments fail the necessary clarity here. The decision leaves enforceability of an adequately explicit union waiver unresolved and neither disfavors genuinely agreed statutory arbitration nor decides the ADA merits.
