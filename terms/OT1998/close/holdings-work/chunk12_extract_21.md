## Holdings

### The lender's pecuniary stake permits appellate review

**Operative rule:** The credit union is sufficiently pecuniarily aggrieved to appeal refusal of this proposed reaffirmation because approval would make the proposed personal car-loan and $1,500 unsecured obligations enforceable. That appellate stake permits review but does not establish a right to approval or remove §524's conditions.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this threshold proposition and its limited consequence.

**Controlling explanation:** Refusal affects the lender's proposed enforceable claims, not merely its abstract preference about bankruptcy administration. The Ninth Circuit therefore correctly rejected the BAP's standing ground and examined the bankruptcy court's ruling. The creditor's interest in obtaining approval and the legal conditions for obtaining it are different questions. Recognizing the former supplies no missing finding of the debtor's best interest or absence of undue hardship.

### Mandatory notice and performance do not create an exclusive retention election

**Operative rule:** For the scheduled consumer debts secured by estate property covered by the then-effective §521(2), the debtor must file the statement of retention or surrender within thirty days after filing or by the creditors' meeting, whichever is earlier, with a for-cause extension fixed within that period; the statement must specify exemption, redemption or reaffirmation when applicable, and the debtor must perform the lawful stated intention within forty-five days after that notice, with a for-cause extension fixed within those forty-five days. Read with (C)'s preservation of the debtor's and trustee's rights, those mandatory duties do not themselves make surrender, redemption or reaffirmation the exclusive substantive options: a debtor may maintain payments and retain collateral insofar as existing law and the enforceable agreement permit, without stripping the lien, excusing an actual lawful default or obtaining judicially reduced installment redemption.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the complete statutory rule, timing qualifications and retained-rights limitation.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted duties disclose the debtor's lawful intention and require timely performance. Their mandatory character does not establish an additional consequence that the statute does not state. The qualification concerning applicable elections and subparagraph (C)'s nonalteration language must operate alongside the list. Section 524's notice that reaffirmation is not required, and its independent approval safeguards, reinforce the distinction between disclosure and a command to create enforceable personal liability.

The creditor argues that the list otherwise lacks force and that possession without personal liability imposes an involuntary new bargain. Notice and performance retain their stated work, while the creditor retains the lien and remedies for a legally established default. Personal discharge and survival of a property claim are distinct, as Johnson and Dewsnup recognize. Dewsnup's particular Chapter 7 lien rule does not add a compulsory election here. Nobelman's Chapter 13 principal-residence protection likewise turns on its own enacted rights clause, not a universal requirement of continued personal liability for every retained asset. Ron Pair requires attention to this provision's actual text; Butner preserves state-defined interests subject to the Code's specified consequences.

The Court does not construe an unseen bankruptcy-default, insurance or impairment clause or hold that every such clause is invalid. Continued possession remains subject to the actual lawful agreement, applicable default rules, the valid lien and the Code's independent stay and discharge provisions. Section 521(2) alone supplies no new acceleration or forfeiture command.

### This agreement remains subject to independent debtor-protection approval

**Operative rule:** For this unrepresented individual, §524(c)(6)(A) requires judicial approval that the reaffirmation both does not impose undue hardship on the debtor or a dependent of the debtor and is in the debtor's best interest; (6)(B)'s exception for consumer debt secured by real property does not cover this car and unsecured card debt. Signing the agreement does not satisfy those distinct conditions, and on the reviewed continued-payment-retention premise refusal of this agreement was within the bankruptcy court's discretion.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the approval rule and the agreement-specific affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The proposed agreement renewed personal liability for the car loan and added $1,500 in otherwise unsecured debt. The reduction in monthly car payments from $308.73 to $280, reduction of the unsecured amount, and reinstatement as a member in good standing upon discharge, and the agreement to disregard the debt in future loan applications only if the secured account is paid in full on time were benefits to consider; they did not compel approval or guarantee a future loan. Because lawful retention did not itself require reaffirmation, the court could assess those benefits against the renewed obligations rather than treat signing as the necessary price of keeping the vehicle.

The no-undue-hardship and best-interest inquiries are separate requirements. The lender's appellate standing does not displace either, and mutual assent does not substitute for the approval required for this unrepresented debtor. This decision concerns approval under §524(c)(6), not compliance with the other enforceability conditions in §524(c) and (d). Section 524(f) permits voluntary repayment, which does not restore enforceable discharged personal liability.

The affirmance concerns this agreement and this reviewed statutory premise. It does not presume every reaffirmation unwise, force the lender to extend credit, reduce the debt by judicial valuation or approve a replacement bargain. Ordinary lien enforcement remains subject to its actual legal prerequisites.

## Precedent Treatment

- Johnson v. Home State Bank: applies the distinction between extinguished personal liability and a surviving claim against property.
- Dewsnup v. Timm (January 15, 1992): preserves its limited Chapter 7 real-property lien holding; it supplies no additional §521 election or immunity from express Code provisions.
- Nobelman v. American Savings Bank (June 1, 1993): distinguishes the express Chapter 13 principal-residence rights protection from this Chapter 7 automobile setting.
- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises: applies attention to the actual provision and its qualifications rather than an added consequence.
- Butner v. United States: preserves state-defined property and contract rights subject to the consequences Congress enacted; no unknown contract term is adjudicated.

## Law After Decision

Under the then-effective Code, timely statement and performance remain mandatory with their enacted extension conditions. Section 521(2) alone does not compel reaffirmation or redemption as a condition of retention otherwise permitted by the actual agreement and law. Discharged personal liability, voluntary payment and a surviving enforceable lien remain distinct. For the automobile and unsecured debts here, the unrepresented-debtor approval requirements of both no undue hardship and best interest apply independently. The decision neither excuses defaults nor supplies a general rule invalidating bankruptcy clauses.
