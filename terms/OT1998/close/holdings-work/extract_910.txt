FILE richardson_v_united_states_merits_1999-06-01.md
**Case and dockets:** Richardson v. United States; No. 97-8629.
Richardson v. United States; No. 97-8629. Merits decision, June 1, 1999. Argued February 22, 1999. On review of the Seventh Circuit's judgment, 130 F.3d 765, affirming Richardson's continuing-criminal-enterprise conviction. The question is whether jurors must agree on the particular drug violations composing the continuing series under 21 U.S.C. section 848.

The trial charge required at least three narcotics violations, including one after March 24, 1989, but expressly permitted jurors to disagree about which violations occurred. Richardson objected to the refusal of a requested predicate-unanimity instruction. The indictment alleged repeated distribution and possession offenses; the reported conspiracy and CCE convictions are not separate verdicts on three particular distributions. The number and date conditions just described belong to the trial charge; the Court does not separately construe a statutory minimum number of violations.
JUDGMENT
**Supporting vacatur and remand:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. **Opposing:** O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg, who would affirm against this unanimity objection.

The Seventh Circuit must consider in the first instance whether the preserved instructional error is subject to harmless-error review and, if so, whether it is harmless under the governing standard. The CCE conviction must be set aside unless it may lawfully be sustained in proceedings consistent with the predicate-unanimity rule. The Court decides neither harmlessness question and orders no automatic new trial, acquittal, release or fixed sentence. Ordinary double-jeopardy and retrial principles remain applicable. The separate conspiracy conviction receives no independent disposition here.
HOLDINGS
### Unanimous agreement on the constituent violations

**Operative rule:** In a prosecution under 21 U.S.C. section 848, each particular violation necessary to compose the continuing series is an element that the jury must find unanimously beyond a reasonable doubt. Jurors must agree on the same qualifying violations, although they need not agree on every evidentiary detail or means of committing an agreed violation; an instruction expressly allowing disagreement about the constituent violations is erroneous.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, supplies six votes for this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute makes a continuing series of independently criminal violations part of the greater offense. The additional requirements of management of five or more persons and substantial income or resources do not turn those violations into interchangeable evidence. Otherwise jurors could agree only that the defendant ran a criminal business while relying on wholly different crimes to supply the required series. Gaudin assigns actual offense elements to the jury; the statutory language here identifies the elements to which that protection attaches. Rutledge distinguishes the series requirement from the narcotics agreement, so the conspiracy verdict does not itself establish the required predicate transactions.

Schad permits genuine alternative means of committing an offense, but its traditional alternatives for a single homicide do not answer whether distinct criminal episodes constitute this statutory series. The breadth of the violations that could otherwise be combined reinforces the ordinary unanimity reading and avoids serious constitutional doubt without deciding a universal constitutional classification rule. The Government's focus on the continuing enterprise explains the offense's target; it does not erase the enacted violations requirement.

The charge expressly permitted the disagreement the statute forbids. The Court does not require unanimity about every item of proof, every overt act, the identity of each supervised person or every managerial relationship. Those distinct questions are not decided. Roy's treatment of a personal-intent omission and Sullivan's treatment of a defective reasonable-doubt standard retain their own scopes. Neither supplies a present classification of this express nonunanimity instruction, and the remand leaves both availability and application of harmless-error review open.
TREATMENT
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applied to jury determination of identified offense elements; it does not itself resolve the antecedent elements-versus-means question or make every instructional defect structural.
- **Rutledge v. United States:** Applied to the distinction between an agreement and the continuing series. Its duplicate-conviction rule remains intact but supplies no unpresented Count One remedy here.
- **Schad v. Arizona:** Distinguished because accepted alternative means for a traditional single offense do not establish that separate episodes composing this federal series are mere evidence. Its result and bounded elements/means distinction remain intact.
- **Griffin v. United States:** Its treatment of a factually unsupported lawful conspiracy object does not resolve the independent unanimity defect expressly reserved by that decision.
- **California v. Roy:** Its rejection of automatic reversal for the personal-intent omission and its qualified actual-verdict inquiry remain effective within their stated category; neither is extended here to decide reviewability of express predicate nonunanimity.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Its structural rule for a vitiated reasonable-doubt standard remains intact; this decision does not decide whether the present defect has the same consequence.
- **Johnson v. United States:** Its forfeited-error disposition does not replace the governing rules for this preserved objection.
- **Jones v. United States:** Its statutory identification of offense-defining facts remains consistent with the present construction; no universal constitutional maximum-fact rule is added here.
LAW
From June 1, 1999, section 848 requires unanimous agreement on the particular violations necessary to constitute the continuing series. Series-wide agreement alone cannot satisfy that requirement when the jurors are expressly permitted to rely on different violations. The distinction between offense elements and evidentiary means survives. No separately contested minimum number of violations, supervisee-identification rule or universal constitutional element test is decided.

Harmless-error availability and application remain unresolved for this defect. Sufficient evidence to convict is not itself an adjudication that the actual instructional error was harmless. If constitutional harmless-error review is available, its applicable beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden remains the Government's; the Court does not decide that the inquiry applies or that its showing succeeds or fails.

FILE bruneau_v_south_kortright_central_school_district_merits_1999-06-03.md
**Case and dockets:** Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District; No. 98-1541.
Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District; No. 98-1541. Merits decision, June 3, 1999, after submission on the merits. On review of the Second Circuit's judgment, 163 F.3d 749. The question is whether Title IX displaces section 1983 enforcement of the statutory sex-discrimination right or an independently secured Equal Protection right.

The student alleged harassment by male sixth-grade classmates during the 1993-94 school year. A jury returned a general defense verdict on the Title IX claim against the District. The Second Circuit affirmed that judgment, upheld dismissal of the District and Board section 1983 claims on statutory-displacement grounds, and separately upheld qualified immunity for teacher Parker and assistant superintendent Race. It did not decide displacement as to those individuals. The present review concerns the displacement question; no argument date is established.
JUDGMENT
| Component | Disposition | Supporting | Opposing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent Equal Protection claims against the District and Board | Reverse the categorical displacement dismissal and remand, 9-0. | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | No opposing Justice. |
| Section 1983 enforcement of the identified Title IX statutory right against the District and Board | Reverse the categorical foreclosure ruling and remand, 8-1. | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela, who would retain dismissal of this statutory route. |

The existing Title IX trial judgment and the independent individual-immunity affirmance remain undisturbed. The remand restores only the opportunity to litigate claims improperly barred by the displacement rule. It decides no defendant's liability, establishes no discriminatory purpose, awards no damages and orders no new Title IX trial. Ordinary elements and defenses, including any properly demonstrated effect of the existing verdict, remain for the lower courts.
HOLDINGS
### 1. Independent constitutional claims remain available

**Operative rule:** Title IX does not displace a section 1983 claim enforcing an independently secured Equal Protection right merely because the same school harassment also supports a Title IX theory. The claimant must establish the constitutional violation, state action, defendant-specific responsibility or proper municipal attribution, causation and an available remedy; correcting displacement supplies none of those elements.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion on this proposition and the common remedial limits is joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Fourteenth Amendment imposes duties independent of a funding recipient's statutory obligations. Blessing requires attention to the particular right and actual congressional foreclosure, not an assumption that overlapping facts create identical rights. The funding-termination process and the implied Title IX action do not provide an exclusive personal remedial system incompatible with the independent constitutional claim. The differing duties and defendants make categorical extinction especially unwarranted.

Sea Clammers and Smith v. Robinson concerned actual incompatibility with the statutory enforcement designs before the Court; neither holds that every private federal action extinguishes constitutional remedies arising from the same events. Kinman kept Title IX and constitutional supervisory and municipal liability distinct. Gebser and Davis refine responsibility under Title IX; they do not turn every peer's misconduct into unconstitutional official action or silently repeal Equal Protection enforcement.

The District and Board claims were foreclosed categorically and must receive ordinary consideration. The individual defendants prevailed on an independent immunity ground, and the general Title IX verdict establishes no new, specified factual finding in this Court. Neither judgment is overturned by correcting displacement. Purposeful official discrimination, municipal policy or final-authority attribution, causation and any actual preclusive effect remain separate questions; institutional rank, negligence and receipt of funds do not alone prove them.

### 2. The identified statutory right is not categorically foreclosed under section 1983

**Operative rule:** Title IX's individual right against sex discrimination in a covered educational program is not categorically excluded from section 1983 enforcement by the administrative funding process or the established implied private action. A statutory section 1983 claim must prove the actual Title IX right and violation, including the statute's defendant coverage and recipient-responsibility conditions; the enforcement vehicle does not expand the underlying duty.

**Authority:** Thomas's statutory portion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Controlling explanation:** Blessing separates identification of an individual statutory right from the further inquiry whether Congress excluded section 1983 expressly or through an incompatible remedial scheme. This claim identifies a student's right against sex discrimination, rather than an aggregate demand for better program administration. Title IX contains no express exclusion. Its funding oversight does not give this student an exclusive adjudicatory forum, and the implied action recognized in Cannon and Franklin supplies no elaborate mandatory claim-processing route that section 1983 would evade.

The principal objection is that another enforcement label might undo the limits of the recipient's funding obligation. That objection is answered by enforcing the actual statutory right: section 1983 does not make a nonrecipient liable under Title IX, erase statutory attribution conditions or create a new harassment standard. The scheme therefore does not require categorical foreclosure of the identified right. The remand permits consideration of properly pleaded statutory claims against the District and Board subject to those limits, ordinary defenses and the existing verdict's demonstrated legal effect. No merits finding or new trial follows automatically.
TREATMENT
- **Blessing v. Freestone:** Applied to the separate inquiries into a particular individual right and actual congressional foreclosure; its rejection of aggregate program-compliance claims remains intact.
- **Cannon v. University of Chicago:** Preserved as the source of Title IX's established implied private action; that action's existence does not itself demonstrate exclusivity.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools:** Preserved as recognizing compensatory relief for established intentional Title IX discrimination; this decision creates no new liability or damages finding.
- **Middlesex County Sewerage Authority v. National Sea Clammers Association:** Distinguished by the incompatible enforcement structure there; the actual foreclosure principle remains available when established.
- **Smith v. Robinson:** Distinguished by the remedial incompatibility there; factual overlap alone does not reproduce that holding.
- **Kinman v. Omaha Public School District:** Applied to the separation of statutory recipient liability, personal constitutional responsibility and municipal attribution, without treating its adjudication as an earlier decision of every preclusion question.
- **Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District:** Its controlling teacher-attribution route and unappealed-claim limits remain unchanged; no current rule is used to reopen the separate immunity judgment here.
- **Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education:** Its peer-harassment conditions govern actual statutory recipient liability, but do not extinguish an independent official-discrimination claim or establish liability on this record.
LAW
From June 3, 1999, Title IX supplies no categorical bar to the independently sourced Equal Protection claims or the identified statutory-right claims enforced through section 1983 here. The constitutional holding has nine votes; the statutory enforcement holding has eight. Different sources of rights retain their different substantive conditions and defendant coverage.

The statutory enforcement vehicle does not enlarge the Title IX duty. The independent constitutional claim requires its own purposeful discrimination and responsibility; it is not a means of treating all peer harassment or negligent supervision as unconstitutional conduct. Existing Title IX attribution law, conduct-date immunity rules, municipal-liability requirements and valid preclusion defenses remain in force.

FILE reno_v_goncalves_merits_1999-06-03.md
**Case and dockets:** Reno v. Goncalves; No. 98-835; First Circuit judgment, 144 F.3d 110.
Reno v. Goncalves, No. 98-835. Merits decision, June 3, 1999. Argued April 21, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit, 144 F.3d 110, which reversed the dismissal of Goncalves's habeas petition and required administrative consideration of his request for discretionary relief. The questions concern whether section 2241 jurisdiction survives the 1996 immigration amendments, whether AEDPA section 440(d) governs a former section 212(c) application pending at enactment, and the interpretive weight due Matter of Soriano. Goncalves applied in September 1994, received an adverse discretionary decision in January 1995, and had a timely administrative appeal pending on April 24, 1996. The Board later applied section 440(d)'s eligibility bar. Following immigration custody, he filed this habeas petition in August 1997.
JUDGMENT
Affirmed, 6–3. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing the eligibility disposition: Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. All nine sustain district-court jurisdiction over the actual section 2241 petition. The Board shall consider Goncalves's former section 212(c) application under the eligibility law applicable to the pending application when AEDPA took effect and exercise the discretion that law supplies. The immigration judge's earlier discretionary denial does not replace the Board's decision on the timely appeal. No favorable exercise of discretion, waiver, release or termination of deportation proceedings is awarded; equal protection is not adjudicated.
HOLDINGS
#### I. The actual statutory habeas channel survives

**Holding and operative rule:** AEDPA and IIRIRA did not clearly withdraw the independently enacted jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. section 2241 to decide Goncalves's substantial legal claim that the agency unlawfully denied eligibility for discretionary relief. That channel reaches substantial statutory and constitutional objections to lawful executive authority when its ordinary custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other predicates are met; it does not authorize judicial reweighing of discretionary equities or retrial of disputed deportability facts.

**Authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by every other Justice, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Kolster distinguishes withdrawal of ordinary INA review from repeal of the independently enacted writ and includes unlawful denial of legal eligibility within the writ's conditional scope. Here there is actual custody, an actual petition and a completed administrative legal determination; no separate jurisdictional defect is established. Felker, McCardle and Yerger require attention to the particular channel Congress withdrew. AEDPA section 401(e) deletes the specialized INA habeas provision; section 440(a) limits ordinary direct review. Neither clearly repeals section 2241. IIRIRA section 309(c)(4)(G) bars the covered transitional appeal but does not silently eliminate the independent writ. Reyes-Hernandez and Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee confine section 242(g) to the named executive actions; this legal challenge to completed adjudication is not absorbed into that provision. The permanent section 242(b)(9) regime cannot be imported wholesale into these transitional proceedings. The Government's combined-channel argument therefore cannot do what the particular provisions do not establish. Jurisdiction rests on the surviving statute, not the seriousness of a claim alone. Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee's separate section 1331 constitutional-selection route is neither needed nor enlarged. The Court does not decide the Suspension Clause's independent minimum or license review of discretionary merits.

#### II. Section 440(d) does not govern this pending application

**Holding and operative rule:** AEDPA section 440(d)'s new categorical eligibility restriction does not govern a former section 212(c) application filed before AEDPA took effect on April 24, 1996 and still awaiting final administrative action at enactment. Goncalves's pending administrative appeal must be considered under the former eligibility rule; the holding neither covers applications first filed after enactment nor determines the distinct temporal operation of IIRIRA's later repeal.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; the six-Justice full-opinion coalition stated in Opinion Topology directly adopts this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf and Rivers first require identification of Congress's temporal choice for the particular provision. Lindh permits ordinary statutory construction to establish that choice through express and omitted temporal directions. Sections 413(g) and 421(b) expressly reach pending applications, including applications for discretionary relief; section 440(d) does not. Section 440(f)'s separate instruction concerns subsection (e), not subsection (d). The enactment thus supplies more than silence considered alone. The objection that the Attorney General is deciding whether to grant present discretionary relief overlooks Congress's express treatment of pending discretionary requests elsewhere. Treating all eligibility restrictions as automatically applicable to such requests would erase that distinction. This amendment changes legal eligibility, not merely the tribunal or channel of review. Goncalves had invoked the discretionary process and pursued an appeal before enactment. The provision-specific construction preserves that pending application without requiring an invented plea bargain, subjective reliance or a vested right to a favorable waiver. Hincapie-Nieto, Salazar-Haro and Kolster resolved different review-channel restrictions; they do not settle section 440(d)'s substantive eligibility change. Reyes-Hernandez's completed surrender of a defense is not the exclusive possible temporal predicate, and no such surrender is found here. The Court adopts no rule that pending status alone preserves every former law.

#### III. Agency interpretation cannot override this statutory conclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Matter of Soriano receives no controlling weight on section 440(d)'s temporal application because ordinary interpretation resolves that question against the agency's construction; its reasoning is not persuasive enough to overcome the enacted distinctions. The Attorney General likewise cannot conclusively determine the district court's section 2241 jurisdiction, and the Board must decide the pending request without applying section 440(d) as an eligibility bar.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court; the same six-Justice coalition directly adopts this rule and its administrative remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** MCI enforces the limits of enacted authority; NationsBank and Aguirre-Aguirre allow agency interpretation within delegated authority and permissible statutory meaning. Those decisions do not empower an agency to override a temporal choice established through ordinary construction. Soriano gives insufficient effect to the express treatment of pending requests in neighboring provisions and would make silence in section 440(d) carry a consequence that the statutory structure does not support. The Court does not replace the existing deference framework or hold that every temporal question excludes agency expertise. Authorized judgments on genuinely unresolved statutory details, policy and technical administration retain their proper weight. The judgment removes a legal barrier to consideration; it does not decide how the Board must balance the lawful discretionary factors. Equal protection remains outside the grant.
TREATMENT
- **Kolster v. INS:** Applied to an actual petition satisfying the previously reserved predicates; legal eligibility falls within its retained authority category, while discretionary reweighing remains excluded.
- **Felker v. Turpin:** Applied for channel-specific repeal analysis; withdrawal of one review mechanism does not itself repeal a separate jurisdictional grant.
- **Ex parte McCardle:** Its effect is confined to the appellate route Congress withdrew; it does not establish repeal of every independent habeas route.
- **Ex parte Yerger:** Supports preservation of an independently available writ despite withdrawal of another review channel.
- **Reyes-Hernandez v. INS:** Supplies the named-action boundary under section 242(g); its completed surrender of a defense is a distinct temporal predicate, not a universal prerequisite.
- **Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee:** Supplies the named-action/completed-adjudication distinction. Its separate constitutional section 1331 route is not extended.
- **Hincapie-Nieto v. INS:** Its ordinary-review restriction and rejection of pending status alone remain controlling; they do not decide section 440(d)'s different eligibility change.
- **Salazar-Haro v. INS:** Its review-channel rule remains intact and supplies no general immediate-effect rule for every substantive eligibility restriction.
- **Landgraf v. USI Film Products:** Supplies the sequence requiring identification of Congress's temporal choice; the Court applies ordinary statutory construction without adopting a universal pending-case exception.
- **Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc.:** Preserves provision-specific temporal analysis and supplies no agency power to override Congress's choice.
- **Lindh v. Murphy:** Its express/omitted temporal comparison is applied to these provisions, not extended to every AEDPA amendment.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T:** Requires adherence to the enacted bounds of agency authority.
- **NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.:** Preserves agency interpretation only within permissible statutory meaning and delegated authority.
- **INS v. Aguirre-Aguirre:** Retains appropriate immigration deference for authorized unresolved choices; it does not authorize a contrary temporal rule after ordinary construction resolves Congress's choice.
LAW
The surviving section 2241 route can decide this covered criminal alien's substantial legal eligibility challenge when its actual predicates are satisfied. Applications for former section 212(c) relief already pending on April 24, 1996 remain outside section 440(d)'s new eligibility bar. Eligibility permits the agency to exercise its lawful discretion; it does not predetermine the result. Ordinary-review restrictions, later-filed applications and IIRIRA's distinct repeal remain governed by their own provisions. No precedent is overruled and no general right to review discretionary immigration decisions is created.

FILE amoco_production_co_v_southern_ute_indian_tribe_merits_1999-06-07.md
**Case and dockets:** Amoco Production Co. v. Southern Ute Indian Tribe; No. 98-830; Tenth Circuit en banc judgment, 151 F.3d 1251.
Amoco Production Co. v. Southern Ute Indian Tribe, No. 98-830. Merits decision, June 7, 1999. Submitted on full merits briefing. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, sitting en banc, 151 F.3d 1251. The question is whether the 1909 and 1910 Coal Lands Acts' reservations of coal to the United States include distinct coalbed methane gas. The Tribe succeeded in 1938 to the retained coal interests, subject to outstanding patent rights. The district court resolved ownership against the Tribe and certified its judgment under Rule 54(b), leaving other matters unresolved. The court of appeals held the reserved coal included methane trapped in and produced from the coal reservoir; it left migrated-gas title and source allocation for further proceedings.
JUDGMENT
Reversed and remanded, 8–1. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Opposing: Ginsburg. The coal reservations themselves did not vest title to the distinct methane at issue in the United States and then the Tribe. Further proceedings must apply that construction without treating it as a blanket license to extract gas or as a disposition of every pleaded claim.
HOLDINGS
#### The reservation of coal does not include this distinct methane gas

**Holding and operative rule:** The 1909 and 1910 Coal Lands Acts' reservations of coal do not reserve naturally occurring, chemically distinct methane held in coal by physical adsorption or in its fractures merely because the gas is associated with the coal reservoir. The reserved estate includes coal and its expressly accompanying rights; separate gas ownership does not decide lawful access, injury to coal, compensation or title created by a different gas reservation.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by the other seven Justices in the full coalition stated in Opinion Topology, controls directly.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress reserved a particular resource while conveying the remaining estate. In the enactment's historical setting, coal denoted the solid mined fuel; methane was separately known as a gas, often a dangerous byproduct that mining released. Physical association does not make the gas the substance Congress named. The Tribe's strongest response is that coal is a heterogeneous deposit, containing associated hydrocarbons, and that conservation of all coal preserved the deposit's future energy value. But the methane at issue remains a separately understood substance. Improved extraction changes its profitability, not its identity or the statutory words. Coal that lacked commercial value at enactment remains reserved coal, and new profitable uses of that substance do not change title. The rule does not rest on a general claim that newly valuable materials always belong to the grantee. Western Nuclear interpreted the broader reservation of coal and other minerals; its sovereign-land-grant canon operates within the enacted text. Leo Sheep likewise requires fidelity to the actual conveyance rather than an enlargement through an asserted general purpose. Here text, contemporary usage and the specific estate structure resolve the distinction without adding a gas reservation through a canon. The 1938 transfer placed the existing retained coal estate in trust; it did not enlarge that estate. Nor does the ownership answer authorize destruction of the reserved coal, extinguish its associated rights or resolve every constituent of a coal deposit. The Court reverses the contrary title premise and leaves independently governed rights and remedies to their proper proceedings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not determine statutory or lease rights in gas separately reserved by another instrument, methane migrated into non-coal strata, quantitative source allocation, limitations or other common defenses, compensation for trespass to coal seams, actual extraction interference, surface access, tax or collusion theories, facilities claims, or remaining federal trust questions. No damages award or general extraction license follows.

The coal-access provisions continue to operate in their own terms. Under the 1909 Act, entry to prospect for, mine or remove coal requires the patent owner's prior consent or court-determined conditions securing and paying all resulting damage to that owner. The patent owner may mine coal for domestic use on the land before federal disposal of the deposit. Locators, selectors and entrymen retain a hearing on land character; satisfactory final proof of good faith and legal compliance supports an unreserved patent unless the land is then shown chiefly valuable for coal. Under the 1910 Act, a person qualified to acquire federal coal deposits or mining rights may prospect after the Secretary approves security for all resulting crop and improvement damage. One who has acquired the deposit or mining right may occupy the surface as reasonably required for mining and removal upon paying the owner's damage or providing sufficient security in a competent court's damages proceeding. Domestic coal use on the land remains available before federal disposal, and applications challenging a coal classification to obtain an unreserved patent retain prompt consideration. The Court does not convert these provisions into a gas-access code.
TREATMENT
- Western Nuclear is distinguished because its reservation covered coal and other minerals. Its sovereign-grant canon remains available for genuine uncertainty within the statutory transaction; it does not add a distinct gas reservation after this term's meaning is resolved.
- Leo Sheep supplies fidelity to the estate Congress actually conveyed and reserved; a general land-grant policy cannot enlarge the reservation beyond the enacted arrangement.
- Yankton's transaction-specific approach is consistent with preserving the original reservation's limits and questions concerning other interests; it does not itself decide this mineral-title question.
- Bourland concerns the consequences of a different federal land transaction for regulatory authority. Its distinction between transferred interests and residual rights is preserved, without importing its regulatory result into methane ownership.
LAW
The original federal coal reservations, and the Tribe's succession to them, do not by themselves establish title to the distinct coalbed methane at issue. This construction does not reduce the coal estate's independently protected rights or adjudicate a different gas reservation. Ownership, lawful access, extraction injury and compensation remain separate questions. No precedent is overruled, and no general rule governing every mineral or every later-developed use is announced.

FILE osullivan_v_boerckel_merits_1999-06-07.md
**Case and dockets:** O'Sullivan v. Boerckel; No. 97-2048; Seventh Circuit judgment, 135 F.3d 1194.
O'Sullivan v. Boerckel, No. 97-2048. Merits decision, June 7, 1999. Argued March 30, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, 135 F.3d 1194. The question is whether a prisoner must present federal claims in the ordinary petition for discretionary review to the State's highest court before seeking federal habeas relief. Boerckel presented claims concerning knowing and intelligent Miranda waiver, confession voluntariness and evidentiary sufficiency to the Illinois intermediate appellate court but omitted them from his petition for leave to appeal to the Illinois Supreme Court. Other claims were included in that petition. The Seventh Circuit held the three omitted claims were not defaulted for this reason and remanded; it did not decide their merits. His federal application was filed in September 1994 and amended in March 1995.
JUDGMENT
Reversed and remanded, 6–3. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Opposing: Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. The three omitted claims are procedurally defaulted because their ordinary Illinois review opportunity has expired, subject to the governing exceptions and the bounded further review stated below. The Court awards no writ, new trial, release or new evidentiary hearing and decides no underlying federal claim.
HOLDINGS
#### I. An available ordinary discretionary petition is part of one complete round

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the pre-AEDPA law governing this application, a prisoner must fairly present each federal claim through one complete round of the State's established ordinary appellate process, including a petition for discretionary review to its highest court when that petition is an ordinary available step. Former section 2254(b) preserves exceptions for absence of a state corrective process or circumstances making that process ineffective to protect the applicant's rights; actual state-law unavailability must be respected, and the rule does not require every extraordinary remedy or repetitive collateral presentation after a complete ordinary round.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, commands six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh makes former chapter 153 law govern Boerckel's pre-enactment application. Former section 2254(c) turns on a state-law right to raise the question through an available procedure, not a right to obtain a merits decision. Illinois Rule 315 supplies a regular petition for leave to appeal. Its selective criteria—importance, conflict, supervisory need and finality—allow the Illinois Supreme Court to choose cases; they do not make the ordinary filing unavailable. The State's highest court must have its own opportunity to consider each federal claim. The contrary argument stresses the burden of repeating rejected claims in a selective system. But selection belongs to the state court, and the ordinary petition gives it the choice. Picard supplies fair presentation; it does not permit omission of an otherwise required ordinary step. Castille involved a claim first raised through discretionary review and therefore does not mechanically answer whether an already presented claim must pass through the next ordinary stage. Lonchar requires the governing procedural sources, not a generalized finality preference. Here Illinois supplied the ordinary leave procedure and no established directive excluded it from the process. Mere discretion and unlikely acceptance do not establish unavailability. Petitioning this Court is not a substitute for giving the State's own highest court the opportunity, and no equivalent federal-certiorari exhaustion requirement follows.

#### II. Expiration of the omitted opportunity produces default, with existing gateways preserved

**Holding and operative rule:** When a prisoner omits a claim from an otherwise required ordinary state-review step and the opportunity to use that step has expired, the claim is procedurally defaulted rather than awaiting exhaustion through a still-available remedy. Boerckel's three omitted claims fall within that rule, subject to recognized cause-and-prejudice and actual-innocence gateways at their existing scope; further proceedings must respect the actual prior gateway rulings and the governing pre-AEDPA law.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; the same six-Justice coalition directly adopts the classification and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Coleman distinguishes failure to exhaust an available remedy from default when a required remedy has closed. The three claims were omitted from an ordinary petition and their opportunity has expired. The Seventh Circuit's contrary premise therefore cannot support further consideration without addressing default. Gray and Lambrix preserve claim-specific procedural treatment; a ruling on these claims does not decide all claims in the application. Nor does default eliminate the recognized gateways. The district court invited a cause showing, and Boerckel instead pursued actual innocence. It heard evidence in July 1996, incorrectly invoked AEDPA to exclude it, and alternatively concluded that evidence of other participants did not establish Boerckel's absence. Because the court of appeals found no default, it did not resolve any properly preserved challenge to that gateway treatment. Such review must apply pre-AEDPA law and consider the significance of the alternative ground. The Court makes no new finding of innocence, credibility, cause, prejudice or forfeiture, changes no gateway burden and orders no second hearing. The remand permits only further proceedings consistent with these existing procedural conditions; the constitutional merits remain undecided.

#### III. The effect of an express state opt-out retaining occasional review remains open

**Reservation and operative limit:** The Court does not decide whether a State may expressly identify an occasionally available discretionary procedure as outside its standard review process and state that prisoners need not invoke it before federal habeas. No such Illinois directive is established here. The decision neither requires a State to eliminate every possibility of occasional discretionary relief nor conclusively defines what an effective express opt-out must contain.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. It is a five-Justice reservation, not an adopted rule resolving the hypothetical procedure's availability. Stone-Zsela does not join this reservation.

### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding and operative rule:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutes use fraud as a legal term with settled content. Morissette supplies the principle that Congress ordinarily carries that established meaning into the statute; the absence of a separate materiality adjective does not strip fraud of its significance requirement. Durland includes fraudulent future promises, but does not make an inconsequential falsehood a fraudulent scheme or import every element of a completed civil tort. The statutory focus on schemes, and §1344's inclusion of attempts, explains why actual reliance and completed injury are unnecessary. Mail or wire use remains a distinct requirement, and the communication need not itself contain the falsehood if it executes the scheme.

Section 1344 separately covers executing or attempting a scheme to defraud a covered institution and executing or attempting a scheme to obtain its property by false or fraudulent pretenses. Both routes retain materiality for the deceptive schemes presented here; their other possible differences, including mental-state requirements in other applications, remain open. Wells construes the different wording of §1014, which does not require objective materiality in addition to its false-statement or overvaluation and purpose-to-influence requirements. It does not turn fraud language into an unrestricted false-statement prohibition. A purpose to influence under §1014 does not automatically establish the separate fraud element.

The natural-tendency formulation, reflected in Kungys, concerns the targeted decision in context. The Court does not exhaust every common-law formulation, unusual recipient preference, omission duty, or nonrepresentational scheme. Nor does it decide materiality under every differently worded offense. The judgment rejecting materiality under the three fraud provisions is reversed.

### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding and operative rule:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

**Controlling explanation:** Gaudin assigns the application of an actual element to the jury. Section 7206(1) independently supplies materiality through its express prohibition of willfully subscribing a return declared under penalties of perjury that the signer does not believe true and correct as to every material matter. Part I establishes the element under the fraud statutes. The express tax and bank directions withheld that application, while the mail and wire charges omitted it. The judge's separate materiality ruling is not a jury finding.

Chapman places the burden on the Government for these preserved constitutional errors. Failure to offer a separately labeled factual materiality defense supplies neither waiver nor forfeiture and does not require Neder to prove innocence. Johnson's refusal to correct forfeited error under the fourth condition of plain-error review does not govern these preserved objections. Genuine findings elsewhere in an actual verdict can establish an equivalent determination; whether they do so here, and whether other supported inferences suffice, are distinct questions addressed in the following parts. Neither the Court's recognition of error nor Gaudin alone decides those remedial questions.

### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding and operative rule:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

**Controlling explanation:** Roy permits examination of a particular omitted element without converting every omission into a structural defect requiring automatic reversal. Its qualified method concerns the error's effect on the jury that returned this verdict. Yates supplies the instructional-focus requirement: a reviewing court must examine whether the instructions directed the jury to evidence supporting the proposed inference, rather than assume the jury decided a question removed from its task. Necessary equivalent findings are sufficient, but not invariably indispensable to a reliable noncontribution conclusion.

Sullivan addresses a different defect, an invalid reasonable-doubt standard vitiating all factual findings. These instructions left an otherwise valid reasonable-doubt standard governing the submitted elements. Their express removal of materiality heightens the need to justify the connection; it does not itself destroy every remaining finding or forbid the inquiry Roy allows. Gaudin's allocation rule remains fully operative at trial.

Powerful and unchallenged evidence may support an inference, but neither label creates an automatic safe harbor. A reviewing court must assess defense disputes for what they concern and determine whether the actual findings resolve them. Sufficient evidence for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction is not the required showing. Mitchell likewise distinguishes actual contribution from enough other evidence to sustain a result. This holding does not classify every omitted element, constructive amendment, or total denial of jury trial. Richardson's reserved harmlessness questions concerning its distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect remain unresolved.

### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. **Authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

It did not permit conviction for an unidentified collateral entry. The jury considered the defenses that the receipts were repayable loans and that professional advice or good faith negated culpability. The verdict does not resolve every subsidiary advice dispute, but its particular subject and culpability findings furnish the connection required by Roy's actual-contribution inquiry. Annual income is information used to determine income-tax liability and administer the returns.

The objection is substantial: the jury was expressly forbidden to decide materiality, so a court must not supply that missing decision. The Court does not suppose that jurors disobeyed the instruction, treat the judge's ruling as theirs, or declare that they necessarily made the omitted evaluation. It instead asks what effect that withdrawal had on an otherwise valid verdict about these identified income statements. The conclusion requires no unanimous numerical finding of the alleged additional income, acceptance of every transaction or witness, unpaid-tax calculation, actual IRS response, or successful deception. ### The first condition includes a proved functional ability-to-elect route

For a claim by one protected group, the first Gingles condition may be met either through the retained district-majority route or through rigorous proof that a lawful, geographically compact proposed district would provide the cohesive group dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its candidates of choice. Speculation, an isolated favorable election, near-majority population arithmetic or mere influence does not suffice; the relevant majority-bloc showing, causal connection and independent totality remain necessary.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this express modification and its limits.

The first condition links the challenged electoral arrangement to an achievable opportunity to elect. Numerical majority provides a predictable demonstration of that capacity, but an exclusive number can reject the same actual opportunity when dependable voting evidence proves it. Section 2 protects equal participation and opportunity, without guaranteeing proportional outcomes. The Court therefore expressly modifies the numerical exclusivity reflected in Gingles and Growe; it does not declare that earlier reservations already adopted this alternative.

De Grandy rejects numerical maximization and conclusive safe harbors while preserving actual local opportunity. Brooks fits proof to a different nomination barrier. Those decisions support the inquiry's purpose but do not themselves decide this new district-capacity rule. Dependability matters because a contingent alliance or favorable candidate alone cannot establish lost opportunity attributable to the system. Plaintiffs must prove cohesion and actual voting behavior within the proposed district and the relevant majority-bloc defeat in the challenged arrangement; successful crossover in one setting does not silently negate or establish bloc defeat in another. The majority route survives. So do lawful district population and geography, constitutional limits and the separate totality inquiry. States retain lawful choice among effective arrangements; a court-compelled remedy still requires an established violation.

The accepted 47.9% and 48.3% Hispanic citizen voting-age shares neither prove nor foreclose dependable ability to elect. **Authority:** The same five Justices join Stone-Zsela's Part I and this application and remedy.

The proposed district must be lawful in population and geography; an 8.1% underpopulation is not approved merely because it appears in the calculation. If admissible proof does not establish dependable opportunity, the claim fails. If capacity is established, the remaining conditions and unequal opportunity under totality still require determination. Voinovich preserves the difference between state choice and judicially compelled remedy; De Grandy permits neither automatic liability nor a numerical safe harbor. The remand commands no map or voting system and guarantees no favorable result.

The credited proof of concrete post-census housing, occupancy and growth changes supports the present demographic findings under clear-error review. Evidence previously immaterial under an exclusive numerical threshold may bear on the changed inquiry. The trial court may determine whether fair supplementation is needed under ordinary rules, with adequate notice and response; reopening is neither automatic nor forbidden. No new trial, mandatory study or automatic admission of the excluded survey is ordered.

Jones v. United States, No. 97-9361. Argued February 22, 1999; decided June 21, 1999. ### Actual sentencing deadlock requires a lawful nondeath sentence

When completed penalty deliberations end without a unanimous recommendation, §3594's otherwise clause requires the judge to impose a lawful nondeath sentence. For this death-resulting kidnapping the alternative is life without release. Section 3593(b)(2)(C) does not authorize another attempt to obtain death solely because the jury deadlocked.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I-A has eight direct votes from all Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition without joining that rationale.

Section 3593(e) requires a unanimous jury recommendation. Section 3594 directs the judge to impose recommended death or life without release and otherwise to impose an authorized lesser sentence. The Government's construction would confine otherwise to a unanimous lesser recommendation and use good-cause discharge to repeat the penalty hearing. That reading denies operative effect to the nondeath command when the jury cannot agree. Good-cause discharge has independent work, including disqualification, without treating failure of agreement itself as authorization for another death effort. Section 1201's offense-specific penalty fixes the available nondeath sentence; §3593's general reference to other lesser sentences creates none for this offense.

The Government's concession that this offense permits only death or life does not itself concede the deadlock question; the Court rejects the contrary new-jury construction on the statutes. Neither the Eighth Amendment nor a supervisory rule requires a fixed deadlock-consequence instruction in every capital case. In this case, however, the preserved request for an accurate account of the lawful consequence required correction of the charge's materially misleading below-life alternative; refusing that correction requires a new penalty proceeding.

The Court does not require the request verbatim, a nonunanimous jury life verdict or a reasonable-doubt burden for the ultimate moral choice. **Authority:** These accounts identify the limits of the Court's decision, not additional factor holdings. No Marks synthesis supplies a validity or cure rule from the divided positions.

Overlap is not automatically unconstitutional, relevant victim-impact evidence is not categorically forbidden, and remaining valid eligibility does not by itself cure an independent selection error. The Court creates no new factor test, appellate moral-reweighing power or permission to repeat misleading instructions.

### 1. The enacted fee language supplies no command repricing completed services

### Probable cause and ready mobility require no additional emergency

A readily mobile automobile may be searched without a warrant when supported by probable cause; officers need not additionally show unforeseen urgency, imminent departure or insufficient opportunity to obtain a warrant. The accepted probable cause and moving Toyota satisfy that rule for the federal ground on which the state judgment rests, despite the earlier 13–14-hour warrant opportunity.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

Labron/Kilgore directly rejects the added warrant-impracticability condition. Carroll and Chambers establish automobile authority based on its settled predicates; Carney also recognizes the reduced privacy of vehicles in their regulated public setting. Johns makes custody alone insufficient to eliminate that authority. Earlier investigative time cannot restore the extra requirement rejected by those cases. This record supplies both actual automobile movement and accepted abundant probable cause, rather than merely a citation or an unverified suspicion.

The rule does not dispense with probable cause, justify searches of persons, authorize entry onto protected premises or resolve known mechanical incapacity. Knowles's rejection of a citation-only search leaves this independent probable-cause ground intact. No new balancing or exception is required, and this is an application of the rule to the actual erroneous suppression ground rather than an instruction to reconsider the same added condition.

### The object sought supplies the relevant scope limit

The cocaine object permits the challenged search of a trunk duffel capable of containing it within the justified automobile search. This application creates no authority to search a passenger's person or decide a different known passenger-owned-effect dispute.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela does not join this specific-container application.

Ross limits automobile search scope to places capable of containing the object supported by probable cause. The trunk duffel searched for cocaine satisfies that limit here. Houghton's separate passenger-effects disagreement supplies no additional automobile-exigency requirement, and no such ownership issue is established in this case. The Court neither infers a personal search nor decides a protected-premises or different execution claim. It preserves only genuinely distinct issues that remain properly available; it requires no new factual hearing to reconsider the settled automobile predicates.

### Reversal leaves only genuinely remaining matters for ordinary state proceedings

The state appellate judgment is reversed and the case remanded consistently with these holdings. Correction of the actual federal error does not automatically establish admissibility on every possible ground or a particular ultimate prosecution result.

**Authority:** Part IV is joined by the seven Justices supporting reversal.

Labron/Kilgore distinguishes application of the federal automobile rule from adjudication of every independent state or search-execution question. The State must follow the federal rule on remand and cannot reimpose the rejected urgency condition as federal law. An actually independent state ground or distinct properly preserved issue may receive its ordinary lawful treatment, but the Court establishes neither its existence nor its merit. No conviction, acquittal, new trial, reopening of a forfeited claim or new hearing is commanded.
TREATMENT
- Labron/Kilgore: applies its settled automobile rule, Cox/Long framework and bounded remand; no additional federal exigency is introduced.
- Cox Broadcasting v. Cohn: applies the third practical-finality category, without creating a general interlocutory-suppression exception.
- Michigan v. Long and Arizona v. Evans: apply review of the actual federal ground and preserve genuinely independent state law; possible greater state protection is not itself an existing jurisdictional bar.
- Carroll, Chambers, Carney and Johns: retain automobile probable cause and ready mobility without an extra warrant-opportunity condition; independent delay and protected-premises limits remain.
- United States v. Ross: applies object-based scope to the cocaine search of the duffel.
- Knowles v. Iowa: distinguishes citation-only authority from independent automobile probable cause.
- Wyoming v. Houghton: preserves its specific detached passenger-effect rule; no new passenger-search issue is decided here.
LAW
The established automobile exception applies without a separate case-specific exigency or lack-of-time requirement. The actual federal suppression ground cannot stand; probable cause, ready mobility and object-based limits retain their force. No precedent is overruled or new search exception created. Ordinary state procedures govern genuinely remaining issues within the Court's mandate.

FILE sutton_v_united_air_lines_inc_merits_1999-06-22.md
**Case and dockets:** Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc.; No. 97-1943; OT1998.
Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc., No. 97-1943. Argued April 28, 1999; decided June 22. On certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 130 F.3d 893, which affirmed dismissal under Rule 12(b)(6). The questions concern mitigating measures in the original ADA's actual-disability definition, the proper weight of the contrary EEOC Appendix guidance, and the sufficiency of the pleaded regarded-as-working theory.

The twin applicants allege uncorrected right-eye vision of 20/200 or worse and left-eye vision of 20/400 or worse, corrected vision of 20/20, commercial experience and first-class FAA medical certificates. United requires uncorrected visual acuity of 20/100 or better for the hiring at issue. The complaint alleges actual disability substantially limiting seeing and a perceived disability substantially limiting working. The lower court's assumption that United rejected the applicants from all its pilot positions is maintained for this appeal. Those allegations and assumptions are not adjudicated findings of unsafe flight, employer intent or universal airline qualification.
JUDGMENT
Affirmed, 6–3. Supporting: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Opposing: Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Breyer.

The Court affirms dismissal of both presented coverage theories. Stevens and Breyer would reverse and remand on actual disability without deciding the separate regarded-as application. Stone-Zsela would vacate the adverse coverage judgment and remand, agreeing only that exclusion from the specialized global-airline-pilot position alone does not establish a broad working limitation. The decision orders no remand, employment, reinstatement, accommodation, damages or new evidentiary proceeding and does not validate every airline hiring criterion.
HOLDINGS
### Holding 1— Measures actually used are part of individual actual-disability assessment

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the original 42 U.S.C. §12102(2)(A), an individual's actual substantial limitation must be assessed with the mitigating measures the individual actually uses, including their beneficial and adverse effects. Effective correction does not erase an impairment or categorically foreclose disability; coverage still depends on an actual substantial limitation of that person's major life activity.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court commands the complete agreement of O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six directly adopt this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The original statute requires an impairment that substantially limits the individual. The existence of an impairment and the magnitude of its present effect are distinct questions. Assessing the person as if an effective measure were absent replaces that individualized inquiry with a hypothetical condition. Conversely, acknowledging correction does not mean that treatment always removes substantial limitation: residual restrictions and adverse effects remain part of the person's actual condition. Bragdon required individual substantial limitation and left this general mitigation issue open because even the treated transmission danger supported Abbott's specific reproductive limitation.

The EEOC Appendix's contrary interpretation does not overcome the enacted individualized limitation requirement. Haggar requires courts to enforce statutory boundaries before accepting an agency construction. The Court therefore need not decide the general extent of EEOC authority to define disability, the force of every regulation or Appendix statement, or whether every term of the regulatory framework warrants deference. The guidance's protective aim and the Act's distinct qualification and accommodation provisions do not eliminate the threshold Congress enacted. Those latter inquiries remain separate once coverage exists.

### Holding 2 — These allegations do not state an actual substantial limitation of seeing

**Holding and operative rule:** The actual-disability theory fails where these applicants allege corrected vision and visual functioning equivalent to people without their impairment and identify no additional actual substantial limitation. Their severe uncorrected myopia and continuing dependence on corrective lenses do not, on these allegations, establish the required limitation of seeing under the governing mitigation rule.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court-opinion coalition directly adopts this application; Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Breyer reject the beneficial-mitigation premise.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court accepts the severe underlying myopia, the use of lenses and the favorable allegations concerning corrected function. It does not reject myopia as an impairment; United does not press that separate contention. The complaint's own account of normal corrected seeing supplies the decisive distinction from an impairment with actual residual limitations or adverse effects. Constant dependence on lenses, without an alleged actual substantial restriction inconsistent with that normal function, does not supply the missing limitation. The Court makes no finding that all lenses, medication or other treatment eliminate disability.

FAA certification and commercial experience do not establish statutory disability, while United's uncorrected-acuity criterion does not itself prove dangerousness or lawful exclusion. Qualification, accommodation, business necessity and direct threat are distinct matters and need not be decided after the asserted coverage theory fails. No record-of claim or regarded-as-seeing theory is created to replace the theories presented.

### Holding 3 — The alleged perceived restriction is confined to a specialized job category

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming working is a major life activity, a regarded-as-working claim requires an alleged perceived restriction in a class or broad range of jobs; exclusion from the specialized global-airline-pilot category alleged here does not suffice. The pleaded exclusion remains insufficient even under the assumption that United rejected these applicants for all of its pilot positions.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court-opinion coalition directly adopts the governing rule and this dismissal application. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the specialized-position principle in his separate dissent; Stevens and Breyer leave this application unresolved because their actual-disability disposition would require remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Perceiving an impairment is not necessarily perceiving a substantial limitation of working. The distinction reflected in 29 C.F.R. §1630.2(j) and (l) separates a class or broad range of jobs from a particular specialized occupation. The alleged perception concerns the global-airline-pilot category, while other pilot work provides context for why that category is specialized. The Court does not hold that actual employment elsewhere conclusively proves United's belief, require rejection by every employer, or decide disputed facts about its mental state. The complaint fails because the alleged perceived limitation does not reach the required breadth, even accepting the favorable scope assumption below.

This is a coverage ruling on the theory actually presented. It does not decide whether working invariably qualifies as a major life activity, whether every airline position shares the same qualification, whether United's criterion would be justified against a covered applicant, or whether a different properly presented perceived limitation would satisfy the Act.
TREATMENT
- **Bragdon v. Abbott:** Applies its individualized disability framework and resolves the general mitigation question it expressly reserved. Its reproduction holding, substantial residual-risk application and distinct Title III objective direct-threat rules remain intact.
- **United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. and Chevron:** Preserve actual delegation and statutory fit as conditions of accepting an agency construction. The contrary Appendix interpretation fails the statutory reading adopted here; the Court makes no general decision about ADA definitional authority or every EEOC regulation.
LAW
Under the original ADA, actual-disability assessment accounts for mitigating measures actually used, including their beneficial and adverse effects, while continuing to require a substantial limitation of that individual's major life activity. This resolves Bragdon's express mitigation reservation; it neither erases an impairment nor excludes every successfully treated condition. The actual-seeing allegations here fail under that rule.

Assuming working is a major life activity, the regarded-as theory presented here also fails because the alleged perception concerns a specialized global-airline-pilot category rather than a class or broad range of jobs. Separate coverage branches, individual proof, qualification and lawful defenses retain their distinct elements. No precedent is overruled; no general delegation or airline-safety rule is decided.
