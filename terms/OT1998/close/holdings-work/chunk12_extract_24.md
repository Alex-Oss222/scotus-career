## Holdings

### This vertical supplier substitution is not a per se group boycott

**Operative rule:** A single buyer's agreement to purchase services from one supplier instead of another, without horizontal concerted exclusion or another established per se basis, is not a per se group boycott merely because it intentionally injures a rival, serves a fraudulent purpose or lacks an apparent efficiency justification. The pleaded vertical purchasing arrangement must therefore be assessed under the rule of reason on the surviving §1 competitive-effects theory; genuine horizontal restraints, actual price fixing and other established categories retain their distinct requirements.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this classification and its limitations.

**Controlling explanation:** Per se treatment dispenses with inquiry into actual competitive effects only for a sufficiently established class of restraints. Klor's involved coordinated exclusion of a rival through a structure supporting that treatment. Business Electronics, Northwest Wholesale Stationers and Indiana Federation of Dentists prevent the label group boycott from replacing the required inquiry into the agreement's character. Khan likewise preserves competitive-effects review when the characteristics of a vertical restraint do not justify automatic condemnation, while leaving genuine horizontal and price-agreement rules distinct.

MECo purchased removal services for its affiliated telephone company; it did not supply those services or compete in their overall market. Its separate incorporation and resale to the affiliate do not supply a horizontal agreement among removal-service competitors. The allegation that it preferred AT&T Technologies to Discon describes the vertical supplier choice at issue, not concerted exclusion among competing suppliers.

Discon's strongest argument is that choosing a more expensive supplier to destroy a rival has no plausible competitive benefit. The alleged purpose may matter in ordinary review, but reprehensible motive does not establish the class of restraint required for automatic condemnation. Otherwise any buyer's questionable supplier choice could become a per se boycott without proof that competition was reduced. The Court rejects that classification; it neither finds the purchasing arrangement reasonable nor immunizes independently actionable fraud or exclusion.

### Regulatory fraud and harm to one supplier do not replace competitive injury

**Operative rule:** Regulatory deception, consumer overcharges and elimination of one supplier do not by themselves establish that the challenged purchasing agreement injured the competitive process; Discon must establish the competitive injury and other elements of the theory actually pursued. The lower courts must reconsider the surviving §1 effects claim and the derivative rationale for the distinct §2 conspiracy claim without treating the rejected per se classification or fraud alone as sufficient, while leaving previously dismissed claims outside this remand.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the requirement, its application and the bounded remand for both affected components.

**Controlling explanation:** Brunswick and Atlantic Richfield require antitrust injury rather than loss to a competitor alone. Consumer harm may arise when a regulated monopolist uses deception to charge more through its existing position, without that harm resulting from diminished rivalry in the removal-services market. The allegations of inflated purchases, rebates and Discon's elimination therefore do not themselves establish the competitive mechanism necessary for the antitrust theory.

That distinction is not a finding that the arrangement was harmless. Kodak requires attention to actual competitive conditions rather than an unsupported categorical assumption, and Khan preserves liability for restraints shown to have harmful competitive effects outside a rejected per se category. The lower court had retained a §1 effects inquiry as well as possible per se treatment. Its error in allowing the latter supplies no automatic judgment against every version of the former.

The separately reinstated §2 conspiracy claim must likewise be reconsidered insofar as it relies on the rejected premises concerning the same purchasing practices. Spectrum Sports preserves the elements of the actual offense; an attempted-monopolization requirement cannot simply be transferred to conspiracy. The Court decides neither that conspiracy claim's ultimate sufficiency nor a final market-power or causation fact. The limited grant does not authorize wholesale dismissal of all remaining theories or reopening of the already dismissed vertical-price-fixing, monopoly, attempt and RICO components. No damages or injunction follows from this classification decision.

## Precedent Treatment

- Klor's, Inc. v. Broadway-Hale Stores, Inc.: distinguishes the coordinated exclusionary structure supporting its group-boycott rule from this vertical supplier substitution.
- Business Electronics Corp. v. Sharp Electronics Corp.: applies the distinction between the character of the agreement and an exclusionary label or purpose; actual price-agreement and horizontal questions retain their own requirements.
- Northwest Wholesale Stationers v. Pacific Stationery & Printing Co.: preserves the need for a restraint fitting an established category before automatic boycott condemnation.
- FTC v. Indiana Federation of Dentists: preserves attention to the restraint's actual character and competition rather than extending per se treatment from a label.
- State Oil Co. v. Khan (November 4, 1997): applies its category-based reason for competitive-effects review without changing its distinct vertical maximum-price holding or granting immunity to harmful restraints.
- Brunswick Corp. v. Pueblo Bowl-O-Mat and Atlantic Richfield Co. v. USA Petroleum Co.: preserve the independent requirement of antitrust injury; competitor loss alone is insufficient.
- Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services: preserves inquiry into actual competitive conditions without finding market power or injury here.
- Spectrum Sports v. McQuillan: preserves offense-specific elements; attempted monopolization is not merged with conspiracy to monopolize.

## Law After Decision

The pleaded vertical supplier switch cannot proceed as a per se group boycott on fraud, favoritism or rival harm alone. The surviving §1 claim requires the ordinary competitive-effects inquiry and antitrust injury. The distinct §2 conspiracy claim remains for proper consideration, including correction of any dependence on the rejected premises. The Court finds neither a competitive injury nor competitive harmlessness and creates no immunity for regulated firms or fraud. Dismissed claims remain outside the remand.
