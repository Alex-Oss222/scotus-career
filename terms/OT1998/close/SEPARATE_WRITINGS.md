## 1998-10-05 — United States v. Wright; Eleventh Circuit No. 95-8397; judgment below, 117 F.3d 1265. No separately established Supreme Court docket.

**Stevens, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** Part A, joined by all seven other Associates, independently sustains the pipe-bomb registration conviction under Hale's tax-administration and registration rules. Registration identifies and traces lawful making and transfer; personal possession and military usefulness do not establish an exemption. No denied application or legal inability to register these devices is established, and no universal late-registration duty is imposed. The eight adopt that sufficient ground without requiring exclusion of every explosive device from coverage.

In Part B, joined by Breyer, Stevens joins the commerce, personal-invocation, Ninth Amendment and sentencing grounds and agrees that the machinegun affirmance must return for the actual arms application. Love, Hale and Rybar already require that result; they would leave selection of a comprehensive severity framework until a developed application requires it. They do not join the categorical home rule or the three tiers. Stevens emphasizes that supported legislative predictions and the particular statutory operation must receive their proper weight. Breyer shares that reservation because the competing individual and public interests must be examined in their concrete setting. Neither treats public safety as conclusive, restores a militia prerequisite, requires an evidentiary study or would disturb the separate registration conviction. Their disagreement concerns the governing framework, not any judgment component.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** They join the same specified common portions and all dispositions, but withhold assent to Part II-B and its applications. Love's personal-holder rule and Hale's weapon-specific inquiry require a constitutional answer grounded in text, history and the actual restriction. They do not regard those authorities as warranting a universal severity scale or its separate categorical home formulation in this machinegun case. Their objection does not imply that ordinary defensive possession can be abolished; they reserve the precise historical boundary rather than join this formulation. Miller makes military usefulness relevant without granting military-weapon immunity. They would remand under those existing directions and sustain registration under Hale, preserving the distinction between the two counts. Neither would affirm the machinegun count without its required arms application or award automatic acceptance credit.

## 1998-10-07 — Fraternal Order of Police v. United States; D.C. Circuit No. 97-5304; judgment below, 152 F.3d 998.

**Justice Scalia, concurring, joined by Justice Thomas.** They join the Court's opinion in full. They retain their Wright objection to a general tiered methodology and their preference for text and history, but accept Wright as the governing rule. The present restriction rests on adjudicated violent conduct, process safeguards and the practical availability of an official weapon, rather than denial of personal protection or an unbounded public-safety assertion. That concrete ground supports the Court's intermediate-scrutiny application without an absolute official-use entitlement. Their concurrence decides no broader category of disqualified persons.

**Justice Stevens, concurring, joined by Justice Breyer.** They join the Court's opinion in full. Their Wright reservation concerned announcing broad categories before the relevant burdens were presented. This case presents a defined conviction class and an actual official-access claim. The relationship between the adjudicated domestic violence and armed recurrence, together with the statutory safeguards, supplies the concrete justification required by Wright. They emphasize that this application establishes no automatic rule for every misdemeanor or every individual private-possession dispute.

## 1998-10-09 — Hiley v. Barrett; Eleventh Circuit No. 97-9006; 155 F.3d 1276. No separately established Supreme Court docket.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment, unjoined.** He agrees with the entire affirmance on his stated grounds without joining the Court's opinion. He preserves a distinct personal-right and classification ground. *Love* protects persons but does not make adjudicated violent conduct irrelevant. The force, attempted-force or deadly-weapon predicate within a domestic relationship, together with counsel and jury-where-entitled protections and the statute's relief provisions, connects the disability to preventing armed recurrence. A misdemeanor label and armed employment do not eliminate that connection. Hiley's particular predicate is treated as qualifying here; no new conviction is invented.

He would not rewrite retained civil rights as restored rights merely because state systems differ or some misdemeanors caused no initial rights loss. That statutory position goes beyond the Court's decision to leave the never-lost-rights construction unresolved. His personal-right discussion supplies his alternative ground for rejecting the preserved classification challenge; it does not adjudicate an arms cause or revive procedural due process. He recognizes that a preserved Second Amendment challenge would be governed by Wright's Core-Preservation Review as Fraternal Order of Police applies it; Hiley abandoned that claim, so no further arms question is decided here. He approves no disarmament based on any misdemeanor, an allegation or a nonviolent status, and leaves a genuinely different as-applied record open.

He retains jurisdiction because successful federal relief would restore Hiley's armed-deputy assignment, while respecting the local settlement. His reasoning preserves the identified supply-and-possession mechanism, the distinct rejection of a state-administration command, and the prospective, nonpunitive character of this restriction. He would neither issue a criminal judgment, order reinstatement, reopen settled claims nor decide a future prosecution's notice defense or a claimed breach of the settlement.

## 1998-10-15 — Peoples Rights Organization, Inc. v. City of Columbus; Sixth Circuit Nos. 96-3468 and 96-3495; 152 F.3d 522. No separately established Supreme Court docket.

**Justice Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part, unjoined.** He joins the Court's threshold, fault, definition and noncity-exception analyses and its common severability principles. He would affirm invalidity of both former-city-registration selectors. His Nordlinger objection to entrenched ownership and family preferences and his Heller and Romer positions require a meaningful relation to an impartial public purpose. Lawful pre-existing owners who registered and those unable to identify the invalid old duty are alike in the ownership reliance invoked here. Owners first covered by the replacement ordinance had no meaningful opportunity to enter the only registry. For him those defects disconnect the closed registry from the claimed protection, even though actual registration occurred. A clear prospective cutoff or an opportunity for all covered prior owners would present a different case. He does not require universal grandfathering, invalidate the independent NFA or inert-object exceptions, or design a replacement scheme.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part, unjoined.** He would affirm the Sixth Circuit's relief against all five challenged definition categories and both former-city-registration distinctions, while sustaining the remaining NFA magazine exception. Smolak and Walker's actual possession, closed registration window and threatened prosecution justify present review; P.R.O. seeks common relief for similarly situated members. No advisory judgment about a wholly hypothetical weapon is warranted.

In his view, the rifle definition does not choose among actual magazine possession, commercial availability and merely possible manufacture; the shotgun definition does not select a measuring shell length. Undefined modification and inaccessible original handgun-design history compound the handgun defect. Restoration and parts provisions inherit those uncertainties and add uncertain assembly conditions. Criminal exposure without a clear culpability safeguard makes those defects consequential for ordinary owners and grants enforcement discretion the text does not constrain. The City's conflicting suggested knowledge standard cannot rewrite the ordinance. He therefore does not adopt the Court's default-fault and definition constructions. Love reinforces intelligible notice for protected keeping but establishes neither a right to every listed weapon nor a judicially chosen capacity limit.

He rejects grandfathering based on success in guessing an invalid 1989 registration duty. That objection reaches section 2323.31(B)(3) and the matching city clause in section 2323.32(B)(2). NFA registration instead supplies an independent, intelligible reliance basis. Its broader coverage than magazines individually used with the registered firearm survives ordinary rationality under Romer without exact tailoring. He also agrees with the judgment sustaining the separate related inert-object magazine exception, adding no ground on that component. He would implement the declaratory and injunctive relief for the invalid provisions, preserve separable valid exceptions, and enact neither damages nor a fresh registry. He decides no unpresented substantive arms claim and joins no part of the Court's opinion.

## 1998-10-22 — United States v. Franklyn / United States v. Gonzalez; Second Circuit Nos. 97-1392 and 97-1427; 157 F.3d 90. No separate Supreme Court docket is established.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment, unjoined.** He agrees that the consolidated judgments should be affirmed while keeping each appellant's issues separate. Franklyn's completed automatic weapon falls within the national stock restriction: without the possession rule, prohibited new stock could be retained and circulated beyond transfer enforcement. Hale, Rybar and Kirk therefore supply the fitted mechanism, without a new interstate-travel element or a general claim that local violence affects national productivity. He supplies no Second Amendment ruling.

He regards the late strike objection, made after the venire members had been excused, as a genuine preservation problem because the ordinary opportunity for immediate correction was lost. His counsel analysis also depends on the reported alternative determination actually crediting the prosecutor's explanations in context. Hernandez permits respect for that supported ultimate finding; neutral wording alone would not suffice. On that finding, earlier objection would not probably have produced a different outcome and Strickland prejudice is unproved. If the actual transcript showed only facial neutrality, the alternative ground could not substitute for a missing ultimate determination. He preserves that condition and supplies no missing juror explanation.

For Gonzalez's sentence, jointly undertaken conduct and foreseeability must connect the machinegun and discharge to him; physical proximity alone cannot do so. The ammunition and coordinated episode supply the reported basis. Actual discharge adds danger not exhausted by the possession calculation, supporting section 5K2.6. Separately, prior weapons and drug conduct insufficiently reflected in the initial criminal-history score can support section 4A1.3 if the selected category is explained. Koon permits respect for the contextual decision while forbidding an unexplained increase or repeated punishment for an already-counted factor. He adds no factual finding, increases no sentence and adopts no automatic rule for all ammunition offenders.

He also agrees with the dispositions of Gonzalez's separate section 922(g) commerce objection and the other-trial-evidence and within-range-selection components, adding no grounds on those components. No new trial or resentencing follows from the reported record. He joins no part of the Court's opinion.

## 1998-11-10 — Coalition for Economic Equity v. Wilson; Ninth Circuit Nos. 97-15030 and 97-15031; 122 F.3d 692. No separately established Supreme Court docket.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, agrees that voluntary-action permission does not establish blanket Title VII preemption, but would affirm vacatur of the preliminary injunction. The dissent treats Proposition 209 as a statewide substantive restriction on officials' classifications, leaving ordinary antidiscrimination protection available. Crawford preserves the State's choice to withdraw optional policies; lawful remedies under Hopwood and Engineering Contractors are not compulsory policies. Hunter concerned unequal access to ordinary racial protection, and Seattle concerned specially displaced desegregation authority. The reciprocal sex provision likewise does not establish the asserted facial denial of equal protection. This position preserves scrutiny of actual unequal administration, binding federal obligations and properly presented later applications, and does not declare every affirmative measure unconstitutional.

## 1998-11-12 — Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. v. Kravitz, No. 97-1768.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring; no joins.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's knowing-deception holding and affirmance. He would also hold that reckless, materially misleading acceptance of customer orders under the existing best-execution undertaking can constitute primary § 10(b) and Rule 10b-5 fraud when every ordinary private-action requirement is proved. Acceptance communicates an intention to honor that undertaking; an actionable false representation need not be confined to a defendant's express statement. His broader culpability position goes beyond the sufficient ground the Court adopts and does not become the Court's rule.

He agrees that best execution depends on terms reasonably available under contemporary market conditions, that industry custom informs but does not conclusively resolve culpability, and that later SEC rules cannot create retroactive liability. Later market studies may corroborate evolving feasibility without supplying a retroactive legal command. He would leave knowing or reckless falsity, reliance and actual damage for case-specific proof. Neither breach of fiduciary duty alone, execution at the national best bid and offer nor the existence of a better price automatically establishes fraud, and the concurrence recognizes no reliance presumption from price disparity.

## 1998-11-24 — Wisconsin Right to Life, Inc. v. Paradise, No. 98-118; Seventh Circuit No. 97-3668.

Scalia dissents, joined by Thomas. They accept that a credible threatened injury may support review before prosecution and that a facial invalidity claim differs from an advisory instruction about future litigation. They would nevertheless affirm dismissal because the existing official and private-enforcement facts already defeat this action. The officials agree that WRTL's comparative guides do not require registration, and no threat directed at WRTL is shown. ## 1998-12-01 — Minnesota v. Carter and Minnesota v. Johns, No. 97-1147.

**Breyer, Part II, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** These four would conclusively hold that this observation was not a search. The trial findings and testimony establish an unenclosed area actually used by residents and nonresidents, including access beside the window for bicycles, and a view obtainable by natural sight without touching the window, lighting it or using equipment. The initial position near the projecting brickwork already disclosed the table and packaging; it was a window-side position, not a proven sidewalk view. The later move gave a closer view of the same activity.

They assume in respondents' favor the disputed climbing and crouching. Crossing shrubs not shown to enclose the space and adjusting posture do not, by themselves, turn the actual natural view from this ordinarily used position into a search. The approximate 12–18-inch finding is no constitutional minimum, and uncertain gap geometry, the exact crossing of one entrance walk and the asserted failure to seek rehearing are unnecessary to this answer. They do not find the disputed acts occurred or discredit the appellate account through photographs.

Fields's limited-sharing rule remains controlling: cotenant visibility alone is not publication. Their answer rests on the fuller actual-use record, including ordinary nonresident use, and the natural view. Pinson and Myers protect otherwise concealed information and do not require disregarding what this opening actually exposed. Drawn blinds, sustained proximity, the nonordinary entrance route and the disputed bodily movements are serious contrary facts, but the four regard the actual access and unenhanced view as sufficient together. Their rationale would not resolve opening or moving blinds, crossing a protected enclosure, intruding into the dwelling or using equipment to obtain otherwise unavailable information. It creates no common-yard immunity, fixed distance or time rule, or exterior-location exception.

They accept vacatur and ordinary remand as sufficient removal of the adverse judgments while publishing that conclusive view. They do not join O'Connor's reason for further actual-vantage analysis. They accept that the absence of a Court search holding leaves lawful further consideration available; they order neither automatic admission nor unconditional conviction reinstatement. Breyer also joins the Court's affirmative personal-interest holding. The other three retain the contrary privacy position expressed in O'Connor's Part A.

**O'Connor, concurring in the judgment and dissenting in part.** In Part A, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, O'Connor would reject the accepted invitation-duration-task basis for personal privacy. Fields protects actual independent control and, separately, developed recurring residential sharing. Permission for this brief compensated task does not establish either relationship in their view. Their conclusion rests on the supported combination, not a rule that illegal activity forfeits rights, that commerce is inherently unprotected, that only an owner has privacy or that an overnight visit is indispensable. A temporary rental carrying control or genuinely shared residential or social relationship could differ.

Carter's counsel's key affidavit cannot be treated as nonexistent or as an accepted control finding. A properly usable finding of authorized independent access could change the application of Fields's control branch. Its procedural status, weight and actual permission implications are not decided; no new hearing or revived waiver is ordered. Johns has no corresponding proffer. Breyer does not join this personal-interest disagreement.

In Part B, O'Connor alone would resolve the actual-view question through renewed source-accurate analysis once the Court recognizes personal privacy. Ordinary access, the standing natural view, bicycle storage, nonresident use and lack of enclosure support the lawful-view route; screening, prolonged proximity and the position outside ordinary entrance passage support the search route. Her Fields plurality and Riley qualification preserve the claimant's burden without equating limited sharing with publication. If the constitutional answer requires rejecting an appellate factual characterization, factual-review and preservation objections must actually be addressed. If the same answer follows while assuming the disputed additions, that ground can be stated instead. Ornelas and Fields do not authorize invention of historical facts. She orders no new hearing or photographic reconstruction and imposes no new public-access burden. This is her reason for the judgment, not a Court finding that the record is insufficient or renewed analysis necessary.

**Souter, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Part A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg, would affirm both reviewed judgments. Each respondent has his own interest in the deliberately shared domestic privacy. An officer conducts a search by deliberately obtaining screened home-interior information from a residential window-side vantage outside ordinary public observation. The close, sustained examination through drawn blinds from the nonordinary entrance approach satisfies that rule on the trial-level minimum. No finding of climbing, crouching, a state offense or a universal shared-yard curtilage classification is required.

The grass's common uses establish access, not ordinary exposure of this screened interior to the precise examination. The abutment position already required the close window-side approach; neither it nor the informant's unexplained outside view supplies a lawful sidewalk source. Fields rejects equating limited residential sharing or imperfect blinds with publication. Katz protects privacy without physical penetration. Pinson and Myers explain the protection of concealed home information, but their technological holdings did not already decide this naked-eye observation. The contrary actual-use and natural-visibility argument is substantial; these Justices find it insufficient to convert this inspection into ordinary public observation.

The conclusion preserves unaided observation from a street, walkway, common approach, open window or other position genuinely available to ordinary public observation. It requires no fence or posted sign whenever the actual residential vantage is otherwise shown not to be such a position. Neither approximately fifteen minutes nor approximately a foot is a fixed constitutional threshold. The disputed appellate additions are unnecessary to the conclusion, so this ground does not decide the factual-correction and rehearing disputes.

In Part B, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg additionally conclude that no warrant, pre-view probable cause, consent or supported exception justified the search. An investigatory tip and modest asserted intrusion supply no reasonable-suspicion exception for protected home information. Stone-Zsela does not join that additional rationale. All four would leave the actual state suppression judgments in force, subject to properly available independent issues; none orders acquittal or a new blanket exclusion of every later item. The three Associates additionally join the Court's conditional remedial rules. The separate writing does not turn the prosecutor's invalid-arrest condition into unconditional exclusion.

## 1998-12-03 — Equality Foundation of Greater Cincinnati, Inc. v. City of Cincinnati; Sixth Circuit Nos. 94-3855 and 94-3973 (merits), No. 94-4280 (fees); 128 F.3d 289.

Scalia dissents, joined by Thomas. They would affirm the merits judgment, dissolution of the injunction and dependent fee vacatur. They accept that Article XII reaches ordinary nondiscrimination protection and that municipalities remain subject to equal protection. In their view, regulatory restraint, limited resources, private association and the electorate's authority to make a local policy durable rationally support the measure despite imperfect coverage. Higher-level and generally applicable remedies remain available, and a local body's control over its own delegated policies differs from Colorado's restriction of state and local channels.

The dissent maintains its disagreement with Romer's application of ordinary rationality and proposes the municipal distinction as an additional limit. It does not claim Romer's majority adopted that reasoning or that every affected protection is a quota. O'Connor's Coalition writing supplies support for withdrawal of optional policies, while Hurley preserves actual expression; neither is treated as making every orientation protection unlawful. The dissent rejects a new racial political-process extension and finds no duty to adopt or preserve these municipal protections. Its fee position follows its merits conclusion only and supplies no separate bar to a fee award resting on an independently established surviving basis.

## 1998-12-14 — Calderon v. Coleman, No. 98-437; Ninth Circuit Nos. 97-99013 and 97-99014; District Court No. C89-1906 RMW.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He would use demanding whole-record substantial-influence review in this category, with genuine grave doubt preventing a finding of harmlessness. His Brecht and O'Neal positions distinguish collateral review while requiring the State to explain why an established violation did not substantially influence the actual decision. That is neither a sufficiency-of-aggravation test nor a rule that the prisoner loses whenever the evidence is evenly balanced. He accepts the binding Chapman categories but would not extend them here. Stringer's distinct weighing-error rule does not make every capital habeas claim identical. The District Court's plausible account of diminished mitigation must be assessed against the full charge and independent institutional-danger case; he supports a return without finding harmlessness or directing denial of relief.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** They would require the prisoner to establish substantial injurious influence for this category outside the previously controlling Chapman holdings. Their O'Neal position rests on the limited collateral remedy and §2254(a)'s custody-in-violation requirement, while recognizing that the statute does not expressly state a tie-breaking rule. An evenly balanced showing does not satisfy their proposed burden. They respect Roy's particular personal-intent rule without extending it universally. Potential diversion and instructional misunderstanding do not themselves establish the substantial effect they require. Their objection concerns the unfinished substantive inquiry, not missing words; serious aggravation is not an automatic cure. They rely neither on New's excluded account nor on a universal innocence prerequisite, and they do not require factor-specific capital findings derived from Scalia's different omitted-purpose analysis in Roy.

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He would affirm under substantial-influence review with grave doubt preventing harmlessness. The District Court did more than identify inaccurate words: it connected the misleading nondeath alternative to mitigation explaining violent behavior, the prominence of the prior convictions and the prosecution's danger case. The jury's conviction-document request shows salience without proving an actual release discussion. He considers the corrective charge, repeated instructions, extensive violence, prison-safety argument and reported short deliberation, but finds that those matters do not dispel grave doubt about substantial influence. The broader danger language and the instruction itself leave a concrete connection to the sentencing choice even without an express prosecutorial reference to commutation. His Roy position permits affirmance when a proper substantive application already supports relief despite an overbroad lower formulation. Another remand would repeat that task. He relies on the trial record, not excluded deliberation evidence or mitigation developed later in habeas, and would retain sentence-only relief with a reasonable state sentencing opportunity.

**Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** She joins the Chapman rule for this defined capital-selection error but concludes that the State cannot establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt on the existing record. The misleading account could turn explanations of violent behavior into reasons to doubt the nondeath option; the convictions emphasized in aggravation also restricted commutation. She considers the corrective direction and the substantial independent institutional-danger case, but neither establishes that the misinformation made no contribution. The charge first purported to explain life without parole through inaccurate information and then directed the jury to disregard that subject. The District Court's contextual explanation of that conflict supports its judgment. Her conclusion does not depend on a universal rule against corrective instructions, a specific parole question, New's excluded proffer or later habeas mitigation. Her O'Neal reservation of a universal Chapman extension remains, and her Roy position does not require express findings on every capital factor. She would affirm conditional sentence relief rather than require another application of the same substantive inquiry.

## 1999-01-12 — Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation, Inc.; No. 97-930; 120 F.3d 1092.

**Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** Thomas agrees that every reviewed restriction is invalid but applies traditional strict scrutiny to the direct speech and severe associational burdens. Registration reduces the Meyer-protected speaker pool without a narrowly fitted traceability justification. The personal-name badge directly intrudes on core political speech and fails narrow tailoring even assuming a compelling anti-fraud interest. The reports' effect on circulation is more attenuated; compelled disclosure independently burdens association and belief. Singling out paid circulators poorly fits general identification, and the need to expose each individual's identity and compensation has not been demonstrated. Monthly exposure is considered separately from final reporting. As an alternative, the challenged packages also fail Buckley's more forgiving substantial-relationship test. He does not displace Buckley's surviving disclosure holdings, adopt the Court's entire method, invalidate retained reports or decide reserved fields.

**O'Connor, joined by Breyer, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join Part II's complete personal-name-badge explanation: direct intrusion into political conversation requires narrow tailoring, and the retained affidavit and enforcement tools defeat this demand on this record. They would sustain registration and the challenged final and monthly reports. Under Anderson–Burdick, readily available enrollment provides an administrable connection to the formal petition process without the severe burden found by the Court; Meyer does not make every qualification equivalent to a ban on paid circulators. Reports are indirect financial-disclosure burdens rather than badges worn during persuasion. Identifying payees and individual disbursements can permit checking campaign activity beyond aggregate sums, and contemporaneous monthly information can expose abuse before the vote. They find the relationship sufficient even under more demanding disclosure review. They acknowledge the excluded unregistered speakers and possible exposure costs, but reach different applications. They propose no automatic admission rule, universal minimum scrutiny or independent disposition of reserved fields.

## 1999-01-12 — El Al Israel Airlines, Ltd. v. Tsui Yuan Tseng; No. 97-475; OT1998.

**Justice Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens reads Article 24's reference to Article 17 as covering injuries arising from an accident. He agrees that an accident causing only psychic injury remains governed by the Convention and cannot support a separate local recovery. He disagrees that the same displacement follows for a nonaccident: the text does not clearly extinguish local remedies for that residual category. Saks separates the event from the injury, Floyd leaves independent local claims open, and Zicherman's limited domestic supplementation does not settle the distinction. Uniformity cannot extend the treaty beyond the class its language identifies. Taking the Second Circuit's nonaccident determination as the premise of this review, without independently approving it, he would leave local proceedings available. He would award no treaty recovery and decides no battery, false imprisonment, loss or local defense.

## 1999-01-13 — City of West Covina v. Perkins, No. 97-1230.

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** The concurrence agrees that officials need not give personalized instruction about generally available return law and that the missing warrant number does not sustain the judgment on this record. ## 1999-01-13 — Marcus v. Iowa Public Television, No. 98-710.

**Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Souter and Ginsburg, dissenting in part.** The dissent joins the committee-based jurisdictional holding and the nonpublic-forum classification, including the permission for reasonable selection and the prohibition of viewpoint discrimination. It maintains the standards objection stated in the Forbes dissent: allocation of a government-provided candidate platform requires sufficiently definite and reviewable restraints on selection. Iowa's fairness policies and illustrative factors describe professional judgment, but Instruction 15 still leaves officials to decide which candidacies interest the audience without adequate operative limits. The dissent accepts the adverse findings about viewpoint motive. Its objection is that sincere discretion and absence of hostility do not cure inadequate standards under Cornelius, Shuttlesworth and Rosenberger. The candidates' assertions about missing information, publicity and comparisons show why meaningful review matters; the dissent does not treat those assertions as findings of equal electoral significance or discrimination.

The dissent would vacate rejection of the committee's common policy claim and remand for consideration under adequately constrained selection standards. It would return the program-guide request for consideration of its connection to permissible equitable relief and any continuing injury the relief could redress, without establishing an independent guide claim or an entitlement to correction. It would order neither automatic ballot-based admission, a universal numerical threshold, a replacement 1996 broadcast, compulsory future programming nor damages. These views remain noncontrolling; they do not change the operative Forbes rule.

## 1999-01-14 — State Street Bank & Trust Co. v. Signature Financial Group, Inc.; Federal Circuit No. 96-1327; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; OT1998.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Stone-Zsela agrees that the business-method exclusion and the useful-result sufficiency rule are too broad. ## 1999-01-20 — California Public Employees' Retirement System and Florida State Board of Administration v. Paul Felzen et al., No. 97-1732.

**Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, dissenting.** The dissent would recognize a confined appellate role for a shareholder who responds to the Rule 23.1 settlement notice, appears, presents objections, and has those objections heard and rejected in the disposition of the corporate claim. The procedural role, rather than mere ownership, financial exposure, receipt of notice or potential binding effect, supplies status to seek review of that disposition. It does not make the objector a plaintiff controlling prosecution or give a nonappearing shareholder a right to appeal.

Tryforos, which Justice Stevens authored as a circuit judge, supplies the direct distinction between objecting to disposition and undertaking prosecution. Its footnote expressly includes proposed settlements, although the actual objection there concerned dismissal with prejudice and the corporation's lack of service. Marino presents a serious contrary example because its objectors were heard too. The dissent distinguishes their adverse personal interests in another action from the Rule 23.1 shareholder's defined role concerning disposition of the represented corporate claim. Izumi involved an undisturbed intervention denial and a different presented question; it does not foreclose deciding that this recognized role supplies limited status. Johnson offers an equity analogy linking invitation, appearance, objection and appeal, but its adjacent intervention discussion and receivership setting prevent treating it as a universal nonintervention rule. Richards protects independent nonparty rights without itself creating appellate status.

The dissent would reverse only the status dismissal and remand for further appellate proceedings subject to ordinary preservation and appeal requirements. It would not invalidate the settlement, find collusion or inadequate representation, permit new claims, promise success on the objections or establish reviewability or entitlement on every fee contention. It proposes no independent prospective-only ruling, reopening, tolling or automatic late intervention. Its limited representative-objector rule does not control the judgment.

Diagnosis alone remains insufficient, but Murphy’s uncontested evidence of severe untreated hypertension, approximately 250/160, and the medical account of possible hospitalization establish actual disability when taken as true. They require neither stopping medication nor a finding of existing end-organ injury.

Successful treatment remains relevant to qualification, accommodation and safety. Because those matters were not decided by the circuit, they would return the case for ordinary proceedings on necessary preserved remaining issues, without employment, damages, certification, accommodation, a mandatory trial or final liability. They join the general perceived-class framework and Supreme Court nonreach but make no independent regarded-as application, contrary triability ruling or alternative treated-state remand.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting.** He would vacate the adverse coverage judgment and remand for reassessment without using beneficial medication or correction to defeat actual-disability coverage. Individual evidence remains necessary: neither hypertension as a label nor use of a prescription proves substantial limitation. Arline and Bragdon support examination of the individual condition while keeping qualification and safety separate; successful treatment should not make protection depend on continuing vulnerability.

He joins the perceived-class framework but does not agree that the present proof finally defeats that route. A driving qualification does not establish what the employer believed about other mechanical work; any preserved regarded-as theory must satisfy its own broad-class requirement. On his remand the court would also determine essential driving functions and the operative safety qualification on their proper elements, without presuming entitlement to a waiver. His Supreme Court nonreach join does not abandon those lower-court directions. He makes no final disability finding and orders no automatic hiring, reinstatement, accommodation or other award.

## 1999-06-22 — Olmstead v. L.C.; No. 98-536.

**O'Connor, joined by Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, concurring on plan sufficiency.** A State meets the reasonable-modification standard in the stated circumstances by demonstrating a comprehensive, effectively working plan to place qualified persons in less restrictive settings and a waiting list moving at a reasonable pace not controlled by an effort to keep institutions full. A court then may not advance a litigant by displacing persons properly ahead. The four require actual operation and reasonable movement, not a paper plan or inert list; they require no separate additional disruption showing once all stated conditions are proved. They find no Georgia compliance and prescribe no universal waiting period or required planning procedure. Their position supplies an important proposed limit but lacks majority support.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins both Court parts and both component judgments. A working plan and lawful queue may supply substantial evidence, but the State must connect them to fundamental alteration from the requested modification. Plan compliance alone does not conclusively resolve each person's entitlement. His position preserves actual resource constraints and proper priorities without accepting generalized fiscal preference or indefinite postponement; this additional qualification is not the Court's holding.

**Stevens, concurring.** He joins the integration holding and accepts the confined mixed judgment, although he would prefer affirming the existing resource remand. Its nonexclusive factors already allow relevant program evidence, while the later restriction and defense ruling belong in their own appeal. His judgment agreement does not adopt the Court's corrective rationale or the separate sufficient-plan rule.

**Kennedy, joined by Breyer in Part I only.** Their clinical and administrative cautions warn against inadequately staffed placements and pressures to discharge people into settings unable to provide necessary care. Responsible professional and funding judgments deserve substantial respect, but not an unsupported or unreviewable veto. Breyer retains his full integration, resource, and plan-concurrence joins.

In Part II, Kennedy alone would vacate the liability determination for an inquiry into discriminatory treatment, potentially comparing treatment of medical problems of comparable seriousness and unjustified institutional conditions imposed on people with mental disabilities. No actual nondisabled beneficiary is indispensable: competent direct or circumstantial evidence can prove the statutory treatment and cause. Suitability and existing community services do not alone establish that predicate for him. He would permit ordinary consideration of existing materials and any properly allowed amendment or evidence, without commanding new proceedings. He supports institutional overhead, reasonable cost accounting, and appropriate funding discretion, but neither the full Court resource rationale nor the sufficient-plan rule.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, dissenting.** Section 12132's discrimination requirement cannot be replaced by disability-related need and the clinical availability of another setting. In their view, Davis, Choate, and Traynor preserve the distinction between access or accommodation and a general right to a preferred mode of medical care; the findings and integration regulation must operate within the statutory predicate. They nevertheless reject categorical immunity for disability-only programs and an indispensable actual nondisabled comparator. Their objection concerns the asserted service obligation. They would reverse that statutory ground, remove its dependent remedial support, and preserve unresolved claims and lawful treatment of existing relief. They find neither a legitimate Georgia queue nor exhausted resources and authorize no unlawful confinement.

## 1999-06-22 — Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc.; No. 97-1943; OT1998.

**Stevens, joined by Breyer, dissenting.** They would reverse and remand the actual-disability theory because severe underlying myopia can substantially limit seeing even when corrective lenses permit ordinary activity. The statute's protection and accommodation structure separates the existence of a substantially limiting condition from the person's ability to perform a job with assistance. Otherwise effective self-management can remove protection against impairment-based exclusion precisely when it enables work. The administrative guidance and legislative materials persuasively support that reading; the dissent does not require a general holding that all Appendix language has controlling force. Individual substantiality remains necessary, so every person who wears glasses does not automatically qualify. Qualification, accommodation, business necessity, direct threat, causation and relief remain for ordinary proceedings. The dissent does not order hiring or damages and need not decide the separate regarded-as-working application to support its remand.

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** He would vacate the adverse coverage judgment and remand. Beneficial lenses or medication should not eliminate actual-disability coverage where the underlying impairment substantially limits the person's major life activity. Arline's functional protection and Bragdon's individualized method support examining these applicants' severe visual limitation while retaining proof of substantiality; the construction does not cover every eyeglass wearer. Actual functioning remains relevant to qualification, accommodation and supported safety defenses, which cannot erase the separate coverage inquiry. He agrees that exclusion from the global-airline-pilot position alone does not establish inability to work in a broad class of occupations. The actual seeing theory nevertheless requires assessment under the proper mitigation rule, and any preserved regarded-as theory must satisfy its own elements. He would award no automatic hiring, reinstatement or accommodation.

## 1999-06-23 — Alden v. Maine; No. 98-436; 527 U.S. 706.

**Kennedy, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Parts II–III and the judgment, joined by O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas.** The dissent accepts the review channel, express FLSA authorization, Maine’s lack of consent and continuing federal wage duties. It would nevertheless hold that retained constitutional sovereignty protects a nonconsenting State from this private retrospective treasury action in its own courts. Power to regulate state wages and a court’s duty to enforce valid federal law do not, in its view, resolve whether Congress may impose this particular remedy. The constitutional protection concerns the State’s public fisc and political responsibility, not merely a state common-law rule that Congress may displace.

The dissent acknowledges that Seminole supplied no controlling prohibition and that Union Gas remains precedent, while maintaining its disagreement with Union Gas’s constitutional conclusion. It preserves Hilton’s specialized statutory settlement and compensation reliance, rather than substituting a finding of consent; it distinguishes Howlett’s non-State defendant. New York supports separating a valid objective from permissible means, but neither that decision nor Printz negates Garcia or ordinary state-court duties. The dissent would affirm this immunity dismissal without extinguishing the wage obligation or deciding valid consent, United States enforcement, appropriate §5 legislation, established officer relief or suits against entities that are not arms of a State.

## 1999-06-23 — College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board, No. 98-149; October Term 1998.

**Stone-Zsela's nonmajority Part IV.** He would give effect to Congress's express federal remedy against a State selling competing investment products in interstate commerce. Union Gas supplies a retained Commerce-power conclusion; Seminole supplies no contrary categorical rule. On the federal-question account supported by Stevens's Union Gas concurrence, the Amendment's citizen-State diversity restriction does not prohibit this congressionally created federal action, and valid deliberate legislation may displace nontextual Hans immunity. That view does not depend on guessing Union Gas's corporate citizenship or adopting every statement of its plurality.

He regards the alternative source of congressional authority as an included legal argument for the same preserved federal immunity challenge, on the same established facts and for the same requested relief. He acknowledges the specifically framed §5 question but distinguishes the generic grant from a separate claim, new factual theory or expressly excluded question. Lebron supports the included-argument distinction; he does not rely on a prevailing-respondent defense or briefing alone. His position is confined to enforcement of this federal commercial obligation, not unlimited power to abolish every State immunity. He rejects constructive waiver and automatic constitutional property and finds no advertising violation or entitlement to relief. No other Justice joins these constitutional or scope grounds.

**Breyer, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in part and in the judgment.** They find consent where Congress unmistakably and prospectively makes specified commercial conduct subject to private federal enforcement and the State continues an undertaking it can realistically choose not to operate. The relevant undertaking is competitive investment marketing, an optional way to further education, not education itself. The choice is whether to conduct that undertaking under the stated terms, not whether to violate the law. Clear advance notice, continuing covered activity and meaningful choice distinguish the condition from the displaced inference based on general statutory coverage.

They answer the objection that Congress alone imposed liability by treating this explicit prospective condition as a term accepted through continued optional activity. They do not assume a signed promise, federal subsidy, profit figure, exit-cost finding or withdrawal event. Nor do they revive original Parden, erase Florida Prepaid's arm status under Hess, or transfer Lane's federal consent and Cedar Rapids' accepted funding into an imaginary grant bargain. They would reverse and permit ordinary statutory adjudication without deciding direct Article I abrogation. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the Court's confined §5 rejection; Stevens does not.

**Stevens, further concurring in part and in the judgment.** An existing going business, its economic activity and goodwill are property, and deliberate State destruction of that business can deprive its owner of property. Congress may protect against that category of constitutional injury through a defined unfair-competition remedy without requiring every covered advertisement itself to constitute a completed constitutional deprivation. He finds a reasonable preventive relationship, assessed under Boerne's congruence-and-proportionality rule and the presumption of legislative validity, between protection of the existing business interest and this private remedy.

He accepts the distinction between prevention and constitutional redefinition, rather than substituting general rational-basis review for Boerne. Haddle's statutory-injury distinction does not deny existing business property, and Sullivan's protection of substantive claims reinforces the need to identify and protect that interest. He would sustain the challenged §5 application as an independent ground for reversal. He finds neither the allegations true nor any remedy presently due. No other Justice joins this ground.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** They join the statutory clarity, §5 and consent holdings but would affirm within the two questions presented. Direct Article I abrogation identifies a different constitutional source; it answers neither whether Congress validly exercised §5 nor whether Florida actually consented. The same claim and the usefulness of an alternative do not themselves establish fair inclusion or an independently justified exception. Lebron concerns an included legal argument, while X-Citement Video permits a prevailing respondent to defend the same favorable judgment; CSB seeks reversal. Murphy and Olmstead reinforce attention to the actual grant rather than preservation or briefing alone.

Their affirmance does not apply a categorical Seminole prohibition that the Court never adopted. Union Gas survives, and they leave the distinct Article I route undecided in this review. They preserve genuine voluntary waiver, valid §5 enforcement and properly available officer relief while finding neither of the presented routes sufficient for this claim. They would leave only the reviewed federal immunity dismissal in force and decide no underlying advertising violation, broader TRCA application or separate proceeding.

## 1999-06-23 — Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank; No. 98-531.

**Stevens's Parts II and III, joined by Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, supply two independent grounds for affirmance.** Part II would sustain §296(a) as preventive Fourteenth Amendment enforcement. A patent is already a defined exclusive property interest. State use can produce completed loss that a prospective injunction cannot repair; proof difficulties and uncertainty about effective compensation, together with exclusive federal patent jurisdiction, support prevention in this defined field. Boerne permits some reach beyond independently unconstitutional conduct when the means fit the protected right and threatened injury. Congress did not redefine the constitutional property right. Uniformity and equal competition inform the practical remedy but are not alone sufficient §5 grounds.

These four do not find every State remedy inadequate or equate alleged willfulness with an established constitutional wrong. They question mechanically transferring Daniels's prison-negligence rule to patent deprivations and need not decide that extension to sustain prevention. They would permit this action without preapproving every §296(b) award; compensation, enhancement, fees, and injunction retain their own conditions and properly presented constitutional limits.

Part III independently treats continued optional commercial activity as acceptance of a clear prospective federal-court condition. Congress has substantive Patent Clause authority over the underlying conduct; the proposed amenability ground is acceptance, rather than general Article I abrogation. Florida's competitive investment products are an optional means of serving education. The combination of enacted notice, covered post-enactment activity, and genuine practical choice answers the objection that a congressional command is not State assent. Welch did not already validate every express condition; Hilton preserved specialized FELA state-court coverage without reviving the old general-language federal-waiver inference. These four distinguish the later express terms and real choice. They assume no federal grant, contract, particular State-owned patent, quantified profit, or exit cost. All commerce, abstract regulatory benefits, a heading, or the mere choice to infringe would not suffice. Neither this ground nor their §5 ground commands a majority.

**Stone-Zsela concurs in Part I and in the judgment.** Congress created a national patent system, concentrated adjudication in federal court, and expressly included States in the ordinary remedy. Uniform patent rights lose important practical content if a State may exploit an invention while withholding that remedy. Retained Union Gas supplies an Article I foundation; extending its Commerce-power conclusion to the Patent Clause is an express constitutional extension, not an issue already resolved by Seminole or adoption of every plurality proposition. CSB's federal patent claim defends the same favorable immunity-denial judgment and seeks no enlarged relief. The interstate private-plaintiff setting does not remove the federal-question character of this expressly authorized patent action; the concurrence's proposed Patent Clause rule reaches this case without assuming Union Gas's corporate citizenship. The power remains tied to enforcement of a valid enumerated-power obligation and an unmistakable congressional choice.

The concurrence does not rely on §5. Boerne prevents recasting all statutory infringements as constitutional deprivations; ordinary infringement may leave adequate compensation or process, and negligence alone does not establish the needed deprivation. Those observations do not adopt the dissent's full conclusion that this preventive statute fails substantive fit. The Patent Clause ground permits the action without imposing a claim-by-claim constitutional tort requirement. Commercial-condition consent and actual litigation waiver are unnecessary to that ground and remain undecided in the concurrence. It determines neither infringement nor relief.

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas, dissents from affirmance while joining Part I.** Boerne permits prevention, but ordinary patent liability includes innocent infringement and circumstances with adequate process. Patent property and the usefulness of a federal remedy do not establish a sufficiently fitted response to deprivation without due process. National uniformity and equal treatment cannot independently supply §5 authority. The objection concerns substantive fit, not a mandatory quantity of evidence, violation in every jurisdiction, formal findings, geographic limits, or sunsets. The dissent does not find Florida's untested claims-bill or takings routes adequate and does not require exhaustion.

On consent, a clear federal declaration states Congress's intent rather than Florida's unequivocal submission. Continuing lawful commerce, even an optional undertaking, differs from accepting a specified federal benefit or affirmatively invoking judicial authority. Actual waiver, properly conditioned grants, and interstate compacts remain separate, including their genuine coercion questions. The dissent does not adopt an automatic exemption for activities serving public purposes or deny substantive federal regulation of the patent conduct.

The dissent would vacate and remand for ordinary treatment of the preserved actual-submission contentions and any other properly available current-law ground before final immunity dismissal. Appearance, transfer, and affirmative relief are not interchangeable or already adjudicated consent. Union Gas remains in force, so rejection of §5 and commercial consent cannot silently apply a categorical Article I prohibition that is not governing law. No new Article I overruling is decided while a genuine-submission ground may avoid it. The dissent orders no discovery, hearing, new proof, amendment, or constitutional-infringement trial, and decides no patent merits.

## 1999-06-23 — Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp.; No. 97-1704.

**Breyer, joined by Stevens, dissenting.** The dissent would sustain the challenged limited-fund and adequate-representation determinations. Rule 23's practical-impairment language permits attention to the actual risk of exhaustion, uncertain insurance coverage, defense costs and limited noninsurance assets. The distinct Fibreboard-insurer negotiation supplied an adequate benchmark because the Trilateral agreement did not release Fibreboard's tort liability, leaving it an incentive to obtain the largest available payment. Perfect valuation is neither possible nor required. Substantial transaction savings can satisfy the whole-fund purpose without demanding literal surrender that produces a smaller real recovery.

The dissent also credits the lower findings on experienced counsel, phased negotiation, later-secured inventory payments, preservation of insurance and individualized awards. It finds no sufficient reason to overturn that circumstance-specific appraisal, given the practical negotiating opportunity. It accepts Amchem's requirement of actual Rule compliance, rather than treating aggregate benefit as a universal cure for conflicts or ruling out the need for subclasses elsewhere. It opposes reversal on the certification grounds decided by the Court. Although it considers the proponents likely to prevail on additional commonality and typicality, notice, Article III, Enabling Act, bankruptcy, jury and due-process objections, it reserves their definitive resolution because the Court has not reached them.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** The concurrence agrees that the Global certification and settlement approval must be vacated. Mandatory aggregation removes individual control and the opportunity to opt out; it therefore requires an independently established maximum practical fund, aggregate insufficiency, substantially whole commitment, equitable distribution and conflict-free representation. The present agreement fails the independent-value and representation requirements. Stone-Zsela joins Parts I, II-A, III, IV and V, including those failures and the confined remedy, while retaining the stated minimum and withholding assent to Part II-B's departure qualification. It does not make retention of every corporate asset independently fatal or bar a lawful mass-tort settlement, bankruptcy trust, opt-out class or genuine limited-fund proceeding.
