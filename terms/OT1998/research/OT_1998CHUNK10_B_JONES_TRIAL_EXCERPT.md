813: (1) The “intent” findings from 18 U.S.C. § 3591(a)(2) 
814: should not be designated as “aggravating factors.” They were 
815: so designated in 21 U.S.C. § 848(n)(1), but this was a 
816: constitutional flaw in the statue [sic], and Congress cor- 
817: rected it in 18 U.S.C. § 3591 by redesignating these find- 
818: ings as threshold or preliminary findings rather than as 
819: aggravation. Therefore the the [sic] Court should change 
820: both Part One of the Special Findings Form, and all 
821: instructions that refer to the intent findings as “category 
822: one aggravating factors.” 
823: 
824: 
825: The importance of this change is that the intent fac- 
826: tors simply establish that the defendant is constitu- 
827: tionally eligible for the death penalty under the eighth 
828: amendment, and this eligibility should not be treated as a 
829: separate aggravating factor (let alone 4 separate ones) 
830: 
831:

833: 
834: 
835: weighing against the mitigating factors and in favor of 
836: the imposition of the death penalty. See Arave v. Creech, 
837: 113 S.Ct. 1534, 1542 (1993) (aggravating factor is invalid if 
838: it applies to every defendant eligible for the death pen- 
839: alty.). If the Court declines to remove the “aggravating” 
840: designation from these threshold findings, defendant 
841: objects (in addition to objections based on statutory con- 
842: struction and legislative intent) on the grounds that 
843: defendant's constitutional eligibility for the death penalty 
844: under Enmund v. Arizona and Tison v. Arizona (i.e. the fact 
845: that the defendant had sufficient homicidal intent to 
846: make him death-eligible under the eighth amendment) 
847: applies by definition to every death-eligible killing, and 
848: thus does not narrow the category of murderers punish- 
849: able by death: this makes the weighing of these findings 
850: on death's side of the sentencing scale invalid under the 
851: principle of Godfrey v. Georgia, Stringer v. Black, and Arave 
852: v. Creech that aggravating factors in death penalty cases 
853: must genuinely and meaningfully narrow the universe of 
854: killings in which death may be imposed. 
855: 
856: 
857: In addition, the Court should require the the [sic] 
858: jury to choose one intent factor, not all four. These four 
859: factors represent a descending scale of personal homici- 
860: dal intent, and the jury should only take up each one 
861: after deciding that the government has failed to prove the 
862: preceding one. This approach avoids the danger (still 
863: present even if the instructions do not mislabel these 
864: findings as “aggravating”) that the jury will be misled 
865: into supposing that a murder is more blameworthy if four 
866: different levels of culpability are all found to apply to the 
867: defendant's conduct.
