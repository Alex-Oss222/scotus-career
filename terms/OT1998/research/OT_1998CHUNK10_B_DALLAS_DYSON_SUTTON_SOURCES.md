- [150 F.3d438](https://openjurist.org/150/f3d/438/dallas-fire-fighters-association-v-dallas-tx-city-a-cantu), original opinion read in full including footnotes and Smith’s separate writing. Cross-checked against the petition’s reproduced appellate judgment and contested record documents; not reliance on an aggregator alone. The webpage’s later citation analytics are not authority.
- Petition pp.8–13 and AppendixL, printed App.62ff (OCR4757ff), distinguish the City’s characterization of the 1976 agreement as a remedial decree from its wording addressing effects of possible earlier discrimination. AppendixM, App.72–75 (OCR5166–5316), is a November14,1972 DOJ letter: it finds TitleVII-inconsistent employment practices, identifies no Black hires before1969 and twelve Black/Mexican-American uniformed employees out of1340, and challenges unvalidated entry tests, education and height requirements. Its described findings concern race/ethnicity; do not label it a sex-discrimination finding. It calls for nondiscriminatory selection and promotion and accelerated minority hiring.
- Petition pp.16–24 describes validated exams, minority recruiting, a second paramedic-course opportunity, elimination of seniority points and tutoring; it argues these have not sufficiently removed effects in senior ranks. Lower opinion¶9/fn13 acknowledges actual alternatives and proposed protections; continued imbalance alone does not establish their ineffectiveness. The City invokes score bands, qualified beneficiaries, five-year duration, rank-based cessation and a50-percent annual ceiling.
- Opposition pp.3–8 disputes the present-promotion causal link, relevant feeder pools, lack of admission in the settlement, and fit. The City's asserted departmental demographics are not independent findings of a proper rank-specific denominator. Neither admissions nor an adjudicated liability judgment is categorically required by controlling simulated law.
- Lower opinion fn10 describes Texas equal-rights law as construed in conformity with federal law; the constitutional issue can affect that derivative ground. TitleVII/Texas statutory issues are expressly not reached below and outside this simulated grant. Smith’s dissent concerns only the excluded deputy-chief claim.

## Dyson

- Archive item [micro_IA40385014_0252](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0252), `2. Petition for Writ of Certiorari`; local `_B_DYSON_petition.txt`. Original petition pp.2–5 and appended Maryland opinion App.11a–17a establish facts and legal ground. No separately listed opposition or JA was present in this item's metadata; that says nothing about the actual Court file or respondent conduct.
- The trial court denied suppression; Dyson was convicted of conspiracy to possess cocaine with intent to distribute. Maryland Court of Special Appeals,122Md.App.413,712A.2d573(1998), reversed on the automobile-search issue. The petition describes Maryland Court of Appeals denial of discretionary review; this is a state-court procedural predicate, not Supreme Court action.
- The lower court found abundant probable cause after an informant report and rental-car corroboration at approximately noon July2,1996. Police located the moving rental Toyota at1a.m. July3; cocaine was found in a duffel bag in its trunk. The court’s objection was that the police had13–14hours to seek a warrant, not an absence of mobility at the stop.
- Source correction: Dyson argued that prior time to obtain a warrant defeated the search; Maryland argued the automobile exception did not demand that extra condition. The runtime reverses this attribution in its positions paragraphs.
- The ground examined is the Fourth Amendment automobile exception. No independent state constitutional basis is established by the retrieved opinion. No respondent waiver, requested response, actual response or refusal to respond is assumed. For a reasoned summary merits decision, the fresh model must evaluate the sufficiency of the existing neutral record and applicable response procedure; an operator’s lack of a response is not litigant default.

## Sutton

- Archive item [micro_IA40385014_0183](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0183), files `02. Petition for Writ of Certiorari`, `05. Joint Appendix`, `06. Petitioners Brief`, `07. Respondents Brief`; local `_B_SUTTON_{petition,ja,petitioner,respondent}.txt`. Petition App.A reproduces130F.3d893 in full; relevant complaint allegations and lower rationale were checked there. The openjurist fetch returned502; record appendix supplies the primary alternative.
- Petition pp.3–4 records allegations of20/200 or worse right-eye and20/400 or worse left-eye vision without correction;20/20 corrected vision; first-class FAA medical certificates; United’s20/100 uncorrected cutoff. Allegations are accepted at Rule12(b)(6), not adjudicated safety findings.
- Lower opinion App.A18,A22–25 discusses actual seeing and regarded-as working. It assumes for that appeal exclusion from all United pilot positions despite United’s narrower oral claim; it regards global-airline piloting as too narrow, considering other pilot jobs requiring similar training.
- Petitioners' merits conclusion p.50 seeks reversal and remand, including consideration of the direct-threat issue. Respondent’s conclusion p.50 seeks affirmance. Runtime’s unqualified requested affirmance is corrected.
- Petitioners’ pp.12–21 seek weight for EEOC guidance that mitigating measures should be disregarded, rely on delegated TitleI authority, notice/comment treatment, Rehabilitation Act continuity, legislative material and the distinction between impairment/coverage and qualification. Respondent’s pp.23–28 disputes both delegation/force of the guidance and consistency with statutory individualized substantial limitation; its fn19 does not press the separate contention that myopia is not an impairment.
- The parties’ regulatory quotations identify29CFR1630.2(h),(i),(j),(l) and Appendix guidance. The guidance on working distinguishes a class/broad range of jobs from one specialized job. Defendant’s brief records petitioners’ focus on regarded-as working; petitioners’ actual-disability theory concerns seeing. Do not create a new preserved regarded-as-seeing theory without checking the pleadings and scope.
- Official1994 statutory copies: `_B_ADA12102_1994.txt`, `_B_ADA12111_1994.txt`, `_B_ADA12112_1994.txt`, downloaded from govinfo USCODE-1994-title42. They preserve the original ADA definition, qualified-individual standard, and discrimination provisions. No later ADA amendments are applied.
