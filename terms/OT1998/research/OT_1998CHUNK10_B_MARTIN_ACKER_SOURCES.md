## Martin v. Hadix, No. 98-262

### Material correction to supplied Section I

The court of appeals did **not** divide fees by the date services were performed. The district court did that. The Sixth Circuit held the PLRA fee limitation inapplicable to **both pre-enactment and post-enactment services in cases pending on April 26, 1996**. It affirmed the district court's exclusion of pre-enactment work and reversed its application to later work. The supplied descriptions of the judgment below and reasoning below require correction before modeling.

Primary support: petition pp. 5–6 and its reproduced court-of-appeals opinion, Pet. App. 1a–3a, 9a–19a; petitioners' merits brief pp. 4–9; respondents' merits brief pp. 1–6. The exact docket collection is [Internet Archive item micro_IA40385014_0221](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0221). ### Verified record and review limits

- The consolidated disputes concern Glover litigation begun in 1977 and Hadix litigation begun in 1980. Each had reached a continuing remedial and monitoring stage before the PLRA. The relevant Glover stipulated fee procedure dates to November 1985; the Hadix monitoring-fee order dates to November 1987. The underlying Hadix consent decree was entered April 4, 1985. These facts establish prior judgments and fee arrangements, not an adjudicated contractual guarantee of unchanged rates for all future work.
- Fee requests at issue included January 1–June 30, 1996 services, straddling April 26, 1996. The established attorney rate was $150 per hour; applying 150 percent of the identified $75 CJA rate would produce a $112.50 ceiling. The temporal question can therefore be separated from the factual calculation of compensable hours.
- The lower decision also considered distinct prevailing-party questions in several monitoring appeals. Those collateral rulings do not authorize this Court to redo the entire underlying litigation or all fee calculations in resolving the supplied temporal questions.
- Respondents raised constitutional objections; the Sixth Circuit did not reach them after concluding the statute did not apply. Their merits brief uses equal-protection and due-process concerns as avoidance arguments. This is not a lower-court judgment invalidating the PLRA.
- The supplied review scope includes services before and after enactment. The original merits briefs address both. The Archive joint-appendix OCR identifies its final page as the November 16, 1998 grant letter, App. 217a, but the recovered OCR ends before the letter's operative wording. Accordingly the question may be stated as a supported paraphrase, not a certified verbatim grant quotation. This is a precision limit, not a demonstrated jurisdictional or preservation failure.

### Statutory support

[42 U.S.C. §1997e(d), 1998 Code](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1998-title42/html/USCODE-1998-title42-chap21-subchapI-A-sec1997e.htm) supplies the primary text. Paragraph (1) addresses qualifying prisoner actions and fee entitlement, including fees reasonably incurred in enforcing ordered relief. Paragraph (3) limits the hourly basis to 150 percent of the CJA rate. Paragraph (2)'s monetary-judgment contribution rules should not be silently conflated with the hourly-rate dispute in this institutional litigation. Paragraph (4) addresses privately paid contractual fees.

[18 U.S.C. §3626, 1998 Code, effective-date notes](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1998-title18/html/USCODE-1998-title18-partII-chap229-subchapC-sec3626.htm) reproduces PLRA §802(b)(1), which expressly reaches prospective relief originally granted before, on, or after enactment. The corresponding §1997e(d) text lacks that same temporal sentence. Whether that comparison supports a filing-date inference is contested; the different provisions' functions matter.

### Supported adversarial alternatives for clean neutral framing

These are competing arguments, not findings about which is correct.

1. **Officials: the award is the relevant event.** Petitioners rely on the breadth of the action and award language, the separability of statutory fees from underlying merits, Congress's control over compensation rules, and Bradley's treatment of intervening fee legislation. They say §802 regulates already-entered prospective relief and therefore required language that §803 did not. Their requested result applies the ceiling to all awards made after enactment, including payment for earlier services. Their account should be tested against effective simulated Landgraf's actual new-consequences inquiry, not accepted merely because a fee order issues later.
2. **Prisoners/counsel: the filing, undertaken representation, and prior fee arrangements matter.** Respondents rely on structural inference from §802, §1988's incentive to undertake meritorious litigation, prior monitoring-fee orders, and ethical and practical difficulty withdrawing once representation has begun. Their proposed result leaves pending litigation outside the new cap. The fee orders and practical reliance support their argument, but neither automatically proves that future services retain an unalterable statutory rate.
3. **Service-date distinction as an analytical alternative.** If effective simulated law does not resolve the text at the first step, completed services and services furnished after notice of the new law can be analyzed separately. This route would modify, rather than simply affirm, the actual court-of-appeals temporal disposition. It must address the reliance and continuation arguments instead of treating ongoing counsel as freely replaceable by assumption.

All routes leave the calculation of actual hours, separately applicable fee restrictions, and unreviewed substantive or fee-entitlement issues to their proper scope. Rejecting one temporal argument does not establish that the PLRA is constitutionally valid in every application.

### Date-eligible Justice-specific source anchor

In **Kaiser Aluminum & Chemical Corp. v. Bonjorno, 494 U.S. 827 (1990)**, O'Connor wrote the Court's interpretation of the postjudgment-interest statute; Stevens, Scalia, and Kennedy joined her, along with Rehnquist. The Court found the statutory temporal answer clear and did not resolve the broader Bradley/Bowen tension. Scalia separately advocated the ordinary presumption against retroactivity, yet expressly acknowledged at 849 that applying a costs-or-fees provision to unfinished litigation might not itself be retroactive. Thus Scalia's presumption cannot be mechanically converted into a pending-cases exemption; the legally relevant event remains essential. O'Connor's opinion also supports resolving clear text first rather than assuming every fee change must receive the same result.

## Jefferson County v. Acker, No. 98-10

This section preserves verified facts and expressly attributed arguments for a fresh clean validator; it does not replace the neutral framing.

### Material source corrections to be validated

- The County contested federal jurisdiction under the Tax Injunction Act. Its requested relief cannot be described simply as affirming removal and reversing on tax immunity. Its merits brief's first question asks whether the district court had jurisdiction in light of the Act; pp. 10–21 argue the federal forum was barred. Its tax-validity arguments supply the merits alternative.
- Whether §1341 bars the removed collection proceeding is disputed, not an established threshold fact. The supplied Section I sentence announcing that it does not require remand belongs among proposed legal paths unless an entering simulated precedent independently resolves it.
- The judges also assert discriminatory treatment through the exemption for holders of specified Alabama licenses. Accordingly, describing the levy as "nondiscriminatory" without attribution prejudges a contested characterization. State judges and other federal employees' actual payment remains relevant record evidence, but does not erase the argument.
- The en banc court sustained federal jurisdiction and restored its tax-immunity disposition. Its merits disposition did not reach the distinct Compensation Clause ground on which the district court had also ruled. This source fact preserves scope; it does not establish an entitlement to prevail on that reserved ground.

Primary sources: [Archive item micro_IA40385014_0205](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0205), documents 02 (petition and lower opinions/ordinance), 05 (County merits brief), 06 (judges' merits brief), and 08 (United States merits amicus). The original papers, including the copied lower opinions, are historical source material and not current simulated Supreme Court authority.

### Stipulated facts and enactment

The County's merits brief pp. 5–10 reproduces the parties' stipulations. Ordinance 1120 took effect January 1, 1988. It imposes a one-half-of-one-percent levy on compensation attributable to work within the County; the OCR sometimes corrupts the decimal/percentage, so use the spelled-out rate or `.005` multiplier. It exempts persons required by state law to pay specified state privilege, license, or occupational taxes. Its definition reaches federal, state, county, and city officeholders. Its text declares work without payment unlawful. These enacted features support competing legal characterizations; they do not establish that a county court has stopped a federal judge from sitting.

Acker and Clemon were federal district judges with their principal offices in Birmingham. They also performed some duties outside the County. The ordinance provides apportionment for work inside and outside the County. The amount and allocation of any lawful liability are therefore distinct from the federal immunity question. The County filed state small-claims collection actions; the judges removed under §1442(a)(3). The district court adjudicated on stipulated facts and cross-motions for summary judgment. The County had moved to remand under §1341.

### Verified statutes

- [28 U.S.C. §1442, 1998 Code](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1998-title28/html/USCODE-1998-title28-partIV-chap89-sec1442.htm): paragraph (a)(3) addresses federal court officers sued for an act under color of office or in performance of duties. Do not import the later statutory phrase "or relating to" into the 1999 text.
- [28 U.S.C. §1341, 1998 Code](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1998-title28/html/USCODE-1998-title28-partIV-chap85-sec1341.htm): the operative verbs are enjoin, suspend, and restrain state-tax assessment, levy, or collection, with the plain, speedy, and efficient state-remedy condition.
- [4 U.S.C. §111, 1998 Code](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1998-title4/html/USCODE-1998-title4-chap4-sec111.htm): subsection (a) supplies consent to taxation of federal pay or compensation, conditioned against discrimination because of its source. The 1998 hydroelectric-facility amendments do not decide the present classification.
- The County invokes the Buck Act, 4 U.S.C. §§106–110, and the judges dispute its application to immunity rather than federal-area taxation. The ordinance and enabling Alabama Act 406 are reproduced in the petition appendix, pp. 126–139; these are source copies rather than a newly reconstructed enactment.

### Preserved party and government arguments

**County.** The County argues that Alabama provides an adequate forum; the Tax Injunction Act bars federal interference; and a special exception should not be extended to judges litigating without the United States as a party. On the merits it treats the levy as a generally applicable charge measured by compensation to which §111 consents, notwithstanding the state-law occupational-license label. It invokes application to state as well as federal workers and the alternative-license arrangement.

**Judges.** Their brief pp. 17–20 argues that the County's own collection suit requests no injunction, suspension, restraint, or declaratory relief; removal changes the tribunal, not the relief. Pages 20–26 also invoke the federal-officer forum, official duties, a federal immunity defense, and judicial independence from executive permission to litigate. The judges contend that losing the federal defense on its merits does not retrospectively defeat a colorable removal basis. Pages 27–39 distinguish a tax on compensation from an asserted licensing condition on performing federal functions, relying on the ordinance's unlawful-work language, compensation entitlement rather than receipt, and full exemption for holders of other state licenses. They additionally invoke §111's nondiscrimination condition and Davis.

**United States.** Its retrieved merits amicus, document 08, is captioned as supporting the County. Its separate positions must not be merged with the County's. The United States argues that §1341 does not itself bar a defensive collection proceeding but that §1442(a)(3) does not authorize removal of personal tax liability merely because the compensation is earned in federal office. On tax merits it supports applicability of the federal salary-tax consent. This makes the official-act nexus a live independent jurisdictional fork. The stated characterization is the Government's argument, not a verified premise that official-duty nexus is absent.

### Pre-divergence Justice-specific anchors for clean validator

**Davis v. Michigan Department of Treasury, 489 U.S. 803 (1989).** Kennedy's Court opinion, joined by O'Connor and Scalia among others, evaluates source discrimination and whether different treatment rests on significant differences between the comparator classes. Stevens's dissent places the federal employees within the much broader generally taxed class and challenges the inference of prohibited discrimination from a limited state-benefit exemption. These are distinct, published approaches; neither answers whether the Alabama license exemption or the judges' asserted licensing burden is legally equivalent. Full-text Cornell reproduction was cross-checked against the [official Reports PDF](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep489/usrep489803/usrep489803.pdf), especially Court pp. 814–817 and Stevens pp. 818–830. Local sources: `OT_1998CHUNK10_B_authority_davis.txt`, `OT_1998CHUNK10_B_official_davis.pdf/.txt`.

**Mesa v. California, 489 U.S. 121 (1989).** O'Connor wrote for a unanimous Court that federal-officer removal requires a federal defense; federal employment alone does not suffice. Its discussion distinguishes the office nexus and federal-defense requirements. [Cornell full opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/489/121) is available locally as `OT_1998CHUNK10_B_authority_mesa.txt`; a Library of Congress PDF attempt timed out, so no official-PDF cross-check is claimed. The official 1998 removal text and the original parties' actual dispute provide separate primary support for the jurisdictional issue. This anchor permits a Justice-specific inquiry into colorability and nexus; it does not resolve either by assuming the tax defense succeeds or fails.
