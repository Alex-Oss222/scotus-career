## Dates, posture and trial instruction

1. **Trial date:** the JA I table of contents and chronology give **June 18–21, 1990**. Verdict forms are dated **June 21, 1990** (JA 200–201); the district court independently identifies the June trial and September 19 sentencing (JA 384, 387–388). A preparation reference to a May 1990 trial should be corrected by the fresh validator. September 19, 1990 is the judge's final sentencing date, not the jury-trial date.
2. JA 200–201 records guilty verdicts of capital murder, robbery and abduction, with life sentences for robbery and abduction. The district court states that the jury recommended death on the capital count, which the judge imposed September 19 (JA 384). Respondent brief p.8 states the jury found both vileness and future dangerousness. The actual penalty verdict sheet and complete penalty transcript have not been read in this task.
3. The capital instruction required proof beyond reasonable doubt that Strickler killed Whitlock, willfully/deliberately/premeditatedly, during a listed capital predicate. It defined a deadly weapon by the object's capacity to cause death or serious injury in the manner and circumstances used. The instruction distinguished a person merely aiding and abetting from an active and immediate participant in the acts causing death, and expressly allowed capital conviction for joint participation in the fatal beating. It did **not** require proof that Strickler alone dropped the rock. JA 159–161.
4. The given predicate instruction included armed robbery, abduction with intent to defile or extort pecuniary benefit, and rape language. The parties' merits briefs agree that abduction of a person over twelve with intent to defile was not a valid statutory capital predicate at the offense date. Respondent pp.45–48 describes the direct instruction challenge as defaulted in state habeas and the related ineffective-assistance claim as rejected; petitioner pp.35–36 disputes the effect of the invalid predicate on the surviving judgment and criticizes reliance on a later alternative theory. The fresh validator must preserve scope/preservation distinctions rather than silently convert this Brady proceeding into a new standalone instruction claim.
5. Defense closing asked for first-degree rather than capital murder, accepting enough of the abduction/robbery evidence to contest active killing participation. It argued that physical links could arise from an earlier struggle, challenged Tudor's credibility, and emphasized the absence of direct proof of which man delivered the fatal blows. JA 183–193, especially 191–193. These are counsel's trial arguments; their legal consequences for Brady are disputed.

## The eight documents and disclosure dispute

The district court expressly separates five undisputedly withheld documents, Exhibits **1, 3, 4, 5 and 6**, from disputed disclosure of **2, 7 and 8**. It used the disputed items to show relationships and contradictions, but held that the five alone sufficed even assuming the other three had been disclosed. JA 392. Respondent's merits brief p.13 says the district court did not mention 2, 7 or 8; that assertion conflicts with the actual text of JA 392 and should not be treated as the record. The petitioner reply pp.3–4 identifies the conflict.

The prosecutor said that 2, 7 and 8 were in his open file; trial counsel Bobbitt and Henderson's counsel Franklin supplied contrary recollections. Claytor recalled giving his typed report to the Rockingham prosecutor but not distributing the other documents before/during trial. The district court treated disclosure of 2, 7 and 8 as genuinely disputed and the five others as not genuinely disputed. JA 397–401. It accepted co-counsel Roberts's recollection as true for summary-judgment purposes but found that his general awareness that the witness's story evolved and she had delayed coming forward did not establish that the five documents' specific contents had been disclosed. JA 399–400. A finding that Roberts lacked credibility was not the basis for that summary-judgment ruling.

The district court also considered the June 17, 1990 Roanoke newspaper account. It acknowledged overlapping information, including viewing victim photos with Dean, while finding that the article did not convey most of the suppressed details. JA 400. Respondent relies on the article and Roberts's recollection as showing the defense knew the substance; petitioner disputes equivalence. This is a live source-grounded disagreement, not proof that every document was undisclosed or every detail was new.

## Testimony, physical evidence and phase-specific party submissions

**Eyewitness scope:** Stoltzfus testified about the mall encounters and abduction, not the killing. The district court reports the prosecutor identifying her as the only abduction eyewitness. JA 387. Her confident trial identifications and detailed account differ from the early expressions of uncertainty reproduced in the documents. Whether those differences establish unreliability, confabulation, ordinary memory reconstruction, or police suggestion is disputed; the source materials alone are not a finding of fabricated evidence or intentional prosecutorial concealment.

**Other evidence described in the submissions:** a security guard and acquaintance placed the men at the mall; Massie identified Strickler as driving the blue car toward the field; Strickler subsequently possessed the car and the victim's property, including a bank card and earrings; the State cited forensic evidence, blood/dirty clothing, and Tudor's account of a conversation about a rock killing. Respondent pp.1–6, 45–49; petitioner pp.7–10, 33–36. The parties dispute the weight, interpretation and independence of those facts. Massie's visibility, the number and identity of vehicle occupants, and uncertainty about physical evidence must not be replaced with a more precise finding than the underlying testimony supports.

**Henderson-related defense evidence:** the district court described his bloody clothing, possession of the victim's watch and vehicle, wallet near the body, and Workman's account of a same-night admission to killing a person identified by a racial epithet. JA 395; petitioner p.34 n.13. The defense relied on those matters to contest which participant led or actively killed. Such evidence does not itself negate joint active participation.

**Tudor:** defense closing attacked her account, her incentive to shift suspicion, the chronology, her attribution of overheard statements, and the claimed blood observations; the prosecutor defended the account and argued that the men acted jointly. JA 189–198. Petitioner reply pp.12–17 disputes respondent's description of her account as undisputed. It separately alleges a June 1994 letter admitting perjury and states that photographs and a defense expert's authenticity assessment were lodged. Those lodged materials have not been recovered/read here; the allegation is not certified as a genuine recantation or adjudicated fact, and cannot simply be added to the original jury record.

**Capital-guilt argument:** petitioner pp.33–36 contends that destroying confidence in the supposedly disinterested eyewitness would change the whole evidentiary picture, undermine the abduction/armed-robbery predicates and permit conviction of a lesser offense. Respondent pp.44–49 contends that circumstantial, possession, medical and Tudor evidence independently establish abduction/armed robbery and active participation, and that the 69-pound rock satisfies the deadly-weapon concept under Virginia law. Both the actual instruction and the different valid/invalid predicate issues must be retained when these submissions are evaluated.

**Death-selection argument:** petitioner p.36 expressly makes an additional punishment argument even if capital guilt is supported: the witness alone portrayed Strickler as the violent instigator/leader at the mall and supported an inference of leadership in the murder. Petitioner reply p.12 n.8 cites **Sentencing Transcript p.15** for the prosecutor's reliance on that portrayal when seeking death. That is a specific predecision brief citation and quotation; the complete sentencing transcript itself has not been recovered in this task. Respondent p.49 answers that Tudor attributed the rock-killing statement to Strickler and emphasizes equal capital culpability of active joint perpetrators. The submitted response concerning equal eligibility should remain accurately attributed; this handoff makes no independent ruling on whether it resolves the separate choice between death and life.

**Admissions dispute:** respondent pp.8–10 and 33–36 relies on counsel's guilt closing and Strickler's later presentence/allocution statements. The latter acknowledged presence at the mall, in the car and near the killing while blaming Henderson; petitioner reply p.16 states that he expressly denied murder, robbery and abduction in the presentence report. These later statements occurred after the jury's verdicts. Respondent invokes evidence potentially admissible on retrial; petitioner disputes the use, voluntariness and independence of these statements and argues that defense concessions were shaped by the missing evidence. Do not redescribe acknowledgment of presence as an unqualified murder confession, or counsel's argument as sworn testimony. The presentence report and complete September 19 transcript are not independently recovered here; the guilt closing itself is reproduced and read at JA 183–193.

## Default, discovery and lower decisions

The parties agree the Stoltzfus Brady claim was not presented in state court and is procedurally barred absent an applicable excuse. Petitioner's brief p.41 n.18 disputes the precise state procedural rule explaining present unavailability but accepts default. Neither party's source supports treating the Fourth Circuit as having accepted cause.

Trial counsel repeatedly used the prosecutor's open-file procedure without a formal Brady motion. State habeas began after appointment of counsel in December 1991; the petition filed September 1, 1992 included ineffective-assistance allegations concerning failure to seek Brady material and cross-examination, but no independent Stoltzfus suppression claim. Respondent's November 12, 1992 motion to dismiss asserted, in answering the ineffective-assistance allegations, that counsel had received full disclosure and all Brady information. These statements are reproduced/referenced at JA 212–213. Respondent stresses that they came after the petition had been filed, did not purport to report a new examination of every police file, and did not stop later federal discovery; petitioner says they reinforced the open-file assurance throughout pending state proceedings. Respondent pp.10–11, 28–30; petitioner pp.42–46.

Respondent says no state-habeas discovery motion under Virginia Rule 4:1(b)(5) was filed and points to known interviews, evolving recollection, press accounts and counsel's knowledge as grounds for investigation. It distinguishes discretionary access from entitlement and identifies other state capital cases in which police-file discovery was ordered. Petitioner invokes the open-file representations, nonpublic police documents, and the particular showing required to obtain state discovery. Neither a granted federal subpoena nor an unfiled state motion proves how a hypothetical state court would actually have ruled. Respondent pp.20–31; petitioner pp.42–45; reply pp.18–20.

JA 287 contains the federal district court's **January 16, 1997** cause ruling: defense counsel lacked independent access and the Commonwealth repeatedly withheld the material through state habeas. The final district order and opinion at JA 382–402 are marked **October 15, 1997**, although respondent's prose says October 16. The order granted the petitioner's summary-judgment motion on Claims J and U and denied the State's cross-motion. It expressly withdrew the ineffective-assistance issue by agreement and reached the suppression claim on the submitted materials. No determination here relies on the one-day date discrepancy; an eventual record should prefer the signed/filed document or identify any distinct docket-entry date.

The parties' briefs identify the Fourth Circuit's reversal as based on lack of cause and an alternative no-materiality/no-prejudice conclusion. The district court's favorable cause finding and final merits ruling therefore must not be substituted for the appellate judgment under review. The full appellate opinion at JA 403–434 was retrieved but not read in this task; parent neutral review should use its already recovered lower source or assign that limited remaining review.
