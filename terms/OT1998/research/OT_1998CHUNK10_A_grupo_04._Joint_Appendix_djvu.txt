FACTS 


The Notes and Defendants’ Default 


11. GMD issued the Notes on February 17, 1994 in 
the principal amount of $250 million. The Notes are a 
corporate obligation of GMD, the terms and conditions of 
which are contained in a note instrument, and rank pari 
passu with all other present or future unsecured and un- 
subordinated debt of GMD. 


12. The Notes are not registered under the Securities 
Act of 1933, and were offered and sold to investors, in- 
cluding plaintiffs, in a so-called “Eurobond offering” pur- 
suant to exceptions from the registration requirements of 
the federal securities laws. 


(26 
13. Plaintiffs are institutional investors and, in the 


aggregate, beneficially own approximately $75.8 million 
(principal amount) of the Notes. 


14. The Notes bear interest at an annual rate of 
814%, payable semi-annually in arrears in U.S. dollars 
on February 17 and August 17 of each year. 


15. The Issuer defaulted on the August 17, 1997 in- 
terest payment of approximately $10.4 million, including 
about $5.1 million due plaintiffs. 


16. The Notes are unconditionally and irrevocably 


guaranteed as to payment of principal and interest by the 
Guarantors pursuant to a guarantee instrument. 


17. Despite this “unconditional and irrevocable” guar- 
antee, the Guarantors have not paid the interest due on 
August 17, 1997. 


18. On December 11, 1997, each plaintiff duly caused 
the acceleration of the principal amount of the Notes it 
beneficially owns. 


19. As a result, defendants, jointly and severally, are 
currently indebted to plaintiffs in the amount of some 
$80.9 milion, representing some $75.8 million in principal 
plus about $5.1 million in interest through the date of 
this Complaint. 


20. All conditions precedent, if any, to the mainte- 
nance of this action and the granting of the monetary 
relief requested have occurred, have been waived by de- 
fendants or have otherwise been satisfied.

Creditors” ) :

GMD;

* Trade creditors, most of whom have invoices to

* Terminated employees who are owed severance

22. cpg Bnd per few years one of the principal 

activities of GMD (and two other major Mexican con- 


struction companies) has been the construction of a vast 
toll road system throughout Mexico. 


23. Much of the Mexican toll road system has not 
been financially successful. Toll road traffic and revenue 
is far below originally projected levels. Lenders that fi- 
nanced construction of the toll roads have billions of 
dollars of debt in default and GMD, along with the other 
two major Mexican construction companies, have unpaid 
invoices (the “Receivables”) related to their work in build- 
ing the toll roads. In total, these unpaid Receivables are 
several hundred million dollars. 


24. In an effort to address these systemic problems, 
the Mexican Government's Secretariat of Communications 
and Transportation (the “SCT”), by special decree dated 
August 27, 1997 (the “SCT Decree”), announced a res- 
cue plan for 23 of the country’s most financially troubled 
toll roads (the “Toll Road Restructuring Plan”). 


25. All of the toll roads encompassed by the Toll 
Road Restructuring Plan were owned and operated by 
special purpose entities known as “concessionaires.” The 
dominant stockholder, in the case of most of these con- 
cessionaires, was GMD or one of the other two major 
Mexican construction companies. The principal indebted- 


28 


ness of the concessionaires was project finance loans pro- 
vided, on a road-by-road basis, by virtually every Mexican 
bank. This concessionaire bank debt represents a multi- 
billion dollar liability. 

26. The cornerstone of the Toll Road Restructuring 
Plan is the commitment by the Mexican Government to 
guarantee billions of dollars of rights or notes issued or 
to be issued by a special purpose trust of the Mexican 
Government (the “Government Notes”). Most of the 
Government Notes will be used to pay back the conces- 
sionaire bank debt, in full, cancelling that indebtedness. 
The remainder of the Government Notes are to be paid or 
distributed to, among others, GMD (or its designees), 
following an audit of the Receivables that remain unpaid 
to defendants by the concessionaires. As a result of these 
measures, the Mexican Government will, in effect, become 
the new owner and operator of all the toll roads encom- 
passed in the Toll Road Restructuring Plan.
