### Issue 2: Discrimination against foreign corporations

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Commerce Clause merits after rejecting preclusion.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Alabama's franchise-tax structure burdens foreign corporations on actual capital employed while permitting domestic corporations to set par value well below actual value. The domestic shares tax does not compensate because it reaches a different event and is not a substantially equivalent intrastate burden.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Alabama's tax structure discriminates against foreign corporations, and the domestic shares tax does not qualify as a compensatory burden.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The practical tax bases favor domestic corporations competing with foreign corporations. Under *Oregon Waste* and *West Lynn Creamery*, labels cannot substitute for equivalence in event, purpose, and burden, which Alabama failed to establish.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding permits evenhanded franchise taxation and genuinely equivalent compensatory charges.

### Issue 3: Remedy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the need for a constitutionally adequate state remedy and leaves its precise form to Alabama in the first instance.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The remedy must cure the discriminatory exaction consistently with *McKesson*, but federal law does not require the Court to choose among all lawful state remedial mechanisms on this record.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** State law may supply a refund, retrospective equalization, prospective correction, or another adequate mechanism, provided it does not preserve the unconstitutional competitive disadvantage.
