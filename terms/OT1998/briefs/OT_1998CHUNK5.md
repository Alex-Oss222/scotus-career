## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Presidential public finance equalization

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Objective eligibility and historical support do not create a constitutional duty of parity.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Federal subsidies are government spending rules and cannot make exercise of campaign speech contingent on surrendering constitutional rights; here no ceiling restrains nonrecipient political expression and the statutory support gradient is tied to ascertainable past vote totals. There is no constitutional command to fund competing parties equally, although administration must not discriminate by viewpoint. General dissatisfaction with commission partisanship does not establish personal redressable injury.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Objective eligibility and historical support do not create a constitutional duty of parity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Buckley v. Valeo upheld public-financing distinctions based on prior electoral support while reserving a properly supported discriminatory application. The Party received a substantial subsidy and argues that the contribution cap prevents matching the major parties’ larger subsidy; inequality alone repeats the feature Buckley sustained. It does not establish that the rules actually suppress its candidacies below the position available without public financing. The contribution limit and the voluntary spending condition are distinct: nonrecipients do not accept a subsidy-based spending ceiling, but generally applicable contribution restrictions remain. No viewpoint discrimination or concrete exclusionary administration is demonstrated here. Stone would sustain the formula on the presented record while leaving a factually different discriminatory-application challenge available.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No declaration that all major-party advantages or commission structures are immune from evidence of deliberate exclusion or unlawful source-based restrictions.

### Issue 2: FEC composition claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented question within the established review posture and the stated preservation limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Standing and authorized enforcement must be demonstrated independently.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Federal subsidies are government spending rules and cannot make exercise of campaign speech contingent on surrendering constitutional rights; here no ceiling restrains nonrecipient political expression and the statutory support gradient is tied to ascertainable past vote totals. There is no constitutional command to fund competing parties equally, although administration must not discriminate by viewpoint. General dissatisfaction with commission partisanship does not establish personal redressable injury.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Standing and authorized enforcement must be demonstrated independently.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute prevents a single party from occupying more than three of six seats; it does not reserve seats by name to Democrats and Republicans or prohibit appointment of Reform Party members. Striking that cap would allow the same six appointees and would not itself increase the prospect of a Reform appointment. Lujan therefore defeats the asserted causal and redressability chain on these allegations. The proper consequence is dismissal of this composition claim without deciding that every partisan practice or appointment arrangement is constitutional. The lower judgment’s separate damages and certification holdings remain outside the specified merits grant.
