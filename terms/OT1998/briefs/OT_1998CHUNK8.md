### Issue 1: All Writs authority of a military appellate court

### Issue 1: Summary judgment on racial predominance

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Johnson v. Miller (June 29, 1995) holds that predominant racial consideration in composing a common territorial electorate does not itself establish unequal treatment or trigger strict scrutiny, even when traditional criteria are subordinated. A cognizable status, participation or other constitutional injury remains actionable under its own elements. Thus a factual contest between race and party cannot alone make the asserted racial-predominance theory viable; first identify the deprivation claimed.
* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 56.
* **Material Other Authority:** When race and party are highly correlated, supported political and racial explanations must be tested through ordinary factfinding rather than resolved against the nonmovant on a disputed record.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** A three-judge district court entered summary judgment invalidating District 12 as an unconstitutional racial gerrymander.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Plaintiffs rely on race’s role in drawing the district; the State offers partisan explanations and contests both facts and the legal theory. Johnson v. Miller requires an independently cognizable unequal-treatment injury and does not make racial predominance alone actionable. The disputed motive must be material to such a claim.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

An injunction based only on predominance cannot stand under current law. A preserved independent participation or status injury may warrant ordinary evidentiary proceedings; no trial is required solely to resolve a factual dispute immaterial under the governing rule.

### Issue 1: Planned warrantless forfeiture seizure of a parked automobile

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Pennsylvania v. Labron; Pennsylvania v. Kilgore* permits a warrantless evidentiary search of a readily mobile vehicle when probable cause exists and rejects an additional exigency requirement, while confining that summary holding to the automobile-search setting and leaving other scope questions open. *United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property* separates forfeiture authority from the process required to seize property and requires particularized justification for dispensing with prior judicial process.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Federal automobile exception without an additional exigency requirement; pre-seizure process for civil forfeiture of real property, distinguished as to the movable property category but relevant to source separation.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourth Amendment; Florida forfeiture statute; warrant and seizure doctrine.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Carroll*, *Chambers*, and *Coolidge* distinguish contemporaneous evidence searches justified by vehicle mobility from a planned forfeiture seizure undertaken after officers have had ample time to obtain process.
* **Open Point:** Whether *Labron*'s no-additional-exigency rule for an evidentiary automobile search also authorizes a planned seizure of the vehicle itself as forfeitable property months after the alleged facilitating use.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Supreme Court of Florida suppressed evidence obtained after officers seized White’s car without a warrant and inventoried it.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Florida invokes probable cause that the car was forfeitable and the practical mobility of vehicles. White distinguishes seizure for retrospective forfeiture from an evidentiary automobile search and points to the absence of an immediate need to act. Labron’s rule and Good’s real-property process rule each have limited fields.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The court must decide the seizure authority before treating an inventory as lawful. Extending the automobile rationale to this forfeiture seizure requires justification; rejecting that extension does not alter Labron’s existing probable-cause search rule or Good’s distinct real-property holding.

### Issue 1: Planned warrantless forfeiture seizure of a parked automobile

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone distinguishes *Labron*. Its automobile exception concerns a contemporaneous evidentiary search of a readily mobile vehicle believed to contain contraband or evidence; it does not create a standing warrant exception for a planned civil-forfeiture seizure based on historical use. Probable cause establishes the basis for a warrant, but where officers have a reasonable opportunity to obtain one and identify no concrete urgency, it does not itself excuse neutral judicial process.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Police may not make a planned warrantless seizure of a parked automobile for civil forfeiture solely because probable cause exists and the vehicle is inherently mobile. They identified no attempted flight, immediate transfer, evidence-destruction risk, or other reason process could not be obtained. *Labron* therefore does not govern, and the later inventory search cannot validate the antecedent unlawful seizure.
