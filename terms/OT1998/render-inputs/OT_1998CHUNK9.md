## Event

Richardson v. United States; No. 97-8629. Merits decision, June 1, 1999. Argued February 22, 1999. On review of the Seventh Circuit's judgment, 130 F.3d 765, affirming Richardson's continuing-criminal-enterprise conviction. The question is whether jurors must agree on the particular drug violations composing the continuing series under 21 U.S.C. section 848.

The trial charge required at least three narcotics violations, including one after March 24, 1989, but expressly permitted jurors to disagree about which violations occurred. Richardson objected to the refusal of a requested predicate-unanimity instruction. The indictment alleged repeated distribution and possession offenses; the reported conspiracy and CCE convictions are not separate verdicts on three particular distributions. The number and date conditions just described belong to the trial charge; the Court does not separately construe a statutory minimum number of violations.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No recusal or nonparticipation is established.

## Public Action

The Seventh Circuit's judgment is vacated insofar as it sustained the challenged continuing-criminal-enterprise conviction, and the case is remanded. The vote is 6-3.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Supporting vacatur and remand:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. **Opposing:** O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg, who would affirm against this unanimity objection.

The Seventh Circuit must consider in the first instance whether the preserved instructional error is subject to harmless-error review and, if so, whether it is harmless under the governing standard. The CCE conviction must be set aside unless it may lawfully be sustained in proceedings consistent with the predicate-unanimity rule. The Court decides neither harmlessness question and orders no automatic new trial, acquittal, release or fixed sentence. Ordinary double-jeopardy and retrial principles remain applicable. The separate conspiracy conviction receives no independent disposition here.

## Holdings

### Unanimous agreement on the constituent violations

**Operative rule:** In a prosecution under 21 U.S.C. section 848, each particular violation necessary to compose the continuing series is an element that the jury must find unanimously beyond a reasonable doubt. Jurors must agree on the same qualifying violations, although they need not agree on every evidentiary detail or means of committing an agreed violation; an instruction expressly allowing disagreement about the constituent violations is erroneous.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, supplies six votes for this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute makes a continuing series of independently criminal violations part of the greater offense. The additional requirements of management of five or more persons and substantial income or resources do not turn those violations into interchangeable evidence. Otherwise jurors could agree only that the defendant ran a criminal business while relying on wholly different crimes to supply the required series. Gaudin assigns actual offense elements to the jury; the statutory language here identifies the elements to which that protection attaches. Rutledge distinguishes the series requirement from the narcotics agreement, so the conspiracy verdict does not itself establish the required predicate transactions.

Schad permits genuine alternative means of committing an offense, but its traditional alternatives for a single homicide do not answer whether distinct criminal episodes constitute this statutory series. The breadth of the violations that could otherwise be combined reinforces the ordinary unanimity reading and avoids serious constitutional doubt without deciding a universal constitutional classification rule. The Government's focus on the continuing enterprise explains the offense's target; it does not erase the enacted violations requirement.

The charge expressly permitted the disagreement the statute forbids. The Court does not require unanimity about every item of proof, every overt act, the identity of each supervised person or every managerial relationship. Those distinct questions are not decided. Roy's treatment of a personal-intent omission and Sullivan's treatment of a defective reasonable-doubt standard retain their own scopes. Neither supplies a present classification of this express nonunanimity instruction, and the remand leaves both availability and application of harmless-error review open.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applied to jury determination of identified offense elements; it does not itself resolve the antecedent elements-versus-means question or make every instructional defect structural.
- **Rutledge v. United States:** Applied to the distinction between an agreement and the continuing series. Its duplicate-conviction rule remains intact but supplies no unpresented Count One remedy here.
- **Schad v. Arizona:** Distinguished because accepted alternative means for a traditional single offense do not establish that separate episodes composing this federal series are mere evidence. Its result and bounded elements/means distinction remain intact.
- **Griffin v. United States:** Its treatment of a factually unsupported lawful conspiracy object does not resolve the independent unanimity defect expressly reserved by that decision.
- **California v. Roy:** Its rejection of automatic reversal for the personal-intent omission and its qualified actual-verdict inquiry remain effective within their stated category; neither is extended here to decide reviewability of express predicate nonunanimity.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Its structural rule for a vitiated reasonable-doubt standard remains intact; this decision does not decide whether the present defect has the same consequence.
- **Johnson v. United States:** Its forfeited-error disposition does not replace the governing rules for this preserved objection.
- **Jones v. United States:** Its statutory identification of offense-defining facts remains consistent with the present construction; no universal constitutional maximum-fact rule is added here.

## Law After Decision

From June 1, 1999, section 848 requires unanimous agreement on the particular violations necessary to constitute the continuing series. Series-wide agreement alone cannot satisfy that requirement when the jurors are expressly permitted to rely on different violations. The distinction between offense elements and evidentiary means survives. No separately contested minimum number of violations, supervisee-identification rule or universal constitutional element test is decided.

Harmless-error availability and application remain unresolved for this defect. Sufficient evidence to convict is not itself an adjudication that the actual instructional error was harmless. If constitutional harmless-error review is available, its applicable beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden remains the Government's; the Court does not decide that the inquiry applies or that its showing succeeds or fails.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor and Ginsburg, dissenting:** The statute defines a continuing criminal business through its duration, management of five or more persons, and substantial income or resources. On this reading, the jury must agree on the continuing series and the other enterprise elements, while individual transactions are means of proving that same enterprise's operation. Schad recognizes a bounded legislative choice between elements and means; Gaudin protects actual elements after that choice is identified. The dissent regards the interrelated enterprise requirements as sufficient limits and does not authorize combining unrelated crimes without restraint.

The dissent therefore finds no instructional error and would affirm. It does not announce a present harmlessness finding. Its members' conditional acceptance of an initial appellate inquiry under the Court's contrary construction supplies neither a vote for vacatur nor assent to the Court's predicate-unanimity rule.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Seventh Circuit for the two reserved harmlessness questions and proceedings consistent with the statutory rule. The CCE conviction cannot be sustained on the erroneous instruction alone; lawful sustainment or further relief depends on that examination. No new evidentiary proceeding, automatic retrial, acquittal, release, sentence or future Supreme Court return is ordered. The separate conspiracy count is not reopened by this decision.

## Event

Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District; No. 98-1541. Merits decision, June 3, 1999, after submission on the merits. On review of the Second Circuit's judgment, 163 F.3d 749. The question is whether Title IX displaces section 1983 enforcement of the statutory sex-discrimination right or an independently secured Equal Protection right.

The student alleged harassment by male sixth-grade classmates during the 1993-94 school year. A jury returned a general defense verdict on the Title IX claim against the District. The Second Circuit affirmed that judgment, upheld dismissal of the District and Board section 1983 claims on statutory-displacement grounds, and separately upheld qualified immunity for teacher Parker and assistant superintendent Race. It did not decide displacement as to those individuals. The present review concerns the displacement question; no argument date is established.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at merits submission and decision: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No recusal or nonparticipation is established.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed insofar as it sustains dismissal of the District and Board section 1983 claims on Title IX-displacement grounds, and the case is remanded. The constitutional-claim component is unanimous; the statutory-claim component is 8-1.

## Holdings

### 1. Independent constitutional claims remain available

**Operative rule:** Title IX does not displace a section 1983 claim enforcing an independently secured Equal Protection right merely because the same school harassment also supports a Title IX theory. The claimant must establish the constitutional violation, state action, defendant-specific responsibility or proper municipal attribution, causation and an available remedy; correcting displacement supplies none of those elements.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion on this proposition and the common remedial limits is joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Fourteenth Amendment imposes duties independent of a funding recipient's statutory obligations. Blessing requires attention to the particular right and actual congressional foreclosure, not an assumption that overlapping facts create identical rights. The funding-termination process and the implied Title IX action do not provide an exclusive personal remedial system incompatible with the independent constitutional claim. The differing duties and defendants make categorical extinction especially unwarranted.

Sea Clammers and Smith v. Robinson concerned actual incompatibility with the statutory enforcement designs before the Court; neither holds that every private federal action extinguishes constitutional remedies arising from the same events. Kinman kept Title IX and constitutional supervisory and municipal liability distinct. Gebser and Davis refine responsibility under Title IX; they do not turn every peer's misconduct into unconstitutional official action or silently repeal Equal Protection enforcement.

The District and Board claims were foreclosed categorically and must receive ordinary consideration. The individual defendants prevailed on an independent immunity ground, and the general Title IX verdict establishes no new, specified factual finding in this Court. Neither judgment is overturned by correcting displacement. Purposeful official discrimination, municipal policy or final-authority attribution, causation and any actual preclusive effect remain separate questions; institutional rank, negligence and receipt of funds do not alone prove them.

### 2. The identified statutory right is not categorically foreclosed under section 1983

**Operative rule:** Title IX's individual right against sex discrimination in a covered educational program is not categorically excluded from section 1983 enforcement by the administrative funding process or the established implied private action. A statutory section 1983 claim must prove the actual Title IX right and violation, including the statute's defendant coverage and recipient-responsibility conditions; the enforcement vehicle does not expand the underlying duty.

**Authority:** Thomas's statutory portion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Controlling explanation:** Blessing separates identification of an individual statutory right from the further inquiry whether Congress excluded section 1983 expressly or through an incompatible remedial scheme. This claim identifies a student's right against sex discrimination, rather than an aggregate demand for better program administration. Title IX contains no express exclusion. Its funding oversight does not give this student an exclusive adjudicatory forum, and the implied action recognized in Cannon and Franklin supplies no elaborate mandatory claim-processing route that section 1983 would evade.

The principal objection is that another enforcement label might undo the limits of the recipient's funding obligation. That objection is answered by enforcing the actual statutory right: section 1983 does not make a nonrecipient liable under Title IX, erase statutory attribution conditions or create a new harassment standard. The scheme therefore does not require categorical foreclosure of the identified right. The remand permits consideration of properly pleaded statutory claims against the District and Board subject to those limits, ordinary defenses and the existing verdict's demonstrated legal effect. No merits finding or new trial follows automatically.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Blessing v. Freestone:** Applied to the separate inquiries into a particular individual right and actual congressional foreclosure; its rejection of aggregate program-compliance claims remains intact.
- **Cannon v. University of Chicago:** Preserved as the source of Title IX's established implied private action; that action's existence does not itself demonstrate exclusivity.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools:** Preserved as recognizing compensatory relief for established intentional Title IX discrimination; this decision creates no new liability or damages finding.
- **Middlesex County Sewerage Authority v. National Sea Clammers Association:** Distinguished by the incompatible enforcement structure there; the actual foreclosure principle remains available when established.
- **Smith v. Robinson:** Distinguished by the remedial incompatibility there; factual overlap alone does not reproduce that holding.
- **Kinman v. Omaha Public School District:** Applied to the separation of statutory recipient liability, personal constitutional responsibility and municipal attribution, without treating its adjudication as an earlier decision of every preclusion question.
- **Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District:** Its controlling teacher-attribution route and unappealed-claim limits remain unchanged; no current rule is used to reopen the separate immunity judgment here.
- **Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education:** Its peer-harassment conditions govern actual statutory recipient liability, but do not extinguish an independent official-discrimination claim or establish liability on this record.

## Law After Decision

From June 3, 1999, Title IX supplies no categorical bar to the independently sourced Equal Protection claims or the identified statutory-right claims enforced through section 1983 here. The constitutional holding has nine votes; the statutory enforcement holding has eight. Different sources of rights retain their different substantive conditions and defendant coverage.

The statutory enforcement vehicle does not enlarge the Title IX duty. The independent constitutional claim requires its own purposeful discrimination and responsibility; it is not a means of treating all peer harassment or negligent supervision as unconstitutional conduct. Existing Title IX attribution law, conduct-date immunity rules, municipal-liability requirements and valid preclusion defenses remain in force.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** The Chief joins the independent Equal Protection holding and its limited remand. A funding statute does not silently remove the Fourteenth Amendment's independently sourced duties. Each defendant must have the requisite discriminatory purpose or legally sufficient deliberate conduct, causal responsibility and actionable injury; supervisory title alone does not suffice. Municipal liability requires its own policy or final-decision link, and personal immunity is measured under conduct-date law.

He would retain dismissal of the duplicative Title IX statutory-right route. Section 1983 cannot be used to circumvent Congress's deliberate funding-recipient liability limits by relabeling the same statutory right. He therefore would restore only the independent constitutional claims improperly foreclosed by displacement. He would leave the favorable Title IX verdict intact absent a separately preserved trial error with the required prejudice showing; correcting constitutional pleading itself supplies no such error and defeats no properly established preclusion defense. He does not find a constitutional violation, revive claims defeated on independent immunity grounds or award relief on the merits.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Second Circuit for ordinary proceedings on the District and Board section 1983 claims within the two controlling holdings. Proper pleading, defendant coverage, constitutional intent, statutory attribution, municipal responsibility, causation, injury and supported defenses remain to be adjudicated. The Title IX general defense verdict and Race/Parker immunity affirmance remain in place. No new trial, damages, reopening of individual claims or future Supreme Court filing is directed.

The case is decided after merits submission; no oral-argument date is supplied.

## Event
Reno v. Goncalves, No. 98-835. Merits decision, June 3, 1999. Argued April 21, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit, 144 F.3d 110, which reversed the dismissal of Goncalves's habeas petition and required administrative consideration of his request for discretionary relief. The questions concern whether section 2241 jurisdiction survives the 1996 immigration amendments, whether AEDPA section 440(d) governs a former section 212(c) application pending at enactment, and the interpretive weight due Matter of Soriano. Goncalves applied in September 1994, received an adverse discretionary decision in January 1995, and had a timely administrative appeal pending on April 24, 1996. The Board later applied section 440(d)'s eligibility bar. Following immigration custody, he filed this habeas petition in August 1997.

## Participation
All nine Justices participated in argument and decision.

## Public Action
The judgment of the First Circuit is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy
Affirmed, 6–3. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing the eligibility disposition: Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. All nine sustain district-court jurisdiction over the actual section 2241 petition. The Board shall consider Goncalves's former section 212(c) application under the eligibility law applicable to the pending application when AEDPA took effect and exercise the discretion that law supplies. The immigration judge's earlier discretionary denial does not replace the Board's decision on the timely appeal. No favorable exercise of discretion, waiver, release or termination of deportation proceedings is awarded; equal protection is not adjudicated.

## Holdings
#### I. The actual statutory habeas channel survives

**Holding and operative rule:** AEDPA and IIRIRA did not clearly withdraw the independently enacted jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. section 2241 to decide Goncalves's substantial legal claim that the agency unlawfully denied eligibility for discretionary relief. That channel reaches substantial statutory and constitutional objections to lawful executive authority when its ordinary custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other predicates are met; it does not authorize judicial reweighing of discretionary equities or retrial of disputed deportability facts.

**Authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by every other Justice, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Kolster distinguishes withdrawal of ordinary INA review from repeal of the independently enacted writ and includes unlawful denial of legal eligibility within the writ's conditional scope. Here there is actual custody, an actual petition and a completed administrative legal determination; no separate jurisdictional defect is established. Felker, McCardle and Yerger require attention to the particular channel Congress withdrew. AEDPA section 401(e) deletes the specialized INA habeas provision; section 440(a) limits ordinary direct review. Neither clearly repeals section 2241. IIRIRA section 309(c)(4)(G) bars the covered transitional appeal but does not silently eliminate the independent writ. Reyes-Hernandez and Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee confine section 242(g) to the named executive actions; this legal challenge to completed adjudication is not absorbed into that provision. The permanent section 242(b)(9) regime cannot be imported wholesale into these transitional proceedings. The Government's combined-channel argument therefore cannot do what the particular provisions do not establish. Jurisdiction rests on the surviving statute, not the seriousness of a claim alone. Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee's separate section 1331 constitutional-selection route is neither needed nor enlarged. The Court does not decide the Suspension Clause's independent minimum or license review of discretionary merits.

#### II. Section 440(d) does not govern this pending application

**Holding and operative rule:** AEDPA section 440(d)'s new categorical eligibility restriction does not govern a former section 212(c) application filed before AEDPA took effect on April 24, 1996 and still awaiting final administrative action at enactment. Goncalves's pending administrative appeal must be considered under the former eligibility rule; the holding neither covers applications first filed after enactment nor determines the distinct temporal operation of IIRIRA's later repeal.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; the six-Justice full-opinion coalition stated in Opinion Topology directly adopts this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf and Rivers first require identification of Congress's temporal choice for the particular provision. Lindh permits ordinary statutory construction to establish that choice through express and omitted temporal directions. Sections 413(g) and 421(b) expressly reach pending applications, including applications for discretionary relief; section 440(d) does not. Section 440(f)'s separate instruction concerns subsection (e), not subsection (d). The enactment thus supplies more than silence considered alone. The objection that the Attorney General is deciding whether to grant present discretionary relief overlooks Congress's express treatment of pending discretionary requests elsewhere. Treating all eligibility restrictions as automatically applicable to such requests would erase that distinction. This amendment changes legal eligibility, not merely the tribunal or channel of review. Goncalves had invoked the discretionary process and pursued an appeal before enactment. The provision-specific construction preserves that pending application without requiring an invented plea bargain, subjective reliance or a vested right to a favorable waiver. Hincapie-Nieto, Salazar-Haro and Kolster resolved different review-channel restrictions; they do not settle section 440(d)'s substantive eligibility change. Reyes-Hernandez's completed surrender of a defense is not the exclusive possible temporal predicate, and no such surrender is found here. The Court adopts no rule that pending status alone preserves every former law.

#### III. Agency interpretation cannot override this statutory conclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Matter of Soriano receives no controlling weight on section 440(d)'s temporal application because ordinary interpretation resolves that question against the agency's construction; its reasoning is not persuasive enough to overcome the enacted distinctions. The Attorney General likewise cannot conclusively determine the district court's section 2241 jurisdiction, and the Board must decide the pending request without applying section 440(d) as an eligibility bar.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court; the same six-Justice coalition directly adopts this rule and its administrative remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** MCI enforces the limits of enacted authority; NationsBank and Aguirre-Aguirre allow agency interpretation within delegated authority and permissible statutory meaning. Those decisions do not empower an agency to override a temporal choice established through ordinary construction. Soriano gives insufficient effect to the express treatment of pending requests in neighboring provisions and would make silence in section 440(d) carry a consequence that the statutory structure does not support. The Court does not replace the existing deference framework or hold that every temporal question excludes agency expertise. Authorized judgments on genuinely unresolved statutory details, policy and technical administration retain their proper weight. The judgment removes a legal barrier to consideration; it does not decide how the Board must balance the lawful discretionary factors. Equal protection remains outside the grant.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Kolster v. INS:** Applied to an actual petition satisfying the previously reserved predicates; legal eligibility falls within its retained authority category, while discretionary reweighing remains excluded.
- **Felker v. Turpin:** Applied for channel-specific repeal analysis; withdrawal of one review mechanism does not itself repeal a separate jurisdictional grant.
- **Ex parte McCardle:** Its effect is confined to the appellate route Congress withdrew; it does not establish repeal of every independent habeas route.
- **Ex parte Yerger:** Supports preservation of an independently available writ despite withdrawal of another review channel.
- **Reyes-Hernandez v. INS:** Supplies the named-action boundary under section 242(g); its completed surrender of a defense is a distinct temporal predicate, not a universal prerequisite.
- **Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee:** Supplies the named-action/completed-adjudication distinction. Its separate constitutional section 1331 route is not extended.
- **Hincapie-Nieto v. INS:** Its ordinary-review restriction and rejection of pending status alone remain controlling; they do not decide section 440(d)'s different eligibility change.
- **Salazar-Haro v. INS:** Its review-channel rule remains intact and supplies no general immediate-effect rule for every substantive eligibility restriction.
- **Landgraf v. USI Film Products:** Supplies the sequence requiring identification of Congress's temporal choice; the Court applies ordinary statutory construction without adopting a universal pending-case exception.
- **Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc.:** Preserves provision-specific temporal analysis and supplies no agency power to override Congress's choice.
- **Lindh v. Murphy:** Its express/omitted temporal comparison is applied to these provisions, not extended to every AEDPA amendment.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T:** Requires adherence to the enacted bounds of agency authority.
- **NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.:** Preserves agency interpretation only within permissible statutory meaning and delegated authority.
- **INS v. Aguirre-Aguirre:** Retains appropriate immigration deference for authorized unresolved choices; it does not authorize a contrary temporal rule after ordinary construction resolves Congress's choice.

## Law After Decision
The surviving section 2241 route can decide this covered criminal alien's substantial legal eligibility challenge when its actual predicates are satisfied. Applications for former section 212(c) relief already pending on April 24, 1996 remain outside section 440(d)'s new eligibility bar. Eligibility permits the agency to exercise its lawful discretion; it does not predetermine the result. Ordinary-review restrictions, later-filed applications and IIRIRA's distinct repeal remain governed by their own provisions. No precedent is overruled and no general right to review discretionary immigration decisions is created.

## Separate Writings
Stone-Zsela concurs and joins the Court's opinion. He also explains that Goncalves had invoked the former process, obtained an immigration-judge decision on discretionary merits and pursued an administrative appeal before enactment. Removing eligibility at that stage attaches a new legal disability to the pending proceeding, rather than merely changing its procedure. Under Landgraf and Rivers, that consequence reinforces the enacted temporal contrasts and the absence of a clear direction to impose this new categorical bar on already-filed applications. The argument preserves the opportunity for a lawful discretionary decision; it establishes no entitlement to a favorable waiver, covers no later-filed application and decides no distinct IIRIRA-repeal question.

Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the jurisdiction holding and dissents from the eligibility disposition. The operative event for section 440(d) is the Attorney General's present decision whether to grant relief. Goncalves has received no waiver, and this record establishes no surrendered legal option requiring protection of a future discretionary grant. Sections 413 and 421 address different restrictions and do not, in the dissent's view, create a saving instruction for section 440(d). The dissent therefore regards Soriano's application to the unresolved agency decision as lawful, without treating the agency as authoritative on federal habeas jurisdiction or permitting deference contrary to enacted text. It would reverse the eligibility relief while retaining jurisdiction; it does not decide the ungranted equal-protection claim or declare all collateral grounds meritless.

## Procedure After Action
The First Circuit's judgment stands. The matter returns for the Board's determination of the timely appeal under former eligibility law and for the exercise of legally available discretion. The decision orders no new evidentiary hearing, automatic stay, release, waiver or execution of deportation and resolves no excluded equal-protection question.

## Event

Amoco Production Co. v. Southern Ute Indian Tribe, No. 98-830. Merits decision, June 7, 1999. Submitted on full merits briefing. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, sitting en banc, 151 F.3d 1251. The question is whether the 1909 and 1910 Coal Lands Acts' reservations of coal to the United States include distinct coalbed methane gas. The Tribe succeeded in 1938 to the retained coal interests, subject to outstanding patent rights. The district court resolved ownership against the Tribe and certified its judgment under Rule 54(b), leaving other matters unresolved. The court of appeals held the reserved coal included methane trapped in and produced from the coal reservoir; it left migrated-gas title and source allocation for further proceedings.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated in submission and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Tenth Circuit on the statutory ownership question is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 8–1. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Opposing: Ginsburg. The coal reservations themselves did not vest title to the distinct methane at issue in the United States and then the Tribe. Further proceedings must apply that construction without treating it as a blanket license to extract gas or as a disposition of every pleaded claim.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. The complete Court-opinion coalition is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Ginsburg filed a dissent.

## Holdings

#### The reservation of coal does not include this distinct methane gas

**Holding and operative rule:** The 1909 and 1910 Coal Lands Acts' reservations of coal do not reserve naturally occurring, chemically distinct methane held in coal by physical adsorption or in its fractures merely because the gas is associated with the coal reservoir. The reserved estate includes coal and its expressly accompanying rights; separate gas ownership does not decide lawful access, injury to coal, compensation or title created by a different gas reservation.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by the other seven Justices in the full coalition stated in Opinion Topology, controls directly.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress reserved a particular resource while conveying the remaining estate. In the enactment's historical setting, coal denoted the solid mined fuel; methane was separately known as a gas, often a dangerous byproduct that mining released. Physical association does not make the gas the substance Congress named. The Tribe's strongest response is that coal is a heterogeneous deposit, containing associated hydrocarbons, and that conservation of all coal preserved the deposit's future energy value. But the methane at issue remains a separately understood substance. Improved extraction changes its profitability, not its identity or the statutory words. Coal that lacked commercial value at enactment remains reserved coal, and new profitable uses of that substance do not change title. The rule does not rest on a general claim that newly valuable materials always belong to the grantee. Western Nuclear interpreted the broader reservation of coal and other minerals; its sovereign-land-grant canon operates within the enacted text. Leo Sheep likewise requires fidelity to the actual conveyance rather than an enlargement through an asserted general purpose. Here text, contemporary usage and the specific estate structure resolve the distinction without adding a gas reservation through a canon. The 1938 transfer placed the existing retained coal estate in trust; it did not enlarge that estate. Nor does the ownership answer authorize destruction of the reserved coal, extinguish its associated rights or resolve every constituent of a coal deposit. The Court reverses the contrary title premise and leaves independently governed rights and remedies to their proper proceedings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not determine statutory or lease rights in gas separately reserved by another instrument, methane migrated into non-coal strata, quantitative source allocation, limitations or other common defenses, compensation for trespass to coal seams, actual extraction interference, surface access, tax or collusion theories, facilities claims, or remaining federal trust questions. No damages award or general extraction license follows.

The coal-access provisions continue to operate in their own terms. Under the 1909 Act, entry to prospect for, mine or remove coal requires the patent owner's prior consent or court-determined conditions securing and paying all resulting damage to that owner. The patent owner may mine coal for domestic use on the land before federal disposal of the deposit. Locators, selectors and entrymen retain a hearing on land character; satisfactory final proof of good faith and legal compliance supports an unreserved patent unless the land is then shown chiefly valuable for coal. Under the 1910 Act, a person qualified to acquire federal coal deposits or mining rights may prospect after the Secretary approves security for all resulting crop and improvement damage. One who has acquired the deposit or mining right may occupy the surface as reasonably required for mining and removal upon paying the owner's damage or providing sufficient security in a competent court's damages proceeding. Domestic coal use on the land remains available before federal disposal, and applications challenging a coal classification to obtain an unreserved patent retain prompt consideration. The Court does not convert these provisions into a gas-access code.

## Precedent Treatment

- Western Nuclear is distinguished because its reservation covered coal and other minerals. Its sovereign-grant canon remains available for genuine uncertainty within the statutory transaction; it does not add a distinct gas reservation after this term's meaning is resolved.
- Leo Sheep supplies fidelity to the estate Congress actually conveyed and reserved; a general land-grant policy cannot enlarge the reservation beyond the enacted arrangement.
- Yankton's transaction-specific approach is consistent with preserving the original reservation's limits and questions concerning other interests; it does not itself decide this mineral-title question.
- Bourland concerns the consequences of a different federal land transaction for regulatory authority. Its distinction between transferred interests and residual rights is preserved, without importing its regulatory result into methane ownership.

## Law After Decision

The original federal coal reservations, and the Tribe's succession to them, do not by themselves establish title to the distinct coalbed methane at issue. This construction does not reduce the coal estate's independently protected rights or adjudicate a different gas reservation. Ownership, lawful access, extraction injury and compensation remain separate questions. No precedent is overruled, and no general rule governing every mineral or every later-developed use is announced.

## Separate Writings

Ginsburg dissents. At enactment, hazardous methane was an incident of the coal estate in practical control and responsibility. In her view, the statute and historical setting do not clearly establish that dominion over the gas would change when technology converted it from a liability into an asset. She therefore finds genuine ambiguity in the reservation rather than treating the solid/gas distinction as decisive. Western Nuclear's sovereign-land-grant canon resolves that uncertainty in favor of the estate originally retained by the United States, to which the Tribe later succeeded. Her conclusion does not depend on enlarging title merely because its successor is a Tribe, adopting blanket deference to Interior, or awarding damages. She would affirm the ownership construction for methane trapped in and produced from the coal reservoir while preserving the lower court's unresolved migrated-gas, allocation, defense and remedy questions.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for proceedings consistent with the statutory ownership construction. The remand does not direct judgment on every pleaded claim, decide an unadjudicated defense, award compensation or authorize extraction without applicable property protections. The panel's earlier treatment of the 1981 Solicitor opinion was not reconsidered en banc; this statutory decision announces no general rule that every Interior title interpretation binds courts or never receives weight.

## Source Notes

The statutory reservations, trust succession, bifurcated proceedings, certified judgment and appellate limits are reported in [Southern Ute Indian Tribe v. Amoco Production Co., 151 F.3d 1251](https://static.case.law/f3d/151/html/1251-01.html). The relevant enactments are the Coal Lands Acts of March 3, 1909 and June 22, 1910. The decision establishes no finding of extraction damage, quantitative gas origin or satisfaction of a separate access right.

## Event

O'Sullivan v. Boerckel, No. 97-2048. Merits decision, June 7, 1999. Argued March 30, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, 135 F.3d 1194. The question is whether a prisoner must present federal claims in the ordinary petition for discretionary review to the State's highest court before seeking federal habeas relief. Boerckel presented claims concerning knowing and intelligent Miranda waiver, confession voluntariness and evidentiary sufficiency to the Illinois intermediate appellate court but omitted them from his petition for leave to appeal to the Illinois Supreme Court. Other claims were included in that petition. The Seventh Circuit held the three omitted claims were not defaulted for this reason and remanded; it did not decide their merits. His federal application was filed in September 1994 and amended in March 1995.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated in argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Seventh Circuit is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 6–3. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Opposing: Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. The three omitted claims are procedurally defaulted because their ordinary Illinois review opportunity has expired, subject to the governing exceptions and the bounded further review stated below. The Court awards no writ, new trial, release or new evidentiary hearing and decides no underlying federal claim.

## Opinion Topology

| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–II | Souter | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; also Stone-Zsela | Reverses on ordinary Illinois review and the resulting claim-specific default | Entire ordinary-review, actual-unavailability, application and remand propositions; six |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III | Souter | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas | Preserves the scope of the decision | Reserves the effect of an express state opt-out retaining occasional discretionary relief; five |

## Holdings

#### I. An available ordinary discretionary petition is part of one complete round

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the pre-AEDPA law governing this application, a prisoner must fairly present each federal claim through one complete round of the State's established ordinary appellate process, including a petition for discretionary review to its highest court when that petition is an ordinary available step. Former section 2254(b) preserves exceptions for absence of a state corrective process or circumstances making that process ineffective to protect the applicant's rights; actual state-law unavailability must be respected, and the rule does not require every extraordinary remedy or repetitive collateral presentation after a complete ordinary round.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, commands six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh makes former chapter 153 law govern Boerckel's pre-enactment application. Former section 2254(c) turns on a state-law right to raise the question through an available procedure, not a right to obtain a merits decision. Illinois Rule 315 supplies a regular petition for leave to appeal. Its selective criteria—importance, conflict, supervisory need and finality—allow the Illinois Supreme Court to choose cases; they do not make the ordinary filing unavailable. The State's highest court must have its own opportunity to consider each federal claim. The contrary argument stresses the burden of repeating rejected claims in a selective system. But selection belongs to the state court, and the ordinary petition gives it the choice. Picard supplies fair presentation; it does not permit omission of an otherwise required ordinary step. Castille involved a claim first raised through discretionary review and therefore does not mechanically answer whether an already presented claim must pass through the next ordinary stage. Lonchar requires the governing procedural sources, not a generalized finality preference. Here Illinois supplied the ordinary leave procedure and no established directive excluded it from the process. Mere discretion and unlikely acceptance do not establish unavailability. Petitioning this Court is not a substitute for giving the State's own highest court the opportunity, and no equivalent federal-certiorari exhaustion requirement follows.

#### II. Expiration of the omitted opportunity produces default, with existing gateways preserved

**Holding and operative rule:** When a prisoner omits a claim from an otherwise required ordinary state-review step and the opportunity to use that step has expired, the claim is procedurally defaulted rather than awaiting exhaustion through a still-available remedy. Boerckel's three omitted claims fall within that rule, subject to recognized cause-and-prejudice and actual-innocence gateways at their existing scope; further proceedings must respect the actual prior gateway rulings and the governing pre-AEDPA law.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; the same six-Justice coalition directly adopts the classification and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Coleman distinguishes failure to exhaust an available remedy from default when a required remedy has closed. The three claims were omitted from an ordinary petition and their opportunity has expired. The Seventh Circuit's contrary premise therefore cannot support further consideration without addressing default. Gray and Lambrix preserve claim-specific procedural treatment; a ruling on these claims does not decide all claims in the application. Nor does default eliminate the recognized gateways. The district court invited a cause showing, and Boerckel instead pursued actual innocence. It heard evidence in July 1996, incorrectly invoked AEDPA to exclude it, and alternatively concluded that evidence of other participants did not establish Boerckel's absence. Because the court of appeals found no default, it did not resolve any properly preserved challenge to that gateway treatment. Such review must apply pre-AEDPA law and consider the significance of the alternative ground. The Court makes no new finding of innocence, credibility, cause, prejudice or forfeiture, changes no gateway burden and orders no second hearing. The remand permits only further proceedings consistent with these existing procedural conditions; the constitutional merits remain undecided.

#### III. The effect of an express state opt-out retaining occasional review remains open

**Reservation and operative limit:** The Court does not decide whether a State may expressly identify an occasionally available discretionary procedure as outside its standard review process and state that prisoners need not invoke it before federal habeas. No such Illinois directive is established here. The decision neither requires a State to eliminate every possibility of occasional discretionary relief nor conclusively defines what an effective express opt-out must contain.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. It is a five-Justice reservation, not an adopted rule resolving the hypothetical procedure's availability. Stone-Zsela does not join this reservation.

**Explanation:** Ordinary selective review and a State's express definition of its standard process present different questions. The former is established here; the latter is not. Treating actual state-law unavailability as controlling therefore does not require a decision about a procedure that a State retains for occasional use while expressly excluding it from the standard exhaustion route. The Court leaves that question open without displacing the complete-round rule for Illinois's existing ordinary procedure.

## Precedent Treatment

- Lindh supplies pre-AEDPA chapter 153 for this application; the decision does not import amended exhaustion or evidentiary provisions merely because it is delivered after AEDPA.
- Picard's fair-presentation requirement is applied to each claim through the ordinary state-review round.
- Castille is distinguished: first presentation through a discretionary mechanism differs from omission at the next ordinary stage after proper presentation below. Its proper-presentation rule remains intact.
- Coleman supplies the distinction between an available unexhausted remedy and procedural default after an otherwise required route closes; it does not independently identify Illinois's required sequence.
- Lonchar preserves source-governed habeas procedure and does not itself decide which Illinois steps constitute exhaustion.
- Gray preserves separate treatment of distinct claims and defaults; it does not convert this exhaustion ruling into a decision of the constitutional merits.
- Lambrix preserves default as an independent procedural issue with its recognized gateway requirements; it does not convert this exhaustion ruling into a decision of the constitutional merits.

## Law After Decision

Illinois's ordinary petition for leave to appeal must include each federal claim sought to be preserved through a complete ordinary review round. Selective acceptance alone does not excuse omission. Expiration of that required opportunity produces default, subject to the existing gateways and actual procedural posture. Actual state-law unavailability and the former statute's absent-process and ineffective-process exceptions remain distinct from mere discretion. The effect of an express opt-out retaining occasional review is unresolved. No precedent is overruled, no universal duty to pursue extraordinary remedies is adopted and no new innocence standard is imposed.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He agrees that Illinois supplied an ordinary available petition and had not told prisoners their federal claims could bypass it. He therefore joins the actual-case exhaustion and default holdings. He would also state affirmatively that a clear state rule making a step unnecessary controls: the inquiry concerns the process the State actually makes ordinarily available. His rule does not demand repetitive extraordinary remedies, applications the State expressly labels unnecessary, or futile petitions after final resolution through another ordinary route. He does not join the Court's reservation of the express-state-opt-out question. His remand preserves cause and prejudice, actual innocence, waiver and any clear applicable Illinois exclusion; it finds none of those predicates satisfied and directs no new hearing or favorable habeas result.

Stevens, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, dissents. The three claims were fairly presented for merits decision at trial and through Illinois's appeal as of right. In the dissent's view, that process gave the State a full opportunity; omission from the selectively administered second tier should not create an additional federal default. Technical exhaustion because no remedy remains does not itself establish that the prisoner failed to provide a fair opportunity while remedies existed. Rule 315's institutional criteria matter to what the State's ordinary process requires, and mandatory repetition of every rejected error can burden a selective system without supplying a materially new merits opportunity. The dissent reads the statutory reference to available procedures against established limits on endless exhaustion, rather than treating every opportunity to request extraordinary or selective relief as mandatory. It accepts proper-presentation rules, genuine claim-specific state defaults and existing exceptions. It would affirm the no-default holding and allow ordinary habeas proceedings on these three claims, subject to other actual conditions, without awarding a writ or release.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for proceedings consistent with the three claims' procedural default. Any properly preserved review of the existing innocence-gateway ruling must use pre-AEDPA law and consider the district court's independent alternative ground. No new evidentiary hearing, cause showing, innocence finding or merits result is directed. Other claims and independent rulings remain in their existing posture.

The filing dates, omitted claims, Illinois procedure, prior cause invitation and innocence-hearing rulings are reported in [Boerckel v. O'Sullivan, 135 F.3d 1194](https://static.case.law/f3d/135/html/1194-01.html). Former 28 U.S.C. section 2254(b) and (c) and Illinois Rule 315 supply the governing procedural texts. The decision establishes no new state opt-out directive, waiver, credibility determination or finding of innocence.

## Event

## Holdings
**Nonparty preclusion.** A judgment cannot extinguish an absent pilot's individual ADEA claim merely because earlier plaintiffs challenged the same employer policy, shared broadly similar interests and used counsel later retained by that pilot. The lower record's findings concerning participation and relationships do not establish the authority American invokes, and later common counsel cannot create it retrospectively. The Court does not find every conceivable relationship affirmatively disproved, disturb judgments against actual parties, or diminish the ordinary precedential force of earlier decisions. The asserted preclusion ground fails; statutory liability remains for further proceedings.

**ADEA collective-action participation.** Section 626(b)'s incorporation of §216(b) requires an employee's written consent before that employee becomes a party plaintiff to an ADEA collective action. **Authority:** The Court's statutory ruling, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela does not join this distinct ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress made affirmative written participation a condition of party-plaintiff status. American does not claim that these pilots consented in writing to an earlier ADEA group action. Similar policy interests cannot do what the statute requires the employee to do. This defeats the proposed collective-action party analogy independently of its constitutional representation defect. It does not make written consent necessary for every recognized form of nonparty binding or decide the employer's substantive ADEA defenses.

None makes identical interests sufficient representation.

Earlier judgments retain their lawful force against persons actually bound and their ordinary precedential influence. They do not extinguish these individual statutory claims merely because the same employer policy has previously survived a challenge. An ADEA employee becomes a party plaintiff to a collective action only through the written consent required by the incorporated statute; ordinary opt-out class analogies do not replace that requirement. ## Event
City of Chicago v. Morales, No. 97-1121. Argued December 9, 1998; decided June 10, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Illinois, 177 Ill. 2d 440, 687 N.E.2d 53 (1997), which invalidated the gang-loitering ordinance and reversed the convictions. The question is whether the conditions for mandatory dispersal and punishment for disobedience supply constitutionally sufficient enforcement standards and notice.

The ordinance applies when an officer reasonably believes a criminal street gang member is remaining in a public place with one or more persons with no apparent purpose. The officer must order everyone to disperse and remove themselves from the area; failure promptly to obey is punishable. Absence of an actual gang member is an affirmative defense. Public place includes publicly accessible private locations. Penalties are a $100–$500 fine, up to six months' imprisonment, or both; up to 120 hours of community service may be imposed additionally or instead. General Order 92-4 restricts enforcement to designated officers and areas and provides gang-identification criteria, but the designated areas are not publicly disclosed.

## Participation
Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action
The judgment of the Supreme Court of Illinois is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy
**Affirmed, 7–2.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas. The dismissals and reversals resulting from invalidation remain in force. The dissenters would reverse the facial-vagueness disposition and remand unresolved preserved grounds, without independently reinstating every conviction through new factual findings.

## Opinion Topology
| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirmance | The seven supporting Justices join the enforcement-guidance holding and central-trigger basis for facial relief. |
| Concurrence | Stevens | Souter and Ginsburg | Joins the Court and adds separate grounds | Fair notice and the bounded liberty interest in innocent public presence; neither is the Court's additional holding. |
| Dissent | Thomas | Scalia | Would reverse facial vagueness and remand | Intelligible core, practical enforcement guidance, actual warning and deliberate disobedience; unresolved individual applications remain open. |

## Holdings
**Meaningful standards for dispersal.** A criminal ordinance is facially vague when every prosecution depends on an officer's conclusion that people remain in public with no apparent purpose, and the law supplies no meaningful objective criterion governing that conclusion. A later refusal to disperse cannot cure the standardless condition authorizing the order.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven directly adopt the enforcement-guidance ground and the remedy tied to its common trigger; no aggregation of distinct notice or liberty theories supplies their majority.

**Controlling explanation:** Kolender requires minimum legal guidance for enforcement independently of whether an individual knows that an officer demands compliance. Peoples Rights Organization distinguishes difficult factual proof from the absence of a central legal criterion; fault and apparent clear examples cannot automatically supply a missing criterion. Chicago's ordinance provides gang-identification, personnel and geographic limits, but none answers why a group's otherwise lawful presence may be ended. An innocent purpose need not be apparent. The ordinance requires no obstruction, threat, trespass, intimidation, criminal conduct or minimum duration before the officer must order everyone away. General Order 92-4 addresses officers, areas and gang identification, not this missing substantive condition. Illinois rejected the proposed narrowing constructions; the Court cannot insert an intimidation element. A warning makes departure avoid arrest but leaves the original choice of whom to displace uncontrolled.

The defect governs the common legal trigger of every dispersal order. Facial relief therefore rests on that central defect, not uncertain margins of an otherwise intelligible rule, nor a claim that every gang gathering is immune from regulation. Hoffman Estates preserves contextual limits on facial review; it does not transform an undefined predicate into a definite offense. The Court does not rely on an independently sufficient fair-notice ground or recognize an unlimited substantive right to remain anywhere. Objectively bounded public-order laws remain available. No severable independent offense survives within this single mechanism.

## Precedent Treatment
- Kolender supplies an independent enforcement-guidance requirement; an individual warning does not replace a legal standard for initiating the command.
- Peoples Rights Organization applies the distinction between difficult proof and a missing central legal criterion; its defect-specific approach supports relief here without a universal criminal facial-review rule.
- Hoffman Estates retains contextual facial-review and clear-core principles; it is distinguished because the Court finds a defect in the common legal trigger rather than merely uncertain margins.
- Grayned preserves intelligible notice and enforcement standards.
- Papachristou illustrates the danger of discretionary criminalization of ordinary public activity.
- Coates rejects criminal boundaries depending on variable personal reactions.

The decision applies those principles without establishing a new substantive liberty immunity.

## Law After Decision
Chicago cannot predicate punishment on this standardless dispersal mechanism. Defined rules addressing obstruction, trespass, intimidation, threats, criminal conduct or emergency dispersal remain available subject to their own constitutional requirements. The decision establishes neither an unlimited right to remain in every place nor a rule invalidating every law involving discretionary factual judgments.

## Separate Writings
Stevens, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, also finds inadequate fair notice. A command informs the listener that departure is demanded, but does not make the antecedent grounds intelligible or adequately settle how far and how long the listener must leave. Ordinary innocent public presence also implicates liberty against arbitrary displacement. That explanation is bounded: it does not confer immunity on threatening gatherings or invalidate reasonable, definite public-order laws. Those additional propositions have three votes and are noncontrolling.

Thomas, joined by Scalia, regards the enacted sequence as sufficiently ascertainable: a reasonable gang-membership belief, observed remaining without apparent purpose, an express order and deliberate disobedience. In their view, the ordinance thus differs from the missing modification relationship in Peoples Rights Organization. Gang-identification rules and practical administration add genuine constraints. Any excessive reach or unconstitutional particular application does not establish that the law lacks every intelligible core. They would reject facial vagueness while leaving unresolved preserved objections for further proceedings; they do not insert an intimidation element or hold that a warning cures every undefined law.

## Procedure After Action
The Illinois judgment remains effective. The Court does not decide status-offense, Fourth Amendment probable-cause, First Amendment overbreadth or the omitted detailed gang-definition objections. No replacement ordinance, damages or new criminal findings are ordered.

## Event
Dickinson v. Zurko, No. 98-377. Argued March 24, 1999; decided June 10, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Federal Circuit, reviewing In re Zurko, 142 F.3d 1447 (1998) (en banc). The Federal Circuit's judgment is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy
**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. **Opposing:** Kennedy and Ginsburg. The Federal Circuit must identify and apply the proper APA §706 category to the PTO's factual findings on the whole record. | Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Reversal and remand | Complete APA-framework holding and bounded remedy |

## Holdings
**APA review of PTO factual findings.** Direct Federal Circuit review of PTO factual findings is governed by the applicable APA court-agency standard, not the clearly-erroneous standard used to review district-court findings. Section 559 preserves additional requirements imposed by statute or otherwise recognized by law, but the asserted pre-APA direct-review requirement has not been sufficiently established; the Federal Circuit must identify and apply the proper §706 category on the whole record.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The PTO is an agency subject to the APA's review structure. Section 559 does preserve antecedent legal requirements; its expressness rule for later statutes is not a command that every pre-APA rule must have appeared in an express statute. The asserted exception nevertheless needs an identifiable legal foundation. The earlier decisions use varied standards in different settings, including interference suits, equity proceedings and direct appeals. Morgan v. Daniels addressed priority between contestants in a subsequent equity proceeding. Its requirement of a thorough conviction does not establish a general direct-appeal relationship equivalent to review of district-court facts. Neither the later specialized court's practice nor patent uniformity supplies the missing preserved requirement.

Universal Camera demonstrates that agency review remains meaningful on the whole record, including contrary evidence, while preserving agency choice between fairly conflicting views. Allentown Mack requires application of the actual governing standard; it does not authorize substitution of judicial factfinding. Haggar concerns statutory interpretation and does not determine the standard for this factual appeal. - Morgan v. Daniels retains its priority-dispute rule in the later equity-suit setting; it does not establish the claimed general direct-review clear-error standard.

The Federal Circuit must use the APA framework in this direct PTO appeal. Section 559 continues to preserve additional requirements actually established by prior law; the asserted general patent-review exception fails. Section 706(2)(E) applies on its statutory hearing-record predicates, while (A) and authorized trial-de-novo review under (F) retain their separate terms. ## Separate Writings

## Holdings
**The asserted firmly rooted exception.** A State cannot establish a firmly rooted federal constitutional hearsay exception merely by placing an unavailable accomplice's custodial, self-exculpatory and blame-shifting accusations within its declaration-against-penal-interest exception. Genuinely disserving admissions and accusations of another cannot automatically be treated as one reliable narrative; genuinely self-inculpatory, equally inculpatory and noncustodial statements remain subject to their actual circumstances.

**Authority:** The lead opinion's exception ruling, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This is a direct seven-Justice proposition, not a synthesis of different rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** White preserves accepted, firmly rooted exceptions; it does not turn every police interview admitted under a state label into an established constitutional exception. Lee identifies the distinctive danger that an accomplice will shift or spread blame. Mark's admissions of misconduct do not automatically make his assignment of the killing to Benjamin reliable. Williamson requires context-sensitive attention to individual assertions under federal evidence law and illustrates why a general confession does not carry every accusation with it; it does not itself decide Virginia evidence law or the constitutional question. The narrow custodial, blame-shifting category presented here fails the firmly rooted route. That conclusion alone does not abolish the separate constitutional route of particularized trustworthiness or prohibit all accomplice declarations.

**Particularized guarantees and the challenged accusations.** Under the governing confrontation framework, the prosecution must establish particularized guarantees of trustworthiness from the circumstances surrounding the actual statement when no genuinely applicable firmly rooted exception supports admission. Independent corroborating trial evidence cannot substitute for those guarantees. Mark's challenged custodial accusations lack them, and their substantive admission without confrontation violated Benjamin's right.

**Authority:** The lead opinion's reliability holding and application, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five supply controlling reasoning at this scope. Scalia and Thomas independently agree that admission violated confrontation on different grounds; neither supplies a reliability-framework join.

**Controlling explanation:** Roberts and Wright distinguish constitutional guarantees arising from the statement's circumstances from evidence elsewhere at trial that might suggest the accusation is true. Mark minimized his violence and denied responsibility for the shooting while accusing Benjamin. Custodial questioning and pressure concerning grave charges created concrete incentives to curry favor and reduce personal exposure; his reported intoxication and inclusion of some self-inculpation do not establish that cross-examination would add little. No express leniency bargain need be found to recognize those incentives. Cross-examination could directly test the allocation of responsibility. Virginia's treatment of blame shifting as weight alone, and its resort to corroboration as a substitute for intrinsic guarantees, did not meet the constitutional requirement. The supported circumstances permit resolution of these challenged accusations without declaring every sentence of the interviews inadmissible under every possible theory. The Court adopts no categorical doctrine replacing the received framework for all testimonial statements, no universal atomized exclusion rule, and no new de novo standard of constitutional review. Genuinely self-inculpatory remarks severable from blame shifting, forfeiture by wrongdoing, dying declarations, and nonhearsay uses with effective limiting safeguards are not barred by this holding.

**Harmlessness and relief.** An established confrontation trial error permits the conviction and sentence to stand only if a properly available harmless-error inquiry establishes beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the verdict; the Commonwealth bears that burden. The present record supports no finding of harmlessness, automatic acquittal or unconditional retrial order.

**Authority:** All nine Justices accept this remedy rule for an established constitutional error. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer find the error now; O'Connor and Kennedy would first have the state court assess particularized trustworthiness under the corrected rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Chapman supplies the Commonwealth's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden; Gray keeps the admission error, preservation of a harmlessness contention and ultimate relief distinct. The trial-wide record and properly available arguments must govern that inquiry. The mandate creates no otherwise unavailable harmlessness argument. Because seven Justices find the challenged admission unconstitutional now, that issue is not left open on remand; only the bounded remedial inquiry remains. Unless Virginia satisfies that burden in a properly available inquiry, the conviction and sentence must be vacated. Virginia may retry without the challenged statement or after securing constitutionally sufficient confrontation. The Court makes no prediction about any retrial or the complete record's harmlessness result.

## Event

## Holdings

### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding and operative rule:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutes use fraud as a legal term with settled content. Morissette supplies the principle that Congress ordinarily carries that established meaning into the statute; the absence of a separate materiality adjective does not strip fraud of its significance requirement. Durland includes fraudulent future promises, but does not make an inconsequential falsehood a fraudulent scheme or import every element of a completed civil tort. The statutory focus on schemes, and §1344's inclusion of attempts, explains why actual reliance and completed injury are unnecessary. Mail or wire use remains a distinct requirement, and the communication need not itself contain the falsehood if it executes the scheme.

Section 1344 separately covers executing or attempting a scheme to defraud a covered institution and executing or attempting a scheme to obtain its property by false or fraudulent pretenses. Both routes retain materiality for the deceptive schemes presented here; their other possible differences, including mental-state requirements in other applications, remain open. Wells construes the different wording of §1014, which does not require objective materiality in addition to its false-statement or overvaluation and purpose-to-influence requirements. It does not turn fraud language into an unrestricted false-statement prohibition. A purpose to influence under §1014 does not automatically establish the separate fraud element.

The natural-tendency formulation, reflected in Kungys, concerns the targeted decision in context. The Court does not exhaust every common-law formulation, unusual recipient preference, omission duty, or nonrepresentational scheme. Nor does it decide materiality under every differently worded offense. The judgment rejecting materiality under the three fraud provisions is reversed.

### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding and operative rule:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

**Controlling explanation:** Gaudin assigns the application of an actual element to the jury. Section 7206(1) independently supplies materiality through its express prohibition of willfully subscribing a return declared under penalties of perjury that the signer does not believe true and correct as to every material matter. Part I establishes the element under the fraud statutes. The express tax and bank directions withheld that application, while the mail and wire charges omitted it. The judge's separate materiality ruling is not a jury finding.

Chapman places the burden on the Government for these preserved constitutional errors. Failure to offer a separately labeled factual materiality defense supplies neither waiver nor forfeiture and does not require Neder to prove innocence. Johnson's refusal to correct forfeited error under the fourth condition of plain-error review does not govern these preserved objections. Genuine findings elsewhere in an actual verdict can establish an equivalent determination; whether they do so here, and whether other supported inferences suffice, are distinct questions addressed in the following parts. Neither the Court's recognition of error nor Gaudin alone decides those remedial questions.

### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding and operative rule:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

**Controlling explanation:** Roy permits examination of a particular omitted element without converting every omission into a structural defect requiring automatic reversal. Its qualified method concerns the error's effect on the jury that returned this verdict. Yates supplies the instructional-focus requirement: a reviewing court must examine whether the instructions directed the jury to evidence supporting the proposed inference, rather than assume the jury decided a question removed from its task. Necessary equivalent findings are sufficient, but not invariably indispensable to a reliable noncontribution conclusion.

Sullivan addresses a different defect, an invalid reasonable-doubt standard vitiating all factual findings. These instructions left an otherwise valid reasonable-doubt standard governing the submitted elements. Their express removal of materiality heightens the need to justify the connection; it does not itself destroy every remaining finding or forbid the inquiry Roy allows. Gaudin's allocation rule remains fully operative at trial.

Powerful and unchallenged evidence may support an inference, but neither label creates an automatic safe harbor. A reviewing court must assess defense disputes for what they concern and determine whether the actual findings resolve them. Sufficient evidence for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction is not the required showing. Mitchell likewise distinguishes actual contribution from enough other evidence to sustain a result. This holding does not classify every omitted element, constructive amendment, or total denial of jury trial. Richardson's reserved harmlessness questions concerning its distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect remain unresolved.

### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The instruction required knowing, purposeful falsity of the reported total income: $53,625 for 1985 and minus $980,377 for 1986. It did not permit conviction for an unidentified collateral entry. The jury considered the defenses that the receipts were repayable loans and that professional advice or good faith negated culpability. Its findings reject those defenses insofar as they would make the specified totals truthful or their falsity innocent. The verdict does not resolve every subsidiary advice dispute, but its particular subject and culpability findings furnish the connection required by Roy's actual-contribution inquiry. Annual income is information used to determine income-tax liability and administer the returns.

The objection is substantial: the jury was expressly forbidden to decide materiality, so a court must not supply that missing decision. The Court does not suppose that jurors disobeyed the instruction, treat the judge's ruling as theirs, or declare that they necessarily made the omitted evaluation. It instead asks what effect that withdrawal had on an otherwise valid verdict about these identified income statements. The charge's specific focus and findings establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt under Roy's permitted method.

The conclusion requires no unanimous numerical finding of the alleged additional income, acceptance of every transaction or witness, unpaid-tax calculation, actual IRS response, or successful deception. It does not excuse future courts from submitting materiality. The Government retains the burden in each case; this record-specific conclusion neither creates a tax exception to Gaudin nor decides the fraud counts.

### V. Initial harmlessness review of the affected fraud convictions

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. Scalia and Ginsburg join V-A only and would limit review to genuine equivalent findings. Stone-Zsela joins the statutory reversal but dissents from the remand for another harmlessness inquiry; he would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now.

**Controlling explanation:** The court of appeals rejected the antecedent statutory element and therefore did not undertake the required fraud harmlessness analysis. Unlike the tax charge's specified annual-income findings, the fraud instructions and theories require a distinct inquiry into what each verdict resolved. Inflated appraisals, concealed relationships and down payments, presales, and advance requests may bear strongly on lending decisions. Yet the charge did not require proof of every alleged detail, expressly removed bank materiality, and used cross-references for mail and wire fraud. A generic intent-to-deceive finding does not automatically resolve capacity to influence, and Wells prevents treating a §1014 purpose-to-influence finding as an automatic equivalent. The lending-purpose and transaction-significance disputes must be considered in context.

Part V-B applies Roy's qualified method and Chapman's burden to that unfinished appellate task. The tax affirmance proves neither harmlessness nor harmfulness for any fraud count. No present determination requires a new trial on every affected fraud count or sustains any one of them automatically. If the Government fails the governing showing on an affected conviction, instructional-error relief permits retrial; it does not establish evidentiary insufficiency or require acquittal.

The mandate does not independently dispose of §1014, conspiracy, or racketeering claims, reopen acquittals, or calculate a sentence, restitution, or release date. Any properly available dependent consequence must follow the actual predicate and ordinary mandate proceedings. Those boundaries are part of the limited remand, not an advance resolution of unpresented claims.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Morissette v. United States:** Applies the borrowed-term principle to fraud's established materiality requirement; statutory silence alone does not discard that content.
- **Durland v. United States:** Preserves coverage of fraudulent future promises while distinguishing that breadth from elimination of materiality or importation of every completed-tort requirement.
- **Kungys v. United States:** Uses its natural-tendency or capacity formulation for the financial deceptions presented here; its civil allocation and burden do not govern the criminal jury question.
- **United States v. Wells:** Preserves the different §1014 construction without an added objective-materiality element; it neither removes materiality from fraud statutes nor automatically cures their instructional error through a purpose-to-influence finding.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies jury determination of each actual materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt; harmlessness review does not authorize withdrawal of that element at trial.
- **Chapman v. California:** Applies the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden to establish that the preserved error did not contribute to the actual verdict.
- **California v. Roy:** Extends its qualified actual-verdict method to the materiality errors presented here; necessary equivalence is sufficient but not exclusive, and its instructional-focus limitation remains essential.
- **Yates v. Evatt:** Applies attention to the actual charge, findings, and evidence considered under the instructions; proof sufficient for a different verdict cannot replace contribution analysis.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Distinguishes removal of a particular element from invalidation of the reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings; its structural-error rule remains intact.
- **Johnson v. United States:** Distinguishes its discretionary plain-error disposition after forfeiture; it neither supplies a waiver here nor reduces Chapman's burden.
- **Jones v. United States:** Preserves its protection for charged statutory elements without treating it as a classification or harmlessness decision for this error.
- **Mitchell v. United States:** Applies its distinction between an error's actual contribution and sufficient other evidence without transferring its separate privilege holding.
- **Richardson v. United States:** Leaves intact its reservation of availability and application of harmless-error review for the distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect.

## Law After Decision

Effective June 10, 1999, materiality must be submitted for the charged mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud schemes; §7206(1)'s express materiality element and §1014's different Wells rule retain their respective force. For the materiality errors presented here, the Government may establish harmlessness through Roy's qualified actual-verdict inquiry, but uncontested or overwhelming evidence is not an automatic substitute for that showing. The tax verdicts meet the standard on their specific charge and record; the affected fraud verdicts require initial application below.

The six-Justice tax ground is noncontribution to the actual verdict, not a rule that the jury necessarily decided every omitted materiality question. Stevens's supplementary view and Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive equivalent-findings method do not control. The Chief accepts the governing broader method but disagrees with its application and the further fraud inquiry. The decision establishes no universal omitted-element classification, generic tax shortcut, new no-contest waiver, or automatic effect on a differently worded offense. Richardson's separate harmlessness reservation remains open.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I, II, and III. Fraud's settled meaning supplies materiality, Wells concerns another enactment, and Gaudin requires submission of actual elements. Roy makes these omissions reviewable trial error and permits reliable whole-record inferences beyond necessary equivalent findings. He does not adopt a universal structural-error rule or Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive method.

The Government's case must be weighed: the alleged omissions exceeding $1 million and $4 million plainly concern tax computation, the alleged inflated appraisals and nonexistent down payments concern lending decisions, and Neder did not separately contest materiality as a factual matter. Those considerations are substantial, not disqualified evidence. Nevertheless, the instructions removed tax and bank materiality and never asked the jury to assess that dimension of mail or wire fraud. The findings of knowing falsity and fraudulent intent do not reliably show the omission's lack of contribution to the actual verdict on each affected count. The annual-income findings identify the statements and culpability, but do not close that gap. The Government has shown what a properly instructed jury would almost certainly find, not harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt under these instructions and verdicts.

He would reverse the conviction-sustaining judgment, vacate every conviction affected by removal or omission of materiality, and remand for new trials if the United States elects to retry. He therefore dissents both from tax affirmance and from another fraud harmlessness inquiry. Sullivan forbids inventing a verdict, while Roy supplies the governing review; Johnson's forfeited-error disposition does not shift the burden. He would leave unaffected counts to their own preserved issues, disturb no §1014 conviction merely because of this holding, and order no insufficiency acquittal. A valid admission or a record establishing actual-verdict harmlessness could produce a different result.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Ginsburg.** Both join Parts I, II, and V-A. They agree on fraud materiality, the jury's role, the preserved claim and Government's burden, and a first appellate inquiry into the affected fraud convictions. Their Roy position permits review for actual findings that genuinely embrace the omitted element; it does not permit appellate certainty about an unresolved element to replace its jury determination. Gaudin and Sullivan protect that division of responsibility. They withhold assent from Part III's broader method and its classification of the express materiality withdrawal, while retaining Roy's classification within that decision's own personal-purpose category.

The tax verdict establishes knowing, purposeful false annual income. It does not establish the jury's own application of the omitted materiality requirement, which the instruction forbade it to consider. A judge's conclusion about the significance of annual income supplies the missing evaluation rather than identifies a genuine equivalent finding. They accordingly reject both Part IV's application and Stevens's additional rationale. Their ground permits new tax trials under correct instructions; it is not evidentiary insufficiency and does not require acquittal or unconditional release.

For fraud, they join the limited remand because the court of appeals' no-element ruling left a real initial inquiry unfinished. They would confine it to genuine equivalent findings on the affected counts, and do not join Part V-B's wider inference instruction. A valid equivalent determination can establish harmlessness, so their position is not automatic reversal for every omitted element. Ginsburg joins this writing throughout.

**Justice Stevens, concurring.** He joins the entire Court opinion, including its qualified actual-verdict method, tax noncontribution conclusion, and limited fraud remand. He adds that the required finding of knowing falsity of these particular annual-income totals embraces their material relationship to determining income-tax liability. His additional ground rests on the actual findings and their specific subject, not on appellate prediction about what another jury would decide. It requires no finding that every alleged dollar was income, that an unpaid-tax amount was established, or that the IRS was successfully deceived. His view supplies no additional holding beyond the Court's sufficient common ground. He continues to reject hypothetical-verdict substitution and does not use his disagreement with Wells to reopen the different §1014 offenses.

## Procedure After Action

Supreme Court merits review ends with a mixed judgment. The judgment sustaining tax counts 86 and 87 stands against the materiality-instruction challenge. The Eleventh Circuit must conduct the first proper harmlessness inquiry for the affected convictions under §§1341, 1343, and 1344, applying the qualified actual-verdict method and the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden count by count unless an established common finding lawfully covers multiple counts. If an affected conviction cannot be sustained, it must be set aside, and the United States may retry under instructions submitting materiality and every other element beyond a reasonable doubt. The mandate predetermines neither that review's result nor any otherwise available dependent sentencing or count disposition.

## Event
Cunningham v. Hamilton County, No. 98-727. Argued April 19, 1999; decided June 14, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, which dismissed the interlocutory appeal in Starcher v. Correctional Medical Systems, Inc., 144 F.3d 418 (1998). Counsel challenges a personal $1,494 discovery sanction under then-operative Rule 37(a)(4) while her former client's civil-rights action remains pending. The district court upheld that sanction and disqualified her as a material witness. The question is whether the sanction is immediately appealable under the collateral-order doctrine despite the absence of final judgment in the underlying action.

## Participation
Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action
The Sixth Circuit's judgment dismissing the interlocutory appeal is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy
**Affirmed, 9–0.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The appeal remains dismissed. The Court neither affirms the sanction on its merits nor vacates it, and decides neither the sanction amount nor the validity of disqualification. No payment stay or substitute appellate route is granted.

## Opinion Topology
| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirmance | Both independently sufficient grounds and bounded judgment |
| Concurrence | Kennedy | No additional joins | Joins the Court's opinion and judgment | Exceptional mandamus and genuine contempt reservations under their independent requirements |

## Holdings
**Complete separation from the merits.** A monetary Rule 37 discovery sanction against counsel during ongoing litigation is not immediately appealable under §1291's collateral-order doctrine merely because counsel's representation has ended. The category does not satisfy the requirement of an important question completely separate from the merits when review of discovery relevance, responses and compliance remains intertwined with the case's substantive issues and litigation record.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices. This ground independently requires dismissal of the interlocutory appeal.

**Controlling explanation:** Cohen and Coopers & Lybrand require every collateral-order condition, not conclusiveness alone. Assuming the monetary determination is conclusive, evaluating whether discovery was relevant, responses sufficient or orders obeyed may require review of the underlying dispute. Counsel's personal financial interest differs from the client's but does not detach those questions from the litigation. Digital Equipment requires category-level assessment rather than an exception whenever one order looks especially separate, and Swint rejects convenience as a substitute for appellate jurisdiction. Cunningham's disqualification ended her representation; it did not end the civil-rights action or alter the connection between the discovery sanction and that action. The Court decides appealability of the $1,494 order, not whether the sanction, its amount or the disqualification was correct.

**Effective review after final judgment.** Independently, this monetary discovery sanction remains effectively reviewable after final judgment: the attorney personally subject to it may challenge her own sanction even though she no longer represents the client. Ordinary payment, delay and professional or reputational burdens do not establish an important right that will be irretrievably lost without immediate review.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices. This ground independently defeats the collateral-order appeal.

**Controlling explanation:** Digital Equipment distinguishes the costs of delayed review from the loss of a right sufficiently important to justify immediate appeal. Later reversal can vacate the monetary obligation and direct repayment or other correction. Cunningham's own right to seek review does not depend entirely on her former client's decision to appeal. Behrens preserves qualifying legal-immunity appeals, but this sanction establishes no immunity or right to avoid the trial itself. The Sixth Circuit reserved effective unreviewability; the Court resolves it as a legal question here. Ending representation alone does not make later relief ineffective. The holding does not resolve a materially different final-withdrawal situation establishing genuine independent nonparty finality, another sanctions regime, or a contempt adjudication carrying its own finality. Section 1292(b), extraordinary mandamus and genuine independent contempt routes retain their own requirements; none is satisfied or granted by this decision.

## Precedent Treatment
- Cohen retains the narrow collateral-order exception and its cumulative conditions.
- Coopers & Lybrand preserves those cumulative requirements; conclusiveness does not excuse a failure of complete separation or effective unreviewability.
- Digital Equipment supplies category-level discipline and preserves adequate final review despite irretrievable ordinary litigation costs; those principles apply to this monetary discovery sanction.
- Swint rejects convenience-based appellate jurisdiction and distinguishes ordinary liability from legal immunity.
- Behrens retains otherwise qualifying legal-immunity appeals, not immediate review of every personal monetary or professional burden.

## Law After Decision
The final-judgment rule governs this monetary Rule 37 sanction even after representation ends through disqualification. The attorney retains review of her own sanction after final judgment. Different statutory appeal provisions, extraordinary remedies and genuinely distinct finality categories retain their existing prerequisites. There is no general attorney-status exception and no holding that every order against an attorney is unappealable.

## Separate Writings
Kennedy joins both Court grounds and stresses that exceptional circumstances need not leave an attorney without a remedy. If a trial court refuses to stay enforcement and exceptional resulting hardship is itself likely to cause injustice, mandamus might permit review of the sanction or the refusal to stay under that remedy's demanding requirements. A genuine contempt adjudication against a person whose interests are not congruent with a litigant's may raise a different finality question. He identifies no such hardship, contempt finding, stay entitlement or established alternative route here; the reservation creates no automatic right of immediate appeal.

## Procedure After Action
The civil-rights litigation continues. Cunningham may seek review of her personal sanction after final judgment, independently of whether her former client chooses to appeal. Other independently authorized routes remain conditional on their own requirements. This decision orders no stay, fee reduction, merits reconsideration or relief from discovery obligations.

The Sixth Circuit reserved effective unreviewability; adequate later review is the Court's legal conclusion here. ## Event
Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association, Inc. v. United States, No. 98-387. Argued April 27, 1999; decided June 14, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 149 F.3d 334 (1998). Louisiana broadcasters seek declaratory and injunctive relief against §1304 and its companion FCC restriction as applied to truthful advertisements for lawful private-casino gambling in Louisiana and Mississippi. The question is whether that application satisfies commercial-speech scrutiny in light of the federal scheme's actual exceptions and asserted interests.

Section 1304 prohibits federally licensed radio or television broadcasts of advertisements or information concerning a lottery, gift enterprise or similar scheme offering prizes dependent wholly or partly on chance, including prize lists, and prohibits an operator's knowing permission of such broadcasting. It authorizes a fine, up to one year's imprisonment, or both, with each broadcast day a separate offense. The materially relevant exceptions have distinct conditions:

- Section 1307(a)(1) permits information concerning a lottery conducted by a State under its law when the station is licensed in that State or another State conducting such a lottery.
- Section 1307(a)(2) covers a different scheme only if authorized or not prohibited where conducted and conducted by a nonprofit or governmental organization, or as clearly occasional commercial promotion ancillary to the organization's primary business. For this section, nonprofit status means an organization that would qualify as tax exempt under Internal Revenue Code §501.
- Section 2720 exempts tribal gaming conducted pursuant to and consistently with the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act's chapter requirements.
- Section 1307(b)'s distinct transport/mail exemptions concern state-conducted lottery material sent to in-state addresses or material intended for a lawful foreign lottery and sent to an addressee there; they do not independently exempt §1304 broadcasts. Its special definition of lottery concerns pooled ticket/chance proceeds distributed by chance and excludes sports bets; that definition applies to subsection (b), not automatically to the broadcasting prohibition.

These provisions do not allow every gambling advertisement other than private-casino advertising.

## Participation
Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action
The Fifth Circuit's sustaining judgment is reversed and the case returned for conforming declaratory and appropriate injunctive relief.

## Judgment & Remedy
**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The Government may not enforce §1304 against these truthful, nonmisleading advertisements for lawful private-casino gambling. Relief concerning the companion FCC restriction conforms to that same as-applied prohibition. Section 1304 and the rule are not facially invalidated; no damages or replacement gambling policy is ordered.

## Opinion Topology
| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Reversal and as-applied relief | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join both independent advancement and fit holdings. Stevens's application is conditional under existing scrutiny; Scalia retains broader doctrinal reservations while joining both applications. |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Thomas | No other Justice | Same judgment and relief | Categorical objection to suppressing truthful lawful information to manipulate consumer choices; no join in either balancing holding |

## Holdings
**Direct and material advancement.** Section 1304 cannot be enforced against these Louisiana broadcasters' truthful, nonmisleading advertisements for lawful private-casino gambling because the federal scheme's actual exceptions and operation do not directly and materially advance its asserted gambling-harm reduction and state-policy accommodation interests. Potential reception in a State prohibiting private casinos does not itself make the advertised lawful transactions unlawful.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens joins this application conditionally under the existing framework; Scalia joins the application while retaining broader doctrinal reservations. Thomas supports the judgment on a different ground and adds no balancing vote.

**Controlling explanation:** Central Hudson requires a substantial interest and direct material advancement; Edenfield requires real harm and material alleviation without invariably demanding studies. Even accepting the stated interests as substantial, the Government must explain how this restriction serves them in its actual setting. The conditioned state, tribal, nonprofit, governmental and occasional promotional exceptions allow materially comparable gambling promotion that undercuts the claimed demand-reduction mechanism. Different policies supporting those exceptions do not explain why suppression of these lawful private-casino advertisements materially serves that mechanism. Rubin requires attention to those contradictions rather than the prohibition viewed alone.

Edge upheld a licensing-State restriction that meaningfully reduced promotion and accommodated lottery and nonlottery policies on its record. This differently structured private-casino scheme does not establish that mechanism merely because some signals may cross a border. The Court does not require regulation of every source at once, regard leakage or any exception as automatically fatal, or demand numerical causal proof. It does not decide whether deficiencies in advertising/social-cost evidence independently fail the Government's evidentiary burden or find that advertising never increases demand. Stronger evidence of the assumed causal chain would not cure the structural defects identified here; this is not a remand to rescue this scheme with additional causal studies.

**Reasonable fit.** Independently, suppressing these truthful lawful-casino advertisements lacks a reasonable proportional fit with the asserted interests, given the scheme's extensive comparable permissions and available direct regulation of gambling. The Government may pursue genuine incremental policies, but the actual informational burden requires justification; the First Amendment does not require the least restrictive imaginable policy.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Court-opinion coalition directly joins this independently sufficient ground, with the same stated reservations.

**Controlling explanation:** Fox requires a reasonable fit, not the least restrictive means. Rubin and 44 Liquormart treat the burden on truthful information as an inquiry distinct from material advancement. Here the restriction broadly suppresses lawful gaming information while permitting the described comparable promotion. The ability to address gambling conduct directly bears on whether that informational burden is proportionate. Different interstate reach or administrative convenience does not itself explain the actual imbalance. The Court orders no tax, casino limit or replacement program and does not assume every alternative would achieve identical effects. It holds only that the restriction before it lacks justified proportionality on this record. Ordinary Central Hudson review remains governing; the decision creates no heightened tier, vice-speech immunity or general ban on incremental economic regulation. Relief is as applied to the advertisements and stations before the Court, not facial invalidation of §1304 or its implementing rule.

## Precedent Treatment
- Central Hudson supplies ordinary lawful/nonmisleading commercial-speech review, substantial interest, material advancement and reasonable fit.
- Fox preserves the distinction between reasonable fit and least-restrictive means.
- Edenfield requires justification responsive to real harms and material alleviation without invariably requiring empirical studies.
- Ibanez tests the actual truthful-information restriction and its responsive justification.
- Rubin applies independent advancement and fit inquiries to a materially contradictory scheme and requires an actual state-accommodation mechanism.
- 44 Liquormart preserves those separate inquiries under ordinary scrutiny; its alcohol-specific rule does not create a gambling exception or a new tier.
- Edge retains its record-specific meaningful-reduction and state-accommodation holding; the different statutory permissions and mechanism here do not reproduce it.
- Posadas is not overruled through this as-applied decision, and no categorical constitutional rule against all vice regulation is adopted.

## Law After Decision
The described lawful private-casino advertising is protected against this federal prohibition because each of two ordinary commercial-speech requirements fails independently. False or misleading advertisements, promotion of unlawful transactions, targeting minors and materially narrower evidence-supported restrictions remain outside the holding. Direct regulation of gambling survives. Other asserted applications require their own justification; neither §1304 nor the FCC restriction is erased in all settings.

## Separate Writings
Thomas agrees with the judgment because government may not keep consumers ignorant of truthful information about lawful commercial activity in order to manipulate their choices. The casino advertisements present that concern directly; he does not require balancing the asserted interests or measuring precise market effects. His ground remains separate from the eight-Justice advancement and fit holdings and does not change the Court's governing scrutiny standard.

## Procedure After Action
The case returns for declaratory and appropriate injunctive relief conforming to the established as-applied invalidity. The Government may not enforce the prohibition against the advertisements and stations before the Court. No new merits trial, causal-evidence reopening, damages award, gambling-policy replacement or relief for materially different advertising is ordered.
