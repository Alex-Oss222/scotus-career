## Event

In re Sealed Case, No. 98-93. Argued January 13, 1999; decided April 13, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the District of Columbia Circuit, which affirmed an order compelling Secret Service personnel to testify before a federal grand jury. The questions are whether Rule 501 recognizes the asserted protective-function privilege and, if so, whether it is absolute or qualified and what need could overcome it. The accepted immediate appeal protects the Secretary's asserted privilege without requiring the agents first to incur contempt.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the District of Columbia Circuit is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The compulsion order remains enforceable subject to ordinary evidentiary objections, other properly asserted privileges and independently lawful protective measures. No privilege-specific showing of investigative need is required.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Stevens delivers the unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate writing is issued.

## Holdings

### Rule 501 does not recognize the proposed protective-function privilege

**Holding and operative rule:** Information obtained by Secret Service personnel while protecting the President is not, on this record, protected from federal grand-jury testimony by the asserted protective-function privilege. Because the Court rejects recognition, it establishes neither an absolute or qualified privilege nor a special test for overcoming one; other lawful objections and concrete security accommodations retain their own requirements.

**Authority:** Stevens's unanimous Opinion of the Court controls the complete rule, reasoning, reservations and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court recognizes the exceptional importance of presidential protection. Rule 501 nevertheless requires a reason-and-experience basis for withholding a class of relevant evidence. Trammel supplies that inquiry; Jaffee shows that principled common-law development remains possible, while requiring attention to the confidential relationship and its practical justification. The proposed protection extends beyond confidential communications to ordinary observations acquired through proximity. In a federal investigation or prosecution, its exception permits disclosure when an observation or statement, at the time made, gave reasonable grounds to believe a felony had been, was being, or would be committed. That contemporaneous understanding may exclude observations whose significance appears only later; it does not establish a predictable protected relationship. Director Merletti's declaration and former President Bush's letter support the prediction that compelled testimony could impair confidence and access. The Court considers that concern seriously, but the supported prediction and proposed scope do not justify this evidentiary class. No adjudicated impairment is established, and the Court does not make an actual security failure a prerequisite for every new privilege. University of Pennsylvania counsels against creating institutional confidentiality from insufficiently supported chilling predictions. Swidler protects an established attorney-client relationship; it does not automatically extend to operational observations. Neither it nor Jaffee is subjected to routine investigative-need balancing. Once recognition fails, an extraordinary-need test would supply an advisory rule for a nonexistent privilege. Clinton's accommodation of concrete official duties supports preserving independently lawful security measures, not categorical evidentiary immunity.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision concerns this federal grand-jury claim and record. It decides no classified-information, state-secrets, executive-privilege or distinct constitutional-security claim; no finding about a particular agent's knowledge, criminal conduct, actual impaired access, waiver or future protective arrangement is made.

## Precedent Treatment

- Trammel v. United States supplies reason-and-experience development with attention to the cost of withholding evidence.
- Jaffee v. Redmond permits recognition of demonstrated confidential relationships; its protection against routine case-by-case balancing remains intact.
- Swidler & Berlin v. United States protects established attorney-client confidentiality after death; operational observations do not enter that relationship by analogy alone.
- University of Pennsylvania v. EEOC supports rejecting an insufficiently justified institutional privilege.
- United States v. Nixon rejects absolute insulation from criminal process; it supplies no universal balancing rule for all recognized privileges.
- Clinton v. Jones preserves concrete accommodation of official duties in its civil setting; it creates no protective-agent evidentiary privilege.

## Law After Decision

The asserted protective-function privilege is unavailable on this record. Courts must distinguish recognition of a privilege from a test for overcoming one already recognized. Established confidential-relationship privileges remain governed by their own law, and supported security accommodations remain available without a new privilege. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is issued.

## Procedure After Action

The affirmed compulsion order remains in force. The supervising court may consider ordinary lawful objections and supported security measures under existing authority. The Court orders no new factual hearing, special necessity showing, testimony date or contempt consequence.

## Source Notes

The reviewed judgment and published record recitations appear in [In re Sealed Case, 148 F.3d 1073](https://openjurist.org/148/f3d/1073). Those recitations describe the proposed exception and the supporting access concerns; they do not establish an adjudicated impairment of protection. The questions are stated in substance.

## Event

UNUM Life Insurance Co. of America v. Ward, No. 97-1868. Argued February 24, 1999; decided April 20, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 135 F.3d 1276. Ward sought insured disability benefits under ERISA after late proof of claim and communications through his employer. The questions concern California's notice-prejudice rule and the separate employer-agency ground used in reversing UNUM's summary judgment.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

|---|---|---|

| No-relation premise for the employer-agency instruction | Reversed; any material agency issue must be reconsidered under the corrected rule | 9–0 |

**Supporting both components:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

UNUM must establish actual prejudice; lateness alone or possible prejudice does not suffice. No benefits award or actual-agency finding is entered.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Ginsburg delivers the unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. The common coalition controls both holdings and the complete remand.

## Holdings

### Notice-prejudice is saved insurance regulation

**Holding and operative rule:** California's insurance-specific rule prevents an insurer from forfeiting coverage for late notice unless it proves actual prejudice and is saved by ERISA §514(b)(2)(A) as applied to this insured policy. The rule informs Ward's federal benefits action under §502(a)(1)(B); it creates no independent state enforcement remedy and establishes neither absence of prejudice nor entitlement to benefits.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's unanimous Opinion of the Court controls this holding and the agency holding below, including their qualifications and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the insurance-saving clause to the rule's operation. Metropolitan Life preserves regulation of the insurer-insured bargain. Notice-prejudice restricts an insurer's forfeiture defense and thus directly governs that bargain. The common-sense insurance inquiry and the McCarran-Ferguson considerations support that conclusion: the rule is directed to insurance and integral to the policy relationship. Those considerations are guideposts, not three indispensable conjunctive elements; the Court need not decide whether this rule independently transfers or spreads risk. Pilot Life rejects a competing state enforcement remedy, whereas Ward invokes ERISA's own benefits action. The written deadline cannot nullify a substantive insurance rule that Congress saved. Travelers and De Buono preserve federal control of plan administration but do not erase that express reservation. John Hancock likewise requires respect for compatible insurance obligations while preserving genuinely conflicting federal commands; no command requiring absolute forfeiture is shown here. UNUM's uniformity argument therefore does not justify preemption of this policy term. The record still requires the actual-prejudice inquiry. Self-funded plans, the deemer clause's distinct protection and other timing rules are not decided.

### Mandatory administrative agency cannot rest on a no-relation premise

**Holding and operative rule:** A state rule assigning the employer an insurer-agency role because it performs benefit-plan administration directly connects with that administration under ERISA §514(a); the Ninth Circuit's contrary no-relation premise and contingent instruction cannot stand. The categorical administrative reassignment may not be used here, but the decision does not preempt every ordinary agency principle or resolve a properly presented voluntary or fact-specific agency undertaking.

**Controlling explanation:** Travelers and De Buono distinguish remote economic effects from direct commands to administration. A mandatory rule making an employer the insurer's agent for notice and claims processing reallocates administrative responsibility itself. That is materially different from the saved content of an insurance contract. Ward disputes the breadth of Elfstrom and invokes ordinary agency principles; the Court accordingly does not declare that every application of California agency law is an irrebuttable employer-agency rule. Nor does it find actual authorization, apparent authority or their absence. Ward did not press insurance saving for this agency theory, so the notice-prejudice holding cannot silently supply that distinct argument. Reversal corrects the legal premise, not the disputed agency facts. If actual prejudice makes agency material, the lower courts must assess any properly presented theory under the corrected rule, giving the policy's written-authorization provision and the legal source of the asserted agency their proper effect. No general federal agency code or automatic ineffectiveness of notice to the employer follows.

## Precedent Treatment

- Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Massachusetts preserves substantive insurance-contract regulation; the saving clause covers this forfeiture limitation.
- Pilot Life Insurance Co. v. Dedeaux excludes a competing state enforcement scheme; Ward's §502 benefits action presents a distinct question.
- Travelers and De Buono distinguish indirect economic effects from direct regulation of benefits or administration; that distinction separates the two state rules here.
- John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust preserves compatible insurance obligations while requiring attention to actual federal conflict.
- Hughes Aircraft Co. v. Jacobson distinguishes entitlement, administrative function and remedy; it supplies neither automatic benefits nor an additional state cause of action.

## Law After Decision

Insured-plan benefits may incorporate California's saved actual-prejudice requirement through ERISA's federal enforcement channel. A separate mandatory reassignment of plan-administration agency cannot survive on the ground that it has no connection with the plan. Actual consensual or fact-specific agency, when properly presented, remains a distinct inquiry. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is issued.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for determination of actual prejudice from late notice. If prejudice makes agency material, the lower courts must apply the corrected relation rule to the actual theory and policy provision. They may not reuse the rejected categorical allocation. The Court awards no benefits, finds no agency and authorizes no independent state damages action.

## Event

United States v. Haggar Apparel Co., No. 97-2044. Argued January 11, 1999; decided April 21, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Federal Circuit, 127 F.3d 1460. The questions are whether the promulgated Customs regulation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation and whether its exclusion of permapressing reasonably implements the statutory allowance for operations incidental to assembly. The dispute concerns baking resin-treated fabric after assembly abroad to activate permanent-press characteristics.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is vacated and the case remanded under the correct statutory and administrative-review framework.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing that disposition:** Stevens and Ginsburg, who would reverse outright and sustain the challenged Customs denial.

All nine reject categorical exclusion of Chevron review. The Court leaves the regulation's reasonableness and application unresolved; no refund, duty amount or new hearing is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Delegation, statutory limits and Chevron framework |
| Opinion of the Court, Part IV | Kennedy | O'Connor, Scalia, Stone-Zsela, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Unresolved validity/application and remand |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stevens | Ginsburg | Joins Parts I–III; would sustain validity and actual denial now |

## Holdings

### Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation

**Holding and operative rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing Congress's actual delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation: courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and the delegation's boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The Court of International Trade's duty to decide on its own record does not repeal that legal framework; agency labels and notice and comment alone do not establish authority or validity.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Parts I–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer and control this complete framework.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes governing law from judicial determination of facts. Sections 1500(b) and 1502(a) and the tariff delegation provisions authorize implementing rules within their scope. Section 2640(a)(1)'s own-record review does not assign all substantive tariff lawmaking exclusively to courts, and §10.11's preservation of judicial review does not require disregarding a lawful regulation. Chevron supplies the division of responsibility; NationsBank permits reasonable gap filling within actual authority. MCI and NCUA require courts to enforce enacted limits before deferring. The importer correctly insists that review remain independent on those limits and effective on the facts. That does not justify categorically withholding the weight due a sufficiently authoritative delegated rule. Conversely, recognizing that framework does not make every Customs interpretation lawful. Informal classification letters and later administrative doctrines are not decided.

### This regulation's statutory fit and actual application require the corrected inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment must be vacated because categorical nondeference left unresolved whether the regulation reasonably respects the tariff's incidental-assembly allowance and lawfully applies to this process. On remand the courts must enforce the enacted conditions, examine the regulation's actual introductory terms and examples, and resolve relevant application questions under the proper review standards; neither regulatory validity nor the claimed exemption is established in advance.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part IV is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, seven votes. Stevens and Ginsburg do not join this holding or remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The tariff requires United States fabricated components exported ready for assembly without further fabrication, retention of physical identity, and no advancement in value or improvement abroad except through assembly and incidental operations, including cleaning, lubricating and painting. The allowance reduces duties by qualifying component cost or value, with proportional treatment for specific or compound rates; it does not exempt the whole article. Regulation §10.16(b) permits incidental operations before, during or after assembly, including ordinary final pressing and folding. Section 10.16(c) addresses a significant nonassembly process whose primary purpose is fabrication, completion or physical or chemical improvement, or a significant process unrelated to assembly. Its chemical-treatment examples, including permapressing, do not erase those introductory alternatives. Timing after sewing alone settles nothing, and ordinary pressing is not necessarily resin-activating permapressing. The significance, purpose, relationship to assembly and new characteristics of the actual process bear on the statutory-regulatory boundary, not merely on duty arithmetic. NationsBank's framework does not answer those unfinished questions. AT&T distinguishes general authority from particular validity; NFFE distinguishes available discretion from its actual lawful exercise without requiring a new agency proceeding here. Both questions were granted, but that scope does not make this intertwined inquiry complete. The lower courts must undertake it without the erroneous categorical premise. The Court commands no new evidentiary hearing, burden, deadline or finding that existing litigation papers are missing.

## Precedent Treatment

- Chevron supplies review within actual delegated authority and statutory ambiguity.
- NationsBank of North Carolina v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co. permits reasonable administrative classification within statutory limits.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T and National Credit Union Administration v. First National Bank require enforcement of enacted boundaries before deference.
- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board separates general authority, particular rule validity and unresolved applications; it creates no automatic validation or universal remand rule.
- NFFE/FLRA v. Department of the Interior distinguishes legally available discretion from its lawful exercise; its distinct remedy does not compel a new Customs proceeding.

## Law After Decision

The Federal Circuit may not categorically exclude this promulgated tariff regulation from Chevron review. Judicial enforcement of statutory limits and trade-court factual review remain intact. The regulation's ultimate reasonableness and its application to Haggar's processing remain for the corrected inquiry. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens, joined by Justice Ginsburg, agrees with Parts I–III but would reverse outright. Chevron and NationsBank permit the agency to distinguish assembly and ordinary finishing from significant chemical treatment that imparts a new characteristic. In their view the actual ovenbaking activates a lasting permanent-press property and reasonably falls on the excluded side of that line. They preserve the regulation's introductory terms and do not treat every chemical effect or use of heat as disqualifying. Because review includes reasonableness and the supported process facts establish the application, they see no remaining reason to return validity or the challenged denial for decision. They would sustain that denial without calculating a new duty amount or deciding unrelated shipment issues.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Federal Circuit for consideration of regulatory reasonableness and application, with further trade-court proceedings as lawfully needed under the correct framework. The Court neither upholds the denial nor grants the exemption. No mandatory hearing, new agency proceeding or collateral shipment adjudication is imposed.

## Source Notes

The tariff and regulation, process description and two-question scope appear in [526 U.S. 380](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/97-2044.ZO.html) and the [United States petition](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/us-v-haggar-apparel-co-petition). Commercial sequencing remains the importer's contention; it is not treated as a new finding that baking is necessarily incidental assembly.

## Holdings

### A primary barrier must be tested against actual nomination opportunity

**Holding and operative rule:** A Section 2 challenge to a primary-runoff barrier turns on protected voters' equal opportunity in the affected nomination or election process, supported political cohesion and majority bloc voting in the affected electoral settings, and the totality of circumstances. It does not require proof that every additional minority-preferred nominee would win the general election, and neither candidate race, the rarity of runoffs among all primaries nor a conclusive statewide offset of winners can replace the inquiry into the affected voters' actual opportunity.

**Authority:** Souter's Parts I–II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer, control the complete statutory rule, application and remand, six votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Gingles requires proof of political cohesion and majority bloc voting in the affected electoral settings, with the necessary causal connection to the challenged barrier and a separate totality inquiry; Voinovich keeps that burden with the challengers. The inquiry must fit the barrier challenged. De Grandy rejects automatic liability and numerical safe harbors alike; Holder requires a legally and practically grounded comparison. The lower court used the small proportion of primaries reaching runoffs and a statewide net of Black and white winners to reject the relevant bloc-voting condition. It also required proof that additional plurality nominees would win general elections. Those premises constrain the very findings invoked as independent reasons to affirm. The Court therefore cannot detach the failure-of-proof conclusion from the legal errors. Candidate race is not necessarily voter preference, and gains elsewhere do not conclusively answer a local opportunity loss. The 56 adverse and 29 favorable candidate-race reversals establish neither a number of unlawfully lost offices nor negligible harm. SCLC preserves supported subsidiary facts but permits correction when a mistaken legal standard compromises the determination. The State's interest in broadly supported nominees and resistance to vote-splitting deserves concrete consideration. It does not make an extreme hypothetical plurality winner a conclusive answer. Conversely, polarization alone does not establish a violation or require plurality. The lower courts must reconsider on the existing record and any supplementation independently allowed by ordinary procedure; no new trial or particular study is automatically required.

### The supported purpose findings sustain constitutional rejection

**Holding and operative rule:** A constitutional challenge requires proof of discriminatory purpose attributable to the challenged enactment, assessed from the whole relevant record rather than electoral outcomes alone. The supported findings distinguishing the failed 1963 proposal from the enacted 1964 measure are not clearly erroneous; the historical racial climate and earlier motives do not compel a contrary purpose finding for the later enactment.

**Authority:** Souter's Part III is unanimous and controls this ground and the independent causation ground below.

**Controlling explanation:** Arlington Heights makes historical sequence, contemporaneous evidence and decisionmaking context relevant. The trial court considered those matters, including the committees and the roles of the proponents, and credited witnesses with firsthand involvement. Hunter does not make every related measure inherit the purpose of an earlier failed bill. The Court adopts no requirement of a direct admission and no categorical preference for retrospective testimony over contemporaneous evidence. The stated findings nonetheless permit the constitutional judgment. This inquiry remains separate from the statutory results errors; the Court need not resolve the full reach of Fifteenth Amendment dilution coverage because the claims fail even assuming that coverage.

### The independent same-enactment finding also defeats causation

**Holding and operative rule:** If discriminatory purpose was a substantial or motivating factor, the defenders bear the burden of showing that the same measure would have been enacted without that factor. The supported independent finding that the 1964 measure would have passed for the credited anti-manipulation and election-administration reasons satisfies that burden here and independently sustains constitutional affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Hunter and Mt. Healthy distinguish a motivating factor from a measure that would have been adopted without it. The trial court made the latter finding separately; it does not depend on the statutory denominators or proof of later general-election success. The challengers' historical evidence does not establish clear error in that supported alternative. This ground does not make mixed motives invariably lawful, transfer the same-decision burden to plaintiffs or permit benign present effects to cure a purpose violation.

Supported constitutional purpose and independent same-enactment findings sustain the constitutional judgment. No plurality arrangement or racial vote guarantee is established and no precedent is overruled.

Gingles and the totality inquiry require an actual showing of unequal opportunity; occasional reversals of a Black candidate's first-round lead do not establish it. ## Event

United States v. Sun-Diamond Growers of California, No. 98-131. Argued March 2, 1999; decided April 27, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the District of Columbia Circuit, 138 F.3d 961. The question is whether an illegal gratuity under 18 U.S.C. §201(c)(1)(A) must be linked to an identifiable official act. Review concerns Count One and the Government's harmless-error contention; other sentencing and reporting matters are outside the case.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment reversing the challenged gratuity conviction and remanding for a new trial is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Count One remains reversed. A new trial is available if otherwise lawful under an instruction requiring the official-act link. No acquittal or final determination of indictment or evidentiary sufficiency is entered.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Scalia delivers the unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate writing is issued.

## Holdings

### An illegal gratuity requires an identifiable official-act link

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 201(c)(1)(A) requires proof that a thing of value was given for or because of an identifiable official act performed or to be performed by the official. Officeholding, general goodwill and the donor's interests before the agency do not alone satisfy that connection; the Government need not prove bribery's corrupt exchange, an express agreement or direct evidence of the link.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court controls the complete construction, error and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court gives the statute's relational language its ordinary work. Its object is an official act within §201(a)(3), not the recipient's status. A reward for an act already performed can qualify without purchasing that act; a future act can also qualify, but future timing does not eliminate the need for an act. The Government invokes anticorruption purposes and the distinction from bribery. Those points explain why gratuity need not include a corrupt bargain, but do not convert it into a general ban on gifts to officials. Aguilar illustrates the need to preserve each offense's operative object, without importing its separate obstruction elements. Holloway likewise requires offense-specific analysis. Here the jury was expressly allowed to convict without identifying an act connected to the gifts. Reading the statutory words elsewhere in the charge did not cure that contrary permission. Gaudin preserves the jury's responsibility to find the enacted elements. Circumstantial proof and multiple act-linked gratuities remain possible. The Court gives no comprehensive definition of every official act and does not legalize gifts prohibited by ethics rules, campaign-finance law or another statute.

### The supported harmless-error argument does not sustain this conviction

**Holding and operative rule:** The error is not harmless on the Government's offered ground that pending regulatory interests and repetition of statutory words necessarily supplied the missing act-linked jury determination. The Count One reversal and lawful new-trial remand therefore stand; this is no rule that every error concerning an element is structural or that the Government lacks all sufficient evidence.

**Controlling explanation:** The explicit instruction allowed a conviction for cultivating goodwill or honoring official status. The vice-president's business role, company reimbursement and two regulatory interests may support act-linked proof, but do not make that inference unavoidable. His friendship with the Secretary confirms that motive was a real issue without creating a friendship defense. The jury's general verdict cannot be treated as a finding of an element it was told it need not find. Gaudin protects that actual allocation of decisionmaking while leaving other harmless-error settings unresolved. The Court conducts no new exhaustive sufficiency review. Indictment sufficiency was not granted and is not decided; any district-court power to consider a properly available indictment question remains intact.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Aguilar preserves the offense's required object; its obstruction knowledge and nexus rules are not imported into gratuity.
- United States v. Gaudin preserves jury determination of the enacted elements; it does not make all errors concerning elements structural.
- Holloway v. United States supplies offense-specific construction; its conditional-intent holding is not an additional gratuity requirement.
- Jones v. United States retains its distinct statutory and jury safeguards; this case creates no new sentencing classification rule.

## Law After Decision

The gratuity statute reaches act-linked gifts, including rewards for past acts, without requiring a bribery bargain. Status-only or goodwill-only instructions cannot support this conviction. Other gift restrictions, lawful circumstantial proof and ordinary proceedings on remand remain available. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is issued.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns under the existing new-trial remand. Any retrial must otherwise be lawful and use the correct act-linked instruction. This Court does not determine the indictment's sufficiency, exhaustively assess proof, order acquittal or reopen separate probation, reporting and resentencing questions. ## Event

Russell v. Burris and Citizens for Clean Government v. Russell, Nos. 98-397 and 98-399. Argued February 22, 1999; decided April 28, 1999. On writs of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, 146 F.3d 563. Review concerns Arkansas's candidate contribution ceilings, the annual ordinary-PAC receipt ceiling and the qualified small-donor PAC allowance. The distinct independent-expenditure-committee limit and local-authorizing provision are outside review.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded for conforming declaratory and injunctive proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

| $200 annual per-person/per-ordinary-PAC receipts | Invalidation reversed, 5–4 | Same five | Same four would affirm invalidation |

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | O'Connor | All other Justices | Governing standard; two invalidated candidate categories |
| Opinion of the Court, Parts II–IV | O'Connor | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Residual $100, ordinary-PAC receipts and small-donor differential |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stone-Zsela | None | Broader cap invalidation; small-donor remand |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Kennedy | Scalia, Thomas | Would invalidate all reviewed components |

## Holdings

### Contribution limits require actual anticorruption fit; two candidate categories fail here

**Holding and operative rule:** Candidate contribution limits must be closely drawn to a sufficiently important interest in preventing corruption or its appearance while preserving meaningful political association. On this record the $300 per-person/per-election ceilings for Governor, Lieutenant Governor, Secretary of State, Treasurer of State, Auditor of State, Attorney General and Commissioner of State Lands, and the $100 ceilings for Arkansas Supreme Court Justice and Court of Appeals Judge, fail that requirement.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I is unanimous. The three holdings that follow are controlled by her Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, five votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Buckley distinguishes contributions from expenditures and allows prophylaxis without a completed bribe; it does not permit equality of political influence as an independent justification. Neither a dollar figure nor an inflation comparison establishes a permanent constitutional boundary. The statewide campaign scale and fundraising burden make the legislative examples inadequate to justify the enumerated $300 limits. The two appellate judicial offices face a distinct combined burden: large electoral areas, no personal solicitation or acceptance, and committee fundraising confined to 180 days before the primary through 45 days after the last contested election. The supported findings establish inadequate resources for effective advocacy under that combination. Suster recognizes judicial impartiality but does not ignore the practical restriction or decide a contribution case through expenditure rules. The Court invalidates neither the solicitation canon nor every judicial limit. Arkansas Supreme Court races belong to the statutory $100 category despite their statewide geography. Successful campaigns, self-financing and a small gift's relative share are relevant circumstances, not automatic answers.

### The residual $100 restriction survives the tried categorical challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** The record does not establish the asserted categorical invalidity of the $100 per-person/per-election ceiling for covered offices outside the seven-office $300 list and the two named appellate judicial offices. This residual class includes other covered judicial offices; rejection of the blanket challenge does not decide a genuinely different office-specific application without its own presented grounds and record.

**Controlling explanation:** The trial evidence included perceived influence from concentrated contributions, the Maddox fundraiser and tobacco-related giving, even though actual improper access or undue influence was not found. Candidate agreement with donors is not itself corruption. The actual special-election experience, reported $24,000 and $40,000 Senate fundraising and resetting of limits for each election support the State's assessment in the demonstrated legislative setting. Against that stand professional fundraisers' contrary testimony, sharp fundraising changes and effects on up to eighty percent of earlier legislative contributions. The Court finds the practical evidence sufficient for this tried challenge without requiring campaign impossibility or equating hypothetical donor counts with actual recruitment. Kentucky Right to Life requires analysis of the actual contribution channel, not automatic invalidation from the amount. The two appellate offices' cumulative burden cannot be imputed to every judge merely because solicitation restrictions are shared. Nor are legislative successes findings about every other office. The decision rejects the grounds offered for blanket invalidation; it orders no universal office survey and invents no waiver or absent distinct claim.

### The ordinary-PAC receipt limit survives on this operational record

**Holding and operative rule:** The $200 annual limit from one person to any one ordinary PAC survives the presented challenge where the tried operational record preserves effective collective advocacy and the restriction is fitted to the actual candidate-conduit channel. It is a per-recipient limit, not one aggregate pool across all committees, and supplies no general approval for materially different recipient functions or demonstrated pooling burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Day protects meaningful political pooling and requires a real fit inquiry. Here years of operation and robust candidate-directed PAC activity support the lower findings of no appreciable impairment in the tried case. Ordinary Arkansas PACs lack the federal multicandidate qualifications of at least fifty contributors and contributions to at least five candidates, so the candidate-transfer channel gives the circumvention concern concrete work. Kentucky Right to Life's aggregate-pool problem is distinct. Annual duration, recipient function, actual operation and amount must be considered together. Supporting other committees does not alone cure a burden on the chosen association, and aggregate success is not an automatic safe harbor. The combination established here nevertheless supports the restriction. California Medical permits genuine conduit regulation without making its fractured lowest-protection rationale a universal rule. A different operational burden or independent recipient function remains open.

### The exact small-donor qualification supports a different candidate allowance

**Holding and operative rule:** Arkansas may permit a qualifying registered small-donor PAC to give $2,500 per candidate per election while ordinary sources face lower ceilings, where that committee receives contributions exclusively from individuals and no more than $25 cumulatively from any individual during a calendar year. The financial distinction is justified by the anticorruption interest and fit of the dispersed-donor structure; it is neither a viewpoint preference nor a rule that any small-donor label suffices.

**Controlling explanation:** Annual individual aggregation disperses financing and attenuates concentrated donor control; ordinary PACs may receive larger individual amounts and entity contributions. Those differences support differentiated treatment under the asserted compelling anticorruption interest and the required fit of this exact classification; the ruling does not rest on rational-basis review. A committee's $2,500 transfer can still create recipient-end influence, the principal contrary argument. The Court acknowledges that risk but finds this precise qualification sufficient to distinguish the financing channels without requiring identical caps. It does not find small-donor committees incapable of corruption, authorize unlimited candidate giving or rely merely on the possibility of forming a different association. California Medical recognizes that financing structures can matter; Day preserves actual association rather than nominal permission. The $25 condition is annual cumulative intake, not a repeatable allowance per gift. The $2,500 figure governs outgoing candidate contributions, not committee receipts. Every qualification remains operative; sham or materially different structures gain no adjudicated protection.

## Precedent Treatment

- Buckley v. Valeo preserves contribution/expenditure distinctions, sufficient interest and close fit without a permanent dollar floor.
- Day v. Holahan protects actual pooling and requires a supported circumvention fit; the Court distinguishes its burden record rather than overruling it.
- Kentucky Right to Life preserves separate direct-contribution, aggregate-pool and sourcewide-receipt analyses and their actual claim limits.
- California Medical Association v. FEC supports actual candidate-conduit regulation and attention to financing structures; its fractured lowest-protection rationale is not a universal rule.
- Colorado Republican and Clifton preserve genuine independence and require actual candidate connections; a committee label bypasses neither protection.
- Suster preserves judicial impartiality while distinguishing expenditures from contributions and requiring examination of the actual restraint.
- Reform Party and Brower reject resource-parity entitlements while leaving practical prohibitory burdens reviewable.

## Law After Decision

The seven enumerated $300 limits and two named appellate-office $100 applications remain unenforceable. The residual $100 categorical challenge and challenges to the ordinary-PAC receipt cap and exact small-donor differential fail on this record. The residual category includes other covered judicial offices without new findings about every such race. Contribution and expenditure rules, meaningful association and fact-specific future claims retain their distinct requirements. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with the two invalidated candidate categories but would invalidate the remaining $100 and ordinary-PAC $200 limits as well. Buckley permits prevention of corruption and its appearance, but the severe restrictions on effective pooling require a demonstrated connection to the actual corruption or circumvention risk. He finds that fit lacking and treats the PAC receipt burden as arising before any contribution to a candidate. He proposes no permanent dollar minimum. On the small-donor allowance he would vacate and remand rather than validate or invalidate it now: actual donor aggregation, control, earmarking, operation and safeguards, together with any consequent severability issue, must be examined. Diffuse financing can justify distinction, but neither its label nor nominal inequality decides the question.

Justice Kennedy, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, would affirm all invalidations. Their Day and Kentucky Right to Life grounds distinguish three defects: the residual candidate cap's substantial fundraising burden is not sufficiently answered by appearance evidence and limited successful campaigns; the annual chosen-PAC receipt restraint reaches beyond the identified conduit problem; and a committee can induce candidate obligation through a $2,500 transfer despite diffuse intake. They preserve tailored conduit and earmark regulation and do not demand completed bribery, expenditure scrutiny for candidate cash, identical source ceilings or a permanent dollar floor.

The lower courts must conform declaratory and injunctive relief to the component judgments and implement actual continuing statutory relationships under the existing severability framework. ## Event

Bank of America National Trust & Savings Ass’n v. 203 North LaSalle Street Partnership, No. 97-1418. Argued November 2, 1998; decided May 3, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 126 F.3d 955. The question is whether a plan may reserve to old equity the opportunity to contribute new capital and obtain reorganized ownership over an impaired, unpaid senior class's objection. The Bank preserves that absolute-priority objection but does not challenge the property valuation or other confirmation findings, appeal the refusal to terminate proposal exclusivity, or seek review of claim classification.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment approving confirmation is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens.

The confirmation order cannot stand. Further lawful plan proceedings must remove the absolute-priority defect; the Court does not directly reverse the unappealed exclusivity ruling, reopen classification or adjudge the accepted valuations incorrect.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Kennedy delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela also concurs separately on market exposure. Justice Stevens dissents. The eight-Justice property-and-causation rule controls; Stone's further position is not a holding of the Court.

## Holdings

### Old equity cannot reserve this valuable investment opportunity because of its former ownership

**Holding and operative rule:** When an impaired senior unsecured class objects and is not paid in full, §1129(b)(2)(B)(ii) forbids a plan from reserving to old equity alone the opportunity to contribute new capital and acquire reorganized ownership where old ownership supplies that exclusive eligibility. The opportunity is property received under the plan on account of the old interest, even when the proposed investment purchases new ownership and the underlying valuations are unchallenged.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, eight votes for the complete rule, application, reservations and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court identifies the right the plan distributes. New money explains payment for new ownership; it does not explain why only former owners receive the chance to acquire it. Ahlers recognizes economic ownership rights and confines equitable treatment to the Code. The promised $6.125 million over five years, worth about $4.1 million presently, does not erase the separate eligibility preference. The Court finds no numerical valuation error and presumes no higher outside bid. Section 1121 governs proposal exclusivity, while §1129 governs a resulting plan's substantive acceptability. The lawful, unappealed proposal period does not independently make an ownership preference confirmable. Noland and Reorganized CF&I preserve actual statutory priorities and exceptions; Nobelman illustrates why valuation and the legal treatment of rights are different inquiries, without deciding this Chapter 11 question itself. Stevens's contrary construction gives decisive weight to the new consideration and accepted confirmation findings. Those facts do not answer why old ownership alone confers the opportunity. The preserved priority objection therefore succeeds without reopening the unappealed procedural order or claim classification. The Court reserves whether a general new-value corollary survives and its complete conditions. It prescribes no auction, bidder, automatic termination of exclusivity or universal valuation method, and approves no future plan. The same exclusive allocation cannot sustain this confirmation; a changed proposal or process must satisfy the Code through lawful further proceedings.

## Precedent Treatment

- Norwest Bank Worthington v. Ahlers recognizes economic ownership rights and limits equity to the Code; its reservation concerning actual new capital remains open.
- Case v. Los Angeles Lumber Products identifies the historical new-capital distinction; the Court neither recognizes nor rejects every modern new-value plan.
- United States v. Noland and United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators preserve statutory ranking and genuine exceptions rather than judicially preferred priorities.
- Nobelman v. American Savings Bank distinguishes valuation from the legal rights protected by statute; its Chapter 13 mortgage rule is not extended wholesale to this Chapter 11 dispute.

## Law After Decision

The exclusive old-equity investment opportunity defeats this plan under the absolute-priority rule. Accurate asset or contribution valuation and lawful proposal exclusivity do not cure that distinct statutory preference. General new-value survival, complete conditions and the confirmability of a different plan remain unresolved. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the Court and would require genuine market exposure before old equity can receive this ownership opportunity in a later plan. Its value cannot be established merely by calling the cash contribution substantial. In his view termination of exclusivity, competitive bidding or another process must test whether the contribution reflects market value and the opportunity carries no residual old-interest preference, while satisfying all statutory requirements. He prescribes no single auction format and neither approves nor rejects every new-value plan. This additional market-testing instruction does not command the Court's majority.

Justice Stevens would affirm. Los Angeles Lumber's capital distinction and Ahlers's reservation support treating the new ownership as acquired for the new contribution. The property valuation and other confirmation findings are unchallenged, and the lawful proposal-exclusivity ruling is not before the Court. He does not regard the plan's investment eligibility as a separate distributed asset requiring another exchange or a compulsory competitive process. Noland and Reorganized CF&I demand fidelity to enacted conditions rather than an additional judicial condition. He acknowledges that exclusivity does not immunize every plan and that eligibility may have economic value; he rejects the Court's legal conclusion here without finding this opportunity valueless, treating the installment promise as immediate payment or approving every proposed new-value arrangement.

## Procedure After Action

The confirmation order must be vacated and further plan proceedings conducted consistently with the priority holding. The unappealed exclusivity order, unreviewed classification and accepted numerical valuations are not separately reversed. The Court orders no liquidation, immediate payment, new price, superior bidder, mandatory hearing or particular auction, and approves no replacement plan in advance.

The promised contribution's present value is distinct from its nominal five-year amount. No actual competing bid or independently priced opportunity is assumed.

## Event

El Paso Natural Gas Co. v. Neztsosie, No. 98-6. Argued March 2, 1999; decided May 3, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 136 F.3d 610. Navajo plaintiffs brought tribal-court claims alleging uranium-related injuries. The companies sought federal relief under Price-Anderson and appealed partial injunctions; the plaintiffs did not cross-appeal. The questions concern federal determination of statutory coverage before tribal exhaustion and the appellate enlargement of nonappealing plaintiffs' relief.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is vacated and the cases remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting both the coverage-first and cross-appeal rulings:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The federal district court must determine Price-Anderson coverage itself. The appellate enlargement dissolving unappealed partial injunctions is set aside; those orders return to their preappeal position, without a new merits adjudication of their propriety.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivers the unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine join both holdings and the complete remand.

## Holdings

### Federal courts determine Price-Anderson coverage before imposing tribal exhaustion

**Holding and operative rule:** When the invoked Price-Anderson federal proceeding requires deciding whether pleaded claims are covered public-liability actions, the federal court must decide that predicate itself before imposing prudential tribal exhaustion. Covered claims follow the Act's specific federal channel; uncovered claims retain their own jurisdictional, tribal-authority and exhaustion analysis.

**Authority:** Thomas's unanimous Opinion of the Court controls this holding and the cross-appeal holding below.

**Controlling explanation:** National Farmers Union and Iowa Mutual ordinarily allow tribal courts to consider their authority first as a matter of judicial comity. Price-Anderson creates a specific federal cause and jurisdiction, together with removal and consolidation mechanisms for covered public-liability claims. That arrangement cannot perform its assigned work if a court-created exhaustion rule postpones the federal coverage decision. The removal provision expressly names state courts; the Court does not rewrite it to say tribal courts or invent a tribal removal filing. The relevant question is the federal court's already invoked coverage task, which the Act's jurisdiction and consolidation design place before ordinary comity. Quackenbush keeps jurisdiction distinct from discretionary surrender. Strate likewise requires an actual source of tribal governing authority; exhaustion itself supplies none. The Court does not decide whether these allegations establish coverage, whether every mining injury is a covered nuclear incident, or whether any uncovered claim may proceed in a tribal forum. Mille Lacs requires attention to actual instruments and rights, not a presumed extinguishment of unexamined treaty protections.

### Nonappealing appellees may defend a decree but may not enlarge it

**Holding and operative rule:** An appellee may defend the existing decree on an alternative preserved ground but may not, without a cross-appeal, obtain an enlargement of its own rights or reduction of the appellant's rights under that decree. The Ninth Circuit could not dissolve the partial injunctions for plaintiffs who did not appeal them; that enlargement is vacated.

**Controlling explanation:** American Railway Express draws the distinction between a ground supporting the existing judgment and a request for a better judgment. Respondents' proposed dissolution was the latter. Comity does not convert their defense into an appeal, and no independent jurisdictional defect supplies a basis requiring that dissolution here. The Court need not classify every cross-appeal omission as jurisdictional or decide every possible exception. Restoring the prior decrees also does not decide their substantive merits or authorize broader relief. Any additional coercive relief must have an actual jurisdictional and equitable basis, respect proper-party and immunity limits, and fit the coverage determination.

## Precedent Treatment

- National Farmers Union Insurance Cos. v. Crow Tribe and Iowa Mutual Insurance Co. v. LaPlante retain their ordinary prudential exhaustion rule; it yields to this specific federal coverage task.
- American Railway Express Co. v. Levee preserves alternative grounds for defending a decree but prohibits an unappealed enlargement.
- Strate v. A-1 Contractors requires a substantive source of tribal authority and preserves proper-party, immunity and equitable limits; it does not decide these mining claims by label.
- Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co. keeps jurisdiction and prudential abstention distinct.
- Minnesota v. Mille Lacs Band preserves instrument-specific rights; no unexamined treaty right is extinguished here.

## Law After Decision

Price-Anderson coverage is a federal threshold task in these proceedings. Covered claims cannot be delayed by ordinary prudential tribal exhaustion, while uncovered claims remain governed by their actual jurisdictional rules. Appellees still need a cross-appeal to obtain the favorable decree enlargement at issue. No precedent is overruled.

No separate writing is issued.

## Procedure After Action

The federal district court must determine actual statutory coverage. The existing partial injunctions resume their preappeal posture; further proceedings and any additional relief must conform to the actual coverage, party, immunity and equitable limits. The Court orders no finding that all claims are covered, no injunction against every tribal actor, no mandatory new hearing and no tort-liability determination.

## Event

INS v. Aguirre-Aguirre, No. 97-1754. Argued March 3, 1999; decided May 3, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 121 F.3d 521. The questions concern review of the Board of Immigration Appeals' interpretation and application of the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception to withholding under former §1253(h). The existing agency motion concerning alleged translation and transcription errors remains distinct from the legal questions presented.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed and the case remanded for proceedings under the correct legal framework.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Court rejects the Ninth Circuit's mandatory additional conditions but makes no new final withholding or asylum determination. ## Holdings

### Courts must respect the Board's permissible construction within actual delegated authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §§1103(a)(1) and former 1253(h), together with the Board's adjudicative assignment in 8 C.F.R. §3.1(d)(1), courts must respect a permissible Board construction of the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception after independently enforcing enacted limits. Interpretation, application and factual review retain their distinct requirements; neither agency status nor a general appeal to foreign-affairs expertise supplies automatic correctness.

**Authority:** Breyer's Parts I–II are joined by all nine Justices and control this holding and the next two merits holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Haggar requires authority before deference and distinguishes a lawful interpretive choice from particular validity and application. The present statute and Board assignment establish authority independently of Haggar's tariff rulemaking setting. NationsBank permits reasoned classification within genuine ambiguity; MCI and NCUA prohibit erasing statutory conditions. The exception concerns serious reasons for considering that the applicant committed a serious nonpolitical crime outside the United States before arriving here. No criminal conviction is required, but the territorial, temporal and substantive conditions remain operative. The humanitarian importance of withholding supports careful enforcement of those limits, not an exclusively judicial factor code. Cardoza-Fonseca and Stevic illustrate independent enforcement of statutory meaning; Elias-Zacarias preserves the separate standard for reviewing supported findings. The Board must apply its announced lawful construction to the actual record and explain its decision. The Court adopts neither unconditional deference nor an automatic new agency hearing whenever judicial review used a mistaken premise.

### A properly established crime exception requires no additional persecution-risk balance

**Holding and operative rule:** Once the serious-nonpolitical-crime exception is properly established, former §1253(h)(2) makes the paragraph (1) withholding protection inapplicable without an additional balancing of feared persecution against offense seriousness. The Board may address that exception while assuming the applicant could show that life or freedom would be threatened because of race, religion, nationality, membership in a particular social group or political opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute separately states the protection and its exceptions. A greater threatened harm does not dissolve a properly established crime exclusion through an additional condition Congress did not supply. The Handbook paragraph 156 argument relied on below presents a serious humanitarian objection, but does not change those distinct statutory inquiries. The Court finds no absence of persecution risk and creates no general discretion to deny withholding to an otherwise eligible applicant. Relevant political circumstances remain material under the lawful crime analysis. Discretionary asylum is a different question and receives no automatic disposition from this withholding rule.

### The political-criminal comparison does not require every judicially preferred subsidiary finding

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board may compare an offense's political character with its ordinary criminal character and consider gross disproportionality or atrocious conduct in determining which predominates. The statute requires neither a separate atrocity finding in every case nor findings that the violence was necessary to or successful in achieving its political objective; the Board must still assess actual political purpose, context and conduct under its announced standard.

**Controlling explanation:** Political motive alone does not establish a political offense, just as unlawfulness alone does not make one nonpolitical. The Board's explanation considered protest objectives alongside property destruction and civilian assaults and found the criminal character disproportionate. The Court reviews that actual explanation rather than adopting new factual findings from the contested testimony. The Ninth Circuit’s comparison with more severe conduct is a consistency objection, not necessarily a universal atrocity prerequisite. The Board’s actual disproportionate-conduct explanation answers that objection; the greater atrocity of another case is no minimum threshold for this alternative ground. Handbook paragraph 152 and the suggested McMullen comparison reinforce the importance of political context and disproportion, but the absence of a separate comparison with McMullen's atrocious acts does not defeat a determination resting on the supported political-criminal comparison. Atrocious means may defeat political characterization; atrocity is not necessary in every case. A clear causal relation to a political objective would not necessarily overcome a supported disproportion finding, and the Court reserves any independent causal-link requirement in a different case. Relevant comparisons and reasoned treatment of genuine inconsistency remain possible. No categorical rule about bus burnings, assault severity or unsuccessful protest is announced.

### The late record-error contention remains for its existing agency procedure

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court declines to decide the translation/transcription contention first raised here rather than before the Board or Court of Appeals; the applicant's existing BIA motion for a new hearing remains for determination through that lawful channel. If the BIA determines that modification of the record is necessary, it may determine whether the modified record warrants further consideration of withholding; no error, new hearing or outcome is presumed.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part III is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, eight rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins this component's disposition only and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged errors may be material if established, but this Court is not the first forum for resolving a contention not presented below. The existing motion supplies an actual avenue for that inquiry; its filing proves neither entitlement nor futility. The 1995 regulation's burden applies only if evidence indicates a withholding-denial ground; then the applicant must prove by a preponderance that the indicated ground does not apply. On the existing unrevised agency account, repeated gasoline-fueled bus burnings and civilian assaults, assessed against the political objective, indicate this exclusion and trigger that allocation. This uses conduct evidence, not silence, and makes no new credibility determination. The allocation is distinct from statutory serious reasons and judicial factual review. Failure to file a Board brief despite an extension is procedural history, not default, a concession, an adverse factual inference or proof that the conditional burden was unmet. The merits holdings rest independently on the lawful framework and stated Board explanation. A corrected record may affect their application without changing the statutory rule. The appellate remand itself commands no new Board hearing, stay or removal.

## Event

Redmon v. United States, No. 98-243. Argued March 23, 1999; decided May 10, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit's en banc judgment, 138 F.3d 1109. The preserved conditional-plea question concerns police inspection of closed refuse containers beside attached garages on an unenclosed shared approach to two homes. The separate sentencing question is not before the Court.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The reviewed suppression determination and dependent appellate affirmance are vacated, and the case is remanded for application of the proper Fourth Amendment framework.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg accept this minimum relief while also concluding that the inspection was a search. Stone-Zsela and O'Connor leave that application for reconsideration. The Court orders no automatic exclusion, acquittal, release, plea withdrawal, new sentence or new evidentiary hearing.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg | Negative access/discard rule, legal correction and minimum vacatur/remand |
| Concurrence | Stevens | Souter, Ginsburg | Joins Court; additionally finds this inspection a search |
| Dissent | Kennedy | Scalia, Thomas, Breyer | Would affirm on actual exposure and collection facts |

Five directly join the Court's bounded rule and relief. Only three find a search conclusively; the Court announces no such holding and relies on no synthesis of competing rationales.

## Holdings

### Future discard and ordinary approach access do not themselves authorize inspection in protected residential space

**Holding and operative rule:** Greenwood does not authorize warrantless inspection of closed refuse containers merely because their owner intends later collection or private persons can approach them; courts must determine whether police entered or exploited constitutionally protected residential space and assess the actual location, exposure and intrusion. The lower determination must be set aside because collection-point equivalence and ordinary passage access cannot substitute for that legal inquiry into this home-side physical inspection; the Court leaves its ultimate search classification open.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, controls the complete bounded rule and vacatur/remand. The three concurring Justices' additional search conclusion is not a majority holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenwood concerns refuse exposed for collection outside the home's protected surrounding area, or curtilage. Dunn examines proximity to the home, enclosure, the area's uses and measures shielding it from passersby; no one consideration controls. The layout here is known: the open common driveway supplies the approach to both front doors, and the containers stood near the connected garages about twenty-four feet from the sidewalk for routine collection. The lower court discussed Dunn and announced a fact-specific result. The legal objection is not that it ignored every circumstance. Ordinary passage and future transfer to a collector do not, without the proper residential-space inquiry, establish authority to remove concealed contents. Fields and Carter distinguish actual lawful access and natural observation from access to protected information or effects. The shared approach is substantial contrary evidence, and collection placement also matters; neither becomes a universal investigative license. The three concurring Justices find these facts already establish a search, but five agree that the adverse determination cannot stand on the legal equivalence used below. Reconsideration preserves supported facts and requires no finding that every driveway is curtilage. If no protected space was entered or exploited and the exposure fits Greenwood, the judgment may be reinstated. If a constitutional search occurred, ordinary warrant-fruit, independent-source, attenuation and suppression rules and the actual conditional-plea consequences apply on their established predicates. No exception, exclusion or outcome is presumed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision creates no general privacy right in curbside garbage and does not decide a collector's private inspection, a private search later reported to police, or containers outside protected residential space freely accessible from a public route. It orders no new facts, automatic hearing or sentence and does not treat limited private access as general police permission.

## Precedent Treatment

- California v. Greenwood retains its outside-curtilage public-exposure holding; it is not extended into a universal discard rule.
- United States v. Dunn supplies the four contextual curtilage considerations without making proximity or enclosure alone decisive.
- Fields/Crawley and Carter/Johns preserve distinctions among actual access, natural observation, protected space and concealed information, with their actual separate joins intact.
- Ornelas preserves independent review of legal application with appropriate deference to supported facts and reasonable inferences.
- Knowles and Wyoming v. Houghton retain their separate roadside and automobile rules; neither supplies residential investigative authority here.

## Law After Decision

Courts must examine the actual protected residential space, exposure and manner of police access rather than treat intended disposal or ordinary approach access as sufficient by themselves. The Court has not decided that this inspection was a search. Greenwood remains applicable to its actual exposure setting, and lawful findings and ordinary conditional remedies remain necessary. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Souter and Ginsburg, joins the Court's legal rule and accepts vacatur and remand as minimum relief, but would hold that this inspection was a search. Closed containers immediately adjoining the dwelling do not become public simply because visitors may pass or an authorized collector will later take them. Fields and Carter protect concealed domestic information against equating limited access with publication; Greenwood's outside-curtilage exposure is different. The shared approach is the strongest contrary fact, but permits approach rather than investigative rummaging. They would return ordinary warrant-fruit, suppression and conditional-plea consequences without automatic exclusion or release.

Justice Kennedy, joined by Justices Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, would affirm. They agree that future discard does not authorize entering otherwise protected residential space everywhere. Here, however, the actual open route used to approach two front doors and deliberate placement for collection together establish sufficient exposure under Greenwood and Dunn. They acknowledge that opening closed containers is more intrusive than viewing from a route but regard collection placement and actual ordinary access as decisive on these facts. Their Fields/Carter grounds reject mere possible access as a universal test. They adopt no automobile exception, curtilage abolition, intent-alone abandonment rule or permission to inspect enclosed home areas and ordinary personal effects.

## Procedure After Action

The lower courts must reconsider the protected-space and access questions on the supported record under the corrected rule. A no-search conclusion consistent with Greenwood may permit reinstatement. If a search occurred, the actual relation to the warrant and any properly supported independent-source, attenuation or other available remedial ground must be assessed before suppression and conditional-plea consequences follow. The Court orders no new hearing, automatic exclusion, acquittal, release, unconditional plea withdrawal or sentencing decision.

## Source Notes

The complete reviewed opinion and separate writings are [United States v. Redmon, 138 F.3d 1109](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/138/138.F3d.1109.96-3361.html). The [United States opposition](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/redmon-v-us-opposition) corroborates the posture and described layout while presenting the Government's legal position. The decision rests on the stated facts and legal access distinction, not an inferred absence of the lower record or a reconstructed image.

## Event

Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation v. CF&I Fabricators of Utah, Inc., No. 98-1440. Decided May 13, 1999, after full merits submission. On writ of certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 150 F.3d 1293. Review concerns tax priority for unpaid pension contributions, additional administrative priority and valuation of the unfunded-benefit claim. The prior §4971 assessment case involved a different obligation. Recovery remains limited by the agreed Appeal Fund arrangement.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed in part, vacated in part, and remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Component | Disposition | Supporting | Opposing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tax priority for unpaid minimum contributions | Vacated and remanded, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela would affirm denial |

| Unfunded-benefit claim valuation | Vacated and remanded, 8–1 | Same eight Associates | Stone-Zsela would preserve the lower valuation |

No tax priority or claim amount is awarded. The allowed postpetition-service administrative component and Appeal Fund limitation remain intact.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and III | Souter | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Tax linkage and valuation vacatur/remand |

| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stone-Zsela | None | Would affirm tax-priority denial and existing valuation too |

## Holdings

### The actual tax-treatment connection must be read with its lien conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** A court may not reject §412(n)(4)(C)'s incorporation of rules similar to ERISA §4068(c), including its express Title 11 tax treatment, solely because the Bankruptcy Code does not refer back to the pension provision. It must determine the incorporated treatment's scope and satisfy the specified lien conditions and applicable pre-1994 §507(a)(7) requirements before granting tax priority; the Court grants none here.

**Authority:** Souter's Parts I and III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, control the tax and valuation holdings, eight votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 412(n) addresses covered unpaid required contributions exceeding $1 million; its pertinent rule makes the lien arise on the sixtieth day after the installment's due date. The tax direction concerns an amount with respect to which that lien is imposed and imports rules similar to §4068(c), (d) and (e). Section 4068(c), codified at §1368(c), expressly addresses Title 11 or insolvency and treatment as a tax for Title 11 purposes. Congress's instruction does not disappear because of its location. Reorganized CF&I examined a separate ten-percent assessment whose label did not determine its operation; it established neither a universal checklist nor one-way incorporation. This actual connector must be construed before functional private-plan purpose decides the matter. Noland preserves enacted priorities and genuine exceptions. The strongest affirmance alternative remains open: the bankruptcy and district courts found the installment due fewer than sixty days before filing and concluded no lien arose, with the stay preventing later attachment. The circuit did not decide that ground. The Court returns it without finding attachment, declaring the lien unnecessary, deciding every stay exception or disturbing the reported timing. A lawful resolution may still defeat priority. Remand requires neither new evidence nor a lien proceeding and selects no payment.

### Prepetition-service compensation does not become a new administrative debt merely because payment is later due

**Holding and operative rule:** The additional pension-contribution claim, attributable to prepetition employee services, does not obtain §503(b)(1)(A) administrative priority merely from a later due date, continuing funding duty or later plan termination. The already allowed component for actual postpetition services remains, and Reading's distinct postpetition-liability route without an estate-benefit requirement remains intact.

**Authority:** Souter's Part II is joined by all nine Justices and controls this complete debt-origin ground and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Noland requires the actual statutory category, not a preferred ranking based on creditor hardship. Hughes Aircraft and UNUM distinguish an enforceable obligation from the particular relief it supports. Reading supplies a real alternative: postpetition negligence liability received administrative treatment without a measurable estate benefit. Even assuming a qualifying statutory liability can receive comparable treatment, these additional amounts remain compensation earned by earlier labor, not a newly incurred postpetition injury or debt. Operating the business and retaining a statutory funding duty do not excuse nonpayment, but neither changes the origin of every dollar owed. The Court adopts no universal benefit prerequisite, no rule that all pension duties are merely contractual and no categorical exclusion of postpetition statutory wrongs. The Coal Act analogy has a different statutory trigger; §1114's retiree-medical category is also distinct. Neither makes these earlier-service contributions newly incurred. This independent ground survives the separate tax remand and preserves the existing postpetition-service allowance.

### The statutory liability measure must be reconciled with bankruptcy allowance

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 1362(b)(1)(A) and 1301(a)(18), including the latter's incorporation of PBGC-prescribed assumptions, define the underlying unfunded-benefit liability and cannot be displaced categorically merely because the claim is in bankruptcy. Courts must apply that lawful measure together with §502(b)'s petition-date allowance command, enforce actual statutory limits and conflicts, and consider properly presented validity or application objections; the Court validates no universal methodology and fixes no amount.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1362 measures liability at termination; §1301 measures benefit liabilities with assumptions prescribed for §1344 purposes, less current plan assets. That is actual delegated authority, not deference from agency identity. Haggar requires enforcement of clear limits and then respect for a reasonable choice within genuine delegated ambiguity. The introductory ERISA-purpose language does not erase the measure of the ERISA-created debt. Nor does §1123(a)(4)'s equal treatment within a class require every legally different claim to originate under identical assumptions. Section 1144(d) preserves other federal law; an actual incompatibility must be identified. The estate's petition-date and distribution concerns are substantial, but §502 must be reconciled with the liability measure rather than used to discard it categorically. Conversely, the statutory reference does not establish PBGC's proposed figure without the allowance inquiry. Rash follows the valuation provision governing its transaction, and LaSalle distinguishes legal rights from numerical value; neither supplies a universal prudent-investor rate or market auction here. The resulting $124,441,000 valuation is vacated. Remand permits ordinary consideration of properly raised statutory-fit, reasonableness and application challenges without inventing one, ordering new rules or requiring a hearing. Different statutory dates do not themselves authorize duplicate discounting.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators retains its operation-based treatment of the separate §4971 assessment and its subordination limits; unpaid contributions with this conditional Title 11 instruction are a different debt.
- United States v. Noland preserves statutory priority and actual exceptions rather than judicial reprioritization.
- Reading Co. v. Brown preserves administrative treatment of qualifying postpetition liability without a measurable estate benefit; the additional debt here remains prepetition in origin.
- Hughes Aircraft and UNUM distinguish obligation, function and remedy; UNUM's state-insurance saving clause is not transplanted into this federal scheme.
- Haggar supplies actual-authority and statutory-limit review, distinct from particular validity and application.
- Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash follows its particular retained-collateral valuation rule; it supplies no universal pension discount method.
- LaSalle separates the legal allocation of rights from their numerical valuation and imposes no general market test on this claim.

Additional administrative priority remains denied for prepetition-service debt while the postpetition-service component survives. ## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the denial of additional administrative priority but would affirm the tax and valuation rulings as well. His tax analysis examines what the payment does rather than PBGC's public status; a private plan-funding obligation does not become general fiscal revenue merely because a public insurer asserts it. ## Event

Saenz v. Roe, No. 98-97. Argued January 13, 1999; decided May 17, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed a single judge's preliminary injunction in Roe v. Anderson, 134 F.3d 1400. The question is whether California may limit otherwise eligible bona fide residents during their first year to the benefits available in their former State, and whether federal statutory permission sustains that classification.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment affirming the preliminary injunction is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Thomas.

The preliminary injunction remains in effect against the first-year differential. No permanent decree, damages award or fixed benefit amount is entered.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Kennedy delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Justice Thomas dissents. All eight majority Justices directly join both holdings and the bounded remedy.

## Holdings

### Bona fide new citizens receive equal ordinary welfare treatment

**Holding and operative rule:** A United States citizen who establishes bona fide residence in a State becomes its citizen on equal terms; the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship and Privileges or Immunities provisions forbid lowering that otherwise eligible citizen's ordinary welfare benefits solely because of recent arrival or the former State's benefit amount. California's first-year differential violates that guarantee even if the person never received benefits before moving or moved for reasons unrelated to assistance.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls both holdings, eight votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court compares current citizens within their present State. Slaughter-House specifically recognizes a citizen's ability to settle in another State and become its citizen; the Court applies that limited proposition without reopening the decision's whole account of the Clause. Shapiro rejects durational welfare penalties, Dunn distinguishes genuine residence from added duration, and Zobel rejects allocation of ordinary resident benefits by arrival history. The State cannot answer that inequality by observing that a newcomer receives no less than in the former State. Prior benefits do not define present citizenship, and eventual equality does not cure twelve months of inferior treatment. Saving money is a legitimate general objective, but selecting new citizens for the saving merely restates the challenged disadvantage. This is equality within a program California has chosen, not a constitutional entitlement to a program or a national benefit level. Bona fide residence verification and genuinely equal prospective eligibility rules remain available. The decision does not govern distinct services or contributions earned elsewhere or supply a general catalogue of privileges. The implementing instructions' exclusion of families newly arriving from another country does not cure the discrimination among current citizens who moved from another State.

### Federal permission cannot lower the constitutional floor

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may structure and condition federal welfare assistance, but may not authorize a State to violate the Fourteenth Amendment's equal-citizenship guarantee. Section 604(c)'s permission to use prior-State benefit levels therefore cannot sustain California's unconstitutional differential; permission is not a federal command.

**Controlling explanation:** Boerne distinguishes enforcement of a constitutional guarantee from replacement of its content. The federal provision does not change the fact that California administers unequal treatment among bona fide current citizens. Statutory permission cannot remove the protection that makes the classification invalid. The Court does not invalidate every federal welfare provision, require a nationwide program design or decide a constitutionally distinct nationwide rule. Thomas accepts the general inability to authorize a proved violation but disputes the underlying guarantee's application; that disagreement does not make the permission defense sufficient once this classification is held unconstitutional.

## Precedent Treatment

- The Slaughter-House Cases supply the specific recognition of becoming another State's citizen through bona fide residence; their broader construction is not reopened.
- Shapiro v. Thompson supplies the prohibition on durational welfare penalties; a reduced payment still burdens current equality.
- Dunn v. Blumstein distinguishes actual residence from additional duration.
- Zobel v. Williams rejects ordinary resident-benefit allocation by arrival history.
- City of Boerne v. Flores prevents statutory replacement of constitutional content.
- Lunding and Romer retain their source-specific equality rules; neither is converted into a general judicial guarantee of welfare amounts.

## Law After Decision

California must treat otherwise eligible bona fide current citizens equally in ordinary welfare benefits without the first-year prior-State ceiling. Legitimate residence verification and equal eligibility conditions remain enforceable. The ruling rests on narrow equal state citizenship, creates no national welfare amount and cannot be displaced by federal permission. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

Justice Thomas would reverse. His provision-specific approach, reflected in Lunding and Boerne, requires an established constitutional basis before ordinary benefit distinctions receive heightened protection. He does not regard the historical foundation offered for Privileges or Immunities as sufficient to constitutionalize this welfare-equality rule. He distinguishes Shapiro's complete denial of essential assistance from a temporary continuation of prior-State benefits and would sustain the fiscal classification under ordinary review. He acknowledges that Congress cannot authorize an actual Fourteenth Amendment violation, but finds none here. He does not direct any unrelated residence or eligibility finding.

## Procedure After Action

The preliminary injunction continues, and ordinary proceedings resume consistent with the resolved constitutional rule. California may verify bona fide residence and apply lawful equal eligibility rules, but may not use recent arrival or former-State rates to impose this first-year reduction. No permanent injunction, damages, national redesign or particular payment amount is ordered by this judgment.

The state rule's prior-benefit and motive independence is preserved; no new individual eligibility finding is made.
