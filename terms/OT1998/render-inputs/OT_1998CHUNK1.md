## Event

United States v. Wright, Eleventh Circuit No. 95-8397; judgment below, 117 F.3d 1265 (July 24, 1997), with the sentencing portion replaced on limited rehearing January 28, 1998. October Term 1998. Merits decision, October 5, 1998, on certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit after merits briefing. No oral-argument or precise submission date is specified.

The judgment under review affirmed the Northern District of Georgia's two convictions and, after rehearing, its 41-month sentence. The questions are whether the personal arms guarantee permits the machinegun-possession and destructive-device-registration convictions; whether section 922(o) is within the commerce power, including the derivative Tenth Amendment objection; whether the Ninth Amendment supplies an independent defense; and whether denial of acceptance-of-responsibility credit rested on lawful grounds. The derivative request for an arms instruction is addressed within the preserved plea's procedural limits.

Wright pleaded guilty while reserving constitutional challenges. The admitted machinegun count includes the completed .223-caliber Olympic Arms automatic weapon and the disassembled .50-caliber machinegun; the separate registration count concerns three pipe bombs. No companion case is added.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at merits submission and decision. Nine participate; five constitute the judgment majority. No case-specific nonparticipation is established.

## Holdings

**Common authority.** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Parts I-V, is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those eight Justices directly adopt every holding and controlling explanation below. Stone-Zsela does not join the Court's opinion. His agreement with several judgment components does not enlarge their rationale coalition. No fractured-decision inference is needed.

### I. Congress may reach this machinegun possession through the national transfer-and-stock regime

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(o) may reach the admitted machinegun stock without proof of a defendant-specific interstate or commercial transaction because excluding possession would permit acquisition, retention, concealment or continued circulation that materially impairs Congress's valid national restriction on additions to civilian machinegun stock. The Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause supplies that authority; the derivative Tenth Amendment objection fails because it identifies no independent interference with state administration.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Hale*, which connects possession to enforcement of the valid transfer-and-stock restriction, and *Kirk*, which does not require a sale or individual interstate transaction for completed prohibited stock. Wright admitted the completed .223 automatic weapon and the disassembled .50-caliber machinegun within one possession count. No preserved issue asks whether the admitted objects fall outside the enacted statutory class. The claim that all possession here was local therefore does not sever the identified regulatory connection. *Lopez* still requires a constitutionally adequate interstate mechanism before aggregation; dangerousness, marketability, remote manufacture or movement, and generalized crime costs cannot replace it. *Rybar* applies the same mechanism without adding a commerce element. The Tenth Amendment objection is derivative of the enumerated-power challenge; no commandeering claim is presented. The Court does not hold that commerce authority defeats every independent Tenth Amendment objection.

**Operative qualifications:** Section 922(o)(2)(A) excepts transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. Section 922(o)(2)(B) separately excepts lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986; earlier manufacture alone is insufficient. No exception is newly established here. No interstate element, general firearms police power, universal formal-findings requirement, or constitutional conclusion about every receiver, conversion part or incomplete assembly is adopted. Commerce authority does not resolve the arms challenge.

### II-A. Personal invocation does not depend on state-militia affiliation

**Holding and operative rule:** Wright may invoke the personal arms guarantee without proving membership in, service to, or preparation for a militia actively maintained and trained by a State. Military usefulness neither excludes an arm from the guarantee nor gives an unrestricted exemption from regulation; the actual weapon, conduct and restriction must be considered.

**Controlling explanation:** *Love* identifies the individual holder of the right, and *Hale* applies that rule to federal weapons prosecutions. *Miller* retains its inquiry into the actual weapon and record without an enrollment requirement or immunity for every military-capable weapon. The court below instead made a relationship to a state-maintained militia a prerequisite to constitutional protection. Wright's private group's purpose does not supply that prerequisite, but the absence of that relationship cannot defeat his personal claim. Nor does the asserted use of these weapons by military forces settle the lawfulness of their regulation. The Court adopts no comprehensive scrutiny tier, closed weapons catalogue or categorical exclusion of dangerous arms.

### II-B. The machinegun-count affirmance requires a preserved arms application

**Holding and operative rule:** When the appellate affirmance of this machinegun conviction materially rests on the invalid affiliation requirement and supplies no adequate independent application of the personal arms guarantee to the actual weapons and restriction, that affirmance must be vacated for the preserved constitutional application. On this record, the lower courts must assess the completed weapon and the disassembled weapon under their actual statutory bases where material, keeping constitutional coverage distinct from the justification for the restriction.

**Controlling explanation:** *Hale* and *Rybar* require the actual arms application that the affiliation rule displaced. The original opinion's footnote 18 expressly left the government's justification unresolved. The known automatic-fire function of the completed .223 weapon and the restriction on additions to civilian stock supply substantial arguments for sustaining this application; the Court need not demand new empirical proof merely to recognize those arguments. But *Rybar* also involved identified completed automatic weapons and the same stock restriction, and its required application was not supplied by classification alone. Wright presents no adequate independent lower-court application that distinguishes that remedial premise. His intended burial and private group's preparation establish no adjudicated criminal purpose beyond these possession offenses. The separate pipe-bomb registration count cannot substitute for the missing analysis of the machinegun possession prohibition. Immediate acquittal would be equally unwarranted: personal coverage and military usefulness do not establish an unrestricted right to these weapons.

**Remedy and limits:** Only the appellate affirmance of the one section 922(o) count is vacated. The existing record may suffice; ordinary additional proceedings are permitted only as needed. The Court creates no new statutory element, second machinegun count or severance of the conditional plea, and does not reopen admitted possession. It orders no empirical study, hearing, new trial, acquittal, release or return of arms. The alternative jury-instruction request returns only insofar as it remains procedurally material under the preserved plea after lawful disposition of the arms claim; no particular instruction or trial is ordered. This is not an automatic-remand rule for every arms case.

### III. The separate pipe-bomb registration conviction remains valid

**Holding and operative rule:** The personal character of the arms guarantee and the asserted military usefulness of the three pipe bombs do not exempt their possession from the separate NFA registration requirements administering lawful making and transfer. Section 5861(d)'s prohibition of receipt or possession of a covered firearm not registered to the possessor has an independent tax-administration basis where it supports assessment, collection and tracing of taxable making and transfer; these presented objections therefore do not disturb the registration conviction.

**Controlling explanation:** *Hale* supplies both the independent taxing-power ground and the distinction between personal arms protection and registration. The lower court's affiliation rationale is erroneous, but the valid registration ground does not depend on it. The count concerns three pipe bombs, not machinegun registration or an asserted inability to register post-1986 machineguns. The supported record establishes no refusal of a registration application or inability lawfully to approve these destructive devices. Thus vacating this conviction merely because the lower court used an invalid general arms premise would disregard the independently sufficient rule already governing the precise registration objection. The Court need not hold every explosive device outside constitutional protection to sustain this duty.

**Operative qualifications:** Section 5841 registers a transferred firearm to the transferee through the transferor; it does not impose a universal duty on each possessor to seek late registration. Section 5812 requires advance transfer approval and denial if transfer, receipt or possession would violate law. Section 5822 separately requires advance making approval and denial if making or possession would violate law. A prosecution involving registration that could not lawfully be approved remains a different question. No unpreserved statutory element, tax exemption, knowledge issue or registration-impossibility defense is decided.

### IV. The Ninth Amendment supplies no established independent exemption here

**Holding and operative rule:** Invoking privacy and natural self-defense under the Ninth Amendment does not itself establish an independent constitutional immunity for this machinegun possession and destructive-device nonregistration. The asserted exemption fails without resolving the separately preserved Second Amendment application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Ninth Amendment preserves the distinction between enumerating rights and denying other rights retained by the people. That reservation does not, merely upon assertion, establish the scope or existence of the particular weapons exemption Wright seeks. *Love* and *Hale* recognize personal protection while distinguishing it from unrestricted possession and from exemption from registration. Wright receives an adjudication of his actual arms claim; its unresolved application is not assumed away. The Court does not hold that enumeration exhausts constitutional liberty, that unenumerated rights do not exist, that the Ninth Amendment has no significance, or that every privacy claim fails. No new substantive-due-process claim is presented or decided.

### V-A. A reserved legal challenge alone does not defeat acceptance credit

**Holding and operative rule:** A sentencing court may not deny an otherwise warranted section 3E1.1 acceptance-of-responsibility adjustment solely because the defendant presents a nonfrivolous legal or constitutional challenge unrelated to admitted factual guilt. A guilty plea reserving such a challenge does not automatically disqualify the defendant, but neither the plea nor the prosecutor's recommendation guarantees the adjustment.

**Controlling explanation:** *Stinson* makes interpretive commentary authoritative unless it violates the Constitution or a federal statute, conflicts with the Guideline, or plainly misreads it; commentary cannot amend contrary text. Application note 2 permits acceptance in the unusual case tried to preserve a legal issue unrelated to factual guilt. A plea reserving the same kind of legal question cannot itself create a categorical bar. Notes 1 and 3 require examination of actual acceptance, not surrender of legal review. The rehearing court's alternative permission to deny credit exclusively for Wright's constitutional challenges conflicts with that distinction. That denying a reduction is not always unconstitutional punishment does not answer the Guidelines question. False factual denials, sham factual assertions or conduct inconsistent with acceptance can remain relevant, including when associated with litigation. The unsuccessful legal position alone is not such conduct. The Court decides the meaning and application of section 3E1.1, not a categorical constitutional immunity from every litigation-related sentencing consequence.

### V-B. The asserted remorse ground does not independently preserve this sentence

**Holding and operative rule:** Where the reported sentencing remarks intertwine acceptance with an erroneous affiliation premise and do not establish a sufficiently independent lawful remorse determination, the sentence cannot be preserved by characterizing those same remarks as a conclusive factual alternative. Wright's sentence and its appellate affirmance must be vacated for lawful reconsideration under section 3E1.1, coordinated with the machinegun count's disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** *Koon* distinguishes correction of legal error from deference to genuine factual and sentencing judgments and forbids appellate replacement reasons or speculation that the same sentence might recur. The sentencing judge addressed whether Wright believed he belonged to a group carrying out state law, and the reported earlier remarks focused on counsel's constitutional assertion. The rehearing majority's reading of that discussion as lack of remorse is an appellate characterization, not another trial-court finding. The judge's opportunity to observe Wright matters, but cannot make the displaced militia rule a lawful acceptance criterion. Admissions, cooperation, a timely plea and the assurance of no weapons possession after arrest are evidence of acceptance; they do not establish entitlement. The Court cannot conclude on these passages that an independent, legally valid finding controlled the denial. Resentencing is therefore required independently of the still-open arms application.

**Remedy and limits:** The sentencing court may assess actual remorse, candor, sincerity and conduct on supported lawful grounds, with appropriate deference to genuine factual findings. The Court orders no two- or three-level reduction, prescribed term of imprisonment or conclusive finding of acceptance. It does not resolve credibility anew, and supported contrary conduct may outweigh evidence favoring credit.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Love v. Pepersack (December 11, 1995):** applied for the personal holder of the arms guarantee without formal militia affiliation; its incorporation and necessary-acquisition rules remain intact, and incorporation is unnecessary in this federal prosecution.
- **United States v. Hale (February 12, 1996):** applied for the fitted possession/transfer mechanism, independent tax-administration and registration grounds, and separation of personal coverage from the actual weapons restriction. Its statutory exceptions and unresolved registration-impossibility question remain intact.
- **United States v. Rybar (November 24, 1997):** applied for the same commerce mechanism and the limited arms remand when an invalid affiliation ground lacks an adequate independent application. Its completed weapons do not establish either automatic affirmance or an automatic-remand rule.
- **United States v. Kirk (February 14, 1998):** applied for possession of prohibited completed stock without a defendant-specific sale or interstate transaction. It supplies no Second Amendment holding.
- **United States v. Lopez (April 26, 1995):** preserved and applied for a constitutionally identifiable commerce connection before aggregation and against judicial addition of an absent interstate element. The decision does not revive generalized downstream crime-cost reasoning.
- **United States v. Miller, 307 U.S. 174 (1939):** applied through the surviving weapon-and-record inquiry, without a formal-enrollment prerequisite, categorical military-weapon immunity or exclusion based on military usefulness alone.
- **Stinson v. United States, 508 U.S. 36 (May 3, 1993):** applied to the consistent section 3E1.1 commentary; its constitutional, statutory and textual limits remain unchanged.
- **Koon v. United States; Powell v. United States, 518 U.S. 81 (June 13, 1996):** applied for legal-error correction, genuine factual deference and the prohibition on preserving the actual sentence through invented replacement grounds. Its separate departure classifications are not converted into a new acceptance-of-responsibility test.

No earlier Supreme Court decision is overruled. The lower court's affiliation requirement and legal-challenge-only sentencing ground cannot sustain the judgment.

## Law After Decision

Effective October 5, 1998, the commerce and registration rules are applied without expansion, and the personal arms guarantee remains independent of militia affiliation. The validity of this machinegun restriction under that guarantee remains for the required application; the Court adopts neither a comprehensive scrutiny tier nor a categorical automatic-weapons exception. The registration conviction remains affirmed on grounds independent of that unresolved application.

The decision supplies the controlling section 3E1.1 application that a nonfrivolous legal challenge unrelated to admitted factual guilt alone cannot disqualify an otherwise deserving defendant. Genuine acceptance remains an individualized determination, and a legally compromised sentence cannot be sustained by an appellate characterization that does not establish an independent lawful ground. The narrow Ninth Amendment ruling rejects this asserted independent exemption without establishing a general theory of unenumerated rights. Stone-Zsela's proposed burden-and-fit framework and affirmative machinegun application are not the Court's law.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part, unjoined.** He would affirm both convictions and vacate the sentence. He proposes an arms inquiry requiring the strongest justification for a prohibition that destroys ordinary lawful armed self-defense and a substantial, demonstrable fit for other serious burdens. The inquiry asks what protected conduct is foreclosed, how severely, and whether the government's actual justification fits that burden. Public safety is weighty but cannot substitute for that examination. Dangerous uses, demonstrably dangerous persons and unusually hazardous weapon functions call for attention to the reasons for the restriction's scope. History informs protection and permissible regulation without becoming a closed catalogue. *Love* establishes the personal holder and incorporation; *Hale* and *Rybar* reject affiliation-based exclusion and military-utility immunity. Those decisions did not already adopt his proposed tier of review.

He would sustain the one machinegun count on the admitted completed .223 automatic weapon: repeated fire from one trigger function presents rapid, difficult-to-control discharge beyond ordinary semiautomatic operation. Restricting additions to civilian automatic-weapon stock directly addresses that function while leaving ordinary defensive arms available. The post-1986 scope and existing-stock exception inform the burden; military labels do not decide it. The completed weapon independently supports his count-specific affirmance, without a ruling on all disassembled receivers or conversion parts. He reads *Rybar* to require the previously missing arms analysis, which the Court may perform on an adequate record, rather than a return in every case. His affirmative disposition depends on the plea or charge independently establishing that completed-weapon basis. He adopts no universal numerical magazine limit, militia exclusion, categorical military-weapon immunity or comprehensive prohibited-arms list.

For the separate pipe-bomb count, he concludes that registering explosive destructive devices does not extinguish ordinary defensive keeping; *Hale* supports keeping registration separate. He rejects the commerce objection because removing possession would permit acquisition, retention and recirculation of prohibited stock under *Hale*, *Rybar* and *Kirk*, while preserving *Lopez*'s limit on generalized crime costs and declining a rule based merely on out-of-state manufacture. He rejects an unlimited Ninth Amendment exemption from these fitted restrictions.

On sentencing, he agrees that the quoted remarks mix sincerity with the erroneous affiliation premise and do not establish that an independent lawful finding controlled. The section 3E1.1 commentary forecloses denial solely for the reserved legal challenge. Resentencing must evaluate actual remorse, candor and conduct without treating that legal defense as factual nonacceptance. He would order no automatic adjustment and would not resolve credibility on appeal. His proposed disposition entails no acquittal, return of weapons or new trial. He does not join the Court's opinion.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Every holding below has nine direct joins; no judgment-only aggregation or fractured-decision rule is used.

### Standing for this official-use controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** An association may seek prospective, uniform relief against this official-use exclusion when its chief law-enforcement members suffer a concrete inability lawfully to arm affected officers, the claim is germane to the association, and the requested relief requires no individualized member participation. The chiefs' direct exposure under the supply prohibition and their relationship to the affected officers also permit assertion of the officers' equality interest in this controversy; no injury supporting every individual private-possession or restoration claim is established.

**Controlling explanation:** *Hunt* supplies the member-injury, germaneness and participation requirements. The chiefs allege an actual operational constraint and exposure when they supply firearms or ammunition knowing or having reasonable cause to believe that a recipient has a qualifying conviction. The desired prospective relief is uniform, without individual damages calculations. *United Food* preserves the constitutional requirement of real member injury and distinguishes the prudential participation condition; its WARN-specific relaxation is not treated as a general waiver here. *Craig* and *Barrows* permit attention to the directly regulated supplier relationship: compliance by the chiefs with the challenged restriction is the act that withholds the asserted benefit from officers. The Court does not create a general third-party standing exception or assume injuries unrelated to this official-use claim.

### Equal protection does not require extending the older exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may withhold section 925(a)(1)'s governmental-use exception from the domestic-violence-misdemeanor supply and possession provisions while retaining that exception for the felony provision when the distinction rationally closes a practical gap in restrictions on access to weapons. The Fifth Amendment does not require Congress simultaneously to perfect every older exemption, and the challenged exclusion satisfies that rule on the developed official-use claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Domestic-violence misdemeanants are not a suspect class. *Beach* and *Lee Optical* permit incremental regulation on a conceivable legitimate ground, while *Heller v. Doe* and *Romer* require a relation between the actual disadvantage and a legitimate end. The covered offenses concern force, attempted force or threatened deadly-weapon use within the statutory domestic relationships, with the conviction safeguards and relief qualifications stated below. Congress could address violent conduct treated as a misdemeanor even though the older felony regime and overlapping state or professional rules already remove many felons from armed work. Preventing official issuance from defeating the newly enacted disability fits that gap. This is an available analytical justification, not an assertion about an argument actually made in the government's brief or an invented congressional finding.

The contrary comparison is substantial: a less serious offense may produce a heavier official-use disability, and state restrictions and relief differ. But Congress need not conclude that misdemeanants are more dangerous than felons to close an inadequately covered route of access. The solution's incompleteness does not make that connection irrational. The Court assumes neither that every State excludes every felon nor that a public-safety label alone answers equality review. The statutory avenues for conviction relief also foreclose describing this disability as literally without relief. No claim of a universal constitutional restoration procedure is decided.

### Personal arms protection does not compel this governmental-use exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Recognition of an individual's arms right does not itself require a governmental-use exemption for persons with a qualifying domestic-violence conviction. On this developed official-use claim, the adjudicated connection to violent misuse and the statute's conviction safeguards supply a concrete justification for retaining the exclusion; public employment does not eliminate that connection or make a service weapon inaccessible to its possessor.

**Controlling explanation:** *Love*, *Hale*, *Rybar* and *Wright* require personal invocation and an actual examination of the restriction, rather than a collective-militia premise. Official work alone cannot extinguish a personal right, just as the commercial setting in *Rybar* did not. Here, however, the claim seeks an exemption allowing government issuance to a category defined by qualifying violent conduct. Preventing armed recurrence bears directly on that predicate. The force or deadly-weapon element, domestic relationship and counsel, jury-where-entitled and conviction-relief provisions constrain the category. This ground concerns actual conduct and statutory safeguards, not unpopular identity or an unsupported declaration that all misdemeanants are dangerous. A governmental label does not neutralize the firearm's availability to the person whose conviction supplies the concern.

The Court sustains this exclusion without deciding every application to private keeping, every misdemeanor disability, an improperly classified conviction or a missing statutory safeguard. It adopts neither a universal scrutiny tier nor a rule that absence of an entitlement to public employment alone defeats an arms claim. *Wright* required an application left expressly undone in a different machinegun case; it did not establish automatic remand whenever a personal right is invoked.

### Remedy and questions not reached

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the developed constitutional objections do not invalidate this exclusion, section 928 supplies no basis to extend the governmental-use exception. Reversal returns the case for a consistent judgment and any grounds that are both properly preserved and still undecided; it does not decide their preservation, revive an undeveloped theory, or automatically reinstate every District Court rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 928 preserves valid provisions and applications after invalidity; it does not command extension of a statutory exception for administrative convenience. No invalidity requiring such a choice is found here. The Court therefore does not select between extension and removal as remedies for a different constitutional defect. The section 922(o) commerce decisions do not by themselves decide the different section 922(g)(9) regime, and direct regulation of possession or supply is distinct from compelled state administration. Any properly preserved unresolved commerce or commandeering ground must be identified and considered on its own; the undeveloped labels in the original complaint receive no merits judgment or automatic revival. Whether civil rights never lost count as restored remains unresolved. No individual defective-conviction claim or universal constitutional restoration procedure is decided.

### Statutory scope retained throughout

The holding concerns firearms and ammunition in the governmental channels defined by section 925(a)(1): transportation, shipment, receipt, possession or importation of items imported for, sold or shipped to, or issued for the use of the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. That provision separately withholds its exception for sections 922(d)(9), 922(g)(9), and provisions concerning firearms prohibited by section 922(p). It is not an exemption for every privately held weapon of a public employee and is distinct from the machinegun exceptions in section 922(o)(2).

Section 922(d)(9)'s supply prohibition requires knowledge or reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction. Section 922(g)(9)'s separate language addresses interstate or foreign shipment or transportation, possession in or affecting commerce, and receipt of an item previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Neither provision receives the other's elements by implication. Section 922(d)'s existing exception for specified licensees permitted to continue under section 925(b), or persons granted section 925(c) relief, remains. Section 925(b) concerns an importer, manufacturer, dealer or collector indicted for a crime punishable by more than one year, continuing under an existing license, with a timely renewal application when required, during indictment and until any conviction becomes final. Section 925(c)'s discretionary public-safety and public-interest relief and review of an actual denial establish no funded administrative process, actual denial, judicial jurisdiction or entitlement for an individual in this case.

The qualifying misdemeanor must be under federal or state law and have an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian; a person sharing a child with the victim; a person cohabiting or formerly cohabiting as spouse, parent or guardian; or a person similarly situated to a spouse, parent or guardian. Counsel or a knowing and intelligent waiver is required. If the person was entitled to a jury under the jurisdiction's law, there must have been a jury trial or a knowing and intelligent waiver by plea or otherwise; no new jury entitlement is created.

A conviction does not count when expunged or set aside, or when the person was pardoned or had civil rights restored if the jurisdiction provides for loss of civil rights for that offense, subject to the statute's express-firearms-reservation clause. That clause names a pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibiting shipment, transportation, possession or receipt of firearms; it does not separately repeat set-aside. The Court does not enlarge that wording or resolve its application to an individual. These are statutory limits on the rule, not newly imposed evidentiary procedures.

- *Craig v. Boren* and *Barrows v. Jackson* support the directly regulated relationship for the officers' equality claim; no universal third-party standing rule follows.
- *FCC v. Beach Communications* and *Williamson v. Lee Optical* permit fitted incremental regulation; they do not make a generic public-safety assertion conclusive.
- *Heller v. Doe* retains both its actual proof-burden and family-participation holdings; its requirement of a rational relation to the challenged disadvantage is applied here.
- *Romer v. Evans* retains its protection against the unrelated blanket exclusion it addressed; this conduct-defined access restriction is distinguished by its actual regulatory connection.
- *Love*, *Hale*, *Rybar* and *Wright* are applied to personal invocation and separate examination of a restriction. None supplies a universal scrutiny tier, categorical misdemeanor rule or automatic arms remand. Their machinegun commerce and tax-registration grounds do not decide this separate statute's constitutional power question.

## Holdings

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Parts I-X, is joined in full by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those eight Justices adopt every holding below. Stone-Zsela agrees in every component judgment without joining the opinion and states his grounds separately. No fractured-decision inference supplies controlling law.

### I. The continuing employment injury supports standing and present review

**Holding and operative rule:** Hiley's continuing exclusion from armed-deputy work, traceable to application of the federal disability and redressable through the settlement's restoration-if-successful condition, establishes a concrete controversy despite his reassignment to detention work. NAGE may pursue this uniform prospective legal challenge through affected members where the claim is germane to its representative purpose and neither the claim nor the requested relief requires individual participation; the already operative injury makes the controversy ripe.

**Controlling explanation:** The settlement did not end the disadvantage Hiley challenges; it expressly connects success in the federal action with restoration of his deputy assignment. The sheriff's intervening employment choice therefore does not make causation or redress speculative. *Hunt* supplies the separate member-injury, germaneness and participation requirements. *United Food* preserves those constitutional injury requirements and does not dispense with the participation condition outside its statutory exception. Here the uniform legal question and prospective relief require no individual damages determination. The present employment effect is sufficiently concrete for decision; possible future pardon or collateral relief does not eliminate it. No threatened prosecution is alleged or needed for this employment-based standing conclusion.

### II. Correcting the arms premise does not revive abandoned claims

**Holding and operative rule:** The recorded abandonment of strict-scrutiny review and the separately recorded abandonment of procedural due process limit this review; correcting the lower court's collective-only arms premise neither withdraws those abandonments nor creates a separately preserved Second Amendment claim. The surviving federal challenges remain for decision, while claims against the county and sheriff settled and dismissed with prejudice remain closed.

**Controlling explanation:** *Love*, *Hale*, *Rybar* and *Wright* recognize personal invocation of the arms guarantee independently of militia affiliation. The incorporated district order's contrary general premise cannot be adopted. But its footnotes 11 and 16 separately record the actual abandonments. A changed governing premise is not proof that a party preserved a different claim. The Court therefore analyzes the surviving equality and employment theories under their actual scope, without treating personal arms as categorically unprotected. *Fraternal Order of Police* sustains its developed official-use exclusion; it does not create a private-possession claim in this different pleading or reopen the local settlement. No universal rule for every misdemeanor or private-arms application is decided.

### III. The possession restriction supports the distinct prohibited-recipient supply regime

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented facial commerce challenge to section 922(g)(9) fails because possession by the qualifying recipient class is a fitted anti-evasion and anti-impairment component of the valid federal restriction on supplying firearms and ammunition to that class. The Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses permit that inclusion, while section 922(g)'s enacted interstate conditions remain separate requirements rather than an automatic constitutional cure.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 922(d)(9) restricts sale or other disposition to a recipient known, or reasonably believed, to have the qualifying conviction. Allowing that recipient class to acquire and retain covered firearms free of the possession restriction would permit evasion and materially impair that supply rule. *Lopez* requires identification of this mechanism before aggregate consequences can do constitutional work. *Hale* developed necessary inclusion for a different machinegun-stock regime; *Kirk* applied that regime without requiring a defendant-specific transaction. Their method supports this new application, but their results do not dictate it merely because both statutes concern guns. The specific supply-and-recipient relationship supplies the connection here. Congress's authority over the separately enacted interstate shipment, transportation and receipt branches also remains; this facial demand does not remove those direct interstate applications. No individual firearm's interstate facts are found in this civil action, and any prosecution must establish the actual statutory conditions. Remote historical movement, dangerousness or general crime costs alone supplies no universal firearms power.

### IV. Ordinary equality review permits the domestic-violence category

**Holding and operative rule:** On the preserved ordinary-review challenge, Congress may restrict firearm access following a qualifying domestic-violence misdemeanor without extending the restriction to every other violent misdemeanor at the same time. The specified violent-conduct and domestic-relationship predicates rationally connect this classification to preventing further violence.

**Controlling explanation:** *Lee Optical* and *Beach* permit incremental regulation, while *Heller v. Doe* and *Romer* require a rational relation to the actual disadvantage imposed. The category requires force, attempted force or a threatened deadly weapon within the statutory domestic relationships, with conviction safeguards and relief qualifications. That is a specific connection to the risk addressed, rather than mere dislike of a group. The argument that Congress should also address other violent misdemeanants proposes a broader reform; it does not negate the relation supporting this one. The Court does not resolve the abandoned heightened-review request, declare every misdemeanor disqualifying, or dispense with meaningful review whenever public safety is invoked.

### V. Different restoration systems do not invalidate this federal selector

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may recognize the convicting jurisdiction's expungement, set-aside, pardon and qualified civil-rights-restoration arrangements without guaranteeing identical firearm consequences across States or between offenses that do and do not cause loss of civil rights. The presented facial equality challenge based on those differences fails; the Court need not decide whether rights never lost can count as restored in a particular statutory application.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute identifies actual conviction relief under local law and separately conditions the restoration route on that jurisdiction providing loss of civil rights for the offense. The strongest objection is that a less serious offense can therefore produce a more persistent firearm disability than some felonies. But recognizing actual local relief is a rational selector, and expungement, set-aside and pardon remain distinct textual avenues even when restoration is unavailable. Their existence does not establish that Hiley can practically obtain any particular relief. *Caron* distinguishes the jurisdiction supplying restoration status from federal law determining its consequences; it does not decide this never-lost-rights question. Ordinary review does not require uniform state practices or compel a new federal restoration process. The statutory firearms-reservation qualification remains operative, and no individual restoration application is adjudicated.

### VI. The distinct occupational-effects theory lacks a discriminatory-purpose allegation

**Holding and operative rule:** The greater employment effect of a qualifying-conviction firearm restriction on occupations requiring weapons does not itself establish the asserted occupational discrimination where the rule applies beyond those occupations and the complaint alleges no purpose to disadvantage law-enforcement officers. That uneven-effects theory fails without changing the separate analysis of an express statutory classification.

**Controlling explanation:** *Feeney* distinguishes unequal effects of an otherwise rational rule from discrimination attributable to a purpose directed at the disadvantaged group. Hiley's lost armed assignment is substantial, but the conviction-based rule applies to persons outside law enforcement as well. The supported complaint supplies no allegation that Congress chose it to disadvantage officers. This theory is distinct from the express official-use exclusion considered in *Fraternal Order of Police*, which survives for its own fitted-gap and conduct-based reasons. The Court does not substitute an intent requirement for rational review of every express classification or erase the practical employment burden.

### VII. Public employment alone supplies no fundamental substantive entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** A state-created employment interest and a wish to retain an armed public position do not alone establish a fundamental substantive federal right to that position. The qualifying-conduct firearm restriction rationally serves prevention of armed recurrence, so the preserved legislative employment claim fails.

**Controlling explanation:** *Love* distinguishes a state-created interest from the substantive federal guarantee claimed. The serious effect on Hiley's employment does not make the federal rule arbitrary: the qualifying violent conduct and prospective access restriction supply the actual safety connection. Armed employment does not remove that connection. This conclusion does not declare personal arms outside constitutional protection, dispose of the abandoned procedural-due-process claim, invalidate ordinary employment procedures, or adjudicate a breach of the settlement. The settled local claims are not before the Court for renewed relief.

### VIII. The prohibition governs later firearm conduct, not punishment for the earlier battery

**Holding and operative rule:** Applying section 922(g)(9) to firearm conduct after its September 30, 1996 effective date does not violate the Ex Post Facto Clause merely because the qualifying conviction occurred earlier. The conviction identifies the regulated class; the statute neither criminalizes completed preenactment firearm possession nor increases the sentence for Hiley's 1995 battery.

**Controlling explanation:** *Calder* and *Weaver* supply the prohibition against retrospective criminal punishment. Here the relevant prohibited conduct occurs after enactment. *Landgraf* helps distinguish use of antecedent facts from attaching new consequences to completed regulated acts; its civil temporal framework does not replace the Ex Post Facto inquiry. The continuing employment burden does not turn the stated prospective conduct rule into an offense committed in 1995. The Court makes no ruling on notice or mental state in a later criminal prosecution, and the existence of an earlier conviction is not a general exemption from retrospective-punishment review. The separate attainder analysis below is not adopted as an interchangeable Ex Post Facto test.

### IX. This restriction is not legislative punishment without trial

**Holding and operative rule:** The general, prospective firearm disability tied to adjudicated qualifying domestic violence is not a bill of attainder under the historical, functional and purpose inquiries. Its fitted prevention of further violence and its serious but incidental employment consequences do not establish legislative occupational punishment or a congressional adjudication of guilt for a future firearm offense.

**Controlling explanation:** *Nixon* and *Selective Service System* require examination of the kind and severity of the burden, its relation to nonpunitive purposes, and evidence of punitive legislative purpose. The restriction regulates access after a judicial conviction; Congress did not adjudicate Hiley's guilt by name. Unlike the occupational proscription in *Brown*, it is a general firearm rule with effects on jobs requiring possession, not legislative exclusion from a profession based on the targeted association there. Prevention of armed recurrence supplies a concrete regulatory purpose. No congressional purpose to punish this claimant or his occupation is established. Any future firearm prosecution requires ordinary adjudication of that offense. The holding does not make every disability based on a past conviction automatically nonpunitive.

### X. The federal rule does not command the sheriff's administration

**Holding and operative rule:** The challenged possession-and-supply rule does not violate the Tenth Amendment on the asserted staffing theory because it does not command the sheriff to terminate Hiley, legislate, prosecute federal offenses, administer background checks or appropriate funds. Its consequences for lawful staffing choices do not themselves make it a federal direction to administer a regulatory program.

**Controlling explanation:** *New York* distinguishes direct regulation from compelled state legislation and program responsibility. *Printz* preserves its confined source-and-function rule and unresolved fiscal objections concerning affirmative officer duties. Hiley presents no such statutory command: the employer chose termination and then reassignment, while the federal law regulates the relevant firearm conduct. The Court therefore rejects this particular structural objection independently of the commerce holding. It does not adopt the lower court's broader proposition that every exercise of commerce authority necessarily defeats every Tenth Amendment claim, nor does it turn *Printz*'s fiscal reservation into immunity from this direct rule.

### Statutory qualifications governing these holdings

Section 922(g)(9) separately covers interstate or foreign shipment or transportation, possession in or affecting commerce, and receipt of firearms or ammunition previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Section 922(d)(9)'s distinct sale-or-disposition rule requires knowledge or reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction; that mental-state condition is not silently imported as the complete culpability rule of section 922(g)(9). The present civil action decides no prosecution's proof or notice defense.

Under section 921(a)(33), the qualifying offense must be a federal or state misdemeanor with an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian; a person sharing a child with the victim; a person cohabiting or formerly cohabiting as spouse, parent or guardian; or a person similarly situated to a spouse, parent or guardian. The person must have had counsel or knowingly and intelligently waived counsel. If entitled to a jury under the jurisdiction's law, the person must have received a jury trial or knowingly and intelligently waived it by plea or otherwise; this creates no additional jury entitlement. Hiley's predicate is treated as qualifying in this action, without supplying an unproved different conviction or deciding a challenge to one of these conditions.

An expunged or set-aside conviction, a pardon, or restoration of civil rights where the jurisdiction provides for loss of those rights for the offense can remove the conviction from the definition, subject to the express-firearms-reservation clause. That clause names a pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibiting shipment, transportation, possession or receipt of firearms; it does not separately repeat set-aside. The Court neither enlarges that wording nor decides its application to Hiley. No actually available pardon, administrative relief process or individual relief entitlement is found.

Section 925(a)(1)'s governmental-use exception does not extend to sections 922(d)(9) or 922(g)(9); it concerns its defined governmental channels, not every privately held gun of a public employee. Section 922(d)'s existing exception for specified licensees permitted to continue under section 925(b), or persons granted section 925(c) relief, remains. Section 925(b) concerns an importer, manufacturer, dealer or collector indicted for a crime punishable by more than one year, continuing under an existing license, with timely renewal when required, during indictment and until a conviction becomes final. Section 925(c)'s discretionary public-safety and public-interest relief and review of an actual denial establish no funded process, actual denial, judicial jurisdiction or entitlement here. The Court does not decide any of those individual applications.

## Holdings

**Authority and scope:** The Court's unsigned supplemental decree, paragraphs 1-5, supplies binding decretal relief between the United States and Texas in the continuing original action. ### Lands and resources on either side of the described line

**Operative rule:** As against the United States and subject to section 5 of the Submerged Lands Act, Texas is entitled to the lands, minerals and other natural resources underlying the Gulf of Mexico landward of the paragraph 3 line; as against Texas, the United States is entitled to those seaward of that line. Both allocations are bounded on the south by the international boundary with Mexico and on the east by the Texas-Louisiana boundary and its extension.

**Controlling explanation:** Paragraphs 1 and 2 implement the entitlement settled in the December 12, 1960 decree and the Texas determinations culminating in the May 5, 1969 supplemental decree. The agreed description identifies the federal-state line more particularly; agreement does not enlarge either sovereign's statutory entitlement. The geographic limits prevent this instrument from supplying a new boundary for a nonconsenting sovereign. The exceptions below remain part of Texas's landward allocation, without a new finding that any parcel or interest meets them.

**Complete section 5 exceptions:** Under 43 U.S.C. section 1313(a), section 1311 does not transfer tracts or parcels, together with their accretions, resources and improvements, whose title the United States lawfully and expressly acquired from a State or from a person whose title vested under state or federal law; lands the United States lawfully holds under state law; lands expressly retained by or ceded to the United States at state admission, apart from a general retention or cession of lands beneath the marginal sea; lands the United States acquired in a proprietary capacity by eminent domain, purchase, cession, gift or otherwise; lands it filled, built up or otherwise reclaimed for its own use; or federal rights in lands then presently and actually occupied by the United States under claim of right. Section 1313(b) separately excepts submerged lands, or interests in them, held by the United States for the benefit of an Indian tribe, band, group or individual. Section 1313(c) separately excepts structures and improvements constructed by the United States in exercising its navigational servitude. These temporal, title and purpose qualifications remain operative. The reservation supplies no new parcel finding, tribal-interest adjudication or authority based merely on future occupation; neighboring section 1314 regulatory powers are not substituted for these ownership exceptions.

### The complete coordinates fix and immobilize the boundary

**Operative rule:** Paragraph 3's complete coordinate line is the Texas-United States seaward boundary in the described area, using every supplied straight segment and center-defined arc. Paragraph 4 refers the plane coordinates, in feet, to the Texas South or South Central Coordinate System as indicated, all on the North American Datum of 1927; under 43 U.S.C. section 1301(b), the boundary remains immobilized at those coordinates and does not move with later coastline change.

**Controlling explanation:** The prior Texas rulings established the three-marine-league entitlement by reference to the historical coastline and its then-applicable current-coastline limit. ## Holdings

**Authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens joins every part except Part IX. Accordingly, eight Justices directly adopt Parts I-VIII and X-XI; seven directly adopt Part IX. Stone-Zsela's component judgment votes add no opinion join. Each holding below is majority law on that direct support; no fractured-decision inference is needed.

### I. The identified owners may seek present review

**Holding and operative rule:** Owners who identify existing possession affected by a criminal ordinance, a closed exemption window and an express enforcement threat may obtain pre-enforcement review without inviting prosecution. P.R.O. may seek the common prospective relief through affected members whose injury is concrete, whose interests are germane to its purpose, and whose claims and requested relief require no individualized participation.

**Controlling explanation:** *Lujan* requires actual injury, causation and redressability; *Abbott Laboratories* and *Babbitt* permit review of a concrete present restraint backed by threatened enforcement. These owners face local surrender or storage elsewhere versus prosecution, rather than merely unsupported plans to obtain unidentified weapons. Their requested declaration and injunction would address that restraint. *Hunt* and *United Food* preserve member injury, germaneness and the distinct participation condition; the latter's statutory exception does not abolish that condition here. A possible future Ohio narrowing decision does not eliminate this present controversy. Neither a completed prosecution nor an advisory judgment about a wholly hypothetical weapon is required or authorized.

### II. The enacted fault rule and the meaning of the offense are distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.31(A)'s silence does not plainly negate section 2301.21(B)'s enacted recklessness default: culpability is unnecessary only where the offense plainly indicates strict liability; otherwise recklessness suffices for the silent offense. In applying these definitions, the City must prove heedless indifference and perverse disregard of a known risk that the prohibited circumstance was likely to exist; that fault condition can address ignorance of an intelligibly defined characteristic but cannot supply an undefined legal boundary.

**Controlling explanation:** Deletion of the former express knowledge term, while the magazine offense retains one, establishes different wording but does not by itself erase the express default. The lower court's strict-liability conclusion was its construction, not an enacted command. The City's first invocation of the default on appeal and its inconsistent proposed knowledge gloss are distinct: the Court applies the enacted default, not a prosecutor-created amendment. *Fargo* and *Women's Medical* require separate examination of definition, fault and substantive validity. *Grayned* supplies notice and practical guidance; *Kolender* independently requires standards constraining enforcement. *Hoffman Estates* makes context, sanctions and protected activity relevant without automatically invalidating every criminal prohibition. *Staples* separates knowledge of characteristics from knowledge of illegality but does not impose its federal actual-knowledge construction on this ordinance. *Love* defeats the premise that personal keeping has no constitutional protection; it supplies neither First Amendment overbreadth nor the abortion-specific *Casey* facial test. The following rulings depend on their express constructions, not on an invented earlier authoritative Ohio interpretation. No universal knowledge-of-illegality or actual-knowledge minimum is adopted.

### III. An existing compatible rifle magazine supplies an intelligible criterion

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.11(G)(1) identifies a semiautomatic center-fire rifle or carbine accepting an existing detachable magazine with capacity of twenty rounds or more, whether or not its owner possesses that magazine. A merely imaginable future magazine does not establish that physical characteristic, and the City must separately establish the enacted fault condition; the presented categorical vagueness challenge fails on that construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The words describe the weapon's actual capacity to accept an existing magazine, not the owner's purchase package. Walker's affidavit identifies a compatible thirty-round magazine; his preference for five- or fifteen-round magazines does not erase that objective characteristic. Smolak's four-round rifle and lack of knowledge of a larger magazine establish neither a nonexistent larger-magazine fact nor the required fault. The principal objection combines future manufacture with strict liability for unknowable facts. Requiring actual compatibility and separate fault answers that objection without selecting a numerical capacity different from the enacted threshold. *Grayned* does not convert disputed physical proof into an absent legal rule. Neither owner is found liable, and no duty to predict future manufacture is imposed.

### IV. Shotgun capacity concerns actual compatible ammunition

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.11(G)(2) covers a semiautomatic shotgun whose magazine accepts more than six rounds of actual compatible ammunition, subject to the enacted fault condition. Different lengths of compatible shells do not make that physical-capacity criterion facially unintelligible; imaginary ammunition or an unproved alteration cannot establish it.

**Controlling explanation:** The owners urge a single measuring shell. The enacted criterion instead asks whether the magazine can hold more than six compatible rounds of an actual length. That inquiry is measurable despite different available lengths. Fault remains separate, so an owner's unknowing encounter with a shorter shell is not strict liability. *Fargo* and *Women's Medical* distinguish an intelligible criterion from proof of fault about its application. No new measuring standard, universal knowledge requirement or hypothetical weapon alteration is supplied. A disputed individual application remains subject to proof of every applicable element.

### V. Both modification branches lack the relationship that defines the offense

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.11(G)(3)(a)'s two branches—semiautomatic handguns that are modifications of the referenced rifle or of an automatic firearm—are invalid as written because they fail to identify whether actual conversion, design descent or resemblance makes the handgun prohibited. Recklessness does not cure that missing legal relationship, and dependent restoration or parts references cannot independently impose liability through those invalid branches.

**Controlling explanation:** A person may know a handgun's features and history yet lack a rule deciding which kind of modification the ordinance means. The expert's reported account presents this concrete uncertainty. The City's absence of a provision-specific defense does not create litigant default; the undefined criterion supplies the defect. *Kolender* requires minimum enforcement guidance independently of whether an officer can identify an apparently clear example. Physical conversion may be such an example, but listing it leaves the same undefined relationship governing design descendants. The ambiguity affects the central classification, protected ordinary keeping and criminal exposure, rather than merely an obscure edge. No authoritative Ohio construction selects one meaning, and a federal limitation to personally converted weapons would substitute a contested category. Branch-wide relief therefore follows from the actual classification defect, not *Casey*, First Amendment overbreadth or a universal rule invalidating statutes with uncertain margins. Ohio severability governs the remaining provisions.

The local automatic-firearm predicate includes a firearm designed or specifically adapted to fire successive cartridges through one trigger function and separately a semiautomatic firearm designed or specifically adapted to fire more than thirty-one cartridges without reloading. That second branch excludes a firearm chambering only .22-caliber short, long or long-rifle cartridges. It is not silently confined to federal machineguns. The printed reference to division (a)(1), which does not exist, is understood as the referenced rifle provision (G)(1), as in the judgment below; that cross-reference does not supply the missing modification relationship.

### VI. Original handgun design is distinct from undefined modification

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.11(G)(3)(b) asks whether a semiautomatic handgun was originally designed to accept a detachable magazine holding more than twenty rounds, subject to separate fault. That objective historical criterion is not facially invalid merely because design-history proof may be difficult, and it does not extend to every subsequently conceivable magazine.

**Controlling explanation:** Original design fixes both the relevant time and characteristic. *Posters 'N' Things* distinguishes objective article design from a person's relevant knowledge; its complete federal culpability rule is not imported. The lower court's derivative invalidation from the rifle definition and its broader premise that inaccessible design history is necessarily vagueness do not establish a missing rule here. Unknown facts can matter to culpability and proof without destroying the criterion itself. Ownership alone establishes neither the design condition nor fault, and the Court makes no finding about any plaintiff's handgun.

### VII. Restoration requires a former condition and actual capacity of return

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.11(G)(4) reaches an existing firearm capable of restoration to a former operable condition satisfying an independently valid (G)(1), (G)(2) or (G)(3)(b) definition, with separate fault. It does not permit enforcement based on an invalid modification predicate, raw materials treated as a restored firearm, or unsupported theoretical reconstruction.

**Controlling explanation:** Restoration denotes return to a former condition, and the word may states actual capability. The absence of a readily qualifier broadens the range beyond easy repair; it does not erase that intelligible relationship. *Grayned* does not demand a numerical standard for every practical criterion. The City must establish the former covered condition and real capability of return. The Court does not invent a universal time, cost, skill or machine-shop threshold. Disagreement about necessary work can present an application question, but the lower court's blanket invalidity does not follow. References to the invalid modification branches remain unavailable as independent predicates.

### VIII. The two parts branches retain different conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** The first branch of section 2323.11(G)(5) requires a part or combination designed or intended to convert a firearm into a weapon satisfying a valid (G)(1), (G)(2) or (G)(3)(b) predicate; objective conversion design or the identified conversion purpose must connect the parts to that result. The second requires a combination from which such a weapon may readily be assembled, possessed or controlled by the same person; practical assembly and separate fault remain necessary, and invalid modification predicates cannot independently support either branch.

**Controlling explanation:** Generic unrelated parts do not establish conversion design or purpose, and remote possibilities requiring rebuilding do not establish ready assembly. Common possession or control constrains the second branch; the City may not select arbitrary combinations from unrelated owners. *Grayned* permits practical standards without a numerical assembly formula. *Posters 'N' Things* illustrates the need to identify the article's bounded connection, but supplies neither an NFA parts-package result nor its own exclusive intended-use construction. Borderline practical applications differ from the absent modification relationship in Part V. No personal-use or general gunsmithing immunity is created. No plaintiff's parts, purpose, control or fault is found.

### IX. The former-city-registration selectors survive the presented equality challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2323.31(B)(3) may exempt a person who lawfully possessed and actually registered that particular assault weapon under former section 2323.05 in 1989, and section 2323.32(B)(2) may exempt a magazine belonging to or forming part of an assault weapon registered under former section 2323.05(C). Protecting that documented registration reliance while limiting the exempt stock rationally supports those selectors under the presented ordinary equality challenge, despite the former ordinance's invalidity and the expired window.

**Controlling explanation:** *Nordlinger* and *Dukes* permit protection of actual reliance without an immutable contractual entitlement; *Beach* permits imperfect classification, while *Heller* and *Romer* require a relation to the actual distinction. The earlier notice defect prevents punishment under the invalid law but does not erase actual registration and its documented reliance. The strongest objection is that lawful owners newly covered by the replacement law could not have known to register during the thirty-day November 1989 window. That exclusion is real, but ordinary review permits the narrower documented class rather than requiring a benefit for every prior owner. Registration of another weapon does not satisfy the firearm exception, and the magazine clause retains its particular related-weapon requirement. No proof that each registrant studied the ordinance is required. The ground is actual records and finite stock, not praise for compliance with invalid law or blame for nonregistration. *Hiley*'s different conviction-relief selectors do not decide this classification. The Court does not resolve an independent substantive arms claim or prescribe a replacement registry.

### X. The other magazine exceptions retain their full alternatives

**Holding and operative rule:** Against the presented ordinary equality challenge, section 2323.32(B)(2) may exempt a large-capacity magazine belonging to an NFA-registered firearm or possessed by the owner of such a firearm; the owner alternative requires neither that the magazine itself be registered nor that it fit that firearm. The separate inert-object branch covers a magazine belonging to or forming part of an assault weapon rendered totally inoperable or inert, which cannot readily be rendered operable or activated and is kept as a trophy, souvenir, curio or museum piece; ownership of an unrelated inert object is insufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** The NFA branch protects identified regulated ownership and reliance. Its coverage of magazines beyond those used with the registered firearm makes it imperfect, not irrational under *Nordlinger*, *Dukes* and *Romer*. Ordinary equality review does not require exact device-by-device tailoring. The inert-object branch preserves a different qualifying relationship and its express protection against ready reactivation; it is not a general owner's exemption. Neither alternative is rewritten to cure the challenged city-registration distinction. Section 2323.11(F) separately defines the magazine as a box, drum, clip or other container holding more than twenty rounds for continuous feeding into a semiautomatic firearm, excluding a magazine designed only for .22-caliber rimfire cartridges. Section 2323.32(A) expressly requires knowing possession; that requirement is distinct from section 2323.31(A)'s default recklessness and from the rifle definition's twenty-or-more threshold.

### XI. Relief follows the invalid branches and actual Ohio severability

**Holding and operative rule:** The invalidity of the two modification branches does not itself eliminate every other definition or exception. The lower courts must determine Ohio severability of those branches and their dependent references, then conform declaratory and injunctive relief to that determination and the component judgments; no enforcement may rest independently on an invalid modification predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** *Women's Medical* requires actual state-law severability rather than automatic whole-enactment invalidation. Ohio's independent-operation, legislative-intent and no-judicial-insertion principles govern. Reversal of the categorical notice rulings establishes only the stated construction-dependent answers, not every firearm's coverage, every prosecution's proof or substantive arms validity. The Court neither creates an offense nor reopens registration, orders arrests or directs blanket enforcement. Stevens's contrary city-selector disposition does not alter his agreement with the common severability principles. Relief against the established modification defects remains; the lower court must determine the proper scope of the surviving ordinance under Ohio law.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part, unjoined.** He joins the Court's threshold, fault, definition and noncity-exception analyses and its common severability principles. He would affirm invalidity of both former-city-registration selectors. His Nordlinger objection to entrenched ownership and family preferences and his Heller and Romer positions require a meaningful relation to an impartial public purpose. Lawful pre-existing owners who registered and those unable to identify the invalid old duty are alike in the ownership reliance invoked here. Owners first covered by the replacement ordinance had no meaningful opportunity to enter the only registry. For him those defects disconnect the closed registry from the claimed protection, even though actual registration occurred. A clear prospective cutoff or an opportunity for all covered prior owners would present a different case. He does not require universal grandfathering, invalidate the independent NFA or inert-object exceptions, or design a replacement scheme.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part, unjoined.** He would affirm the Sixth Circuit's relief against all five challenged definition categories and both former-city-registration distinctions, while sustaining the remaining NFA magazine exception. Smolak and Walker's actual possession, closed registration window and threatened prosecution justify present review; P.R.O. seeks common relief for similarly situated members. No advisory judgment about a wholly hypothetical weapon is warranted.

In his view, the rifle definition does not choose among actual magazine possession, commercial availability and merely possible manufacture; the shotgun definition does not select a measuring shell length. Undefined modification and inaccessible original handgun-design history compound the handgun defect. Restoration and parts provisions inherit those uncertainties and add uncertain assembly conditions. Criminal exposure without a clear culpability safeguard makes those defects consequential for ordinary owners and grants enforcement discretion the text does not constrain. The City's conflicting suggested knowledge standard cannot rewrite the ordinance. He therefore does not adopt the Court's default-fault and definition constructions. Love reinforces intelligible notice for protected keeping but establishes neither a right to every listed weapon nor a judicially chosen capacity limit.

He rejects grandfathering based on success in guessing an invalid 1989 registration duty. That objection reaches section 2323.31(B)(3) and the matching city clause in section 2323.32(B)(2). NFA registration instead supplies an independent, intelligible reliance basis. Its broader coverage than magazines individually used with the registered firearm survives ordinary rationality under Romer without exact tailoring. He also agrees with the judgment sustaining the separate related inert-object magazine exception, adding no ground on that component. He would implement the declaratory and injunctive relief for the invalid provisions, preserve separable valid exceptions, and enact neither damages nor a fresh registry. He decides no unpresented substantive arms claim and joins no part of the Court's opinion.

The two modification-branch defects remain established. No independent enforcement can rest on those predicates, and reversal of the other categorical rulings does not itself establish any owner's criminal liability or direct blanket enforcement. ## Event

United States v. Franklyn / United States v. Gonzalez; Second Circuit Nos. 97-1392 and 97-1427, 157 F.3d 90. Consolidated merits decision, October 22, 1998, on certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit after full merits briefing. No argument or exact submission date is stated. The September 11, 1998 appellate judgment affirmed the Southern District of New York judgments.

Robert Franklyn, also known as Franklin, was convicted by a jury in March 1997 of Count One, machinegun possession under 18 U.S.C. sections 922(o) and 2, and Count Two, felon ammunition possession under sections 922(g) and 2. ## Holdings

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Parts I-VIII, is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Those eight Justices directly adopt every holding and independently sufficient ground below. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgments without joining the opinion; no inference from his votes supplies additional rationale support. No fractured-decision analysis is necessary.

### I. Franklyn's retained machinegun falls within the transfer-and-stock mechanism

**Holding and operative rule:** The Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses permit section 922(o)'s possession restriction as a fitted means of preventing evasion or material impairment of the national post-1986 machinegun transfer-and-stock regime. Franklyn's possession of a completed automatic weapon within that regime does not require proof of an individual interstate journey or sale absent from the offense; his commerce challenge fails.

**Controlling explanation:** *Lopez* requires identification of the interstate mechanism before aggregate effects can do constitutional work. *Hale*, *Rybar*, *Kirk* and *Wright* supply that mechanism: exempting possession of prohibited stock would allow retention and circulation beyond transfer enforcement. The completed .45-caliber automatic weapon falls within the regulated class. The contrary objection correctly rejects general violence, productivity costs, dangerousness and remote manufacture as sufficient grounds, but does not answer this particular anti-evasion connection. The ammunition stipulation neither supplies constitutional power over the machinegun nor adds a jurisdictional element to section 922(o). No inference that every local firearm possession lies within federal power is adopted.

Section 922(o)(2) retains transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, a State, or a State's department, agency or political subdivision; it also retains lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986. Franklyn did not assert the preexisting-lawful-possession exception. The Court decides no new exception application or burden concerning it, and no Second Amendment issue.

### II. Gonzalez's ammunition conviction rests on a distinct supply-and-recipient connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(g)(1)'s restriction on a qualifying convicted recipient's acquisition and retention of firearms and ammunition is a fitted anti-evasion component of section 922(d)(1)'s convicted-person supply restriction, which forbids sale or other disposition of firearms or ammunition when the supplier knows or has reasonable cause to believe the recipient has been convicted of a crime punishable by imprisonment exceeding one year. That connection defeats Gonzalez's separate commerce objection; the stipulated interstate movement of the ammunition satisfies the enacted nexus here, but past movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism.

**Controlling explanation:** For the convicted-person branch at issue, section 922(d)(1) prohibits sale or other disposition when the supplier knows or has reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction. For the qualifying convicted-person class at issue, a corresponding possession restriction prevents the recipient from defeating that supply rule by acquiring and retaining the prohibited supply. *Hiley* establishes the method for the different domestic-violence-recipient structure; this is an application to the separately identified felon-supply provision, not a claim that Hiley already decided section 922(g)(1). The machinegun-stock rule of Part I does not automatically answer this count. The independent nexus remains part of the enacted offense, and no new conviction category, conviction-status finding or mental-state rule is supplied. Gonzalez's plea does not replace examination of his presented constitutional objection. No independent arms claim is decided.

### III. The actual ultimate discrimination finding sustains rejection of Franklyn's Batson claim

**Holding and operative rule:** After explanations are offered, the court must actually determine whether purposeful racial discrimination has been proved; facially neutral wording alone does not complete that inquiry. A supported ultimate credibility and purpose finding is reviewed for clear error, and the actual finding of no purposeful discrimination here supplies an independent ground to reject Franklyn's claim.

**Controlling explanation:** *Batson* preserves the initial inference, production of explanations and ultimate persuasion inquiry. *Hernandez* requires attention to the actual credibility finding and supports clear-error review. The Government used three of six peremptory strikes against three of four Black panel members. That pattern requires examination; it is not dismissed as irrelevant. The trial judge heard explanations and separately found no discriminatory intent, and the appellate court examined that determination. No identified clear error warrants displacement of that finding on the supported record. The individual explanations are not reproduced in the published account, so the Court supplies neither invented reasons nor a comparative-juror analysis. *Trevino* and *McCollum* retain the ordinary staged burdens; *J.E.B.* confirms that a neutral label cannot defeat supported pretext, without converting this racial inquiry into a cause challenge. If the transcript established only facial neutrality, the missing ultimate finding could not be replaced by deference. The reported actual finding supports this disposition. Counsel's objection came after voir dire, dismissal of the challenged jurors and lunch recess, impairing contemporaneous correction. The alternative merits ground makes a universal objection deadline unnecessary; no categorical forfeiture rule is announced.

### IV. The related counsel claim fails on prejudice, not on lateness alone

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming without deciding that counsel performed deficiently by objecting late, Franklyn has not established a reasonable probability of a different result from a timely objection because the supported ultimate Batson determination defeats the underlying challenge. Untimeliness itself neither proves absence of prejudice nor excuses an inadequate discrimination inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** *Strickland* requires both deficient performance and prejudice, and a court may resolve the latter without deciding the former. The decisive ground is the actual merits finding discussed in Part III. An earlier objection would not probably have produced relief from the challenged strikes on that supported determination. The argument that lost contemporaneous correction necessarily establishes prejudice would bypass the required inquiry into the underlying claim. Conversely, merely accepting facial neutrality could not support this conclusion. No general approval of counsel's delayed objection or new limitation on effective assistance is adopted.

### V. Supported assistance and joint activity permit the machinegun base level

**Holding and operative rule:** For conduct during the offense of conviction, in preparing for it, or in attempting to avoid detection or responsibility for it, section 1B1.3(a)(1)(A) permits attribution of supported acts aided or abetted by Gonzalez; subsection (B) separately requires another participant's conduct to fall within jointly undertaken criminal activity, further it and be reasonably foreseeable in connection with it. The supported findings satisfy both routes here, permitting level 20 under section 2K2.1(a)(4)(B) for this prohibited person's offense involving the machinegun described in 26 U.S.C. section 5845(a), rather than proposed level 14 under section 2K2.1(a)(6).

**Controlling explanation:** Gonzalez's matching loaded magazine and ammunition and the coordinated episode support the finding that he assisted Franklyn's machinegun possession. The court also found the relevant joint activity and its connection to the foreseeable weapon possession. The same coordinated episode supplies the required connection to the ammunition offense. Mere proximity or foreseeability divorced from agreed criminal scope and furtherance would not suffice. The findings were established by a preponderance and are not clearly erroneous. The base-level determination is application of the relevant-conduct guideline, distinct from the two later departures, although the lower opinion grouped them as three sentencing departures. Neither the prosecutor's recommendation, plea estimate nor presentence recommendation binds the judge to the lower level. *Koon* preserves independent legal review and deference to supported factual application; *Stinson* preserves consistent authoritative commentary without permitting it to amend the guideline. No new machinegun conviction for Gonzalez, automatic attribution to every ammunition offender or altered offense element follows.

### VI. Discharge supports the separate three-level departure

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 5K2.6 permits an increase where a weapon or dangerous instrumentality was used or possessed in committing the offense, with extent ordinarily depending on dangerousness, manner of use and danger to others; firearm discharge may warrant a substantial increase. Supported recent discharge in the attributed joint activity justifies three levels here, because the possession base level does not already account for that different conduct.

**Controlling explanation:** The gunshot report, bystanders' directions, spent shell jammed in the weapon and bore carbon support the discharge finding. Disputed testimony about the weapon's condition does not establish clear error. *Koon* requires a legally permissible factor, factual support and an independently reasonable extent; this encouraged factor addresses risk beyond the possession accounted for in the base level. The three-level increase is within that supported discretion. The double-counting argument fails because possession and discharge are distinct in this calculation, not because every weapon circumstance may always be counted twice. The judge also invoked a densely populated neighborhood's violent-crime history; the Court does not reach or approve that alternative. Acceptance credit then reduced level 23 by three to level 20. No acceptance-of-responsibility dispute is decided.

### VII. The presented criminal-history objections do not defeat category IV

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 4A1.3 permits departure when reliable information shows that the initial category inadequately reflects the seriousness of past criminal conduct or the likelihood of further crime; section 4A1.2 application note 8 permits consideration of stale convictions for similar or serious dissimilar conduct. The supported total-history assessment and explained selection of category IV defeat Gonzalez's claim that the move beyond category III mechanically counted only an ineligible old misdemeanor; no empty category-by-category recital is required when the reasons support the actual extent.

**Controlling explanation:** The two serious juvenile felonies, involving narcotics and a loaded weapon, could inform inadequacy despite their exclusion from baseline points and would support category III alongside the adult record. The judge's assessment of repeated offending and response to prior dispositions supplied the distinct total-history and recidivism basis for category IV. *Koon* permits supported contextual judgment but not an unexplained increase or invented replacement reasons. The principal objection misdescribes that assessment as arithmetic based solely on the third, stale misdemeanor. The Court rejects that objection and the demanded mechanical intermediate-category ritual.

The judge also mentioned education, employment and drug use. Those references are not erased or declared categorically permissible. Sections 5H1.2 and 5H1.5 make education, vocational skills and employment record ordinarily irrelevant to departure; their different potential uses in conditions of probation or supervised release do not authorize an upward prison-range departure. Section 5H1.4's distinct drug-dependence or abuse provisions are not an unconditional upward-departure authorization. The published appeal presents the mechanical-misdemeanor and extent objections, not a separate preserved challenge under those offender-characteristic provisions. This decision resolves the presented objections only. It makes no finding that an established invalid factor was harmless or that the same sentence necessarily would recur. A genuinely presented offender-characteristic challenge would require Koon's factor classification and, if invalid reliance were established, its separate whole-record and reasonable-extent remedy inquiry.

### VIII. Other-trial evidence and within-range selection remain limited sentencing decisions

**Holding and operative rule:** Reliable evidence from Franklyn's trial is not categorically barred at Gonzalez's sentencing merely because Gonzalez was neither a defendant nor represented there; the applicable preponderance burden, Rule 32 opportunity to address material factual assertions and independent evidentiary and Guidelines safeguards remain. The reported flight consideration explains selection of 63 months within the lawfully calculated 51–63-month range; it is not a newly imposed obstruction enhancement, and the presented objections supply no basis to disturb that sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** Gonzalez's categorical exclusion argument would make the source of otherwise reliable evidence dispositive. *Witte* and the limited sentencing principles preserved in *Watts* distinguish ordinary relevant-conduct consideration from a new conviction or separate disguised punishment, while retaining conviction-based authority and independent safeguards. No acquitted-conduct extension is necessary here. Neither decision makes another trial's assertions conclusively true, dispenses with reliable proof or eliminates the opportunity to contest material facts. No particular unreliability or denied opportunity requiring a new hearing is established on this record. The Court sustains the supported use of unacquitted joint-activity evidence. It also keeps the reported choice at the top of the range separate from the earlier base level and departures. It imposes no section 3C1.1 adjustment and announces no categorical flight rule or universal below-maximum sentencing safe harbor.

### I. The supply restriction and recipient-possession rule supply the constitutional connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may regulate these qualifying convicted recipients' possession under section 922(g)(1) as a fitted counterpart to section 922(d)(1)'s convicted-person supply restriction, which forbids sale or other disposition of firearms or ammunition when the supplier knows or has reasonable cause to believe the recipient has been convicted of a crime punishable by imprisonment exceeding one year. Preventing the recipients' acquisition and retention from defeating that supply restriction supplies the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clause connection; a pistol's former interstate movement, burglary or generalized dangerousness alone does not supply it.

**Controlling explanation:** *Lopez* requires the actual interstate mechanism before aggregation. *Franklyn*, Part II, directly identifies the section 922(d)(1)/section 922(g)(1) relationship for firearms and ammunition: permitting the prohibited recipient class freely to acquire and retain the restricted supply would permit evasion or material impairment through redistribution and concealment. Each defendant's pistol possession falls within that relationship; no separate commercial transaction by either defendant is needed for this constitutional ground. *Hale*, *Rybar*, *Kirk* and *Wright* explain analogous necessary inclusion in the different machinegun-stock regime, but do not automatically decide every prohibited-person statute. *Hiley* likewise supplies its distinct recipient-class application, not universal approval of every status disability. The objection that remote manufacture cannot alone support federal power is correct but does not defeat the independent mechanism here. The restaurant burglary makes the prosecution concrete without creating a federal burglary power or an uncharged commercial element. The enacted nexus remains independently necessary. The supplier's knowledge or reasonable-cause condition is not imported as the complete recipient-offense culpability rule, and statutory conviction exclusions or relief provisions are not displaced. No arms claim is adjudicated.

### II. The requested direct-effect instruction would add an element

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(g)(1)'s retained nexus construction does not require contemporaneous interstate movement or an individually substantial or direct interstate effect from the defendant's possession. Because the regulated class has the independent constitutional connection stated in Part I, the court properly refused each defendant's requested additional direct-effect instruction; the prosecution still must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Controlling explanation:** *Bass* supplies the statutory requirement of a commerce connection, and *Scarborough* establishes that the retained nexus can be satisfied by the firearm's prior interstate movement. *Lopez* addresses congressional authority and does not silently add a per-defendant direct-impact requirement. Constitutional avoidance therefore supplies no reason to insert the requested condition: the actual supply-and-recipient mechanism resolves the constitutional objection without changing the statute. The jury decides the required offense facts, not whether Congress's national scheme is constitutionally sufficient. This holding rejects only the additional legal demand described in the lower opinion. It supplies no verbatim replacement charge, approves no unidentified wording in the instructions actually given and decides no different preserved instructional objection.

### III. The evidence independently supports the nexus for each pistol

**Holding and operative rule:** Viewed favorably to the verdict under *Jackson v. Virginia*, testimony that each pistol was manufactured in Connecticut, together with each defendant's possession of his pistol in North Carolina, permits a rational jury to find that firearm's prior interstate movement beyond a reasonable doubt. Neither a precise shipment date, carrier or seller nor a personal state-line crossing by the defendant is necessary to establish this nexus; both motions for acquittal were properly denied.

**Controlling explanation:** The expert's testimony concerned both weapons. Connecticut manufacture followed by North Carolina possession permits the interstate inference for Griffin's pistol and separately for Langley's. *Scarborough* identifies the statutory fact to be proved, while *Jackson* supplies the rational-jury sufficiency standard. Constitutional authority over the class does not itself prove that fact, and one pistol's history could not substitute for evidence about the other. The account supplies no disputed expert qualification, authentication failure or contrary-evidence defect; none is invented. Gonzalez's ammunition stipulation in *Franklyn* establishes no fact in these appeals. The absence of a detailed shipping record does not defeat this supported inference. The Court makes no additional credibility finding or ruling on possession knowledge, the prior-conviction element, conviction exclusions, weapon classification or sentence.

## Event

Marquez v. Screen Actors Guild, Inc., No. 97-1056. Merits decision, November 3, 1998. Argued October 5, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 124 F.3d 1034 (1997).

## Holdings

### 1. Statutory membership wording does not alone breach fair representation

**Holding and operative rule:** A union does not breach its duty of fair representation merely by negotiating a union-security clause that uses section 8(a)(3)'s statutory membership terms without restating every judicial qualification. The wording remains subject to the governing limits on compulsory financial obligations and does not authorize compelled full membership, misleading notice, nonchargeable collections or unlawful enforcement.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court; all nine participating Justices adopt this rule, its explanation and its limits directly.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the clause must be read with the law governing it. General Motors limits enforceable membership to financial obligations rather than full formal membership. Beck limits an objecting nonmember's compulsory charges to collective bargaining, contract administration and grievance adjustment. Omitting those qualifications from the agreement does not enlarge the union's lawful authority.

Vaca supplies the separate grounds of arbitrariness, discrimination and bad faith; O'Neill applies them to negotiation as well as administration. Under the arbitrariness ground, a negotiated choice must fall outside the broad range of reasonableness assessed against the relevant facts and law. Using statutory terminology with an established legal meaning does not, by itself, cross that boundary or establish discriminatory treatment or dishonest purpose.

Marquez's strongest objection is that an employee may understand membership language to demand more than the law permits. That concern matters to actual notice and enforcement; it does not establish that using the statutory terms always breaches the duty. The Court does not presume that employees know every governing decision or hold that the clause itself adequately informed Marquez. Its wording may be relevant evidence of actual misconduct. A supported deception claim remains available, and both existing remand proceedings remain untouched.

### 2. A standalone statutory timing claim belongs initially to the Board

**Holding and operative rule:** A claim alleging only that a union's grace-period arrangement violates the NLRA belongs initially to the National Labor Relations Board; labeling the alleged statutory inconsistency a fair-representation breach does not create an independent judicial basis. A federal court may resolve a statutory issue necessary to a representation claim supported by operative facts independently showing arbitrariness, discrimination or bad faith.

**Authority:** The same Opinion of the Court; all nine participating Justices adopt this rule, its application and its limits directly.

**Controlling explanation:** Garmon assigns the independent unfair-labor-practice question to the Board in the first instance. Vaca preserves the judicial action for a breach of the union's duty of fair representation, and Beck permits a court deciding such a claim to resolve the labor-law question necessary to it. Those authorities distinguish a genuine representation claim from a statutory dispute carrying a different label.

Marquez's thirty-day theory, as presented here, contests compliance with the statute without an independently supported arbitrary, discriminatory or bad-faith feature of representation on that theory. The Court therefore affirms the refusal to decide that statutory question. The other enforcement and notice claims already pending below do not automatically transfer every collateral statutory issue to a federal court.

This conclusion neither makes intent indispensable to every representation breach nor bars judicial statutory construction whenever a labor-law issue appears. If a statutory question is necessary to an independently supported representation claim, Vaca and Beck continue to govern at that scope. The Court does not decide whether the thirty-day period restarts after a break, whether prior employment counts, or whether SAG's calculation was lawful.

## Precedent Treatment

- Vaca v. Sipes, 386 U.S. 171: applies the distinct arbitrariness, discrimination and bad-faith grounds and preserves judicial enforcement of an independently supported representation duty.
- Air Line Pilots Association v. O'Neill, 499 U.S. 65: applies the duty to negotiation and the broad but bounded range of reasonable bargaining choices; no intent-only test replaces it.
- NLRB v. General Motors Corp., 373 U.S. 734: applies the limited financial meaning of compulsory membership rather than compelled full formal membership.
- Communications Workers v. Beck, 487 U.S. 735: applies the limit on an objecting nonmember's chargeable representational expenses and the court's ability to decide a statutory issue necessary to a genuine representation claim. It does not impose an exhaustive drafting code in every agreement.
- San Diego Building Trades Council v. Garmon, 359 U.S. 236: applies the Board's initial responsibility for the independent statutory timing dispute while preserving the distinct judicial representation action.

No precedent is overruled.

Statutory membership language may be used in a collective-bargaining agreement without reproducing all its legal qualifications, but the union remains bound by those qualifications in its conduct. The drafting rule supplies no safe harbor against supported notice, collection or enforcement claims.

A statutory administration dispute remains initially with the Board unless the operative facts independently support a judicial representation claim requiring resolution of that statutory issue. The two distinct routes retain their own predicates; neither the duty label nor an unrelated pending claim supplies the missing judicial basis.

## Holdings

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion controls Parts I, II and IV with the five votes of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part III has all nine Justices' support. The following four holdings carry those respective coalitions.

### I. Selective racial restructuring requires a political-process inquiry

**Rule:** A State cannot answer a supported claim that it selectively moved authority over otherwise lawful racial remedies to a more remote political process merely by calling the foreclosed measures preferences. On the district court's preliminary findings, California's removal of those measures to constitutional amendment, while ordinary preferences remain available through ordinary officials, supports confined protection against the resulting unequal political burden; it neither requires a preference nor validates an unconstitutional program.

**Controlling explanation:** Hunter addresses a special political hurdle for racial protection; Seattle applies that principle to selective relocation of decisionmaking over racial remedies even when the underlying measure exceeds federal requirements. Romer preserves that limited doctrine without promising favorable legislation at every governmental level. The district court found both lost opportunities and the special amendment hurdle. Equal words about all races do not dispose of how that allocation operates. Crawford permits withdrawal of a voluntary policy, but does not eliminate Seattle's distinct inquiry into selectively changing who may decide racial questions. The State can enforce the constitutional limits on each proposed program; its interest in preventing unlawful classifications does not alone justify excluding measures that satisfy those limits. Croson, Hopwood and Engineering Contractors continue to require justification and fit. The Court decides the basis for limited preliminary relief, not the permanent validity of every provision or any particular program, and finds no need to attribute animus to voters.

### II. The sex theory requires a distinct inquiry

**Rule:** The racial political-process rule does not by itself resolve a challenge to removing authority over sex-conscious measures. The lower court must examine the asserted sex-based barrier on its own terms, applying the important-objective and substantial-relationship standard to an actual sex classification without presuming that every statewide policy withdrawal creates such a classification.

**Controlling explanation:** Virginia and Engineering Contractors require a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and a sex classification; they do not treat a stereotype as justification. Hunter and Seattle concern racial political structure and do not automatically decide every sex-conscious policy. The appellate categorical treatment omitted the separate inquiry. Section 31(c) preserves bona fide sex qualifications reasonably necessary to normal governmental operations in the three covered fields; it is neither a general permission for sex preferences nor proof that the initiative rests on a stereotype. The Court remands the distinct theory without finding all sex-neutrality rules invalid or extending racial strict scrutiny to them.

### III. Federal permission does not establish blanket preemption

**Rule:** Title VII's permission for defined voluntary affirmative action does not alone preempt a State's withdrawal of that policy or guarantee every subordinate governmental entity power to adopt it. An actual conflict with a federal statutory duty or binding federal remedy remains governed by federal supremacy and the applicable savings provisions; no such conflict is decided categorically for every operation of this initiative.

**Controlling explanation:** Weber and Johnson recognize lawful voluntary measures; they do not make every such measure compulsory. Section 703(j) prevents treating numerical imbalance in employment, union membership or related categories, measured against community, geographic or available-workforce figures, as itself requiring preferential treatment. It does not excuse actual unlawful discrimination. Section 708 separately preserves state duties except where state law requires or permits an unlawful employment practice. Section 1104 disclaims occupation of the field and preserves state law unless inconsistent with a provision or purpose of the Civil Rights Act. Those provisions cannot be collapsed into an unlimited exception. Proposition 209 protects existing orders, necessary federal-program eligibility and maximum lawful implementation on its actual terms. The district court's broad statutory theory therefore cannot sustain its entire injunction. Concrete federal conflicts and any distinct question of deference to administrative guidelines remain undecided.

### IV. Preliminary relief must correspond to the supported injury

**Rule:** Interim relief must protect against the demonstrated threatened loss of access to otherwise available lawful racial remedies, while leaving independently lawful provisions operative to the extent severable under state law. The lower court must conform its order to that constitutional injury and the ordinary preliminary-relief requirements, separately resolve the sex theory and concrete federal conflicts, and avoid turning interim protection into a permanent entitlement to a program.

**Controlling explanation:** The district findings supply a present burden on ordinary political access for preliminary consideration, not merely disagreement with an election result. They do not resolve the lawfulness of every affected program, the exact threatened injury defining an injunction's scope, or all remaining equitable prerequisites. The original order extended to implementation insofar as the initiative prohibited or affected affirmative-action programs throughout employment, education and contracting. That formulation may reach beyond the supported injury and does not justify automatic restoration of every part of the order. Section 31(h) requires maximum lawful implementation and severance of an invalid portion. The distinct ban on discrimination, genuine operational qualifications and other independently valid applications must be respected. The Court vacates the appellate judgment that categorically foreclosed the constitutional claim and returns the matter for a fitted order and further proceedings. No injunction validates a program that fails Croson, Hopwood or Engineering Contractors.

**Operative statutory limits:** Section 31 bars governmental discrimination and preferential treatment based on race, sex, color, ethnicity or national origin in public employment, education and contracting. It applies only to action after its effective date. Its sex exception is limited to bona fide qualifications reasonably necessary to normal public employment, education or contracting. It does not invalidate an order or consent decree already in force on the effective date; this protection is not confined to federal decrees and does not automatically extend to every later decree. It preserves action necessary to establish or maintain federal-program eligibility when ineligibility would cost the State federal funds, rather than every federally permitted action. Its governmental coverage includes the State and its local governments, public university systems including the University of California, community-college and school districts, special districts and other political subdivisions or instrumentalities. Remedies are the same regardless of the injured person's race, sex, color, ethnicity or national origin and are those otherwise available under then-existing California antidiscrimination law. The section is self-executing, must operate to the maximum extent federal law and the Constitution permit, and severs an invalid provision from the remainder. These conditions do not themselves establish whether a particular program is lawful.

## Holdings

### Commercial offer and readiness for patenting

**Operative rule.** The on-sale bar in then-effective 35 U.S.C. § 102(b) applies when, before the date one year before the patent application in the United States, the actual claimed invention is both the subject of a commercial offer for sale in the United States and ready for patenting. Readiness may be shown by reduction to practice or by drawings or descriptions sufficiently specific to enable a person skilled in the art to practice the invention; a working prototype is therefore sufficient but not indispensable.

**Authority.** The opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine Justices, controls both conditions, their application and the following limits.

**Controlling explanation.** The statute addresses an invention offered for sale, not the manufacture of a first physical specimen. Pennock v. Dialogue supplies the principle that an inventor cannot prolong commercial exploitation and then obtain a later period of exclusivity. The Telephone Cases recognize that an enabling description can establish an operative invention without an indispensable successful prototype. Neither decision supplies a different statutory grace period or itself resolves the present on-sale question.

Both conditions must be proved. A substantial commercial commitment cannot make an incomplete conception ready for patenting, and later successful development cannot retroactively establish readiness before the critical date. Requiring enabling drawings or descriptions of the claimed invention gives that requirement an objective content. An open-ended judgment that development was merely substantially complete is not a substitute.

Here, the Texas Instruments purchase order establishes the commercial commitment, and the engineering drawings were complete enough to permit production before the critical date. The later manufacture of a physical embodiment does not postpone the bar. Pfaff's proposed prototype prerequisite would let the manufacturing schedule extend the period for exploiting an already enabling design.

Mere preliminary negotiations, proposals for future inventive research and incomplete conceptions do not meet the two conditions. Elizabeth v. Pavement Co. preserves the distinction between genuine controlled experimentation and ordinary commercial exploitation. The Court decides no separate experimental-use dispute and invalidates no unidentified claims beyond those under review.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Pennock v. Dialogue, 27 U.S. 1:** Applied for the restriction on prolonged commercial exploitation followed by later exclusivity. Its older statutory setting does not alter the one-year period governing this case.
- **The Telephone Cases, 126 U.S. 1:** Applied for the significance of an enabling description without an indispensable successful prototype; not treated as independently deciding the on-sale trigger.
- **Elizabeth v. Pavement Co., 97 U.S. 126:** Preserved. Genuine controlled experimentation remains distinct from ordinary commercial exploitation; no experimental-use question is separately adjudicated here.
- **Markman v. Westview Instruments:** Its allocation of claim-construction and infringement functions remains intact. It does not itself decide when an invention is on sale.
- **Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co.:** Its claim-element and prosecution-history rules remain intact. The present inquiry concerns readiness of the actual claimed invention, without changing those rules.

## Law After Decision

Courts applying the then-effective on-sale bar must separately determine whether a commercial offer in the United States and enabling readiness existed more than one year before the patent application in the United States. A prototype is one way to establish readiness; enabling drawings or descriptions are another. Neither commercial purpose alone nor later successful development supplies a missing precritical-date condition.

No argument date is supplied.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Third Circuit's judgment reversing summary judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Affirmed, 9–0. The Third Circuit's reversal of summary judgment stands. The customers' claims return for further proceedings on the actual undertaking, reasonably available execution terms, contemporaneous knowledge and all remaining private-action elements. The Court awards no damages, establishes no liability and orders no automatic trial of every issue regardless of ordinary lawful dispositive procedure.

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** None.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I-III | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Entire opinion: the knowing-deception sufficient ground, application to the disputed execution evidence, preservation of all private-action elements and limited disposition. |
| Opinion concurring | Stone-Zsela | None | Joins the whole Court opinion and judgment; would also recognize reckless materially misleading order acceptance under the existing undertaking, subject to every private-action requirement. |

## Holdings

The following two holdings are supported by the opinion of the Court and all nine Justices. They resolve the knowing-deception theory and the summary-judgment record. They do not establish a general primary-fraud recklessness rule.

### A knowingly false execution undertaking can be primary securities fraud

**Operative rule.** A broker-dealer that accepts a customer order without price instructions while knowingly intending to disregard the best-execution undertaking it thereby represents can commit primary deception under § 10(b) and Rule 10b-5 when the representation is materially false and the other requirements of a private securities-fraud action are proved. Breach of care or fiduciary duty, inferior execution or the broker's status alone does not establish such fraud; the customer must establish legally sufficient reliance, causation and actual loss as well as the required deception and culpability.

**Controlling explanation.** The parties agreed below that accepting an order without price instructions implies an undertaking consistent with best execution and that acceptance with an intention to breach that undertaking would be a material misrepresentation. Their agreement does not establish what the undertaking required in each transaction or concede any defendant's liability.

First Jersey recognizes a broker's own knowing deception through implied transaction representations. That principle reaches a knowingly false execution undertaking, but First Jersey's proved facts and SEC-enforcement posture do not establish these private customers' reliance, injury or damages. Santa Fe Industries v. Green excludes fiduciary unfairness without deception, and Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder excludes negligence. The present theory respects both limits because it requires a false undertaking made with knowledge of its falsity, rather than a later failure to exercise due care.

The securities firms contend that customary execution at the national best bid and offer defined their undertaking and defeated culpability. Such custom is relevant to the actual representation and state of mind. It cannot conclusively immunize an undertaking that the firms understood to be false when made. The customers must prove the representation actually made, including material instructions or disclosed limits, and every other private-action element. A less favorable price creates no presumption of reliance or automatic damage award.

The evidence permits the knowing-deception theory to proceed. The Court therefore does not decide a general primary-liability recklessness standard or transfer Central Bank's separate affirmative-assistance standard to this direct claim.

### The disputed contemporary execution evidence precludes the categorical summary judgment

**Operative rule.** Execution at the national best bid and offer, or through the Small Order Execution System, does not by itself defeat a primary-fraud claim where evidence supports a genuine dispute that better terms were reasonably available and the firm knowingly accepted the order on a false undertaking. Reasonable availability is assessed under contemporary conditions, including order size, the security's trading characteristics, speed, clearing costs and the expense and difficulty of market access, as well as quoted price; later adoption of a rule or development of technology does not itself establish earlier duties, access or culpability.

**Controlling explanation.** The claims concern transactions between November 4, 1992, and November 4, 1994. Customers identify three possible sources of better terms: SelectNet or Instinet quotations, crossing opposing customer market orders, and matching customer orders with in-house limit orders. Expert and industry declarations, evidence of the firms' own use of alternative systems and evidence of particular trades with contemporaneous better quotations support a factual dispute. The firms' evidence of expense, delay and ordinary industry practice supports their defense, but does not conclusively resolve that dispute in their favor.

The relevant question is the best terms reasonably available to the customer. A nominally better quotation need not improve the customer’s execution after practical costs and timing are considered. Conversely, a customary published benchmark is not conclusive when a firm knowingly ignores a feasible improvement inconsistent with its undertaking. Small-order execution through the Small Order Execution System is part of that practical inquiry; the record establishes no universal safe harbor, and not all orders used that system.

This reasoning applies separately to the asserted electronic quotations, crossed market orders and in-house limit orders. It mandates no particular venue, guarantees no best possible price and imposes no universal search of every quotation for every order. Later execution regulations cannot be imposed retroactively on these transactions. A later study of these markets may supply reliable retrospective evidence of contemporary feasibility; adopting a later rule is not itself proof of earlier conditions. Particular-trade evidence supports a real damages controversy, but does not prove injury in every trade, determine class certification or establish reliance, causation or recoverable amounts. The ordinary rules for resolving the remaining issues continue to apply.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Securities and Exchange Commission v. First Jersey Securities, Inc.:** Applied for knowing primary deception through a broker's implied transaction representations. Its established findings and SEC-enforcement posture are distinguished from the disputed facts and private-action elements here.
- **Central Bank of Denver v. First Interstate Bank of Denver:** Its separate culpable-affirmative-assistance route remains intact. Its severe-recklessness standard for that route is not made a general primary-fraud standard, and assistance liability is unnecessary to this disposition.
- **United States v. O'Hagan:** Its requirement of actual deception connected to a securities transaction remains intact. The Court does not extend its distinct source-deception theory to supply the representation here.
- **Santa Fe Industries v. Green, 430 U.S. 462:** Followed. Fiduciary unfairness or breach alone does not constitute federal securities fraud.
- **Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185:** Followed. Negligence is insufficient; the Court does not treat Hochfelder as resolving every primary-liability recklessness question.
- **Basic Inc. v. Levinson:** The requirement of legally sufficient reliance is preserved. A worse execution price supplies no automatic reliance presumption in this case.

## Law After Decision

A customer's claim may proceed on evidence of a broker's own knowingly false best-execution undertaking. Neither a published quotation benchmark nor industry custom categorically forecloses that claim. Practical execution circumstances and the full requirements of private fraud remain essential. The Court leaves the broader primary-recklessness question unresolved, and no new execution regulation or automatic price guarantee follows.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring; no joins.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's knowing-deception holding and affirmance. He would also hold that reckless, materially misleading acceptance of customer orders under the existing best-execution undertaking can constitute primary § 10(b) and Rule 10b-5 fraud when every ordinary private-action requirement is proved. Acceptance communicates an intention to honor that undertaking; an actionable false representation need not be confined to a defendant's express statement. His broader culpability position goes beyond the sufficient ground the Court adopts and does not become the Court's rule.

He agrees that best execution depends on terms reasonably available under contemporary market conditions, that industry custom informs but does not conclusively resolve culpability, and that later SEC rules cannot create retroactive liability. Later market studies may corroborate evolving feasibility without supplying a retroactive legal command. He would leave knowing or reckless falsity, reliance and actual damage for case-specific proof. Neither breach of fiduciary duty alone, execution at the national best bid and offer nor the existence of a better price automatically establishes fraud, and the concurrence recognizes no reliance presumption from price disparity.

## Procedure After Action

The Third Circuit's reversal of summary judgment remains in effect, and the litigation returns for further proceedings consistent with the Court's holdings. The actual undertaking, practical alternatives and contemporary knowledge remain disputed. Reliance, causation, injury, class issues and recoverable amounts remain subject to the applicable law and proof. No compensation or regulatory injunction is ordered, and no automatic trial or favorable merits determination is guaranteed.
