## Event

Wright v. Universal Maritime Service Corp., No. 97-889. Merits decision, November 16, 1998. Argued October 7, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, No. 96-2850, judgment of July 29, 1997, reported at 121 F.3d 702.

Ceasar Wright's ADA action was dismissed without prejudice for failure to pursue the collective grievance procedure, and the Fourth Circuit affirmed. The question is whether the general collective agreement or the separate Longshore Seniority Plan clearly and unmistakably submits his independent federal discrimination claim to arbitration. The Court does not adjudicate the employers' asserted disability-based defense or Wright's discrimination claim.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Fourth Circuit judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Vacated and remanded, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The exhaustion-based dismissal without prejudice cannot stand on these instruments. The ADA case returns through the Fourth Circuit to the District Court for ordinary proceedings unless another lawful, independently established ground disposes of it. No arbitration or grievance proceeding is compelled.

## Holdings

### Neither instrument clearly waives the statutory judicial forum

**Operative rule:** A union-negotiated surrender of an employee's judicial forum for an independent federal discrimination claim must be clear and unmistakable: the agreement must identify that statutory claim or an equivalent class of federal rights rather than merely submit contractual disputes. Neither this CBA nor the separate Seniority Plan does so, and their grievance provisions cannot support dismissal of Wright's ADA action for nonexhaustion; whether a sufficiently explicit collective waiver would be enforceable remains undecided.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the clarity rule, both instrument-specific applications and the bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Gardner-Denver distinguishes an individual's statutory discrimination right from rights created by collective bargaining. Metropolitan Edison requires clarity before a union is understood to waive protected statutory rights. Gilmer involved an individual's agreement to arbitrate a statutory claim; it does not supply missing assent in this collective agreement. AT&T Technologies and First Options require determination of the agreement actually made before invoking arbitral authority. First Options concerns antecedent assent and delegation, not a prior resolution of every collective statutory-forum question.

The employers emphasize that qualification for employment concerns a subject comprehensively covered by the CBA and that its grievance decisions are final. Clause 15(B)'s machinery addresses disputes within the collective relationship. Clause 15(F)'s employment coverage, together with its exclusion of matters outside the agreement, does not expressly incorporate the ADA or submit independent federal claims. Clause 17 prevents the contract from violating law; a legality safeguard is not an agreement to arbitrate every statutory cause of action. Mastrobuono requires reading the actual instrument together, not presuming the broadest imaginable submission.

The Seniority Plan is a separate instrument. Its coverage of disputes about its own terms, interpretation, application and implementing rules likewise identifies no surrender of this ADA forum. The same employment event may give rise to both contractual and statutory questions without merging their sources.

The Court therefore need not decide whether an explicit union-negotiated waiver would be enforceable, resolve general FAA coverage or find the union's alleged advice to sue established. Wright's qualifications, benefits history, discrimination claim and independent defenses remain for the proper court. No ADA violation or entitlement to recovery follows from removal of the grievance-exhaustion bar.

## Precedent Treatment

- Alexander v. Gardner-Denver Co.: applies the distinction between collective contractual rights and the individual's statutory discrimination claim; no categorical decision on explicit waiver enforceability is added.
- Metropolitan Edison Co. v. NLRB: applies the requirement of a clear waiver of protected statutory rights to the forum question presented.
- Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp.: distinguishes its actual individual statutory-claim agreement; enforceability there does not create assent here.
- AT&T Technologies v. Communications Workers: preserves judicial determination of the submission actually made before arbitration may be compelled.
- First Options of Chicago v. Kaplan (May 22, 1995): applies antecedent assent and independent judicial determination; its arbitrability-delegation rule is not expanded into an already-decided universal collective-waiver rule.
- Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton: applies integrated, instrument-specific construction; no contractual presumption supplies missing statutory-forum language.

## Law After Decision

Broad collective grievance language and a contractual legality clause do not alone surrender an employee's forum for an independent federal discrimination claim. Both instruments fail the necessary clarity here. The decision leaves enforceability of an adequately explicit union waiver unresolved and neither disfavors genuinely agreed statutory arbitration nor decides the ADA merits.

## Event

Dodd v. Hood River County, No. 98-54; Ninth Circuit No. 97-35124. Merits decision, November 19, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 136 F.3d 1219, decided February 13, 1998, affirming summary judgment on the owners' federal takings claims.

The Dodds bought a 40-acre forest-use parcel in 1983 for a retirement home. Oregon proceedings determined that profitable timber use remained. The owners reserved their federal claim and obtained state judicial review. The Ninth Circuit applied issue preclusion narrowly to profitable use and separately rejected the remaining federal grounds; it did not adopt the District Court's equation of the entire state and federal takings inquiries. County authorization to build issued July 1, 1996, leaving the earlier period as the subject of the claimed deprivation.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit's judgment is affirmed on narrow issue preclusion and the independently stated federal grounds.

## Judgment & Remedy

Affirmed, 8–1. Supporting: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: Stone-Zsela, who would preserve the narrow preclusion ruling but reserve and remand the federal issues outside the state determination.

All nine directly join the preclusive effect of the necessary, fairly litigated finding of profitable timber use. That agreement concerns a legal premise within this single judgment; it does not separately dispose of the federal cause or establish a precise residual valuation. Eight Justices join the independent federal grounds sustaining the judgment.

No new permit, compensation award or jury trial is ordered. The July 1996 permission limits duration; it does not itself erase an earlier taking.

## Holdings

### I. Reservation of the federal cause does not erase a necessary, fairly litigated state finding

**Operative rule:** Section 1738 gives the Oregon judicial determination its lawful state preclusive effect: reserving a federal takings cause does not exempt an identical factual issue actually and necessarily litigated with a full and fair opportunity by parties bound in proceedings Oregon recognizes. Those requirements establish preclusion of profitable timber use here, but not of the entire federal claim, every federal element, a precise residual valuation or the legal assessment of partial deprivation.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker requires lawful rendering-state effect without a general public-policy exception; Allen applies that principle to actually litigated issues. England preserves federal questions following federal abstention, not a unilateral right to contradict a necessary finding in separate state compensation litigation. Williamson County's then-governing compensation requirement does not create the claimed exemption, and the Dodds completed the state process. Richards requires lawful authority to bind and adequate opportunity; these owners were actual parties.

Each Oregon condition is satisfied at the proper level of specificity. The existence of profitable timber use is the same factual issue relevant to total federal deprivation. The competing appraisal and forester evidence were presented, and the use finding was necessary to the final state no-taking determination. Available evidentiary procedures, including a hearing available on request and witness examination, together with actual presentation and judicial review, supplied a full and fair opportunity. The Dodds themselves were parties. The LUBA adjudication and ensuing judicial review are proceedings Oregon recognizes for this purpose. The reported failure to request the available hearing does not eliminate the opportunity; Kremer distinguishes adequacy from use of every available procedure.

The federal cause remains distinct. The state finding establishes retained profitable use, not the federal significance of every restriction or a particular dollar value. The Court therefore rejects complete preclusion and separately addresses the federal grounds. No rule makes every administrative proceeding fair or every reserved federal issue precluded.

### II. Profitable timber use defeats only the complete-deprivation category

**Operative rule:** The binding finding that this parcel retains profitable timber use defeats a Lucas claim that the restriction eliminated all economically beneficial use. It does not itself decide partial deprivation under Penn Central, and no background-property limitation need be found when the necessary complete loss has not been established.

**Authority:** Part II has the eight Associate Justices' direct support; Stone-Zsela does not join this federal merits determination.

**Controlling explanation:** Lucas supplies a categorical compensation rule after complete deprivation, subject to a substantiated antecedent property or nuisance limitation. Profitable timber use prevents the owners from establishing that categorical premise. The Court need not decide that Oregon's Goal 4 restriction already excluded the proposed dwelling from title or shift Lucas's background-law burden before total deprivation is shown. Prior enactment or a legislative description of harm would not alone satisfy that burden in a case that reached it. Del Monte Dunes preserves the separate partial-taking inquiry; the failure of the total-loss category does not end that inquiry here.

### III. The independent contextual federal inquiry supports this summary judgment

**Operative rule:** A partial-taking claim requires consideration together of economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations and the character of governmental action; retained value, acquisition under an existing restriction or a legitimate regulatory objective alone is not dispositive. On this undisputed record, continued profitable timber use, the existing necessary-and-accessory dwelling condition, nonassuring County communications and the forest-management character of the restriction support rejection of the claim; the restriction also bears the required relationship to the specified forestry purposes under the received Agins inquiry, without a demanded conveyance triggering Dolan.

**Authority:** Part III has the eight Associate Justices' direct support, including its distinct contextual and purpose-relationship applications; Stone-Zsela does not join those merits grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** Penn Central requires the combined inquiry, not an equation between any remaining use and constitutional validity. The property continued to support profitable timber production. The claimed retirement dwelling was subject at acquisition to an existing statewide requirement that it be necessary and accessory to forest use. County communications did not promise the required permits. Those facts bear on the reasonableness of this particular expectation; they do not establish that later purchasers lose constitutional protection.

The regulation restricts forest use to serve actual forestry, fire-protection and chemical-application objectives. It neither occupies the parcel physically nor demands a property conveyance. Its actual relationship to those purposes satisfies the received Agins inquiry at the bounded scope preserved in Del Monte Dunes. Dolan's special nexus-and-proportionality burden for a demanded conveyance therefore does not govern.

The owners correctly distinguish their federal cause from the narrower Oregon finding and insist that retained use alone cannot answer their claim. The Court addresses the remaining legal restriction, actual communications, continuing use and governmental character independently. It neither fixes a percentage diminution nor calls an unknown loss trivial. The reported undisputed record supplies no material factual conflict requiring another trial under the governing standards. Del Monte Dunes protects a properly supported factual taking claim while retaining judicial sufficiency review; it does not guarantee a trial on a precluded fact or unsupported dispute. Personal residential plans receive ordinary property protection, and the result does not depend on labeling them noncommercial.

### IV. Later permission limits the claimed duration without curing a prior taking

**Operative rule:** The July 1, 1996 building authorization ends treatment of this dispute as an ongoing prohibition but does not itself defeat compensation for an earlier proved taking. Because the independently considered total- and partial-taking grounds establish no antecedent violation here, the actual-period claim supplies no compensation or basis for new factfinding.

**Authority:** Part IV has the eight Associate Justices' direct support; Stone-Zsela would reserve and remand the federal issues rather than join this final resolution.

**Controlling explanation:** First English, Lucas and Del Monte Dunes preserve compensation for the attributable duration of an actual taking. Permission issued later does not make an earlier unconstitutional deprivation disappear. Equally, delay in permission does not establish the antecedent violation without the substantive inquiry. That inquiry has been resolved against the owners on the stated record. The Court therefore awards no compensation, orders no new permit and requires no additional factfinding. Criticism of how quickly the owners pursued development supplies no constitutional forfeiture rule.

A federal-claim reservation preserves questions outside the actual state adjudication but does not erase the ordinary preclusive effect of necessary, fairly litigated common facts. Here profitable timber use binds the owners and defeats complete deprivation. The distinct federal partial-taking and purpose-relationship inquiries are independently resolved on the actual record; continued use alone does not decide them. ## Event

Wisconsin Right to Life, Inc. v. Paradise, No. 98-118. Merits decision, November 24, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, 138 F.3d 1183, No. 97-3668. The appellate opinion issued March 13, 1998; rehearing was denied April 16. The matter was submitted for merits review.

The question is whether WRTL may pursue its pre-enforcement challenge to Wisconsin political-committee registration and enforcement provisions under Article III. The District Court found standing but abstained under Railroad Commission v. Pullman Co.; the Seventh Circuit vacated and directed jurisdictional dismissal. WRTL sought facial invalidity and nonenforcement, while the officials disputed credible threatened injury and the ability of relief against them to address independent private suits.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Seventh Circuit's judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the Court's opinion.

## Judgment & Remedy

Vacated and remanded, 7–2. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: Scalia and Thomas, who would affirm the direction to dismiss without prejudice for want of jurisdiction.

The remand requires consideration of the actual claims, threatened personal injury, proper defendants and proposed relief. It neither affirms the District Court's standing finding nor decides whether Pullman abstention was warranted. No Wisconsin provision is invalidated and no injunction issues.

## Holdings

### Credible personal injury and effective relief remain necessary

**Operative rule:** A speaker seeking pre-enforcement relief must establish concrete actual or imminent personal injury, fairly traceable to a defendant and likely to be redressed by the requested relief, with support appropriate to the litigation stage; a completed prosecution is unnecessary when an objectively credible threat burdens the speaker's intended expression. Self-censorship, a facial label or the existence of private enforcement provisions alone does not establish those requirements, and a threat from independent private parties must be connected to relief lawfully available against the defendants before the court.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice Opinion of the Court identified in Opinion Topology directly adopts this rule, its qualifications and the application requiring reconsideration; no aggregation of separate writings is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan supplies the personal-injury, causal and remedial requirements and the proof appropriate to each stage. Babbitt and Virginia v. American Booksellers permit supported pre-enforcement review without waiting for punishment. Maine Right to Life likewise recognizes recurring speech actually stopped by an effective rule's credible application, while retaining dismissal of an official who posed no supported threat. The speaker's decision to remain silent therefore does not answer whether these defendants threaten this speech.

The parties agree that WRTL's ordinary comparative guides and flyers do not require registration. No official has threatened WRTL or required it to register. Those facts weigh against the asserted official threat. The Board's Travis proceedings and private suits concern other speakers and cannot be treated as enforcement against WRTL. They nevertheless require consideration in assessing the actual claimed exposure; they are not erased by broad agreement about the governing speech principles. WRTL must identify its intended communications and the threatened burden.

Bennett requires a supported connection to the defendant's conduct; Pic-A-State permits meaningful partial relief without requiring removal of every independent barrier. Lee v. Harcleroad requires inquiry into an actually applicable duty and present burden. Together they require a defendant-specific assessment rather than either automatic standing or automatic defeat from the existence of an additional private impediment. The Court determines neither whether WRTL has standing nor whether persuasive effects on absent litigants could suffice in a materially different case.

### The actual facial claim must receive the correct jurisdictional inquiry

**Operative rule:** A court evaluating this facial invalidity and nonenforcement request may not dispose of the entire action solely by treating the requested relief as a command prescribing future state-court construction, or by treating predicted failure of an otherwise substantial constitutional claim as lack of jurisdiction. The Seventh Circuit must reconsider the actual injuries, defendants and available relief; contested merits and the District Court's abstention ruling remain for proper consideration only after the necessary Article III inquiry.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court directly adopts the rule, its application and the confined remand.

**Controlling explanation:** A declaration that an enforceable provision is invalid differs from a continuing federal instruction telling state tribunals how to decide hypothetical future cases. The former may remove an identified official constraint when a plaintiff establishes the required personal threat and remedial connection. The latter cannot be supplied merely because a speaker wants assurance about every future state interpretation. The appellate analysis acknowledged WRTL's facial attack but chiefly evaluated the latter kind of decree. It did not adequately resolve the actual request on its own terms.

Steel Co. distinguishes jurisdiction from success on a substantial federal claim, and NAACP requires an actual standing determination before contested election-law merits. A prediction that a narrowing construction would defeat the claim is not that determination. Conversely, the facial form of the claim supplies no missing threatened injury. Baker preserves the limits on a judgment's reach: a declaration against officials does not automatically bind absent candidates or state judges. The Court does not adopt the suggestion that invalidation would itself end all private suits.

The official assurances and independent private enforcement remain substantial objections on remand. They must be assessed against the actual communications, defendants and relief, rather than an assumed demand for universal judicial supervision. Ordinary further proceedings may resolve that inquiry; no new claim, waiver, discovery, amendment or hearing entitlement is created. The Court expresses no view on Wisconsin's constitutional validity, detailed statutory exceptions or the ultimate propriety of Pullman abstention.

## Precedent Treatment

- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife: applies the controlling personal-interest, traceability, redressability and stage-specific support rules; its noncontrolling absent-agency application is not adopted.
- Babbitt v. United Farm Workers: preserves pre-enforcement review of a credible personal threat without a completed prosecution.
- Virginia v. American Booksellers Ass'n: preserves supported pre-enforcement speech injury; the speech setting does not eliminate Article III requirements.
- Federal Election Commission v. Maine Right to Life Committee, Inc. (September 14, 1998): applies the distinction between a credible effective speech constraint and an unthreatening defendant. Its contextual express-advocacy holding remains governing and is not replaced by an exclusive verbal formula; its unresolved constitutional questions remain open.
- Bennett v. Spear: applies the need for a supported defendant-linked causal and remedial connection without demanding certainty about every later choice.
- Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno: preserves meaningful partial relief from an actual constraint; not every independent barrier must disappear.
- Lee v. Harcleroad: applies the actual-duty, present-burden and proper-defendant inquiry without finding jurisdiction or ordering an automatic hearing.
- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.: applies the distinction between Article III power and disputed merits; no exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues is prescribed.
- NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones (November 5, 1998): applies its requirement of actual standing adjudication before contested election-law merits.
- Baker v. General Motors Corp.: preserves lawful judgment reach and the requirements for binding absent persons; facial relief is not universal preclusion.
- Railroad Commission v. Pullman Co.: leaves its distinct abstention inquiry available for proper consideration after jurisdiction is addressed; its application here is not decided.

## Law After Decision

Pre-enforcement speech challenges remain subject to the existing concrete-injury, causation and redressability requirements. This decision requires the facial invalidity and nonenforcement claims actually presented to be assessed separately from a demand to control all future state adjudication. Wisconsin's provisions remain uninvalidated, and the Court has neither found standing nor rejected every possible basis for it. Official enforcement and independent private enforcement retain their distinct jurisdictional and remedial questions.

## Separate Writings

Scalia dissents, joined by Thomas. They accept that a credible threatened injury may support review before prosecution and that a facial invalidity claim differs from an advisory instruction about future litigation. They would nevertheless affirm dismissal because the existing official and private-enforcement facts already defeat this action. The officials agree that WRTL's comparative guides do not require registration, and no threat directed at WRTL is shown. Proceedings involving different speakers do not supply the missing official connection. Independent private plaintiffs and state judges are absent, so an injunction against the named officials cannot simply stop their actions.

The dissent distinguishes Maine's effective rule credibly reaching ceased speech and Bennett's announced governmental action affecting the plaintiffs. NAACP corrected a court's bypass of disputed standing to reach merits; it does not require another inquiry when the defendant-specific defect has already been addressed. A facial prayer cannot create that missing connection, and hoped-for persuasive effect against nonparties does not itself supply likely redress here. The dissent adopts no universal prosecution prerequisite, no rule that every facial challenge is advisory and no conclusion about Wisconsin's constitutional merits. It would affirm jurisdictional dismissal without prejudice.

No injunction, statutory invalidation, state-registration command or direction governing an unpresented state case issues. Further proceedings below remain open; no additional Supreme Court proceeding or deadline is set.

## Event

AT&T Universal Card Services v. Rembert, No. 98-352. Merits decision, December 1, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, 141 F.3d 277. The appellate opinion issued April 9, 1998; rehearing was denied May 29. The matter was submitted for merits review.

The question is when an insolvent debtor's credit-card debt is nondischargeable for actual fraud under 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(2)(A), including what card use communicates about repayment. The bankruptcy court found the debts nondischargeable; the District Court reversed, and the Sixth Circuit affirmed that reversal. AT&T seeks reinstatement of nondischargeability. The Court reviews only the matter brought by AT&T; the common lower record does not establish a separate Citibank proceeding here.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Sixth Circuit concerning AT&T's debt is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The presented actual-fraud exception fails for lack of the required contemporaneous deceptive-intent proof. The Court neither reinstates the bankruptcy nondischargeability judgment nor directs a new fraud hearing. It makes no unnecessary finding about AT&T's reliance, causation or the amount of a nondischargeable debt and awards no new fees.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Unanimous Court | Both controlling propositions, their application, limits and affirmance. |

All nine Justices join the opinion in full. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### Card use communicates an intent to repay, not a warranty of solvency

**Operative rule:** When a cardholder obtains an advance by using the card, she communicates a present intention to repay the resulting debt, not a warranty of current solvency or an objectively reasonable prospect of repayment; on the actual-fraud theory presented, the creditor must prove by a preponderance that the professed contemporaneous intention was false and deceptive. Financial condition, losses, borrowing patterns and other circumstances may establish that state of mind, but inability to pay or unreasonable optimism alone cannot substitute for it, and the debtor's denial is not conclusive.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified in Opinion Topology directly adopts the rule, its evidentiary qualifications and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 523(a)(2)(A) excepts debt obtained through actual fraud; it does not equate financially reckless borrowing with deceit. Field v. Mans supplies the established common-law content of actual fraud, while Grogan v. Garner supplies the ordinary preponderance burden. A promise concerns the debtor's present intention to perform. Use of revolving credit does not itself assert that the borrower has the money now or that a projected source of repayment is objectively sound.

AT&T correctly observes that a dishonest intention can be inferred from conduct. Repeated advances, severe financial distress and mounting gambling losses can be persuasive circumstantial evidence. The distinction is between using those facts to find actual deceit and making financial improbability a substitute for that finding. A debtor may honestly intend repayment through an irrational plan, and an assertion of hope may also be disbelieved on a supported record. Neither conclusion follows categorically from gambling or insolvency.

No confession, mandatory factor count, heightened burden or additional plan to file bankruptcy is required. Nor does repayment create a safe harbor for a deliberately false promise. The Court decides the presented actual-fraud theory, not every false-pretense or representation-only theory. Field's independent actual-and-justifiable-reliance requirements and the statutory causal connection remain necessary when otherwise material; resolving intent does not eliminate them.

### The record supports affirmance without a new fraud inquiry

**Operative rule:** A finding of fraudulent repayment intent cannot stand on an objective inability-to-pay substitute for the subjective inquiry; factual findings remain subject to clear-error review and the governing legal standard to independent review. Here the reported bankruptcy explanation rests on that erroneous substitute, while substantial actual repayments corroborate the intent to repay and no independent contrary contemporaneous-intent finding is identified, so the Sixth Circuit's dischargeability judgment concerning AT&T is affirmed without further factfinding.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this record-specific application and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Rembert used casino advances while her income was reduced and testified that she expected winnings to fund repayment. Her evidence was not testimony alone: she obtained second-mortgage funds, paid identified balances and made substantial later payments. The published appellate account identifies no independent evidence that her repayment intention was false when the advances were taken. The bankruptcy explanation instead inferred lack of intent from the objective unreality of her gambling expectations.

That reasoning does not become a valid subjective finding merely because it uses the word intent. Nor does recognizing its defect authorize this Court to reassess demeanor or discard a supported independent credibility finding. The actual stated premise and reported record support the intermediate courts' rejection of nondischargeability under the ordinary standards of review.

Field requires application of the proper standard where a material issue remains unresolved. It does not require a new hearing whenever a trial court has applied an incorrect rule. Here the intermediate courts have already corrected the objective substitute against the existing repayment evidence, and no unresolved independent contrary intent finding requires another trial-level determination. A remand to seek a new ground for the creditor's failed proof is unwarranted. The Court does not decide whether AT&T actually or justifiably relied, whether a debt was obtained by another proved fraud, or what amount would be excluded from discharge if fraud had been established.

## Precedent Treatment

- Field v. Mans (November 28, 1995): applies its common-law actual-fraud framework and preserves independent actual and recipient-specific justifiable reliance. Its correct-standard remand rule applies when a material application remains unresolved; it does not compel a redundant remand on this record. The prior reliance holding is not treated as an earlier universal decision about cardholder intent.
- Grogan v. Garner, 498 U.S. 279: applies the creditor's preponderance burden without raising it or requiring an admission of deceit.
- In re Cohen (June 30, 1997): preserves the requirement of a qualifying fraudulent acquisition before its connected-liability rule can operate. That debt-extent rule supplies no missing fraud predicate here.

## Law After Decision

The presented card-use actual-fraud theory requires proof of contemporaneous deceptive repayment intent, which may be circumstantial but is not established as a matter of law by insolvency, gambling losses or unrealistic expectations. The decision creates no gambling exemption and no rule requiring acceptance of a debtor's claimed intent.

Field's separate reliance requirements remain intact: justifiability depends on the recipient's knowledge, capacities and circumstances, without a universal credit-check or periodic-investigation duty. Reliance on falsity the recipient knows or that is obvious to that recipient is not justifiable; a warning actually encountered may require inquiry under that recipient-specific standard. Card issuance, continued authorization or nonrevocation does not automatically establish actual or justifiable reliance. None of those matters is factually decided here. Causation and debt extent remain separate as well.

Section 523(a)(2)(A) excludes statements respecting the financial condition of the debtor or an insider from its coverage. Subsection (B) separately requires use of a materially false written statement respecting that financial condition, reasonable reliance by the creditor, and the debtor's causing the statement to be made or published with intent to deceive. Those distinct conditions remain intact and are not adjudicated on this card-use actual-fraud theory. Different false-pretense or representation-only theories, the recent-debt presumption under §523(a)(2)(C), and a fee claim under §523(d) likewise remain undecided.

## Holdings

### I. Each respondent has a personal interest in the shared domestic privacy

**Operative rule:** Invited occupants who use a resident's deliberately screened home for a private activity possess their own Fourth Amendment interest against police acquisition of interior information; brief duration, commercial purpose and illegality, separately or together, do not automatically defeat that interest. Carter and Johns each have that interest on the actual host-authorized joint occupancy here; permission merely to enter, another person's rights or a desire to conceal crime alone does not establish it.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The proposition controls despite their disagreement over the ultimate disposition. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Rakas requires a person's own protected interest and rejects a claim derived solely from someone else's rights. Jones, as preserved by Rakas, shows that ownership is unnecessary. Olson recognizes privacy shared with a guest. Fields applies those principles both to independent control and, separately, to expressly permitted sharing of domestic privacy without an overnight arrangement. Its protection of Crawley's recurring closed-room use did not make recurrence indispensable to every shared-privacy claim.

The resident deliberately admitted Carter and Johns to the screened living interior, joined their private undertaking and allowed that use for approximately two and one-half hours. Their interest rests on that actual sharing, not her title alone. The State stresses the brief compensated transaction and absence of an established prior relationship or continuing control. Those facts bear on the relationship, but do not erase the personally shared private occupancy actually shown. Payment informs the arrangement without turning the domestic interior into a public business area. Criminal activity neither supplies the interest nor forfeits one otherwise established.

Neither respondent needs an accepted key finding for this application. Carter's disputed proffer is not adjudicated and is not attributed to Johns. The Court does not protect every casual visitor, unauthorized occupant, person permitted merely to enter, or activity plainly exposed to ordinary public observation. Recognition of personal privacy leaves the distinct question whether the officer's particular observation invaded it.

**Precedent treatment:** Rakas retains the personal-right requirement and exclusion of mere presence. Jones remains relevant to protected use without ownership. Olson's shared-home principle is applied beyond its overnight facts through Fields. Fields's relationship inquiry extends beyond Crawley's recurring use to the particular host-authorized sharing here; its independent-control branch and rejection of automatic protection for every visitor remain intact.

### II. Conditional suppression rules remain distinct from the observation question

**Operative rule:** If an antecedent intrusion is held unlawful in properly available further proceedings, a later warrant is an independent source only when both the decision to seek it and its issuance are genuinely independent of the illegality. A warrant's arrival does not retroactively authorize the earlier intrusion, and consent, attenuation, inevitable discovery and other asserted grounds require their own factual and procedural predicates; neither automatic exclusion nor automatic admission follows.

**Authority:** Part III of Breyer's opinion has all eight Associate Justices' direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional remedial rationale. These conditional rules govern the remand if their triggering conditions arise; they do not find that this observation was unlawful or that any particular later item must be excluded.

**Controlling explanation:** Fields and Murray require genuine independence of both the decision to seek a warrant and its issuance. Karo preserves a supported untainted-warrant ground; Pinson and Myers separate correction of an antecedent search premise from final exclusion and from revival of unavailable arguments. The trial memorandum here treated the observation as central to the stop and warrants. Its rejection of the particular Franks attack on descriptions of the investigation and surveillance does not establish an independent source.

The stop, door opening, handgun discovery, arrests, apartment and vehicle warrants, physical evidence and statements require their actual personal-right and causal inquiries. The prosecutor's concession that an invalid arrest would bring down statements and physical evidence retains that condition. It does not concede that every defect in the observation automatically excludes every later item for both respondents. The ninety-minute surveillance gap is not erased.

Consent requires actual authority, scope, voluntariness and any applicable taint analysis. Leon affords no protection for knowing or reckless deception, abandoned judicial neutrality, an affidavit wholly lacking reasonable probable-cause indicia or a facially deficient warrant; the conduct of obtaining and supplying officers and proper execution scope matter. Evans remains confined to qualifying court-clerical error with objectively reasonable reliance, not a general exception for deliberate police investigation. Attenuation and inevitable discovery need their actual predicates. Ordinary preservation rules remain operative, and no new statement, Miranda or Franks claim is created.

The Court orders neither an evidence-by-evidence exclusion nor unconditional admission or reinstatement. The remaining claims may be resolved only to the extent properly available under ordinary procedure.

**Precedent treatment:** Fields and Murray's genuine-independence rule is applied without expansion. Karo's supported untainted-warrant possibility remains available. Pinson and Myers retain the distinction between antecedent error and final suppression. Leon and Evans retain their stated institutional, factual and procedural limits; Franks's false-affidavit inquiry does not substitute for independent source.

## Event

Equality Foundation of Greater Cincinnati, Inc. v. City of Cincinnati; Sixth Circuit Nos. 94-3855 and 94-3973 (merits), No. 94-4280 (fees). Merits decision, December 3, 1998. On review of the Sixth Circuit's October 23, 1997 judgment, 128 F.3d 289, reversing the District Court, vacating its permanent injunction and fee award, and directing judgment for defendants.

The question is whether Cincinnati Charter Article XII's withdrawal and prospective prohibition of municipal sexual-orientation protection satisfies equal protection under Romer, and what follows for the injunction and fees. The charter provision followed ordinances covering City employment and, separately, private employment, housing and public accommodations. Article XII provides:

> NO SPECIAL CLASS STATUS MAY BE GRANTED BASED UPON SEXUAL ORIENTATION, CONDUCT OR RELATIONSHIPS.

## Holdings

### The municipal class-specific foreclosure fails ordinary rational-basis review

**Operative rule:** Article XII's combined withdrawal of existing sexual-orientation nondiscrimination protection and prospective prohibition of ordinary municipal protection for the named class violates equal protection because its actual breadth bears no rational relationship to the asserted fiscal, associational, regulatory and local-policy interests. Municipal scope, continued general-law remedies and access to higher government do not cure that unexplained selective disability; the rule does not make every repeal unlawful or require the City to enact a particular protection.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice Opinion of the Court identified in Opinion Topology directly adopts the ordinary rationality rule, application and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Romer requires a rational connection between legitimate objectives and the actual class-specific disadvantage. Heller's separate holdings preserve that connection while allowing reasonable generalizations, conceivable grounds and imperfect categories without formal findings, empirical certainty or least restrictive means. Moreno and Cleburne forbid bare harm and irrational prejudice; no finding about every voter's private motive is required.

Local fiscal management, regulatory restraint, private association and durable policy are legitimate subjects. Yet Article XII reaches the City's own employment and ordinary protection across housing, public accommodations and private employment, regardless of a particular protection's expense or operation. Avoiding some complaint and defense costs does not explain why this class alone becomes prospectively ineligible for all that ordinary protection. Charter authority explains who sets policy and its durability, not the missing rational connection to the full selective burden.

Hurley protects actual private expression; it does not make impersonal services or the City's own conduct categorically expressive association. Those distinct concerns cannot justify this entire public and private reach. Bowers's specified-conduct holding likewise does not explain the broader disability based on orientation or status. No particular expression or religious exemption is adjudicated and Bowers is not overruled.

The City emphasizes that Ohio institutions and general laws remain accessible. Romer already assumed generally applicable protection remained. The relevant breadth is the civic activity excluded from ordinary protection, not simply the number of governmental levels affected. Ordinary repeal remains permissible; the reported concession about repeal is not assent to prospective charter foreclosure. Coalition's distinct racial political-process inquiry supplies neither a heightened orientation standard nor a universal entitlement to favorable legislation. This decision applies ordinary rationality to Article XII's own operation.

### Equitable protection must address the discriminatory barrier without writing municipal law

**Operative rule:** The appellate direction to enter judgment for defendants cannot stand, and declaratory and injunctive protection must be restored against Article XII's unconstitutional ordinary-protection foreclosure. The lower courts must conform the injunction to that common operation and any genuinely independent lawful remainder under applicable severability law, without rewriting the charter federally, imposing a nondiscrimination code or reopening the resolved rational-basis merits.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court directly adopts this remedial holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Removing the barrier returns ordinary lawmaking to its lawful operation; it does not require the City to adopt every requested remedy or prevent a lawful policy revision. Romer's relief followed the violation and an authoritative Colorado inseparability determination. This record supplies no corresponding final Ohio severability determination or established autonomous preference-only remainder. The Court therefore neither performs textual severance nor declares every conceivable separate quota restriction invalid.

The remand concerns fitting the injunction to the decided constitutional defect and respecting any genuinely independent lawful operation under the applicable state rule. It is not permission to continue the invalid common foreclosure or to require a new rationality showing from the plaintiffs. ## Event

Knowles v. Iowa, No. 97-7597. Merits decision, December 8, 1998. Argued November 3, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Iowa Supreme Court, 569 N.W.2d 601 (1997), which sustained the search and Knowles's conviction under a state rule authorizing a full search after issuance of a citation.

## Holdings

### I. A citation does not trigger the full custodial-arrest search exception

**Operative rule:** Issuing a traffic citation without a custodial arrest does not authorize the full search incident to arrest; unexercised authority to make an arrest cannot substitute for the custody that triggers that exception. A full search requires an independently applicable recognized basis, and the citation, general roadside risk and observed speeding supply none for this search; supported exit orders, protective measures, consent searches and searches based on independent probable cause retain their own conditions and scope.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support. Part II's ordinary-remedial-law return has the eight Associates' support; Stone-Zsela would require the specific suppression stated in his concurrence.

**Controlling explanation:** Chimel ties arrest-related search to officer protection and preservation of evidence. Robinson permits a categorical full search after a qualifying lawful custodial arrest without a new necessity showing in every case. Belton addresses the automobile setting of such an arrest; none converts every citation into custody.

The officer chose to issue the speeding citation before searching. Transport and station-custody risks had not arisen, and the observed speeding offense supplied no further evidence ordinarily to be preserved by a full automobile search. The Court does not invent a later arrest to validate the intrusion retrospectively. State permission cannot by itself establish Fourth Amendment reasonableness.

Iowa correctly emphasizes that danger can remain at roadside and that the officer had authority to arrest. Those considerations may support recognized limited precautions, not transfer the full arrest-search category to an encounter lacking its predicate. Whren preserves independent limits on an intrusion beyond the initial stop; Robinette distinguishes lawful traffic business from later detention or consent. Maryland v. Wilson's limited exit-direction authority is not automatic frisk or search authority. Ricci likewise distinguishes enforcement power from justification for an added restraint without deciding a general prohibition on arrest for this offense.

A Terry frisk requires supported reasonable suspicion that the person is armed and dangerous. A Michigan v. Long protective vehicle search requires its own reasonable belief, based on specific facts, that the person is dangerous and may gain immediate control of weapons, and remains limited to areas where a weapon may be hidden. Voluntary consent and the automobile exception retain their independent predicates. None supplies an established justification for this search. The Court neither assumes every stop safe nor imposes least restrictive means, and it leaves Robinson's rule intact for actual qualifying custody.

### II. The dependent judgment returns under the corrected constitutional rule

**Operative rule:** The citation-search theory cannot sustain this search or the dependent judgment, which is reversed and remanded for proceedings under the corrected constitutional rule and ordinary, properly available remedial law. No alternative justification is established here, and the Court orders no acquittal, release, replacement sentence, civil relief or universal exclusion rule for traffic encounters.

**Authority:** Part II has the eight Associate Justices' direct support. Stone-Zsela joins reversal and remand but would add the express suppression direction stated in his separate opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The question decided concerns the asserted authority for this search. Rejecting that authority removes the ground sustaining the judgment and requires reversal. The receiving court must apply that determination in further proceedings; it cannot sustain the search merely by repeating the citation rationale. The Court establishes no alternative search ground or new remedial exception and makes no automatic disposition of every evidence item. Stone-Zsela would give a more specific instruction to suppress the fruits and proceed on the remaining admissible record. His additional direction is not part of the eight-Justice remand formulation.

## Precedent Treatment

- Chimel v. California: applies the officer-safety and evidence-preservation purposes of arrest-incident authority; actual custody matters to the exception's reach.
- United States v. Robinson: preserves the categorical full-search rule after a qualifying lawful custodial arrest; no individualized necessity requirement is added to that setting.
- New York v. Belton: distinguishes the vehicle search incident to arrest from a full search after citation alone.
- Whren v. United States (June 10, 1996): preserves objective justification for the ordinary initial stop and independent limits on later search, scope and duration.
- Ohio v. Robinette (November 18, 1996): preserves the distinction between traffic business, subsequent restraint, voluntary consent and any prior illegality.
- Maryland v. Wilson (February 19, 1997): preserves the limited passenger-exit authority without creating automatic frisk or search power.

- Terry v. Ohio: preserves the limited protective frisk on its own supported danger conditions.
- Michigan v. Long: preserves a limited protective vehicle search on its distinct danger, weapons-access and scope predicates.

## Law After Decision

An issued citation and unused arrest authority do not trigger the full arrest-incident search exception. Actual lawful custodial arrests retain Robinson's categorical rule, while independent protective, consent and probable-cause search grounds retain their own requirements. Iowa's authorization cannot sustain this search on the citation theory, but the decision does not invalidate every application involving an independently constitutional search or establish general roadside arrest immunity.

On that established scope, he would not leave treatment of its fruits to a general remand formulation.

His concurrence concerns this search's fruits, not a universal remedy for every traffic encounter. He would not order acquittal, assumes no civil liability and supplies no new holding limiting searches after actual lawful custodial arrest.

The citation-search rationale no longer supports the search or dependent judgment; no alternative justification is established by this decision. ## Event

McClellan Federal Credit Union v. Parker, No. 98-618. Merits decision, December 9, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 139 F.3d 668, decided March 17, 1998. The matter was submitted for merits review. The question is whether a Chapter 7 debtor may continue payments and lawfully retain collateral without mandatory reaffirmation or redemption under §521(2).

Parker, unrepresented, signed a proposed reaffirmation of his $9,977.56 car loan and $1,500 of unsecured card debt in exchange for reduced monthly car payments and reinstatement as a member in good standing upon discharge and an agreement to disregard the debt in future loan applications only if the secured account is paid in full on time. The bankruptcy court refused approval and discharge followed. The BAP dismissed the creditor's appeal for lack of standing; the Ninth Circuit recognized its pecuniary stake and affirmed the refusal of approval on the merits.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit's judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Refusal of this proposed reaffirmation stands. Section 521(2) does not itself compel renewed personal liability or forfeiture of otherwise lawfully retained collateral. The discharge and valid lien retain their separate consequences; no new default finding, foreclosure, repossession or substitute agreement is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Unanimous Court | All three controlling propositions, their qualifications, application and affirmance. |

All nine join the opinion in full. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### The lender's pecuniary stake permits appellate review

**Operative rule:** The credit union is sufficiently pecuniarily aggrieved to appeal refusal of this proposed reaffirmation because approval would make the proposed personal car-loan and $1,500 unsecured obligations enforceable. That appellate stake permits review but does not establish a right to approval or remove §524's conditions.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this threshold proposition and its limited consequence.

**Controlling explanation:** Refusal affects the lender's proposed enforceable claims, not merely its abstract preference about bankruptcy administration. The Ninth Circuit therefore correctly rejected the BAP's standing ground and examined the bankruptcy court's ruling. The creditor's interest in obtaining approval and the legal conditions for obtaining it are different questions. Recognizing the former supplies no missing finding of the debtor's best interest or absence of undue hardship.

### Mandatory notice and performance do not create an exclusive retention election

**Operative rule:** For the scheduled consumer debts secured by estate property covered by the then-effective §521(2), the debtor must file the statement of retention or surrender within thirty days after filing or by the creditors' meeting, whichever is earlier, with a for-cause extension fixed within that period; the statement must specify exemption, redemption or reaffirmation when applicable, and the debtor must perform the lawful stated intention within forty-five days after that notice, with a for-cause extension fixed within those forty-five days. Read with (C)'s preservation of the debtor's and trustee's rights, those mandatory duties do not themselves make surrender, redemption or reaffirmation the exclusive substantive options: a debtor may maintain payments and retain collateral insofar as existing law and the enforceable agreement permit, without stripping the lien, excusing an actual lawful default or obtaining judicially reduced installment redemption.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the complete statutory rule, timing qualifications and retained-rights limitation.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted duties disclose the debtor's lawful intention and require timely performance. Their mandatory character does not establish an additional consequence that the statute does not state. The qualification concerning applicable elections and subparagraph (C)'s nonalteration language must operate alongside the list. Section 524's notice that reaffirmation is not required, and its independent approval safeguards, reinforce the distinction between disclosure and a command to create enforceable personal liability.

The creditor argues that the list otherwise lacks force and that possession without personal liability imposes an involuntary new bargain. Notice and performance retain their stated work, while the creditor retains the lien and remedies for a legally established default. Personal discharge and survival of a property claim are distinct, as Johnson and Dewsnup recognize. Dewsnup's particular Chapter 7 lien rule does not add a compulsory election here. Nobelman's Chapter 13 principal-residence protection likewise turns on its own enacted rights clause, not a universal requirement of continued personal liability for every retained asset. Ron Pair requires attention to this provision's actual text; Butner preserves state-defined interests subject to the Code's specified consequences.

The Court does not construe an unseen bankruptcy-default, insurance or impairment clause or hold that every such clause is invalid. Continued possession remains subject to the actual lawful agreement, applicable default rules, the valid lien and the Code's independent stay and discharge provisions. Section 521(2) alone supplies no new acceleration or forfeiture command.

### This agreement remains subject to independent debtor-protection approval

**Operative rule:** For this unrepresented individual, §524(c)(6)(A) requires judicial approval that the reaffirmation both does not impose undue hardship on the debtor or a dependent of the debtor and is in the debtor's best interest; (6)(B)'s exception for consumer debt secured by real property does not cover this car and unsecured card debt. Signing the agreement does not satisfy those distinct conditions, and on the reviewed continued-payment-retention premise refusal of this agreement was within the bankruptcy court's discretion.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the approval rule and the agreement-specific affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The proposed agreement renewed personal liability for the car loan and added $1,500 in otherwise unsecured debt. The reduction in monthly car payments from $308.73 to $280, reduction of the unsecured amount, and reinstatement as a member in good standing upon discharge, and the agreement to disregard the debt in future loan applications only if the secured account is paid in full on time were benefits to consider; they did not compel approval or guarantee a future loan. Because lawful retention did not itself require reaffirmation, the court could assess those benefits against the renewed obligations rather than treat signing as the necessary price of keeping the vehicle.

The no-undue-hardship and best-interest inquiries are separate requirements. The lender's appellate standing does not displace either, and mutual assent does not substitute for the approval required for this unrepresented debtor. This decision concerns approval under §524(c)(6), not compliance with the other enforceability conditions in §524(c) and (d). Section 524(f) permits voluntary repayment, which does not restore enforceable discharged personal liability.

The affirmance concerns this agreement and this reviewed statutory premise. It does not presume every reaffirmation unwise, force the lender to extend credit, reduce the debt by judicial valuation or approve a replacement bargain. Ordinary lien enforcement remains subject to its actual legal prerequisites.

## Holdings

### I. Chapman governs this available capital-selection misinformation claim

**Holding and operative rule:** For a constitutional trial error in the commutation instruction affecting this jury's choice between death and life without parole, when the claim is properly available on federal habeas, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt from the whole penalty record that the error did not contribute to the actual death sentence. If an available constitutional violation fails that showing, conditional sentence relief must permit a reasonable opportunity for a lawful new penalty proceeding; a proper harmlessness showing permits denial of relief.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Ginsburg disagrees with the remand disposition but directly joins this rule and its conditions. No combination of different standards establishes its authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that uncertainty about this constitutional error's contribution to the jury's punishment choice remains with the State. Brecht retained Chapman for preserved protected-silence error, and O'Neal and Roy extended it to the specified personal-intent category. Those decisions did not already settle every other habeas error. The present instruction's description of the sole nondeath alternative bears directly on the reliability of capital selection and the jury's treatment of actual mitigation. That relationship justifies the same noncontribution burden here.

Collateral finality and respect for state judgments remain substantial, but they do not supply a sufficient reason to lower the constitutional burden for this category. Independent habeas requirements continue to operate, and Chapman permits a harmless sentence to stand. The inquiry concerns this jury's actual decision, not whether a properly instructed hypothetical jury could have imposed death. The reviewing court must consider the complete charge, mitigation, prosecution theory and supported jury requests. A direction to disregard commutation may matter; it does not itself prove that the earlier misinformation made no contribution.

The Court assumes the constitutional error found below to resolve the presented effect question. It neither newly decides the unpresented accuracy rule nor treats every capital instruction as structural. The rule is confined to this defined capital-selection error and does not settle every remaining constitutional-habeas category. A final writ requires an available established violation and failure of the State's showing, not merely an appellate method error.

**Precedent treatment:** Brecht, O'Neal and Roy are extended to this defined capital-selection category while their prior holdings and reservations otherwise remain. Chapman retains the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden; Kotteakos is not substituted for that burden here.

### II. The required effect inquiry is substantive, not a verbal formula

**Holding and operative rule:** Identifying this instructional error under Boyde does not by itself establish the effect necessary for relief under the applicable harmlessness rule. A court may perform that inquiry within its existing discussion without prescribed words, a particular citation or a separate heading, but its actual reasoning must assess the error's relationship to the sentence under the governing burden and treatment of uncertainty.

**Authority:** Part III of the per curiam opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices. That agreement concerns the governing distinction and record boundaries; it does not resolve their disagreement about the sufficiency of the analysis in this case.

**Controlling explanation:** Boyde asks whether an instruction was reasonably likely to operate in a constitutionally impermissible way. The consequence of that error remains a distinct inquiry here. Sochor requires an actual constitutional cure rather than a label, while rejecting any indispensable verbal formula. Its principle informs the inquiry without converting this commutation instruction into an invalid aggravating factor or authorizing a federal court to select a sentence through fresh weighing.

The District Court examined the actual prosecution argument, aggravation, mitigation, conviction-document request and record as a whole. It also explained why the corrective direction did not cure the misinformation. The Ninth Circuit expressly received the State's effect objection. It would therefore be incorrect to describe these courts as having ignored the record or the issue. Conversely, discussing the record while identifying likely interference with mitigation does not automatically establish the separate causal conclusion the selected harmlessness rule requires.

The trial evidence must remain distinct from mitigation first developed during habeas. New's excluded account of deliberations supplies no finding of actual release discussion. The jury's request for conviction documents and its separate general instruction rereading do not establish a specific commutation inquiry. The trial judge's automatic-review findings are not jury findings on individual mitigators. Supported inferences remain available, but neither strong aggravation nor an imagined jury's preferred sentence can substitute for the required actual-effect determination. The Court orders no new admission ruling or factual hearing.

**Precedent treatment:** Boyde retains its error-identification function. Sochor's substantive-correction principle is applied without transferring its distinct invalid-aggravator facts. Roy's actual-verdict approach informs the inquiry without requiring factor-specific jury findings or supplying an appellate substitute sentence.

### III. The limited vacatur requires renewed review without deciding harmlessness

**Holding and operative rule:** The commutation-based appellate sentence disposition is vacated and returned for a genuine whole-record inquiry into the error's effect under the governing rule. The receiving court may reach the same sentence-relief result, but neither the constitutional defect alone nor a failure to use a particular formula decides the inquiry; guilt, genuinely independent habeas conditions and the actual appellate status of other penalty claims remain undisturbed.

**Authority:** Part IV and the judgment have Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. These seven adopt the confined return without a common join in one harmlessness standard. The five direct joins in Part II independently establish Chapman as the rule governing further proceedings; no fractured-opinion synthesis is used.

**Controlling explanation:** The District Court identified a concrete mechanism: overstating the possibility of release could diminish the mitigating force of testimony explaining violent behavior, particularly when prior convictions were prominent yet also restricted commutation. That analysis is substantial. Its ultimate likely-preclusion and potential-diversion account, however, did not complete the required assessment against the corrective charge, the actual institutional-danger argument and the limits of what the conviction request establishes. The appellate court then answered the effect objection with Boyde's reasonable-likelihood formulation.

The Court returns that unfinished causal task rather than demanding another label. The receiving court must consider the entire appropriate trial record, including the State's substantial aggravation and prison-safety case, the broader danger language described below, the mitigation actually heard and the complete instructions. It must determine the error's effect without presuming subjective deliberations or treating the court's own sentencing preference as the jury's verdict. Neither the corrective direction nor the strength of aggravation is conclusive by itself.

No finding that the error was harmless, or that the State has already failed its governing burden, issues now. The court may retain sentence relief after the proper inquiry. No new evidentiary hearing, admission of excluded material, execution permission, release order or fixed sentencing deadline follows. Other penalty claims were addressed below but not reached on appeal; this disposition neither decides them nor revives an abandoned claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Roy's distinction between an actual method defect and an absent citation supports the limited return; it does not supply a result on this record. Brecht and O'Neal preserve the distinction between corrective remand and conditional relief after an available violation fails the applicable showing. Simmons's different express parole question is not imported.

### IV. Carriger supplies the former-law rule for this pending capital application

**Holding and operative rule:** Because this capital habeas application was pending before April 24, 1996, and chapter 154 qualification is not established, amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)'s replacement factual presumption do not govern. Former §2254(d) governs that factual inquiry: its presumption requires a historical fact determination after a merits hearing by a competent state court in a proceeding involving the applicant and the State or its officer or agent, evidenced by a written finding, opinion or other reliable and adequate written indicia; paragraph (8) requires examination of whether the pertinent produced state record as a whole fairly supports the finding.

**Authority:** Part V of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. This is application of Carriger's existing controlling rule; the Chief Justice supplies no additional temporal-law ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Carriger extended Lindh's identified application-date construction to a capital application already pending at enactment outside established chapter 154 qualification. The reported 1989 filing and continuing amended proceeding place this case within that rule. Capital status does not establish chapter 154 qualification, and the later judgment date does not convert this into a newly filed application.

The legal harmlessness inquiry remains distinct from the proper treatment of historical state findings. Former §2254(d) protects findings satisfying its predicates while retaining paragraph (8)'s whole-record qualification; neither unrestricted appellate factfinding nor conclusive deference follows. Separately directed provisions retain their own operation. The Court does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA amendment, decide §2254(e)(2)'s temporal reach or establish an entitlement to new evidence or a hearing.

**Precedent treatment:** Carriger's April 16, 1998 pending-capital holding is applied without change. Lindh's noncapital holding and preserved statutory exceptions remain intact.

## Event

Haddle v. Garrison, No. 97-1472. Merits decision, December 14, 1998. Argued November 10, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, 132 F.3d 46, which affirmed dismissal of Haddle's §1985(2) claim because he lacked a constitutionally protected property interest in at-will employment.

Haddle alleged that officers of his employer conspired to procure his discharge because he cooperated with a federal grand jury and was expected to testify at a federal criminal trial. The question is whether the alleged loss of employment and wages can constitute injury to person or property under the statute despite his at-will status. The allegations are considered at the pleading stage, not found true after trial.

## Holdings

### Loss of at-will employment may be the injury caused by a forbidden federal-witness conspiracy

**Operative rule:** When two or more persons conspire to deter a party or witness by force, intimidation or threat from attending federal court or testifying there freely, fully and truthfully, or to injure a party or witness for having attended or testified, loss of at-will employment and wages caused by acts furthering that conspiracy may constitute injury to person or property under §1985's civil-remedy clause without a constitutional entitlement to continued employment. Haddle's allegations of concrete employment loss cannot be dismissed merely because the employment was terminable at will; the prohibited conspiracy and object, required federal-witness nexus, acts, causation, recoverable damages and properly presented defenses remain to be adjudicated.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this proposition, its application to the categorical ground of dismissal and the bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute protects federal judicial proceedings from the specified conspiratorial intimidation and retaliation. Its civil-remedy language distinguishes injury to person or property from deprivation of a federal right or privilege. Requiring a separate constitutional employment entitlement for the injury branch would collapse that distinction. Kush v. Rutledge explains why the federal-process clauses must be read on their own terms rather than importing the equal-protection-purpose conditions of different clauses; Kush did not itself decide this damages question.

At-will status defines the employer's ordinary authority to terminate the relationship. It does not make the employee's existing work and wages valueless against prohibited interference. Truax v. Raich recognizes that distinction. Roth's constitutional-property rule concerns an entitlement protected against deprivation without due process, a different inquiry from injury caused by this statutory conspiracy. Darden's attention to common-law context supports reading the employment interest in its actual legal setting, without importing its separate employee-classification factors.

Respondents' argument correctly denies guaranteed employment but draws the wrong consequence for the alleged economic harm. Bray requires attention to the particular §1985 clause and does not make its distinct private-right requirements govern this federal-witness injury branch. Robinson and Inter-Modal likewise distinguish protection against conduct forbidden by a statute from a permanent right to the underlying job or benefit. Haddle alleges lost employment and wages from the forbidden object, which defeats this categorical pleading objection.

The Court does not find that the alleged conspiracy occurred or that these corporate agents are legally independent third parties capable of conspiring. It decides neither every witness-definition question nor damages amount, reinstatement or punitive relief. The statutory object, causation and other applicable requirements prevent this holding from becoming a federal remedy for every discharge.

## Event

NYNEX Corp. v. Discon, Inc., No. 96-1570. Merits decision, December 14, 1998. Argued October 5, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, 93 F.3d 1055, decided August 26, 1996. ## Holdings

### This vertical supplier substitution is not a per se group boycott

**Operative rule:** A single buyer's agreement to purchase services from one supplier instead of another, without horizontal concerted exclusion or another established per se basis, is not a per se group boycott merely because it intentionally injures a rival, serves a fraudulent purpose or lacks an apparent efficiency justification. The pleaded vertical purchasing arrangement must therefore be assessed under the rule of reason on the surviving §1 competitive-effects theory; genuine horizontal restraints, actual price fixing and other established categories retain their distinct requirements.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this classification and its limitations.

**Controlling explanation:** Per se treatment dispenses with inquiry into actual competitive effects only for a sufficiently established class of restraints. Klor's involved coordinated exclusion of a rival through a structure supporting that treatment. Business Electronics, Northwest Wholesale Stationers and Indiana Federation of Dentists prevent the label group boycott from replacing the required inquiry into the agreement's character. Khan likewise preserves competitive-effects review when the characteristics of a vertical restraint do not justify automatic condemnation, while leaving genuine horizontal and price-agreement rules distinct.

MECo purchased removal services for its affiliated telephone company; it did not supply those services or compete in their overall market. Its separate incorporation and resale to the affiliate do not supply a horizontal agreement among removal-service competitors. The allegation that it preferred AT&T Technologies to Discon describes the vertical supplier choice at issue, not concerted exclusion among competing suppliers.

Discon's strongest argument is that choosing a more expensive supplier to destroy a rival has no plausible competitive benefit. The alleged purpose may matter in ordinary review, but reprehensible motive does not establish the class of restraint required for automatic condemnation. Otherwise any buyer's questionable supplier choice could become a per se boycott without proof that competition was reduced. The Court rejects that classification; it neither finds the purchasing arrangement reasonable nor immunizes independently actionable fraud or exclusion.

### Regulatory fraud and harm to one supplier do not replace competitive injury

**Operative rule:** Regulatory deception, consumer overcharges and elimination of one supplier do not by themselves establish that the challenged purchasing agreement injured the competitive process; Discon must establish the competitive injury and other elements of the theory actually pursued. The lower courts must reconsider the surviving §1 effects claim and the derivative rationale for the distinct §2 conspiracy claim without treating the rejected per se classification or fraud alone as sufficient, while leaving previously dismissed claims outside this remand.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the requirement, its application and the bounded remand for both affected components.

**Controlling explanation:** Brunswick and Atlantic Richfield require antitrust injury rather than loss to a competitor alone. Consumer harm may arise when a regulated monopolist uses deception to charge more through its existing position, without that harm resulting from diminished rivalry in the removal-services market. The allegations of inflated purchases, rebates and Discon's elimination therefore do not themselves establish the competitive mechanism necessary for the antitrust theory.

That distinction is not a finding that the arrangement was harmless. Kodak requires attention to actual competitive conditions rather than an unsupported categorical assumption, and Khan preserves liability for restraints shown to have harmful competitive effects outside a rejected per se category. The lower court had retained a §1 effects inquiry as well as possible per se treatment. Its error in allowing the latter supplies no automatic judgment against every version of the former.

The separately reinstated §2 conspiracy claim must likewise be reconsidered insofar as it relies on the rejected premises concerning the same purchasing practices. Spectrum Sports preserves the elements of the actual offense; an attempted-monopolization requirement cannot simply be transferred to conspiracy. The Court decides neither that conspiracy claim's ultimate sufficiency nor a final market-power or causation fact. The limited grant does not authorize wholesale dismissal of all remaining theories or reopening of the already dismissed vertical-price-fixing, monopoly, attempt and RICO components. No damages or injunction follows from this classification decision.
