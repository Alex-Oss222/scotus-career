## Event

Clinton v. Goldsmith, No. 98-347. Merits decision, May 17, 1999; argued March 22, 1999. On certiorari to the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces, 48 M.J. 84. The question is whether the All Writs Act permits that court to enjoin an administrative drop from the Air Force rolls outside its review of court-martial findings and sentence. Goldsmith's sentence imposed confinement and forfeiture of pay, not dismissal; the later drop action was separate.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the judgment granting the drop-from-rolls injunction and vacates that injunction.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed; injunction vacated, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The injunction must be dissolved. The court-martial findings and sentence remain undisturbed. Goldsmith may pursue any independently available administrative or judicial route, subject to its own jurisdictional and remedial requirements; no entitlement to relief or adequacy determination is entered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Entire opinion |

## Holdings

#### A writ cannot enlarge military appellate jurisdiction

**Holding and operative rule:** The All Writs Act permits the military appellate court to protect jurisdiction independently conferred over court-martial findings and sentences. It does not authorize an injunction against a separate executive personnel action outside that review merely because the action follows a court-martial conviction.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls the complete rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a useful remedy cannot supply the jurisdiction it is supposed to protect. Judicial Council v. McBryde establishes that an auxiliary writ must aid existing or potential appellate jurisdiction; Your Home Visiting Nurse Services v. Shalala likewise separates authority to review from the asserted wrong. Here the final sentence imposed confinement and forfeiture. Dropping Goldsmith from the rolls was not part of that sentence and would not amend its findings or punishment. The absence of dismissal from the sentence did not adjudicate an entitlement against every separate personnel action. The constitutional importance of Goldsmith's objections therefore cannot bring this act within CAAF's statutory subject matter. The Court preserves writ authority genuinely needed to protect or enforce a lawful military appellate judgment. It decides neither the removal's constitutional validity nor the medication claim or the availability and adequacy of any particular outside remedy.

## Precedent Treatment

- Judicial Council v. McBryde is applied: protection of existing or potential appellate jurisdiction supplies no general supervision of unrelated administrative acts.
- Your Home Visiting Nurse Services v. Shalala is applied for the distinction between a lawful review channel and the underlying merits; its statutory setting and preserved limits remain intact.

## Law After Decision

The existing rule that auxiliary writs create no independent jurisdiction now governs this separate military personnel action. Constitutional challenges and properly authorized proceedings elsewhere remain open; no rule on their merits or adequacy is established.

## Separate Writings



## Procedure After Action

The military appellate injunction is dissolved. The Court directs no new sentence, administrative outcome or further Supreme Court proceeding. A challenge elsewhere proceeds only under that forum's independent authority.

## Source Notes

The reported chronology, sentence and lower-court posture appear in the [official United States Reports, volume 526, pages 529–540](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/526bv.pdf). The question is stated in substance. This decision resolves the injunction's statutory basis and expresses no constitutional judgment on the administrative drop.

## Event

Florida v. White, No. 98-223. Merits decision, May 17, 1999; argued March 23, 1999. On certiorari to the Supreme Court of Florida, 710 So. 2d 949. The appeal concerns suppression in a possession prosecution. Police seized White's readily movable car from a public workplace lot on probable cause that he had used it for earlier cocaine deliveries. They had no stated probable cause that drugs were then inside; cocaine was found during the subsequent inventory. The question is whether the forfeiture seizure required a warrant or an additional emergency.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Florida Supreme Court's judgment and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 6–3. Supporting: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Opposing: Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg. Reversal rejects the federal warrant/additional-emergency ground for suppressing the fruits of this seizure. It does not unconditionally reinstate the conviction, order admission of every item, or enter a forfeiture award. Ordinary properly available proceedings and independent grounds remain for the state courts.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer | Entire opinion |
| Concurrence | Souter | Breyer | Joins the Court in full; explains the limited vehicle rule |
| Dissent | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, Ginsburg | Would affirm the seizure-based suppression judgment |

## Holdings

#### Seizure of a forfeitable automobile in a public place

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers with probable cause that a readily movable automobile itself is forfeitable may seize it from a public place without a warrant or an additional case-specific emergency. The rule rests on the automobile's characteristics and the public-place seizure, not merely on a legislature's decision to label property contraband.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; these six Justices control the rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the established automobile rationale reaches custody of the automobile that probable cause identifies as the forfeitable object. Pennsylvania v. Labron supplies mobility and the reduced privacy associated with regulated vehicles without requiring a further showing that process was impracticable. Wyoming v. Houghton makes the object supported by probable cause important to the exception's scope; here that object is White's own car. The seizure occurred in public and required no additional invasion of a home. United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property requires prior process for the real-property deprivation it decided while expressly distinguishing movable property; its land-specific rule does not decide this case. G. M. Leasing Corp. v. United States supports distinguishing public seizures from intrusions into protected premises. The dissent correctly identifies a possessory interest and the retrospective purpose of forfeiture, but those differences do not remove this vehicle's mobility or the direct probable-cause connection. Earlier opportunity to obtain a warrant therefore does not itself defeat the exception.

The Court does not decide whether excessive delay can make probable cause stale. The holding authorizes no entry into a home or protected curtilage, and does not adjudicate inventory policy, prolonged retention, notice, ultimate forfeiture or a distinct state-law protection.

## Precedent Treatment

- Pennsylvania v. Labron is extended from a vehicle evidence search to seizure of the readily movable vehicle itself when probable cause identifies it as forfeitable and it is taken in public.
- Wyoming v. Houghton is applied for attention to the object and scope supported by probable cause; it did not itself decide forfeiture seizure.
- United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property is distinguished: its real-property process requirement and movable-property reservation retain their force.
- G. M. Leasing Corp. v. United States supports the public-place/protected-premises distinction; its revenue-enforcement holding is not converted into unlimited forfeiture authority.
- Knowles v. Iowa remains intact: an inventory or adjacent enforcement power cannot itself furnish authority for the antecedent seizure.

## Law After Decision

The automobile exception permits this public seizure of the forfeitable vehicle without a separate urgency showing. It supplies no general warrant exception for all property designated contraband, and leaves distinct seizure, search, retention, hearing and final-forfeiture questions separate.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, joined by Breyer, concurring:** Both join the Court's complete rule. They emphasize that statutory designation alone cannot permit warrantless seizure of any property a legislature calls contraband. The automobile and public-place limits do the constitutional work. G. M. Leasing involved traditional revenue enforcement and does not validate unlimited forfeiture practice.

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela and Ginsburg, dissenting:** The Fourth Amendment protects possession as well as privacy. A contemporary search for evidence and permanent forfeiture custody based on past use are different intrusions; regulation of a car does not erase its owner's possessory interest. Officers had a reasonable opportunity to obtain a warrant and identified no concrete urgency. The later inventory cannot cure that antecedent seizure. G. M. Leasing involved an antecedent tax assessment with the force of a judgment, unlike the officers' own retrospective assessment here. The dissent would affirm suppression on the reviewed ground while preserving prosecution based on untainted evidence and forfeiture through lawful process. Its rule leaves genuine roadside exigency, a proper Labron evidence search, possession of contraband itself, lawful temporary securing while a warrant is diligently sought, and ordinary regulatory impoundment distinct.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the state courts for proceedings consistent with the federal seizure ruling. No acquittal, final forfeiture, particular new hearing or later Supreme Court event is directed.

## Source Notes

The chronology and lower-court posture are reported in the [official United States Reports, volume 526, pages 559–573](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/526bv.pdf). The decision addresses the federal warrant question, without deciding the complete statutory notice or forfeiture procedure.

## Event

Hunt v. Cromartie, No. 98-85. Merits decision, May 17, 1999; argued January 20, 1999. On direct appeal from a three-judge District Court, 34 F. Supp. 2d 1029. The court entered summary judgment invalidating North Carolina's Twelfth Congressional District before discovery or an evidentiary hearing. The State disputed the court's inference that race, rather than partisan voting patterns, predominated. The appeal requires the Court to identify the governing constitutional injury before determining which factual disputes matter. The State used a replacement plan for the 1998 elections with district-court approval. Section 1.1 of 1998 North Carolina Session Laws chapter 2 governs that plan's use in 1998 and 2000 subject to this Court's reversal of the judgment declaring the prior G.S. 163–201(a) unconstitutional. That conditional operation leaves a live controversy; the Court does not decide the replacement map's validity or whether this vacatur satisfies the statutory condition.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the summary judgment and dependent injunction and remands under Johnson v. Miller.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting | Opposing or differently aligned |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existing summary judgment and injunction | Vacated, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | No opposition to vacatur |
| Governing remand | Reject predominance alone as a constitutional deprivation; consider only actually presented independently cognizable claims under their own elements and ordinary procedure, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas would preserve the predominance claim for factfinding |

No trial is required solely to choose between racial and political motives for the design-only theory. The Court does not finally validate the map, supply a new claim, deem an unknown claim forfeited, or independently decide the State's own summary-judgment motion.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |

| Concurrence in part and in the vacatur, dissenting from the remand limitation | Kennedy | O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas | Joins Part I; would retain the predominance claim |

## Holdings

#### I. Predominance alone cannot support this injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** A districting injunction cannot rest solely on a finding that race predominated in forming a common territorial electorate, even if ordinary districting criteria were subordinated. A plaintiff must establish a separately cognizable denial of equal participation or treatment; remand evaluates any such actually presented claim under its own elements and ordinary procedural rules.

**Authority:** Part II and the governing remand in Part III are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five direct joins control.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that factual disagreement matters only when the governing law makes the fact material. Shaw v. Reno and Johnson v. Miller distinguish racial composition of a shared electorate from unequal civic status, racial eligibility or representation legally limited to one race. Predominance may exist without establishing any of those deprivations. The district court therefore could not invalidate the district merely by resolving the design's motivation against the State. A trial on the same legally insufficient theory would not cure that error. Coalition for Economic Equity v. Wilson illustrates a concrete burden on political access; it does not turn racial motivation alone into that burden. An independently cognizable injury may be noneconomic and need not arise from a second act or later electoral loss, but its own elements must be established. The Court neither invents such a claim nor rules it forfeited because its preservation is not established here. It leaves narrow tailoring, statutory vote dilution, the applicable scrutiny for an actually supported independent injury and the district's validity against other claims undecided.

#### II. Summary judgment cannot resolve a genuine material inference against the nonmovant

**Holding and operative rule:** When an actually cognizable claim makes motive material, a supported genuine dispute between racial and political explanations cannot be resolved against the nonmoving party on summary judgment. Circumstantial proof can support summary judgment in an appropriate case; neither district shape nor racial and partisan correlation is automatically conclusive.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I at this ordinary procedural level, without agreeing on which constitutional claim may proceed.

**Controlling explanation:** The State offered a supported political explanation for the boundaries. The court could not treat the contrary motive inference as a trial finding on this summary record. Johnson's evidentiary treatment separates proof from legal consequence. Under the Court's substantive holding, no motive trial follows on a design-only theory; under any actually preserved independent claim, ordinary materiality and evidentiary rules govern. The Court does not treat new reply-brief material as evidence that was before the district court or decide the denial of the State's cross-motion as a separate component.

## Precedent Treatment

- Shaw v. Reno is applied: territorial racial composition alone does not establish the asserted unequal-treatment injury.
- Johnson v. Miller controls both the substantive insufficiency of predominance alone and the distinction between evidentiary dispute and legal materiality; its genuine-injury protections and limits remain.
- Coalition for Economic Equity v. Wilson is distinguished as an actual political-access burden, not authority to infer such a burden from motive or constituency composition.

## Law After Decision

The Court applies the existing Shaw/Johnson rule and requires a conforming remand. Predominance alone does not sustain an injunction or require a trial. Ordinary summary-judgment rules remain available for facts material to a properly presented independent claim; the four-Justice proposed predominance trigger is not governing law.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas:** These Justices agree that the existing summary judgment and injunction cannot stand. They maintain their position that predominant racial assignment subordinating ordinary districting criteria should trigger strict scrutiny, and would retain that claim for factfinding. Mere racial awareness, a numerical racial percentage and an unusual shape are not automatic invalidity even under their rule; the State's supported political explanation precludes the present adverse summary judgment. Justification and fit would follow only if the proposed trigger were proved. Their disagreement with the Court concerns the constitutional premise and the remand's foreclosure of the design-only theory, not a vote to affirm the district court's invalidation.

## Procedure After Action

The district court must proceed consistently with the Court's rejection of the design-only theory and its preserved-claim limits. No replacement map, new election, mandatory motive trial or later Supreme Court event is ordered. The Court orders neither automatic restoration of the predecessor map nor displacement of the replacement map and does not invalidate the completed 1998 elections.

## Source Notes

The argument date, district-court posture and contested evidence are reported in the [official United States Reports, volume 526, pages 541–558](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/526bv.pdf). The constitutional rule and remand are governed by Shaw and Johnson; no independent claim or preservation finding is supplied beyond the adjudicated record. The replacement-plan condition is set out in [1998 North Carolina Session Laws chapter 2, §1.1](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/SessionLaws/HTML/1997-1998/SL1998-2.html); its application to this judgment is left open.

## Event

Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co., No. 98-470. Merits decision, May 17, 1999; argued March 22, 1999. On certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 145 F.3d 211. In a removed Texas action concerning a Norwegian gas venture, the District Court dismissed for lack of personal jurisdiction without resolving three asserted removal grounds. ## Participation

The Court reverses the en banc judgment and remands for further appellate proceedings.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |

## Holdings

#### A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while the subject-matter issue is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first; the choice must respect applicable statutory remand commands and review bars and must decide no contested merits.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls the rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that declining authority over a defendant does not assume authority to decide liability. Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. forbids assumed Article III power for contested merits, and NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones applies that boundary to constitutional claims. Neither exhausts the order among nonmerits thresholds. Personal jurisdiction protects a defendant's interest in being subject to the court's authority; its waivability does not make it an optional prerequisite to a binding merits judgment. Here choosing that straightforward ground avoids an unnecessary difficult subject-matter determination without deciding the tort claims. The State's forum interest and the possible consequences of jurisdictional determinations remain relevant to the choice, but do not establish an absolute order. If subject matter is as readily resolved and personal jurisdiction raises difficult state law, comity ordinarily favors deciding remand first. Murphy Brothers keeps removal timing distinct from actual jurisdiction, and El Paso Natural Gas gives effect to a particular statutory priority rather than a universal hierarchy. An established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence; an unresolved difficult issue is not the same thing. The Court leaves the correctness of the actual personal-jurisdiction dismissal to the Court of Appeals and decides none of the three asserted removal grounds.

## Precedent Treatment

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. is applied within its express scope: jurisdiction must precede contested merits, but its nonmerits-sequencing reservation permits today's rule.
- NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones is applied on the same merits boundary; its prohibition on assumed standing is unchanged.
- Murphy Brothers, Inc. v. Michetti Pipe Stringing, Inc. is distinguished as removal timing and formal process, which establish no subject-matter jurisdiction or universal sequence.
- El Paso Natural Gas Co. v. Neztsosie is distinguished as a statute-specific priority over prudential exhaustion, not a general order between jurisdictional grounds.
- Insurance Corp. of Ireland v. Compagnie des Bauxites de Guinee supplies the distinction between the individual interest protected by personal jurisdiction and institutional subject-matter limits; the distinction does not establish an invariable order.

## Law After Decision

Federal courts may use the bounded personal-jurisdiction-first route in removed as well as originally filed actions. The rule adds discretion among nonmerits thresholds while preserving ordinary comity, actual statutory commands and the prohibition on deciding contested merits without jurisdiction. It establishes no universal preclusion rule or actual jurisdiction over these claims.

## Event

Maine Green Party v. Maine, First Circuit No. 98-1309. Merits decision, May 20, 1999, on review of the First Circuit's March 2, 1999 judgment, 173 F.3d 1, affirming summary judgment for Maine. The question concerns the five-percent presidential-vote condition for retaining statewide party recognition and the asserted right to organize locally. No Supreme Court argument date is established.

The party qualified through Jonathan Carter's 6.4% gubernatorial vote in 1994 and lost recognition after Ralph Nader received less than five percent in the 1996 presidential election. ## Participation

## Holdings

### A refined legal argument does not itself forfeit the original associational claim

**Holding and operative rule:** A party that initially challenged the presidential-vote condition and asserted its right to choose its organizational structure does not lose that legal organizational-autonomy objection merely by sharpening it later. That conclusion neither proves an exclusive local philosophy nor permits new summary-judgment facts to be assumed; the national-organizing affidavit and the factual basis of the as-applied theory retain their ordinary evidentiary significance.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's concurrence and Stevens's dissent, joined by Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, directly adopt this same limited proposition. Those five votes control the legal-argument distinction, without supplying a majority rationale for the judgment or a command to reopen this case.

**Controlling explanation:** Yee permits a different legal argument supporting a properly presented federal claim while preserving distinct limits on facts and the question presented. Here the original challenge identified the same presidential-vote condition and the asserted freedom to organize as the members chose. The later legal formulation therefore is not wholly new merely because it gives that associational objection greater precision. The contrary position correctly identifies a possible difference between inability to build a national party and a philosophical refusal to do so. That difference prevents a court from assuming the asserted philosophy; it does not itself erase the originally presented legal objection. Thomas v. Arn permits reasonable, nonjurisdictional presentation rules, but its failure-to-object setting does not automatically decide an argument raised in objections. The district court's silence establishes neither a new fact nor an express waiver ruling. This conclusion makes no factual finding about exclusive-local intent and resolves no constitutional merits.

### The affirmance has no controlling constitutional rationale

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** Five Justices affirm, but the four-Justice preservation ground and the Chief's contextual merits ground are not logical subsets of one another. Marks yields no common narrower rationale. The judgment binds the parties; it establishes no general five-percent safe harbor, no duty to participate nationally, and no Court holding approving every alternative ballot route or rejecting every concrete as-applied exclusion claim.

## Precedent Treatment

- Yee v. City of Escondido is applied by the five Justices supporting the preserved-legal-argument proposition; its separate factual and granted-question limits remain.
- Thomas v. Arn remains a rule allowing nonjurisdictional presentation requirements, not a mandatory forfeiture rule for every theory raised in objections.
- Marks v. United States supplies no common affirmance rationale because the procedural and merits grounds are independent.
- Anderson v. Celebrezze retains contextual review of actual burden, precise interests and fit; no majority adopts a new retention-rule application.
- Burdick v. Takushi retains its calibrated framework and complete-system limits; no new constitutional application commands five here.
- Norman v. Reed retains its actual-access and separate-electorate protections without a majority retention-rule application.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party retains its specific nonfusion holding; no majority extends it to validate this condition.
- National Committee of the Reform Party v. Democratic National Committee retains its support-sensitive financing scope and supplies no general retention safe harbor.
- Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation retains its petition-advocacy protections; no majority changes them in this case.

## Law After Decision

The reviewed judgment stands. The bounded legal-argument preservation proposition has five direct supporters, but the Court establishes no constitutional safe harbor for presidential-vote retention and no unconditional right to statewide party privileges based on local success. Existing contextual ballot-access scrutiny remains unchanged. Concrete as-applied exclusion claims require their own properly available proceedings and supported record; this judgment orders no new proceeding.

## Separate Writings

**Breyer, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, announcing the judgment:** These Justices would affirm because an as-applied case based on practical difficulty building a national organization differs materially from one based on an exclusive philosophical commitment to local politics. Yee protects new legal arguments, not assumed new facts. The affidavit describing national organization efforts makes the change consequential. The State's undeveloped objection and the district court's unexplained adoption do not prevent enforcement of ordinary presentation requirements. Arn supports reasonable nonjurisdictional management but does not make every omitted theory jurisdictionally barred. They reach no constitutional merits, approve no universal presidential-support proxy and find no alternative route necessarily adequate.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment:** The initial organizational-autonomy argument and Eu and Tashjian citations preserve the legal objection, while the affidavit prevents an unsupported finding of an exclusively local philosophy. Applying Anderson–Burdick and Timmons to this record, he would reject the facial challenge because statewide administrative privileges may depend on demonstrated statewide support and workable alternative access remains available. The party can organize, endorse and campaign; its 1994 qualification through a nonparty gubernatorial candidate demonstrates an operative route. The record does not establish practically prohibitive requalification or alternative access. This is not automatic deference to the statewide label, a duty to nominate a presidential candidate, or approval of every retention and qualification rule. A developed record of compelled abandonment of genuinely local organization without a workable ballot alternative would require a different burden-and-fit inquiry.

The routes remain distinct: section 301 governs retention, with caucus, convention and certification conditions; section 302 permits formation through a consenting qualifying nonparty candidate, together with enrollment, caucuses in all sixteen counties and a convention; section 303 provides petition qualification. Its approved form, verified and certified signatures and legal addresses equal to five percent of the last gubernatorial vote, and filing before 5 p.m. on the 180th day before a primary are not another presidential-vote condition. Section 302's March 20 caucus-notice date is separate. None receives blanket constitutional approval.

**Stevens, joined by Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, dissenting:** They agree that the original legal associational objection remains available, but would return the case for ordinary consideration of its actual factual basis, electoral burdens, precise state interests and feasible alternatives. Yee permits that legal refinement; the affidavit presents an issue to examine rather than a categorical loss of the claim. Norman and Anderson–Burdick require attention to real organizing burdens. Timmons's nonfusion holding and Reform Party's funding rule do not decide this retention condition, and nominal statutory access does not establish its actual cost. They neither find exclusive-local intent nor declare the condition unconstitutional, and they require no recognition, injunction or new election.

## Holdings

### Coverage includes an association serving its members' commercial profit

**Holding and operative rule:** The FTC Act reaches a nonprofit professional association organized to carry on business for its members' profit when its activities confer proximate, substantial economic benefits on their for-profit operations. CDA satisfies that condition through its insurance, financing, commercial support and other member-business activities; nonprofit form, tax status and absence of distributions to the association do not eliminate the members'-profit branch of section 44.

**Authority:** Souter's coverage opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, directly controls this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory alternative concerning members' profit would do little work if only the association's own earnings counted. CDA materially advances its members' commercial practices, so the outside suppliers' role does not make those benefits merely incidental. This application does not require exclusive devotion to member profit, a substantial percentage of total activity devoted to it, or proof that each member makes a net profit from membership. The connection must be real and proximate; professional education's presumed or incidental benefit alone is not held sufficient. The Court does not decide coverage of every charity, educational organization, nonprofit with an annual surplus, or commercial competitor without for-profit members. Ordinary statutory interpretation suffices, without deciding deference to the Commission's jurisdictional view. Section 45's sector exceptions remain; none is established for CDA. Neither professional status nor an unverified guideline's description of state law supplies immunity. Coverage authorizes inquiry, not condemnation.

### The inquiry must test the restraint's actual competitive tendency and material explanation

**Holding and operative rule:** An abbreviated rule-of-reason inquiry may suffice when experience and the restraint's actual character make its likely anticompetitive tendency sufficiently clear to assess both the restraint and its material competing explanation. Where professional-information disparities make those effects insufficiently obvious, fuller analysis fitted to the restraint is required; neither a fixed sequence of labels nor a complete industry or market-definition study is invariably compulsory.

**Authority:** Souter's competitive-analysis opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, supplies five direct joins for this rule and both applications below.

**Controlling explanation:** NCAA permits inquiry calibrated to an established competitive mechanism. Professional Engineers rejects the claim that competition itself is undesirable; it does not dispense with examining a plausible claim that a practice improves reliable comparison. Indiana Federation of Dentists permits direct analysis of an agreement withholding a desired service, but does not make every professional-information restriction identical to that agreement. Khan and NYNEX require attention to the actual restraint and mechanism without converting this horizontal association into a vertical arrangement. The FTC's concrete enforcement findings are adverse evidence, not a substitute for the missing analysis of information quality. The Court corrects legal sufficiency and does not reweigh supported findings under section 45(c), independently affirm every economic conclusion, or decide whether substantial evidence supports ultimate liability. It finds neither restraint reasonable and creates no professional immunity. Legitimate restraints on materially false or misleading advertising remain possible under their actual conditions; no categorical per se issue is independently decided.

### Price restrictions require more examination on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** The practical restrictions on fee descriptions and broad discount advertisements cannot be sustained on the abbreviated analysis employed here without adequately examining their asserted effect on the reliability of price comparison. Further inquiry must test the actual enforcement and claimed information benefits; the mere reduction of advertising does not settle the net competitive tendency.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice competitive-analysis opinion directly adopts this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Price communication ordinarily helps consumers compare and dentists compete. Bates recognizes that value while preserving legitimate deception controls. But an across-the-board discount without an intelligible baseline or scope may be unreliable, and a disclosure condition might improve comparison, obstruct it or do both. The reported restrictions on truthful descriptions and practically prohibitive disclosures remain serious evidence against CDA. Their existence alone does not adequately test whether these particular conditions improve reliable information before demanding empirical justification on the assumption of an already established harmful net tendency. This is no finding that every advertisement misleads, that every condition helps, or that CDA's explanation ultimately succeeds. The lower proceedings must perform the missing analysis without automatically adopting the ALJ's no-market-power conclusion, a local-share threshold or mandatory comprehensive market definition.

### Quality advertising is not equivalent to service output

**Holding and operative rule:** Quality-advertising restrictions whose effects depend on the reliability and verifiability of professional information require fuller consideration than equating fewer advertisements with a restriction of dental-service output. The actual conditions and competitive explanation must be assessed; possible effects on demand do not themselves establish a producer-imposed service-output restriction.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice opinion directly controls this distinct application.

**Controlling explanation:** Restricting useful quality information can impede service rivalry, yet patients may have difficulty verifying professional claims. A restraint addressing unverifiable or misleading claims may improve information. Bates recognizes that quality-claim problem, while Indiana Federation's established desired-service restraint provides no automatic answer to this different mechanism. The court below could not settle the matter merely by translating fewer advertisements into reduced dental output. The Court requires neither measured output decline in every antitrust case nor acceptance of a blanket ban. Supported enforcement against truthful information remains material. Fuller examination may condemn either or both restrictions, but the present analysis does not resolve their competitive explanation. The discontinued fear-allaying policy is not treated as a continuing restraint.

## Precedent Treatment

- NCAA v. Board of Regents supplies flexible inquiry fitted to established competitive tendencies; it creates no compulsory full-market procedure.
- National Society of Professional Engineers v. United States remains a bar to defending restraints on the premise that competition itself is undesirable; an information-quality explanation must be tested on its actual competitive terms.
- FTC v. Indiana Federation of Dentists is distinguished at the level of the restraint and information mechanism; its direct-effects method and separation of supported findings from legal sufficiency remain.
- Bates v. State Bar of Arizona supplies the value of truthful price information and the distinct difficulty of unverifiable professional quality claims.
- State Oil Co. v. Khan retains its vertical maximum-price holding and horizontal distinctions; its attention to competitive mechanisms informs this inquiry without conferring immunity.
- NYNEX Corp. v. Discon retains its vertical supplier-substitution holding and distinct horizontal categories; labels or improper purpose do not replace analysis of the actual mechanism.

The required inquiry is proportionate to actual restraints and live competitive explanations. The Court establishes neither final liability nor professional immunity, and imposes no universal market-definition trial or required proof of measured service-output decline. ## Event

## Holdings

### SSDI does not create an automatic bar or special adverse presumption under the ADA

**Holding and operative rule:** Applying for or receiving SSDI neither automatically estops an ADA plaintiff from proving qualification nor creates a special strong presumption against that proof. The statutes ask different questions, and qualification under the ADA still depends on the ability to perform the position's essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls this holding, the explanation requirement below and the bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The ADA expressly includes reasonable accommodation in its qualification inquiry. SSDI's impairment, duration and vocational provisions do not conduct that same workplace-accommodation inquiry. Administrative listings and trial-work provisions, changes over time and properly alternative legal theories may also explain why the two claims coexist. Bragdon requires individual statutory analysis and founded proof; a label under one scheme cannot replace the elements of another. The Fifth Circuit recognized some of these distinctions but confined compatibility to a highly unusual class under its negative presumption. That presumption still treats the differing legal conclusions as ordinarily inconsistent and is unwarranted. The employer's concern about obtaining incompatible advantages is substantial, but ordinary evidentiary requirements address it without a label-based presumption. None of these possible explanations is a finding that Cleveland was qualified, that any accommodation was reasonable, or that discrimination occurred. The holding supplies no universal immunity from the consequences of contradictory historical facts and leaves general law governing purely factual contradictions and other statutes undisturbed.

### An apparent inconsistency still requires an adequate explanation

**Holding and operative rule:** An ADA plaintiff confronting an apparently inconsistent earlier total-disability representation must provide an explanation sufficient for a reasonable juror, accepting the earlier statement as true or as made in good faith, still to find that the plaintiff could perform the essential functions at the relevant time with or without reasonable accommodation. The plaintiff retains the ordinary qualification burden; good faith alone, a bare contradiction or a statutory label without an answer to the material factual inconsistency does not satisfy it.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this requirement and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 56 asks whether the evidence permits a reasonable finding on the actual ADA element. Prior sworn assertions remain evidence. Statutory differences may explain an assertion about disability without erasing a contradictory statement about what the person actually could do. The explanation must address the relevant time, job functions and accommodation to the extent they create the apparent conflict. Cleveland's return to work, requested accommodations, asserted later worsening and the disputed status of her first application may matter, but the Court does not resolve their truth. The continuous-disability award also remains part of the record and must be addressed rather than assumed compatible. On remand both sides may present and contest the explanation, in sworn form where appropriate. A sufficient explanation permits ordinary assessment; it guarantees neither trial nor success. Summary judgment may again follow if the explanation is inadequate or another essential element is absent. The decision changes no benefits award, compels no unreasonable accommodation and makes no credibility finding.

## Precedent Treatment

Bragdon v. Abbott is applied at the shared level of individual statutory elements and founded objective proof. Its disability-definition and direct-threat holdings remain distinct and unchanged. Ordinary summary-judgment requirements remain; the Court does not adopt every lower-court rule concerning a later affidavit that contradicts earlier factual testimony.

## Law After Decision

Apparent contradictions remain material evidence and require an adequate explanation under ordinary proof rules. Qualification, reasonable accommodation, discrimination and defenses retain their independent elements. ## Event

Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education, No. 97-843. Argued January 12, 1999; decided May 24, 1999. On certiorari to the en banc Eleventh Circuit, 120 F.3d 1390, which affirmed dismissal of the district's Title IX damages claim. The question is whether the funding recipient may be responsible for its own deliberate response to serious student-on-student sexual harassment within its substantial control.

A fifth-grade student allegedly endured repeated touching and sexual propositions by a classmate over several months. The child and her mother reportedly informed teachers and the principal; officials allegedly failed to protect her while the conduct continued and interfered with education. The harasser's later guilty plea is a procedural recital, not proof of every civil element or earlier institutional notice. The case concerns pleading sufficiency, not findings of recipient fault or damages.

## Participation

## Holdings

### A school's own culpable response can support damages for controlled peer harassment

**Holding and operative rule:** A Title IX funding recipient may be liable for its own response to peer harassment because of sex when an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial danger has actual notice, the recipient substantially controls both the harasser and the relevant educational context, and deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that danger causally contributes to further harassment or vulnerability producing educational deprivation and compensable injury. A peer's attendance does not impute the peer's acts to the recipient; knowledge, culpability, control, sex-based discrimination and causal loss are independent requirements.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer; six direct joins establish this sufficient route.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin permits compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination but leaves recipient responsibility to be established. Kinman's actual-notice route identifies a recipient's own deliberate disregard of a known educational danger. The Court extends that sufficient route to peer conduct where the school has substantial remedial control; it does not claim Kinman already decided peers. The school's knowing, culpable continuation of educational deprivation supplies the institutional wrong, answering the objection that a student cannot personally bind the funding recipient. Mere discipline authority or an ineffective result does not suffice. Actual notice must reach an official empowered to correct the danger; generic constructive notice, the harasser's own knowledge and technical policy noncompliance do not establish this route. A reasonable response is lawful even if harassment continues, and courts prescribe neither a selected punishment nor a victim's preferred remedy. Constitutional and statutory limits on discipline inform control and response. Gebser's school-delegated teacher authority does not transfer to classmates; its conjunctive teacher route and the other teacher holdings remain intact. The Court neither adopts ordinary negligence or general agency law nor rejects every distinct theory of direct official discrimination. Receipt of assistance remains separately necessary under NCAA v. Smith. Causal injury cannot be inferred solely from harassment predating legally sufficient recipient notice.

### Educational deprivation follows the contextual severe-or-pervasive standard

**Holding and operative rule:** The harassment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive and impair educational access or benefit in its full context. Sexually charged conduct or offense alone does not establish discrimination because of sex, the required educational harm or the independent institutional elements.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer, directly controls with five votes. Stone-Zsela does not join this disjunctive threshold; no combination of separate rationales is needed for its authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Kinman adopted severe-or-pervasive, objectively abusive and actually experienced educational harassment without mandatory psychiatric injury, measurable grade loss or a rigid list of factors. Its reliance on Meritor and Harris preserves attention to welcome, objective and experienced abuse, and the actual setting. The peer relationship affects the contextual application and recipient-control inquiry; it does not silently replace the substantive standard with a requirement that conduct be both severe and pervasive. Age, relationship, educational setting and actual effect may matter without becoming a mandatory checklist. Neither physical exclusion nor total loss of schooling is invariably required, and no one proxy is sufficient by itself. Ordinary teasing or offense fails without the substantive and institutional requirements. No universal rule determines whether one incident always suffices or never can. These allegations involve repeated touching and propositions over months with educational interference, a materially different claimed environment. The Court decides no ultimate fact and does not treat every unwelcome interaction as actionable discrimination.

### The pleaded recipient claim warrants ordinary further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** Allegations of repeated school-setting harassment, reports including to the principal, continued conduct, deliberately inadequate protection and resulting educational interference state a claim for examination under the sufficient recipient-fault route. The categorical dismissal must be reversed, but an absent element, reasonable response, inadequate control or notice, missing causation or a supported defense may defeat recovery on the developed record.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six directly support this allegation-stage application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The alleged response concerns the district's own use of authority after reports of a known danger, rather than automatic responsibility for the classmate. The asserted repeated abuse and educational disruption permit the necessary elements to be tested. Allegations that reports reached the principal do not finally establish every official's corrective power, knowledge or opportunity; the criminal plea supplies no earlier notice or complete causal showing. The plaintiff must prove the institutional and substantive conditions, including postnotice causal injury. Existing compensation principles therefore require ordinary further adjudication, not immediate damages or judicial control of discipline. No compulsory trial follows if ordinary procedure establishes a missing element or valid defense. The decision reaches only the appealed district Title IX claim and leaves unrelated and individual claims outside the mandate.

- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District is extended at its sufficient actual-notice/culpable-disregard route to substantially controlled peer conduct. Its severe-or-pervasive contextual educational-harm standard remains; the peer extension is stated expressly.

## Event

Hanlon v. Berger, No. 97-1927. Argued March 24, 1999; decided May 24, 1999, on certiorari to the Ninth Circuit's judgment in Berger v. Hanlon, 129 F.3d 505. ## Holdings

### A search warrant supplies no unrelated media access to protected space

**Holding and operative rule:** Officials may not use authority to execute a warrant to introduce or facilitate nonassisting media intrusion into a place in which the claimants possess their own Fourth Amendment protection, absent consent or a separate lawful basis for the added intrusion. Legitimate civilian assistance tied to lawful execution remains distinct; official authority to enter does not itself grant unrelated private visitors the same access.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices, directly controls this bounded rule. The favorable record supports a protected-space intrusion claim without establishing that every filmed place or act falls within it.

**Controlling explanation:** Payton protects residential entry, while Summers permits reasonable incidental measures serving execution rather than every added private use. Hicks and Garrison preserve the relationship between authorized intrusion and its actual extent. Current Carter and Redmon require each claimant's own protected interest and the actual manner of access; Ramirez likewise separates lawful execution from objective-unrelated intrusion. The ranch warrant therefore does not authorize a broadcast enterprise merely because agents were lawfully present. Publicity and general accountability do not establish actual assistance. Conversely, assistance need not be forbidden merely because a warrant does not name each genuine helper. Consent or independent lawful authority can supply a different basis, but neither can be assumed for the media from consent to an officer. The Court does not equate every recording with a seizure, constitutionalize every provision of section 3105, or declare every ranch location protected. The additional media purpose does not invalidate every otherwise lawful official act.

### The particular protected place and intrusion must still be identified

**Holding and operative rule:** Commercial purpose, trespass or ownership alone does not eliminate the open-fields doctrine or establish a claimant's Fourth Amendment interest. Courts must assess the actual residential surroundings, enclosed structure, access, use and intrusion, preserving supported favorable evidence without treating any one fact as conclusive for the entire ranch.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by all nine Justices. The Court rejects the proposition that absence of a law-enforcement purpose alone makes otherwise unprotected open fields protected.

**Controlling explanation:** Oliver preserves open-fields treatment and Dunn directs attention to the actual residential surroundings, including proximity, enclosure, use and shielding. The outbuildings' proximity and guard-dog evidence cannot be ignored, but do not classify all recorded areas. Carter's personal-interest rule and Redmon's actual-space inquiry require separation of limited access from investigative authority. Rejecting the lower opinion's broad purpose exception does not reject its separate evidence supporting protection of particular structures or resolve a disputed fact against the Bergers. No new evidentiary hearing or ranch-wide classification is required. The warrant's exclusion of the residence likewise does not answer whether a particular later entry was consensual. The physical crew, consensual conversation, undisclosed microphone transmission and alleged later residential search remain distinct acts.

### March 1993 law does not defeat immunity on this reviewed media theory

**Holding and operative rule:** The federal petitioners receive qualified immunity on the reviewed media-accompaniment, observation and recording-enterprise theory because the particular constitutional application was not sufficiently clear in March 1993, even accepting the strongest supported protected-space account. That defense does not declare the enterprise lawful or protect every genuinely distinct, properly preserved nonconsensual residential entry or search.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; these seven directly adopt the immunity conclusion. Stone-Zsela and Stevens disagree as to supported physical intrusion into protected space.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson protect reasonable legal mistakes at the particularized level; Hunter requires using the owners' supported favorable account rather than resolving historical disputes against them. Bills supplies serious contrary evidence: it condemned a private employee's unrelated inspection after the warrant's object was found, while separately accepting genuine identification assistance under a later warrant. The Court does not read its later-officer immunity as protection for every original invitation. But that distinct inspection does not conclusively settle the accompanying media enterprise. Prahl rejected the presented federal theory that filming and broadcasting an otherwise reasonable search, without more, made it unreasonable; its separate trespass ruling does not erase that qualified contrary federal evidence. Dietemann upheld state privacy liability and expressly reserved federal constitutional liability, so its later characterization cannot establish a federal recording rule. White, Hoffa and Lewis supply a substantial distinct invited-participant argument. Advance planning and the commercial agreement make the conduct deliberate, not clearly classified under then-established constitutional law. The absence of an identical case is not decisive, and no policy or hypothetical assistance supplies legality. Elder requires all relevant earlier authority; Crawford-El preserves the separate defense. Later media, privacy and execution rulings cannot give retrospective notice. The disputed Moncrief and Higbee accounts are unnecessary to the conclusion.

### The mandate is confined to the reviewed official component

**Holding and operative rule:** Only the reviewed federal-official media-participation determination is vacated and remanded; private-media joint-action and damages questions, the Wiretap Act, state trespass, conversion and emotional-distress claims, and publication-injunction rulings receive no disposition here. A genuinely distinct residential entry or search remains subject to its own preserved factual, legal and procedural predicates; no new or waived claim is revived.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join Part IV's limited-vacatur and scope conclusion. Seven also require the immunity direction in Part III; Stevens refuses that direction for the physical protected-space intrusion. Stone-Zsela would affirm denial of immunity and remand instead.

**Controlling explanation:** The officer petition does not enlarge merely because the lower court disposed of other claims in the same opinion. The shared correction separates protected-space accompaniment from open-field observation, consensual participant transmission and distinct alleged searches. White, Hoffa and Lewis present a question different from the crew's physical entry, and Dietemann does not supply the federal answer. The Court therefore adopts no categorical participant-recording or broadcast rule; the seven-Justice legal immunity ground makes that additional merits ruling unnecessary. The favorable outbuilding evidence is preserved, not rejected, and immunity on the reviewed theory does not await conclusive classification of every location. The mandate does not decide prosecutorial absolute immunity, create a new Bivens action, determine every participant's acts, revisit the distinct criminal warrant grounds or award damages.

## Event

Wilson v. Layne, No. 98-83. Argued March 24, 1999; decided May 24, 1999. On certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 141 F.3d 111, which granted the officers qualified immunity without deciding the constitutional question. The homeowners seek review of that immunity judgment. The questions are whether inviting nonassisting media into a home during arrest-warrant execution violates the Fourth Amendment and whether the officers are personally immune under the law existing when they acted.

At about 6:45 a.m. on April 16, 1992, federal and local officers entered the Wilson home to execute an arrest warrant for the homeowners' son. Invited reporters observed the domestic interior and occupants and took photographs under a ride-along arrangement. They did not assist execution; the photographs were never published. The District Court had denied immunity on the media-participation claim. The federal and local defendants' damages channels are Bivens and section 1983, respectively, at their existing scope. Separate lower rulings on probable cause and force are outside this review.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Fourth Circuit's reviewed qualified-immunity judgment is affirmed. The Court unanimously holds the added warrant-unrelated media entry unconstitutional.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela and Stevens.

## Holdings

### A home warrant does not authorize unrelated media visitors

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers violate residents' Fourth Amendment rights when, while executing a warrant inside the home, they introduce or permit invited media to enter for purposes unrelated to assisting lawful execution, without consent or another lawful basis for the added entry. Reasonable civilian assistance serving execution remains permissible on its actual basis; the warrant need not identify every genuine assistant.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices, controls the complete rule and this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Payton protects the home and confines arrest-warrant authority. Summers permits reasonable incidental measures serving execution, while Hicks and Garrison preserve the connection between lawful authority and the intrusion's extent. The officers' authority therefore did not make the home available to additional private observers. These reporters identified no suspect, preserved no evidence, protected no safety interest and performed no other actual execution function. General publicity, accurate reporting or public accountability does not supply the missing connection. Their observation added an intrusion even if they followed the officers' route; independent rummaging and eventual publication are not necessary elements. Nonpublication of the photographs does not erase the entry, and the Court need not classify the images as a separate seizure of property. Consent and independent legal authority remain distinct possibilities, neither established here. Interpreters, medical personnel, technical specialists, witnesses and other persons genuinely and reasonably assisting execution are not barred. The rule adds no obligation to admit observers and does not decide every official documentary practice, public-street observation, private-media liability or exclusionary consequence. Section 3105's search-warrant assistance language is separate statutory background, not an automatic constitutional damages rule governing this state arrest warrant. Lawful official presence is not retroactively invalidated by the additional visitors' wrong.

### The particular April 1992 application was not sufficiently clear for personal damages

**Holding and operative rule:** The respondent officers receive qualified immunity because, on the April 16, 1992 legal landscape, the particular restriction on accompanying, nonassisting media observers during otherwise authorized warrant execution was not sufficiently clear to impose personal damages liability. The absence of a factually identical case is neither required nor sufficient for immunity; the Court considers the particular application and all relevant conduct-date law while accepting the supported nonassistance facts.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven directly adopt the immunity conclusion and affirmance; Stone-Zsela and Stevens reject that application.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson preserve reasonable legal mistakes without making every constitutional violation immune. Hunter requires a judicial answer on the supported nonmovant record, not hypothetical assistance or selection of a more favorable factual version. Bills is substantial adverse authority predating this entry: it rejected police sponsorship of a private employee's unrelated inspection after the warrant's object was recovered and separately approved genuine identification help during a later warrant. Its later-officer immunity did not immunize every original invitation. The Court nevertheless finds its independent inspection materially different from observation accompanying ongoing authorized execution. Prahl supplies qualified contrary federal evidence: it rejected the claim that filming and broadcasting an otherwise reasonable search, without more, made it unreasonable, while separately preserving trespass recovery. That federal result is not a blanket license, and its tort ruling is not a constitutional holding. Together these actual authorities leave the particular observer application insufficiently settled despite the strong home-and-warrant principles supporting today's merits rule. A ride-along policy and supposed good motives supply no constitutional authority. Elder requires considering relevant outside-circuit law, so the Fourth Circuit's geographic exclusion is not approved. Crawford-El preserves the independent defense. Later execution and privacy decisions do not supply April 1992 notice, and disputed Moncrief and Higbee characterizations are unnecessary to the result. The Court chooses to decide the presented constitutional question here without imposing a mandatory merits-first sequence.

## Law After Decision

Officials may not bring nonassisting private media into a home merely under the authority of an execution warrant. Consent, separate lawful authority and genuine reasonable execution assistance remain distinct. These officers nevertheless receive personal immunity for this April 1992 application. The judgment creates no future media-access privilege and changes neither ordinary immunity procedure nor the requirement of individual responsibility and injury in a properly available damages claim.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** They join the complete constitutional rule but would deny immunity.
