### Statutory coverage: shared operative proposition

Section 4's corporation definition reaches a nonprofit professional association organized to conduct substantial activities that serve its members' commercial profit, even when the association distributes no profits to itself. CDA's substantial business assistance and commercially directed membership activities place it within that definition; a nonprofit or tax designation does not remove the express members'-profit branch.

The business services, commercial subsidiary benefits and member-practice assistance supply the connection here. The proposition is not that every incidental financial benefit to a charitable or educational organization's participants establishes FTC coverage. The association's organization and substantial activities must satisfy the statutory business-and-profit terms. No asserted sector exception removes CDA, and no state-action immunity follows from a guideline's account of California advertising law. Coverage permits the Commission's competition inquiry; it does not establish a restraint's illegality.

**Competing path and strongest objection:** CDA's separate nonprofit identity and the outside suppliers' role could support excluding an association whose own activity is genuinely charitable or educational, with only incidental member benefit. The stronger textual objection to exclusion here is Congress's express inclusion of business for members' profit, which would lose substantial work if only the association's own earnings counted. Conversely, the FTC's broadest economic-benefit theory would sweep too far. The adopted substantial commercial-activity application rejects both shortcuts without attempting a comprehensive rule for every nonprofit.

Each Justice accepts this construction directly from §§44–45 and the validated activities. The shared joins in Khan and NYNEX concern competitive analysis, not an antecedent holding already resolving nonprofit coverage; no such holding is assumed.

### Competing rule-of-reason paths

#### Path R: further inquiry before either condemnation

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter would hold that the abbreviated inquiry used below is inadequate for both challenged categories. Abbreviated analysis is lawful when the restraint and record make its harmful competitive tendency sufficiently clear to resolve the material competitive explanation without fuller market inquiry. Here, the potential for disclosure and substantiation rules to improve the reliability of professional-service information requires closer analysis than the lower court's inference from restricted advertising to reduced competition.

**Price:** The actual prohibitions on fee descriptions and the practically prohibitive disclosure conditions for broad discounts are serious restraints and may be unlawful. But suppressing an advertisement does not invariably reduce useful price information: a discount lacking an intelligible baseline or adequate scope may distort comparison. The record's enforcement findings must be retained and the claimed information benefit tested against those practices. The defect is the sufficiency of the competitive analysis, not a judicial finding that every advertisement was deceptive or every CDA condition useful.

**Quality:** Suppression of quality claims without examining their truth is likewise substantial adverse evidence. Yet information that is difficult for patients to verify can operate differently from a withheld, independently useful service. Whether the challenged restrictions improve reliable comparison, suppress useful rivalry, or do both requires more than equating fewer advertisements with fewer dental services. These four do not assume every quality claim false or valueless and do not excuse a blanket restriction simply because its facial rule invokes ethics.

**Strongest counterargument and answer:** The Commission and court below found actual suppression of truthful price and quality information; Indiana Federation of Dentists permits a direct assessment without an exhaustive market definition, and professional status supplies no exemption. Those propositions remain valid. But the rule of reason still must distinguish a restriction on reliable comparison from a measure plausibly improving it. Unlike a naked refusal to supply a desired service whose usefulness is established, these rules bear on the reliability and conditions of the offered information. The explanation has not been sufficiently examined in relation to these particular practices. Deference to supported findings does not settle the independent legal sufficiency of the abbreviated analysis.

**Remedy:** Vacate the enforcement judgment and return the matter to the Ninth Circuit for further proceedings consistent with the required inquiry, including Commission proceedings as appropriate. Retain supported findings under §45(c). Do not require a maximally elaborate market study in every case, automatically reinstate the ALJ's no-market-power conclusion, set a local share threshold, find CDA reasonable, or prescribe a new administrative evidentiary hearing. The needed analysis must fit each actual restraint. Statutory coverage remains resolved.

#### Path A: the existing analysis is sufficient for both categories

Stevens, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would sustain the existing enforcement judgment under the abbreviated rule of reason. The actual enforcement, not the facial ethical aspiration, establishes collective suppression of useful truthful competition. A sufficiently supported direct showing that competitors suppressed a basic means of comparing their services may establish unreasonable restraint without measuring every local market or proving a separate decline in the number of dental procedures.

**Price:** Preventing dentists from advertising truthful low-price descriptions and making broad discount advertising practically unavailable obstructs customers' comparison and dentists' price rivalry. Truthful discounts can convey useful information even without printing the undiscounted charge for every covered service. These four accept genuine disclosure conditions but find that the supported enforcement goes beyond that justification. This does not approve deception, require all discounts to be advertised or condemn every disclosure obligation.

**Quality:** The relevant restraint suppresses quality information without examining whether the particular claim is false or misleading. Dentistry's verification problems justify attention to false claims and inadequate substantiation; they do not establish a competitive benefit from suppressing the supported class of useful truthful information. The inference concerns competition in communicating services and attracting patients, not an invented measured-output finding. The challenged discontinued fear-allaying policy is not revived as a current restraint.

**Strongest counterargument and answer:** Professional-information defects can make restrictions beneficial; statewide membership does not establish each local share, and the ALJ and Commission differed on market power. These four regard those cautions as insufficient to displace the concrete enforcement findings and the competitive mechanism demonstrated here. The asserted justification must answer the conduct found, and the existing inquiry sufficiently shows that the restraint exceeds prevention of deception. They rely on direct competitive effects rather than turning 75% statewide membership into a finding about every locality. This is an application of the rule of reason, not per se condemnation of professional rules.

**Remedy:** Affirm the reviewed enforcement judgment on the existing alternative rule-of-reason ground. No new order term, sanction, damages award, state-law requirement or ban on legitimate antideception enforcement is supplied. ### Shared coverage holding and operative qualifications

The FTC Act reaches this nonprofit professional association because it is organized to carry on business for the profit of its members and its activities confer proximate, substantial economic benefits on their for-profit practices. CDA's advantageous insurance and financing arrangements, commercial support and member-oriented business activities satisfy the statute. Nonprofit form, tax status or the absence of profits distributed to the association itself does not eliminate the members'-profit branch of §44.

The reference to substantial commercial activities in the model describes CDA's established activity, not a new percentage or proportional-activity test. Neither exclusive devotion to member profit nor a substantial fraction of the organization's total activity devoted to that purpose is required. The requisite connection to member profit must be real and proximate; presumed or incidental benefit from professional education alone is not decided sufficient. No ruling is made on every charitable or educational organization, nonprofits with annual surpluses, or entities that compete commercially without serving for-profit members. Coverage does not depend on proof that each member earns a net profit from membership.

All eight adopt this construction under ordinary statutory interpretation. They do not decide whether deference is owed to the Commission's view of its jurisdiction. ### Path R — O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas

#### Rule, price application and quality application

An abbreviated rule-of-reason inquiry can suffice when experience and the restraint's actual character make the likely anticompetitive tendency clear enough to assess it and the material competing explanation. Here the professional market's information disparities make the effects of the challenged restrictions insufficiently obvious for the analysis employed below. More examination of the actual price and quality restraints is required. No fixed sequence of labels or mandatory full industry study replaces the rule of reason; the inquiry must fit the circumstances, details and logic of each restraint.

**Price advertising:** Price communication ordinarily supports comparison and rivalry, and the reported enforcement impedes fee descriptions and broad discount advertisements. But an across-the-board discount without an intelligible baseline or scope may convey less reliable information than accurately specified services and prices. Requiring particular disclosure might improve comparison, harm it or do both. Treating the practical restriction on discount advertising as sufficient proof of harmful net competitive tendency, and demanding empirical justification from CDA on that basis alone, does not adequately examine the professional-information explanation. The point is the legal adequacy of the analysis, not a finding that every advertisement is deceptive, every disclosure condition useful, or CDA's justification ultimately correct.

**Quality advertising:** Restrictions can hinder useful comparison and service rivalry, but professional quality can be difficult for patients to verify. Restrictions on unverifiable or misleading claims may improve information. The court below could not resolve that competing account merely by equating fewer advertisements with a supply limitation in dental services. A possible effect on demand is not itself proof of producer-imposed restriction of service output. This does not deny that quality-advertising restrictions may harm competition, require proof of a measured output decline in every antitrust case, or approve a blanket ban. The actual effect and justification need the fuller analysis missing here.

NCAA supplies flexible analysis where competitive tendencies are established. Professional Engineers rejects a defense that competition itself is undesirable. Indiana Federation of Dentists permits direct analysis of a horizontal agreement withholding a desired service; it does not make every professional information restriction identical to that agreement. Bates recognizes the value of truthful professional price information while preserving the distinct problem of unverifiable quality claims. Khan and NYNEX require attention to the actual restraint and competitive mechanism, without converting these horizontal rules into vertical agreements or giving them immunity.

## Public Projection

## Event

California Dental Association v. Federal Trade Commission, No. 97-1625. Argued January 13, 1999; decided May 24, 1999. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 128 F.3d 720, which affirmed and enforced the Commission's cease-and-desist order. The questions are whether the FTC Act reaches this nonprofit association organized to benefit for-profit dentists and whether the actual price- and quality-advertising restrictions received sufficient rule-of-reason analysis.

CDA's roughly 75% statewide membership, commercial benefits and enforcement are described in the appellate record. The ALJ found practical restrictions on low-fee descriptions, broad discounts and quality claims without individualized truthfulness review. The Commission relied on per se treatment of price restrictions and alternatively on abbreviated analysis; the Ninth Circuit rejected the per se basis but sustained abbreviated review of price and quality separately. The ALJ and Commission differed on market power. Those positions are distinct from a Supreme Court factual finding.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The FTC's statutory coverage is sustained. The enforcement judgment is vacated and the case remanded for further proceedings.

## Holdings

### Coverage includes an association serving its members' commercial profit

**Holding and operative rule:** The FTC Act reaches a nonprofit professional association organized to carry on business for its members' profit when its activities confer proximate, substantial economic benefits on their for-profit operations. CDA satisfies that condition through its insurance, financing, commercial support and other member-business activities; nonprofit form, tax status and absence of distributions to the association do not eliminate the members'-profit branch of section 44.

**Authority:** Souter's coverage opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, directly controls this proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory alternative concerning members' profit would do little work if only the association's own earnings counted. CDA materially advances its members' commercial practices, so the outside suppliers' role does not make those benefits merely incidental. This application does not require exclusive devotion to member profit, a substantial percentage of total activity devoted to it, or proof that each member makes a net profit from membership. The connection must be real and proximate; professional education's presumed or incidental benefit alone is not held sufficient. The Court does not decide coverage of every charity, educational organization, nonprofit with an annual surplus, or commercial competitor without for-profit members. Ordinary statutory interpretation suffices, without deciding deference to the Commission's jurisdictional view. Section 45's sector exceptions remain; none is established for CDA. Neither professional status nor an unverified guideline's description of state law supplies immunity. Coverage authorizes inquiry, not condemnation.

### The inquiry must test the restraint's actual competitive tendency and material explanation

**Holding and operative rule:** An abbreviated rule-of-reason inquiry may suffice when experience and the restraint's actual character make its likely anticompetitive tendency sufficiently clear to assess both the restraint and its material competing explanation. Where professional-information disparities make those effects insufficiently obvious, fuller analysis fitted to the restraint is required; neither a fixed sequence of labels nor a complete industry or market-definition study is invariably compulsory.

**Authority:** Souter's competitive-analysis opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, supplies five direct joins for this rule and both applications below.

**Controlling explanation:** NCAA permits inquiry calibrated to an established competitive mechanism. Professional Engineers rejects the claim that competition itself is undesirable; it does not dispense with examining a plausible claim that a practice improves reliable comparison. Indiana Federation of Dentists permits direct analysis of an agreement withholding a desired service, but does not make every professional-information restriction identical to that agreement. Khan and NYNEX require attention to the actual restraint and mechanism without converting this horizontal association into a vertical arrangement. The FTC's concrete enforcement findings are adverse evidence, not a substitute for the missing analysis of information quality. The Court corrects legal sufficiency and does not reweigh supported findings under section 45(c), independently affirm every economic conclusion, or decide whether substantial evidence supports ultimate liability. It finds neither restraint reasonable and creates no professional immunity. Legitimate restraints on materially false or misleading advertising remain possible under their actual conditions; no categorical per se issue is independently decided.

### Price restrictions require more examination on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** The practical restrictions on fee descriptions and broad discount advertisements cannot be sustained on the abbreviated analysis employed here without adequately examining their asserted effect on the reliability of price comparison. Further inquiry must test the actual enforcement and claimed information benefits; the mere reduction of advertising does not settle the net competitive tendency.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice competitive-analysis opinion directly adopts this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Price communication ordinarily helps consumers compare and dentists compete. Bates recognizes that value while preserving legitimate deception controls. But an across-the-board discount without an intelligible baseline or scope may be unreliable, and a disclosure condition might improve comparison, obstruct it or do both. The reported restrictions on truthful descriptions and practically prohibitive disclosures remain serious evidence against CDA. Their existence alone does not adequately test whether these particular conditions improve reliable information before demanding empirical justification on the assumption of an already established harmful net tendency. This is no finding that every advertisement misleads, that every condition helps, or that CDA's explanation ultimately succeeds. The lower proceedings must perform the missing analysis without automatically adopting the ALJ's no-market-power conclusion, a local-share threshold or mandatory comprehensive market definition.

### Quality advertising is not equivalent to service output

**Holding and operative rule:** Quality-advertising restrictions whose effects depend on the reliability and verifiability of professional information require fuller consideration than equating fewer advertisements with a restriction of dental-service output. The actual conditions and competitive explanation must be assessed; possible effects on demand do not themselves establish a producer-imposed service-output restriction.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice opinion directly controls this distinct application.

**Controlling explanation:** Restricting useful quality information can impede service rivalry, yet patients may have difficulty verifying professional claims. A restraint addressing unverifiable or misleading claims may improve information. Bates recognizes that quality-claim problem, while Indiana Federation's established desired-service restraint provides no automatic answer to this different mechanism. The court below could not settle the matter merely by translating fewer advertisements into reduced dental output. The Court requires neither measured output decline in every antitrust case nor acceptance of a blanket ban. Supported enforcement against truthful information remains material. Fuller examination may condemn either or both restrictions, but the present analysis does not resolve their competitive explanation. The discontinued fear-allaying policy is not treated as a continuing restraint.

## Precedent Treatment

- NCAA v. Board of Regents supplies flexible inquiry fitted to established competitive tendencies; it creates no compulsory full-market procedure.
- National Society of Professional Engineers v. United States remains a bar to defending restraints on the premise that competition itself is undesirable; an information-quality explanation must be tested on its actual competitive terms.
- FTC v. Indiana Federation of Dentists is distinguished at the level of the restraint and information mechanism; its direct-effects method and separation of supported findings from legal sufficiency remain.
- Bates v. State Bar of Arizona supplies the value of truthful price information and the distinct difficulty of unverifiable professional quality claims.
- State Oil Co. v. Khan retains its vertical maximum-price holding and horizontal distinctions; its attention to competitive mechanisms informs this inquiry without conferring immunity.
- NYNEX Corp. v. Discon retains its vertical supplier-substitution holding and distinct horizontal categories; labels or improper purpose do not replace analysis of the actual mechanism.

## Law After Decision

CDA falls within FTC coverage, but the existing price- and quality-advertising liability determination cannot stand on the analysis used. The required inquiry is proportionate to actual restraints and live competitive explanations. The Court establishes neither final liability nor professional immunity, and imposes no universal market-definition trial or required proof of measured service-output decline. Supported agency findings retain their statutory review status. No majority specifies whether the Ninth Circuit may complete the examination on the existing record or must direct Commission reconsideration.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment:** The Chief joins coverage and the holdings requiring fuller, restraint-sensitive inquiry. The suppression of truthful comparison and the possible value of substantiation or discount disclosure must both be tested. He would direct the Commission itself to conduct that examination rather than permit the Ninth Circuit to complete it without Commission reconsideration. He requires no full market-definition trial in every case and grants no immunity for restraints on truthful, nonmisleading information.

**Souter's remedial discussion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas:** These four would leave the Ninth Circuit to decide whether it can adequately assess the Commission's decision on the existing record or should return it for more extensive analysis and an enhanced record. Correcting legal sufficiency neither authorizes judicial reweighing of supported facts nor mandates a new administrative hearing. They make no ultimate substantial-evidence determination.

**Breyer, joined by Stevens, Kennedy and Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** They join coverage and the flexible rule-of-reason method but find the existing analysis sufficient for both categories. Actual enforcement, rather than the Code's facial aspiration against deception, suppresses low-fee descriptions and practically excludes broad discounts. Truthful discounts can inform consumers without listing every undiscounted service price. The supported quality restraint suppresses useful information without examining whether particular claims mislead; the mechanism is interference with communicating qualities and matching dentists to patients, not a finding that the number of procedures fell. Verification problems do not justify the actual breadth once those effects are shown.

On that showing CDA bears the burden of establishing an offsetting competitive benefit. A merely possible advantage does not require the Commission to refute every conceivable theory. These Justices find the inquiry and record adequately answer the reliability claim. Membership, entry barriers, economic benefits and dentists' interest in remaining members support practical effect; the statewide figure is not a finding of equal shares in every locality. Indiana Federation supplies direct-effects analysis and respect for supported Commission findings, while NCAA calibrates depth and Professional Engineers requires a competitive justification. They would affirm the existing enforcement judgment without a new order, sanction or damages. They endorse neither every disclosure restriction nor all professional advertising, and find no need to decide the Commission's per se alternative.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit with coverage resolved and the enforcement judgment vacated. Further proceedings must respect the five-Justice insufficiency holdings and section 45(c)'s treatment of supported findings. The four-Justice appellate option and the Chief's mandatory Commission direction remain separately stated, without either becoming a majority command. No final finding of reasonableness, liability, damages, market power or new sanction is entered.
