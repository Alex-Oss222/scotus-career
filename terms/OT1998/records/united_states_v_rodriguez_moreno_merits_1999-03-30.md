## Holdings

### Continuing kidnapping supplies essential conduct for venue

**Holding and operative rule:** When the charged §924(c) firearm offense is committed during and in relation to a continuing kidnapping, venue lies in any district where that kidnapping continued, even if the firearm was physically used in another district, because commission of the ongoing kidnapping is essential conduct incorporated into the firearm offense. The Government must still prove the distinct firearm act and its required relation to the kidnapping; the predicate's status as a necessary element does not make every related circumstance a venue-conferring act.

**Authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court has the complete seven-Justice coalition identified in Opinion Topology. All seven adopt this rule, its New Jersey application and its limits, supplying direct majority authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court identifies the crime Congress enacted and the conduct constituting it. United States v. Anderson supplies that inquiry; Article III, the Sixth Amendment and Rule 18 tie trial to the charged offense, while §3237(a) permits venue where a continuing offense begins, continues or ends. Section 924(c) requires both firearm use or carriage and commission of the violent crime during and in relation to which that act occurs. Here the defendant continued confining the victim in New Jersey. That continuing kidnapping is incorporated conduct of this charged offense, not a geographical fact borrowed from an unrelated or completed crime. Cabrales forbids locating completed laundering transactions solely by the earlier criminal origin of their proceeds. Its distinction between conduct and circumstance supports, rather than defeats, examining what this different statute makes part of the offense. The contrary construction would require the firearm act and kidnapping to occur simultaneously in the venue district. The statute's incorporation of the continuing kidnapping and §3237(a) permit venue where that essential conduct occurred, although the firearm act occurred elsewhere. This does not dispense with proof of either requirement or make every statutory element a geographical act. It authorizes no venue based solely on remote effects, preparatory acts or evidentiary circumstances, or on a predicate completed before the firearm conduct began.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the Government's broader effects theory or establish a universal prohibition governing every effects-based venue statute. It decides no different conspiracy, aiding-and-abetting, transportation or possession charge, no number-of-violations question, no independent sentencing issue and no other conviction. Cabrales's separate charging reservations remain open.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Stevens, would affirm. Section 924(c) punishes firearm use or carriage occurring during and in relation to the predicate crime. Both acts are elements, and the firearm offense may continue through multiple districts when those acts coincide there. But the unarmed portion of a kidnapping does not extend that distinct firearm offense to every place the victim was held. Cabrales's attention to the particular charged conduct reinforces that limit. The continuing nature of kidnapping therefore does not answer where the combined firearm offense occurred. The dissent does not adopt a rule identifying conduct from statutory verbs alone, disregard the predicate element or resolve how many violations may arise from one predicate. Its venue vacatur would not be an acquittal or a direction concerning a new prosecution.
