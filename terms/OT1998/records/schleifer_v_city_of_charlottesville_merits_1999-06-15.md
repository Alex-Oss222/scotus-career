## Ordinance and material record

The complete contemporaneous text, effective March 1, 1997, governs. It covers unemancipated persons under seventeen, from 12:01 a.m. to 5 a.m. Monday through Friday and 1 a.m. to 5 a.m. Saturday and Sunday. Remaining means lingering or staying, or failing to leave upon a request by an authorized officer or person controlling the place; a prior dispersal order is not invariably an element. Covered locations include public places and their specified common areas, vehicles operating or parked there, and profit-operated establishments inviting the public. Parent includes a biological or adoptive parent with legal custody, including shared custody, or with whom the minor regularly resides; a judicially appointed guardian; and a person at least eighteen standing in loco parentis, as shown by authorization from such a parent or guardian or other circumstances. Emancipation means a court order under Virginia Code §16.1-333.

The eight exceptions cover: accompaniment by a parent; an unforeseen emergency requiring immediate protection of life, limb or property; employment and direct travel to work or home without stop or detour; the sidewalk directly abutting the residence where the minor lives with a parent, without requiring the parent to stand beside the child; attendance at an adult-supervised school, religious, civic, public or similar sponsored activity and direct travel without stop or detour; a parent-directed errand with the required signed writing; interstate travel through, beginning or ending in the City; and exercise of constitutionally protected First Amendment rights, including religion, speech and assembly. The errand document must identify parent and minor, give the parent's address and telephone and a telephone where the parent can be reached, bear the signature, describe the errand and destinations, and specify authorized hours. It is not a general parental-permission exception.

Parents and vehicle owners/operators face distinct offenses for knowingly permitting, allowing or encouraging a violation. Establishment operators/employees face their own knowing-permission offense; prompt police notification that a minor refuses to leave supplies a defense to that establishment offense alone. False identifying information is independently prohibited. Adult knowledge elements are not imported into the minor's presence offense.

Officers must immediately investigate before enforcement against a minor. A first violation produces a verbal warning followed by a mailed written warning to minor and parents; any prior warning for such a violation triggers charge and summons. Release to a parent or bounded temporary care follows as soon as practicable even on the first-warning route. Care must be nonlocked, nonrestrictive and visually supervised, without handcuffing or securing to an object, and may not extend beyond the remaining curfew hours. Refusal to identify minor or parents, inability to locate a parent before curfew ends, or a located parent's failure to accept custody permits the specified nonsecure crisis/shelter or juvenile-court procedures under governing law. The separate adult enforcement provision also uses an initial verbal and mailed warning, followed by charge and summons upon an earlier warning. Violations are Class 4 misdemeanors; no unsupported fine is assigned. A one-year reporting direction to the City Manager is neither a sunset nor proof that a report was completed.

The plaintiffs described movies, meals, music, concerts and socializing; their allegations do not establish that every proposed activity is outside every exception. Local crime and victimization evidence, overnight offense increases and juvenile vulnerability supported the City's protective case. The lower majority credited such evidence, including a 25% rise in juvenile delinquency caseload from 1991 to 1996; that is not a finding that this curfew caused a reduction. The chief's uncertain classifications of coffeehouse political conversation and an unpaid restaurant musician were hypotheticals, not arrests. His promises to favor protected activity in doubtful cases and seek legal advice likewise are testimony, not a binding construction.

## Holdings

### The presented facial vagueness challenge fails

A juvenile curfew that specifies age, hours, covered places and the prohibited act of remaining is not facially vague on this record merely because its exception for constitutionally protected First Amendment activity requires legal application in particular encounters. The exception preserves the full constitutional protection; uncertain hypotheticals do not establish that this ordinance's common criminal predicate lacks an intelligible standard, and genuinely distinct unconstitutional applications remain open.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, has eight direct votes for the rule, explanation and limits.

Morales requires meaningful guidance for the predicate authorizing dispersal and holds that a warning cannot repair its absence. This ordinance supplies age, time, place and conduct criteria absent from Chicago's apparent-purpose rule. Peoples Rights Organization distinguishes difficult application of an existing criterion from the omission of a central criterion. Grayned and Hoffman Estates require attention to the actual law and its intelligible operation; Kolender's independent demand for enforcement guidance remains controlling. The expression exception refers to constitutional law, not an officer's personal approval of a speaker's purpose. Religion, speech and assembly illustrate its coverage, and protected conversation or music does not lose protection because it occurs in a commercial venue.

The chief's uncertain answers about political discussion and musical performance deserve attention, but they are not findings of arrests or binding interpretations that exclude protected activity. His assurance of restraint likewise is no authoritative cure. The Court relies on the enacted criteria and preserved constitutional rights, not that assurance or a claim that warnings remove all restraint. The first-warning route can still require release or temporary care. A proven unconstitutional expression restriction remains subject to challenge; the facial claim here does not establish a missing standard common to every application. The Court neither deletes the speech exception nor rewrites the ordinance to narrow it.

### The substantive judgment rests on separate grounds

Five Justices sustain the judgment rejecting the presented substantive liberty and parental-authority claims. No five join a common full scrutiny rationale: three assume significant interests and find the actual protective fit sufficient, while two apply ordinary review because the asserted entitlement has not been established.

**Authority:** The judgment has the five votes identified above. O'Connor's Part II has only the three votes of O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer; Scalia's concurrence has Scalia and Thomas. Neither becomes a Court-wide scrutiny rule through aggregation. The assumed-interest approach does not decide the nonfundamentality proposition, and a restraint failing its fit inquiry could still survive ordinary review. Neither rationale is shown to be the governing logical subset of the other under Marks. The Court therefore enters no general standard for every juvenile curfew or fit-parent objection.

## Separate Writings

O'Connor's Part II, joined by Kennedy and Breyer, assumes significant interests in ordinary public activity and parental direction. On this record, the narrow age band, late hours, operative avenues for parental accompaniment, supervised activity, errands, work, emergencies, travel and expression, together with local protective evidence, sufficiently support the restraint. They acknowledge that permission alone is not an exception and that even a first encounter can cause removal or temporary care. Prince's protective authority does not erase minors' rights; the actual interests and regulation must be examined together. Their approach follows the assumed-interest method of O'Connor's Glucksberg concurrence and Breyer's precise-interest analysis. They neither endorse the lower court's unnecessary strict-scrutiny alternative nor declare every family burden subject only to rational review.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurs in Part I and the judgment. The precedents do not establish the asserted constitutional entitlement to override this bounded protective regulation. Glucksberg's claim-specific treatment and the objective operation recognized in their Morales position support ordinary review. The actual age, hours, exceptions and protective grounds rationally serve child safety and public order. They do not join Part II's assumed-interest formulation, impose a heightened framework, treat every warning as a cure, import the adults' knowledge elements into the minor's offense or deny the restraint involved in temporary care. Actual protected expression remains protected.

Stevens, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, concurs in Part I and dissents from the substantive disposition. Defined boundaries answer the presented facial claim, but do not settle whether the resulting restraint is justified. The ordinance still reaches an identified class of otherwise lawful, parent-authorized activity outside its exceptions, including unaccompanied ordinary activity that the limited errand provision does not authorize. Their Glucksberg analyses and Morales liberty explanation require justification responsive to both the child's liberty and the parent's direction. General juvenile crime and vulnerability evidence does not sufficiently justify that displacement on this record. Souter's commensurate-justification approach supports the same bounded application without a universal scrutiny formula. They would remand for declaratory and injunctive relief fitted to established plaintiffs and supported applications, leaving ordinary remedial and state-law severability analysis below. They recognize neither an absolute parental veto nor a right to remain anywhere at night and would not automatically invalidate independent false-information liability.

Stone-Zsela dissents. Applying fair-notice and enforcement-guidance principles, he regards the undefined First Amendment exception as part of the criminal boundary itself. Minors, parents and officers must decide the constitutional status of speech, association, assembly and related movement during an encounter; uncertain classifications of political conversation and performance reveal the practical defect. A warning postpones prosecution but does not cure the initial stop, removal, recorded violation, parental exposure or repeat-offense consequences. Morales's warning principle and the existing anti-discretion rule therefore require reversal. He would order declaratory and prospective relief unless a binding state-law construction legally available on remand, consistent with ordinary saving-construction rules, supplies objective and sufficiently precise boundaries. He reserves the full substantive liberty and parental-authority questions, permits a narrower curfew with clear exceptions and enforcement standards, and decides no post-judgment application or damages claim. His position does not invalidate clear exceptions for work, direct travel, emergencies, parental accompaniment or specified expressive events.
