## Event

Equality Foundation of Greater Cincinnati, Inc. v. City of Cincinnati; Sixth Circuit Nos. 94-3855 and 94-3973 (merits), No. 94-4280 (fees). Merits decision, December 3, 1998. On review of the Sixth Circuit's October 23, 1997 judgment, 128 F.3d 289, reversing the District Court, vacating its permanent injunction and fee award, and directing judgment for defendants.

The question is whether Cincinnati Charter Article XII's withdrawal and prospective prohibition of municipal sexual-orientation protection satisfies equal protection under Romer, and what follows for the injunction and fees. The charter provision followed ordinances covering City employment and, separately, private employment, housing and public accommodations. Article XII provides:

> NO SPECIAL CLASS STATUS MAY BE GRANTED BASED UPON SEXUAL ORIENTATION, CONDUCT OR RELATIONSHIPS.
>
> The City of Cincinnati and its various Boards and Commissions may not enact, adopt, enforce or administer any ordinance, regulation, rule or policy which provides that homosexual, lesbian, or bisexual orientation, status, conduct, or relationship constitutes, entitles, or otherwise provides a person with the basis to have any claim of minority or protected status, quota preference or other preferential treatment. This provision of the City Charter shall in all respects be self-executing.
>
> Any ordinance, regulation, rule or policy enacted before this amendment is adopted that violates the foregoing prohibition shall be null and void and of no force or effect.

Generally applicable laws and access to higher levels of Ohio government remain available. The Court considers the provision's removal of ordinary municipal protection, not merely quotas or preferential treatment.

## Holdings

### The municipal class-specific foreclosure fails ordinary rational-basis review

**Operative rule:** Article XII's combined withdrawal of existing sexual-orientation nondiscrimination protection and prospective prohibition of ordinary municipal protection for the named class violates equal protection because its actual breadth bears no rational relationship to the asserted fiscal, associational, regulatory and local-policy interests. Municipal scope, continued general-law remedies and access to higher government do not cure that unexplained selective disability; the rule does not make every repeal unlawful or require the City to enact a particular protection.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice Opinion of the Court identified in Opinion Topology directly adopts the ordinary rationality rule, application and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Romer requires a rational connection between legitimate objectives and the actual class-specific disadvantage. Heller's separate holdings preserve that connection while allowing reasonable generalizations, conceivable grounds and imperfect categories without formal findings, empirical certainty or least restrictive means. Moreno and Cleburne forbid bare harm and irrational prejudice; no finding about every voter's private motive is required.

Local fiscal management, regulatory restraint, private association and durable policy are legitimate subjects. Yet Article XII reaches the City's own employment and ordinary protection across housing, public accommodations and private employment, regardless of a particular protection's expense or operation. Avoiding some complaint and defense costs does not explain why this class alone becomes prospectively ineligible for all that ordinary protection. Charter authority explains who sets policy and its durability, not the missing rational connection to the full selective burden.

Hurley protects actual private expression; it does not make impersonal services or the City's own conduct categorically expressive association. Those distinct concerns cannot justify this entire public and private reach. Bowers's specified-conduct holding likewise does not explain the broader disability based on orientation or status. No particular expression or religious exemption is adjudicated and Bowers is not overruled.

The City emphasizes that Ohio institutions and general laws remain accessible. Romer already assumed generally applicable protection remained. The relevant breadth is the civic activity excluded from ordinary protection, not simply the number of governmental levels affected. Ordinary repeal remains permissible; the reported concession about repeal is not assent to prospective charter foreclosure. Coalition's distinct racial political-process inquiry supplies neither a heightened orientation standard nor a universal entitlement to favorable legislation. This decision applies ordinary rationality to Article XII's own operation.

**Controlling explanation:** Removing the barrier returns ordinary lawmaking to its lawful operation; it does not require the City to adopt every requested remedy or prevent a lawful policy revision.
