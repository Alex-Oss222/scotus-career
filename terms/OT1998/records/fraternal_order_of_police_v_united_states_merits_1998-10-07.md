## Holdings

**Common authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Every holding below has nine direct joins; no judgment-only aggregation or fractured-decision rule is used.

### Standing for this official-use controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** An association may seek prospective, uniform relief against this official-use exclusion when its chief law-enforcement members suffer a concrete inability lawfully to arm affected officers, the claim is germane to the association, and the requested relief requires no individualized member participation. The chiefs' direct exposure under the supply prohibition and their relationship to the affected officers also permit assertion of the officers' equality interest in this controversy; no injury supporting every individual private-possession or restoration claim is established.

**Controlling explanation:** *Hunt* supplies the member-injury, germaneness and participation requirements. The chiefs allege an actual operational constraint and exposure when they supply firearms or ammunition knowing or having reasonable cause to believe that a recipient has a qualifying conviction. The desired prospective relief is uniform, without individual damages calculations. *United Food* preserves the constitutional requirement of real member injury and distinguishes the prudential participation condition; its WARN-specific relaxation is not treated as a general waiver here. *Craig* and *Barrows* permit attention to the directly regulated supplier relationship: compliance by the chiefs with the challenged restriction is the act that withholds the asserted benefit from officers. The Court does not create a general third-party standing exception or assume injuries unrelated to this official-use claim.

### Equal protection does not require extending the older exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may withhold section 925(a)(1)'s governmental-use exception from the domestic-violence-misdemeanor supply and possession provisions while retaining that exception for the felony provision when the distinction rationally closes a practical gap in restrictions on access to weapons. The Fifth Amendment does not require Congress simultaneously to perfect every older exemption, and the challenged exclusion satisfies that rule on the developed official-use claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Domestic-violence misdemeanants are not a suspect class. *Beach* and *Lee Optical* permit incremental regulation on a conceivable legitimate ground, while *Heller v. Doe* and *Romer* require a relation between the actual disadvantage and a legitimate end. The covered offenses concern force, attempted force or threatened deadly-weapon use within the statutory domestic relationships, with the conviction safeguards and relief qualifications stated below. Congress could address violent conduct treated as a misdemeanor even though the older felony regime and overlapping state or professional rules already remove many felons from armed work. Preventing official issuance from defeating the newly enacted disability fits that gap. This is an available analytical justification, not an assertion about an argument actually made in the government's brief or an invented congressional finding.

The contrary comparison is substantial: a less serious offense may produce a heavier official-use disability, and state restrictions and relief differ. But Congress need not conclude that misdemeanants are more dangerous than felons to close an inadequately covered route of access. The solution's incompleteness does not make that connection irrational. The Court assumes neither that every State excludes every felon nor that a public-safety label alone answers equality review. The statutory avenues for conviction relief also foreclose describing this disability as literally without relief. No claim of a universal constitutional restoration procedure is decided.

### Personal arms protection does not compel this governmental-use exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Recognition of an individual's arms right does not itself require a governmental-use exemption for persons with a qualifying domestic-violence conviction. On this developed official-use claim, the adjudicated connection to violent misuse and the statute's conviction safeguards supply a concrete justification for retaining the exclusion; public employment does not eliminate that connection or make a service weapon inaccessible to its possessor.

**Controlling explanation:** *Love*, *Hale*, *Rybar* and *Wright* require personal invocation and an actual examination of the restriction, rather than a collective-militia premise. Official work alone cannot extinguish a personal right, just as the commercial setting in *Rybar* did not. Here, however, the claim seeks an exemption allowing government issuance to a category defined by qualifying violent conduct. Preventing armed recurrence bears directly on that predicate. The force or deadly-weapon element, domestic relationship and counsel, jury-where-entitled and conviction-relief provisions constrain the category. This ground concerns actual conduct and statutory safeguards, not unpopular identity or an unsupported declaration that all misdemeanants are dangerous. A governmental label does not neutralize the firearm's availability to the person whose conviction supplies the concern.

The Court sustains this exclusion without deciding every application to private keeping, every misdemeanor disability, an improperly classified conviction or a missing statutory safeguard. It adopts neither a universal scrutiny tier nor a rule that absence of an entitlement to public employment alone defeats an arms claim. *Wright* required an application left expressly undone in a different machinegun case; it did not establish automatic remand whenever a personal right is invoked.

### Remedy and questions not reached

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the developed constitutional objections do not invalidate this exclusion, section 928 supplies no basis to extend the governmental-use exception. Reversal returns the case for a consistent judgment and any grounds that are both properly preserved and still undecided; it does not decide their preservation, revive an undeveloped theory, or automatically reinstate every District Court rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 928 preserves valid provisions and applications after invalidity; it does not command extension of a statutory exception for administrative convenience. No invalidity requiring such a choice is found here. The Court therefore does not select between extension and removal as remedies for a different constitutional defect. The section 922(o) commerce decisions do not by themselves decide the different section 922(g)(9) regime, and direct regulation of possession or supply is distinct from compelled state administration. Any properly preserved unresolved commerce or commandeering ground must be identified and considered on its own; the undeveloped labels in the original complaint receive no merits judgment or automatic revival. Whether civil rights never lost count as restored remains unresolved. No individual defective-conviction claim or universal constitutional restoration procedure is decided.

### Statutory scope retained throughout

The holding concerns firearms and ammunition in the governmental channels defined by section 925(a)(1): transportation, shipment, receipt, possession or importation of items imported for, sold or shipped to, or issued for the use of the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. That provision separately withholds its exception for sections 922(d)(9), 922(g)(9), and provisions concerning firearms prohibited by section 922(p). It is not an exemption for every privately held weapon of a public employee and is distinct from the machinegun exceptions in section 922(o)(2).

Section 922(d)(9)'s supply prohibition requires knowledge or reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction. Section 922(g)(9)'s separate language addresses interstate or foreign shipment or transportation, possession in or affecting commerce, and receipt of an item previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Neither provision receives the other's elements by implication. Section 922(d)'s existing exception for specified licensees permitted to continue under section 925(b), or persons granted section 925(c) relief, remains. Section 925(b) concerns an importer, manufacturer, dealer or collector indicted for a crime punishable by more than one year, continuing under an existing license, with a timely renewal application when required, during indictment and until any conviction becomes final. Section 925(c)'s discretionary public-safety and public-interest relief and review of an actual denial establish no funded administrative process, actual denial, judicial jurisdiction or entitlement for an individual in this case.

The qualifying misdemeanor must be under federal or state law and have an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian; a person sharing a child with the victim; a person cohabiting or formerly cohabiting as spouse, parent or guardian; or a person similarly situated to a spouse, parent or guardian. Counsel or a knowing and intelligent waiver is required. If the person was entitled to a jury under the jurisdiction's law, there must have been a jury trial or a knowing and intelligent waiver by plea or otherwise; no new jury entitlement is created.

A conviction does not count when expunged or set aside, or when the person was pardoned or had civil rights restored if the jurisdiction provides for loss of civil rights for that offense, subject to the statute's express-firearms-reservation clause. That clause names a pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibiting shipment, transportation, possession or receipt of firearms; it does not separately repeat set-aside. The Court does not enlarge that wording or resolve its application to an individual. These are statutory limits on the rule, not newly imposed evidentiary procedures.

- *Craig v. Boren* and *Barrows v. Jackson* support the directly regulated relationship for the officers' equality claim; no universal third-party standing rule follows.
- *FCC v. Beach Communications* and *Williamson v. Lee Optical* permit fitted incremental regulation; they do not make a generic public-safety assertion conclusive.
- *Heller v. Doe* retains both its actual proof-burden and family-participation holdings; its requirement of a rational relation to the challenged disadvantage is applied here.
- *Romer v. Evans* retains its protection against the unrelated blanket exclusion it addressed; this conduct-defined access restriction is distinguished by its actual regulatory connection.
- *Love*, *Hale*, *Rybar* and *Wright* are applied to personal invocation and separate examination of a restriction. None supplies a universal scrutiny tier, categorical misdemeanor rule or automatic arms remand. Their machinegun commerce and tax-registration grounds do not decide this separate statute's constitutional power question.

## Law After Decision

Effective October 7, 1998, the developed official-use exclusion survives the equality and personal-arms challenges, and the judicial extension of section 925(a)(1) is reversed. The Court applies existing associational-standing and rationality doctrine to this uniform official-supply controversy and sustains a conduct-linked restriction without creating a universal arms-disability test. All statutory conviction safeguards, relief exclusions and governmental-channel qualifications remain operative. Private-possession applications, individual classification and restoration disputes, never-lost civil rights, and any properly preserved unresolved commerce or commandeering ground remain outside this decision's merits holding.
