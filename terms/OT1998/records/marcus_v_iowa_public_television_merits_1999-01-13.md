## Controlling holdings

#### 1. The Natural Law Party committee's policy challenge is capable of repetition yet evading review

**Holding and operative rule:** A challenge to completed pre-election broadcasts remains justiciable for the same continuing party committee when its established relationship to several excluded candidates, general electoral function, the broadcaster's general selection practice and its recurring program support a reasonable expectation of another short candidate-access controversy involving that committee. Both the period too short for complete review and the same claimant's reasonable prospect of recurrence are required; neither election timing nor an abstract organizational purpose alone suffices.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees that the Court reaches the merits but does not join this threshold explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Natural Law Party committee's common selection-policy challenge survives the 1996 broadcasts. The stipulated background distinguishes that general party organization from the separate committee formed for Marcus's 1996 campaign. Its participation with excluded candidates across congressional districts connects its electoral function to this broadcaster's actual selection practice. That concrete pattern, the general policy and the established recurring Iowa Press program support a reasonable expectation that this same committee will again confront the disputed practice. The opportunity for pre-election appearances is too short for complete ordinary appellate review. Spencer v. Kemna requires both facts and forbids a merely possible injury to others from supplying recurrence. Norman v. Reed supplies the comparison to continuing party interests, although its distinct party-qualification consequences are absent here. Chandler v. Miller established recurrence through an individual's stated intention to run again; it does not make that particular proof indispensable for a continuing organizational claimant. Honig v. Doe permits a grounded assessment of likelihood rather than requiring certainty of repetition. The unknown next candidate and unscheduled next debate make recurrence less certain, but do not erase the established relationship between this committee and the general practice. The conclusion is an inference from those facts, not a finding of a new candidacy, later exclusion, unchanged policy in 1999 or later campaign activity. The absence of contrary evidence does not shift the jurisdictional burden.

**Limits:** The surviving common claim does not separately preserve every individual's expired demand, establish future standing for every named plaintiff or supply an independent current injury from program-guide language. There is no general election exception, relaxed jurisdiction because review was granted, presumed reputational consequence or order to recreate a past broadcast.

#### 2. The actually selective debate is a nonpublic forum

**Holding and operative rule:** A state broadcaster's candidate debate is a nonpublic forum when the broadcaster actually retains editorial selection rather than opening the program generally to all qualified candidates. Restrictions must reasonably serve the program's journalistic purpose and remain viewpoint neutral; public ownership, a journalism label and ballot qualification alone decide neither access nor validity.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion is joined by all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Agreement on classification and the governing requirements does not imply agreement that Iowa's standards satisfy them.

**Controlling explanation:** Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes supplies the governing rule because Iowa Press retained selection by invitation. The District Court independently adopted the finding that the broadcaster intended admission only for invited candidates, and the actual format paired selected candidates with a host and political reporters. Calling the programs debates, as the District Court did, does not transform them into an open microphone; calling them bona fide news interviews does not immunize them. Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educational Fund and Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators' Association direct attention to actual access and permitted purpose. Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia preserves the speakers' private viewpoints within the actual opportunity. The question is therefore whether this selective platform was administered reasonably and without viewpoint discrimination. It is not whether every ballot-qualified candidate must be admitted or whether the broadcaster's public status makes all candidate speech its own message.

**Limits:** A program genuinely opened to qualifying candidates presents a different access question. Reasonable selection is permitted; sham criteria, incumbent favoritism, inconsistent administration and unpopular-viewpoint exclusions remain subject to review.

#### 3. Iowa's adjudicated selection satisfies Forbes; the forum claim and its derivative relief fail

**Holding and operative rule:** On this record, the selective format, legitimate informing purpose, editorial-integrity policies, evidence of electoral engagement and public interest, and independently credited findings support reasonable and viewpoint-neutral selection. Forbes imposes no additional universal numerical formula, written-policy, advance-publication or prescribed investigative prerequisite; the affirmed absence of a forum violation defeats declaratory, injunctive and guide-correction relief sought as remedies for that violation.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer: six Justices. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg dissent from the sufficiency of Iowa's standards and would vacate the rejection of the common policy claim and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Forbes permits the editorial judgment established here. Instruction 15 described newsworthiness through sufficient audience and public interest. It operated alongside policies of fairness, objectivity, balance, independence from pressure and an informed citizenry, and testimony about campaign activity, organization, prior performance, fundraising breadth, media recognition and public engagement. Those considerations bear on journalistic purpose; they are not a binding checklist or findings that every candidate lacked each characteristic. The judge independently assessed credibility and adopted findings of genuine selective editorial judgment, rather than exclusion because of disagreement with political views. That finding matters, but does not alone settle constitutional reasonableness. Petitioners also asserted insufficient individual inquiry, missing candidate information, substantial publicity, invitations from opponents and fundraising comparisons. Those contentions do not establish clear error in the credited findings or compel a different constitutional judgment. The reported figures prove neither an adopted dollars rule nor materially equal electoral significance. The Court does not declare every allegation false or reduce the challenge to ballot qualification. Regan v. Time, Inc., which concerns governmental restrictions on another publisher's content, does not invalidate all content-sensitive selection in a nonpublic candidate forum. Nor does Forbes make professional sincerity conclusive. The actual record supplies the reasonable connection here without judicial substitution of a different editorial ranking.

**Limits and remedy:** The Court does not approve categorical exclusion of idea-centered campaigns, manipulation favoring incumbent parties, or rejection of a genuinely viable minor-party candidate simply because of party status. Offering another program does not cure viewpoint discrimination. The program-guide correction request fails only as derivative relief for the rejected forum claim; no independent cause of action, current guide dissemination, reputational injury or correction entitlement is decided. The judgment orders no new broadcast, future inclusion, damages or statutory access remedy.

Iowa's fairness policies and illustrative factors describe professional judgment, but Instruction 15 still leaves officials to decide which candidacies interest the audience without adequate operative limits. The dissent accepts the adverse findings about viewpoint motive. Its objection is that sincere discretion and absence of hostility do not cure inadequate standards under Cornelius, Shuttlesworth and Rosenberger. The candidates' assertions about missing information, publicity and comparisons show why meaningful review matters; the dissent does not treat those assertions as findings of equal electoral significance or discrimination.

The dissent would vacate rejection of the committee's common policy claim and remand for consideration under adequately constrained selection standards. It would return the program-guide request for consideration of its connection to permissible equitable relief and any continuing injury the relief could redress, without establishing an independent guide claim or an entitlement to correction. It would order neither automatic ballot-based admission, a universal numerical threshold, a replacement 1996 broadcast, compulsory future programming nor damages. These views remain noncontrolling; they do not change the operative Forbes rule.

## Procedure After Action
The Eighth Circuit judgment rejecting the common forum claim and derivative relief stands. No new broadcast, particular future appearance or corrective guide statement is required. The decision does not create an independently live individual claim or resolve a separate guide cause of action.
