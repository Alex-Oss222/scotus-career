### Hanlon v. Berger

#### Review scope and separable conduct

The reviewed judgment is the Ninth Circuit's rejection of the federal officials' qualified immunity on media participation in the March 1993 ranch search. The warrant covered the ranch and appurtenant structures but expressly excluded the residence. The appellate record, read favorably to the Bergers, reports an advance media arrangement, cameras on official vehicles, media observation and recording, and Scrafford's concealed microphone transmitting conversations inside the home. The first entry with Berger's consent is undisputed; later entry, consent and residential search allegations remain disputed. The lower court's collateral-estoppel discussion distinguishes the criminal warrant challenge from this media-execution issue; no new criminal disposition follows.

The physical media-entry rule must be applied to a place in which the Bergers actually have a protected interest. The 75,000-acre title, a camera's presence on the ranch, proximity of outbuildings to the home, and the presence of a guard dog do not by themselves establish the constitutional status of every recorded area. The apparent curtilage and enclosed-building evidence cannot be disregarded either. Dunn, Oliver and the present actual-access cases require the particular space and intrusion. Mere commercial purpose does not turn otherwise unprotected open fields into a protected Fourth Amendment place.

An officer's consensual participation in a conversation, undisclosed transmission of that conversation, an additional unauthorized residential entry or search, and a physically accompanying camera crew are distinct acts. White, Hoffa and Lewis are substantial contrary authority to a constitutional recording theory; the distinction based on commercial dissemination is arguable but is not a constitutional holding of Dietemann. Neither a decision about invited media nor immunity on that theory conclusively resolves a separately preserved, independently unlawful home search. The exact scope of the officer petition remains controlling; no private-media Bivens, joint-action, Wiretap Act, state tort, conversion or injunction judgment is determined here.

### Hanlon v. Berger

#### Reconciled propositions, components and scope

**H1 — Protected-space entry principle:** Officials may not use authority to execute a warrant to introduce or facilitate nonassisting media intrusion into a place in which the claimants possess their own Fourth Amendment protection, absent consent or a separate lawful basis for the added intrusion. All eight Associates join the principle independently from common entering law. The Bergers' favorable record supports a protected-space media-intrusion claim; the decision does not adjudicate that every filmed place or every alleged act falls within it.

The warrant covers the ranch and appurtenant structures but excludes the residence. That description is not a finding that every place on the ranch is constitutionally protected or that every alleged home entry was nonconsensual. The close outbuildings and guard-dog evidence cannot be ignored, but neither establishes the constitutional status of all filmed locations. The supported favorable account must remain favorable under Hunter and ordinary summary judgment; uncertainty is not resolved against the Bergers.

**H2 — Actual protected interest remains necessary:** Commercial purpose or trespass alone does not convert open fields into Fourth Amendment protected space. Oliver and Dunn retain their actual roles; the particular residential surrounding area, enclosed structure, access, use and intrusion must be assessed. All eight reject the lower opinion's alternative suggestion that absence of a law-enforcement purpose by itself disables the open-fields doctrine. This rejects that broad legal proposition, not the lower court's separate recognition of evidence supporting protection of the actual outbuildings. It does not reverse every factual characterization or require a new evidentiary hearing.

**H3 — March 1993 personal immunity:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer grant the federal petitioners immunity on the reviewed media-participation theory, including the accompanying observation/recording enterprise, even on the strongest supported protected-space account. Harlow and Anderson govern; Bills, Prahl and Dietemann retain the actual limits described above. Advance planning and the written commercial arrangement make the challenged conduct deliberate, but do not supply a missing settled constitutional application. The seven do not infer subjective innocence, actual execution assistance, consent to the crew, or legality from the immunity defense.

Stevens refuses immunity for a supported official facilitation of unauthorized physical media intrusion into protected residential space. His preconduct warrant/home rule and Bills make that wrong apparent despite the television medium and elaborate arrangement. He does not extend this conclusion automatically to consensual participant recording, every transmission, every official or a legally unprotected field. His protected-space physical-entry immunity position remains contrary to the seven.

**H4 — Distinct conduct and claims:** Scrafford's initially consensual entry and participation in conversation, the undisclosed microphone transmission, disputed subsequent consent and residential search, and physical accompaniment by the crew are distinct acts. White, Hoffa and Lewis present the invited-participant counterargument; Dietemann's tort holding does not settle its constitutional answer. All eight refuse to turn H1 into a categorical participant-recording or broadcast holding. The seven may resolve personal immunity on the reviewed media-enterprise theory without deciding that additional merits question. A genuinely distinct nonconsensual home entry or search, if properly preserved and available, must retain its own legal and procedural treatment; this immunity ruling is not an all-operation license. No new claim or waived issue is revived.

The officer petition does not bring the private defendants' Bivens/joint-action ruling, Wiretap Act judgment, state trespass, conversion, emotional-distress claims or publication-injunction ruling within review merely because those rulings appear in the same reported appellate decision. No reversal, vacatur or affirmance of those distinct components is supplied. No absolute-prosecutorial-immunity ruling, new Bivens extension, damages determination or final individual-act finding is made.

## Holdings

### A search warrant supplies no unrelated media access to protected space

**Holding and operative rule:** Officials may not use authority to execute a warrant to introduce or facilitate nonassisting media intrusion into a place in which the claimants possess their own Fourth Amendment protection, absent consent or a separate lawful basis for the added intrusion. Legitimate civilian assistance tied to lawful execution remains distinct; official authority to enter does not itself grant unrelated private visitors the same access.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices, directly controls this bounded rule. The favorable record supports a protected-space intrusion claim without establishing that every filmed place or act falls within it.

**Controlling explanation:** Payton protects residential entry, while Summers permits reasonable incidental measures serving execution rather than every added private use. Hicks and Garrison preserve the relationship between authorized intrusion and its actual extent. Current Carter and Redmon require each claimant's own protected interest and the actual manner of access; Ramirez likewise separates lawful execution from objective-unrelated intrusion. The ranch warrant therefore does not authorize a broadcast enterprise merely because agents were lawfully present. Publicity and general accountability do not establish actual assistance. Conversely, assistance need not be forbidden merely because a warrant does not name each genuine helper. Consent or independent lawful authority can supply a different basis, but neither can be assumed for the media from consent to an officer. The Court does not equate every recording with a seizure, constitutionalize every provision of section 3105, or declare every ranch location protected. The additional media purpose does not invalidate every otherwise lawful official act.

### The particular protected place and intrusion must still be identified

**Holding and operative rule:** Commercial purpose, trespass or ownership alone does not eliminate the open-fields doctrine or establish a claimant's Fourth Amendment interest. Courts must assess the actual residential surroundings, enclosed structure, access, use and intrusion, preserving supported favorable evidence without treating any one fact as conclusive for the entire ranch.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by all nine Justices. The Court rejects the proposition that absence of a law-enforcement purpose alone makes otherwise unprotected open fields protected.

**Controlling explanation:** Oliver preserves open-fields treatment and Dunn directs attention to the actual residential surroundings, including proximity, enclosure, use and shielding. The outbuildings' proximity and guard-dog evidence cannot be ignored, but do not classify all recorded areas. Carter's personal-interest rule and Redmon's actual-space inquiry require separation of limited access from investigative authority. Rejecting the lower opinion's broad purpose exception does not reject its separate evidence supporting protection of particular structures or resolve a disputed fact against the Bergers. No new evidentiary hearing or ranch-wide classification is required. The warrant's exclusion of the residence likewise does not answer whether a particular later entry was consensual. The physical crew, consensual conversation, undisclosed microphone transmission and alleged later residential search remain distinct acts.

### March 1993 law does not defeat immunity on this reviewed media theory

**Holding and operative rule:** The federal petitioners receive qualified immunity on the reviewed media-accompaniment, observation and recording-enterprise theory because the particular constitutional application was not sufficiently clear in March 1993, even accepting the strongest supported protected-space account. That defense does not declare the enterprise lawful or protect every genuinely distinct, properly preserved nonconsensual residential entry or search.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; these seven directly adopt the immunity conclusion. Stone-Zsela and Stevens disagree as to supported physical intrusion into protected space.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson protect reasonable legal mistakes at the particularized level; Hunter requires using the owners' supported favorable account rather than resolving historical disputes against them. Bills supplies serious contrary evidence: it condemned a private employee's unrelated inspection after the warrant's object was found, while separately accepting genuine identification assistance under a later warrant. The Court does not read its later-officer immunity as protection for every original invitation. But that distinct inspection does not conclusively settle the accompanying media enterprise. Prahl rejected the presented federal theory that filming and broadcasting an otherwise reasonable search, without more, made it unreasonable; its separate trespass ruling does not erase that qualified contrary federal evidence. Dietemann upheld state privacy liability and expressly reserved federal constitutional liability, so its later characterization cannot establish a federal recording rule. White, Hoffa and Lewis supply a substantial distinct invited-participant argument. Advance planning and the commercial agreement make the conduct deliberate, not clearly classified under then-established constitutional law. The absence of an identical case is not decisive, and no policy or hypothetical assistance supplies legality. Elder requires all relevant earlier authority; Crawford-El preserves the separate defense. Later media, privacy and execution rulings cannot give retrospective notice. The disputed Moncrief and Higbee accounts are unnecessary to the conclusion.

### The mandate is confined to the reviewed official component

**Holding and operative rule:** Only the reviewed federal-official media-participation determination is vacated and remanded; private-media joint-action and damages questions, the Wiretap Act, state trespass, conversion and emotional-distress claims, and publication-injunction rulings receive no disposition here. A genuinely distinct residential entry or search remains subject to its own preserved factual, legal and procedural predicates; no new or waived claim is revived.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join Part IV's limited-vacatur and scope conclusion. Seven also require the immunity direction in Part III; Stevens refuses that direction for the physical protected-space intrusion. Stone-Zsela would affirm denial of immunity and remand instead.

**Controlling explanation:** The officer petition does not enlarge merely because the lower court disposed of other claims in the same opinion. The shared correction separates protected-space accompaniment from open-field observation, consensual participant transmission and distinct alleged searches. White, Hoffa and Lewis present a question different from the crew's physical entry, and Dietemann does not supply the federal answer. The Court therefore adopts no categorical participant-recording or broadcast rule; the seven-Justice legal immunity ground makes that additional merits ruling unnecessary. The favorable outbuilding evidence is preserved, not rejected, and immunity on the reviewed theory does not await conclusive classification of every location. The mandate does not decide prosecutorial absolute immunity, create a new Bivens action, determine every participant's acts, revisit the distinct criminal warrant grounds or award damages.

## Law After Decision

Warrant authority does not permit unrelated media intrusion into the claimants' own protected space. Open fields remain distinct, and no ranch-wide privacy rule or categorical broadcast rule is established. The federal petitioners receive immunity on the reviewed media-enterprise theory under the March 1993 legal landscape; that conclusion does not authorize similar future conduct or dispose of distinct available home-search and unreviewed claims. No mandatory constitutional-first sequence is adopted.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** He joins the actual-space principles and accepts a limited vacatur to separate physical accompaniment, open-field observation, participant transmission and distinct acts. He would deny immunity where officials facilitated unauthorized physical media entry into protected residential space. Payton and Summers already confined warrant authority, and Bills confirmed that police could not sponsor an unrelated private intrusion. A planned commercial arrangement supplies no excuse, and the novelty of television does not remove notice of that physical wrong. He rejects both a purpose-based abolition of open fields and Dietemann's characterization as settled federal recording law. He decides no categorical immunity denial for consensual conversation transmission, every official's conduct, damages or private-media liability.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** The Chief would affirm the denial of immunity and remand for supported constitutional injury and available remedies. He joins the requirement of the Bergers' own protected space and a warrant-unrelated intrusion; ownership of open fields alone is insufficient. Officially admitting a private crew to use protected property for independent broadcast production is an obvious purpose-and-scope violation under the existing warrant limits. Exact factual precedent is unnecessary for that obvious application, while the constitutional conclusion does not automatically settle immunity in other cases. Later Wilson and Ramirez decisions supply no earlier notice. He preserves genuine execution assistance, each officer's personal responsibility, causation, injury, other defenses and the separate elements of any private-actor claim. He finds no disputed damages, independent crew tort, conclusive admissible-proof question or universal constitutional rule against essential police documentation.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit must apply personal immunity on the reviewed federal-official media-participation theory and conduct ordinary proceedings within the limited vacatur. The Court does not vacate the whole appellate judgment. Distinct properly preserved residential-entry or search grounds and unreviewed claims retain their own procedural positions; no particular new hearing, evidence, damages or publication relief is ordered.
