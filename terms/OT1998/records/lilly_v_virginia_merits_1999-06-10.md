## Law of the decision
**The asserted firmly rooted exception.** A State cannot establish a firmly rooted federal constitutional hearsay exception merely by placing an unavailable accomplice's custodial, self-exculpatory and blame-shifting accusations within its declaration-against-penal-interest exception. Genuinely disserving admissions and accusations of another cannot automatically be treated as one reliable narrative; genuinely self-inculpatory, equally inculpatory and noncustodial statements remain subject to their actual circumstances.

**Authority:** The lead opinion's exception ruling, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This is a direct seven-Justice proposition, not a synthesis of different rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** White preserves accepted, firmly rooted exceptions; it does not turn every police interview admitted under a state label into an established constitutional exception. Lee identifies the distinctive danger that an accomplice will shift or spread blame. Mark's admissions of misconduct do not automatically make his assignment of the killing to Benjamin reliable. Williamson requires context-sensitive attention to individual assertions under federal evidence law and illustrates why a general confession does not carry every accusation with it; it does not itself decide Virginia evidence law or the constitutional question. The narrow custodial, blame-shifting category presented here fails the firmly rooted route. That conclusion alone does not abolish the separate constitutional route of particularized trustworthiness or prohibit all accomplice declarations.

**Particularized guarantees and the challenged accusations.** Under the governing confrontation framework, the prosecution must establish particularized guarantees of trustworthiness from the circumstances surrounding the actual statement when no genuinely applicable firmly rooted exception supports admission. Independent corroborating trial evidence cannot substitute for those guarantees. Mark's challenged custodial accusations lack them, and their substantive admission without confrontation violated Benjamin's right.

**Authority:** The lead opinion's reliability holding and application, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five supply controlling reasoning at this scope. Scalia and Thomas independently agree that admission violated confrontation on different grounds; neither supplies a reliability-framework join.

**Controlling explanation:** Roberts and Wright distinguish constitutional guarantees arising from the statement's circumstances from evidence elsewhere at trial that might suggest the accusation is true. Mark minimized his violence and denied responsibility for the shooting while accusing Benjamin. Custodial questioning and pressure concerning grave charges created concrete incentives to curry favor and reduce personal exposure; his reported intoxication and inclusion of some self-inculpation do not establish that cross-examination would add little. No express leniency bargain need be found to recognize those incentives. Cross-examination could directly test the allocation of responsibility. Virginia's treatment of blame shifting as weight alone, and its resort to corroboration as a substitute for intrinsic guarantees, did not meet the constitutional requirement. The supported circumstances permit resolution of these challenged accusations without declaring every sentence of the interviews inadmissible under every possible theory. The Court adopts no categorical doctrine replacing the received framework for all testimonial statements, no universal atomized exclusion rule, and no new de novo standard of constitutional review. Genuinely self-inculpatory remarks severable from blame shifting, forfeiture by wrongdoing, dying declarations, and nonhearsay uses with effective limiting safeguards are not barred by this holding.

**Harmlessness and relief.** An established confrontation trial error permits the conviction and sentence to stand only if a properly available harmless-error inquiry establishes beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the verdict; the Commonwealth bears that burden. The present record supports no finding of harmlessness, automatic acquittal or unconditional retrial order.

**Authority:** All nine Justices accept this remedy rule for an established constitutional error. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer find the error now; O'Connor and Kennedy would first have the state court assess particularized trustworthiness under the corrected rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Chapman supplies the Commonwealth's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden; Gray keeps the admission error, preservation of a harmlessness contention and ultimate relief distinct. The trial-wide record and properly available arguments must govern that inquiry. The mandate creates no otherwise unavailable harmlessness argument. Because seven Justices find the challenged admission unconstitutional now, that issue is not left open on remand; only the bounded remedial inquiry remains. Unless Virginia satisfies that burden in a properly available inquiry, the conviction and sentence must be vacated. Virginia may retry without the challenged statement or after securing constitutionally sufficient confrontation. The Court makes no prediction about any retrial or the complete record's harmlessness result.

## Holdings
**The asserted firmly rooted exception.** A State cannot establish a firmly rooted federal constitutional hearsay exception merely by placing an unavailable accomplice's custodial, self-exculpatory and blame-shifting accusations within its declaration-against-penal-interest exception. Genuinely disserving admissions and accusations of another cannot automatically be treated as one reliable narrative; genuinely self-inculpatory, equally inculpatory and noncustodial statements remain subject to their actual circumstances.

**Authority:** The lead opinion's exception ruling, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This is a direct seven-Justice proposition, not a synthesis of different rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** White preserves accepted, firmly rooted exceptions; it does not turn every police interview admitted under a state label into an established constitutional exception. Lee identifies the distinctive danger that an accomplice will shift or spread blame. Mark's admissions of misconduct do not automatically make his assignment of the killing to Benjamin reliable. Williamson requires context-sensitive attention to individual assertions under federal evidence law and illustrates why a general confession does not carry every accusation with it; it does not itself decide Virginia evidence law or the constitutional question. The narrow custodial, blame-shifting category presented here fails the firmly rooted route. That conclusion alone does not abolish the separate constitutional route of particularized trustworthiness or prohibit all accomplice declarations.

**Particularized guarantees and the challenged accusations.** Under the governing confrontation framework, the prosecution must establish particularized guarantees of trustworthiness from the circumstances surrounding the actual statement when no genuinely applicable firmly rooted exception supports admission. Independent corroborating trial evidence cannot substitute for those guarantees. Mark's challenged custodial accusations lack them, and their substantive admission without confrontation violated Benjamin's right.

**Authority:** The lead opinion's reliability holding and application, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five supply controlling reasoning at this scope. Scalia and Thomas independently agree that admission violated confrontation on different grounds; neither supplies a reliability-framework join.

**Controlling explanation:** Roberts and Wright distinguish constitutional guarantees arising from the statement's circumstances from evidence elsewhere at trial that might suggest the accusation is true. Mark minimized his violence and denied responsibility for the shooting while accusing Benjamin. Custodial questioning and pressure concerning grave charges created concrete incentives to curry favor and reduce personal exposure; his reported intoxication and inclusion of some self-inculpation do not establish that cross-examination would add little. No express leniency bargain need be found to recognize those incentives. Cross-examination could directly test the allocation of responsibility. Virginia's treatment of blame shifting as weight alone, and its resort to corroboration as a substitute for intrinsic guarantees, did not meet the constitutional requirement. The supported circumstances permit resolution of these challenged accusations without declaring every sentence of the interviews inadmissible under every possible theory. The Court adopts no categorical doctrine replacing the received framework for all testimonial statements, no universal atomized exclusion rule, and no new de novo standard of constitutional review. Genuinely self-inculpatory remarks severable from blame shifting, forfeiture by wrongdoing, dying declarations, and nonhearsay uses with effective limiting safeguards are not barred by this holding.

**Harmlessness and relief.** An established confrontation trial error permits the conviction and sentence to stand only if a properly available harmless-error inquiry establishes beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the verdict; the Commonwealth bears that burden. The present record supports no finding of harmlessness, automatic acquittal or unconditional retrial order.

**Authority:** All nine Justices accept this remedy rule for an established constitutional error. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer find the error now; O'Connor and Kennedy would first have the state court assess particularized trustworthiness under the corrected rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Chapman supplies the Commonwealth's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden; Gray keeps the admission error, preservation of a harmlessness contention and ultimate relief distinct. The trial-wide record and properly available arguments must govern that inquiry. The mandate creates no otherwise unavailable harmlessness argument. Because seven Justices find the challenged admission unconstitutional now, that issue is not left open on remand; only the bounded remedial inquiry remains. Unless Virginia satisfies that burden in a properly available inquiry, the conviction and sentence must be vacated. Virginia may retry without the challenged statement or after securing constitutionally sufficient confrontation. The Court makes no prediction about any retrial or the complete record's harmlessness result.

## Precedent Treatment
- Roberts remains the received exception-sensitive framework; the Court applies it without replacing it for all hearsay.
- White preserves its accepted exception-specific holdings and does not validate this custodial blame-shifting accusation through a state label.
- Hale preserves White's received framework and its own exception-specific holding; it supplies no validation of this different custodial accusation.
- Lee supplies the special concern about accomplice blame shifting; it supports scrutiny of this accusatory material without banning every self-inculpatory remark.
- Wright supplies the intrinsic-guarantee requirement and bars independent trial corroboration as its substitute; it remains distinct from harmlessness.
- Williamson supplies context-sensitive separation of genuinely disserving assertions under federal Rule 804(b)(3); it does not directly rewrite Virginia evidence law or independently settle the confrontation question.
- Chapman supplies the Commonwealth's burden for constitutional trial error.
- Gray preserves only properly available harmlessness review and separates admission error from ultimate relief.

## Law After Decision
The narrow exception ruling has seven direct votes. The intrinsic-reliability rule and its application to these challenged accusations have five direct votes and control. Seven Justices find confrontation error, with Scalia and Thomas relying on distinct grounds. No broader categorical accomplice ban, categorical testimonial replacement doctrine, formal-accusation-only limit, or new de novo-review rule is adopted. The state court cannot reinstate admission through the rejected firmly rooted label or reconsider the established violation; the remaining inquiry concerns lawful remedial consequences.

## Separate Writings
O'Connor, joined by Kennedy, agrees that Virginia's penal-interest label cannot establish constitutional firm rootedness for these custodial blame-shifting accusations. She would permit the state court first to identify any genuinely self-inculpatory assertions that independently satisfy the correct trustworthiness standard. Williamson requires context, not wholesale acceptance or automatic exclusion of every collateral assertion. Governing Wright limits that inquiry despite Kennedy's different view of corroboration; a constitutional error established below would then receive only properly available Chapman review. They do not join the Court's present trustworthiness application or a new rule requiring independent appellate factfinding.

Scalia finds the uncross-examined police accusation at the core of the confrontation right. Formal police questioning generated an accusation offered against the accused, who could not test it. A judicial estimate of probable truth is not a complete substitute for that right. He accepts reversal and the constitutional-error remedy while declining to settle every hearsay category or comprehensively replace Roberts.

Thomas rests on the formalized character of the recorded custodial accusation, within the concern he preserved in White and Hale. He agrees that admission violated confrontation and that the case must return for the bounded remedy. He does not endorse the entire reliability analysis, a blanket prohibition on accomplice declarations, or a rule for all informal statements; he also preserves the objection that the lower court had not performed the second Roberts inquiry. His vote for violation supplies no additional vote for the Court's five-Justice reliability rationale.

## Procedure After Action
Virginia must apply the properly available Chapman inquiry to the complete record, with the Commonwealth bearing the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden. Unless that burden is satisfied through a legally available inquiry, the conviction and sentence must be vacated. The State may retry without the challenged accusation or after securing constitutionally sufficient confrontation. The Court orders neither acquittal nor unconditional retrial and makes no trial-wide harmlessness finding.
