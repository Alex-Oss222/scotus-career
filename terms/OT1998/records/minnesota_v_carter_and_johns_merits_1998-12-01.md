## Controlling holdings kernel

### I. Each respondent has a personal interest in the shared domestic privacy

**Operative rule:** Invited occupants who use a resident's deliberately screened home for a private activity possess their own Fourth Amendment interest against police acquisition of interior information; brief duration, commercial purpose and illegality, separately or together, do not automatically defeat that interest. Carter and Johns each have that interest on the actual host-authorized joint occupancy here; permission merely to enter, another person's rights or a desire to conceal crime alone does not establish it.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The proposition controls despite their disagreement over the ultimate disposition. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Rakas requires a person's own protected interest and rejects a claim derived solely from someone else's rights. Jones, as preserved by Rakas, shows that ownership is unnecessary. Olson recognizes privacy shared with a guest. Fields applies those principles both to independent control and, separately, to expressly permitted sharing of domestic privacy without an overnight arrangement. Its protection of Crawley's recurring closed-room use did not make recurrence indispensable to every shared-privacy claim.

The resident deliberately admitted Carter and Johns to the screened living interior, joined their private undertaking and allowed that use for approximately two and one-half hours. Their interest rests on that actual sharing, not her title alone. The State stresses the brief compensated transaction and absence of an established prior relationship or continuing control. Those facts bear on the relationship, but do not erase the personally shared private occupancy actually shown. Payment informs the arrangement without turning the domestic interior into a public business area. Criminal activity neither supplies the interest nor forfeits one otherwise established.

Neither respondent needs an accepted key finding for this application. Carter's disputed proffer is not adjudicated and is not attributed to Johns. The Court does not protect every casual visitor, unauthorized occupant, person permitted merely to enter, or activity plainly exposed to ordinary public observation. Recognition of personal privacy leaves the distinct question whether the officer's particular observation invaded it.

**Precedent treatment:** Rakas retains the personal-right requirement and exclusion of mere presence. Jones remains relevant to protected use without ownership. Olson's shared-home principle is applied beyond its overnight facts through Fields. Fields's relationship inquiry extends beyond Crawley's recurring use to the particular host-authorized sharing here; its independent-control branch and rejection of automatic protection for every visitor remain intact.

### II. Conditional suppression rules remain distinct from the observation question

**Operative rule:** If an antecedent intrusion is held unlawful in properly available further proceedings, a later warrant is an independent source only when both the decision to seek it and its issuance are genuinely independent of the illegality. A warrant's arrival does not retroactively authorize the earlier intrusion, and consent, attenuation, inevitable discovery and other asserted grounds require their own factual and procedural predicates; neither automatic exclusion nor automatic admission follows.

**Authority:** Part III of Breyer's opinion has all eight Associate Justices' direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional remedial rationale. These conditional rules govern the remand if their triggering conditions arise; they do not find that this observation was unlawful or that any particular later item must be excluded.

**Controlling explanation:** Fields and Murray require genuine independence of both the decision to seek a warrant and its issuance. Karo preserves a supported untainted-warrant ground; Pinson and Myers separate correction of an antecedent search premise from final exclusion and from revival of unavailable arguments. The trial memorandum here treated the observation as central to the stop and warrants. Its rejection of the particular Franks attack on descriptions of the investigation and surveillance does not establish an independent source.

The stop, door opening, handgun discovery, arrests, apartment and vehicle warrants, physical evidence and statements require their actual personal-right and causal inquiries. The prosecutor's concession that an invalid arrest would bring down statements and physical evidence retains that condition. It does not concede that every defect in the observation automatically excludes every later item for both respondents. The ninety-minute surveillance gap is not erased.

Consent requires actual authority, scope, voluntariness and any applicable taint analysis. Leon affords no protection for knowing or reckless deception, abandoned judicial neutrality, an affidavit wholly lacking reasonable probable-cause indicia or a facially deficient warrant; the conduct of obtaining and supplying officers and proper execution scope matter. Evans remains confined to qualifying court-clerical error with objectively reasonable reliance, not a general exception for deliberate police investigation. Attenuation and inevitable discovery need their actual predicates. Ordinary preservation rules remain operative, and no new statement, Miranda or Franks claim is created.

The Court orders neither an evidence-by-evidence exclusion nor unconditional admission or reinstatement. The remaining claims may be resolved only to the extent properly available under ordinary procedure.

**Precedent treatment:** Fields and Murray's genuine-independence rule is applied without expansion. Karo's supported untainted-warrant possibility remains available. Pinson and Myers retain the distinction between antecedent error and final suppression. Leon and Evans retain their stated institutional, factual and procedural limits; Franks's false-affidavit inquiry does not substitute for independent source.

## Holdings

### I. Each respondent has a personal interest in the shared domestic privacy

**Operative rule:** Invited occupants who use a resident's deliberately screened home for a private activity possess their own Fourth Amendment interest against police acquisition of interior information; brief duration, commercial purpose and illegality, separately or together, do not automatically defeat that interest. Carter and Johns each have that interest on the actual host-authorized joint occupancy here; permission merely to enter, another person's rights or a desire to conceal crime alone does not establish it.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The proposition controls despite their disagreement over the ultimate disposition. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Rakas requires a person's own protected interest and rejects a claim derived solely from someone else's rights. Jones, as preserved by Rakas, shows that ownership is unnecessary. Olson recognizes privacy shared with a guest. Fields applies those principles both to independent control and, separately, to expressly permitted sharing of domestic privacy without an overnight arrangement. Its protection of Crawley's recurring closed-room use did not make recurrence indispensable to every shared-privacy claim.

The resident deliberately admitted Carter and Johns to the screened living interior, joined their private undertaking and allowed that use for approximately two and one-half hours. Their interest rests on that actual sharing, not her title alone. The State stresses the brief compensated transaction and absence of an established prior relationship or continuing control. Those facts bear on the relationship, but do not erase the personally shared private occupancy actually shown. Payment informs the arrangement without turning the domestic interior into a public business area. Criminal activity neither supplies the interest nor forfeits one otherwise established.

Neither respondent needs an accepted key finding for this application. Carter's disputed proffer is not adjudicated and is not attributed to Johns.
