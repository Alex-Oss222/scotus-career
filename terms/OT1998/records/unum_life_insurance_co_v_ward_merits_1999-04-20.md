#### 2. UNUM Life Insurance Co. of America v. Ward — No. 97-1868 — April 20, 1999

##### Notice-prejudice component: common decisional ground

California's insurance-specific notice-prejudice rule falls within ERISA §514(b)(2)(A)'s saving clause as applied to this insured disability policy. It limits the insurer's contractual forfeiture defense: late notice does not relieve the insurer without proof of actual prejudice, and neither lateness itself nor possible prejudice suffices. It is part of the substantive insurer-insured bargain, not merely a state-created damages remedy for administering an ERISA claim. Its application in Ward's §502(a)(1)(B) suit leaves the federal enforcement channel intact. The self-funded-plan question and the deemer clause's distinct protection are not decided.

The common-sense insurance inquiry and the McCarran-Ferguson guideposts address actual operation. The rule specifically governs insurance, directly regulates the policy relationship and preserves the insurer's assumed coverage absent actual prejudice. The parties may dispute whether that last feature independently transfers or spreads risk in a technical sense; these guideposts do not require transforming every consideration into an indispensable conjunctive element. The decision need not invent a replacement saving-clause test or decide every timing rule.

The strongest opposing argument is that permitting state variation in claim timing defeats uniform administration and rewrites an express plan deadline. Metropolitan Life preserves substantive insurance terms despite their effect on insured plans; applying §502(a)(1)(B) to those terms does not create Pilot Life's competing remedy. Travelers and De Buono preserve uniform administration but do not repeal the insurance saving clause. John Hancock preserves both compatible insurance regulation and a concrete-conflict inquiry; no contradictory ERISA command requiring this absolute forfeiture has been identified.

Affirm reversal of the insurer's summary judgment on the notice-prejudice ground and remand for the actual-prejudice inquiry. No finding that prejudice is absent, no award of benefits, and no automatic finding of employer agency follows.

##### Employer-as-agent component: corrected legal premise and bounded remand

The primary contemporaneous United States merits filing confirms that the agency question was litigated as a second question; the short neutral grant statement was incomplete. Reach the question at the scope the actual dispute permits. A state rule assigning an employer the insurer's agency role because the employer performs specified benefit-plan administration has a connection with that administration under §514(a). It directly reallocates notice and claim-processing responsibility; it is not merely an external cost of buying insurance. The Ninth Circuit's unconditional no-relation rationale cannot support its contingent Elfstrom instruction on that understanding.

Ward disputes that characterization and describes ordinary agency principles applied to actual circumstances. That distinction matters. The Court cannot turn the disputed doctrine into a universal irrebuttable employer-agency rule, declare every ordinary agency principle preempted, or find a voluntary insurer designation absent or present. Ward did not press insurance saving for this agency theory; the notice-prejudice holding does not silently save it. The actual policy's written-authorization clause and the legal source of any asserted agency must receive their proper effect without treating the employer's administrative participation alone as conclusive.

Vacate the contingent agency direction insofar as it rests on the erroneous premise that the described mandatory allocation does not relate to a plan. If UNUM proves actual prejudice and the agency issue therefore remains material, the lower courts must identify and consider the actual rule invoked under the corrected relation standard, preserving any properly presented voluntary or fact-specific agency theory. This is not an automatic ruling that notice to MAC was ineffective, and no broad federal agency doctrine is created. The notice-prejudice remand remains independently necessary and is affirmed; the complete disposition is **affirm in part, vacate in part, and remand**.

## Holdings

### Notice-prejudice is saved insurance regulation

**Holding and operative rule:** California's insurance-specific rule prevents an insurer from forfeiting coverage for late notice unless it proves actual prejudice and is saved by ERISA §514(b)(2)(A) as applied to this insured policy. The rule informs Ward's federal benefits action under §502(a)(1)(B); it creates no independent state enforcement remedy and establishes neither absence of prejudice nor entitlement to benefits.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's unanimous Opinion of the Court controls this holding and the agency holding below, including their qualifications and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the insurance-saving clause to the rule's operation. Metropolitan Life preserves regulation of the insurer-insured bargain. Notice-prejudice restricts an insurer's forfeiture defense and thus directly governs that bargain. The common-sense insurance inquiry and the McCarran-Ferguson considerations support that conclusion: the rule is directed to insurance and integral to the policy relationship. Those considerations are guideposts, not three indispensable conjunctive elements; the Court need not decide whether this rule independently transfers or spreads risk. Pilot Life rejects a competing state enforcement remedy, whereas Ward invokes ERISA's own benefits action. The written deadline cannot nullify a substantive insurance rule that Congress saved. Travelers and De Buono preserve federal control of plan administration but do not erase that express reservation. John Hancock likewise requires respect for compatible insurance obligations while preserving genuinely conflicting federal commands; no command requiring absolute forfeiture is shown here. UNUM's uniformity argument therefore does not justify preemption of this policy term. The record still requires the actual-prejudice inquiry. Self-funded plans, the deemer clause's distinct protection and other timing rules are not decided.

### Mandatory administrative agency cannot rest on a no-relation premise

**Holding and operative rule:** A state rule assigning the employer an insurer-agency role because it performs benefit-plan administration directly connects with that administration under ERISA §514(a); the Ninth Circuit's contrary no-relation premise and contingent instruction cannot stand. The categorical administrative reassignment may not be used here, but the decision does not preempt every ordinary agency principle or resolve a properly presented voluntary or fact-specific agency undertaking.

**Controlling explanation:** Travelers and De Buono distinguish remote economic effects from direct commands to administration. A mandatory rule making an employer the insurer's agent for notice and claims processing reallocates administrative responsibility itself. That is materially different from the saved content of an insurance contract. Ward disputes the breadth of Elfstrom and invokes ordinary agency principles; the Court accordingly does not declare that every application of California agency law is an irrebuttable employer-agency rule. Nor does it find actual authorization, apparent authority or their absence. Ward did not press insurance saving for this agency theory, so the notice-prejudice holding cannot silently supply that distinct argument. Reversal corrects the legal premise, not the disputed agency facts. If actual prejudice makes agency material, the lower courts must assess any properly presented theory under the corrected rule, giving the policy's written-authorization provision and the legal source of the asserted agency their proper effect. No general federal agency code or automatic ineffectiveness of notice to the employer follows.
