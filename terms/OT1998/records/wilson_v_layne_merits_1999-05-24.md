## Holdings

### A home warrant does not authorize unrelated media visitors

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers violate residents' Fourth Amendment rights when, while executing a warrant inside the home, they introduce or permit invited media to enter for purposes unrelated to assisting lawful execution, without consent or another lawful basis for the added entry. Reasonable civilian assistance serving execution remains permissible on its actual basis; the warrant need not identify every genuine assistant.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices, controls the complete rule and this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Payton protects the home and confines arrest-warrant authority. Summers permits reasonable incidental measures serving execution, while Hicks and Garrison preserve the connection between lawful authority and the intrusion's extent. The officers' authority therefore did not make the home available to additional private observers. These reporters identified no suspect, preserved no evidence, protected no safety interest and performed no other actual execution function. General publicity, accurate reporting or public accountability does not supply the missing connection. Their observation added an intrusion even if they followed the officers' route; independent rummaging and eventual publication are not necessary elements. Nonpublication of the photographs does not erase the entry, and the Court need not classify the images as a separate seizure of property. Consent and independent legal authority remain distinct possibilities, neither established here. Interpreters, medical personnel, technical specialists, witnesses and other persons genuinely and reasonably assisting execution are not barred. The rule adds no obligation to admit observers and does not decide every official documentary practice, public-street observation, private-media liability or exclusionary consequence. Section 3105's search-warrant assistance language is separate statutory background, not an automatic constitutional damages rule governing this state arrest warrant. Lawful official presence is not retroactively invalidated by the additional visitors' wrong.

### The particular April 1992 application was not sufficiently clear for personal damages

**Holding and operative rule:** The respondent officers receive qualified immunity because, on the April 16, 1992 legal landscape, the particular restriction on accompanying, nonassisting media observers during otherwise authorized warrant execution was not sufficiently clear to impose personal damages liability. The absence of a factually identical case is neither required nor sufficient for immunity; the Court considers the particular application and all relevant conduct-date law while accepting the supported nonassistance facts.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven directly adopt the immunity conclusion and affirmance; Stone-Zsela and Stevens reject that application.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson preserve reasonable legal mistakes without making every constitutional violation immune. Hunter requires a judicial answer on the supported nonmovant record, not hypothetical assistance or selection of a more favorable factual version. Bills is substantial adverse authority predating this entry: it rejected police sponsorship of a private employee's unrelated inspection after the warrant's object was recovered and separately approved genuine identification help during a later warrant. Its later-officer immunity did not immunize every original invitation. The Court nevertheless finds its independent inspection materially different from observation accompanying ongoing authorized execution. Prahl supplies qualified contrary federal evidence: it rejected the claim that filming and broadcasting an otherwise reasonable search, without more, made it unreasonable, while separately preserving trespass recovery. That federal result is not a blanket license, and its tort ruling is not a constitutional holding. Together these actual authorities leave the particular observer application insufficiently settled despite the strong home-and-warrant principles supporting today's merits rule. A ride-along policy and supposed good motives supply no constitutional authority. Elder requires considering relevant outside-circuit law, so the Fourth Circuit's geographic exclusion is not approved. Crawford-El preserves the independent defense. Later execution and privacy decisions do not supply April 1992 notice, and disputed Moncrief and Higbee characterizations are unnecessary to the result. The Court chooses to decide the presented constitutional question here without imposing a mandatory merits-first sequence.

## Law After Decision

Officials may not bring nonassisting private media into a home merely under the authority of an execution warrant. Consent, separate lawful authority and genuine reasonable execution assistance remain distinct. These officers nevertheless receive personal immunity for this April 1992 application. The judgment creates no future media-access privilege and changes neither ordinary immunity procedure nor the requirement of individual responsibility and injury in a properly available damages claim.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** They join the complete constitutional rule but would deny immunity. The relevant specificity is the deliberate addition of unrelated private visitors to a home, not whether an earlier court named a newspaper ride-along. Payton's home protection and the purpose-bound warrant authority recognized in Summers already identify the limit; Bills confirms the same unauthorized private intrusion. Its separate-inspection setting does not make accompanying domestic spectators a reasonable legal mistake. Prahl's limited federal filming theory and separate trespass result do not authorize the compelled domestic exposure here. A publicity policy or unresolved description of another lower decision cannot enlarge a warrant.

This is an application of existing Harlow–Anderson fair-notice law, not a replacement of immunity with automatic liability from a merits violation. Genuine reasonable assistance, independent authority and ordinary reasonable mistakes about execution remain protected on their actual facts. Later Ramirez or a same-day media decision supplies no early notice. The dissent would reverse the immunity judgment and return the remaining personal participation, causation, injury, damages and preserved defenses for ordinary adjudication, without deciding every defendant's responsibility or guaranteeing a trial or award.

## Procedure After Action

The reviewed personal-immunity judgment stands. No damages or further adjudication of this federal media-intrusion damages theory against the respondents is directed. Unreviewed probable-cause and force rulings remain intact; private-media liability, independent claims and exclusionary consequences receive no new disposition.
