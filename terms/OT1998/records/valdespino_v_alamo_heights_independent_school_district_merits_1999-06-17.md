## Holdings

### The first condition includes a proved functional ability-to-elect route

For a claim by one protected group, the first Gingles condition may be met either through the retained district-majority route or through rigorous proof that a lawful, geographically compact proposed district would provide the cohesive group dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its candidates of choice. Speculation, an isolated favorable election, near-majority population arithmetic or mere influence does not suffice; the relevant majority-bloc showing, causal connection and independent totality remain necessary.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this express modification and its limits.

The first condition links the challenged electoral arrangement to an achievable opportunity to elect. Numerical majority provides a predictable demonstration of that capacity, but an exclusive number can reject the same actual opportunity when dependable voting evidence proves it. Section 2 protects equal participation and opportunity, without guaranteeing proportional outcomes. The Court therefore expressly modifies the numerical exclusivity reflected in Gingles and Growe; it does not declare that earlier reservations already adopted this alternative.

De Grandy rejects numerical maximization and conclusive safe harbors while preserving actual local opportunity. Brooks fits proof to a different nomination barrier. Those decisions support the inquiry's purpose but do not themselves decide this new district-capacity rule. Dependability matters because a contingent alliance or favorable candidate alone cannot establish lost opportunity attributable to the system. Plaintiffs must prove cohesion and actual voting behavior within the proposed district and the relevant majority-bloc defeat in the challenged arrangement; successful crossover in one setting does not silently negate or establish bloc defeat in another. The ordinary special-circumstances qualification, including unopposed candidacy, remains.

The majority route survives. So do lawful district population and geography, constitutional limits and the separate totality inquiry. The Court prescribes no mathematical crossover formula, racial floor, proportional-election right or preferred political tradeoff, and decides no combination of distinct minority groups or influence-only entitlement. States retain lawful choice among effective arrangements; a court-compelled remedy still requires an established violation.

### The population figures do not resolve functional opportunity

The accepted 47.9% and 48.3% Hispanic citizen voting-age shares neither prove nor foreclose dependable ability to elect. The judgment resting exclusively on their being below fifty percent must be vacated for application of the changed rule, with all remaining statutory conditions and totality to be established before relief.

**Authority:** The same five Justices join Stone-Zsela's Part I and this application and remedy.

Population is not evidence by itself of the electorate's actual crossover behavior. The lower courts did not make the findings necessary under the functional route, and this Court cannot supply them. The proposed district must be lawful in population and geography; an 8.1% underpopulation is not approved merely because it appears in the calculation. If admissible proof does not establish dependable opportunity, the claim fails. If capacity is established, the remaining conditions and unequal opportunity under totality still require determination. Voinovich preserves the difference between state choice and judicially compelled remedy; De Grandy permits neither automatic liability nor a numerical safe harbor. The remand commands no map or voting system and guarantees no favorable result.

### The independent demographic and disclosure rulings stand

The credited proof of concrete post-census housing, occupancy and growth changes supports the present demographic findings under clear-error review. Excluding the survey disclosed on the eve of trial for surprise under the applicable disclosure rule was within the trial court's discretion; changing the capacity rule does not itself make either independent ruling erroneous.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia and Thomas do not join it unconditionally.

The 1990 census's presumptive accuracy does not require treating later population as unchanged. The April 1997 report supplied documented local changes under the burden the lower court applied; the excluded August report does not become the basis for affirmance. The Court establishes neither a universal heightened demographic burden nor automatic approval of local estimates. Commerce's national-enumeration holding supplies no contrary rule about these later local facts.

The plaintiffs disclosed the survey's existence late Friday and its data late Saturday before Monday trial. The earlier deposition reference did not disclose its basis. Local Rule CV-16(e) permitted good-cause supplementation, but the trial court could enforce disclosure and avoid surprise, particularly after excluding the defense update said to occasion the survey. The Court does not decide the survey's accuracy, whether it was proper rebuttal or unestablished factors in a separate exclusion framework. Preservation of the exclusion is not a substantive rejection of the survey's results.

Evidence previously immaterial under an exclusive numerical threshold may bear on the changed inquiry. The trial court may determine whether fair supplementation is needed under ordinary rules, with adequate notice and response; reopening is neither automatic nor forbidden. No new trial, mandatory study or automatic admission of the excluded survey is ordered.

## Precedent Treatment

- Thornburg v. Gingles: expressly modifies numerical exclusivity in the first condition for the defined single-group functional route; preserves compactness, cohesion, relevant majority-bloc proof, causation and independent totality.
- Growe v. Emison: modifies the same exclusivity while preserving its causal framework and the distinct-group reservation.
- Voinovich v. Quilter: preserves the claimant's burden, relevant bloc-voting requirement and distinction between lawful state districting choice and a court-compelled remedy; the present rule resolves only the defined functional-capacity question.
- Johnson v. De Grandy: applies the rejection of maximization and conclusive ratios; preserves independent totality and actual opportunity without attributing today's change to that earlier decision.

- Department of Commerce v. United States House of Representatives / Clinton v. Glavin: preserves the national enumeration ruling; it neither freezes local demographics at the census date nor validates every projection.

## Law After Decision

A single protected group below a numerical majority may prove the first condition through dependable actual ability to elect in a lawful compact district. A majority-population route remains available, but neither route establishes every other condition or liability. Cohesion, relevant bloc-voting causation with applicable special circumstances, totality, independent constitutional limits and ordinary evidence procedures retain their force. Mere influence and speculative near-majority calculations do not create a right to districting. The present record establishes no functional success or completed violation.

## Separate Writings
