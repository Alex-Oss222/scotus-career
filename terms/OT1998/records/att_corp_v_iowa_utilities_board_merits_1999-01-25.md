## Controlling holdings

### I. National implementing rules coexist with state determinations

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 201(b) authorizes FCC rules implementing the local-competition obligations added to the Communications Act by §§251–252. That authority includes national standards governing the statutory tasks while state commissions retain the negotiations, arbitrations, individual rates and other determinations Congress assigned to them.

**Authority:** Scalia's Parts I–II are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices control the general-authority and associated remand propositions; O'Connor, Thomas and Breyer dissent on their distinct allocation grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's insertion of the local-competition provisions into the Communications Act brings those provisions within its implementing grant. Section 251(d)(1) directs implementation; §252(c) requires state commissions to apply §251 and the Commission's rules. National methodology and state determination of particular rates can coexist. Section 152(b) does not remove the new federal duties from the regulatory authority the amendments create. Louisiana Public Service Commission prevents a general grant from regulating matters Congress left wholly intrastate; it does not disable implementation of newly enacted federal obligations. MCI requires an actual delegation and preservation of operative limits, not a rule that every delegation permits only minor action. The contrary reading gives proper attention to the state role but treats methodology and particular determinations as mutually exclusive when the statute does not. The holding decides authority, not the substantive lawfulness of TELRIC, a confiscatory rate or every instruction the Commission might issue.

### II. The rural-exemption and agreement rules require review within the correct allocation

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical absence-of-federal-authority grounds for vacating Rules 51.405 and 51.303 and displacing otherwise lawful federal rules from state implementation cannot stand. Unresolved substantive objections return for review with the actual statutory rural-exemption, agreement-approval and state-law preservation limits intact.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice coalition controls Part II. The remand does not adopt a new rule resolving the returned substantive objections.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the general implementation rule does not adjudicate every asserted federal instruction. Section 251(f)'s exemption predicates remain for the actual assigned determinations; §§251(d)(3) and 252 retain their preservation and approval limits. MCI requires those specific statutory conditions to retain their force even when a general delegation exists. The lower court therefore must examine any unresolved objection to the rural burden-of-proof rule or preexisting-agreement requirements on its own substance. Rejection of exclusive state regulatory authority supplies no blanket validation of either rule and no permission for state commissions to disregard otherwise lawful federal requirements. The remedial disposition removes the categorical jurisdictional ground and returns the residual questions without selecting a new proof burden or deciding a particular agreement.

### III. A network element need not be a detachable physical object

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 153(29)'s network-element definition includes features, functions and capabilities supplied through facilities or equipment, as well as the physical facilities or equipment themselves. Eligibility under that definition does not establish that §251(d)(2) requires unbundled access to every eligible function.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part III is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Those eight supply a controlling majority; Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on the independent definition component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that operational-support systems, operator and directory services, and vertical-switching functions are not excluded merely because they are not detachable physical components or can also be described as services. The statutory definition itself supplies the answer. The physical-component-only construction would omit its express functional language. But definition and compulsory access are separate inquiries: the Commission must still apply the proprietary-necessity and general-impairment criteria to identify required elements. No general requirement to unbundle every technically available service follows.

### IV. Necessity and impairment must perform independent limiting work

**Holding and operative rule:** In selecting required network elements under §251(d)(2), the FCC must give independent limiting effect to necessity for proprietary elements and impairment for network elements generally, considering realistically available alternatives outside as well as inside the incumbent's network. Rule 51.319 is set aside because the adopted comparison and impairment standard did not perform that task.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, giving the complete rule seven votes. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the invalidity holding that the rule must make element-specific judgments giving necessity and impairment independent constraining force, and joins vacatur and agency remand; he does not supply a separate join in the more specific outside-alternatives requirement. Souter dissents from invalidation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that any increase in cost or decline in quality cannot by itself establish the required impairment while external alternatives are categorically ignored. MCI preserves operative statutory predicates before agency deference. The agency's competitive-entry expertise remains substantial, but it cannot make every purchasing disadvantage a sufficient statutory obstacle. The proprietary standard of significant impairment or thwarting is not invalid simply because it is less than absolute indispensability; its defect includes the cramped alternative comparison limited to the incumbent's nonproprietary elements. The Court does not require access only to an irreplaceable natural monopoly, invent a numerical threshold or choose a replacement list. On remand the agency may explain real entry impairment, actual availability, costs, quality, service requirements and workable selection criteria under the enacted standard. The strongest contrary argument invokes the competitive-entry purpose and danger that theoretical alternatives will require disabling duplication. The Court accepts that real commercial availability matters; that is why an actual inquiry is required rather than either automatic access or theoretical replication.

### V. The Act imposes no freestanding own-facilities prerequisite

**Holding and operative rule:** A requesting telecommunications carrier need not own some local facilities merely to qualify to seek unbundled access under §251(c)(3). Its entitlement to particular elements remains governed by §251(d)(2) and the lawful conditions of access and combination.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part V is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejection of the independent own-facilities prerequisite, without a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(3) reaches any requesting telecommunications carrier and contemplates combining elements into a service. The separate resale route in §251(c)(4) does not insert a minimum-property requirement into the unbundling route. Combining or buying elements entails obligations and commercial consequences distinct from buying a finished retail service for resale; the existence of both routes does not make one redundant. This holding promises no entrant the incumbent's whole network regardless of necessity and impairment and decides no assumed difference in particular rates.

### VI. Existing combinations need not be dismantled

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 51.315(b) lawfully prohibits an incumbent from separating requested network elements already combined in its network unless the requesting carrier asks for separation. Unbundled availability and valuation do not require physical destruction of an existing combination.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Scalia's Parts VI–VII; those parts control the existing-combination and individual-arrangement rules with their stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(3)'s direction to make elements available in a manner permitting the requester to combine them does not require the incumbent first to dismantle a configuration already joined. The Commission reasonably implements nondiscriminatory access by preserving that configuration subject to the requester's option to separate. The contrary reading confuses separately available elements with mandatory physical separation. This holding restores paragraph (b), not the distinct affirmative new-combination duties in paragraphs (c)–(f), and does not require an unestablished configuration to be built. Those duties receive their own disposition. Particular requests remain subject to the Act and valid technical and operational requirements.

### VII. An entrant may select an individual agreement arrangement with its pertinent conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 252(i) permits Rule 51.809's access to an individual interconnection, service or network-element arrangement in an approved agreement on the same pertinent rates, terms and conditions. The rule preserves proof of greater provision costs or technical infeasibility and limits availability to a reasonable period after public filing.

**Authority:** The same nine-Justice coalition joins Part VII and controls this rule and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory reference to any interconnection, service or network element supports selection of an individual arrangement, not a requirement to accept the entire agreement. The entrant may not take an isolated favorable price while discarding its relevant obligations. The incumbent may establish that provision to this requester costs more or is technically infeasible, and the reasonable availability period protects against indefinite use of stale arrangements. The challengers argue that selective access undermines negotiated tradeoffs; the agency reached a contrary practical judgment with nondiscrimination and these safeguards in view. That disagreement does not establish that Congress commanded whole-agreement adoption or that the agency's incentive assessment is irrational. No unrestricted cherry-picking right results.

### VIII. Dialing-parity authority and numbering-cost ripeness remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical absence-of-FCC-authority ground for vacating intraLATA toll-dialing-parity rules is rejected, with remaining objections returned for review. The separate Rule 52.17 numbering-cost challenge remains unripe because its asserted disadvantage depends on particular cost-recovery rate decisions not before the Court.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part VIII parity-authority ground is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. O'Connor agrees on the narrower §251(b)(3)/§251(d)(1) implementation ground stated in her separate opinion. Stone-Zsela agrees in the parity disposition without an additional ground; the five-Justice lead rationale independently controls. All eight Associates join Part VIII's separate numbering-cost ripeness holding; Stone-Zsela agrees only in that disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that federal implementation of §251(b)(3)'s substantive parity duty is authorized. The general implementing grant supports that conclusion, and the narrower substantive-duty ground also supports the parity disposition without resolving the separate state-pricing dispute. Section 271 and other actual statutory limits remain operative. The separate numbering issue concerns who will bear costs under future particular rate decisions. Reversal of the authority ground neither resolves those rates nor creates an accrued numbering injury. The California judgment therefore receives distinct dispositions rather than complete reversal by inference from the general-authority holding. No particular carrier's liability or cost share is fixed.

### IX. Technical feasibility cannot replace the required access criteria

**Holding and operative rule:** The partial vacatur of Rule 51.317's technical-feasibility presumption and Order paragraphs 278 and 281 is affirmed because the presumption and its actual exceptions fail to preserve the required necessity and impairment limits. The separately sustained definition of technical feasibility and other unaffected portions of the rule remain outside that invalidation.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IX is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven control the substantive presumption holding. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the partial invalidation; Souter would remand this component without a final validity ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that technical feasibility may inform element selection: §251(d)(2)'s minimum criteria do not prohibit consideration of additional relevant matters. For elements beyond Rule 51.319's list, once technical feasibility is found, Rule 51.317 permits the state commission to decline unbundling only if either (1) the element is proprietary or would reveal proprietary information, and the requester could offer the same proposed service through other nonproprietary unbundled elements within the incumbent's network; or (2) denial would neither reduce service quality nor increase its financial or administrative cost compared with providing that service through other unbundled elements in that network. Those restrictions reproduce the limiting-standard defect in the required-element list. They do not allow realistic outside alternatives to perform their proper role. The result rests on this independent substantive flaw, not on the mistaken premise that feasibility can never matter. A corrected-delegation remand cannot preserve the existing defective criteria without agency reconsideration. The Court does not forbid every lawful presumption or invalidate all of Rule 51.317.

### X. Superior-quality duties return for review under the correct framework

**Holding and operative rule:** The invalidation of Rules 51.305(a)(4) and 51.311(c) is vacated and remanded for reconsideration of the actual conditions and agency justification under the correct implementing authority. The Court does not decide their ultimate validity or order unconditional reinstatement.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join Part X's corrected-standard remand and its confined review directions. Thomas and Breyer do so under the Court's governing authority framework while retaining their dissent from Part I. Stone-Zsela agrees in the remand without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(2)'s requirement of at-least-equal quality establishes a floor; the absence of an express superior-quality command does not alone show that an implementing rule can never require more. The lower court's existing-network and substantive-conflict objections nevertheless remain for review. Rule 51.305(a)(4) concerns requested, technically feasible interconnection superior to that supplied to the incumbent, affiliates or other interconnection recipients. Rule 51.311(c) separately concerns the element and access superior to the incumbent's own, upon request and to the extent feasible, placing technical-infeasibility proof before the state commission on the incumbent. Lesser quality at the requesting carrier's sole request remains permitted. On remand the actual improvement, statutory relationship and agency explanation must be examined. Compensation alone supplies no missing authority, and nondiscrimination alone does not command every upgrade. The lower court's allowance for modifications necessary to accommodate lawful access is preserved; neither an unconditional superior-facilities right nor a categorical ban on necessary modifications is adopted.

### XI. Affirmative new-combination duties require separate reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule:** The invalidation of Rule 51.315(c)–(f) is vacated and remanded for review under the proper implementing-power standard. Section 251(c)(3)'s direction to enable requesting carriers to combine elements does not unambiguously assign every physical combination task exclusively to the requester; the particular affirmative duties and burdens remain undecided.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part XI is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Breyer's agreement is a remand under the governing authority framework, not a Part I join. Seven Justices control this remand; Stone-Zsela agrees only in its disposition, and Thomas dissents on his independent task-allocation ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the lower construction treated enabling access as an exclusive assignment of all combining labor without adequately addressing the actual delegation, network control, conditions and agency explanation. That differs from preserving existing combinations, which the Court separately upholds. Subsection (c) addresses requested configurations the incumbent does not ordinarily make, only when technically feasible and not impairing other carriers' access or interconnection. Subsection (d) addresses combination with the requester's own elements in a technically feasible manner. The other-carrier condition expressly belongs to (c), not automatically to (d). Subsections (e) and (f) place the relevant infeasibility or other-carrier-impairment showing before the state commission on the incumbent. MCI preserves specific statutory allocations, but the contested sentence does not alone establish the exclusive task allocation the lower court found. The remand neither approves every proof burden nor compels a particular configuration. Voluntary combination and necessary accommodation remain distinct from a mandated new combination.

### XII. The general section 208 announcement is not ripe for review

**Holding and operative rule:** A definite agency announcement of a possible enforcement forum is not ripe merely because the position is complete and the dispute predominantly legal. Because this announcement directs no present change in primary conduct, determines no carrier-specific liability and permits meaningful review of a concrete application, the general challenge must be dismissed without prejudice, without deciding enforcement authority.

**Authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Scalia's Part XII, supplying a controlling majority. Stone-Zsela agrees only in vacatur of the premature enforcement-merits adjudication and dismissal without prejudice as unripe; he adds no enforcement or ripeness ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that finality, standing, fitness and hardship are distinct; it decides ripeness, not independent standing or final-order questions. Ohio Forestry and Toilet Goods distinguish settled policy from presently consequential application. Paragraphs 126–128 definitively assert complaint authority even where a carrier complies with a state-approved agreement, and separately contemplate enforcing agreement terms; a later expedited-procedure rulemaking does not make that assertion tentative. Yet the announcement changes no substantive carrier duty, identifies no operative agreement conflict, revises no state determination and orders no act or liability. A concrete complaint would distinguish statutory enforcement, agreement enforcement and a carrier proceeding that effectively revisits state action.

Footnote 236 says the Commission might decline financial penalties in some state-authorized circumstances; it establishes neither immunity nor a required penalty. The statement that judicially decided issues are unlikely to be revisited likewise creates no absolute preclusion rule. The absence of an absolute safe harbor is not itself withdrawal of an established protection or present liability. Bennett's operative legal-protection change and Franklin's completed determination are therefore different.

Section 208(a) supplies complaint, notice, opportunity to satisfy or answer and investigation under its conditions; §208(b)(3) makes an order concluding a covered investigation appealable under §402(a). Concrete application permits the authority objection in its actual setting. The prospect of defending a future complaint and uncertainty about forum allocation do not alone establish present primary-conduct hardship. Lee preserves review of presently burdensome civil duties, and Pic-A-State concerns an existing business actually constrained by a law; neither condition is established by this instrument. NAACP and Paradise require examination of the actual claim, not an automatic further inquiry into hypothetical applications. The Court creates no universal exhaustion rule, promises no future filing's standing or timeliness, and does not defer every challenge until penalties are collected. A presently binding or burdensome statement remains a different case. The Court decides neither carrier statutory-violation authority, agreement enforcement, review of state determinations under §252(e)(6), nor state-failure authority under §252(e)(5); no carrier complaint, agreement, state order or penalty is adjudicated.

**A1 — General implementation power.** Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg would reverse the categorical lack-of-authority premise. Congress added §§251–252 to the Communications Act; §201(b)'s power to make implementing rules applies to those federal obligations. Section 251(d)(1) expressly directs implementation of the requirements, and §252(c)'s duties to apply §251 and the Commission's rules confirm that national standards coexist with state resolution of individual negotiations, arbitrations and rates. Section 152(b) does not remove the local competition provisions from the regulatory authority the amendments themselves create. **A4 — Necessary, impair and Rule 51.319.** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would set aside the challenged required-element list and return it for application of the enacted access criteria. **Souter's A4 contrary commitment:** Uphold the agency's construction and resulting listed access requirements against this statutory attack. The Act makes unbundled access a means of competitive entry, not a last resort after replication. Cost and quality affect the ability to provide the selected service; the separate higher proprietary threshold and nonproprietary alternative give the clauses distinct operation. An outside-source requirement can let incumbents demand duplication of facilities that is theoretically possible but commercially disables entry. Chevron and his MCI dissent support the actual competition rationale within an express delegation. He does not favor absolute mandatory access to every technically available element or endorse the separately invalidated technical-feasibility presumption. His sufficient ground concerns the reasonableness of this interpretation, not independent judicial findings about every administrative submission.

The strongest challenge to the seven-Associate A4 position is that it risks substituting judicial economic judgments for the agency's authorized competitive-entry judgment. The seven accept the agency's policy domain but require the words Congress selected to perform the limiting task; expertise cannot turn every disadvantage of purchasing elsewhere into statutory impairment. The strongest challenge to Souter's position is that marginal cost or quality differences are virtually ubiquitous. He finds the statutory context and retained proprietary and access restrictions sufficient, and does not claim that breadth alone exempts the rule from review. The technical-feasibility presumption receives its own disposition in A9, not an automatic consequence of either A4 vote.

**Stevens's reconciled A4 ground.** His MCI dissent and Allentown Mack join preserve reasoned agency choice within actual statutory limits; neither makes every competition-promoting rule reasonable. The current rule excludes external alternatives altogether and supplies no genuine selection standard when any cost or quality advantage suffices. The unchanged specific §251(d)(2) predicates supply the limiting distinction missing from the provisional analogy to MCI. He joins the stated invalidation and remand without an essential-facilities-only rule, numerical threshold or direction selecting replacement elements.

**A5 — No freestanding own-facilities prerequisite.** All eight reject a categorical requirement that a requesting carrier own some local facilities before seeking unbundled access. Section 251(c)(3) reaches any requesting telecommunications carrier and contemplates combination into a service. The separate resale route does not insert a minimum-property requirement absent from the unbundling route. A4 still determines which elements must be supplied, and combining or purchasing elements retains distinct obligations and commercial consequences from buying a finished retail service for resale. No holding promises the entrant the incumbent's entire network regardless of the statutory access criteria or decides rate differences assumed in the parties' policy arguments.

**A6 — Existing combinations, Rule 51.315(b).** All eight would reverse its invalidation. Unbundled access requires separate availability and valuation, not destruction of a configuration already joined in the incumbent's network. Paragraph (b) permits separation when the requesting carrier asks; otherwise a nondiscriminatory duty to leave requested existing combinations intact reasonably implements §251(c)(3). The sentence allowing the requester to combine does not command the incumbent first to dismantle. This holding does not restore paragraphs (c)–(f)'s affirmative new-combination duties, require construction of an unestablished configuration or decide their validity; their separate requested reconsideration is addressed in A11. The technical and operational content of a future particular request remains governed by the Act and valid rules.

**A7 — Section 252(i), Rule 51.809.** All eight would reverse its invalidation. The statutory access to any interconnection, service or network element under an approved agreement permits selection of an individual arrangement on the same pertinent rates, terms and conditions. It is not selection of an isolated price with its relevant obligations discarded. The rule preserves the incumbent's proof that providing the particular arrangement to this requester costs more or is technically infeasible, and limits availability to a reasonable period after public filing. Those safeguards, nondiscrimination and the agency's assessment of negotiation incentives support the construction. The challengers' strongest tradeoff objection does not establish that Congress required every entrant to take an entire agreement or that the agency's contrary practical assessment is irrational. No limitless cherry-picking right follows.

**A8 — California.** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg would reverse the intraLATA dialing-parity vacatur based on absent federal authority and remand any actual remaining issues. The five adopting A1 apply its general implementing grant. O'Connor independently accepts implementation of §251(b)(3)'s express national substantive dialing-parity duty under §251(d)(1); her specific state-pricing objection does not extend to this duty. Thomas and Breyer would affirm the intraLATA authority vacatur: their A1-P construction does not treat the implementation deadline as a general jurisdictional grant, and the dialing-parity provision does not itself specifically assign this regulatory task to the FCC. This is a jurisdictional-authority position, not a conclusion that Congress left substantive parity optional.

All eight leave the separate Rule 52.17 numbering-cost challenge unripe on this record: its disadvantage depends on particular rate decisions permitting recovery, none of which is reviewed. Reversing or sustaining the parity authority ground does not settle those rates or manufacture an accrued numbering injury. Section 271 and other actual statutory limits remain; no complete second-judgment reversal follows merely from A1.

**A9 — Rule 51.317's technical-feasibility presumption, separate from its definition and Rule 51.319.** The opening brief expressly seeks corrected-standard reconsideration; this is a supported remedial request, not proof that the lower court decided nothing but jurisdiction. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the partial vacatur of the presumption and corresponding Order paragraphs 278 and 281 on the following independently sufficient ground. Feasibility can be relevant to access, and the words at a minimum allow the Commission to consider additional matters. But this rule presumptively requires additional elements on a feasibility finding and permits refusal only through the actual incumbent-network-only proprietary alternative or the rule's no-quality-loss/no-cost-increase condition. Those restrictions reproduce the failure to give the required necessity and impairment criteria independent limiting force identified in A4. The result does not invalidate every portion of Rule 51.317 or the separately sustained technical-feasibility definition, and does not hold that the Commission may never use a lawful presumption. A corrected-standard remand cannot cure this already resolved substantive defect without agency reconsideration of the defective criteria.

Souter would vacate the lower partial invalidation and remand this presumption for review under the proper delegated-authority standard, without unconditional reinstatement or a final validity ruling. The lower premise that technical feasibility is unmentioned among the minimum criteria is insufficient; the rule has actual exceptions, not feasibility alone. His A4 position removes the seven Associates' independently sufficient objection, but does not decide whether the actual presumption and burdens are reasonably explained or valid in every application. The strongest challenge is that its burden can effectively replace a statutory determination; his remand requires that objection to be considered rather than assuming technical feasibility can never inform selection.

**A10 — Superior quality, Rules 51.305(a)(4) and 51.311(c).** All eight would vacate the reviewed invalidation and remand for reconsideration under the corrected authority and substantive-standard distinction, without deciding final validity or directing immediate unconditional reinstatement. Section 251(c)(2)'s at-least-equal language states a mandatory floor; absence of an express command to provide superior quality does not itself establish that a reasonable implementing rule may never require it. The lower court's existing-network and substantive-conflict grounds remain real objections, not erased by the advocate's claim that nobody challenged statutory consistency. On return, the court must consider the actual requested and technically feasible improvement, the relationship to the statutory duty and the agency's own justification. Compensation alone supplies no authority, and the statutory nondiscrimination floor does not itself require every requested upgrade.

Thomas and Breyer accept this corrective remand if the Court adopts A1, without abandoning their contrary general-authority position. They have not supplied an independently sufficient substantive defense of these invalidations comparable to Thomas's A11 ground. This is agreement in a remand under the Court's governing framework, not a complete join in A1 or unconditional merits reinstatement.

The two rules remain distinct: Rule 51.305(a)(4) concerns interconnection superior to that provided to the incumbent, subsidiary, affiliate or another interconnection recipient; Rule 51.311(c) concerns the element and access superior to the incumbent's own, upon request and to the extent feasible, with the incumbent's technical-infeasibility proof before the state commission. The rule preserves lesser quality at the requester's sole request. The lower necessary-modification exception remains relevant: modifications necessary to accommodate lawful interconnection or access were not forbidden by its ruling. Neither an unconditional right to superior facilities nor a categorical ban on necessary modifications is adopted.

**A11 — Affirmative new combinations, Rule 51.315(c)–(f).** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the invalidation and remand for review under the correct implementing-power standard. The sentence requiring provision in a manner allowing requesting carriers to combine elements describes the access capability; it does not unambiguously assign every physical combining task exclusively to the requester. The lower court's substantive reading must therefore be assessed with the available delegation, the incumbent's control, the actual conditions and the agency's explanation. Unlike A6, this does not decide these affirmative duties valid. Breyer accepts the remand under the Court's A1 framework without joining the general-authority rationale he rejects; this independently presented remedial request has no published historical merits vote to import. Subsection (c) reaches requested combinations not ordinarily made only if technically feasible and not impairing other carriers' access or interconnection. Subsection (d) concerns combination with elements possessed by the requester in any technically feasible manner. Subsections (e) and (f) assign the incumbent the relevant infeasibility or other-carrier-impairment showing before the state commission. The (c) other-carrier condition must not be rewritten as an express condition in (d). The remand does not approve each burden or compel a particular configuration.

Thomas would affirm the invalidation of (c)–(f) on a substantive ground independent of the pricing dispute. He reads Congress's direction to make elements available so that the requesting carrier can combine them, together with the distinct resale route, as assigning the new-combination undertaking to the requester; a general implementation grant cannot shift that affirmative task to the incumbent on demand. Necessary accommodation remains distinct from requiring the incumbent to create combinations it does not use. His strongest counterargument is that the statute speaks in terms of enabling, not exclusive labor assignment. His MCI majority and additional-condition joins give decisive weight to the specific allocation; compensation and the rule's feasibility safeguards do not supply the obligation he finds absent. This position permits preservation of an existing combination under A6, which demands no new combining work, and does not forbid voluntary new-combination agreements.

The request to reconsider these three groups is expressly presented in the sanitized opening brief. No certified grant limitation excludes it in the approved neutral packet. The above dispositions neither accept that brief's characterization of the lower reasoning as fact nor resolve every residual regulatory merit simply because general authority has been corrected.

#### Final A2-208 rule, application and limits

**Operative rule.** A settled agency position asserting a possible enforcement forum is not ripe for judicial review merely because the interpretation is complete and the disputed allocation of authority is predominantly legal. Where the challenged announcement itself directs no present change in primary conduct, makes no carrier-specific liability determination and leaves its disputed authority for concrete application and meaningful judicial review, the general challenge must be dismissed without prejudice rather than decided on the enforcement merits.

This rule does not make commentary categorically unreviewable. An agency statement that actually changes legal protection, presently directs compliance, or imposes another supported immediate legal constraint presents a different question. Neither the absence of a criminal penalty nor the possibility of later review categorically defeats a challenge to a presently burdensome civil duty. Final agency action, a lawful statutory review channel, Article III standing, and fitness and hardship remain distinct. This disposition rests on ripeness and decides neither the independent final-order question nor standing.

**The actual announcement.** Paragraphs 126–127 assert that the Commission already possesses complaint authority; the planned later proceeding concerns expedited procedures and does not make that legal assertion tentative. Paragraph 127 contemplates a complaint alleging statutory or implementing-rule violations even when a carrier complies with a state-approved agreement, and separately contemplates enforcement of agreement terms. Paragraph 128 describes a carrier-focused inquiry while presenting it as an alternative for someone aggrieved by a state determination. These are substantial claims about authority. Their definiteness does not itself make the general challenge ripe.

The announced position does not amend a carrier's substantive section 251 or 252 obligation, identify a present conflict in an operative agreement, revise a state commission's determination, order a carrier or commission to act, or adjudicate a violation. It announces how the agency understands a possible complaint route. A carrier-specific application would identify the challenged acts or omissions, the alleged statutory or contractual violation and the relief claimed. Those particulars can determine whether the Commission is enforcing a carrier duty, enforcing an agreement or using a nominal carrier proceeding to revisit a state determination. Deciding the entire allocation now would answer those materially different settings in the abstract.

**Penalties and state approval.** Footnote 236 says that the Commission might decline financial penalties in some instances of conduct pursuant to state requirements or authorization. It supplies neither immunity nor a command to penalize. The Court therefore does not rest on a promise that state-approved compliance protects a carrier from a complaint or sanction. Nor does the Court treat the announced lack of an absolute safe harbor as a present adjudication of liability or as withdrawal of an established protection comparable to Bennett's operative incidental-take conditions. The statement preserves possible future exercise of asserted authority; it does not itself resolve whether any carrier violated a federal duty or owes a penalty. The qualified statement that the Commission is unlikely to revisit judicially decided issues likewise is not an absolute preclusion rule.

**Later review and hardship.** Section 208(a) supplies a complaint, notice, opportunity to satisfy or answer, and investigation under its enacted conditions. Section 208(b)(3) makes an order concluding an investigation covered by paragraphs (1) or (2) final and appealable under section 402(a). A concrete application therefore permits the asserted enforcement authority to be challenged in its actual setting through the applicable review route. Nothing in this announcement requires a presently commanded act to be performed irreversibly while awaiting that process, or forecloses a challenge to the Commission's authority when it is applied. The possibility of defending a future complaint and uncertainty about the general forum allocation do not alone create the present primary-conduct hardship needed here.

The Court creates no universal exhaustion requirement, guarantees no particular later filing's timeliness or standing, and decides no automatic entitlement to interlocutory relief. A later review must satisfy its own statutory conditions. The conclusion concerns this general announcement and its contingent applications, not a rule that all enforcement proceedings can safely be postponed until penalties are collected. Paragraph 129's identification of other possible enforcement mechanisms does not initiate one or enlarge the present challenge into a decision on every such mechanism.

**Controlling-law application.** Ohio Forestry distinguishes a completed policy from a presently consequential authorization and permits deferral while further application would clarify the claim and meaningful review remains. Toilet Goods supplies the same distinction between completion and a ripe dispute. The particular statement here has that deferred-application character. Franklin's completed census determination and Bennett's conditional change in legal protection involved consequences already attached to an actual determination; the settled opinion about jurisdiction here does not perform either function. Pic-A-State involved an existing business actually stopped by a law reaching its conduct and a sufficiently real enforcement risk, not only uncertainty about the forum for a future claim.

Lee's present-civil-duty rule remains intact. Its participation remand addressed challenged substantive obligations and an incomplete inquiry into their application. Here the challenged instrument is the general forum announcement itself, whose operative text is available and establishes its prospective complaint character. It is not a substantive participation duty mistakenly dismissed only because it lacks a criminal penalty. NAACP forbids assumed jurisdiction before contested merits and does not turn every erroneous lower threshold explanation into an obligatory factual remand. WRTL requires examining the actual claim and remedy; it likewise permits a supported threshold disposition when the pertinent legal character is ascertainable.

**Why no renewed threshold remand.** The independent model correctly identified the lower court's incomplete reliance on finality and legal disagreement, but it treated incomplete knowledge about possible carrier-specific effects as a reason to return this general statement challenge. That conclusion gives a research boundary decisional force it does not possess. The result here does not find that no relevant fact exists anywhere in the administrative record, infer a litigant default or invent a missing complaint. It determines the present effect of the actual instrument under review and confines the disposition to that instrument. Hypothetical presently operative conflicts cannot transform its general authority announcement into an applied directive. If a materially different, actually applied constraint is challenged, its independent review requirements and merits remain open.

## Holdings

### I. National implementing rules coexist with state determinations

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 201(b) authorizes FCC rules implementing the local-competition obligations added to the Communications Act by §§251–252. That authority includes national standards governing the statutory tasks while state commissions retain the negotiations, arbitrations, individual rates and other determinations Congress assigned to them.

**Authority:** Scalia's Parts I–II are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices control the general-authority and associated remand propositions; O'Connor, Thomas and Breyer dissent on their distinct allocation grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's insertion of the local-competition provisions into the Communications Act brings those provisions within its implementing grant. Section 251(d)(1) directs implementation; §252(c) requires state commissions to apply §251 and the Commission's rules. National methodology and state determination of particular rates can coexist. Section 152(b) does not remove the new federal duties from the regulatory authority the amendments create. Louisiana Public Service Commission prevents a general grant from regulating matters Congress left wholly intrastate; it does not disable implementation of newly enacted federal obligations. MCI requires an actual delegation and preservation of operative limits, not a rule that every delegation permits only minor action. The contrary reading gives proper attention to the state role but treats methodology and particular determinations as mutually exclusive when the statute does not. The holding decides authority, not the substantive lawfulness of TELRIC, a confiscatory rate or every instruction the Commission might issue.

### II. The rural-exemption and agreement rules require review within the correct allocation

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical absence-of-federal-authority grounds for vacating Rules 51.405 and 51.303 and displacing otherwise lawful federal rules from state implementation cannot stand. Unresolved substantive objections return for review with the actual statutory rural-exemption, agreement-approval and state-law preservation limits intact.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice coalition controls Part II. The remand does not adopt a new rule resolving the returned substantive objections.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the general implementation rule does not adjudicate every asserted federal instruction. Section 251(f)'s exemption predicates remain for the actual assigned determinations; §§251(d)(3) and 252 retain their preservation and approval limits. MCI requires those specific statutory conditions to retain their force even when a general delegation exists. The lower court therefore must examine any unresolved objection to the rural burden-of-proof rule or preexisting-agreement requirements on its own substance. Rejection of exclusive state regulatory authority supplies no blanket validation of either rule and no permission for state commissions to disregard otherwise lawful federal requirements. The remedial disposition removes the categorical jurisdictional ground and returns the residual questions without selecting a new proof burden or deciding a particular agreement.

### III. A network element need not be a detachable physical object

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 153(29)'s network-element definition includes features, functions and capabilities supplied through facilities or equipment, as well as the physical facilities or equipment themselves. Eligibility under that definition does not establish that §251(d)(2) requires unbundled access to every eligible function.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part III is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Those eight supply a controlling majority; Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on the independent definition component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that operational-support systems, operator and directory services, and vertical-switching functions are not excluded merely because they are not detachable physical components or can also be described as services. The statutory definition itself supplies the answer. The physical-component-only construction would omit its express functional language. But definition and compulsory access are separate inquiries: the Commission must still apply the proprietary-necessity and general-impairment criteria to identify required elements. No general requirement to unbundle every technically available service follows.

### IV. Necessity and impairment must perform independent limiting work

**Holding and operative rule:** In selecting required network elements under §251(d)(2), the FCC must give independent limiting effect to necessity for proprietary elements and impairment for network elements generally, considering realistically available alternatives outside as well as inside the incumbent's network. Rule 51.319 is set aside because the adopted comparison and impairment standard did not perform that task.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, giving the complete rule seven votes. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the invalidity holding that the rule must make element-specific judgments giving necessity and impairment independent constraining force, and joins vacatur and agency remand; he does not supply a separate join in the more specific outside-alternatives requirement. Souter dissents from invalidation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that any increase in cost or decline in quality cannot by itself establish the required impairment while external alternatives are categorically ignored. MCI preserves operative statutory predicates before agency deference. The agency's competitive-entry expertise remains substantial, but it cannot make every purchasing disadvantage a sufficient statutory obstacle. The proprietary standard of significant impairment or thwarting is not invalid simply because it is less than absolute indispensability; its defect includes the cramped alternative comparison limited to the incumbent's nonproprietary elements. The Court does not require access only to an irreplaceable natural monopoly, invent a numerical threshold or choose a replacement list. On remand the agency may explain real entry impairment, actual availability, costs, quality, service requirements and workable selection criteria under the enacted standard. The strongest contrary argument invokes the competitive-entry purpose and danger that theoretical alternatives will require disabling duplication. The Court accepts that real commercial availability matters; that is why an actual inquiry is required rather than either automatic access or theoretical replication.

### V. The Act imposes no freestanding own-facilities prerequisite

**Holding and operative rule:** A requesting telecommunications carrier need not own some local facilities merely to qualify to seek unbundled access under §251(c)(3). Its entitlement to particular elements remains governed by §251(d)(2) and the lawful conditions of access and combination.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part V is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejection of the independent own-facilities prerequisite, without a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(3) reaches any requesting telecommunications carrier and contemplates combining elements into a service. The separate resale route in §251(c)(4) does not insert a minimum-property requirement into the unbundling route. Combining or buying elements entails obligations and commercial consequences distinct from buying a finished retail service for resale; the existence of both routes does not make one redundant. This holding promises no entrant the incumbent's whole network regardless of necessity and impairment and decides no assumed difference in particular rates.

### VI. Existing combinations need not be dismantled

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 51.315(b) lawfully prohibits an incumbent from separating requested network elements already combined in its network unless the requesting carrier asks for separation. Unbundled availability and valuation do not require physical destruction of an existing combination.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Scalia's Parts VI–VII; those parts control the existing-combination and individual-arrangement rules with their stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(3)'s direction to make elements available in a manner permitting the requester to combine them does not require the incumbent first to dismantle a configuration already joined. The Commission reasonably implements nondiscriminatory access by preserving that configuration subject to the requester's option to separate. The contrary reading confuses separately available elements with mandatory physical separation. This holding restores paragraph (b), not the distinct affirmative new-combination duties in paragraphs (c)–(f), and does not require an unestablished configuration to be built. Those duties receive their own disposition. Particular requests remain subject to the Act and valid technical and operational requirements.

### VII. An entrant may select an individual agreement arrangement with its pertinent conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 252(i) permits Rule 51.809's access to an individual interconnection, service or network-element arrangement in an approved agreement on the same pertinent rates, terms and conditions. The rule preserves proof of greater provision costs or technical infeasibility and limits availability to a reasonable period after public filing.

**Authority:** The same nine-Justice coalition joins Part VII and controls this rule and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory reference to any interconnection, service or network element supports selection of an individual arrangement, not a requirement to accept the entire agreement. The entrant may not take an isolated favorable price while discarding its relevant obligations. The incumbent may establish that provision to this requester costs more or is technically infeasible, and the reasonable availability period protects against indefinite use of stale arrangements. The challengers argue that selective access undermines negotiated tradeoffs; the agency reached a contrary practical judgment with nondiscrimination and these safeguards in view. That disagreement does not establish that Congress commanded whole-agreement adoption or that the agency's incentive assessment is irrational. No unrestricted cherry-picking right results.

### VIII. Dialing-parity authority and numbering-cost ripeness remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical absence-of-FCC-authority ground for vacating intraLATA toll-dialing-parity rules is rejected, with remaining objections returned for review. The separate Rule 52.17 numbering-cost challenge remains unripe because its asserted disadvantage depends on particular cost-recovery rate decisions not before the Court.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part VIII parity-authority ground is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. O'Connor agrees on the narrower §251(b)(3)/§251(d)(1) implementation ground stated in her separate opinion. Stone-Zsela agrees in the parity disposition without an additional ground; the five-Justice lead rationale independently controls. All eight Associates join Part VIII's separate numbering-cost ripeness holding; Stone-Zsela agrees only in that disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that federal implementation of §251(b)(3)'s substantive parity duty is authorized. The general implementing grant supports that conclusion, and the narrower substantive-duty ground also supports the parity disposition without resolving the separate state-pricing dispute. Section 271 and other actual statutory limits remain operative. The separate numbering issue concerns who will bear costs under future particular rate decisions. Reversal of the authority ground neither resolves those rates nor creates an accrued numbering injury. The California judgment therefore receives distinct dispositions rather than complete reversal by inference from the general-authority holding. No particular carrier's liability or cost share is fixed.

### IX. Technical feasibility cannot replace the required access criteria

**Holding and operative rule:** The partial vacatur of Rule 51.317's technical-feasibility presumption and Order paragraphs 278 and 281 is affirmed because the presumption and its actual exceptions fail to preserve the required necessity and impairment limits. The separately sustained definition of technical feasibility and other unaffected portions of the rule remain outside that invalidation.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IX is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven control the substantive presumption holding. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the partial invalidation; Souter would remand this component without a final validity ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that technical feasibility may inform element selection: §251(d)(2)'s minimum criteria do not prohibit consideration of additional relevant matters. For elements beyond Rule 51.319's list, once technical feasibility is found, Rule 51.317 permits the state commission to decline unbundling only if either (1) the element is proprietary or would reveal proprietary information, and the requester could offer the same proposed service through other nonproprietary unbundled elements within the incumbent's network; or (2) denial would neither reduce service quality nor increase its financial or administrative cost compared with providing that service through other unbundled elements in that network. Those restrictions reproduce the limiting-standard defect in the required-element list. They do not allow realistic outside alternatives to perform their proper role. The result rests on this independent substantive flaw, not on the mistaken premise that feasibility can never matter. A corrected-delegation remand cannot preserve the existing defective criteria without agency reconsideration. The Court does not forbid every lawful presumption or invalidate all of Rule 51.317.

### X. Superior-quality duties return for review under the correct framework

**Holding and operative rule:** The invalidation of Rules 51.305(a)(4) and 51.311(c) is vacated and remanded for reconsideration of the actual conditions and agency justification under the correct implementing authority. The Court does not decide their ultimate validity or order unconditional reinstatement.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join Part X's corrected-standard remand and its confined review directions. Thomas and Breyer do so under the Court's governing authority framework while retaining their dissent from Part I. Stone-Zsela agrees in the remand without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that §251(c)(2)'s requirement of at-least-equal quality establishes a floor; the absence of an express superior-quality command does not alone show that an implementing rule can never require more. The lower court's existing-network and substantive-conflict objections nevertheless remain for review. Rule 51.305(a)(4) concerns requested, technically feasible interconnection superior to that supplied to the incumbent, affiliates or other interconnection recipients. Rule 51.311(c) separately concerns the element and access superior to the incumbent's own, upon request and to the extent feasible, placing technical-infeasibility proof before the state commission on the incumbent. Lesser quality at the requesting carrier's sole request remains permitted. On remand the actual improvement, statutory relationship and agency explanation must be examined. Compensation alone supplies no missing authority, and nondiscrimination alone does not command every upgrade. The lower court's allowance for modifications necessary to accommodate lawful access is preserved; neither an unconditional superior-facilities right nor a categorical ban on necessary modifications is adopted.

### XI. Affirmative new-combination duties require separate reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule:** The invalidation of Rule 51.315(c)–(f) is vacated and remanded for review under the proper implementing-power standard. Section 251(c)(3)'s direction to enable requesting carriers to combine elements does not unambiguously assign every physical combination task exclusively to the requester; the particular affirmative duties and burdens remain undecided.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part XI is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Breyer's agreement is a remand under the governing authority framework, not a Part I join. Seven Justices control this remand; Stone-Zsela agrees only in its disposition, and Thomas dissents on his independent task-allocation ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the lower construction treated enabling access as an exclusive assignment of all combining labor without adequately addressing the actual delegation, network control, conditions and agency explanation. That differs from preserving existing combinations, which the Court separately upholds. Subsection (c) addresses requested configurations the incumbent does not ordinarily make, only when technically feasible and not impairing other carriers' access or interconnection. Subsection (d) addresses combination with the requester's own elements in a technically feasible manner. The other-carrier condition expressly belongs to (c), not automatically to (d). Subsections (e) and (f) place the relevant infeasibility or other-carrier-impairment showing before the state commission on the incumbent. MCI preserves specific statutory allocations, but the contested sentence does not alone establish the exclusive task allocation the lower court found. The remand neither approves every proof burden nor compels a particular configuration. Voluntary combination and necessary accommodation remain distinct from a mandated new combination.

### XII. The general section 208 announcement is not ripe for review

**Holding and operative rule:** A definite agency announcement of a possible enforcement forum is not ripe merely because the position is complete and the dispute predominantly legal. Because this announcement directs no present change in primary conduct, determines no carrier-specific liability and permits meaningful review of a concrete application, the general challenge must be dismissed without prejudice, without deciding enforcement authority.

**Authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Scalia's Part XII, supplying a controlling majority. Stone-Zsela agrees only in vacatur of the premature enforcement-merits adjudication and dismissal without prejudice as unripe; he adds no enforcement or ripeness ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that finality, standing, fitness and hardship are distinct; it decides ripeness, not independent standing or final-order questions. Ohio Forestry and Toilet Goods distinguish settled policy from presently consequential application. Paragraphs 126–128 definitively assert complaint authority even where a carrier complies with a state-approved agreement, and separately contemplate enforcing agreement terms; a later expedited-procedure rulemaking does not make that assertion tentative. Yet the announcement changes no substantive carrier duty, identifies no operative agreement conflict, revises no state determination and orders no act or liability. A concrete complaint would distinguish statutory enforcement, agreement enforcement and a carrier proceeding that effectively revisits state action.

Footnote 236 says the Commission might decline financial penalties in some state-authorized circumstances; it establishes neither immunity nor a required penalty. The statement that judicially decided issues are unlikely to be revisited likewise creates no absolute preclusion rule. The absence of an absolute safe harbor is not itself withdrawal of an established protection or present liability. Bennett's operative legal-protection change and Franklin's completed determination are therefore different.

Section 208(a) supplies complaint, notice, opportunity to satisfy or answer and investigation under its conditions; §208(b)(3) makes an order concluding a covered investigation appealable under §402(a). Concrete application permits the authority objection in its actual setting. The prospect of defending a future complaint and uncertainty about forum allocation do not alone establish present primary-conduct hardship. Lee preserves review of presently burdensome civil duties, and Pic-A-State concerns an existing business actually constrained by a law; neither condition is established by this instrument. NAACP and Paradise require examination of the actual claim, not an automatic further inquiry into hypothetical applications. The Court creates no universal exhaustion rule, promises no future filing's standing or timeliness, and does not defer every challenge until penalties are collected. A presently binding or burdensome statement remains a different case. The Court decides neither carrier statutory-violation authority, agreement enforcement, review of state determinations under §252(e)(6), nor state-failure authority under §252(e)(5); no carrier complaint, agreement, state order or penalty is adjudicated.

Required-element selection must give necessity and impairment independent effect and consider realistic alternatives; the existing Rule 51.319 list and identified feasibility presumption cannot supply the defective standard. Functional network elements and requests by carriers without their own facilities remain eligible subject to lawful access criteria. Superior-quality and affirmative new-combination requirements return for review without final merits approval. General rulemaking authority decides no particular rate or section 208 enforcement power. ## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Congress authorized federal implementation of §251's express substantive access duties, but §252(d)'s state-pricing assignment does not authorize comprehensive federal replacement of that choice. Rural-exemption and agreement-approval determinations also retain their specific state allocation. National methodology and local rates can sometimes coexist, but that observation cannot itself supply the particular displacement she finds here. She accepts the other rule-specific judgments and joins the lead's other eligible parts. In the California matter, §251(b)(3)'s substantive dialing-parity duty and §251(d)(1)'s implementing instruction support reversal independently of the broader authority rationale. This is no view that States may disregard federal duties, and it decides no TELRIC or actual-rate merits.

**Souter, dissenting in part.** The Act makes unbundled access an instrument of competitive entry rather than a last resort after facilities replication. Increased costs and lost quality affect the requested service; the higher proprietary threshold and nonproprietary alternative give necessity and impairment distinct work. Under Chevron and the contextual delegation analysis of his MCI dissent, the agency reasonably declined a categorical external-source requirement that could demand commercially disabling duplication. He would sustain Rule 51.319 against this statutory attack. He does not demand access to every technically available element. The separate Rule 51.317 presumption still requires review of its burdens and explanation: feasibility may be an additional consideration, and its actual exceptions prevent treating feasibility as its only condition. He would vacate the partial presumption invalidation and remand under the proper authority standard, without unconditional reinstatement or a final validity decision. He joins the rest of the Court's opinion, including the section 208 ripeness disposition.

**Thomas, dissenting in part, joined in the authority and parity grounds by Breyer.** Section 152(b), the interstate context of §201(b), and the Act's particular assignments prevent converting an implementation deadline into general FCC regulatory jurisdiction over every state-assigned task. Louisiana Public Service Commission distinguishes federal substantive obligations from a federal agency's jurisdiction to enforce them by regulation. The new provisions impose national duties but specifically assign pricing and other decisions to state commissions. Detailed federal methodology can replace lawful local policy choices rather than simply establish statutory boundaries. They would retain the identified authority and intraLATA-parity vacaturs, without making substantive parity optional. Both agree with the superior-quality remand under the Court's contrary governing framework and with the section 208 unripe disposition; those agreements do not become general-authority joins.

Thomas alone would also retain invalidation of Rule 51.315(c)–(f). Congress's direction to enable the requesting carrier to combine elements, reinforced by the separate resale route, assigns that affirmative undertaking to the requester. MCI requires the particular allocation to constrain a general implementing grant. The contrary reading stresses that the sentence speaks of enabling rather than exclusive labor, but compensation and feasibility safeguards cannot provide an affirmative duty the statute withholds. Preserving an existing combination requires no new combining work and is therefore different. Necessary accommodation and voluntary new combinations remain available. Breyer does not join this ground and accepts the Court's new-combination remand.

**Breyer, concurring.** He joins the Court's Rule 51.319 criterion holding and adds that the agency has not convincingly explained its treatment of available alternatives, administrative costs and investment incentives. Allentown Mack respects agency expertise when exercised through a reasoned application of its actual lawful standard; it does not supply that explanation here. This additional objection imposes no essential-facilities-only rule, numerical cost threshold or judicial replacement list. It does not withdraw his contrary general-authority position or his conditional remedial agreement on superior-quality and new-combination review.

The Commission must reconsider the vacated required-element list under lawful necessity and impairment criteria; no judicial replacement list issues. The identified feasibility presumption remains invalid, while existing-combination and individual-arrangement protections return to force within their conditions. The superior-quality and new-combination portions return for corrected-standard review without automatic substantive reinstatement. Unresolved rural-exemption, preexisting-agreement and other preserved objections receive ordinary consideration. The section 208 general-announcement challenge must be dismissed without prejudice as unripe; the California numbering-cost challenge remains unripe. No new deadline, carrier-specific rate, complaint, sanction or state determination is entered.
