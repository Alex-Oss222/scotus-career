### Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability

**Governing question:** Who decides agreement to arbitrate arbitrability and how appellate courts review that determination.

**Current rule:** Courts independently decide agreement to arbitrate arbitrability unless ordinary applicable contract law establishes clear and unmistakable delegation. Appearing to object to a panel’s jurisdiction does not itself delegate that question, and a company’s submission does not establish an owner’s personal assent. Actual delegation brings the arbitrator’s arbitrability decision within limited award review. Appellate courts independently review district-court legal conclusions and review findings for clear error; arbitration adds no deference to the district court’s independent determination.

## Holdings

### Neither instrument clearly waives the statutory judicial forum

**Operative rule:** A union-negotiated surrender of an employee's judicial forum for an independent federal discrimination claim must be clear and unmistakable: the agreement must identify that statutory claim or an equivalent class of federal rights rather than merely submit contractual disputes. Neither this CBA nor the separate Seniority Plan does so, and their grievance provisions cannot support dismissal of Wright's ADA action for nonexhaustion; whether a sufficiently explicit collective waiver would be enforceable remains undecided.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the clarity rule, both instrument-specific applications and the bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Gardner-Denver distinguishes an individual's statutory discrimination right from rights created by collective bargaining. Metropolitan Edison requires clarity before a union is understood to waive protected statutory rights. Gilmer involved an individual's agreement to arbitrate a statutory claim; it does not supply missing assent in this collective agreement. AT&T Technologies and First Options require determination of the agreement actually made before invoking arbitral authority. First Options concerns antecedent assent and delegation, not a prior resolution of every collective statutory-forum question.

The employers emphasize that qualification for employment concerns a subject comprehensively covered by the CBA and that its grievance decisions are final. Clause 15(B)'s machinery addresses disputes within the collective relationship. Clause 15(F)'s employment coverage, together with its exclusion of matters outside the agreement, does not expressly incorporate the ADA or submit independent federal claims. Clause 17 prevents the contract from violating law; a legality safeguard is not an agreement to arbitrate every statutory cause of action. Mastrobuono requires reading the actual instrument together, not presuming the broadest imaginable submission.

The Seniority Plan is a separate instrument. Its coverage of disputes about its own terms, interpretation, application and implementing rules likewise identifies no surrender of this ADA forum. The same employment event may give rise to both contractual and statutory questions without merging their sources.

The Court therefore need not decide whether an explicit union-negotiated waiver would be enforceable, resolve general FAA coverage or find the union's alleged advice to sue established. Wright's qualifications, benefits history, discrimination claim and independent defenses remain for the proper court. No ADA violation or entitlement to recovery follows from removal of the grievance-exhaustion bar.
