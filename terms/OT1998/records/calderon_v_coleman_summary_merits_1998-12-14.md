## Supported record and limits

**Research cutoff:** 1998-12-14.

The jury chose between death and life without ordinary parole. The charge described gubernatorial power to commute a life-without-parole sentence into one permitting parole, then told the jury not to consider or speculate about commutation and to consider only the stated aggravating and mitigating factors. For a person with Coleman's prior felonies, California law required consultation with the Board of Prison Terms and approval of four California Supreme Court Justices. ## Controlling-holding section

### I. Chapman governs this available capital-selection misinformation claim

**Holding and operative rule:** For a constitutional trial error in the commutation instruction affecting this jury's choice between death and life without parole, when the claim is properly available on federal habeas, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt from the whole penalty record that the error did not contribute to the actual death sentence. If an available constitutional violation fails that showing, conditional sentence relief must permit a reasonable opportunity for a lawful new penalty proceeding; a proper harmlessness showing permits denial of relief.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Ginsburg disagrees with the remand disposition but directly joins this rule and its conditions. No combination of different standards establishes its authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that uncertainty about this constitutional error's contribution to the jury's punishment choice remains with the State. Brecht retained Chapman for preserved protected-silence error, and O'Neal and Roy extended it to the specified personal-intent category. Those decisions did not already settle every other habeas error. The present instruction's description of the sole nondeath alternative bears directly on the reliability of capital selection and the jury's treatment of actual mitigation. That relationship justifies the same noncontribution burden here.

Collateral finality and respect for state judgments remain substantial, but they do not supply a sufficient reason to lower the constitutional burden for this category. Independent habeas requirements continue to operate, and Chapman permits a harmless sentence to stand. The inquiry concerns this jury's actual decision, not whether a properly instructed hypothetical jury could have imposed death. The reviewing court must consider the complete charge, mitigation, prosecution theory and supported jury requests. A direction to disregard commutation may matter; it does not itself prove that the earlier misinformation made no contribution.

The Court assumes the constitutional error found below to resolve the presented effect question. It neither newly decides the unpresented accuracy rule nor treats every capital instruction as structural. The rule is confined to this defined capital-selection error and does not settle every remaining constitutional-habeas category. A final writ requires an available established violation and failure of the State's showing, not merely an appellate method error.

**Precedent treatment:** Brecht, O'Neal and Roy are extended to this defined capital-selection category while their prior holdings and reservations otherwise remain. Chapman retains the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden; Kotteakos is not substituted for that burden here.

### II. The required effect inquiry is substantive, not a verbal formula

**Holding and operative rule:** Identifying this instructional error under Boyde does not by itself establish the effect necessary for relief under the applicable harmlessness rule. A court may perform that inquiry within its existing discussion without prescribed words, a particular citation or a separate heading, but its actual reasoning must assess the error's relationship to the sentence under the governing burden and treatment of uncertainty.

**Authority:** Part III of the per curiam opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices. That agreement concerns the governing distinction and record boundaries; it does not resolve their disagreement about the sufficiency of the analysis in this case.

**Controlling explanation:** Boyde asks whether an instruction was reasonably likely to operate in a constitutionally impermissible way. The consequence of that error remains a distinct inquiry here. Sochor requires an actual constitutional cure rather than a label, while rejecting any indispensable verbal formula. Its principle informs the inquiry without converting this commutation instruction into an invalid aggravating factor or authorizing a federal court to select a sentence through fresh weighing.

The District Court examined the actual prosecution argument, aggravation, mitigation, conviction-document request and record as a whole. It also explained why the corrective direction did not cure the misinformation. The Ninth Circuit expressly received the State's effect objection. It would therefore be incorrect to describe these courts as having ignored the record or the issue. Conversely, discussing the record while identifying likely interference with mitigation does not automatically establish the separate causal conclusion the selected harmlessness rule requires.

The trial evidence must remain distinct from mitigation first developed during habeas. New's excluded account of deliberations supplies no finding of actual release discussion. The jury's request for conviction documents and its separate general instruction rereading do not establish a specific commutation inquiry. The trial judge's automatic-review findings are not jury findings on individual mitigators. Supported inferences remain available, but neither strong aggravation nor an imagined jury's preferred sentence can substitute for the required actual-effect determination. The Court orders no new admission ruling or factual hearing.

**Precedent treatment:** Boyde retains its error-identification function. Sochor's substantive-correction principle is applied without transferring its distinct invalid-aggravator facts. Roy's actual-verdict approach informs the inquiry without requiring factor-specific jury findings or supplying an appellate substitute sentence.

## Event

Calderon v. Coleman, No. 98-437. Certiorari grant and summary merits disposition, December 14, 1998, on the State's petition from the Ninth Circuit's July 28, 1998 judgment in Nos. 97-99013 and 97-99014. No oral argument is held. The District Court's sentence-relief order was signed March 27, 1997, and filed March 28; the appellate court affirmed denial of conviction relief and relief from the death sentence.

The penalty jury was told that the Governor could change a life-without-parole sentence into one permitting parole, then directed not to consider or speculate about commutation. Because Coleman had at least two prior felony convictions, commutation required consultation with the Board of Prison Terms and approval of four California Supreme Court Justices. The Board's recommendation was advisory; consultation did not replace the separate requirement of judicial approval. The charge omitted those restrictions and thus conveyed that the Governor could act alone. The lower federal courts found constitutional error; this Court assumes that determination for the presented effect inquiry.

The jury heard mitigation concerning childhood and family trauma, prolonged institutionalization and a psychiatrist's tentative diagnoses. Counsel used that evidence to explain Coleman's behavior; the prosecution emphasized violent conduct and danger within and beyond prison. During deliberations the jury requested the prior-conviction documents and, separately, a general instruction rereading that included the corrective language. Neither request was a specific question about commutation. The significance of those events, rather than any presumed release discussion, belongs to the effect inquiry.

The accepted question is whether the lower courts' assessment of the constitutionally defective commutation instruction supplies the required determination of its effect on the capital sentence, or whether a distinct inquiry under the applicable harmlessness standard is necessary. The Court does not reach the separate underlying constitutional accuracy question the State excluded from its petition.

## Holdings

### I. Chapman governs this available capital-selection misinformation claim

**Holding and operative rule:** For a constitutional trial error in the commutation instruction affecting this jury's choice between death and life without parole, when the claim is properly available on federal habeas, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt from the whole penalty record that the error did not contribute to the actual death sentence. If an available constitutional violation fails that showing, conditional sentence relief must permit a reasonable opportunity for a lawful new penalty proceeding; a proper harmlessness showing permits denial of relief.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Ginsburg disagrees with the remand disposition but directly joins this rule and its conditions. No combination of different standards establishes its authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that uncertainty about this constitutional error's contribution to the jury's punishment choice remains with the State. Brecht retained Chapman for preserved protected-silence error, and O'Neal and Roy extended it to the specified personal-intent category. Those decisions did not already settle every other habeas error. The present instruction's description of the sole nondeath alternative bears directly on the reliability of capital selection and the jury's treatment of actual mitigation. That relationship justifies the same noncontribution burden here.

Collateral finality and respect for state judgments remain substantial, but they do not supply a sufficient reason to lower the constitutional burden for this category. Independent habeas requirements continue to operate, and Chapman permits a harmless sentence to stand. The inquiry concerns this jury's actual decision, not whether a properly instructed hypothetical jury could have imposed death. The reviewing court must consider the complete charge, mitigation, prosecution theory and supported jury requests. A direction to disregard commutation may matter; it does not itself prove that the earlier misinformation made no contribution.

The Court assumes the constitutional error found below to resolve the presented effect question. It neither newly decides the unpresented accuracy rule nor treats every capital instruction as structural. The rule is confined to this defined capital-selection error and does not settle every remaining constitutional-habeas category. A final writ requires an available established violation and failure of the State's showing, not merely an appellate method error.

**Precedent treatment:** Brecht, O'Neal and Roy are extended to this defined capital-selection category while their prior holdings and reservations otherwise remain. Chapman retains the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden; Kotteakos is not substituted for that burden here.

### II. The required effect inquiry is substantive, not a verbal formula

**Holding and operative rule:** Identifying this instructional error under Boyde does not by itself establish the effect necessary for relief under the applicable harmlessness rule. A court may perform that inquiry within its existing discussion without prescribed words, a particular citation or a separate heading, but its actual reasoning must assess the error's relationship to the sentence under the governing burden and treatment of uncertainty.

**Authority:** Part III of the per curiam opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices. That agreement concerns the governing distinction and record boundaries; it does not resolve their disagreement about the sufficiency of the analysis in this case.

**Controlling explanation:** Boyde asks whether an instruction was reasonably likely to operate in a constitutionally impermissible way. The consequence of that error remains a distinct inquiry here. Sochor requires an actual constitutional cure rather than a label, while rejecting any indispensable verbal formula. Its principle informs the inquiry without converting this commutation instruction into an invalid aggravating factor or authorizing a federal court to select a sentence through fresh weighing.

The District Court examined the actual prosecution argument, aggravation, mitigation, conviction-document request and record as a whole. It also explained why the corrective direction did not cure the misinformation. The Ninth Circuit expressly received the State's effect objection. It would therefore be incorrect to describe these courts as having ignored the record or the issue. Conversely, discussing the record while identifying likely interference with mitigation does not automatically establish the separate causal conclusion the selected harmlessness rule requires.

The trial evidence must remain distinct from mitigation first developed during habeas. New's excluded account of deliberations supplies no finding of actual release discussion. The jury's request for conviction documents and its separate general instruction rereading do not establish a specific commutation inquiry. The trial judge's automatic-review findings are not jury findings on individual mitigators. Supported inferences remain available, but neither strong aggravation nor an imagined jury's preferred sentence can substitute for the required actual-effect determination. The Court orders no new admission ruling or factual hearing.

**Precedent treatment:** Boyde retains its error-identification function. Sochor's substantive-correction principle is applied without transferring its distinct invalid-aggravator facts. Roy's actual-verdict approach informs the inquiry without requiring factor-specific jury findings or supplying an appellate substitute sentence.

### III. The limited vacatur requires renewed review without deciding harmlessness

**Holding and operative rule:** The commutation-based appellate sentence disposition is vacated and returned for a genuine whole-record inquiry into the error's effect under the governing rule. The receiving court may reach the same sentence-relief result, but neither the constitutional defect alone nor a failure to use a particular formula decides the inquiry; guilt, genuinely independent habeas conditions and the actual appellate status of other penalty claims remain undisturbed.

**Authority:** Part IV and the judgment have Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. These seven adopt the confined return without a common join in one harmlessness standard. The five direct joins in Part II independently establish Chapman as the rule governing further proceedings; no fractured-opinion synthesis is used.

**Controlling explanation:** The District Court identified a concrete mechanism: overstating the possibility of release could diminish the mitigating force of testimony explaining violent behavior, particularly when prior convictions were prominent yet also restricted commutation. That analysis is substantial. Its ultimate likely-preclusion and potential-diversion account, however, did not complete the required assessment against the corrective charge, the actual institutional-danger argument and the limits of what the conviction request establishes. The appellate court then answered the effect objection with Boyde's reasonable-likelihood formulation.

The Court returns that unfinished causal task rather than demanding another label. The receiving court must consider the entire appropriate trial record, including the State's substantial aggravation and prison-safety case, the broader danger language described below, the mitigation actually heard and the complete instructions. It must determine the error's effect without presuming subjective deliberations or treating the court's own sentencing preference as the jury's verdict. Neither the corrective direction nor the strength of aggravation is conclusive by itself.

No finding that the error was harmless, or that the State has already failed its governing burden, issues now. The court may retain sentence relief after the proper inquiry. No new evidentiary hearing, admission of excluded material, execution permission, release order or fixed sentencing deadline follows. Other penalty claims were addressed below but not reached on appeal; this disposition neither decides them nor revives an abandoned claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Roy's distinction between an actual method defect and an absent citation supports the limited return; it does not supply a result on this record. Brecht and O'Neal preserve the distinction between corrective remand and conditional relief after an available violation fails the applicable showing. Simmons's different express parole question is not imported.

### IV. Carriger supplies the former-law rule for this pending capital application

Former §2254(d) governs that factual inquiry: its presumption requires a historical fact determination after a merits hearing by a competent state court in a proceeding involving the applicant and the State or its officer or agent, evidenced by a written finding, opinion or other reliable and adequate written indicia; paragraph (8) requires examination of whether the pertinent produced state record as a whole fairly supports the finding.

**Authority:** Part V of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. This is application of Carriger's existing controlling rule; the Chief Justice supplies no additional temporal-law ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Carriger extended Lindh's identified application-date construction to a capital application already pending at enactment outside established chapter 154 qualification. The reported 1989 filing and continuing amended proceeding place this case within that rule. Capital status does not establish chapter 154 qualification, and the later judgment date does not convert this into a newly filed application.

The legal harmlessness inquiry remains distinct from the proper treatment of historical state findings. Separately directed provisions retain their own operation. ## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He would use demanding whole-record substantial-influence review in this category, with genuine grave doubt preventing a finding of harmlessness. His Brecht and O'Neal positions distinguish collateral review while requiring the State to explain why an established violation did not substantially influence the actual decision. That is neither a sufficiency-of-aggravation test nor a rule that the prisoner loses whenever the evidence is evenly balanced. He accepts the binding Chapman categories but would not extend them here. Stringer's distinct weighing-error rule does not make every capital habeas claim identical. The District Court's plausible account of diminished mitigation must be assessed against the full charge and independent institutional-danger case; he supports a return without finding harmlessness or directing denial of relief.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** They would require the prisoner to establish substantial injurious influence for this category outside the previously controlling Chapman holdings. An evenly balanced showing does not satisfy their proposed burden. They respect Roy's particular personal-intent rule without extending it universally. Potential diversion and instructional misunderstanding do not themselves establish the substantial effect they require. Their objection concerns the unfinished substantive inquiry, not missing words; serious aggravation is not an automatic cure. They rely neither on New's excluded account nor on a universal innocence prerequisite, and they do not require factor-specific capital findings derived from Scalia's different omitted-purpose analysis in Roy.

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He would affirm under substantial-influence review with grave doubt preventing harmlessness. The District Court did more than identify inaccurate words: it connected the misleading nondeath alternative to mitigation explaining violent behavior, the prominence of the prior convictions and the prosecution's danger case. The jury's conviction-document request shows salience without proving an actual release discussion. He considers the corrective charge, repeated instructions, extensive violence, prison-safety argument and reported short deliberation, but finds that those matters do not dispel grave doubt about substantial influence. The broader danger language and the instruction itself leave a concrete connection to the sentencing choice even without an express prosecutorial reference to commutation. **Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** She joins the Chapman rule for this defined capital-selection error but concludes that the State cannot establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt on the existing record. The misleading account could turn explanations of violent behavior into reasons to doubt the nondeath option; the convictions emphasized in aggravation also restricted commutation.
