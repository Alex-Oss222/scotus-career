## Holdings
#### Compatible federal fraud remedies do not impair the actual state insurance scheme

**Operative rule.** A generally applicable federal law impairs protected state insurance law when its operation contradicts the state law or frustrates an actual state policy or the administration of the state scheme; literal contradiction is not the only possible impairment. An additional private action or greater federal remedy does not itself establish impairment, and the Co-Payors' RICO claim may proceed because it targets fraud Nevada forbids while leaving the State's scheme intact and displacing no demonstrated exclusive remedial arrangement.

**Authority.** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court has all nine Justices' direct joins and controls the rule, application and reservations.

**Controlling explanation.** The parties do not dispute here that Nevada enacted the relevant laws to regulate the business of insurance or that RICO does not specifically relate to that business. Those predicates frame the impairment question. Fabe protects policy performance and administration necessary to implement it, so the inquiry must examine the actual state policy that federal law would frustrate. Barnett Bank separately addresses an insurance-specific federal exception; RICO has no such subject matter and receives no exemption by that analogy.

The insurers correctly observe that enforcement machinery and remedial choices can form part of protected state policy. A common prohibition of fraud therefore does not by itself establish compatibility. Here, however, Nevada prohibits the alleged deception, and its regulatory authority and remedies are not established as an exclusive arrangement displaced by this Co-Payor action. RICO neither invalidates the state command nor replaces its regulator or defeats an identified policy forbidding this action or remedy. Larger federal damages alone do not establish the missing interference.

The allegations span 1985–1988. The 1987 addition of an express private remedy in Nevada Revised Statutes §686A.310 is not applied retrospectively, and the Court does not decide whether an earlier statutory action was implied. Even assuming no statutory private remedy for earlier conduct, that absence does not itself establish a policy making administrative sanctions exclusive or excluding independent federal relief. The result rests on the actual nonexclusive arrangement, not invented earlier private rights or a comprehensive state-remedy inventory.

A federal claim requiring conduct the State forbids, nullifying a state-approved practice or displacing an exclusive remedial structure established by enacted law would present impairment. No such operation, conflicting allocation of a finite insolvency estate or displacement of an administrative judgment is established here. The Court decides neither hypothetical exclusive schemes nor unrelated insurance conduct, criminal RICO or other federal laws. ERISA, antitrust, the rejected Premium Payor branch and ordinary RICO prerequisites remain unadjudicated in this review.
