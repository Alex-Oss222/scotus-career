## Controlling holdings

### I. Rule 702 requires reliable specialized knowledge, whatever the field

**Holding and operative rule:** Original Rule 702 requires the trial judge to assess the reliability and fit of scientific, technical and other specialized expert testimony. Experience may supply relevant knowledge, but the expert's professional label does not excuse a reliable connection between the method used and the proposition offered.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt this rule; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Rule addresses knowledge that will assist the factfinder and expressly includes technical and other specialized knowledge. Its reliability requirement cannot turn on whether the witness describes the same practical inference as scientific or experiential. Daubert supplies the preliminary duty to examine foundation and fit; its scientific setting does not establish a categorical exemption for technical testimony. Joiner supplies the requirement that the offered conclusion have an adequately supported inferential connection. Experience can provide that connection, but credentials alone cannot replace it. The Eleventh Circuit therefore erred in excluding experience-based tire analysis from the reliability inquiry. Cross-examination remains available for admissible evidence; it does not eliminate the threshold foundation required by the Rule. This holding does not decide ultimate defect or causation.

**Precedent treatment:** Daubert's reliability-and-fit duty is extended to the other categories of knowledge expressly covered by original Rule 702; its flexible character remains. Joiner's inferential-connection rule applies to this technical proffer, without converting admissibility into a decision on the underlying claim.

### II. The reliability inquiry must fit the proffer and receives abuse-of-discretion review

**Holding and operative rule:** A trial judge may use Daubert's nonexclusive reliability considerations when they help evaluate the particular expertise, method and application, but need not demand every consideration in every field. Appellate abuse-of-discretion review governs both the reasonable choice of how to assess reliability and the ultimate admission or exclusion, while categorical legal error remains independently correctable.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by the same nine-Justice coalition identified for Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Daubert's considerations include testability and testing, peer review and publication, meaningful error information and operational standards, and acceptance. Their usefulness depends on the question and the proposed expertise. A technical practice may yield reliable knowledge without laboratory testing or publication; conversely, the assertion that a method rests on experience does not establish its reliability. The judge must select considerations that examine the method's actual work and explain its connection to the conclusion. Flexibility permits a suitable inquiry, not abandonment of screening. Joiner's allocation of appellate review preserves correction of legal error and deference to permissible evidentiary judgments; an exclusion's effect on summary judgment does not change that allocation. The inquiry concerns preliminary foundation, not proof of the ultimate tort claim. The Court requires no invariant checklist, identical independent study, expert appointment or new hearing, and permits no exclusion merely because the judge disagrees with a well-supported conclusion.

**Precedent treatment:** Daubert's considerations remain useful but nonmandatory tools. Joiner's uniform review rule is applied to technical evidence and to reasonable selection of the reliability inquiry; its protection against unsupported analytical gaps remains intact.

### III-A. The District Court was entitled to reject the unsupported defect inference

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 702 permitted the District Court to reject Carlson's defect inference because he did not reliably explain how his inspection distinguished defect from abuse or why the observations supported the asserted cause. This permissible-exclusion proposition does not itself settle the distinct instruction governing appellate review and summary judgment on remand.

**Authority:** Part III-A of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela joins this bounded application and its unsupported-inference ground; Justice Stevens reserves application for the Court of Appeals. Part III-B's fuller direct-disposition discussion and mandatory conforming remand have a separate coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Carlson's experience was relevant, but experience alone did not establish the connection between his observations and a defect rather than abuse. Assuming that separation caused the blowout did not supply that further inference. Joiner permits the judge to examine the analytical connection without deciding ultimate causation. On this proffer, the District Court was entitled to reject an unsupported bridge between inspection and conclusion. The Court holds rejection permissible, not compulsory for every reasonable judge, and does not discredit visual tire inspection as a category or authorize exclusion merely because a judge disagrees with a well-supported conclusion.

**Precedent treatment:** Joiner's permission to reject an unsupported inference is applied to this technical proffer. Daubert's flexible foundation-and-fit requirement remains distinct from a finding of ultimate causation.

The mandate does not require a new hearing, appointed expert or another appellate determination whether to sustain this exclusion.

**Authority:** Part III-B of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven Justices. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justice Stevens oppose this conforming mandate and retain their distinct appellate-review remedies.

**Controlling explanation:** Carlson proposed to exclude overdeflection unless at least two of four physical signs were significant, then infer a manufacturing or design defect. Thus the initial concern about an inflexible checklist does not describe the ruling under review. Joiner's deferential allocation permits the Court to resolve the supported exclusion now; the Court need not leave that application for another appellate determination. Because no other defect evidence remains on this record, the exclusion carries the stated summary-judgment consequence. This direct disposition does not establish the accident's actual cause.

**Precedent treatment:** Joiner's abuse-of-discretion allocation is applied to the reconsidered ruling and its supported procedural consequence. Daubert's adaptable inquiry is followed as reflected in reconsideration; neither decision requires rejection of every experience-based method.

Experience may supply relevant knowledge, but the expert's professional label does not excuse a reliable connection between the method used and the proposition offered.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt this rule; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Rule addresses knowledge that will assist the factfinder and expressly includes technical and other specialized knowledge. Its reliability requirement cannot turn on whether the witness describes the same practical inference as scientific or experiential. Daubert supplies the preliminary duty to examine foundation and fit; its scientific setting does not establish a categorical exemption for technical testimony. Joiner supplies the requirement that the offered conclusion have an adequately supported inferential connection. Experience can provide that connection, but credentials alone cannot replace it. The Eleventh Circuit therefore erred in excluding experience-based tire analysis from the reliability inquiry. Cross-examination remains available for admissible evidence; it does not eliminate the threshold foundation required by the Rule. This holding does not decide ultimate defect or causation.

**Precedent treatment:** Daubert's reliability-and-fit duty is extended to the other categories of knowledge expressly covered by original Rule 702; its flexible character remains. Joiner's inferential-connection rule applies to this technical proffer, without converting admissibility into a decision on the underlying claim.

### II. The reliability inquiry must fit the proffer and receives abuse-of-discretion review

**Holding and operative rule:** A trial judge may use Daubert's nonexclusive reliability considerations when they help evaluate the particular expertise, method and application, but need not demand every consideration in every field. Appellate abuse-of-discretion review governs both the reasonable choice of how to assess reliability and the ultimate admission or exclusion, while categorical legal error remains independently correctable.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by the same nine-Justice coalition identified for Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Daubert's considerations include testability and testing, peer review and publication, meaningful error information and operational standards, and acceptance. Their usefulness depends on the question and the proposed expertise. A technical practice may yield reliable knowledge without laboratory testing or publication; conversely, the assertion that a method rests on experience does not establish its reliability. The judge must select considerations that examine the method's actual work and explain its connection to the conclusion. Flexibility permits a suitable inquiry, not abandonment of screening. Joiner's allocation of appellate review preserves correction of legal error and deference to permissible evidentiary judgments; an exclusion's effect on summary judgment does not change that allocation. The inquiry concerns preliminary foundation, not proof of the ultimate tort claim. The Court requires no invariant checklist, identical independent study, expert appointment or new hearing, and permits no exclusion merely because the judge disagrees with a well-supported conclusion.

**Precedent treatment:** Daubert's considerations remain useful but nonmandatory tools. Joiner's uniform review rule is applied to technical evidence and to reasonable selection of the reliability inquiry; its protection against unsupported analytical gaps remains intact.

### III-A. The District Court was entitled to reject the unsupported defect inference

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 702 permitted the District Court to reject Carlson's defect inference because he did not reliably explain how his inspection distinguished defect from abuse or why the observations supported the asserted cause. This permissible-exclusion proposition does not itself settle the distinct instruction governing appellate review and summary judgment on remand.

**Authority:** Part III-A of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela joins this bounded application and its unsupported-inference ground; Justice Stevens reserves application for the Court of Appeals. Part III-B's fuller direct-disposition discussion and mandatory conforming remand have a separate coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Carlson's experience was relevant, but experience alone did not establish the connection between his observations and a defect rather than abuse. Assuming that separation caused the blowout did not supply that further inference. Joiner permits the judge to examine the analytical connection without deciding ultimate causation. On this proffer, the District Court was entitled to reject an unsupported bridge between inspection and conclusion. The Court holds rejection permissible, not compulsory for every reasonable judge, and does not discredit visual tire inspection as a category or authorize exclusion merely because a judge disagrees with a well-supported conclusion.

**Precedent treatment:** Joiner's permission to reject an unsupported inference is applied to this technical proffer. Daubert's flexible foundation-and-fit requirement remains distinct from a finding of ultimate causation.

### III-B. The remand must conform to the exclusion and resulting summary judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** On the reconsidered proffer, the case is remanded for a disposition sustaining the exclusion and resulting defense summary judgment, because the record supplies no remaining evidence of defect. The mandate does not require a new hearing, appointed expert or another appellate determination whether to sustain this exclusion.

**Authority:** Part III-B of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven Justices. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justice Stevens oppose this conforming mandate and retain their distinct appellate-review remedies.

**Controlling explanation:** Carlson proposed to exclude overdeflection unless at least two of four physical signs were significant, then infer a manufacturing or design defect. He acknowledged shoulder wear, a bead groove, discoloration, rim-flange marks and inadequately repaired punctures, and explained why he regarded the symptoms as insufficient. Those explanations did not require acceptance of his significance threshold or the resulting elimination of misuse. After reconsideration, the District Court recognized Daubert's flexibility and possible accepted uses of visual inspection; its remaining objection concerned the analysis connecting this inspection to defect. Thus the initial concern about an inflexible checklist does not describe the ruling under review. Joiner's deferential allocation permits the Court to resolve the supported exclusion now; the Court need not leave that application for another appellate determination. Because no other defect evidence remains on this record, the exclusion carries the stated summary-judgment consequence. This direct disposition does not establish the accident's actual cause.

**Precedent treatment:** Joiner's abuse-of-discretion allocation is applied to the reconsidered ruling and its supported procedural consequence. Daubert's adaptable inquiry is followed as reflected in reconsideration; neither decision requires rejection of every experience-based method.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Original Rule 702 governs. The Court adopts no later Rule amendment, universal technical checklist or substantive finding that misuse rather than defect caused the accident. The ruling establishes neither automatic admissibility of experiential evidence nor categorical unreliability of visual inspection.

## Precedent Treatment

Daubert supplies flexible foundation and fit and now reaches all knowledge covered by original Rule 702, while its nonexclusive considerations remain adaptable. Joiner supplies uniform abuse-of-discretion review and permission to examine an unsupported analytical connection; both apply to technical expertise. Neither decision becomes a mandatory-factor checklist or a merits causation rule. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Scientific, technical and other specialized testimony receives reliability-and-fit screening suited to the actual proffer. The judge's reasonable screening method and evidentiary ruling receive abuse-of-discretion review, subject to correction of legal error. This particular exclusion and the resulting summary judgment are sustained; experience-based tire analysis as a category remains available when adequately supported.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins Parts I–II and Part III-A's permissible-exclusion proposition and unsupported-inference ground, and concurs in reversal, but dissents from Part III-B's operative remand. Rule 702 addresses the knowledge offered rather than the expert's professional label. Carlson's experience was relevant, but he did not reliably explain how his inspection distinguished defect from abuse or why the observations supported the asserted cause; the District Court was entitled to reject that unsupported analytical bridge. Stone-Zsela nevertheless would direct the Eleventh Circuit to review the reliability ruling for abuse of discretion under the complete Rule 702 framework and reinstate summary judgment if no admissible evidence supports defect and causation. His position preserves flexible reliability screening without mandatory peer review or testing in every technical field, displacing the jury on reliable disputed evidence, or excluding a well-supported conclusion merely because the judge disagrees with it.

Justice Stevens joins Parts I–II but dissents from Part III and the judgment. The common Rule 702 obligation defeats the Eleventh Circuit's categorical exemption, but that court should first apply the correct framework to the complete technical proffer. As in his Joiner position, agreement on appellate review and inferential fit does not settle the actual exercise of discretion. He would vacate and remand for appellate application, without ordering admission, reopening every foundation submission or requiring a new hearing.

## Procedure After Action

The Eleventh Circuit must conform its disposition to the sustained exclusion and resulting defense summary judgment on the record presented. The Court requires no new foundation hearing or expert appointment and makes no finding of the accident's actual cause.
