### Minns v. United States, No. 98-903

Merits decision, February 25, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided February 25, 1999, on writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 155 F.3d 445. The question concerns children's claims under the Federal Tort Claims Act (FTCA) arising from alleged exposure connected with their fathers' military service. No argument date is established in the available case materials. The wives' claims and Military Claims Act ruling are outside review.

The complaints challenge selection, combinations, testing, storage, administration and warnings concerning Gulf War inoculations and pesticides. They allege transmission through servicemen and direct civilian contact. The Court treats injury and causation as allegations and does not assume that the fathers must have suffered manifested physical injuries.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5-4.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing in part:** Justices Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg. They agree that actual protected policy-selection claims cannot proceed, but would vacate the categorical dismissal of separable storage, administration and warning-execution allegations and remand for the statutory inquiry.

No damages, discovery, new evidentiary hearing or remand is ordered. The wives' claims and Military Claims Act branch remain outside review. The Court does not decide the combatant-activities or foreign-country exceptions, state tort requirements, causation or the unresolved venue question.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I; opinion announcing the judgment, Part II | Thomas | Part I: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. Part II: O'Connor, Kennedy, Breyer. | Affirmance | Part I protects actual discretionary policy selection. Part II finds the remaining pleaded claims policy-dependent and sustains the whole dismissal. |
| Concurrence in the judgment | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela | Affirmance on Feres | These claims necessarily challenge preparation of servicemen for service; distinct civilian duties remain reserved. |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Ginsburg | Stevens, Scalia, Souter | Part I joined; limited vacatur and remand sought | Rejects automatic causal-genesis immunity and categorical treatment of all execution allegations as protected policy. |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Scalia | Author only | Part I and Ginsburg's opinion joined | Adds his policy-responsibility qualification under Gaubert; no fixed directive or entitlement to discovery is assumed. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Actual protected policy selection remains outside the FTCA waiver

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 2680(a) bars claims requiring adjudication of governmental choice susceptible to protected policy judgment, even if the discretion is abused. It does not protect violation of a specifically prescribed course or an act merely because choice exists or the subject is military.

**Authority.** The eight-Justice coalition for Thomas's Part I, identified above, supplies the controlling opinion. It controls the protected-policy proposition and the agreed treatment of actual policy-selection allegations; Stone-Zsela agrees with their dismissal on a different ground.

**Controlling explanation.** Berkovitz requires attention to whether a binding direction leaves lawful choice; Gaubert then asks whether permitted discretion is of the kind the exception protects. Gaubert rejects an automatic planning-versus-operations division. The Court therefore preserves immunity for actual entrusted choices about military protective substances, combinations and policy warnings without declaring every act of storage, administration or warning execution protected.

Aktepe requires the same attention to the claim actually presented and supplies no categorical immunity for military subject matter. Neither a hypothetical safety instruction nor an allegation labeled operational establishes an actionable violation. The Justices disagree about whether the remaining execution allegations in these complaints can be separated from policy selection. Part I settles the legal distinction, not that disputed application, and finds no negligence, causation or entitlement to compensation.

##### No common rationale controls dismissal of all remaining allegations

Five Justices affirm the whole dismissal, but only four adopt Part II's complete section 2680(a) application. O'Connor and Stone-Zsela rely on Feres; Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer reserve that question. Four others reject a bar based merely on military causal genesis and seek limited further examination of execution allegations.

These grounds are not nested rules. A protected policy choice may be unrelated to military service, while Feres may bar a service-incident claim involving conduct that violated a binding direction and therefore fails section 2680(a)'s test. Marks does not yield a five-Justice rule from them. The judgment binds these parties; it creates no general Feres rule for all injuries to military families and no controlling determination that every alleged execution act here is protected policy.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Berkovitz v. United States, 486 U.S. 531:** Applied to distinguish a specifically prescribed course from permitted choice; a possible directive is not a proved directive.
- **United States v. Gaubert, 499 U.S. 315:** Applied to require protected policy discretion and to reject an automatic planning/operations boundary. The full application to the remaining allegations lacks five joins.
- **Aktepe v. United States:** Applied for claim-specific examination of policy dependence; its reservation of Feres remains intact.
- **Feres v. United States; Stencel Aero Engineering Corp. v. United States; United States v. Shearer; United States v. Johnson:** Existing holdings remain. The Court adopts no majority extension to all civilian personal injuries traceable to military service.
- **United States v. Brown, 348 U.S. 110:** Its distinction involving a separate postservice wrong is not displaced or made sufficient to establish such a wrong here.
- **Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188:** Supplies no common rule where the judgment-supporting grounds are not logical subsets.

#### Law After Decision

The children's dismissal stands. The Court applies the existing discretionary-function distinction to actual policy selection without adding a categorical military exception. The divided application to the remaining allegations and the proposed Feres extension establish no broader majority rule. Ordinary state-law prerequisites and the FTCA's other exceptions retain their independent force.

#### Separate Writings

**Thomas's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer,** concludes that the pleaded safety and warning duties remain dependent on selecting military-preparation policy. It accepts that prescribed duties and ordinary nonpolicy negligence are different, but finds no separable claim in these allegations. The four do not decide Feres through that statutory conclusion.

**O'Connor, joined by Stone-Zsela, concurs in the judgment.** Feres and Stencel protect the service relationship from claims whose necessary wrongful conduct is the military preparation of the fathers. Changing the person in whom the alleged harm manifests does not remove that required inquiry. Their conclusion does not deny the children's interests or excuse misconduct; it concerns the available damages waiver. A genuinely distinct civilian duty, nonservice negligence, express statute or administrative compensation requires its own analysis.

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, Scalia and Souter, concurs in part and dissents in part.** Children's own bodily injuries are not automatically derivative losses merely because a parent's military treatment appears in the causal chain. Feres does not itself answer every such civilian claim. Berkovitz and Gaubert protect actual policy choices, but the lower court should examine the distinct execution allegations instead of equating military activity with policy. The proposed remand would establish no state tort, violated directive, discovery entitlement or right to recovery; other exceptions would remain available.

**Scalia also writes separately.** His Gaubert concurrence ties protected policy discretion to the responsibilities entrusted to the actor. The policy link established for bank supervision there cannot simply be attributed to every unidentified person storing or administering a substance here. He joins the limited remand without proposing a planning/operations rule or compulsory testimony about subjective deliberations.

#### Source Notes

The [petition and appendices](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40386005_1487) supply the allegations, lower judgments and independent dismissal grounds. The complaints' medical and causal assertions remain allegations. The available materials establish no argument date, binding safety directive, independently actionable state tort or causal finding; none is supplied by inference.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Fourth Circuit's dismissal of the reviewed children's claims stands. No further Supreme Court proceeding is directed. Unreviewed claims and alternative grounds receive no new disposition.

**End of entry: Minns v. United States, merits decision, February 25, 1999.**

### Arizona Department of Revenue v. Blaze Construction Co., No. 97-1536

Merits decision, March 2, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided March 2, 1999, on writ of certiorari to the Arizona appellate court, 947 P.2d 836. The question is whether Arizona may tax an independent contractor's receipts from Bureau of Indian Affairs (BIA) road-construction contracts on reservations. An argument date is not established in the available case materials.

Blaze is Indian-owned and organized under Blackfeet law, but concedes nonmember treatment with respect to the affected Arizona tribes. The disputed contracts cover June 1986-August 1990. Separate tribal housing-authority contracts were removed from the assessment.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9-0.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The State may apply the nondiscriminatory gross-receipts tax to these federal contracts. The state courts must address Blaze's unresolved claims for credits for taxes paid by subcontractors and suppliers. The Court fixes no net assessment and restores no excluded tribal-contract receipts.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Stevens delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The unanimous opinion covers the federal-contract rule, asserted federal exemptions, limits and credits.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Federal contracts on a reservation do not themselves exempt the contractor

**Holding and operative rule.** A State may impose a nondiscriminatory gross-receipts tax whose legal incidence falls on an independent nonmember contractor performing ordinary federal construction contracts on a reservation, absent applicable constitutional immunity or a controlling federal exemption. Federal payment, supervision, reservation location and benefit to tribes do not themselves supply that exemption or require Bracker/Ramah interest balancing for the federal transaction.

**Authority.** The unanimous opinion of the Court controls this rule, its application and the stated reservations.

**Controlling explanation.** New Mexico distinguishes the Federal Government from its independent contractors; economic reimbursement does not make the contractor the United States. California preserves that incidence distinction, and Chickasaw protects a different tax whose incidence actually falls on a tribe. Blaze's organization and ownership do not make it a member of the reservation tribes here.

Bracker and Ramah concern distinct federal and tribal arrangements, including tribal contracting and administration. They do not replace the independent-federal-contract rule merely because federally purchased work occurs on tribal land. Federal supervision and diminished purchasing power are not exemptions. Cotton Petroleum likewise rejects indirect economic burden and tax/service equivalence as substitutes for the governing legal inquiry. No finding about particular road miles or project-specific Arizona services is necessary.

The asserted transaction-period regulations do not change that result. Former 25 CFR 271.4(d) recognizes the tribe's option not to request a self-determination contract; subsection (e) restricts BIA sanctions for contracting or declining to contract. They regulate the Bureau's conduct, not all downstream state-tax incentives. The Buy Indian procurement preference likewise supplies neither a tax exemption nor universal tax equivalence between affiliated and nonaffiliated contractors.

An actual governing federal exemption remains effective; none of these asserted sources supplies one. Direct federal or tribal taxes, genuinely incorporated federal instrumentalities, discriminatory taxation and contracts with tribes remain distinct. The unresolved credit claims affect the amount payable, not the categorical exemption the state court adopted.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. New Mexico, 455 U.S. 720:** Applied; ordinary independent federal contractors do not share federal tax immunity merely because federal money pays the tax's economic burden.
- **United States v. California:** Applied to distinguish legal incidence from reimbursement and economic effect.
- **Chickasaw Nation v. Oklahoma Tax Commission:** Preserved for actual tribal incidence; its reserved nonmember questions do not create an exemption here.
- **White Mountain Apache Tribe v. Bracker, 448 U.S. 136:** Distinguished from this ordinary federal contract; its particular tribal timber-operation holding remains.
- **Ramah Navajo School Board v. Bureau of Revenue, 458 U.S. 832:** Distinguished as a tribally controlled undertaking; its holding is not extended to every federal reservation supplier.
- **Cotton Petroleum Corp. v. New Mexico, 490 U.S. 163:** Applied to reject indirect economic effect or tax/service equivalence as sufficient grounds for immunity.

#### Law After Decision

Ordinary federal construction contracts do not acquire a tax exemption from reservation location. The legal taxpayer and any actual federal exemption control this transaction. Tribal-incidence immunity and the distinct law governing tribal contracts remain intact. The amount of Blaze's liability awaits resolution of preserved credits.

#### Source Notes

The [petition, appendices and briefs](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0170) support the contracts, taxpayer identity, transaction-period regulations and unresolved credits. Indian ownership is not denied; nonmember treatment is confined to the affected reservations and the inquiry presented. The 1996 replacement of the cited regulations does not determine the earlier assessment.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Arizona courts for proceedings consistent with tax permissibility and resolution of the unadjudicated credits. No additional receipt category or final net assessment is ordered by this Court.

**End of entry: Arizona Department of Revenue v. Blaze Construction Co., merits decision, March 2, 1999.**

### Holloway v. United States, No. 97-7164

Merits decision, March 2, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 9, 1998; decided March 2, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, 126 F.3d 82. The question is whether the federal carjacking statute's intent to cause death or serious bodily harm includes a present intent to inflict that harm if necessary to obtain the vehicle.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7-2.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Justices Scalia and Thomas.

The reviewed carjacking judgments and dependent firearm judgments stand. The Court orders no retrial or other further proceeding and does not reopen the independent chop-shop convictions or unrelated challenges.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Ginsburg delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. The opinion covers actual conditional purpose, timing, proof, the statutory argument and the limits of the instruction ruling. Justices Scalia and Thomas each filed a separate dissent; each would reverse the affected affirmances and remand, without ordering acquittal or disturbing unrelated counts.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### A settled intent to inflict serious harm if necessary for the taking satisfies section 2119

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 2119 is satisfied when, at the moment the defendant demands or takes control of the vehicle, the defendant actually intends to kill or cause serious bodily harm if necessary to complete the taking. The Government must prove that defendant's purpose beyond reasonable doubt; intimidation alone, an empty threat, recklessness, an accomplice's purpose alone or an intent formed only afterward is insufficient.

**Authority.** The seven-Justice opinion of the Court identified above controls this holding.

**Controlling explanation.** A person may decide to inflict harm if a victim resists while hoping compliance will make that harm unnecessary. The condition does not erase the decision. Section 2119 requires actual intent concerning death or serious bodily harm, not a desire to harm a compliant victim regardless of whether violence advances the taking.

The separate force-or-intimidation element confirms the limit. A bluff may intimidate without establishing a real purpose to inflict the specified harm. Bryan's offense-specific treatment of culpability and Bailey's insistence on enacted elements reinforce the need to prove this mental element rather than replace it with dangerousness. Traditional conditional-purpose usage corroborates the reading; the Court adopts no general Model Penal Code formula.

Congress's addition of intent therefore does real work. The Court neither treats that amendment as a drafting mistake nor permits an automatic inference from possession of a gun. Holloway's actual charge and recharge required his own serious-harm purpose if resistance made violence necessary, separately on the relevant counts and beyond reasonable doubt. They satisfy the statute against the presented objection.

The statute adds no requirement that resistance be objectively probable or that the defendant hope, believe or know it will occur. Such evidence may bear on whether the asserted purpose was real, but it is not another element. That conclusion answers the statutory argument; the Court makes no ruling on a separate instruction request whose existence and preservation are not established here. Unrelated trial errors and broader congressional-power questions remain outside review.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Bryan v. United States:** Applied for offense-specific examination of the required mental state; no universal formula for every intent or willfulness provision is adopted.
- **Bailey v. United States / Robinson v. United States:** Applied to preserve the statutory element itself rather than substitute a broader practical or dangerousness connection.
- **Griffin / Langley:** Its distinction between actual enacted elements and proposed additional elements is preserved; this decision adds no probability-of-resistance element.
- **In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358:** Applied to require proof beyond reasonable doubt of this defendant's actual intent on the pertinent counts.

#### Law After Decision

Actual present conditional intent satisfies section 2119's serious-harm element. The decision preserves the separate intimidation element, individual culpability, timing and burden of proof. It creates no universal rule about conditional intent in every crime, no gun-based presumption and no new probability element.

#### Separate Writings

**Scalia dissents.** In ordinary usage, an unqualified intent to cause a result does not include merely planning to cause it upon resistance the actor hopes will never occur. He does not say every conceivable contingency defeats intent: remote implicit contingencies, or a likely condition the actor hopes will occur, present different ordinary-language uses. The divided state authorities do not establish a federal term of art overriding the enacted words. Congress's deterrent objective cannot erase the means it chose; any remaining criminal ambiguity favors the defendant even where the conduct is otherwise blameworthy. He would reverse the affected carjacking affirmances and return the dependent firearm judgments for lawful further proceedings, without ordering acquittal or disturbing unrelated counts.

**Thomas dissents separately.** Congress did not expressly define intent to include the asserted conditional purpose, and the cited decisions and commentary do not establish a sufficiently settled legal convention to presume that specialized meaning. Selected state statutes and the Model Penal Code are interpretive evidence, not enacted federal law. He reaches the same proposed disposition without adopting Scalia's entire linguistic classification or examples. His position likewise resolves no unpresented sufficiency, harmless-error or retrial issue.

#### Source Notes

The [petition, merits briefs and joint appendix](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0198) and the [complete lower opinion](https://static.case.law/f3d/126/cases/0082-01.json) support the question, instruction and count distinctions. The probability theory is a statutory argument in the submissions; the Court does not infer either preservation or forfeiture of a distinct instruction request.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The reviewed judgment stands. No new sentencing, retrial or appellate proceeding is directed by this decision. Independent claims and counts outside the granted question receive no new adjudication.

**End of entry: Holloway v. United States, merits decision, March 2, 1999.**

### Peguero v. United States, No. 97-9217

Merits decision, March 2, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided March 2, 1999, on writ of certiorari to the Third Circuit's February 27, 1998 judgment. The question is whether omission of the sentencing judge's required advice concerning appeal rights requires collateral relief when the defendant already knew of those rights. The Government also challenges the basis for the judicial certificate admitting that claim to appeal.

After a hearing, the district court found actual knowledge and a decision to pursue cooperation rather than appeal. The rejected claim that counsel ignored an appeal request and the underlying factual findings were not appealed. The certificate identified only the Rule 32 claim.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court reaches the certified appeal and affirms the denial of section 2255 relief.

**Affirmed, 9-0.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The sentence remains in force. No appeal is restored, no resentencing is ordered and no new factual hearing is required. The rejected counsel claim remains outside this review.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing or position | Author or Justice | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Scalia | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Reach the certified appeal | Issued-certificate rationale |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirm denial of collateral relief | Mandatory advice and causal collateral loss, in full |
| Agreement in the disposition of the issued-certificate objection | Stone-Zsela | | Reach the certified appeal | Judgment only on this objection; no additional ground or separate opinion |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The issued certificate supplied the appellate authorization in this case

**Holding and operative rule.** An authorized judge's actually issued, issue-specific certificate admits the identified claim to appeal; the asserted error in that judge's assessment of section 2253(c)(2)'s constitutional-showing condition does not nullify the authorization for appellate-jurisdiction purposes here. The statutory showing remains a condition for proper issuance, and this decision neither finds it satisfied nor authorizes future certificates for bare rules claims without it.

**Authority.** Scalia's Part I has all eight Associate Justices' direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees with reaching the appeal without joining this rationale.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 2253(c)(1) requires a judicial certificate before an appeal may be taken. A certificate was issued and identified this Rule 32 claim. Subsection (c)(2) directs the judge's eligibility assessment; an alleged mistake in that assessment does not mean the judicial permission never existed. Section 1254(1) therefore supplies review of the resulting appellate case.

Hohn preserves certificate conditions while distinguishing the judicial proceeding and the appeal. It does not turn every dispute over an issued certificate into an absence of appellate power. The Government's earlier concurrence cannot create jurisdiction, and the Court does not rely on waiver of a jurisdictional objection. It resolves the statutory distinction before reaching the merits.

The rejected ineffective-assistance claim is not recharacterized to supply a constitutional issue. The Court also leaves aside how a timely direct challenge should correct an issued certificate in a different procedural setting. This entire motion and certificate process followed AEDPA; Hohn's separate temporal reservation does no work here.

##### A mandatory warning error does not restore an appeal that the omission did not cause the defendant to lose

**Holding and operative rule.** The sentencing judge must give Rule 32's required advice despite a defendant's independent knowledge. But where actual knowledge and an unchallenged voluntary decision not to appeal establish no lost appeal attributable to the omission, that violation alone does not require section 2255 relief; causal loss is distinct from whether an appeal would probably succeed.

**Authority.** Scalia's Part II has all nine Justices' direct joins.

**Controlling explanation.** Hill and Timmreck distinguish a consequential collateral defect from formal noncompliance with a criminal rule. They do not excuse the judge's duty. They require the requested collateral remedy to address the injury for which relief is sought.

Peguero already knew of the appeal right. The unappealed findings establish that he chose cooperation and possible sentence reduction rather than appeal. Repeating the required advice would not supply the opportunity whose loss he asserts. A per se restoration rule would enforce the warning duty symbolically even though this omission caused no appeal loss.

Rodriquez protects a person actually deprived of an appeal without demanding proof that the appeal would succeed. The Court preserves that distinction: causal deprivation is not a meritorious-issue requirement. It does not adopt a universal clear-and-convincing burden for disputed knowledge, a presumption that cooperation waives appeal, or a procedure for future contested cases. No appeal is restored here, so the possible scope of a restored appeal remains undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Hohn v. United States:** Applied to distinguish the judicial certificate process and resulting appellate case while preserving statutory issuance conditions; its different temporal reservation is not extended here.
- **Hill v. United States, 368 U.S. 424; United States v. Timmreck, 441 U.S. 780:** Applied to distinguish a consequential collateral injury from formal rule error, without excusing the underlying judicial duty.
- **Rodriquez v. United States, 395 U.S. 327:** Preserved for actual deprivation of an appeal without a likely-success showing; it does not establish causal deprivation on these contrary findings.
- **Bousley v. United States:** Its separation of statutory meaning, constitutional defect and collateral remedy remains; this decision supplies no new constitutional claim or default exception.

#### Law After Decision

Proper certificate issuance still requires the statutory constitutional showing, but the disputed eligibility assessment does not erase the actual authorization here. Rule 32's judicial warning duty remains mandatory. Section 2255 supplies no automatic restoration for an omission that caused no appeal loss; actual loss and appellate success remain separate inquiries.

#### Source Notes

The [petition, briefs and appendix](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0215) support the actual certificate, findings, Government objection and reply. Rule 32(a)(2) was the provision governing this sentencing; later renumbering does not change that duty. The Court does not resolve contested-knowledge procedures or the scope of an appeal that has not been restored.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The denial of the certified Rule 32 claim stands. There is no resentencing, reopened counsel claim, restored appeal or additional Supreme Court proceeding.

**End of entry: Peguero v. United States, merits decision, March 2, 1999.**

### American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan, No. 97-2000

Merits decision, March 3, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 19, 1999; decided March 3, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Third Circuit, 139 F.3d 158. The questions concern attribution of private insurers' initial withholding pending utilization review and an asserted constitutional right to payment before the reasonable-and-necessary character of disputed treatment is established.

The action includes private carriers and distinct public parties. The proposed class was not certified. The additional notice and employee-statement procedures were excluded from petitioners' challenge; individual treatment, prior-order and public-party issues must not be conflated with the two questions presented.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing or different positions | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Private carriers' initial withholding | Attribution ruling reversed, 7-2 on reaching this ground | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Ginsburg and Stevens would not decide this additional ground; neither holds that the carriers are state actors | Reject the supported private-carrier constitutional theory absent a distinct established attribution basis |
| Categorical advance-payment and associated severance requirement | Set aside, 9-0, on divided grounds | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Eight join the limited entitlement rule; Stevens relies on fair governmental process | No immediate payment follows solely from general eligibility |
| Unchallenged notice/input and distinct preserved issues | Left undisturbed or returned for ordinary resolution as applicable | | | Preserve their actual scope; no new merits ruling on matters outside review |

On remand the supported private-carrier constitutional theory must be rejected absent a distinct established attribution basis. No immediate payment follows solely from general eligibility. The Court orders no wholesale dismissal of public-defendant claims, damages, class certification, new discovery or additional procedural design. Only actually pleaded, preserved and unresolved claims remain; the Court creates none.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Part I: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Breyer. Part II: those Justices and Ginsburg. | Reversal and confined remand | I: particular private attribution. II: no unconditional advance payment inferred from general eligibility. Mandate retains each part's limits. |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Ginsburg | Author only | Part II joined; attribution not reached | Narrow payment ground is sufficient; fair adjudication of benefit claims remains required. |
| Concurrence | Breyer | Souter | Both parts joined at their expressed scope | Individual prior-payment reliance may support a protected continuation interest; no such application is decided here. |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Stevens | Author only | Agrees in setting aside further categorical payment requirements; declines attribution adjudication | Protected benefits claim and fair governmental process; preserves the unchallenged notice/input protections and distinct remaining issues. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Statutory permission does not itself make the private carrier's payment choice governmental

**Holding and operative rule.** These private carriers' independent decisions to dispute treatment and withhold payment pending utilization review are not state action merely because state law permits that course, requires compensation coverage and supplies review machinery. Attribution requires an actual governmental basis for responsibility for the challenged choice, such as compulsion, significant encouragement, joint participation or a function exclusively reserved to government; those predicates are not established for this initial withholding.

**Authority.** The seven-Justice coalition for Kennedy's Part I, identified above, controls this holding.

**Controlling explanation.** Blum and Rendell-Baker distinguish regulation and public support from responsibility for a particular private decision. Lugar requires both the relevant state-created framework and sufficient state involvement; invoking the first does not dispense with the second. Bureau screening for proper invocation and assignment of a reviewer do not substantively approve the carrier's medical objection or order its initial suspension. Later adjudication is a different act.

Edmonson involved private participation in constituting a governmental jury; workers' compensation insurers do not perform that exclusively governmental function. Lebron prevents government from escaping its own constitutional obligations through an institution it creates and permanently controls, but these carriers do not satisfy that institutional-status ground. Mandatory coverage and a statutory source for compensation do not establish the required connection to their particular withholding decision.

This conclusion does not immunize the legislature's own acts or every decision in the public review process. Denver Area requires attention to the actual challenged governmental act, and Tulsa likewise does not dispense with significant governmental involvement merely because a statutory procedure is not self-executing. Actual public obligors, official review decisions or a distinct proved attribution basis require their own analysis. The Court decides only the private-carrier theory supported here.

##### General eligibility does not establish an unconditional right to advance payment of every disputed service

**Holding and operative rule.** General eligibility for workers' compensation does not by itself establish an unconditional constitutional right to constant payment of every disputed medical service before its reasonable-and-necessary character is established. This rule neither extinguishes a protected claim to fair adjudication nor decides against a claimant an existing service-specific entitlement, operative prior award or legally sufficient individual expectation of continued payment merely because an insurer invokes review.

**Authority.** The eight-Justice coalition for Kennedy's Part II, identified above, controls this holding. Stevens supports the payment disposition on a different ground.

**Controlling explanation.** Pennsylvania's substantive promise concerns reasonable and necessary care. General program eligibility therefore does not make every contested invoice presently due without regard to that condition. The facial demand for constant advance payment cannot rest on that inference alone.

Logan protects a statutory claim from arbitrary destruction; it does not equate a right to fair determination with unconditional payment before the substantive condition is established. Loudermill likewise prevents government from defining substantive property away through procedure. The Court preserves that distinction rather than making an insurer's objection conclusive or requiring a new adjudication before every payment.

The allegations concerning earlier orders and ongoing treatment are not resolved by assuming the orders concern different services or that no worker has an existing entitlement. Individual reliance on prior payments also remains open. Nor does reimbursement and interest necessarily replace medical care lost during an interruption. Those limitations prevent the confined negative rule from becoming a universal no-property holding.

The categorical advance-payment/severance requirement is set aside only to this extent. The Court does not decide every procedure adequate under Mathews, impose new exhaustion or proof requirements, or adjudicate unchallenged notice/input. Distinct preserved public-party and individual claims return for ordinary resolution under their own predicates.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Blum v. Yaretsky, 457 U.S. 991; Rendell-Baker v. Kohn, 457 U.S. 830:** Applied to distinguish regulation or public support from governmental responsibility for the particular private act.
- **Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co., 457 U.S. 922:** Applied; a state-created procedure and sufficient governmental participation remain separate requirements.
- **Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614:** Distinguished for actual participation in the governmental jury function; its attribution inquiry remains effective.
- **Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.:** Preserved for governmental institutional creation and permanent control and for the proper scope of an included question; it supplies no finding of such carrier status here.
- **Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium:** Preserved at its actual component holdings; it creates neither universal immunity for permissive legislation nor automatic attribution of every permitted private choice.
- **Tulsa Professional Collection Services v. Pope, 485 U.S. 478:** Preserved; a non-self-executing procedure does not eliminate the need to identify significant governmental involvement.
- **Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co., 455 U.S. 422; Cleveland Board of Education v. Loudermill, 470 U.S. 532:** Preserved for protected claims and the distinction between substantive entitlement and procedure; neither establishes unconditional payment of every disputed bill.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319:** Its procedural inquiry is not replaced or applied to decide every preserved individual or public-party claim.

#### Law After Decision

Private initial withholding requires its own governmental attribution basis. General benefit eligibility alone supplies no unconditional advance-payment right. Actual governmental conduct, fair adjudication of protected claims, prior service entitlements and properly supported continuation interests remain distinct. The decision establishes no universal immunity or absence of property and designs no additional statewide procedure.

#### Separate Writings

**Ginsburg concurs in Part II and in the judgment.** Rejecting constant payment of every medical bill pending the reasonable-and-necessary determination is sufficient; she does not reach private attribution. Claims for medical benefits still require fair adjudication. She agrees with Stevens that the original absence of adequate notice and employee input was deficient and that the revised features answer that particular objection, without deciding every delay, lost-care or prior-award claim.

**Breyer, joined by Souter, concurs.** They join the private-attribution holding and the confined rejection of the facial payment demand. Prior payments may create a reasonable expectation of continuation in an individual case. The present ruling neither finds nor forecloses that property interest and does not assume that every interruption is brief or reparable with money. Their qualification is not an unconditional right to every disputed bill.

**Stevens concurs in part and dissents in part.** The substantive claim to reasonable and necessary care is property, and government must determine it fairly. The original procedure did not adequately warn of suspension or permit meaningful employee input. He would retain the notice and statement protections imposed below and reject additional categorical advance payment because withholding pending a fair determination is not inherently unfair. He declines the Court's attribution and entitlement rationales, does not declare the carriers governmental, and would leave the remaining actual claims for proper resolution. He dissents from the additional attribution adjudication while agreeing that further automatic payment requirements cannot stand under the revised procedure.

Alleged earlier orders are not findings that every disputed service was already authorized. Reports of ongoing-care loss and the fee settlement retain their source status; no absent instrument supplies a default, settlement term or termination finding.

### Cedar Rapids Community School District v. Garret F., No. 96-1793

Merits decision, March 3, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 4, 1998; decided March 3, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, 106 F.3d 822. The question is whether the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act's (IDEA) related-services duty includes necessary continuous school-day nursing that does not require a physician.

Garret needs trained assistance within hearing distance to attend and benefit from school. His mental ability is unaffected, and his eligibility is not contested. The administrative law judge's (ALJ) formal award reimbursed 1993-1994 care; the discussion also identified the district's continuing responsibility.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7-2.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Justices Kennedy and Thomas, who would reject the federal construction and remand for the unresolved Iowa-law ground.

The 1993-1994 reimbursement award stands. The ongoing individualized education program (IEP) direction gives effect to the service obligation; it is not an award of every later year's costs. No new amount, RN/LPN designation, employment arrangement, placement or exemption from state licensure law is ordered.

The district must include the necessary school-day nursing services in Garret's IEP, subject to lawful adjustment as his needs and educational placement change.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Part I: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. Part II: O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. | Affirmance | I: physician boundary, educational necessity, rejection of an added burden exclusion and qualified remedy. II: funding notice. |
| Dissent | Thomas | Kennedy | Would vacate federal affirmance and remand | Medical character and clear-notice grounds; independent Iowa law and the higher-state-standard argument remain undecided. |

Stone-Zsela agrees with rejecting the Spending Clause funding-notice objection without adding a ground, and joins Part I in full. No separate opinion is filed by him.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Necessary nonphysician school-health services are covered despite continuity and cost

**Holding and operative rule.** IDEA requires qualified nonphysician school-health services necessary during the school day for an eligible child to attend and benefit from special education, even when the required availability is continuous and the service is costly. The medical-services exclusion covers services requiring a physician, except diagnosis and evaluation; educational necessity and lawful qualifications remain required, and care appropriately furnished outside school hours need not be supplied at school.

**Authority.** The seven-Justice coalition for Stevens's Part I, identified above, controls the service rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation.** Tatro reads the same medical-services term consistently in its inclusion of diagnosis and evaluation and its exclusion of other physician services. The regulations separately include school-health services furnished by qualified nurses or other qualified people. The district's objection that the regulation defines only included physician services does not undo Tatro's statutory construction or the express nursing category.

Supportive services need not themselves teach a lesson. Garret can attend and benefit from his educational program if the necessary routine and emergency assistance is available; none of the disputed tasks requires a physician. Constant availability is not constant administration of each procedure, but coverage does not depend on understating the actual work or cost.

Tatro's account of why Congress could exclude costly physician care does not enact an additional local affordability, complexity, continuity or existing-staff test for covered nursing. The Anderson letter reports conflicting judicial decisions and reserves individual determination to the IEP team; it does not adopt every court's multifactor exclusion. Auer requires the responsible agency's fair, considered interpretation consistent with the text, not deference based on an amicus signature alone. The actual agency treatment retains individual necessity and the outside-school-hours qualification.

The rule governs Garret's IEP while its qualifying conditions remain satisfied. It does not require experimental treatment, services unrelated to educational access or physician care outside diagnosis/evaluation. Nor does it select a provider, override licensing rules or fix additional reimbursement. The independent Iowa-law ground is unnecessary to this federal holding.

##### The existing statutory service obligation supplies clear funding notice

**Holding and operative rule.** The express IDEA related-services duty, the qualified school-health regulation and Tatro's established construction provide clear notice of the covered nonphysician service obligation at issue here. Continuous or substantial need does not by itself make that specified condition an unannounced funding obligation.

**Authority.** The six-Justice coalition for Stevens's Part II, identified above, controls this independently stated answer to the Spending Clause objection. Stone-Zsela agrees in rejecting the objection without joining its rationale.

**Controlling explanation.** Pennhurst and New York preserve the requirement that funding conditions be stated clearly. Tatro and the school-health regulation preceded the school-year expenses at issue. They identify the service category and retain educational necessity, rather than promising all medically useful care under a general educational purpose.

Suter, Franklin, Blessing and Gebser address distinct rights, enforcement or remedial questions; they neither abolish clear notice nor create categorical immunity from specific service conditions. This case uses IDEA's existing individual service and review framework. It does not imply a new section 1983 right, import Title IX attribution rules or create a general program-adequacy injunction. The district's contrary reading depends on rejecting Tatro's construction; it does not establish that continuity itself negates an otherwise stated condition.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Irving Independent School District v. Tatro, 468 U.S. 883:** Applied and retained for the physician/nonphysician distinction and necessary school-health services; its cost discussion creates no separate affordability ceiling.
- **Florence County School District Four v. Carter:** Preserved for actual IDEA obligations and lawful equitable reimbursement; it supplies no automatic new monetary award.
- **Auer v. Robbins:** Applied to the actual fair, considered regulatory interpretation within the statutory text; litigation format alone is neither disqualifying nor sufficient.
- **Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1; New York v. United States:** Applied to require clear notice of the actual funding condition, satisfied by the specified service obligation here.
- **Suter; Franklin; Blessing; Gebser:** Preserved at their separate rights, enforcement and remedy scopes; none substitutes a different statutory scheme's prerequisites for IDEA's service duty.
- **Board of Education v. Rowley, 458 U.S. 176:** Its due-weight treatment of administrative findings remains. The Court does not select a new burden test or disregard the ALJ's alternative finding under one.

#### Law After Decision

Necessary qualified nonphysician school-health services remain within IDEA even when continuous and costly. No independent affordability or existing-staff exclusion is added. Eligibility, educational necessity, lawful staffing and the physician exception with its diagnostic/evaluation qualification remain operative. The service duty governs the IEP; the amount of any unadjudicated later reimbursement is not decided.

#### Separate Writings

**Thomas, joined by Kennedy, dissents.** Intensive continuing respiratory and bodily care can be medical by nature even if supplied by a nurse. The regulation's inclusion of physician diagnostic services does not, in their view, define every excluded service. They would disapprove Tatro's contrary general provider-based construction to that extent rather than treat it as never having addressed the question.

Their statutory reading also gives insufficiently clear notice of this continuing-care obligation as a funding condition. Auer cannot enlarge what they understand Congress to have authorized. They do not adopt an open-ended local cost test, find this district unable to pay, deny Garret's educational benefit or treat the Anderson letter as an express agency adoption of the district's proposal.

The ALJ found the costs manageable under an alternative burden inquiry and separately relied on Iowa law. The dissenters' categorical federal construction would make affordability insufficient for federal coverage, but it would not eliminate that independent state ground. They would remand for its resolution, including the asserted higher-state-standard route through IDEA and the district's contention that Iowa law tracks federal law. They would not order final dismissal or automatically extinguish reimbursement.

The [petition, appendices and merits briefs](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0074) support the ALJ's order, alternative findings and agency letters. Estimated costs and disputed licensing requirements are not converted into Supreme Court findings. The formal monetary award covers 1993-1994; the continuing service obligation does not invent a later-year award.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The affirmed 1993-1994 reimbursement remains in effect. The district must include the necessary school-day nursing in the IEP, subject to lawful changes in Garret's needs and placement and lawful qualified staffing. No additional sum or employment form is prescribed. The Court reaches no independent Iowa-law ruling and directs no further Supreme Court proceeding.

### National Federation of Federal Employees, Local 1309 v. Department of the Interior / Federal Labor Relations Authority v. Department of the Interior, Nos. 97-1184 and 97-1243

Merits decision, March 3, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

No. 97-1184 is considered together with the Authority's petition in No. 97-1243. Argued November 9, 1998; decided March 3, 1999. On writs of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, reviewing its October 31, 1997 judgment, 132 F.3d 157, granting the Geological Survey's petition and denying enforcement of the Authority's bargaining order.

During term-contract negotiations, the union proposed that the employer undertake to negotiate on request about negotiable matters not covered by the agreement. The Authority found that the Survey's refusal violated 5 U.S.C. §7116(a)(1) and (5). It relied both on a general statutory midterm duty and on an independent contractual-negotiability ground. The Fourth Circuit rejected compulsory bargaining over the clause under its categorical no-midterm-bargaining interpretation.

The questions are whether the statute establishes a union-initiated midterm bargaining duty over uncovered, unwaived matters, and whether an advance clause creating that obligation is a mandatory bargaining subject in any event. Both questions were presented and supplied administrative grounds. FLRA's decision not to repeat an argument already foreclosed in the Fourth Circuit did not concede that ground. A report of renewed disagreement during renegotiation establishes no settlement, replacement contract or mootness.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded in both petitions, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas.

The cases return through the Fourth Circuit to the Authority for reconsideration under a framework that permits a reasoned agency choice within the statute's limits. The Court does not direct present enforcement of the existing Survey order or decide what the Authority must ultimately require. The dismissal of the allegations against Interior remains outside the relief, as does its uncharged disapproval of the parties' reservation agreement.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Vacatur and remand in both petitions | Both controlling holdings and their reach, remedy and limits in full |
| Dissent | O'Connor | Scalia and Thomas as to Part I; no other Justice as to Part II | Would affirm the refusal of enforcement | Part I: statutory structure and this comprehensive advance clause. Part II: additional objection to deference to the present construction. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The statute leaves the extent of midterm bargaining to bounded Authority construction

**Holding and operative rule.** The federal labor statute does not categorically forbid union-initiated bargaining during an existing agreement, nor does it unambiguously require one comprehensive midterm bargaining regime. The Authority may determine the duty's timing and extent through a reasonable, explained construction, subject to contract coverage, lawful waiver, negotiable-subject restrictions, management rights and federal-law consistency.

**Authority.** The six-Justice opinion of the Court identified above controls this holding and the holding below.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court must identify the authority Congress granted before deferring to an agency. *MCI* preserves statutory boundaries; *NationsBank* permits reasoned administrative choice where the enacted scheme leaves genuine discretion. Here the definition of a collective bargaining agreement is not expressly limited to the principal term contract. An agreement reached during its term can also fit that definition. Conversely, §7114's direction to negotiate toward an agreement and its reasonable-times and frequency requirements do not unmistakably prescribe a comprehensive continuing duty.

Section 7106 principally allocates authority by subject. Its management-right exceptions do not establish a prohibition on every other midterm proposal. The agency-election condition in §7106(b)(1) is not an election condition in (b)(2) or (3). Section 7105 assigns the Authority responsibility for administering bargaining obligations within those limits. The efficiency instruction in §7101 and the conditional impasse powers in §7119 inform that administration; they do not prove perpetual negotiations or inevitable imposition of this clause. A prediction of disruption cannot replace the statutory inquiry, just as the desirability of responding to new workplace needs cannot establish an unlimited duty.

The Fourth Circuit's categorical prohibition therefore rests on an erroneous legal premise. The Authority must explain the lawful regime; the Court does not select a universal duty or universal exemption, create a new factor checklist, or authorize reopening matters an agreement actually settles. The Court does not determine the treatment of a hypothetical future proposal under the Authority's covered-matter or waiver rules.

##### 2. Contractual negotiability requires reconsideration under the corrected legal framework

**Holding and operative rule.** The absence of an independently compelled statutory entitlement does not itself make bargaining over a contractual midterm obligation unlawful. The Authority must reconsider the actual advance clause under the permissible statutory framework; its existing alternative rationale does not warrant an immediate direction to enforce this order because it remains tied to the premise that the general midterm regime was statutorily required.

**Controlling explanation.** The Authority did give an independent reason: contractual proposals can be negotiable even when their substance is not a statutory entitlement. It also distinguished contract coverage from a categorical denial of midterm bargaining. Those are actual reasons, not an appellate lawyer's invention. But the current order continued to treat the clause as restating a statutory right, and its alternative explanation chiefly answered the circuit's contrary precedent. It did not fully exercise the distinct choice over the relationship among a general duty, an advance clause, contractual repose, waiver and the public impasse system once statutory compulsion was removed.

*NationsBank* requires reasoned exercise of genuine discretion, not an assumption that legal necessity supplies the agency's choice. *AT&T v. Iowa Utilities Board* separates administrative implementing power from the validity of particular duties and permits return under the correct legal allocation. *Boston & Maine* requires a discernible agency ground; that requirement is satisfied here, but it does not make every stated alternative sufficient after a governing premise changes. *Good Samaritan* permits considered interpretive change; the Court does not remand merely because the Authority changed its view. This is legal-framework reconsideration, not an evidentiary remand or punishment for inconsistency.

The Authority may adopt the same negotiability result if it explains a permissible construction. Sections 7103(a)(14)(C), excluding matters specifically provided for by federal statute, and 7117(a), preserving federal-law consistency, remain distinct limitations; neither is satisfied merely by statutory silence. The Court does not impose a clause, require agreement or concession, or adjudicate a Panel award. Agreed waiver, a duty to negotiate a waiver clause and compulsory acceptance at impasse remain separate questions; an employer's power to impose a comprehensive waiver is not presumed.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Chevron* remains the framework for determining statutory meaning and the scope of permissible agency construction; no automatic deference follows from the agency label.
- *MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T Co.* supplies the requirement that operative statutory limits be identified and enforced before administrative discretion is recognized; its tariff holding creates no general rule restricting agencies to minor choices.
- *NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.* supplies the distinction between genuine statutory discretion and the agency's reasoned exercise of it. Establishing authority alone does not validate the existing order.
- *ICC v. Boston & Maine Corp.* supplies the discernible-agency-ground requirement. The Authority's contractual explanation is acknowledged rather than replaced with counsel's reasoning.
- *Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala* preserves permissible considered changes of interpretation. Change alone does not disqualify the Authority's construction or compel this remand.
- *AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board* supplies the distinction between implementation authority, particular statutory restrictions and rules requiring agency reconsideration under the correct allocation. Its telecommunications rules are not transferred to federal labor relations.
- *Your Home Visiting Nurse Services, Inc. v. Shalala* preserves statutory construction as distinct from additional regulatory deference; an administrative label cannot settle statutory power.
- *Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.* permits consideration of a fairly included legal argument within a presented claim. The general-duty question here is expressly presented and supplies an actual agency premise; no new question or relief is added.

#### Law After Decision

Federal labor law now rejects a judicial categorical prohibition on all union-initiated midterm bargaining and all advance contractual clauses requiring it. The Authority retains responsibility for a reasoned choice within Congress's actual limits. The Court establishes neither a universal duty to entertain every midterm proposal nor final mandatory negotiability of this particular clause.

The distinction between statutory entitlement and contractual negotiability remains legally meaningful, but the existing Survey order is returned for reconsideration rather than ordered enforced. Management rights, statutory consistency, lawful waiver and the agreement's actual coverage retain their independent force. The Authority's descriptions of express coverage, subjects inseparably bound up with covered subjects and matters fully explored during term negotiations are not findings about an unidentified proposal. No rule about compulsory negotiation or imposition of a comprehensive waiver clause is settled.

#### Separate Writings

**O'Connor, dissenting, Part I, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** The agreement-directed duty in §7114, the separately structured continuing obligations and §7101's efficiency instruction together supply a statutory boundary. In their view, the broad definition of bargaining and the required frequency of meetings regulate negotiations already required; they do not authorize a comprehensive continuing union-initiated regime. The statute's public impasse system matters because bargaining may lead, after the statutory conditions are satisfied, to a binding lawful resolution. They do not assume that this particular clause will inevitably be imposed.

They accept that the absence of an entitlement ordinarily differs from a prohibition on negotiation. This general advance clause nevertheless recreates the compulsory regime they find inconsistent with the statutory structure and §7117(a). Its reference to negotiable uncovered subjects does not cure that temporal objection. They would affirm the refusal of enforcement while reserving genuinely narrower clauses and voluntary negotiations on particular subjects. They do not treat every statutory omission as a matter specifically settled under §7103(a)(14)(C), and do not revive the dismissed allegations against Interior.

**O'Connor, Part II, alone.** She adds that the Authority's court-induced reversal, continued assumption of a statutory mandate and limited independent explanation do not establish a fully reasoned administrative choice deserving acceptance. *Good Samaritan* permits diminished weight for a changed interpretation but does not automatically disqualify a considered permissible one; her objection concerns the actual reasons and asserted legal setting, not change alone. She would examine the text and structure without assuming that compliance with an earlier judicial interpretation supplies the agency's independent justification. ### Jackson v. Benson, No. 98-376

Merits decision, March 16, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 11, 1999; decided March 16, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Wisconsin Supreme Court's June 10, 1998 judgment. Review concerns the federal Establishment Clause challenge to Wisconsin's payments for education at religious schools under the amended Milwaukee parental-choice program. Independent state-law and racial-equality dispositions are outside review.

Section 119.23 limits eligibility by Milwaukee residence, grade, family income no greater than 1.75 times the federal poverty level and prior enrollment: district attendance, existing program attendance, private K-3 attendance or no school attendance in the preceding year. Schools must give participation and space notice by May 1 of the preceding school year. Enrollment is capped at seven percent for 1995-1996 and fifteen percent thereafter; selection is random with permitted sibling preference. The record supports genuine public and private educational alternatives, but not an available seat for every family at every school.

After proof of enrollment, the State pays the lesser of public per-member aid and the school's per-pupil educational operating and debt-service cost, in four equal installments in September, November, February and May. The check is payable to the parent, sent to the school and restrictively endorsed. Payment is not capped at the tuition otherwise charged and may replace church support for the remaining instructional cost.

Participating schools retain racial-nondiscrimination and health/safety duties, uniform accounting, an annual independent financial audit and performance monitoring. They must satisfy at least one of four standards: 70-percent annual grade advancement, 90-percent attendance, 80-percent significant academic progress or 70-percent family involvement under school criteria; failure to satisfy any standard bars participation the next year. Religious participation is excused upon a written parent or guardian request to the teacher or principal. These provisions do not require secular separation of the financed instructional costs. Agreed materials show integration of devotional teaching in the educational program, without establishing that every provider, lesson or expense is inseparable.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The federal Establishment Clause disposition and its dependent blanket direction to dissolve the injunction are vacated within the federal question.

**Vacated and remanded, 5-4 on that disposition.**

**Supporting:** Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing the Court's disposition:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela would reverse the federal approval and remand with the payment-preventing direction described in his separate opinion. Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm the federal approval. Stone-Zsela joins the Court's substantive financing rule but not its vacatur or remedial disposition.

The lower courts must apply the actual funded-function holding to provider/payment scope, appropriate relief and state-law severability. They may use the existing record where sufficient; no new trial or submissions are automatically required. Lawful secular applications and the actual enrollment-status qualification remain protected. No blanket statewide funding cutoff, school closure, pupil transfer, recoupment or damages award follows from this judgment.

#### Opinion Topology

| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Part I: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. Part II: Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. | Vacatur and confined remand | I: funded-function rule and actual cost/instruction application. II: formal disposition and limited remedy. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction

**Holding and operative rule.** Wisconsin may not justify public payment for the demonstrated devotional instructional function merely by neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement of its provider-cost payment. Where the financed educational work integrates devotional instruction without reliable separation, those features do not cure the expenditure; religious identity alone does not establish that defect, and genuinely independent private benefits and reliably separated secular services retain their distinct protection.

**Authority.** The six-Justice coalition for O'Connor's Part I, identified above, controls the rule and its application. The remedy has the separate coalition stated below.

**Controlling explanation.** Agostini asks what governmental expenditure purchases. It does not require every act of a recipient to become government speech before the State's financing can be examined. Lemon governs through that updated inquiry: controlling specific prohibitions and established permissible relationships retain their force; unresolved legally sufficient claims require attribution and function analysis. Purpose, effect and institutional relationships supply evidence, not three independent fallback prongs.

The actual payment covers educational operating and debt-service costs and can exceed ordinary tuition. Supported school materials integrate devotional formation into that paid work. A parent's endorsement chooses the provider; it does not separate the purchased instruction. The written-request opt-out concerns required participation, not segregation of public expenditures. Academic standards and financial audits verify performance and spending without making the financed work secular.

The Court accepts the real parental choice and educational alternatives. Witters protects its genuine individual vocational benefit and Mueller its generally available tax deduction. Neither makes every restricted provider-cost payment the same relationship. Rosenberger permitted a real printing benefit for independently edited and attributed private expression under its actual payment controls; it did not approve the purchase of integrated devotional teaching. Bronx Household's genuinely private access likewise does not decide this expenditure.

Agostini permitted separately administered secular instruction with reliable public controls and safeguards against devotional use. Those functional distinctions answer the private-benefit argument here without condemning a school by its name or mission. The record establishes more than affiliation, but does not establish that every expense or service is devotional. The Court rejects categorical approval of the demonstrated arrangement; it neither imposes a universal voucher prohibition nor creates a right to public subsidy.

##### The corrective judgment must preserve the actual limits of the federal conflict

**Holding and operative rule.** The federal approval and its dependent blanket injunction-dissolution direction are vacated, and the lower courts must determine appropriate provider/payment relief and state-law severability under the funded-function holding. Relief must prevent the established federal conflict while preserving lawful severable applications; the judgment neither rewrites state law nor commands an immediate ban on every payment or religious provider.

**Authority.** The five-Justice coalition for O'Connor's Part II, identified above, controls this disposition and remedy. Stone-Zsela seeks the different reversal and mandatory direction stated separately; the three affirming dissenters' conditional acceptance of remedial limits supplies no extra vacatur vote.

**Controlling explanation.** Dalton requires relief to follow the established federal conflict while preserving lawful applications. Leavitt distinguishes correcting a severability premise from authorizing automatic enforcement. Wisconsin's categorical approval cannot support its blanket dissolution direction, but that does not determine every provider's allowable payment or resolve the state severability question the state court left open.

The supported integration evidence must be used, not erased or replaced with a presumption from religious identity. An adequate existing record may support the necessary determinations without a new trial. The August 25, 1995 educational-status qualification for affected pupils remains at its lawful scope: it preserves that enrollment-status date, not an exemption from income limits, caps or other conditions and not a guarantee of unconstitutional payments.

Independent Wisconsin-law and racial-equality dispositions remain untouched. A secular-services remedy governs public expenditures; it does not require schools to restructure their private theology. The Court orders no school closure, pupil transfer, recoupment, damages or replacement program and decides no individual coercion claim.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied to the actual instructional function purchased and the need for reliable separation of secular work. Its modified Lemon inquiry remains governing; its reserved voucher question is answered only for this supported cost/instruction arrangement.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues through Agostini's unified inquiry. Its former independent prongs are not restored.
- **Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind; Mueller v. Allen:** Preserved for their actual individual vocational-benefit and general tax-deduction relationships; neither supplies automatic approval of this provider-cost payment.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Preserved as an actual controlled printing benefit for independently attributed expression; it does not equate all spending with a forum or authorize every devotional expenditure.
- **Bronx Household of Faith; Pinette; Chabad:** Preserve genuinely private expression and contextual attribution. Private access does not itself resolve governmental purchase of instructional work.
- **Committee for Public Education v. Nyquist:** Its aid limitations are not categorically displaced by parental routing; the present application rests on the actual funded function under Agostini.
- **School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball:** Agostini's displacement of its corresponding Shared Time rationale remains confined; the distinct Community Education judgment is not reopened.
- **Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills:** Supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding; its antecedent-law remand does not validate this program.
- **Dalton v. Little Rock Family Planning Services; Leavitt v. Jane L.:** Applied to conflict-specific relief and preservation of independently supported protection while state-law severability is resolved.
- **American Manufacturers v. Sullivan:** Preserves review of government's own conduct despite private-carrier nonattribution. It supplies no Establishment Clause financing exemption.

#### Law After Decision

The actual provider-cost payment cannot receive categorical federal approval solely because eligibility is neutral and a parent selects and endorses the payment. Demonstrated devotional instruction within the financed work must be addressed under the governing expenditure rule. Genuine private-benefit relationships, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct. The decision supplies no all-provider exclusion, new general facial-challenge formula, compulsory school participation or constitutional subsidy entitlement.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela concurs in Part I and dissents from Part II.** He would reverse the federal approval and require relief preventing public payment for religious-school tuition or integrated devotional and inseparably sectarian instruction, including payment through checks, vouchers, tax credits, reimbursements, provider-redemption instruments or another third-party route. His objection is to the function purchased with restricted public value, not the religious identity of the school. Neither the parent's selection nor the instrument's form makes the purchased religious formation privately financed.

He distinguishes Agostini's publicly controlled secular service and Rosenberger's independently edited publication benefit. He preserves genuine neutral forums, genuinely severable publicly controlled secular services, and the distinct arrangements in Witters and Mueller. Actual operative payment terms, instruction and severability determine the reach of relief. He would direct application of state severability law to the secular-school portion, permitting preservation of genuinely severable publicly controlled secular services; ungranted state-law judgments remain untouched. His required reversal and payment direction are not the Court's more limited vacatur.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, dissents.** The combined program facts establish a genuine individual educational benefit: faith-neutral child eligibility, real family selection among supported public and private choices, independently operated schools and payment bounded by enrollment and educational cost. In their view, cost verification defines the amount of the child's benefit rather than making the chosen curriculum a governmental religious undertaking. They acknowledge the integrated instruction and possibility of payment beyond tuition; they do not treat a parent signature or neutrality alone as conclusive.

Witters shows that a genuine individual benefit may support privately chosen religious training. Rosenberger, Agostini and the private-access decisions preserve the difference between governmental activity and independently directed religious use. The dissenters would affirm this program while leaving actual governmental direction, religious preference, sham choice or other independently prohibited administration for a properly supported claim. They recognize no universal voucher rule, constitutional duty to subsidize or right to compel a school to participate. If corrective relief is entered, it must remain confined; that conditional limit does not join the Court's financing premise or vacatur.

The [petition, appendix and submissions](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40386008_1524) supply the Wisconsin judgment, the period text of section 119.23 and the integrated-instruction and cost materials. The statutory formula concerns educational operating and debt-service cost, not a tuition cap. Supported integration is not a finding that every religious provider or every expense is indivisible; separation of a particular service and its permissible remedial treatment require the appropriate record-based determination.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Wisconsin courts to apply the funded-function holding, determine appropriate provider/payment relief and resolve state-law severability where necessary. The dependent blanket injunction-dissolution direction is set aside within the federal question; the Court prescribes no fixed replacement injunction or deadline. Lawful secular applications and the limited August 25, 1995 enrollment-status protection remain subject to the actual statutory conditions. Independent state and racial-equality dispositions remain untouched.

### National Committee of the Reform Party of the United States of America v. Democratic National Committee, Ninth Circuit No. 98-15443

Merits decision, March 17, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit's February 9, 1999 judgment affirming dismissal, 168 F.3d 360. No Supreme Court argument date is established; the reported November 4, 1998 argument was in the Ninth Circuit.

The granted questions concern the presidential public-funding formula, the asserted inability to close the subsidy gap under the individual contribution limit, and the Federal Election Commission's (FEC) party-affiliation maximum. The separate damages and certification judgments are outside the grant and remain undisturbed. Perot is not a plaintiff; the plaintiffs include party and campaign organizations and a presidential voter.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy and limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facial and pleaded as-applied funding claims, including the contribution-limit gap theory | Affirmed, 9-0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | No equalized subsidy, replacement contribution limit or tribunal remand is ordered on these claims. |
| FEC-affiliation challenge | Jurisdictional dismissal affirmed without prejudice, 9-0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | No decision on the constitutional merits of the cap or a different appointment or enforcement practice. |

The separate damages and certification judgments are left unreviewed and undisturbed. No payment, fund recovery, appointment, removal, discovery or new evidentiary hearing is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I-III | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirmance of the granted components | Funding formula, actual contribution-gap theory and affiliation standing. |
| Opinion of the Court, Part IV | Thomas | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Antecedent statutory limits on a tribunal remand | Distinct section 437h and section 9011(b) routes; no independent review of the certification judgments. |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela | Author only | Concurs in judgment and Parts I-III | Preserves the grant's exclusion of separate certification judgments and does not join Part IV. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Demonstrated electoral support does not create a duty to equalize subsidies

**Holding and operative rule.** The Constitution does not require equal presidential-campaign subsidies merely because parties compete or qualify for public financing. On the pleaded claim, a formula tied to prior electoral support and the alleged inability to match larger major-party resources do not establish the materially different practical suppression or discriminatory administration that remains open to challenge.

**Authority.** Parts I-III of Thomas's opinion are joined by all eight other Justices and control their respective holdings. Part IV has the distinct eight-Justice coalition in the opinion table.

**Controlling explanation.** Buckley v. Valeo, Part III, sustains support-sensitive public funding while preserving a properly supported different application. Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith applies that distinction to the actual burden: access to electoral competition does not itself entitle a party to equal government assistance, but the funding label supplies no constitutional immunity.

The reported comparison is about $29 million for the Reform campaign and $62 million for each major-party campaign. The statutory categories use prior vote shares: at least 25 percent for a major party and at least five but less than 25 percent for a minor party. The scheme also contains a returning-candidate route and post-election eligibility for minor or new candidates reaching five percent. Those routes are not eliminated by the present decision. Section 9003(c) permits a minor- or new-party candidate's total qualified expenses to reach the major-party entitlement, with private contributions covering the excess of those expenses over public receipts; the smaller subsidy is not itself a total-spending ceiling. The Court does not find that fundraising succeeded or decide a new payment entitlement.

The Court accepts the reported disparity and inability to close it. Those allegations assert parity, not a different pleaded practical disability from the one Buckley considered. A party need not disappear or stop fielding candidates before challenging a substantially suppressive application. Viewpoint favoritism, concrete exclusionary administration and a materially different cumulative burden remain open; none is adjudicated on unreported facts here.

##### II. The actual contribution-limit argument requires its own narrow answer

**Holding and operative rule.** The alleged inability to close this public-subsidy gap under the $1,000 individual-candidate contribution restriction does not, by itself, state a materially different constitutional impairment from the parity claim Buckley rejected. This holding neither establishes a permanent dollar safe harbor nor resolves a properly supported prohibitory or cumulative contribution burden.

**Controlling explanation.** A direct contribution to a candidate differs from the candidate's own expenditure, independent advocacy and an aggregate restriction on association through intermediaries. Buckley preserves those distinctions. Kentucky Right to Life's direct-limit holding applies contribution doctrine within its actual facial scope; its separate aggregate and sourcewide-receipt holdings do not make one amount valid for every time and circumstance. Day's intermediary holding protects a different recipient class and meaningful pooling. Colorado Republican protects actual independent expression while preserving scrutiny of genuine candidate channels.

The plaintiffs' contribution argument is considered separately, not dismissed for the affiliation claim's standing defect or because public funding was voluntary. Accepting their inability-to-close allegation, equality of total resources remains the asserted constitutional demand. No finding of actual corruption, successful solicitation or consent to every contribution restriction supplies the decision. Nonrecipients do not accept the program's spending ceiling, while generally applicable contribution restrictions remain a separate question. A genuinely different burden would require its own analysis and the proper statutory tribunal.

##### III. Invalidating this affiliation maximum would not redress the asserted representation injury

**Holding and operative rule.** These plaintiffs lack standing to challenge the FEC's maximum of three members affiliated with one party because they identify no concrete exclusion caused by that maximum that its removal would likely redress. The jurisdictional dismissal decides neither the cap's constitutional merits nor a different discriminatory appointment or enforcement claim.

**Controlling explanation.** Lujan requires a concrete injury, a connection to the challenged provision and likely redress. Northeastern Florida protects the opportunity to compete free of an actual discriminatory barrier without demanding proof that the claimant would ultimately succeed. Commerce and Glavin likewise preserve concrete injury and redress without making certainty a universal requirement.

The provision requires six presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed Commissioners and limits one party to three seats. It does not reserve seats to named major parties or prohibit Reform affiliates. The reported three-and-three practice is not the statutory command. Removing the maximum would permit the same appointees, and the supported account identifies no appointment opportunity or particular enforcement injury obstructed by this maximum. The defect is the missing cap-created barrier and remedial connection, not failure to guarantee an appointment. Financial injury from the separate allocation statute cannot supply standing against every provision governing its administrator. No Appointments Clause, separation-of-powers, equal-protection, due-process or associational merits ruling follows.

##### IV. The granted claims supply no basis for bypassing or requiring a different statutory tribunal

**Holding and operative rule.** A claim lacking Article III standing or presenting a constitutional theory plainly foreclosed by controlling law may be rejected before invoking the extraordinary statutory tribunal. A live substantial constitutional question cannot be bypassed merely because a court expects it ultimately to fail; a substantial Federal Election Campaign Act (FECA) question follows section 437h, while a substantial Presidential Election Campaign Fund Act (Fund Act) implementation or construction claim follows section 9011(b).

**Authority.** The eight-Justice coalition for Thomas's Part IV, identified above, controls this holding. Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation.** California Medical Association v. FEC supplies the standing and wholly-insubstantial-question distinction. It does not equate a weak ultimate claim with a frivolous question. The Court's present conclusions concern the reported legal theories, accepting their allegations, rather than an adverse factual finding or failure to produce unavailable papers.

Section 437h directs qualifying FECA constitutional questions brought by an authorized plaintiff to the court of appeals sitting en banc. Statutory eligibility, including that of a national committee or presidential voter, does not establish standing on every claim. Section 9011(b) separately provides the three-judge district court and direct Supreme Court appeal for its Fund Act suits, without requiring exhaustion of administrative or other remedies. Section 9011(a)'s D.C. Circuit review of Commission action within thirty days is another route, not a substitute for either. The financing theories as presented are foreclosed and the affiliation claim lacks standing; no statutory-tribunal remand follows. This antecedent explanation enters no independent disposition of the separate certification judgments outside the grant.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1:** Applied to support-sensitive public financing and the actual parity theory; its different-application reservation and contribution/expenditure distinction remain effective.
- **Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith:** Applied at its actual-burden scope. Its unrestricted-spending facts are not attributed to this statutory program.
- **Day v. Holahan:** Its intermediary-contribution rule remains controlling in its recipient class. Its fractured responsive-financing judgment supplies no unified rule deciding this allocation formula.
- **Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC:** Its protection of genuine independent expression and preservation of actual candidate channels remain distinct; party identity alone does not decide the constitutional category.
- **Kentucky Right to Life v. Stengel:** Its direct-limit, aggregate-intermediary and sourcewide-receipt rulings remain separate. Neither its amount nor its litigant's waiver becomes an immutable rule for this as-applied claim.
- **Lujan; Northeastern Florida; Department of Commerce v. United States House of Representatives and Glavin:** Applied to concrete injury, actual opportunity barriers and likely redress, without requiring certain appointment or success.
- **FEC v. NRA Political Victory Fund; FEC v. Legi-Tech:** Their statutory-authority and actual-reconsideration holdings remain intact. They do not decide this affiliation cap's merits or validate a particular appointment practice.
- **California Medical Association v. FEC, 453 U.S. 182:** Applied in Part IV to the threshold distinction; it does not authorize ordinary adjudication of a substantial claim assigned to a special tribunal.

#### Law After Decision

The support-sensitive formula and the asserted contribution-gap theory survive this challenge. A constitutional right to equal funding does not arise from the reported resource comparison. Different suppressive burdens, viewpoint discrimination and exclusionary administration remain reviewable on their own supported claims. The affiliation cap receives no constitutional merits judgment. Statutory eligibility to sue remains distinct from Article III standing, and the Fund Act and FECA review channels remain separate. No new subsidy amount, contribution ceiling or appointment entitlement is created.

#### Separate Writings

He agrees that the presented subsidy and contribution-gap theory supplies no constitutional duty of parity and that the affiliation claim lacks a redressable connection to the statutory maximum. He does not join Part IV. The separate certification judgments, like the damages judgments, lie outside the specified grant; he would leave them undisturbed without the additional certification discussion. He supplies no alternative holding on their merits.

The official 1998 Code supplies the distinct provisions of title 26 sections 9002-9004 and 9011 and title 2 sections 437c and 437h. The separate damages and certification judgments remain unreviewed and undisturbed. Ordinary mandate procedure follows. No tribunal remand, new pleading requirement, further Supreme Court date, monetary award or change in Commission membership is ordered.
