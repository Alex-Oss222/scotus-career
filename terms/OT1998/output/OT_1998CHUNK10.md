## Decisions and Dispositions

### West v. Gibson, No. 98-238

Merits decision, June 14, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 26, 1999; decided June 14, 1999. On certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 137 F.3d 992. The question is whether the EEOC may award the compensatory damages Congress authorized for federal-sector Title VII discrimination, and what follows for the appellate judgment reinstating Gibson's damages claim on the premise that the Commission lacks that power.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, who would affirm the reviewed compensatory-damages component.

The Seventh Circuit must consider whether Gibson properly raised his remaining arguments and, if so, resolve them under the correct rule of EEOC authority. The Court does not reinstate the district court's nonexhaustion dismissal, award damages or decide actual exhaustion or estoppel. Completed promotion/backpay enforcement, rejected frontpay and transfer claims, and the retained fee matter are outside the reviewed component.

#### Opinions and Joins

Souter delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. That opinion controls both holdings below. Kennedy dissented, joined by Scalia and Thomas, and would affirm for lack of unequivocal authority for administrative compensatory awards.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The EEOC may administer the compensation Congress expressly authorized

When federal-sector Title VII intentional discrimination falls within 42 U.S.C. §1981a's damages authorization, §2000e-16(b) permits the EEOC to award that compensation as an appropriate remedy. The agency must preserve §1981a's substantive conditions and monetary limits; its power does not depend on an administrative jury, and a proper court action retains the statutory jury election.

Section 717(b) delegates enforcement through appropriate remedies and introduces the listed equitable forms as examples. Congress subsequently added compensation to the remedies for intentional discrimination under §717. Reading those enactments together gives effect to both the express monetary authorization and the administrative mechanism Congress retained. Section 1981a's reference to an action does not silently confine §717 as a whole to its judicial subsection. Its jury clause regulates trial in court; it neither requires a jury in an administrative proceeding nor creates a governmental appeal from an adverse EEOC award. The Government's inability to demand judicial review therefore does not negate the separately authorized administrative remedy. This conclusion satisfies Lane's requirement of unequivocal enacted consent and Blue Fox's demand for a genuine statutory entitlement; administrative convenience supplies no independent waiver.

The compensation category applies to unlawful intentional discrimination, not disparate impact, and requires that the complaining party cannot recover under §1981. It excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by §706(g). The statutory caps remain applicable, and punitive damages are unavailable against governments, government agencies and political subdivisions. When available §102 damages are sought in court, any party may demand a jury; the court must not tell that jury the statutory caps. No damages under another statute or free-standing appropriations authority follow.

##### Statutory authority does not decide Gibson's preserved procedural alternatives

The Seventh Circuit's no-power premise cannot sustain its judgment, but identifying an available administrative remedy does not establish whether Gibson exhausted it or has a properly preserved alternative answer to the exhaustion defense. The reviewed judgment must be vacated and remanded for consideration of proper presentation and, if appropriate, resolution of those alternatives.

McCarthy requires attention to the governing statutory scheme and the actual administrative remedy; its result involving a compensation-incapable prison process cannot resolve a claim involving an EEOC that may compensate. Gibson's asserted cash-award request, completed complaint procedures and inadequate counseling do not become judicial findings merely because the Court rejects the appellate statutory premise. The Court decides neither preservation nor actual exhaustion, estoppel, compensable loss or amount. It creates no categorical exception to an applicable exhaustion requirement. No new damages award or trial is ordered by this decision itself.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lane v. Peña: applies the requirement of unequivocal remedy-specific consent and distinguishes its academy-program scheme from the express federal-employment compensation authorized here; Lane remains controlling.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products: preserves the separate compensation category, its limits and the dependent jury election. Gibson's 1992 conduct does not implicate the preenactment exclusion.
- Department of the Army v. Blue Fox: applies its demand for an actual statutory entitlement; the coordinated Title VII enactments supply authority that an equitable label alone could not create.
- McCarthy v. Madigan: preserves statutory primacy and the inquiry into the actual administrative scheme; its damages-only BOP result supplies no general Title VII exhaustion exception.

#### Law After Decision

Federal-sector Title VII compensation expressly authorized by §1981a may be administered by the EEOC under §717(b). The statutory damages conditions and court-stage jury rights remain distinct from the agency's remedial authority. Whether an employee exhausted the process, preserved an alternative ground or proved a compensable injury remains a separate inquiry.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, dissents. Consent to a federal monetary remedy must encompass the forum and conditions in which liability is imposed. In their reading, §717(b)'s earlier equitable examples do not clearly authorize the later damages remedy, and §1981a's action language and any-party jury election attach conditions the agency cannot satisfy. The Government cannot obtain judicial review of an adverse EEOC award and so cannot secure the jury that the statute allows any party to demand. The definition of an eligible complaining party does not itself enlarge the Commission's power. The dissent would affirm the reviewed judgment on that statutory ground without denying judicial Title VII damages or determining Gibson's actual exhaustion, injury or amount.

#### Record Limits

The governing provisions are 42 U.S.C. §§2000e-16(b)–(d), 2000e-5(g) and 1981a(a)–(d). The competing exhaustion positions establish neither counselor misconduct nor actual exhaustion or a compensatory amount.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The compensatory-damages component returns to the Seventh Circuit for proceedings consistent with the Court's authority holding and the reserved questions concerning proper presentation and alternative grounds. No further fact or recovery is established by the mandate.

**End of entry: West v. Gibson, Merits decision, June 14, 1999.**

### Schleifer v. City of Charlottesville, No. 98-1146

Merits decision, June 15, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Certiorari granted March 22, 1999; argued April 28; decided June 15. Review of the Fourth Circuit's final merits judgment, 159 F.3d 843, affirming denial of declaratory and injunctive relief against the juvenile curfew. The questions concern minors' nighttime public liberty, fit-parent authority, and notice, enforcement discretion and protection of expression. The Fourth Amendment claims were withdrawn; no criminal conviction or damages award is before the Court.

The ordinance covers unemancipated persons under seventeen during 12:01–5 a.m. Monday through Friday and 1–5 a.m. Saturday and Sunday. It prohibits lingering or staying, or failing to leave on an authorized request, in public places, covered vehicles and profit-operated establishments inviting the public; a prior dispersal order is not invariably required. Parent includes a biological or adoptive parent with legal custody, including shared custody, or with whom the minor regularly resides; a judicially appointed guardian; and a person at least eighteen standing in loco parentis, as shown by authorization from such a parent or guardian or other circumstances. Judicial emancipation means an order under Virginia Code §16.1-333.

Its exceptions protect parental accompaniment; unforeseen emergencies requiring immediate protection of life, limb or property; employment and direct travel to work or home without stop or detour; the sidewalk directly abutting the residence where the child lives with a parent, without requiring parental accompaniment there; adult-supervised school, religious, civic, public or similar sponsored activities and direct travel without stop or detour; parent-directed errands supported by the specified signed writing; interstate travel through, beginning or ending in the City; and constitutionally protected First Amendment activity, including religion, speech and assembly. The errand writing must identify parent and minor, give the parent's address and telephone and a reachable telephone, bear the parent's signature, describe the errand and destinations, and specify the permitted hours. General parental permission alone is not an exception.

Parents and vehicle owners/operators face distinct knowing-permission offenses. Establishment operators/employees have a separate knowing-permission offense and a defense for prompt police notification that a minor refuses to leave. That defense does not govern every subsection, and adult knowledge elements do not become an element of the minor's presence offense. False identifying information is independently prohibited. Violations are Class 4 misdemeanors.

Officers must investigate immediately before enforcement. A minor's first violation produces verbal and mailed written warnings; a previous warning triggers charge and summons. As soon as practicable, the officer must release the minor to a parent or use temporary care bounded by the remaining curfew hours. Care is nonlocked, nonrestrictive and visually supervised, without handcuffing or securing the minor to objects. If the minor refuses to give the officer the minor’s name and address or the parents’ name and address, no parent can be located before curfew ends, or a located parent does not appear to accept custody, the ordinance permits nonsecure crisis/shelter or juvenile-court procedures under governing law. The adult enforcement provision separately uses first warnings and later charge/summons. The ordinance's one-year reporting direction is not a sunset or proof of completed effectiveness research.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or qualification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overall judgment denying relief | Affirmed, 5–4 | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg | No new injunction, damages award or remedial remand |
| Facial vagueness and expression challenge | Rejected, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Stone-Zsela | Stone-Zsela would reverse on facial vagueness |

On substantive liberty and parental authority, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer sustain the judgment on assumed significant interests and case-specific protective fit; Scalia and Thomas sustain it under ordinary review. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would reverse in part and grant fitted relief for supported parent-authorized lawful activity. Stone-Zsela does not reach those substantive questions.

#### Opinions and Joins

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Part II: Kennedy and Breyer. | Part I rejects facial vagueness/expression; Part II sustains the substantive judgment | Part I has eight votes; Part II has three and supplies no Court-wide scrutiny rule |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Scalia | Thomas | Supports affirmance | Both join Part I; separate substantive grounds from Part II |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stevens | Souter and Ginsburg | Would reverse in part on substantive claims | All join Part I |
| Dissent | Stone-Zsela | Unjoined | Would reverse on facial vagueness | Substantive questions reserved |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The presented facial vagueness challenge fails

A juvenile curfew that specifies age, hours, covered places and the prohibited act of remaining is not facially vague on this record merely because its exception for constitutionally protected First Amendment activity requires legal application in particular encounters. The exception preserves the full constitutional protection; uncertain hypotheticals do not establish that this ordinance's common criminal predicate lacks an intelligible standard, and genuinely distinct unconstitutional applications remain open.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, has eight direct votes for the rule, explanation and limits.

Morales requires meaningful guidance for the predicate authorizing dispersal and holds that a warning cannot repair its absence. This ordinance supplies age, time, place and conduct criteria absent from Chicago's apparent-purpose rule. Peoples Rights Organization distinguishes difficult application of an existing criterion from the omission of a central criterion. Grayned and Hoffman Estates require attention to the actual law and its intelligible operation; Kolender's independent demand for enforcement guidance remains controlling. The expression exception refers to constitutional law, not an officer's personal approval of a speaker's purpose. Religion, speech and assembly illustrate its coverage, and protected conversation or music does not lose protection because it occurs in a commercial venue.

The chief's uncertain answers about political discussion and musical performance deserve attention, but they are not findings of arrests or binding interpretations that exclude protected activity. His assurance of restraint likewise is no authoritative cure. The Court relies on the enacted criteria and preserved constitutional rights, not that assurance or a claim that warnings remove all restraint. The first-warning route can still require release or temporary care. A proven unconstitutional expression restriction remains subject to challenge; the facial claim here does not establish a missing standard common to every application. The Court neither deletes the speech exception nor rewrites the ordinance to narrow it.

##### The substantive judgment rests on separate grounds

Five Justices sustain the judgment rejecting the presented substantive liberty and parental-authority claims. No five join a common full scrutiny rationale: three assume significant interests and find the actual protective fit sufficient, while two apply ordinary review because the asserted entitlement has not been established.

**Authority:** The judgment has the five votes identified above. O'Connor's Part II has only the three votes of O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer; Scalia's concurrence has Scalia and Thomas. Neither becomes a Court-wide scrutiny rule through aggregation. The assumed-interest approach does not decide the nonfundamentality proposition, and a restraint failing its fit inquiry could still survive ordinary review. Neither rationale is shown to be the governing logical subset of the other under Marks. The Court therefore enters no general standard for every juvenile curfew or fit-parent objection.

#### Precedent Treatment

- City of Chicago v. Morales: preserves the requirement of meaningful guidance for the common enforcement predicate and the insufficiency of warnings to cure its absence; distinguishes this ordinance's enacted age, time, place and conduct criteria.
- Peoples Rights Organization v. City of Columbus: applies its distinction between an absent legal criterion and difficulty applying an existing criterion.
- Grayned v. City of Rockford: applies its contextual fair-notice and enforcement-guidance principles.
- Kolender v. Lawson: preserves independent legal constraints on enforcement discretion; assurances of restraint do not replace them.
- Village of Hoffman Estates v. Flipside: applies contextual facial-review and intelligible-core principles without immunizing a genuinely standardless predicate.
- Papachristou v. City of Jacksonville: preserves protection against discretionary criminalization of ordinary public activity; it does not dispense with analysis of this law's defined operation.
- Reno v. ACLU: preserves actual constitutional expression and the boundary against judicial rewriting; its adult-network-speech ruling supplies no automatic scrutiny tier for every juvenile presence law.
- Washington v. Glucksberg: remains claim-specific and supplies no exclusive history-only method governing all liberties; the separate substantive writings here adopt no common extension of its rule.

#### Law After Decision

The ordinance survives the presented challenges and the denial of relief remains effective. Its constitutional-expression exception retains its full reach, and materially different applications remain open. The controlling facial rule distinguishes a missing enforcement criterion from difficult application of a defined law. The divided substantive grounds establish no universal curfew scrutiny formula, unrestricted nighttime entitlement, parental veto or rule validating every prosecution. The family-contact and custody-transition decisions retain their separate settings; no curfew hearing or proof burden is derived from them.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

O'Connor's Part II, joined by Kennedy and Breyer, assumes significant interests in ordinary public activity and parental direction. On this record, the narrow age band, late hours, operative avenues for parental accompaniment, supervised activity, errands, work, emergencies, travel and expression, together with local protective evidence, sufficiently support the restraint. They acknowledge that permission alone is not an exception and that even a first encounter can cause removal or temporary care. Prince's protective authority does not erase minors' rights; the actual interests and regulation must be examined together. Their approach follows the assumed-interest method of O'Connor's Glucksberg concurrence and Breyer's precise-interest analysis. They neither endorse the lower court's unnecessary strict-scrutiny alternative nor declare every family burden subject only to rational review.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurs in Part I and the judgment. The precedents do not establish the asserted constitutional entitlement to override this bounded protective regulation. Glucksberg's claim-specific treatment and the objective operation recognized in their Morales position support ordinary review. The actual age, hours, exceptions and protective grounds rationally serve child safety and public order. They do not join Part II's assumed-interest formulation, impose a heightened framework, treat every warning as a cure, import the adults' knowledge elements into the minor's offense or deny the restraint involved in temporary care. Actual protected expression remains protected.

Stevens, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, concurs in Part I and dissents from the substantive disposition. Defined boundaries answer the presented facial claim, but do not settle whether the resulting restraint is justified. The ordinance still reaches an identified class of otherwise lawful, parent-authorized activity outside its exceptions, including unaccompanied ordinary activity that the limited errand provision does not authorize. Their Glucksberg analyses and Morales liberty explanation require justification responsive to both the child's liberty and the parent's direction. General juvenile crime and vulnerability evidence does not sufficiently justify that displacement on this record. Souter's commensurate-justification approach supports the same bounded application without a universal scrutiny formula. They would remand for declaratory and injunctive relief fitted to established plaintiffs and supported applications, leaving ordinary remedial and state-law severability analysis below. They recognize neither an absolute parental veto nor a right to remain anywhere at night and would not automatically invalidate independent false-information liability.

Stone-Zsela dissents. Applying fair-notice and enforcement-guidance principles, he regards the undefined First Amendment exception as part of the criminal boundary itself. Minors, parents and officers must decide the constitutional status of speech, association, assembly and related movement during an encounter; uncertain classifications of political conversation and performance reveal the practical defect. A warning postpones prosecution but does not cure the initial stop, removal, recorded violation, parental exposure or repeat-offense consequences. Morales's warning principle and the existing anti-discretion rule therefore require reversal. He would order declaratory and prospective relief unless a binding state-law construction legally available on remand, consistent with ordinary saving-construction rules, supplies objective and sufficiently precise boundaries. He reserves the full substantive liberty and parental-authority questions, permits a narrower curfew with clear exceptions and enforcement standards, and decides no post-judgment application or damages claim. His position does not invalidate clear exceptions for work, direct travel, emergencies, parental accompaniment or specified expressive events.

#### Record Limits

The ordinance appears in the appendix to the Fourth Circuit's October 20, 1998 opinion, No. 97-1723, pp. 20–26, and at 159 F.3d 843, 855–58. Credited crime evidence does not establish the ordinance's causal effectiveness. No controlling state narrowing construction or severability ruling is established.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Fourth Circuit's final judgment remains in force. No remand, new injunction, damages determination, replacement ordinance or additional hearing is ordered. A materially different constitutional application must be assessed on its own facts and law; no such claim is adjudicated here.

**End of entry: Schleifer v. City of Charlottesville, Merits decision, June 15, 1999.**

### Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo, S.A. v. Alliance Bond Fund, Inc., No. 98-231

Merits decision, June 17, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 31, 1999; decided June 17, 1999. Certiorari to the Second Circuit, 143 F.3d 688, reviewing approval of a preliminary injunction. The questions are whether a live controversy survives the later permanent conversion and whether general equity permits an unsecured creditor seeking money damages to freeze assets in which it claims no proprietary interest.

The district court entered the preliminary restraint December 23, 1997, with a $50,000 bond after findings of near-certain recovery and threatened noncollection. On April 17, 1998, it entered an $82,444,259 money judgment, ordered turnover and converted the restraint to a permanent injunction. The turnover appeal is separate; conversion was no longer challenged. This review concerns the preliminary period.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or qualification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Live controversy over preliminary-period authority | Controversy remains live, unanimous | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | | No bond liability or damages determined |
| Preliminary-injunction judgment | Reversed and remanded, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, who would affirm | The preliminary restraint may not continue or be reinstated on the rejected general-creditor theory |

The preliminary order ceased upon conversion on April 17, 1998. The Court does not dissolve the distinct permanent injunction, disturb the final money judgment or decide the separate turnover appeal. Any actual bond claim remains subject to proof of wrongful restraint, causation and loss and to available defenses; no damages are awarded. Attachment, fraudulent-transfer, receivership, bankruptcy and other remedies remain available only through their independently established legal sources and predicates.

#### Opinions and Joins

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Unanimous live-controversy ruling | Preliminary-period authority controversy; no bond-liability determination |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas | Reversal and bounded remand | General-equity power limit |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Ginsburg | Stevens, Souter and Breyer | Would affirm the preliminary injunction on general-equity preservation grounds | Joins Part I; dissents from Part II |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The preliminary-period authority controversy remains live

When a preliminary restraint is challenged as beyond the court's legal power during the period before judgment, later success on the underlying contract and conversion to a permanent injunction do not necessarily extinguish that distinct controversy. Here the asserted preliminary-period injury and existing bond preserve a consequential authority question, while actual bond liability and damages remain undecided.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part I is joined by all nine Justices and controls at this limited level of generality.

Camenisch distinguishes a surviving case from a surviving preliminary-injunction issue. The Court applies that distinction rather than treating the mere presence of a bond as conclusive. The investors' success on their contract may justify relief after judgment without establishing authority to restrain an unsecured debtor beforehand. GMD's acceptance of the permanent conversion therefore does not resolve whether the earlier restraint was legally authorized. A ruling on that distinct question can matter to the asserted loss during the preliminary period. This is not review of the final money judgment or an adjudication of the separate turnover appeal. Whether a proper bond claim ultimately proves wrongful restraint, causal loss and an amount, and whether independent defenses defeat it, remains for the appropriate proceedings. No universal rule preserves every appeal involving an expired injunction and a bond.

##### General equity does not supply this unsecured-creditor asset freeze

A federal court may not use general equitable authority, at the request of a general unsecured creditor suing solely for money damages, to freeze a defendant's unencumbered assets before judgment when the creditor has no lien, equitable interest, statutory authorization or other recognized source of prejudgment control. Likely success and threatened inability to collect do not supply the missing power.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices adopt the complete rule, explanation and remedy.

The historically conferred equity jurisdiction permits flexibility within its sources and objects. It does not authorize creation of a security interest for an unadjudicated general debt. The judgment and proprietary prerequisites protect the debtor-creditor relationship; merger of law and equity did not erase them. Rule 65 regulates injunction procedure, and Rule 64 preserves authorized prejudgment security routes. Neither makes the inadequacy of collection itself a new source of power. The bondholders sought to secure payment from assets that were not the subject of their contract claim. The strong findings favoring them therefore address the usefulness of a restraint, not its legal availability.

Deckert involved equitable rescission and preservation in that setting. First National City Bank involved federal tax collection and statutory authority. Their preservation reasoning does not create the omitted private-creditor predicate. De Beers confirms that preliminary relief must remain within recognized equitable authority related to the available final remedy. Expanding general equity here would supply security and affect other creditors outside enacted attachment and bankruptcy arrangements. The Court does not restrict injunctions protecting specific property in dispute, statutory freezes, independently authorized fraudulent-transfer remedies, bankruptcy orders, Rule 64 attachment or postjudgment enforcement. Nor does it decide bond recovery, create a lien, establish fraud or adjudicate the rights of absent Mexican creditors.

#### Precedent Treatment

- University of Texas v. Camenisch: applies the distinction between a surviving case and the precise preliminary issue; the distinct legal-power consequence here survives without a present bond-liability adjudication.
- De Beers Consolidated Mines v. United States: applies its limit connecting preliminary relief to recognized authority and the permissible final remedy; it supports rejection of this unsecured-creditor freeze.
- Deckert v. Independence Shares Corp.: distinguishes its equitable rescission setting; its preservation authority remains intact within that setting.
- United States v. First National City Bank: distinguishes its federal-tax and statutory predicates; personal jurisdiction and preservation language do not alone establish private unsecured-creditor power.
- Mertens v. Hewitt Associates: preserves its statutory equitable-category rule; it does not itself govern every private injunction or prohibit all equitable monetary remedies.
- Varity and Peacock: preserve their ERISA rules concerning appropriate individual equity and an independently violated obligation; neither supplies this private-creditor power.
- Department of the Army v. Blue Fox: preserves the distinction between an equitable label and an actual legal entitlement within its sovereign/APA setting.
- West v. Gibson: leaves intact the EEOC's specifically enacted compensation authority; it creates no general private-creditor injunction power.

#### Law After Decision

General federal equity supplies no prejudgment security freeze on the stated unsecured, purely monetary claim without an independent recognized source of control. Equity's flexibility, legally authorized attachment and statutory or proprietary remedies retain their respective scopes. Postjudgment enforcement remains distinct. A surviving preliminary-period authority issue may remain reviewable despite later final relief, but neither a bond nor success on the contract automatically determines bond liability.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer, concurs in the live-controversy ruling and dissents from reversal. General equity's traditional preservation function, in their view, permits this provisional restraint given near-certain contractual recovery and the concrete threat that asset disposition and insolvency would defeat a judgment. They accept that the particular freeze was not historically granted but regard equitable function as adaptable without creating ownership or a lien. Deckert and First National City Bank support preservation by analogy despite their different predicates; De Beers does not foreclose relief directed to making the eventual money judgment effective. They would affirm this order without adopting the Second Circuit's entire preliminary-injunction test, allowing routine freezes for ordinary debts, treating necessity alone as authority, or resolving other creditors' rights and the separate final orders.

#### Record Limits

The record establishes neither bond damages, an adjudicated fraudulent transfer nor a lien in the restrained assets.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The reviewed preliminary-injunction judgment returns to the Second Circuit under the Court's legal-power ruling. Any bond proceedings remain distinct and require their own adjudication. The final money judgment, unchallenged permanent conversion and separate turnover appeal are outside this mandate. No new remedial entitlement or further Supreme Court event is established.

**End of entry: Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo, S.A. v. Alliance Bond Fund, Inc., Merits decision, June 17, 1999.**

### National Aeronautics and Space Administration v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, No. 98-369

Merits decision, June 17, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 23, 1999; decided June 17. Certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, 120 F.3d 1208, enforcing the FLRA's order. The questions are whether this NASA Inspector General investigator is an agency representative for §7114(a)(2)(B), and whether NASA Headquarters must direct compliance with the resulting statutory representation duty.

The employee requested representation and reasonably feared discipline. His union representative attended but was restricted to observation and limited interruption, while refusal by the employee to answer risked dismissal. Before the interview OIG had determined that no criminal action would be taken against this employee. The interference finding was not separately appealed; its legal coverage predicate remains at issue. The Authority ordered OIG and Headquarters compliance, notices and Headquarters direction to OIG. The lower court reached the Headquarters objection under the case-specific extraordinary-circumstances provision of §7123(c), without creating a general exception to preservation.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or qualification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OIG examination's statutory coverage | Affirmed, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas | Existing OIG compliance and notice relief remains enforceable |
| Headquarters compliance duty | Affirmed, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas | Existing limited direction to OIG remains enforceable; Stone-Zsela agrees in this component's judgment only |

The components rest on distinct grounds. Stone-Zsela joins coverage in full and adds no ground on Headquarters' duty. No damages, discipline, reinvestigation, universal interview protocol or new deadline is ordered. The Headquarters instruction requires statutory compliance and authorizes no control of investigative subjects, evidence, conclusions or termination.

#### Opinions and Joins

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg | Affirms statutory coverage | This examination's coverage and bounded representation right |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Breyer | Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg | Affirms Headquarters compliance; Stone-Zsela concurs in this component's judgment only | Independent Headquarters-compliance ground |
| Dissent | Thomas | O'Connor and Scalia | Opposes both enforcement components | Opposes coverage and independently opposes Headquarters direction even if coverage governs |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### This OIG examination is covered by the statutory representation right

An investigator employed in NASA's Office of Inspector General is a representative of NASA when conducting the agency-related examination presented here. Section 7114(a)(2)(B) requires an opportunity for the exclusive representative to assist at the examination of a unit employee in connection with an investigation when the employee reasonably believes discipline may result and requests representation; OIG's independence from ordinary management does not eliminate that relationship.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this rule, explanation and limits.

The chapter defines agency broadly and does not exclude NASA. Its representation provision is not confined to the immediate component that negotiates with the union. Excluding specified investigative employees from membership in a bargaining unit under §7112(b)(7) does not exempt their examinations of covered employees under §7114. This investigator works within NASA on agency-related conduct capable of producing discipline. The Court does not treat every employee or outside examiner as an agency representative.

The IG Act must be independently respected. It creates an independent unit within the agency and protects investigations from managerial interference, while retaining an institutional relationship and general supervision. Those protections do not require elimination of a limited statutory right belonging to the examined employee. NFFE, Haggar and Aguirre-Aguirre require enacted authority and a permissible actual explanation, rather than automatic deference. The Authority's coverage explanation satisfies that inquiry here; its agency label is not the reason it prevails.

Weingarten's limited assistance function reconciles the right with orderly investigation. The representative may assist but may not obstruct questioning, compromise protected information, direct the investigation or require bargaining at the interview; the investigator may insist on the employee's own account. Those limits do not validate reducing representation to silent observation where interference is otherwise established. Reasonable fear and request remain statutory predicates. The Court decides no universal interview protocol, outside-agency or FBI examination, wider criminal inquiry, national-security question or negotiated extension of rights.

##### Headquarters may be directed to secure this limited statutory compliance

Under the retained general-supervision relationship, NASA Headquarters may be required to direct OIG compliance with the limited representation right recognized here. That direction does not authorize Headquarters to determine investigative subjects, evidence, conclusions or termination or to bargain for expanded control over OIG procedures.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part II is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplying five direct votes for the complete rationale. Stone-Zsela's additional vote supports the judgment only.

Coverage and parent-agency responsibility are separate questions. The IG Act preserves general supervision by the head or next-ranking officer while forbidding specified interference with initiation, conduct and completion. Requiring compliance with an independently applicable federal protection respects both provisions. The Authority's order calls for that compliance, not management direction of investigative outcomes. Headquarters' relationship to OIG therefore supports this limited instruction even though it could not lawfully take over the investigation.

NFFE distinguishes available authority from its lawful exercise; the actual compliance ground here answers the distinct remedial question. Haggar and Aguirre-Aguirre require neither automatic enforcement nor an unnecessary new agency proceeding when the statutory analysis and actual explanation support the order. The Court sustains the existing compliance and notice relief without a general rule of derivative liability, a new governance power or an enlarged representational scheme. The case-specific preservation history permits review of this statutory objection; it creates no general exception for objections withheld from an agency.

#### Precedent Treatment

- NLRB v. J. Weingarten, Inc.: applies the limited assistance function of representation, preserving orderly questioning, the employee's own account and the prohibition on obstruction or required bargaining.
- NFFE/FLRA v. Department of the Interior: applies the requirement of actual statutory authority and a reasoned agency choice; distinguishes its remand for an unexercised choice under a mistaken legal premise.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: preserves separate inquiries into delegation, permissible construction and application; supplies no automatic validation or remand rule.
- INS v. Aguirre-Aguirre: applies review of the actual permissible agency explanation without importing an automatic new-hearing requirement.
- Dickinson v. Zurko: preserves its separate factual-review holding; it does not resolve this statutory interpretation or turn it into deferential factfinding.

#### Law After Decision

OIG independence does not remove this agency-related, discipline-capable examination from §7114(a)(2)(B). Request and reasonable fear remain necessary, and representation remains assistance within legitimate investigative limits. A separately justified Headquarters instruction to observe that statutory protection is permissible under retained general supervision. Neither holding confers control over investigative results or resolves every outside-agency, criminal or negotiated-right setting.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Thomas, joined by O'Connor and Scalia, dissents. Representative is a relational term in a labor-management statute, not a synonym for agency member. The Inspector General's independent investigative mandate, reporting responsibilities and insulation from ordinary managerial control distinguish this examiner from a management representative. Possible disciplinary use of answers does not supply the missing relationship, and the Authority's actual explanation does not overcome the statutory structure. The dissent would reverse coverage and both dependent enforcement components. It leaves open exceptional circumstances in which an OIG investigator actually represents management and does not exempt OIG from every employment law.

Independently, those three Justices would remove the disputed Headquarters direction even if coverage governs. They read retained general supervision together with the protections for independent investigation and find insufficient authority for the imposed managerial instruction. That conditional position preserves direct OIG compliance with a coverage rule established by the Court; it does not convert disagreement about Headquarters into a new exemption for OIG itself.

#### Record Limits

The governing texts include 5 U.S.C. §§7103, 7112, 7114 and 7123 and the Inspector General Act. The record establishes neither employee wrongdoing nor a universal interview procedure.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Eleventh Circuit judgment remains in force, sustaining the existing compliance, notice and limited Headquarters-direction order. Further compliance occurs through the existing statutory process. The Court orders no new hearing, investigation, disciplinary decision or representation regulation.

**End of entry: National Aeronautics and Space Administration v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, Merits decision, June 17, 1999.**

### Strickler v. Greene, No. 98-5864

Merits decision, June 17, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 3, 1999; decided June 17. Certiorari to the Fourth Circuit's judgment directing dismissal of the federal habeas petition. The questions concern cause for the defaulted suppression claim and cumulative Brady materiality separately for capital guilt and selection of death. The March 5, 1996 application preceded AEDPA; former law governs the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions. No Chapter 154 qualification follows from capital status alone.

Five suppressed documents—Exhibits 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6—qualify Stoltzfus's identification and early recollection and expose reconstruction and prolonged photo-assisted identification. She witnessed the abduction, not the killing. Suppression of three other documents remains disputed. Independent evidence concerns joint active participation, the victim's car and property, physical connections and a contested account of the killing conversation. The instruction permitted capital guilt for active joint participation in the fatal beating, without requiring a sole fatal actor. No separate instruction or recantation claim is decided.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Suppression and reasonable disclosure reliance establish cause here

Governmental suppression of the five documents, combined with counsel's reasonable reliance on the prosecution's open-file practice and the Commonwealth's later completeness representation during state collateral proceedings, supplies cause for this omitted claim. General awareness of interviews and changing recollection, and the existence of a discretionary discovery avenue, do not on these facts establish knowledge of the missing factual basis or a duty to distrust the asserted disclosure.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part I has eight direct votes; Thomas dissents from this ground.

Murray v. Carrier and Amadeo distinguish an external impediment and unavailable factual basis from ordinary attorney error. The case-team police possessed the missing material, and Kyles does not let the prosecution avoid its disclosure responsibility through personal ignorance. That responsibility is distinct from an entitlement to unrestricted open-file access. Here the actual open-file practice and later response asserting full Brady disclosure reasonably concealed the particular basis of this claim.

The Commonwealth's later response reinforced reliance while state proceedings continued; it is not moved to a time before the state petition was filed. References to several interviews and recollection changes gave general information, not the five documents' specific contents. Nor does a discretionary discovery mechanism prove that a hypothetical request would have produced them. The Court decides this combination, not automatic cause for every Brady allegation or a new right to collateral counsel. Coleman v. Thompson's default rule and recognized excuses remain. Cause permits the materiality inquiry; it does not establish prejudice, a completed Brady violation or entitlement to a writ.

##### The cumulative suppression does not undermine confidence in capital guilt

Considering all lawful combined uses of the five established suppressed documents in the whole record, there is no reasonable probability of a different capital-guilt verdict sufficient to undermine confidence in that verdict. This conclusion assesses revised preparation and presentation as well as impeachment; it does not require proof of innocence or rest merely on legally sufficient remaining evidence.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part II is joined by all nine, with Thomas adopting it as an independent alternative to his default ground.

Kyles requires the documents to be considered together. Their identification uncertainty, reconstructed timing and photo assistance could support a materially different cross-examination and investigation of how a tentative account became confident. Disclosure could also alter the defense presentation; counsel's existing arguments are not binding concessions that erase those uses. The Court assumes substantial damage to Stoltzfus's confident portrayal and considers the Henderson-related defense evidence and challenges to Tudor.

The independent driving, property, bank-card, blood and forensic evidence and the supported account of the killing conversation nevertheless preserve confidence in Strickler's active joint participation. Stoltzfus did not witness the killing. The actual instruction permitted joint participation in the fatal acts and did not require identifying who alone dropped the rock; evidence of Henderson's participation therefore does not necessarily displace Strickler's active role. The Court does not convert possession into eyewitness proof or add later admissions and an unverified recantation to the jury's evidence.

The five-document assessment needs no suppression finding concerning Exhibits 2, 7 and 8. It adjudicates no freestanding challenge to the instruction's disputed predicate. No second harmlessness inquiry is imposed: the claim fails at Brady's initial confidence inquiry on the guilt record itself.

##### The separate death-selection inquiry also retains confidence

After separately considering the five documents' combined lawful effects on the jury's individualized selection of death, the Court finds no reasonable probability of a different penalty recommendation sufficient to undermine confidence in that selection. Reliable capital guilt or a surviving aggravator does not by itself answer this question.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part III is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Thomas's join is an independent alternative. Five other Justices directly adopt the complete rationale without relying on Thomas's default position.

The witness's vivid portrayal of Strickler as the aggressive initiator matters beyond the bare fact of participation. The Court considers the potential for a substantially changed defense account, including greater emphasis on Henderson's role, and the actual jury-closing references to grabbing and abducting a stranger in the dangerousness argument. The longer leadership passage at 20R15 was addressed to the judge, not the penalty jury. The prior-record account describes no convictions involving actual violence; the Court does not invent such convictions or equate that qualification with absence of all dangerousness evidence.

Even with severe impeachment of Stoltzfus's certainty and relative-role portrayal, the independent evidence of active fatal conduct, the brutality of the rock killing and the supported surrounding conduct retain their force in the individualized death choice. The Court evaluates those facts with the revised defense uses; it does not treat argument brevity, lawful eligibility or the existence of an aggravator as conclusive. The complete jury mitigation presentation is not established by the sentencing judge's later account, and no assumed rejection of childhood-abuse testimony supplies this conclusion. The judge's separate leadership comments furnish neither a substitute jury finding nor an independent cure.

Tuggle preserves the distinction between eligibility and selection within its own assistance setting; it does not mandate this Brady result. Kyles governs the present initial materiality inquiry without a second duplicative harmlessness hurdle. No different burden from Coleman, Lilly or Neder is imposed. The claim fails because the collective disclosure counterfactual does not sufficiently change confidence in this actual selection, not because every eligible defendant may receive death.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Murray v. Carrier: applies the objective-external-impediment requirement for cause, preserving the distinction from ordinary attorney error.
- Amadeo v. Zant: applies its treatment of an unavailable factual basis concealed by government conduct.
- Coleman v. Thompson: preserves procedural default and its established excuses; this record satisfies cause for the reasons stated.
- Kyles v. Whitley: applies collective lawful uses, case-team disclosure responsibility and the confidence standard; preserves the prohibition on a second duplicative harmlessness hurdle after a material violation is established.
- Tuggle v. Netherland: preserves the distinct inquiry into death selection despite surviving eligibility, without converting its Ake rule into automatic Brady relief.
- Lindh v. Murphy and Carriger v. Stewart: apply former-law treatment of this pre-AEDPA capital application for the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions; no broader temporal exemption is decided.
- O'Sullivan v. Boerckel: preserves default, exhaustion and recognized gateways without resolving the suppression-specific cause facts.
- Calderon v. Coleman: distinguishes its established penalty-instruction error and collateral harmlessness inquiry from initial Brady materiality.
- Lilly v. Virginia and Neder v. United States: preserve their distinct error rules; neither adds a harmlessness hurdle or supplies automatic reversal for this nondisclosure.

#### Law After Decision

The actual suppression and reasonable-reliance combination establishes cause, while cumulative materiality remains independently necessary. Guilt and death selection require separate confidence assessments, with all supported lawful disclosure uses considered. This record establishes neither phase's materiality. The decision creates no eligibility-only cure, categorical rule about abduction testimony or additional harmlessness burden. The reviewed dismissal stands.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Thomas concurs in the judgment and the merits holdings as independent alternatives. He would also enforce default: known repeated interviews and changes in recollection, together with the available state discovery route, warranted further inquiry despite the open-file practice. He does not assume that a request certainly would have succeeded. His diligence conclusion has no other join.

Souter, joined by Kennedy, concurs in cause and conviction denial but dissents from sentence denial. The five early accounts expose uncertainty and reconstruction behind the apparently disinterested witness's vivid allocation of initiative and dominance. That allocation could materially alter the jury's moral choice even where independent proof preserves active joint guilt. The supported jury references to stranger abduction connect the account to dangerousness; the argument is not confined to how often the prosecutor mentioned the witness. The five suffice without finding the other three suppressed, specifying unknown jury mitigation or treating the judge's comments as a separate violation. They would direct conditional sentence-only relief, allowing Virginia within a reasonable district-court period to provide a lawful disposition correcting the jury-selection injury or a lawful nondeath disposition. If it failed to comply, the death sentence would be vacated, with convictions and valid imprisonment intact. They prescribe no particular jury/judge mechanism or automatic life term.

Stone-Zsela concurs in cause and conviction denial and separately dissents on punishment. Integrating the five documents with the defense and independent evidence preserves guilt confidence, but undermining the dominant-aggressor portrayal creates a reasonable probability of a different penalty recommendation. The trial narrative and actual jury references support that conclusion without transferring the longer court-directed passage to the jury or assuming violent prior convictions or a particular mitigation presentation. He would require a conditional sentence-only writ unless Virginia conducts a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding within a reasonable period fixed by the district court. The conviction remains intact, and Virginia may impose any lawfully available sentence after that proceeding. He does not decide the three disputed documents or the new proceeding's outcome.

#### Record Limits

The complete penalty transcript and alleged later recantation are not established as new evidence here. The sentencing judge's childhood-abuse discussion does not establish exactly what mitigation the jury heard.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Fourth Circuit's reviewed dismissal remains effective on the independent no-materiality grounds, with its cause rationale rejected. No new penalty or guilt proceeding is directed. Unrelated claims and independently available procedures are not resolved, and no execution timetable or release order is entered.

### Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District, lower docket 98-50227

Merits decision, June 17, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Merits decided June 17, 1999, following a grant of review of the substituted Fifth Circuit judgment, 168 F.3d 848, issued March 11 and revised April 1. The questions concern Section 2's first district-capacity condition, the current demographic findings and exclusion of a late survey.

The seven-member school board uses at-large, by-place, majority-vote elections. Updated evidence placed the proposed district at 47.9% Hispanic citizen voting-age population with a northern addition, or 48.3% with 8.1% underpopulation. Those figures measure population rather than turnout or crossover support. The trial court relied on the April 1997 demographic report, excluded the later defense update and excluded plaintiffs' survey disclosed immediately before trial. No finding establishes dependable crossover, cohesion, ordinary majority-bloc defeat or the full totality under the alternative recognized today.

All nine Justices participated in the merits submission and decision.

The lower courts must use the preserved admissible record and any supplementation independently permitted under ordinary procedure. | Dissent | Thomas | Scalia | Would affirm on statutory noncoverage | Evidence assessment is conditional only; adopts neither capacity rule |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The first condition includes a proved functional ability-to-elect route

For a claim by one protected group, the first Gingles condition may be met either through the retained district-majority route or through rigorous proof that a lawful, geographically compact proposed district would provide the cohesive group dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its candidates of choice. Speculation, an isolated favorable election, near-majority population arithmetic or mere influence does not suffice; the relevant majority-bloc showing, causal connection and independent totality remain necessary.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this express modification and its limits.

The first condition links the challenged electoral arrangement to an achievable opportunity to elect. Numerical majority provides a predictable demonstration of that capacity, but an exclusive number can reject the same actual opportunity when dependable voting evidence proves it. Section 2 protects equal participation and opportunity, without guaranteeing proportional outcomes. The Court therefore expressly modifies the numerical exclusivity reflected in Gingles and Growe; it does not declare that earlier reservations already adopted this alternative.

De Grandy rejects numerical maximization and conclusive safe harbors while preserving actual local opportunity. Brooks fits proof to a different nomination barrier. Those decisions support the inquiry's purpose but do not themselves decide this new district-capacity rule. Dependability matters because a contingent alliance or favorable candidate alone cannot establish lost opportunity attributable to the system. Plaintiffs must prove cohesion and actual voting behavior within the proposed district and the relevant majority-bloc defeat in the challenged arrangement; successful crossover in one setting does not silently negate or establish bloc defeat in another. The ordinary special-circumstances qualification, including unopposed candidacy, remains.

The majority route survives. So do lawful district population and geography, constitutional limits and the separate totality inquiry. The Court prescribes no mathematical crossover formula, racial floor, proportional-election right or preferred political tradeoff, and decides no combination of distinct minority groups or influence-only entitlement. States retain lawful choice among effective arrangements; a court-compelled remedy still requires an established violation.

##### The population figures do not resolve functional opportunity

The accepted 47.9% and 48.3% Hispanic citizen voting-age shares neither prove nor foreclose dependable ability to elect. The judgment resting exclusively on their being below fifty percent must be vacated for application of the changed rule, with all remaining statutory conditions and totality to be established before relief.

**Authority:** The same five Justices join Stone-Zsela's Part I and this application and remedy.

Population is not evidence by itself of the electorate's actual crossover behavior. The lower courts did not make the findings necessary under the functional route, and this Court cannot supply them. The proposed district must be lawful in population and geography; an 8.1% underpopulation is not approved merely because it appears in the calculation. If admissible proof does not establish dependable opportunity, the claim fails. If capacity is established, the remaining conditions and unequal opportunity under totality still require determination. Voinovich preserves the difference between state choice and judicially compelled remedy; De Grandy permits neither automatic liability nor a numerical safe harbor. The remand commands no map or voting system and guarantees no favorable result.

##### The independent demographic and disclosure rulings stand

The credited proof of concrete post-census housing, occupancy and growth changes supports the present demographic findings under clear-error review. Excluding the survey disclosed on the eve of trial for surprise under the applicable disclosure rule was within the trial court's discretion; changing the capacity rule does not itself make either independent ruling erroneous.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia and Thomas do not join it unconditionally.

The 1990 census's presumptive accuracy does not require treating later population as unchanged. The April 1997 report supplied documented local changes under the burden the lower court applied; the excluded August report does not become the basis for affirmance. The Court establishes neither a universal heightened demographic burden nor automatic approval of local estimates. Commerce's national-enumeration holding supplies no contrary rule about these later local facts.

The plaintiffs disclosed the survey's existence late Friday and its data late Saturday before Monday trial. The earlier deposition reference did not disclose its basis. Local Rule CV-16(e) permitted good-cause supplementation, but the trial court could enforce disclosure and avoid surprise, particularly after excluding the defense update said to occasion the survey. The Court does not decide the survey's accuracy, whether it was proper rebuttal or unestablished factors in a separate exclusion framework. Preservation of the exclusion is not a substantive rejection of the survey's results.

Evidence previously immaterial under an exclusive numerical threshold may bear on the changed inquiry. The trial court may determine whether fair supplementation is needed under ordinary rules, with adequate notice and response; reopening is neither automatic nor forbidden. No new trial, mandatory study or automatic admission of the excluded survey is ordered.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Thornburg v. Gingles: expressly modifies numerical exclusivity in the first condition for the defined single-group functional route; preserves compactness, cohesion, relevant majority-bloc proof, causation and independent totality.
- Growe v. Emison: modifies the same exclusivity while preserving its causal framework and the distinct-group reservation.
- Voinovich v. Quilter: preserves the claimant's burden, relevant bloc-voting requirement and distinction between lawful state districting choice and a court-compelled remedy; the present rule resolves only the defined functional-capacity question.
- Johnson v. De Grandy: applies the rejection of maximization and conclusive ratios; preserves independent totality and actual opportunity without attributing today's change to that earlier decision.
- Brooks v. Barnes: applies its insistence that proof address the actual electoral barrier; its nomination holding did not already decide this district-formation question.
- Department of Commerce v. United States House of Representatives / Clinton v. Glavin: preserves the national enumeration ruling; it neither freezes local demographics at the census date nor validates every projection.

#### Law After Decision

A single protected group below a numerical majority may prove the first condition through dependable actual ability to elect in a lawful compact district. A majority-population route remains available, but neither route establishes every other condition or liability. Cohesion, relevant bloc-voting causation with applicable special circumstances, totality, independent constitutional limits and ordinary evidence procedures retain their force. Mere influence and speculative near-majority calculations do not create a right to districting. The present record establishes no functional success or completed violation.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

O'Connor, joined by Kennedy, concurs in the demographic and disclosure rulings and dissents from changing the first condition. A numerical citizen-voting-age majority, in their view, supplies a predictable causal link between the challenged electoral structure and a district opportunity that the protected group could exercise. The functional alternative risks making compelled districting turn on contingent electoral alliances. Growe and Voinovich's reservations do not require that extension, and De Grandy and Brooks concern different analytical questions. They would affirm because both accepted shares are below a majority, without prohibiting lawful voluntary crossover districts, deciding every influence or coalition claim, imposing proportional outcomes or approving the proposed population deviation.

Thomas, joined by Scalia, dissents on a different ground. Maintaining their De Grandy and Brooks position, they would limit §2 to voting access, casting and counting rather than the challenged districting dilution, reconsidering the contrary coverage rule. They would affirm the denial of relief without adopting either numerical or functional capacity. Their agreement with the demographic and exclusion assessments is conditional on applying the dilution framework and adds no unconditional join to the Court's holdings.

The earlier Fifth Circuit opinion was withdrawn; the substituted judgment controls this review. Neither the excluded survey's accuracy nor the proposed district's functional electoral capacity is adjudicated here.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The statutory claim returns for bounded reconsideration under the expressly changed capacity rule. Supported demographics and the prior survey exclusion remain intact. Any supplementation follows ordinary procedural requirements and the trial court's determination of need, notice and fair response; no new trial, admission, map or election date is prescribed.

### City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters Association, No. 98-966

Merits decision, June 18, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued May 4, 1999; decided June 18. Certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 150 F.3d 438, reviewing the final judgment invalidating racial/ethnic and sex-based out-of-rank promotions to driver-engineer, lieutenant and captain. The separately sustained deputy-chief appointment, overall plan, Title VII merits and Texas statutory merits are outside review. The Texas constitutional ground follows the federal constitutional inquiry.

The City links identified earlier entry exclusion to present ranks through an internal promotion ladder. The 1972 DOJ letter addressed race/ethnicity-related entry barriers; the 1976 agreement included race and sex commitments but did not adjudicate every allegation. The City employed validated exams, recruiting, tutoring, removal of seniority points and a second paramedic-course opportunity. It also invokes qualification, score bands, an annual fifty-percent cap, five-year continuation periods and cessation when rank imbalance ends. The connection between that history, each present preference and the relevant promotion pools remains the constitutional issue.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Local racial and sex classifications retain distinct constitutional standards

Municipal racial and ethnic promotion preferences must satisfy Adarand's common substantial-justification standard; general inequality, imbalance and good intentions alone are insufficient. Sex preferences separately require an actual important objective and a substantial relationship supported by an exceedingly persuasive justification, without importing the racial-classification standard or a statistical-only proof rule.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I has all nine Justices for these distinct standards and for rejecting an invariably required prior liability judgment or egregious resistance. Eight Justices other than Scalia also accept the bounded affirmative proposition that supported local exclusion may permit fitted repair; Scalia reserves its full reach beyond the rejection necessary here.

Adarand's common standard governs local racial measures, retaining Croson's evidentiary demands as applied in Engineering Contractors. A government must connect the exclusion it identifies to the opportunity and remedy at issue. It must seriously consider reasonably available responsive neutral measures and explain their inadequacy, but need not try and exhaust every conceivable alternative. A prior adjudication or resistance to litigation is not an invariable condition of constitutional repair.

Kelley, Klinger, Virginia and Engineering Contractors require a separate inquiry into the government's actual sex-based justification. Racial evidence cannot be relabeled as a sex finding, and underrepresentation alone supplies no automatic justification. Neither a numerical minimum nor statistics as the exclusive form of proof is required. Subsidiary factual support remains distinct from independent constitutional assessment; summary judgment permits no adverse resolution of a genuine material factual dispute. The Court decides the challenged promotions under their actual standards, not the validity of every employment measure or future remedial program.

##### The racial/ethnic preferences lack the required connected justification

The combined identified history and workforce evidence do not sufficiently connect the asserted exclusion to bypassing higher-ranked eligible candidates in the challenged promotion categories. The racial/ethnic invalidation is affirmed on this independent failure of supported justification.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt this application and the independent fit ground below.

The DOJ letter identifies serious entry barriers, and the internal ladder provides a possible route for continuing effects. The Court considers those facts together rather than demanding an admission or prior judgment. But a possible relationship and general departmental imbalance do not establish the needed connection to each present promotion opportunity and its relevant qualified feeder pool. The 1976 agreement's commitments likewise do not adjudicate every current remedial predicate. The City's account supplies no constitutional entitlement to parity in each rank.

Engineering Contractors requires independent assessment of the actual record; its separate four-Justice evidence conclusion did not settle this case. Here the five-Justice coalition finds the combined showing insufficient for the particular preferences. That conclusion does not deny the reality of earlier exclusion, deem all comparisons conclusive, or impose a statistical-only standard. It does not make the absence of a reproduced dataset a failure by the City or resolve a genuine material factual dispute against it. The legal connection required for these actual departures remains inadequately established on the record presented.

##### The racial/ethnic preferences also fail independent narrow fit

Even assuming an adequate remedial objective, the record does not sufficiently explain why the actual rank departures, beneficiaries and allocation limits repair the identified exclusion. Qualification and score bands, duration and percentage limits do not independently establish the required fit.

**Authority:** The same five Justices join Kennedy's Part II and this independently sufficient racial ground.

Dallas used real neutral measures: validated testing, recruitment, tutoring, removed seniority points and a further paramedic-course opportunity. The Court does not treat those efforts as nonexistent or demand exhaustion of every imaginable alternative. Continued imbalance nevertheless does not by itself show that responsive neutral efforts are inadequate or that these preferences are properly calibrated to each rank's need.

A qualified candidate within a score band is eligible, but qualification does not establish equivalence with every higher-ranked candidate or justify racial allocation within that band. The fifty-percent annual cap, continuation periods and cessation at rank balance limit operation without supplying the missing relationship between remedy and supported injury. Croson and Engineering Contractors require that relationship, including attention to third-party burdens. Promotion delay is neither automatically trivial nor identical to a layoff. The Court imposes no fixed numerical ceiling or new formula; it holds that the actual limits here do not cure the independent fit deficiency. The conclusion leaves lawful neutral measures and future adequately supported, fitted remedies for their own review.

##### The sex preferences lack an independently supported actual justification

The identified record does not adequately establish a connected sex-specific remedial justification for the challenged out-of-rank promotions. The sex component is affirmed under demanding intermediate scrutiny, independently of the racial evidence and narrow-fit conclusions.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III is joined by all eight other Justices. The Court does not independently decide sex fit after this predicate fails; Stone-Zsela's additional fit position is separate.

The 1972 letter's stated findings concern racial and ethnic entry barriers. The agreement's sex commitments and women's underrepresentation are relevant but do not themselves establish the actual sex-specific exclusion and promotion connection necessary to justify these preferences. Kelley and Klinger require an important objective genuinely supporting the classification; Virginia makes the government's justification demanding. None allows racial proof or parity goals to substitute for that showing.

Engineering Contractors applied a distinct sex inquiry and preserved the possibility of sufficient nonstatistical proof. Its competing assessment of combined women's-business evidence does not supply missing affirmative evidence in this employment record. The Court does not require strict scrutiny, every-alternative exhaustion, a numerical minimum or statistics alone. It affirms because the actual connected justification has not been sufficiently established, leaving any independently fitted future measure undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co.: applies its retained evidentiary demands, supported remedial justification and fit within Adarand's common standard, without an invariable prior-adjudication prerequisite.
- Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County: applies its distinct racial and sex rules and responsive-neutral-measures requirement; its prior four-Justice additional racial evidence ground remains noncontrolling, and Dallas's actual neutral efforts are assessed on their own facts.
- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña: supplies the common standard governing Dallas's municipal preferences; Metro Broadcasting's overruled federal tier supplies none.
- Kelley v. Board of Trustees: applies demanding actual sex justification without stereotypes, strict scrutiny or statistics alone.
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections: applies the actual-sex-classification branch while preserving the separate program-specific setting of its comparison rules.
- United States v. Virginia: applies the requirement of an exceedingly persuasive sex-based justification without converting intermediate scrutiny into the racial-classification standard.

#### Law After Decision

These racial/ethnic promotions fail independently for insufficient connected justification and fit; the sex promotions fail their separate actual-justification inquiry. Adarand's racial standard and the demanding sex standard retain distinct operation. Actual neutral efforts matter, but their existence and continued imbalance do not establish inadequacy, constitutional necessity or fit. The judgment creates no universal ban on supported repair and adjudicates neither the full plan nor future or ungranted promotions.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Stevens, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, concurs in the standards and sex affirmance but would vacate the racial/ethnic component. Identified exclusion at entry may persist through an internal promotion ladder, and the comparison pool may itself reflect that exclusion. Dallas undertook neutral measures, removing the exact unperformed-alternatives ground that controlled Engineering Contractors. The combined record, actual efforts and challenged limits therefore require correct independent assessment rather than an invariable prior-judgment or egregious-resistance prerequisite. The writing neither validates the program nor requires new evidence, an immediate preferential promotion or a hearing. It agrees that this record lacks the independently connected sex-specific justification necessary for the sex preferences.

Scalia concurs in the actual rejection and governing scrutiny while reserving the broader affirmative extent of permissible racial repair. He does not make a categorical prohibition necessary to his vote.

Stone-Zsela concurs in the Court's opinion and adds that the sex-based rank departures also lack a supported substantial relationship to the identified objective. A score band or qualification can show eligibility without establishing that preferential allocation repairs actual sex discrimination. This separate ground applies the demanding sex standard and imports neither the racial-classification standard nor an exhaustion requirement. It is not a holding of the Court.

General workforce shares are not adjudicated rank-specific denominators. The 1972 letter's racial and ethnic findings supply no substituted sex finding, and the 1976 agreement does not adjudicate every allegation.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

### Jefferson County v. Acker, No. 98-10

Merits decision, June 21, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 29, 1999; decided June 21, 1999. Certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit's en banc judgment, 137 F.3d 1314, sustaining the federal forum and holding the county occupational levy invalid as applied to the respondent federal judges. The questions concern federal-officer removal, the Tax Injunction Act, the operation of the levy under the federal salary-tax consent, and the renewed source-discrimination defense.

Jefferson County brought state-court collection actions against federal district judges William Acker and U. W. Clemon, who removed under 28 U.S.C. §1442(a)(3). Ordinance 1120, effective January 1, 1988, imposes one-half of one percent of compensation attributable to work within the County, with apportionment for work elsewhere. It includes federal, state and local officeholders, exempts persons paying specified state privilege, license or occupational taxes, and declares working without payment unlawful. No actual county restriction preventing a federal judge from sitting is established.

The district court rejected source discrimination and also accepted a distinct Compensation Clause ground. The earlier appellate account reports no challenge to the discrimination rejection; respondents renewed that defense here as an alternative ground for the same favorable judgment. The en banc merits disposition did not reach the Compensation Clause, which remains outside the questions decided here. #### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Federal-officer removal is proper for these collection actions

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal judge may remove a county collection action under the event-applicable §1442(a)(3) when the action has the required connection to official duties and the judge asserts a colorable federal defense. The work-linked levy and official-duty immunity defense establish those requirements here; the defense's eventual failure does not defeat removal.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer, has six direct votes for this rule, explanation and scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Mesa requires a federal defense in addition to federal office, and Willingham preserves a federal forum for a colorable official-duty defense without requiring the officer first to prevail on it. The County's demand arises from the compensation attributable to these judges' performance of federal judicial duties, and the ordinance makes engaging in the work the asserted basis of liability. Their claim that this exaction impermissibly burdens those duties supplies the necessary connection and a nonfrivolous federal defense. The United States' contrary nexus argument correctly separates personal tax liability from an official act, but gives insufficient weight to the particular work-linked ordinance and defense presented here. Federal employment and a compensation measure alone would not authorize removal of every personal obligation. This holding neither dispenses with the office nexus nor imports a later expansion of the removal statute. Characterizing the defense as colorable decides the forum, not whether federal law ultimately exempts the judges from this levy.

##### The Tax Injunction Act does not bar the defensive adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1341 does not prevent a federal court with removal jurisdiction from adjudicating the federal defenses in a taxing authority's own collection suit. These collection actions seek payment, rather than anticipatory federal restraint of assessment, levy or collection by taxpayers.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II has all nine Justices' joins, with Scalia, Souter and Thomas accepting the Court's removal ruling as the predicate for this independent question.

**Controlling explanation:** The Act limits federal injunctions, suspensions and restraints of state-tax administration; a tax collector's suit to obtain payment is a different proceeding. Removal changes its tribunal without transforming the defendants' defenses into an affirmative taxpayer action. Rejecting the County's asserted liability would resolve its own claim, not grant a separately requested anticipatory injunction. Farm Credit distinguished substantive tax immunity from access to a federal forum and involved private associations seeking affirmative injunctive and declaratory relief with an adequate state remedy conceded. Its entity-specific rule does not decide these removed judicial-officer collection actions. The Court therefore need not find a universal federal-officer exemption from §1341, treat the United States' amicus appearance as party joinder, or supply a new finding about the adequacy of Alabama remedies. Taxpayer-initiated federal restraint remains subject to its own statutory limits.

##### The levy operates as compensation taxation within §111

**Holding and operative rule:** The levy asserted in these collection actions operates as a tax on compensation for federal personal services within 4 U.S.C. §111, notwithstanding its occupational-license label. Section 111's consent does not authorize discrimination because of the compensation's federal source or a local requirement of permission to exercise federal office.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven directly adopt this compensation-tax rule on the jurisdictional predicate stated above.

**Controlling explanation:** Graves and §111 require attention to what the exaction does, rather than a constitutional exemption created by its state-law title. The County seeks a percentage of compensation attributable to services performed within its boundaries, with apportionment for work elsewhere. It has not established or exercised a local veto over the judges' performance of their duties. The unlawful-work language and exemptions for occupations paying other license charges are relevant to the ordinance's operation, but together do not convert the revenue demand presented here into an exercise of licensing control over federal adjudication. A compensation measure alone would not save every direct federal-function license; the actual demand and operation are decisive. Blaze preserves the distinction between legal incidence and indirect economic effect, while reserving direct federal burdens and actual instrumentalities; federal judges are not equated with independent contractors. The Court decides no hypothetical restriction preventing a judge from sitting and need not decide the County's alternative Buck Act theory. The separate Article III Compensation Clause question is not resolved by this intergovernmental-immunity holding.

##### The renewed discrimination defense may be considered on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** Respondents may defend the existing favorable judgment on the renewed, record-supported source-discrimination ground without seeking greater relief, and the Court elects to consider that alternative here. Its earlier rejection and reported intermediate nonpursuit do not establish an automatic cross-appeal defect, jurisdictional bar or intentional waiver.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part IV has the same seven Justices' direct joins for this confined decision to reach the defense. The permission to consider an alternative is distinct from an obligation to consider every omitted ground.

**Controlling explanation:** X-Citement Video applies American Railway Express's rule allowing a prevailing party to support the same judgment on a ground in the record, including one rejected below, without enlarging its rights or reducing its opponent's rights under that judgment. Here the district court adjudicated discrimination and respondents expressly renewed it in their merits brief. They seek the same exemption judgment, not additional relief. The intermediate nonpursuit remains relevant to review discretion, but the established tax and comparison record permits decision without a new factual theory or findings. Zafiro's prudential treatment of an individual petitioner's unpursued claim does not erase this favorable-judgment posture. AT&T declined to construct an unadvanced independent theory; this one is actually presented. Lebron preserves bounded discretion over included legal arguments, not a duty to revive every abandoned ground. Dandridge's remand option and West's distinct alternative-ground remand likewise create no mandatory postponement of this established and renewed legal defense. The Court chooses present consideration without finding an intentional waiver or assuming that the lower rejection establishes the defense's substantive failure.

##### The presented source-discrimination defense fails

**Holding and operative rule:** On this ordinance and record, taxing the respondent judges' compensation while exempting occupations subject to other specified state licensing charges does not discriminate because their compensation comes from a federal source. The intergovernmental-immunity judgment must be reversed, with remand consistent with that determination and the reservation of the separate Compensation Clause question.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies seven direct votes for the source-discrimination determination and limited remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Davis forbids unfavorable treatment attributable to the federal source of compensation and requires attention to the relevant comparison. This levy includes federal, state and local officeholders; the record establishes that state judges pay it. The exemption for occupations paying other state or county licensing charges does not, on this record, distinguish state compensation from federal compensation. Federal judges' inability personally to fall within that alternative-license category does not by itself establish the forbidden source classification. The Court independently rejects the presented comparison rather than presuming the district court correct or treating all possible exemptions as necessarily valid. No unadmitted workforce percentages, individual licensing facts or withholding practices supply the conclusion. An exemption favoring comparable state officials, an actual federal-source distinction or a genuine local restraint of judicial performance would present a different question. The separate Compensation Clause ground was accepted below but not reached by the en banc court and is not decided here. Remand leaves its proper treatment below without reopening discrimination, quantifying a tax obligation or directing collection regardless of that remaining ground.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Mesa v. California:** Applies the distinct federal-defense requirement; federal office alone is insufficient and merits success is not required to establish a colorable defense.
- **Willingham v. Morgan:** Applies the protective federal-officer forum and the connection to official duties without demanding advance victory on the defense; the particular nexus remains necessary.
- **Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas:** Distinguishes its affirmative injunction/declaration action by private associations and conceded adequate state remedy. Its separation of substantive immunity from forum entitlement remains intact; no universal federal-officer exception is created.
- **Graves v. New York ex rel. O'Keefe:** Applies the rejection of categorical immunity for federal compensation taxation and the need to assess the tax's actual operation, subject to governing nondiscrimination and federal-function limits.
- **Davis v. Michigan Department of Treasury:** Applies federal-source nondiscrimination and relevant comparator analysis; it does not require identical treatment of every differently licensed occupation regardless of the reason for classification.
- **Arizona Department of Revenue v. Blaze Construction Co.:** Preserves legal-incidence analysis and the distinction from indirect economic effect; its independent-contractor result is not extended to erase the separate protection of federal judges or direct federal functions.
- **United States v. X-Citement Video and United States v. American Railway Express:** Apply the prevailing respondent's ability to renew a record-supported alternative for the same favorable judgment without seeking enlarged relief.

- **Zafiro v. United States:** Distinguishes a petitioner's personal unpursued claim from this renewed respondent alternative; its prudential preservation rule remains.
- **AT&T v. Central Office Telephone:** Distinguishes refusal to construct an independent theory that the respondent had not advanced; this defense was adjudicated below and renewed here.
- **Dandridge v. Williams and West v. Gibson:** Preserve lawful remand for appropriate unresolved alternatives without compelling remand of this already adjudicated, record-supported discrimination question.

Section 1341 does not bar the defensive adjudication in that collector-initiated posture. The particular occupational levy is compensation taxation within §111 and the presented source-discrimination defense fails. No categorical exemption for federal judges or local power to license their duties follows. The independent Compensation Clause question and proper calculation or procedural matters remain for their lawful treatment below. The discrimination issue is resolved, not remanded for reopening. **Scalia, joined by Souter and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The collection actions seek payment of the judges' personal tax obligations, and the sued-on nonpayment is not an official judicial act merely because the tax is measured by judicial pay. A colorable immunity defense supplies one removal requirement but cannot substitute for the statutory office nexus. Once the Court establishes removal, however, they accept that resolution as the predicate for joining Parts II–IV: §1341 does not bar this defensive collector action, the levy operates as compensation taxation, the renewed discrimination defense fails, and the intergovernmental-immunity judgment should be reversed with the Compensation Clause reserved. **Breyer, joined by O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree that removal is proper and §1341 poses no bar, but would affirm because the ordinance's cumulative operation imposes a direct charge on performing federal judicial functions. Liability is tied to engaging in work and entitlement to compensation; work without payment is declared unlawful; apportionment follows where the work is done; and occupations subject to other licensing charges are exempt. They prescribe no universal checklist for every occupational levy.

### Jones v. United States, No. 97-9361

Merits decision, June 21, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 22, 1999; decided June 21, 1999. Certiorari to the Fifth Circuit's January 5, 1998 judgment affirming the federal kidnapping conviction and death sentence. This is the capital-sentencing case, distinct from the March 24 carjacking decision. The questions concern the requested deadlock instruction, the delivered sentencing choices and forms, and the nonstatutory aggravator and correction issues.

The sentencing jury found two statutory and two nonstatutory aggravators, rejected other proposed aggravators including future dangerousness, and found numerous mitigating circumstances. The charge required unanimity, said death was never mandatory and protected individual conscience, but directed nonunanimity toward a unanimous life-without-release form or a lesser-sentence form even though this kidnapping offense authorized no punishment below life without release. No actual deadlock or jury question occurred.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Death sentence and judgment sustaining it vacated; remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, who would affirm.

The conviction remains intact. If the Government pursues death, the new penalty proceeding must accurately state the lawful alternatives and employ lawful selection procedures. A lawful nondeath disposition remains available. The Court orders no release, automatic life sentence, new guilt trial or execution timetable. A new hearing after reversible sentencing error is distinct from convening another jury merely because the first deadlocked.

Breyer's sufficient instruction ground is abuse of discretion in refusing the accurate core of the preserved request on an erroneous legal premise. He does not join the concurrence's constitutional or plain-error grounds and does not find plain error in the unobjected delivered charge.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Actual sentencing deadlock requires a lawful nondeath sentence

When completed penalty deliberations end without a unanimous recommendation, §3594's otherwise clause requires the judge to impose a lawful nondeath sentence. For this death-resulting kidnapping the alternative is life without release. Section 3593(b)(2)(C) does not authorize another attempt to obtain death solely because the jury deadlocked.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I-A has eight direct votes from all Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition without joining that rationale.

Section 3593(e) requires a unanimous jury recommendation. Section 3594 directs the judge to impose recommended death or life without release and otherwise to impose an authorized lesser sentence. The Government's construction would confine otherwise to a unanimous lesser recommendation and use good-cause discharge to repeat the penalty hearing. That reading denies operative effect to the nondeath command when the jury cannot agree. Good-cause discharge has independent work, including disqualification, without treating failure of agreement itself as authorization for another death effort. Section 1201's offense-specific penalty fixes the available nondeath sentence; §3593's general reference to other lesser sentences creates none for this offense.

The Government's concession that this offense permits only death or life does not itself concede the deadlock question; the Court rejects the contrary new-jury construction on the statutes. No actual deadlock occurred. Reconsideration after reversible sentencing error under §3593(b)(2)(D) remains distinct and supports the present new-hearing mandate.

##### There is no universal deadlock script, but a false sentencing choice must be corrected here

Neither the Eighth Amendment nor a supervisory rule requires a fixed deadlock-consequence instruction in every capital case. In this case, however, the preserved request for an accurate account of the lawful consequence required correction of the charge's materially misleading below-life alternative; refusing that correction requires a new penalty proceeding.

**Authority:** Part I-B's rejection of a universal script is unanimous. Part II's case-specific corrective duty and remedy are directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The Court does not adopt every constitutional or plain-error reason stated in the separate concurrence.

Jurors ordinarily should deliberate and make their own punishment choice. Congress prescribed no mandatory deadlock script, and Simmons's parole-ineligibility protection does not dictate one. But this jury was choosing punishment, unlike Shannon's ordinary guilt jury, and it had to receive an accurate description of the lawful alternatives. Section 1201 allowed only death or life without release. Form C expressly required unanimity for life, while the direction toward C or D on nonunanimity and D's lesser option could communicate that failure to agree on life would permit the judge to impose a punishment below life.

The initial unanimity direction, optional death, conscience protection and instruction not to consider the judge's later sentence are considered together. They do not correct the specific false statutory choice created by that combination. Instruction 5 and repeated references to its accurate core identified the need for correction, notwithstanding counsel's imperfect lesser-sentence wording. The Court does not require the request verbatim, a nonunanimous jury life verdict or a reasonable-doubt burden for the ultimate moral choice. The shared ruling addresses the preserved refusal and the actual charge. A new accurately instructed penalty proceeding is necessary if death remains sought; the ruling rests on objective directions and findings, not juror affidavits or an invented deliberation inquiry.

##### Independent aggravator validity and cure are not resolved for the Court

The instruction disposition removes this death sentence. No majority resolves whether factors 3(B) and 3(C) were independently invalid or harmless: O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas find contextual validity; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg find the specific formulations defective; Scalia, Stone-Zsela and Breyer reserve validity. O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would find narrowly assumed drafting error harmless; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg reject that cure, while Stone-Zsela and Breyer reserve it.

**Authority:** These accounts identify the limits of the Court's decision, not additional factor holdings. No Marks synthesis supplies a validity or cure rule from the divided positions.

Any new proceeding remains governed by Tuilaepa's understandable relevant selection subjects and the requirement of a genuine lawful cure for an actually established invalid factor under Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa and §3595. Overlap is not automatically unconstitutional, relevant victim-impact evidence is not categorically forbidden, and remaining valid eligibility does not by itself cure an independent selection error. The Court creates no new factor test, appellate moral-reweighing power or permission to repeat misleading instructions.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Simmons v. South Carolina: preserves the effective opportunity to understand the only lawful nondeath alternative in its stated setting; supplies no universal deadlock script.
- Shannon v. United States: distinguishes an ordinary nonsentencing guilt jury; its allocation rule does not excuse an affirmatively misleading capital choice.
- Olano and Johnson: retain separate error, plainness, substantial-rights and discretionary conditions. The four-Justice concurrence's alternative application changes no Court-wide rule and does not decide Johnson's reserved initially unsettled-law setting.
- Boyde, Mills and McKoy: preserve contextual examination of the charge and individual mitigation; no verdict-form typography alone decides the case.
- Tuilaepa/Proctor and Payne: retain understandable relevant individualized selection and permissible impact evidence within their limits; no factor-validity majority arises here.
- Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa, Clemons and Chapman: preserve the distinction between valid eligibility, authorized reweighing and actual harmless-error correction. No new general cure or appellate-resentencing authority is adopted.
- Neder: retains its qualified actual-verdict inquiry; the divided factor applications supply no new universal capital harmlessness rule.
- Strickler: preserves distinct initial Brady materiality for guilt and selection; its burden and record do not decide this preserved instruction issue.

#### Law After Decision

Actual federal sentencing deadlock invokes §3594's lawful nondeath command, with no replacement jury solely to seek death after that deadlock. There is no universal deadlock script. The specific misleading statutory choice in this charge, identified by the preserved corrective request, requires a new penalty proceeding. The conviction remains intact. Their four-Justice reasoning supports the Court's remedy without becoming its complete common rationale.

In Part II, Souter, Stevens and Ginsburg additionally find the two separately submitted personal-characteristic factors overlapping and inadequately definite. They permit relevant impact evidence and would require proper §3595 correction if instruction relief were unavailable, without automatic life or a new appellate moral choice. Stone-Zsela and Breyer do not join this factor disposition.

In Part II, O’Connor and Kennedy join Thomas in additionally finding the factors valid in context under Tuilaepa, §3593(a) and Payne. Scalia reserves validity and relies on assumed-error cure.

### Martin v. Hadix, No. 98-262

Merits decision, June 21, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 30, 1999; decided June 21, 1999. Certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, 143 F.3d 246. The question is whether the PLRA's hourly attorney-fee ceiling governs services performed before and after its April 26, 1996 enactment in prisoner actions already pending on that date.

The Glover litigation began in 1977 and Hadix in 1980; both had reached a continuing remedial and monitoring stage. Glover's stipulated fee procedure dates to November 1985, and Hadix's monitoring-fee order to November 1987 after an April 4, 1985 consent decree. The requests include January 1–June 30, 1996 services. The established rate was $150 per hour; 150 percent of the identified $75 Criminal Justice Act rate yields a $112.50 hourly ceiling. These arrangements establish no immutable contractual rate for all future services.

The district court separated services by date. The Sixth Circuit preserved the earlier-work exemption and reversed application of the ceiling to later work, thus exempting both periods in these pending cases. Prison officials seek application to awards made after enactment, including earlier work; respondents defend the both-periods exemption. The temporal review does not reopen the underlying prison decrees, every fee-entitlement determination or all PLRA restrictions.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Services completed before April 26, 1996 | Exemption from the new hourly ceiling affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | | Compensation remains governed by the law applicable when the services were performed |
| Services performed after enactment in these pending actions | Exemption reversed, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer | Stevens and Ginsburg | Apply the enacted hourly ceiling and allocate fees by actual service date on remand |

The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded. The district court must allocate compensation by the dates of actual services, retaining the former compensation regime for completed pre-enactment work and applying the new hourly ceiling to later work. Actual compensable hours, amounts and independently applicable statutory predicates remain for ordinary lower proceedings. No final award, mandatory hearing, decree alteration, new prevailing-party finding or reopening of a completed fee judgment is ordered. Unaddressed constitutional objections remain undecided.

#### Opinions and Joins

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–II | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Affirms the earlier-work exemption | Statutory direction and protection of completed pre-enactment services |

The seven full joins adopt the concrete statutory and service-date rules without announcing agreement on every general retroactivity method. Each holding has direct majority support; no reconstruction of a narrower shared rationale is necessary.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The enacted fee language supplies no command repricing completed services

**Holding and operative rule:** The general action-and-award language in original 42 U.S.C. §1997e(d) does not clearly direct application of its new hourly ceiling to legal services completed before April 26, 1996. The separate express transition for prospective relief in PLRA §802 does not itself establish the fee amendment's treatment of every pending action; each provision's operation must be examined.

**Authority:** Thomas's Part I, directly joined by all nine Justices. The statement does not resolve whether the undertaking of representation or each service controls later work; that disagreement is addressed separately.

**Controlling explanation:** Kaiser and Landgraf make congressional temporal direction the starting question. Section 1997e(d) defines covered actions and limits awards, but its reference to an award does not alone identify the activity whose legal treatment changes. Section 802 expressly addresses prospective relief granted before, on or after enactment; §803's fee amendment contains no equivalent instruction. Lindh permits ordinary structural inference without a prescribed verbal formula, yet does not make every adjacent express transition an automatic direction for a provision with a different function. Neither the text nor that comparison commands a reduced hourly return for services already performed. The remaining temporal application therefore requires attention to the concrete services and the change the cap would make. This conclusion rejects neither all fee changes in pending cases nor Congress's authority to prescribe a different temporal rule within constitutional limits.

##### 2. The new ceiling does not govern pre-enactment services

**Holding and operative rule:** The hourly ceiling in original §1997e(d)(3) does not govern legal services completed before the PLRA's April 26, 1996 enactment, even if the fee request or award follows enactment. Those services remain subject to the compensation law governing when they were performed.

**Authority:** Thomas's Part II, directly joined by all nine Justices at this concrete level of application; broader methodological agreement is not inferred.

**Controlling explanation:** The hourly compensation provision addresses the legal work for which reimbursement is sought. Reducing the permissible rate for completed work would change the legal compensation consequences of services already supplied. The later act of issuing an award does not transform those completed services into future conduct. Landgraf protects completed events absent sufficient temporal direction; its application here turns on actual legal work, not the old prison conduct or the mere pendency of litigation. Bradley remains valid in its actual litigation-fee setting, but does not establish that every later fee statute may reprice completed services. The statutory incentive and the earlier fee arrangements support this treatment without establishing an immutable contract for future rates. Requests spanning January 1 through June 30, 1996 must therefore separate earlier services from later ones. The Court does not determine compensable hours, prevailing-party status, an amount, or the treatment of a vested final fee judgment.

##### 3. Later services are subject to the ceiling despite the older action

**Holding and operative rule:** Original §1997e(d)(3)'s hourly ceiling governs services performed after enactment in these already-pending prisoner actions. On remand, the district court must allocate compensation by when the legal work was actually performed, retaining the prior regime for completed pre-enactment work and applying the enacted ceiling to later work.

**Authority:** Thomas's Part III, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Stevens and Ginsburg dissent from this component. No Marks reconstruction supplies the rule.

**Controlling explanation:** A fee statute regulating compensation for later services operates on that later work even though counsel entered the litigation before enactment. The original representation, decree and monitoring-fee orders do not establish an unalterable statutory rate for all work yet to be performed. Counsel's continuing professional obligations deserve consideration, but do not convert every later service into a completed pre-enactment act. This conclusion assumes neither that counsel could withdraw as of right nor that any withdrawal occurred. The new hourly ceiling supplies the compensation rule for work after its enactment; the earlier filing date alone does not remove that work from the statute. Landgraf requires examining the provision's actual operation, and Lindh's provision-specific habeas construction supplies no universal exemption for pending proceedings. The Court accordingly reverses the Sixth Circuit's exemption of later services. It neither changes the underlying decrees nor decides the unaddressed constitutional objections, privately contracted fee entitlements, other distinct fee restrictions, or a statute carrying materially different express temporal direction.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Landgraf v. USI Film Products:** Applies the concrete inquiry into temporal direction and changed consequences for completed services. The existing general-framework holding retains its prior force and original coalition; this decision does not turn service-date agreement into new assent to every methodological proposition.
- **Rivers v. Roadway Express:** Preserves provision-specific construction; a statute's general remedial purpose does not supply retrospective reach for a particular amendment.
- **Kaiser Aluminum & Chemical Corp. v. Bonjorno:** Applies the priority of enacted temporal direction; its fee discussion does not establish an automatic rule choosing either action filing or fee-award date.
- **Bradley v. School Board of Richmond:** Retains its actual fee-legislation result; distinguishes it from using a new hourly ceiling to reduce compensation for already completed work. It is not overruled.
- **Lindh v. Murphy:** Preserves ordinary structural construction and its particular AEDPA application-date rule. The PLRA provisions' different functions prevent automatic transfer of that result to all fees in old prisoner actions.
- **Hughes Aircraft Co. v. United States ex rel. Schumer:** Preserves examination of the real legal change rather than a jurisdictional, procedural or collateral label. No new rule about its distinct private-enforcement facts is announced.

#### Law After Decision

Before this decision the disputed pending-case application of the original PLRA hourly ceiling remained open. Effective June 21, 1999, the hourly-ceiling temporal rule requires service-date allocation in this setting: prior law for completed pre-enactment services and the new ceiling for later services, even in an older action. The original text's independent fee-entitlement and monetary-judgment provisions retain their own predicates and are not newly construed. No cited precedent is overruled; no final fee award is reopened.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, concurring.** Their agreement rests on identification of the activity regulated. Section 1988's fee mechanism supplies an incentive for legal work, and the new hourly ceiling alters compensation for that work. Isolating the later word award would miss the provision's operation. The cap therefore cannot reprice already performed services absent the necessary temporal direction but governs services performed after enactment. Their Landgraf method calls for the relevant activity and sufficient textual direction, rather than a free-standing assessment of expectations. They join the concrete Court opinion because it does not require adoption of the broader methodology. Bradley's actual fee result remains intact, and the concurrence neither freezes future rates under prior monitoring orders nor decides every fee statute by its award date.

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree that the statute does not require repricing completed services and join their protection, but would also retain prior compensation treatment for later work in these existing representations. The relevant completed undertaking is representation in long-running, court-supervised institutional litigation, carrying continuing professional responsibilities. Its consequences cannot be reduced to a series of freely chosen new engagements after enactment. The absence of §802's express pending-matters transition in the fee amendment reinforces that reading, although the difference alone is not dispositive. Their application of Landgraf and Lindh would affirm the Sixth Circuit's exemption for both periods. They announce no immutable contractual right to every future rate and decide no constitutional invalidity, entitlement to an award or change to the prison decrees.

#### Record Limits

The hourly ceiling appears in original 42 U.S.C. §1997e(d)(3); PLRA §802(b)(1) supplies the distinct prospective-relief transition. This decision does not newly construe paragraph (2)'s monetary-judgment contribution provisions or paragraph (4)'s privately paid contractual fees. The record establishes neither an immutable contractual right to future rates nor an actual withdrawal by counsel.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for fee proceedings consistent with the service-date distinction. Compensation for pre-enactment services is determined under the law governing that work; the new hourly ceiling applies to services performed after enactment. The lower court must make the actual allocation and determine otherwise applicable compensation predicates and amounts. The underlying decrees and unreviewed matters remain outside the mandate, including the constitutional objections not reached below. The Court orders no particular fee amount, new evidentiary hearing or alteration of the prison remedies.

### Maryland v. Dyson, No. 98-1062

Reasoned summary merits decision, June 21, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Reasoned summary merits decision, June 21, 1999, after the separate same-date grant of certiorari. There was no plenary argument. The Court reviews 122 Md. App. 413, 712 A.2d 573 (1998), reversing a conviction for conspiracy to possess cocaine with intent to distribute after denial of suppression; Maryland's highest court declined review October 9, 1998.

The state appellate court accepted abundant probable cause but required additional exigency because police had 13–14 hours to obtain a warrant before encountering the moving rental Toyota at 1 a.m. on July 3, 1996. The search found cocaine in a trunk duffel. The question is whether that earlier warrant opportunity creates an additional Fourth Amendment condition.

All nine Justices participated in consideration and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Stevens and Breyer, who would obtain qualified opposing presentation before deciding the merits. Their agreement with the federal automobile rule is not a vote for this immediate merits judgment.

The federal added-exigency ground is rejected. The Court orders no unconditional evidence admission, restoration of the conviction, acquittal, new trial or hearing. #### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Federal review and immediate summary treatment are available here

The final available state appellate judgment conclusively resolves the federal suppression issue, and the State could obtain no later effective review whether further proceedings ended in conviction or acquittal. Cox's third practical-finality category therefore permits §1257 review; the judgment's actual federal automobile ground supplies jurisdiction under Long, without an established adequate independent state basis.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion is joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela agrees with the threshold disposition without joining this rationale.

Labron/Kilgore applies Cox to a binding suppression issue that cannot effectively await later prosecution proceedings. The present opinion expressly relies on the Fourth Amendment requirement of further exigency. A State's ability to give greater protection does not establish that this judgment already rests on such a ground. Long and Evans preserve genuinely independent state law while permitting correction of this actual federal premise.

Rules 15.1 and 16.1 make opposition optional unless ordered in this noncapital matter and permit appropriate summary merits action. Respondent has filed no opposition, and counsel is not a member of this Court's bar and does not wish to become one. The Court regards the supported record and explicit settled-law error as adequate for immediate correction. It treats neither fact as respondent's consent, waiver or default, and creates no general rule dispensing with needed adversarial presentation.

##### Probable cause and ready mobility require no additional emergency

A readily mobile automobile may be searched without a warrant when supported by probable cause; officers need not additionally show unforeseen urgency, imminent departure or insufficient opportunity to obtain a warrant. The accepted probable cause and moving Toyota satisfy that rule for the federal ground on which the state judgment rests, despite the earlier 13–14-hour warrant opportunity.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

Labron/Kilgore directly rejects the added warrant-impracticability condition. Carroll and Chambers establish automobile authority based on its settled predicates; Carney also recognizes the reduced privacy of vehicles in their regulated public setting. Johns makes custody alone insufficient to eliminate that authority. Earlier investigative time cannot restore the extra requirement rejected by those cases. This record supplies both actual automobile movement and accepted abundant probable cause, rather than merely a citation or an unverified suspicion.

The rule does not dispense with probable cause, justify searches of persons, authorize entry onto protected premises or resolve known mechanical incapacity. Knowles's rejection of a citation-only search leaves this independent probable-cause ground intact. No new balancing or exception is required, and this is an application of the rule to the actual erroneous suppression ground rather than an instruction to reconsider the same added condition.

##### The object sought supplies the relevant scope limit

The cocaine object permits the challenged search of a trunk duffel capable of containing it within the justified automobile search. This application creates no authority to search a passenger's person or decide a different known passenger-owned-effect dispute.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela does not join this specific-container application.

Ross limits automobile search scope to places capable of containing the object supported by probable cause. The trunk duffel searched for cocaine satisfies that limit here. Houghton's separate passenger-effects disagreement supplies no additional automobile-exigency requirement, and no such ownership issue is established in this case. The Court neither infers a personal search nor decides a protected-premises or different execution claim. It preserves only genuinely distinct issues that remain properly available; it requires no new factual hearing to reconsider the settled automobile predicates.

The state appellate judgment is reversed and the case remanded consistently with these holdings. Correction of the actual federal error does not automatically establish admissibility on every possible ground or a particular ultimate prosecution result.

**Authority:** Part IV is joined by the seven Justices supporting reversal.

Labron/Kilgore distinguishes application of the federal automobile rule from adjudication of every independent state or search-execution question. The State must follow the federal rule on remand and cannot reimpose the rejected urgency condition as federal law. An actually independent state ground or distinct properly preserved issue may receive its ordinary lawful treatment, but the Court establishes neither its existence nor its merit. No conviction, acquittal, new trial, reopening of a forfeited claim or new hearing is commanded.

### Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc., No. 97-1943

Merits decision, June 22, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 28, 1999; decided June 22. On certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 130 F.3d 893, which affirmed dismissal under Rule 12(b)(6). The questions concern mitigating measures in the original ADA's actual-disability definition, the proper weight of the contrary EEOC Appendix guidance, and the sufficiency of the pleaded regarded-as-working theory.

The twin applicants allege uncorrected right-eye vision of 20/200 or worse and left-eye vision of 20/400 or worse, corrected vision of 20/20, commercial experience and first-class FAA medical certificates. United requires uncorrected visual acuity of 20/100 or better for the hiring at issue. The complaint alleges actual disability substantially limiting seeing and a perceived disability substantially limiting working. The lower court's assumption that United rejected the applicants from all its pilot positions is maintained for this appeal. Those allegations and assumptions are not adjudicated findings of unsafe flight, employer intent or universal airline qualification.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Breyer.

The Court affirms dismissal of both presented coverage theories. Stevens and Breyer would reverse and remand on actual disability without deciding the separate regarded-as application. Stone-Zsela would vacate the adverse coverage judgment and remand, agreeing only that exclusion from the specialized global-airline-pilot position alone does not establish a broad working limitation. The decision orders no remand, employment, reinstatement, accommodation, damages or new evidentiary proceeding and does not validate every airline hiring criterion.

#### Opinions and Joins

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg | Affirms dismissal of both presented theories | Complete opinion: mitigation rule, actual-seeing application, statutory treatment of guidance, and regarded-as-working rule and application |
| Dissent | Stevens | Breyer | Would reverse and remand the actual-disability theory | Underlying-impairment rule; regarded-as application reserved |
| Dissent | Stone-Zsela | Unjoined | Would vacate and remand | Underlying-impairment rule, separate elements of preserved coverage routes and qualification, and narrow agreement with the specialized-position principle |

Stone-Zsela's narrow agreement supplies no join in the Court's complete regarded-as dismissal holding. All controlling propositions have the six direct votes of the Court-opinion coalition; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Holding 1— Measures actually used are part of individual actual-disability assessment

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the original 42 U.S.C. §12102(2)(A), an individual's actual substantial limitation must be assessed with the mitigating measures the individual actually uses, including their beneficial and adverse effects. Effective correction does not erase an impairment or categorically foreclose disability; coverage still depends on an actual substantial limitation of that person's major life activity.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court commands the complete agreement of O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six directly adopt this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The original statute requires an impairment that substantially limits the individual. The existence of an impairment and the magnitude of its present effect are distinct questions. Assessing the person as if an effective measure were absent replaces that individualized inquiry with a hypothetical condition. Conversely, acknowledging correction does not mean that treatment always removes substantial limitation: residual restrictions and adverse effects remain part of the person's actual condition. Bragdon required individual substantial limitation and left this general mitigation issue open because even the treated transmission danger supported Abbott's specific reproductive limitation.

The EEOC Appendix's contrary interpretation does not overcome the enacted individualized limitation requirement. Haggar requires courts to enforce statutory boundaries before accepting an agency construction. The Court therefore need not decide the general extent of EEOC authority to define disability, the force of every regulation or Appendix statement, or whether every term of the regulatory framework warrants deference. The guidance's protective aim and the Act's distinct qualification and accommodation provisions do not eliminate the threshold Congress enacted. Those latter inquiries remain separate once coverage exists.

##### Holding 2 — These allegations do not state an actual substantial limitation of seeing

**Holding and operative rule:** The actual-disability theory fails where these applicants allege corrected vision and visual functioning equivalent to people without their impairment and identify no additional actual substantial limitation. Their severe uncorrected myopia and continuing dependence on corrective lenses do not, on these allegations, establish the required limitation of seeing under the governing mitigation rule.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court-opinion coalition directly adopts this application; Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Breyer reject the beneficial-mitigation premise.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court accepts the severe underlying myopia, the use of lenses and the favorable allegations concerning corrected function. It does not reject myopia as an impairment; United does not press that separate contention. The complaint's own account of normal corrected seeing supplies the decisive distinction from an impairment with actual residual limitations or adverse effects. Constant dependence on lenses, without an alleged actual substantial restriction inconsistent with that normal function, does not supply the missing limitation. The Court makes no finding that all lenses, medication or other treatment eliminate disability.

FAA certification and commercial experience do not establish statutory disability, while United's uncorrected-acuity criterion does not itself prove dangerousness or lawful exclusion. Qualification, accommodation, business necessity and direct threat are distinct matters and need not be decided after the asserted coverage theory fails. No record-of claim or regarded-as-seeing theory is created to replace the theories presented.

##### Holding 3 — The alleged perceived restriction is confined to a specialized job category

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming working is a major life activity, a regarded-as-working claim requires an alleged perceived restriction in a class or broad range of jobs; exclusion from the specialized global-airline-pilot category alleged here does not suffice. The pleaded exclusion remains insufficient even under the assumption that United rejected these applicants for all of its pilot positions.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court-opinion coalition directly adopts the governing rule and this dismissal application. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the specialized-position principle in his separate dissent; Stevens and Breyer leave this application unresolved because their actual-disability disposition would require remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Perceiving an impairment is not necessarily perceiving a substantial limitation of working. The distinction reflected in 29 C.F.R. §1630.2(j) and (l) separates a class or broad range of jobs from a particular specialized occupation. The alleged perception concerns the global-airline-pilot category, while other pilot work provides context for why that category is specialized. The Court does not hold that actual employment elsewhere conclusively proves United's belief, require rejection by every employer, or decide disputed facts about its mental state. The complaint fails because the alleged perceived limitation does not reach the required breadth, even accepting the favorable scope assumption below.

This is a coverage ruling on the theory actually presented. It does not decide whether working invariably qualifies as a major life activity, whether every airline position shares the same qualification, whether United's criterion would be justified against a covered applicant, or whether a different properly presented perceived limitation would satisfy the Act.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Bragdon v. Abbott:** Applies its individualized disability framework and resolves the general mitigation question it expressly reserved. Its reproduction holding, substantial residual-risk application and distinct Title III objective direct-threat rules remain intact.
- **United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. and Chevron:** Preserve actual delegation and statutory fit as conditions of accepting an agency construction. The contrary Appendix interpretation fails the statutory reading adopted here; the Court makes no general decision about ADA definitional authority or every EEOC regulation.

#### Law After Decision

Under the original ADA, actual-disability assessment accounts for mitigating measures actually used, including their beneficial and adverse effects, while continuing to require a substantial limitation of that individual's major life activity. This resolves Bragdon's express mitigation reservation; it neither erases an impairment nor excludes every successfully treated condition. The actual-seeing allegations here fail under that rule.

Assuming working is a major life activity, the regarded-as theory presented here also fails because the alleged perception concerns a specialized global-airline-pilot category rather than a class or broad range of jobs. Separate coverage branches, individual proof, qualification and lawful defenses retain their distinct elements. No precedent is overruled; no general delegation or airline-safety rule is decided.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, joined by Breyer, dissenting.** They would reverse and remand the actual-disability theory because severe underlying myopia can substantially limit seeing even when corrective lenses permit ordinary activity. The statute's protection and accommodation structure separates the existence of a substantially limiting condition from the person's ability to perform a job with assistance. Otherwise effective self-management can remove protection against impairment-based exclusion precisely when it enables work. The administrative guidance and legislative materials persuasively support that reading; the dissent does not require a general holding that all Appendix language has controlling force. Individual substantiality remains necessary, so every person who wears glasses does not automatically qualify. Qualification, accommodation, business necessity, direct threat, causation and relief remain for ordinary proceedings. The dissent does not order hiring or damages and need not decide the separate regarded-as-working application to support its remand.

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** He would vacate the adverse coverage judgment and remand. Beneficial lenses or medication should not eliminate actual-disability coverage where the underlying impairment substantially limits the person's major life activity. Arline's functional protection and Bragdon's individualized method support examining these applicants' severe visual limitation while retaining proof of substantiality; the construction does not cover every eyeglass wearer. Actual functioning remains relevant to qualification, accommodation and supported safety defenses, which cannot erase the separate coverage inquiry. He agrees that exclusion from the global-airline-pilot position alone does not establish inability to work in a broad class of occupations. The actual seeing theory nevertheless requires assessment under the proper mitigation rule, and any preserved regarded-as theory must satisfy its own elements. He would award no automatic hiring, reinstatement or accommodation.

#### Record Limits

The governing provisions are the original 42 U.S.C. §§12102(2), 12111(8) and 12112, with the relevant 29 C.F.R. §1630.2 definitions and Appendix guidance. FAA certification does not establish the validity of every employer qualification, and an employer's safety contention is not a finding of actual risk.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Tenth Circuit's judgment affirming Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal remains effective. Supreme Court merits review is complete, subject to ordinary mandate procedure. No further factual inquiry, amended pleading, new trial or adjudication of the employer's safety and qualification defenses is directed.
