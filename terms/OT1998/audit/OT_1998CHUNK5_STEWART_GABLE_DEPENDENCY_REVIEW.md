## Chronology and assignment treatment

Same-day peers share their legal entering baseline. The alphabetical sequence used for opinion counting does not turn those peers into substantive precedents for one another. Stewart's certiorari order and application/summary disposition do not count as plenary leads. A signed lead after Gable's post-grant merits submission is counted according to its actual opinion table; its earlier effective date affects counts for later new assignments, not completed assignment choices.

Jackson's and Reform's original internal descriptions that Gable was stopped record the state when those Records were assembled. They are not continuing legal findings and are superseded for present navigation by the new Gable Record and refreshed workspace. Their Public Projections contain no holding that depends on the continued absence of a Gable decision. The original Stewart certiorari Record likewise describes the then-unresolved separate components without purporting to prohibit a later same-date disposition. Preserving those completed Records therefore requires no silent revision.

No preexisting result or author would change. No material-dependence blocker was found. This conclusion depends on keeping the resumed Records within the frozen grounds and the approved Stone positions; a materially different merits holding, remedy, execution fact, or method-law rule would require a new review.
