Claims against the county and sheriff were dismissed with prejudice. NAGE asserted similarly affected members. The district court accepted standing and ripeness but rejected the merits.

### Issue 1: Standing and preserved claims

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Hunt and United Food v. Brown Group permit representative prospective litigation where a member has a concrete redressable injury and individualized participation is unnecessary. Love rejects the premise that private arms possession has no constitutional protection.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The conditional reinstatement agreement connects favorable federal relief to Hiley’s employment injury. Love changes the legal premise used to abandon heightened review, but it does not establish that the abandonment was preserved for appeal.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III; Fifth Amendment; ordinary preservation rules.

### Issue 2: Personal disability and equal protection

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Love v. Pepersack (December 11, 1995) recognizes a personal arms right, incorporation and necessary acquisition without militia enrollment. Hale (February 12, 1996) and Rybar (November 24, 1997) reject both automatic military-utility immunity and affiliation-based exclusion. Their arms remands leave ultimate machinegun applications open; no comprehensive scrutiny tier, automatic-weapons exclusion or mandatory evidentiary hearing was adopted.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A qualifying domestic-violence conviction supplies a different record from an ordinary law-abiding possessor’s claim. Employment and statutory restoration classifications remain distinct from the constitutional scope of arms protection.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§921(a)(33),922(g)(9),925(a)(1); Lewis; Cleburne; Heller v. Doe (1993).

### Issue 3: Commerce and state administration

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lopez requires an identified connection; the machinegun decisions recognize a fitted anti-evasion route but do not decide §922(g)(9). New York and Printz distinguish direct regulation from compelled state administration.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The government must connect the possession disability to the federal prohibition on supplying firearms to qualifying domestic abusers. The sheriff’s employment choice is not a federal command to administer background checks or dismiss an employee.
* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses; Tenth Amendment.

### Issue 4: Prospective regulation and punishment

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The Ex Post Facto Clause concerns retrospective criminal punishment; the bill-of-attainder inquiry examines legislative punishment without judicial trial, including purpose, history and functional effects.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A prior conviction can identify the class for prospective regulation without creating a new sentence for the earlier offense. The employment burden is relevant but not dispositive of punishment.
* **Other Governing Law:** Landgraf; Selective Service System v. Minnesota PIRG; Nixon v. Administrator of General Services; U.S. Const. art. I, §9.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Eleventh Circuit affirmed on the reasons of National Association of Government Employees, Inc. v. Barrett, 968 F. Supp. 1564 (N.D. Ga. July 2, 1997), dismissing the constitutional action. The appeal is not a firearm prosecution.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Hiley argues that the federal disability removes armed employment, treats misdemeanants less favorably than some felons and retrospectively burdens a prior conviction. The government invokes prospective public-safety regulation, adjudicated domestic violence, the statutory interstate limitation and ordinary state restoration differences.
