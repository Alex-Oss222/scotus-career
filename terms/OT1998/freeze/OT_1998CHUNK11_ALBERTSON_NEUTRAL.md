### Event, questions, parties and relief

Albertson’s petitions from the Ninth Circuit’s reversal of employer summary judgment. Kirkingburg was the employee plaintiff and lower-court appellant; Albertson’s was the defendant and lower-court appellee. The employer seeks reversal and restoration of judgment in its favor, relying on the disability and qualification issues. The employee seeks preservation of the reversal and further proceedings. The supplied questions fairly identify individualized actual disability and the effect of federal vision standards and the waiver program, but are issue summaries rather than certified verbatim grant language.

The lower-court judgment is a summary-judgment reversal and remand, not an adjudicated damages award. The lower dissent characterizes the suit as seeking damages for the November 1992 termination, not reinstatement (¶50); the majority also addresses later refusal to reconsider. Do not transform that refusal into a separately adjudicated failure-to-rehire claim or order hiring. If relief turns on a distinct post-waiver claim, the actual complaint and preservation would require controlled supplementation. The ordinary supported paths below do not require that enlargement.

### Source-supported facts and their status

The permitted lower opinion, [albertson-lower-clean.txt](../runtime/chunk11-neutral-sources/albertson-lower-clean.txt), supplies the following recitations and reasoning. They remain subject to its summary-judgment posture.

- Kirkingburg drove commercially since 1979, had one accident not attributed to him and no moving citations, and passed Albertson’s 16-mile driving test before his 1990 hiring. Amblyopia left uncorrectable 20/200 acuity in his left eye; the right eye was 20/20 with correction. Earlier physicians certified him despite failure to meet the bilateral federal standard. The source does not explain that error conclusively (¶2; note 2).
- Following a nondriving injury and nearly a year away, a November 1992 examination correctly identified the visual deficiency and refused certification. Albertson’s required recertification after lengthy absence (¶3).
- Kirkingburg applied for an FHWA vision waiver; Albertson’s stated it would accept only drivers meeting or exceeding the ordinary DOT standard and terminated him. **Several months later he actually obtained a waiver**, notified the employer, and the employer again rejected it and declined reconsideration (¶6). The packet’s statement that the program did not itself confer a waiver cannot be used to deny this actual later issuance. Application, issuance, termination and later refusal are separate events; no exact issuance day is established by this source.
- The lower court described effects on depth perception and peripheral vision and subconscious compensation. That is evidence relevant to individual substantial limitation, not a finding that every monocular person meets the ADA definition (¶15).
- The dissent reports an offered alternative job that Kirkingburg rejected and his proposed leave to obtain a waiver (¶¶44, 52). The majority supplies no full terms or definitive accommodation ruling. Neither offer nor rejection proves reasonable accommodation or resolves every remedy on this packet.

### Reasoning below, alternatives and unresolved issues

The majority held that the evidence permitted disability because the manner of seeing differed significantly, including permanent impairment and compensating mechanisms. It also found a genuine issue on the **alternative regarded-as route**, citing a manager’s description of Kirkingburg as “blind in one eye or legally blind” (¶19). That alternative must not disappear if actual disability is rejected. The employer may answer that perception of impairment is not necessarily perception of substantial limitation; the source does not adjudicate that response under entering Supreme Court law. A disability-only disposition must specify the treatment of the alternative ground and review scope.

For qualification, the majority distinguished ability to perform driving duties from the legality of the employer’s job prerequisites. It treated the waiver program as part of the federal scheme, rejected reliance on the later 1994 invalidation to explain earlier conduct, and treated the agency’s safety determinations as defeating the employer’s categorical threat argument. It reserved further evidentiary issues and the question whether private litigation could collaterally challenge the program under the Hobbs Act (¶¶20–39). Those conclusions are the contested reasoning below, **not neutral factual findings of zero risk or a conclusive federal safety certification**.

Judge Rymer dissented because the ordinary federal vision regulation remained unchanged and the waiver program was experimental. She emphasized that in 1992 FHWA lacked the statutory safety finding, that the D.C. Circuit later invalidated the program on that basis, and that 1994 agency conclusions could not supply knowledge for the 1992 termination. The majority characterized the administrative defect differently and stressed later revalidation. The competing accounts of the program’s legal significance and timing must remain visible. The source’s statutory/regulatory quotations and the dissent’s contemporaneous Federal Register quotation support this legal fork without converting a later judicial or agency event into knowledge available at termination.

### Law immediately entering this event

1. **Actual disability.** Section 12102(2)(A) requires an impairment substantially limiting a major life activity of the individual; (B) and (C) supply distinct record-of and regarded-as routes. The lower source reproduces the operative definition and 29 C.F.R. §1630.2 provisions on seeing, comparative condition/manner/duration and nature, severity, duration and long-term impact (¶¶10–15). Bragdon requires individual substantial limitation and expressly leaves the general treatment of mitigating measures open. The mere distinction between seeing with one eye and two cannot substitute for the statutory degree of limitation; conversely, adaptation and successful driving do not themselves prove absence of a substantial limitation in seeing. Whether natural compensation must be included, and how the actual evidence measures substantiality, remain questions to decide.
2. **Exact operative Standards entry:** “Actual disability and substantial limitation of reproduction”; **Present operation effective: 1998-06-25**; authority: Bragdon Holdings 1–2. Current wording: “HIV disease is a physical impairment before its most serious symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity. Actual-disability coverage still requires a substantial limitation of that individual’s activity; diagnosis alone is insufficient. Substantial reproductive limitation does not require complete inability to conceive, a pre-infection plan, attempted pregnancy or willingness to incur the supported transmission risk.” No distinct Current wording authority is listed. Its reproduction-specific application does not decide monocular sight or general mitigation.
3. **Qualification and objective safety.** Sections 12111(8), 12112(a) and 12113 keep disability, essential functions with reasonable accommodation, and defenses distinct. The lower source quotes the employment prohibition, direct-threat definition and regular vision requirement. The standard requires at least 20/40 acuity in each eye with or without correction and binocular 20/40 (note 1). Program participation did not amend that general rule; whether an employer must accommodate or accept this experimental waiver is disputed. The Court must not assume all federal waivers, all employer-created standards, or all safety restrictions have the same legal character.
4. **Exact operative Standards entry:** “Objective assessment of an ADA direct threat”; **Present operation effective: 1998-06-25**; authority: Bragdon Holdings 3–4. The exact current wording is reproduced in the slice. It requires significant risk that reasonable modification or aids cannot eliminate, an individualized reasonable judgment using current medical knowledge or best objective evidence, conduct-date information and bounded, nonconclusive weight for founded public-health judgments. No separate Current wording authority is listed. Bragdon adjudicated Title III health-care exclusion; its objective method is relevant, but it does not automatically convert binding commercial-driver qualifications into employer-created risk assessments or decide this waiver’s legal force.
5. **Current-term authority:** Cleveland, May 24, 1999, unanimously preserves the ADA’s independent qualification inquiry and ordinary proof. Its special adequate-explanation requirement is triggered by apparently inconsistent earlier SSDI representations; no such representation is established here. It adds no presumption that this employee is qualified and resolves no mitigation or DOT-waiver question.

### Strongest lawful paths and boundaries

- **Employer regulatory/qualification path:** Restore summary judgment if lawful reliance on the operative federal qualification is independently dispositive despite this experimental waiver, with disability left open or separately addressed only as needed. Strongest challenge: an actual lawful waiver and proof of skilled driving may remove the federal obstacle, leaving the employer’s refusal an independent screen that requires ADA justification. The path must explain the waiver’s legal effect and timing, not deny its existence.
- **Employee statutory/individualized path:** Preserve the remand if the employer cannot treat every waiver holder as unqualified or dangerous and the supported evidence presents triable statutory elements. Strongest challenge: the unchanged general federal rule and absence of a contemporaneous statutory safety finding may make ordinary reliance permissible without an independent employer threat trial. A remand does not establish liability, damages, accommodation or employment entitlement.
- **Disability correction path:** Require individual substantiality and decide the treatment of natural compensation; if the lower standard was insufficient, identify whether record application is possible or remand is needed. Strongest challenge to categorical noncoverage is Bragdon’s individual method and the source’s actual depth/peripheral limitations. Strongest challenge to categorical coverage is that difference in manner alone may not satisfy “substantially.” Preserve or expressly resolve the regarded-as alternative and any scope limit.
- **Bounded procedural path:** Reserve an unnecessary collateral-validity, new claim or exact-waiver-term issue rather than inventing facts. The lower majority expressly left Hobbs Act jurisdiction open. Missing research is not a party’s failure of proof and supplies no independent judgment ground.
