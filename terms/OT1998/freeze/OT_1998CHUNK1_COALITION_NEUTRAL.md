### Issue 1: Political process and ordinary state authority

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Romer preserves Hunter v. Erickson and Washington v. Seattle School District as racial political-process precedents while rejecting a universal right to favorable legislation at every governmental level. Crawford permits withdrawal of voluntary measures at its actual scope.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The disputed provision both changes substantive policy and reserves restoration of racial preferences to constitutional amendment. The question is whether that selective relocation burdens minority interests within the existing allocation of authority; merely calling the substantive measure a preference does not answer it.
* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; Hunter; Seattle; Crawford; James v. Valtierra.

### Issue 2: Permissible repair is not compulsory preference

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Engineering Contractors preserves Croson strict scrutiny for local racial preferences and distinct demanding intermediate scrutiny for sex classifications. Hopwood requires a supported justification and fitted repair; Adarand retains its distinct federal rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Some preferences may be invalid and none becomes mandatory merely because it is permitted. But the constitutional validity of an underlying program and the validity of a discriminatory political restructuring are separate issues.
* **Other Governing Law:** Title VII; United States v. Virginia; Croson.

### Issue 3: Preemption and interim relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Title VII permits certain voluntary remedial action under Weber and Johnson but does not make every permissible measure federally required. Its savings and preemption provisions require attention to actual conflict.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** An existing federal duty or binding remedy cannot be defeated by state law, while permission for voluntary action alone does not establish facial preemption of the entire initiative. Preliminary relief requires a supported claim and actual impending injury.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §§2000e-2(j),2000e-7,2000h-4; Supremacy Clause.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** Ninth Circuit vacatur of the Northern District of California preliminary injunction against Proposition 209, with the stay request denied as moot and the case remanded.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Challengers invoke Hunter and Seattle’s prohibition on selectively moving racial remedies out of ordinary political channels, and Title VII’s recognition of voluntary affirmative action. The State invokes equal treatment, statewide policymaking authority, Crawford and the distinction between permission and a duty to prefer.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A facial declaration that every affirmative measure must remain available is not required to assess selective political restructuring. The court can sustain or reject preliminary relief on the constitutional theory independently of statutory preemption, with relief matched to the affected provisions.
