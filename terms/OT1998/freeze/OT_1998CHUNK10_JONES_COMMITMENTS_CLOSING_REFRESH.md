## Reassessed competing paths

**Contextual no error.** The actual argument supports reading 3(B) as the victim's physical and situational vulnerability in the offense and 3(C) as personal/family loss. These are understandable relevant selection subjects under Tuilaepa and §3593(a)/Payne. Their validity does not require the prosecutor to enact a missing legal definition if the delivered ordinary terms, record context and directions already identify constitutionally permissible subjects. Argument is then evidence of the relevant context and presentation, not an authoritative cure for an otherwise standardless instruction. The anti-counting direction addresses the claim that two headings necessarily required two units of aggravating weight. A no-error conclusion on the presented vagueness/overlap challenge does not declare that all conceivable victim characteristics aggravate a crime or that an unexamined passage could never present an independently preserved forbidden-use problem.

**Continuing breadth/overlap objection.** The charge did not say vulnerability, personal characteristics can encompass the age/stature/background listed in 3(B), and the jury made two distinct aggravating findings. The favorable descriptions might be relevant to loss, but an undefined characteristic factor must not authorize adverse comparative worth. The actual argument is therefore relevant without conclusively narrowing the legal task. The strongest response is that contextually understandable selection subjects need neither narrow eligibility definitions nor fixed aggravating weights; overlapping evidence is not itself an unconstitutional weighing rule. The objection cannot prevail solely because a prosecutor rather than a judge supplied the quoted explanation, and the defense of the factors cannot prevail solely because permissible evidence was admitted.

**Assumed error and cure.** If a factor is held or assumed invalid, the passages can inform its operation and the scope of any defect. They do not automatically establish that the jury used only a constitutionally valid narrowed version. Neither distinct purposes nor the anti-counting instruction establishes beyond a reasonable doubt that the invalid legal weighting made no contribution. Conversely, the prosecutor's discussion of both factors does not establish harmful contribution. The excerpts establish no count, frequency, comparative emphasis, exclusivity or dominance; none is inferred in either direction.

The existing Fifth Circuit analysis remains legally deficient on the assumed-error premise for reasons independently visible in its opinion: it treats reweighing and harmlessness as interchangeable, says the statute supplies no standard, and uses remaining eligibility/support for death as the principal explanation of cure. The quoted passages do not repair those propositions or establish that the appellate court performed a materially different actual-contribution inquiry. Section 3595 supplies a Government burden, not a federal appellate power to choose death anew when that burden fails. A remand for that legally required inquiry is supported by the lower court's demonstrated method, **not by the operator's lack of a complete closing transcript**.

No refreshed Justice directly determines ultimate harmlessness or harmfulness of an assumed invalid factor. A proper court may assess the complete actual record and make supported inferences; the supplied excerpts are not disqualified because incomplete. Nor must every harmlessness case contain an exact count of references or an exclusive equivalent finding. If a proposed direct cure depends on exclusivity, comparative emphasis or a particular surrounding qualification, that additional proposition requires its own verified source; the present legal remand does not depend on it.
