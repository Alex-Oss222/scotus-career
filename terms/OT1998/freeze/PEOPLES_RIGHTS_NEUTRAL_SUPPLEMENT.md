**Text and component completeness:** The five section 2323.11(G) definitions are distinct. The rifle/carbine category concerns semiautomatic center-fire weapons accepting a detachable magazine of at least twenty rounds; the shotgun category concerns magazine capacity exceeding six rounds. The handgun category separately covers modification of the referenced rifle, modification of an automatic firearm, and original design for a detachable magazine exceeding twenty rounds. Restoration under (G)(4) and the two branches of parts/conversion/readily-assembled weapons under (G)(5) require separate analysis, including their dependencies on (G)(1)-(3). The opinion reproduces the full text; no paraphrase here replaces it for an exact-text holding.

**Exceptions and scienter:** The separate large-capacity-magazine definition in section 2323.11(F) concerns more than twenty rounds and excludes magazines designed only for .22-caliber rimfire cartridges. The section 2323.32(B)(2) exception has more than an NFA-registration branch: it also contains the former-city-registration branch and an inoperable/inert trophy, souvenir, curio or museum-piece branch, with the condition that the firearm cannot readily be made operable or activated. Do not omit the inert-object branch or turn the NFA branch into a requirement that each magazine be registered or fit the registered firearm. The published text briefly mislabels the magazine provision (B)(3) in its discussion, while the opening and disposition identify (B)(2); retain correct section identification and the complete reproduced text.

Section 2323.31(A) omits an express mental state, whereas section 2323.32(A) expressly requires knowing magazine possession. The general default rule in section 2301.21(B) makes recklessness sufficient unless the offense plainly indicates strict liability. Whether the weapons prohibition plainly indicates strict liability is the lower court's construction, not literal text of a statutory no-scienter command. The City's first invocation of that default on appeal and the lower court's alternative conclusion that recklessness would not cure the notice problem must be kept distinct.

**Supported disputes and competing path:** Rifle capacity may turn on possessed, originally supplied, commercially available or merely possible magazines; shotgun capacity varies with shell length. Handgun design history, restoration capability and parts assembly create separate notice disputes. The prior ordinance's thirty-day November 1989 registration window and later expansion in the replacement ordinance matter to grandfather rationality. The dissent's strongest contrary path is ripeness and narrowing through Ohio courts, including a scienter requirement and readily available magazines, with reliance as a rational grandfather distinction. No authoritative narrowing Ohio construction is established; do not invent one.

No general assault-weapon exclusion or comprehensive scrutiny tier enters from that phrasing.
