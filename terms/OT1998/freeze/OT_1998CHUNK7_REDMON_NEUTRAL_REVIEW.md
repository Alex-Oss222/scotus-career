## Record distinctions

The neutral packet?s phrase that no controlling factual finding resolves curtilage must mean **no distinct controlling legal classification**, not absence of physical-layout or access findings. The majority expressly says the district court did not use the word curtilage (paragraph 11), considers proximity, use, enclosure and screening, and concludes its decision passes those criteria (paragraph 23). The majority treats the reasonable-expectation conclusion as fact-specific and expressly rejects a universal garage-side-garbage rule. The separate opinions disagree about whether the majority implicitly resolves curtilage and about its legal relevance. Evans expressly finds the location outside curtilage; the dissents find it inside. Those are competing legal classifications, not equally authoritative trial findings.

The published factual recitation establishes an end unit in an eight-unit townhouse row. Redmon and the adjacent neighbor share a roughly twenty-five-foot-wide driveway extending roughly twenty-four feet from the attached garages to a four-foot public sidewalk, then less than ten additional feet to the road. Access to either front door requires proceeding up that driveway and then taking a separate walk around the respective garage corner. Redmon?s front door faces the intersecting street. The closed cans were placed between the two garage doors for collection and ordinarily returned inside after pickup; extra plastic bags were sometimes placed outside. A city ordinance prohibited curbside placement. The officers removed contents on January 4, January 22 and March 14, 1996. They used the recovered cocaine-related material to obtain the later residential warrant. Those facts may be used without inventing a missing layout finding or ordering reconstruction of photographs.

The majority characterizes the route as unobstructed and used by the two households? visitors and ordinary callers; that supports a lawful-access argument. It does not establish that ordinary visitors inspect closed cans or possess unlimited permission to do so. The Posner dissent says no home windows were visible from the can location and identifies the precise mechanics of garbage collection as unspecified. Those are respectively a published source characterization and a limit, not proof that a particular collection method occurred. The source does not establish a fence, enclosure, exclusive driveway, all-purpose-container claim remaining live, or actual rummaging by neighbors. Counsel?s oral description of ?some sort of abandonment? is accompanied by the contention that abandonment is not the constitutional inquiry; it is not a concession of legality.

## Supported alternatives and remedies

Greenwood may be applied through actual public accessibility and placement for collection, with location and domestic use still evaluated under Dunn; mere possible access and general discard intent are not self-executing answers. The competing path protects closed garage-side containers against investigative rummaging where the actual site and access establish protected residential space. A reviewing court can apply law to supported facts; absence of a separately labeled curtilage finding does not automatically compel a new evidentiary hearing. An express legal remand, if chosen, must explain the legal error it corrects rather than claim a blank record.

The conditional plea preserved suppression. The separate sentencing issue in paragraphs 24?29 was not included in the supplied simulated grant and does not become a new merits component. Any suppression remedy must address the later warrant and actual causal grounds; a change in the search analysis alone does not automatically order acquittal, release or a new sentence. The known layout remains available on remand; neither missing source images nor disagreement among judges establishes litigant default or absent evidence.
