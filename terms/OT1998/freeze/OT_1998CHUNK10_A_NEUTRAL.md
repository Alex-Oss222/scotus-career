## 2. Schleifer v. City of Charlottesville — June15

### Event, posture and exact enactment

Assigned simulated merits event: June15,1999; simulated Supreme Court docket 98-1146. Review concerns the final merits judgment affirmed at 159 F.3d 843 (4th Cir. October20,1998), not merely the preliminary-injunction decision at 963 F.Supp.534. Plaintiffs withdrew the Fourth Amendment claims. The assigned March22 grant, April28 argument and June15 event are simulation premises supplied in Section I. The questions concern minors' nighttime public liberty, fit-parent authority, and the exceptions' notice, enforcement guidance and expression protection. Plaintiffs seek declaratory and injunctive relief; no damages award or criminal conviction is before the Court.

Official lower opinion: https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/Published/971723.P.pdf; cross-check: https://static.case.law/f3d/159/html/0843-01.html. The complete contemporaneous ordinance appears in the judicial appendix, printed pp.20–26, and is preserved in `../research/OT_1998CHUNK10_A_SCHLEIFER_ORDINANCE.md`. The full authenticated lower-record memo is `../research/OT_1998CHUNK10_A_LOWER_SOURCE_MEMO.md`, Schleifer section. These exact sources govern over the original packet's short paraphrase.

The ordinance regulates unemancipated persons under17 from 12:01–5a.m. Monday–Friday and 1–5a.m. Saturday–Sunday. It reaches remaining in public places, covered motor vehicles, and profit-operated establishments inviting the public. Remaining means lingering or staying, or failing to leave on a proper request; a dispersal command is not invariably required. Public place includes specified common areas. Parent includes qualifying biological/adoptive parents and guardians, and an authorized adult18 or older in loco parentis. Judicial emancipation is specifically defined.

All eight exceptions matter: parent accompaniment; defined emergencies; employment and direct travel home/to work; the sidewalk abutting the residence with a parent; adult-supervised activities sponsored by school, religious, civic, public or similar entities, with direct travel; a parent-directed errand with the required signed writing; interstate travel through, beginning or ending in the city; and exercising First Amendment rights, with religion, speech and assembly given as examples. The errand writing must identify parent and minor, parent address and telephone/reachable telephone, signature, description, destinations and authorized hours. The exception does not say general parental permission is sufficient. Emergency means an unforeseen circumstance or resulting condition requiring immediate action to safeguard life, limb or property; its listed examples are nonexclusive.

Parents, motor-vehicle owners/operators and establishment operators/employees have distinct knowledge-based offenses. Prompt police notification that a minor refuses to leave supplies an express defense to the establishment offense, not a universal defense to every subsection. A separate provision prohibits false identifying information. Do not transfer adults' scienter language to the minor's presence offense.

Before enforcement against a minor the officer must immediately investigate whether the presence violates subsection(b). A first violation brings verbal and mailed written warnings; an earlier warning for any such violation triggers a charge and summons. The ordinance also requires release to a parent or bounded temporary care as soon as practicable, including on the first-warning route. Care must be nonlocked, nonrestrictive, visually supervised and without handcuffing/securing to objects, and cannot exceed the remaining curfew hours. Specified refusal, unavailable-parent or parent-nonacceptance conditions permit nonsecure crisis/shelter or juvenile-court procedures. Each violation is a Class4 misdemeanor; no unsupported fine amount is added. The one-year City Manager reporting requirement is not a sunset or proof that a report actually issued.

### Material record and competing grounds below

The plaintiffs include minors, parents, and an older associate wishing to socialize with younger friends. Desired lawful activities included movies, meals, music, socializing and returning from concerts; these are allegations, not findings that every activity falls outside every exception. The City offered local crime and victimization data, testimony concerning late-night drug activity and juvenile vulnerability, and experiences elsewhere. The majority cited a 25% rise in juvenile delinquency caseload over1991–1996 and overnight juvenile-offense increases; these recitations are evidence presented and credited, not independent proof that a curfew causes a reduction. Plaintiffs challenged fit, crime timing, exclusion of17-year-olds, and other sources of juvenile harm.

The lower majority used intermediate scrutiny and independently held that the law survived strict scrutiny. It relied on the narrow hours/age range, protection of children and others, and exceptions. It distinguished family custody and educational decisions in addressing parental authority. On vagueness it found a clear core, read civic and emergency wording contextually, and treated unsettled hypotheticals as potential as-applied claims. The dissent demanded strict scrutiny, disputed tailoring and displacement of fit-parent permission, and treated the First Amendment exception as part of the criminal boundary rather than a complete answer to chilling and discretion.

The police chief gave uncertain answers concerning political conversation in a coffeehouse and an unpaid musician in a restaurant; those were hypotheticals, not arrests. The chief also testified that doubts would favor protected activity and that legal advice would be sought. Preserve both sides of that testimony. An administrative assurance is not automatically a binding narrowing construction. No controlling state narrowing judgment or severability holding is established by these sources.

### Refreshed entering law and supported alternatives

Use `../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK10_A_SCHLEIFER_CURRENT.md`. Morales, June10, controls the independent need for meaningful enforcement guidance; a warning cannot cure a standardless predicate. Its holding concerns a defect common to every dispersal order and adopts no unlimited right to remain in public. PRO, October15, distinguishes difficult factual application from an absent central legal criterion. Unlike Morales's apparent-purpose trigger, this ordinance supplies age, time and place rules; whether the expression exception leaves an essential criminal boundary to uncontrolled judgment must be analyzed on this enactment, not answered by analogy alone.

Reno v.ACLU, June26,1997, controls its actual content-based network-speech rule and the limit on judicial rewriting; it does not establish that every juvenile activity restriction receives the same scrutiny. The exact current Standard is Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions, effective June26,1997. Glucksberg's current Standard is Treatment refusal, suicide assistance and class-wide constitutional claims, effective June26,1997. That holding does not adopt an exclusive most-specific-history method for all liberties. Saenz, May17, protects equal citizenship in ordinary welfare treatment; it is not a holding about local nighttime movement.

The family Standards Proof before a potentially indefinite complete parental-contact cutoff (A.St.P.C., November20,1995) and Protection against serious avoidable harm in compulsory custody transitions (Baby Richard, June21,1996) recognize the distinct interests of parent and child in their own narrow settings. They do not furnish a curfew proof burden, parental veto, mandated hearing, or constitutional custody redesign. Surviving predivergence Prince, Meyer, Pierce, Bellotti, Stanglin, Grayned, Kolender, Papachristou and Hoffman Estates supply the protective-authority, parental, associational and vagueness questions. Do not treat every citation in the lower opinion to a postdivergence historical Supreme Court decision as in-world authority.

Supported paths for independent testing: (a) uphold on sufficiently intelligible objective triggers and protective tailoring, addressing the lower alternative strict-scrutiny ground, direct parental burden and expression exception separately; the strongest objection is that police classification of protected activity and the limited errand exception may leave substantial lawful conduct exposed despite clear age/time terms; (b) invalidate on an identified notice/enforcement or expression defect without deciding all substantive-liberty questions; the strongest objection is the real objective core and facial-review limits, which require showing more than marginal uncertainty; (c) if the wording survives, decide the precise juvenile-liberty and parent-authority claims under a justified standard, with a separately explained fit analysis. Finding an important interest does not prove tailoring, and finding an uncertain margin does not by itself prove a common-trigger defect.

Any narrower relief must identify the invalid text/application and a lawful severability basis. Deleting a protective exception may enlarge criminal liability and is not an automatic cure. Withdrawn Fourth Amendment claims remain withdrawn. No categorical right to remain anywhere at night, absolute parental exemption, new offense or binding municipal construction is presumed.

## 3. NASA v. FLRA — June17

### Identity, review scope and record

Docket98-369; assigned merits event June17,1999; supplied argument anchor March23. The Eleventh Circuit enforced the Authority's order,120F.3d1208(1997). The assigned question asks whether the investigator is a representative of the agency under5U.S.C.§7114(a)(2)(B). The public petitioners' merits brief expressly presents an additional dependent question: if the OIG examination is covered, whether NASA Headquarters commits an unfair labor practice by failing to require OIG compliance. The below judgment and requested reversal include both components. Preserve HQ responsibility as a distinct subsidiary disposition issue rather than silently equating the two; the controller should confirm the intended simulated grant includes the supplied petition questions if it is narrower than ordinary review of that judgment.

In January1993 the FBI supplied NASA-OIG with information about documents linking a Marshall Space Flight Center employee to threats of violence against coworkers. Before the interview the OIG determined that no criminal action would be taken against this employee. The employee requested counsel and union representation; both were allowed. The agent limited the union representative to observing, restricted interruption, and threatened cancellation if the representative rejected the ground rules. The employee faced dismissal if he refused to answer. The Authority found impermissible restriction of representation; OIG did not appeal that particular interference determination. Reasonable fear of discipline and a representation request are undisputed in the lower account.

The ALJ found OIG responsible but recommended dismissal as to Headquarters. The Authority reversed the latter part, holding both responsible and ordering compliance, notices and HQ direction to OIG. The Eleventh Circuit considered HQ objections despite their nonpresentation to the Authority, identifying extraordinary circumstances under§7123(c) because of the Authority's sua-sponte treatment and same-day enforcement application. It rejected procedural unfairness and sustained the limited compliance direction. That finding is not a general exemption from administrative preservation rules. The merits petition maintains the statutory HQ objection.

### Statutory text, interpretation and limits

The official1994 Code text for§7114,§7103 and§7112 is recovered in `../research/OT_1998CHUNK10_A_USC1994_5-7114.txt`, `_5-7103.txt`, `_5-7112.txt`. Section7114(a)(2)(B) requires an opportunity for the exclusive representative to be represented at an examination of a unit employee by a representative of the agency in connection with an investigation when the employee reasonably believes discipline may result and requests representation. Section7103(a)(3) defines agency by executive-agency status with listed exclusions; NASA is not one of them. Section7112(b)(7) separately excludes specified audit/investigative employees from an appropriate bargaining unit; exclusion from unit membership is not textually an exemption of every investigation from§7114.

The Inspector General Act creates an independent and objective unit within the establishment, with presidential appointment/removal, general supervision by the head or next-ranking officer, no supervision by other officers, and protection against the head preventing/prohibiting initiation, conduct or completion of an audit or investigation. Reporting obligations to Congress and reporting suspected federal criminal violations to the Attorney General reinforce the independence argument. The Government's public merits appendix reproduces the operative IG Act. Independent investigation, membership in the agency, and responsibility for statutory compliance are distinct concepts.

The statutory right has a limited representational function under surviving Weingarten,420U.S.251: assistance need not convert questioning into adversary adjudication, require bargaining at the interview, or allow obstruction; the employer may insist on the employee's own account. This does not negate the already sustained finding that reducing the representative to a silent observer interfered with the right if the statute covers this investigator. This record involves administrative consequences, not a determination of every criminal interview, other-agency investigator, FBI inquiry or negotiated extension of representational rights.

### Refreshed law and competing supported paths

Use `../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK10_A_NASA_CURRENT.md`. NFFE/FLRA v.Interior,March3, rejects automatic deference or a judicially invented categorical rule; statutory boundaries precede reasonable explained agency choice. The Court's majority includes Stevens,Kennedy,Souter,Ginsburg,Breyer; O'Connor,Scalia,Thomas's published dissent relies on the particular agreement-directed statutory structure, not an all-purpose opposition to agency interpretation. Haggar,April21, and unanimous merits holdings in Aguirre-Aguirre,May3, preserve delegation, reasonable construction and application as distinct questions. Zurko,June10, addresses factual-review standards, not this statutory meaning; it cannot transform every agency question into deferential factfinding.

Path supporting the order: the chapter's definition and the OIG's work within NASA allow an agency-representative construction, and the two enactments can operate together because protection from management interference does not necessarily exempt OIG from federal representation law. Headquarters' general supervision may permit an instruction to obey the statute without control of investigative results. Strongest contrary argument: representative is a relational term in a labor-management statute, not a synonym for every employee of an executive agency; independent investigatory mandates and confidentiality could make management-linked representation and HQ liability incompatible with the IG Act.

Path opposing coverage: the term in context identifies management representatives in the bargaining relationship, excluding the independent inspector; because the coverage predicate fails, both OIG and derivative HQ orders fail. Strongest contrary argument: the statute says any examination and defines agency broadly, while the unit exclusion and independence clauses do not expressly withdraw the interview protection; an employee's disciplinary vulnerability persists. Legislative purpose alone cannot settle either reading.

A third supported path distinguishes OIG coverage from HQ responsibility. Even if statutory representation applies, attribution and the permissible scope of a compliance order must be justified independently. Conversely, HQ's inability to dictate results does not necessarily prevent it from directing legal compliance. If any part of the Authority order rests on a legally invalid premise, a bounded remand must identify that defect; current agency cases do not impose automatic remand after every interpretive dispute. No damages, new investigation, new representation regulation, or adverse employee finding is authorized by this source record.

## 6. Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District — June17

### Assigned review and reliable record

Assigned simulated postgrant merits event June17,1999; lower docket98-50227; substituted opinion168F.3d848(March11,1999), official PDF marked revised April1. No historical Supreme Court grant is asserted. The questions separately concern the first Gingles condition for a single Hispanic minority group, current demographic proof against the1990 census, and the late survey exclusion. The school board uses at-large, by-place, majority-vote elections to seven seats. The district court found the first condition unproved; the Fifth Circuit affirmed. No determination of cohesion, majority-bloc defeat or full totality under a functional alternative is supplied.

Official source: https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/98/98-50227.CV0.wpd.pdf. Full neutral source analysis and the preserved limitations are in `../research/OT_1998CHUNK10_A_LOWER_SOURCE_MEMO.md`, Valdespino section. Do not substitute the earlier withdrawn opinion for this one.

The1990 census gave the plaintiffs' demonstration district a Hispanic citizen voting-age majority. Rives documented later changes in an apartment complex inside that district and development outside it, creating underpopulation relative to one-seventh of school-district population. Adding a contiguous northern area—also appearing in some earlier plaintiffs' plans—produced47.9% Hispanic citizen voting-age population; accepting8.1% underpopulation produced48.3%. These are population shares, not turnout or measured crossover-vote shares. A demographic change does not prove that the1990 census was inaccurate when taken.

The trial court credited the April1997 report, not the August update. The latter reflected some favorable apartment changes and more growth elsewhere; both fell below a majority. The plaintiffs' late survey covered a small area south of their proposed district. They disclosed its existence at4:21p.m.Friday September12 and data at4:13p.m.Saturday September13 before Monday September15 trial, intending to use it in rebuttal after a census-based case in chief. The court received an offer of proof but excluded the survey for surprise under Local RuleCV-16(e). It also excluded reliance on the late defense update. A prior deposition reference to Hispanic residents south of the district did not disclose the later survey's bases.

The lower court treated census data as presumptively accurate, rebuttable by thoroughly documented, highly accurate, clear/cogent/convincing evidence of changed conditions; it found the housing/occupancy/electric-hookup proof adequate. Review of subsidiary demographics was for clear error; disclosure exclusion for abuse of discretion. A footnote's survey-method criticism was not pressed as an alternative appellate ground and was not decided. Proper-rebuttal status likewise was not reached. The cited four-factor exclusion framework was mentioned but not briefed by either side; do not invent findings about its factors.

### Entering law and genuine open questions

Gingles's conventional majority, compactness, cohesion and majority-bloc requirements survive. Canonical Growe,February23,1993, applies the framework to single-member fragmentation, leaves the population denominator and influence-only claims open, and only assumes the possible distinct-group coalition claim. Voinovich,March2,1993, distinguishes state districting choice from court-compelled remedy and assumes an influence claim while rejecting it on an independent bloc-voting failure. DeGrandy,June30,1994, preserves necessary conditions and independent totality, rejects maximization and numerical safe harbors, and treats actual opportunity as relevant without converting it into a below-majority entitlement. The exact Standard is Section2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims; present operation effective DeGrandy,June30,1994, with the earlier components retained on their dates.

Use `../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK10_A_VALDESPINO_CURRENT.md`. Brooks v.Barnes,April22,1999, requires actual nomination opportunity in evaluating a runoff barrier and rejects conclusive statewide offsets and a mandatory general-election-winner showing. It does not erase the district-majority condition for the different district-formation claim. Its support for fitting proof to the actual barrier is a relevant legal argument, subject to that distinction. The Department of Commerce census decision concerns statutory authority for national enumeration methods; it neither requires reliance on obsolete local data nor makes every housing projection reliable.

Section2's1994 text is officially recovered in `../research/OT_1998CHUNK10_A_USC1994_42-1973.txt`. It protects equal participation and opportunity to elect representatives of choice under totality and disclaims proportional-election entitlement. No textual majority number alone resolves how the precedential first condition may lawfully be maintained or expressly modified.

### Supported competing paths and remedy limits

Path retaining the conventional threshold: require the protected group to be a numerical majority in a workable district, decide the relevant denominator, and test the demographic/exclusion rulings under their own standards. The lower accepted47.9/48.3 figures do not satisfy that rule. Its strongest objection is that statutory opportunity may exist with dependable crossover support, and Voinovich leaves adaptation of some claims open; the response must explain why this districting claim retains its causal threshold rather than declaring every open influence question already resolved.

Path expressly adapting the first condition: recognize a carefully supported actual ability-to-elect theory for a compact, workable district despite a submajority protected-group population, while preserving independent cohesion, majority-bloc defeat, totality and a nonracial-floor remedy. The strongest objection is departure from Gingles/Growe's conventional majority condition and the problem of distinguishing reliable election capacity from influence or speculative near-majority arithmetic. This is a proposed legal change, not existing law. The recovered opinion supplies no finding of dependable crossover capacity. A change could support only properly bounded reconsideration on the preserved record and lawfully permitted supplementation, not immediate liability or a commanded district.

The demographic and exclusion branches remain distinct even if the legal rule changes. A census-baseline challenge can fail while a functional-rule argument survives; a valid exclusion does not adjudicate the survey's substantive accuracy. Conversely, changing the threshold does not prove an abuse of discretion or require admission of an undisclosed survey. A remand must specify what legal error warrants it and preserve supported subsidiary findings, independent defaults and ordinary procedural limits. No new election system, judicial map, election date or original-source default is presumed.
