### Comparator and disposition reconciliation

**N1 — cross-appeal.** A party that has not appealed may defend an existing decree on a different record-supported ground but may not obtain enlargement of its own rights or reduction of its opponent's rights. Neither general tribal comity nor the preliminary character of these appealable injunctions excuses the absence of the plaintiffs' appeals. The existing partial injunctions therefore cannot be dissolved on the companies' appeals. The rule's ultimate jurisdictional classification and the existence of every conceivable exception remain undecided. The injunctions' substantive merits are not independently endorsed.

**N2 — federal coverage determination.** The federal district court must itself decide whether the pleaded claims are Price-Anderson public-liability actions before deferring their coverage determination to tribal proceedings. The combination of federal characterization, original federal jurisdiction, state-court removal and consolidation displaces the ordinary prudential exhaustion rationale for that task. This does not rewrite the state-court removal provision to authorize literal removal from tribal court. A routine federal preemption defense alone would not have this effect; tribal courts ordinarily may decide federal questions.

### Scope, objections and response

S1 protects a United States citizen's ability to establish bona fide residence in another State and receive equal treatment as a citizen there. California may not delay equal ordinary welfare treatment for less than twelve months by using the prior State's lower benefit level. Shapiro supplies the close welfare and durational-disability precedent; Dunn distinguishes genuine residence from extra duration; Zobel rejects citizen hierarchies based on arrival history; Slaughter-House supplies the specific citizenship passage without wholesale revision of its holdings. Article IV's protection for visitors is distinct from the Fourteenth Amendment protection for new resident citizens.

The strongest counterargument is that continued prior-level support, unlike total exclusion, leaves migration feasible and reasonably permits a transition while a State manages its budget. The seven respond that equal citizenship after residence is established is the relevant right; the current within-State disparity is itself the burden. Temporary duration and another State's amount do not cure it. Saving resources is legitimate generally but does not justify this selection of equally eligible citizens. The decision does not make every residence condition unlawful or decide a genuine dispute about bona fide residence or a distinct portable-benefit setting.

Thomas's refusal remains substantive, not a view that Congress can override the Constitution. His historical original-meaning dissent supplies further confirmation of the frozen Clause objection. The supplied record establishes bona fide residence; his support for a temporary transition must not be converted into a finding that these recipients intend to leave or are abusing the program. Historical Rehnquist arguments about testing residence are contrary legal arguments, not proof of those facts. No unsupported motive, recipient conduct or State concession enters the present record from the comparator.

For the seven, section 604(c) cannot validate the unconstitutional differential. The entering Boerne rule distinguishes enforcement from replacement of a constitutional guarantee, and Shapiro's own federal-authorization limit supplies direct support. The holding does not invalidate every federal welfare provision or turn statutory permission into an obligation. A distinct claim about a different eligibility rule remains outside this decision.
