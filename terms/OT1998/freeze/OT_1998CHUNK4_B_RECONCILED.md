## 2. Wright v. Ernst & Young LLP, No. 98-783 — February 4, 1999

### Final component commitments

All eight Associates support the same component judgment. Private review and oral approval of the figures do not, on these allegations, make BT's expressly unaudited release the auditor's own public certification when no auditor representation was communicated or attributed to investors. General market expectations cannot supply that particular representation. This confined conclusion is not a universal public-name requirement for every scheme, agency relation, deceptive act or indirect communication.

The separately preserved affirmative-assistance claim must be reconsidered under Central Bank. The lower judgment is vacated insofar as it rejects the January claim categorically for nonattribution or the supposed unavailability of private assistance liability. The actual amended complaint remains the pleading for review. The separate 1995 prospectus/duty-to-correct branch remains dismissed; neither a new pleading nor an amendment entitlement is created. The formal disposition is affirmance in part, vacatur in part and remand, with those component boundaries stated.

Central Bank permits knowing or severely reckless affirmative substantial assistance to an underlying securities fraud. Severe recklessness means an extreme departure from ordinary care presenting a specific danger of deception known or so obvious that the assistant must appreciate it. The assistant must appreciate both the fraud and its assisting role. No separate disclosure duty is required for affirmative participation; negligence, professional status, valuation concern alone, ordinary services without qualifying culpability and assisting effect, and duty-free silence are insufficient. Underlying fraud, legally sufficient reliance on that fraud, causation and recoverable loss remain required. Investors need not rely on concealed assistance itself, and no automatic reliance presumption follows.

The remand finds neither fraud nor pleading sufficiency. It does not infer January knowledge from later discovery, turn GAAS failures into scienter, compel discovery, award damages or determine the effect of the BT settlement on an eventual recovery. Properly presented independent pleading defenses remain available. First Jersey distinguishes proved hands-on primary deception from office, ownership and assistance alone; O'Hagan supplies no shortcut around its own deception, duty and transaction predicates.

### Changed historical premise and alternative-path reconciliation

The historical Second Circuit rationale treated Central Bank as excluding private aiding liability and used nonattribution to reject relabeled assistance. That statutory premise is displaced here: the April 19, 1994 Central Bank decision affirmatively preserves the confined assistance action. The lower opinion's description of the auditor's participation as tangential is not an independent factual finding insulated from that legal premise. The complaint and appellate presentation expressly supply substantial participation and affirmative approval after discovery of an under-accrual; remand does not invent a new factual theory.

This difference matters directly to Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg because they adopted the existing Central Bank action. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissented from its original foundation, but each later joined First Jersey's complete direct-deception holding expressly preserving Central Bank's separate domain. Their bounded application here is supported by that later public position; it is not presented as withdrawal of their original objection. Breyer had no Central Bank vote, and his First Jersey and O'Hagan joins provide his date-eligible support.

The strongest independent-affirmance path is that the identified under-accrual and approval show ordinary professional error rather than appreciation of fraud. That remains a lawful pleading issue on remand. It cannot be conclusively answered by absence of public attribution, by the separately defective prospectus branch, or by a lower legal characterization made under the categorical no-assistance rule. The narrower legal-error disposition therefore avoids both unsupported culpability findings and mechanical replication of the historical dismissal.

**Historical departure:** The reconciled assistance vacatur differs from the historical lower-court dismissal because simulated Central Bank preserves knowing or severely reckless affirmative substantial assistance; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg adopted that rule, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas later joined First Jersey's express preservation of it despite their original dissent, and Breyer joined that later preservation without any invented 1994 vote. There is no historical Supreme Court merits vote in Wright from which to depart.

## 4. Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education, No. 97-1835 — February 17, 1999

### Final competing commitments

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas affirm the First Amendment dismissal on Path A: choosing and directing the school's assigned instructional production does not supply this employee a personal First Amendment entitlement over the institutional curricular content. This is a confined antecedent-right ground, not a finding of lawful motive, proven insubordination or a same-decision defense. Approval does not become an irrevocable constitutional delegation of curriculum authority. Separate citizen speech, student expression, independently prohibited discrimination and materially different university questions remain outside the holding.

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer vacate and remand on Path B: an instructor's expressive contribution can retain limited protection against adverse action unrelated to genuine legitimate pedagogy even while school officials retain final curricular authority. Officials may select content and make related personnel decisions reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns. They need not meet strict scrutiny, prove actual disruption in every case, grant an open forum or guarantee the teacher a selected play. But alleged approval, compliance and punishment for unpopular expression cannot be dismissed through an assumed actual ground or the assertion that anything curricular is legitimate by definition. This is a limited proposed extension of Hazelwood, not a claim that Hazelwood or Settle already decided this teacher's entitlement.

Both paths accept the pleaded facts at their proper status: the majority below assumed compliance with the edits, and no trial resolved motive. The auditorium-floor episode is not an adjudicated independent cause. Selecting the play does not establish personal endorsement of every character's views. The non-First-Amendment claims outside the grant are not revived.

### Comparator and alternative-path reconciliation

The historical en banc court affirmed dismissal 7–6. Its judges' alignment is not evidence that any particular Supreme Court Justice would vote the same way. Its two relevant grounds must remain distinct: absence of a personal curricular speech interest, and the alternative assertion that curriculum content is legitimate pedagogy. The former remains a supported open path under current law. The latter cannot be adopted as categorical constitutional immunity consistently with unanimous Settle's genuine-pedagogy limits.

Settle concerned a student's expression within an assignment, not a teacher's claim about administering instructional content. Jeffries concerned unaffiliated citizen speech expressly outside assigned work; Brewster concerned an internal attendance report in its particular immunity posture. Rosenberger concerns a privately edited publication program, and Finley concerns selective support of private art. None mechanically resolves the present attribution question. They nevertheless provide concrete, Justice-specific evidence on the distinction between institutional authority and constitutional limits.

The affirmative group accepts institutional content as the school's program and sees no antecedent personal right on this claim; the vacatur group recognizes a bounded expressive interest against genuinely nonpedagogical punishment of an allegedly approved contribution. That difference changes judgment and cannot be erased by calling both groups supporters of genuine pedagogy. The two complete rationales answer the antecedent question differently; neither is a logical subset of the other. No Marks synthesis or completed equal-division judgment follows from the eight-Associate poll.

The strongest objection to affirmance is that Boring seeks protection from retaliatory punishment after approval, not power to dictate the curriculum. Path A preserves independently protected expression and constitutional prohibitions but concludes that this delegated curricular choice supplies no such personal entitlement. The strongest objection to vacatur is that a pedagogical-justification inquiry converts curricular personnel judgment into federal supervision. Path B preserves broad final school authority and ordinary evaluative error, but treats alleged approved expression and nonpedagogical retaliation as sufficient to prevent categorical extinction of the claim. These are supported legal applications of the cited prior positions, not recovered statements by the Justices in Boring.
