The legislature referred stadium-financing legislation through a statewide special election conditioned on the team affiliate's agreement to reimburse election costs. Public officials administered the election. The condition afforded the affiliate a legally consequential choice whether to undertake the reimbursement obligation; that feature must be examined, not denied. But the supported record establishes no ballot cast by the affiliate as an entity, no unequal weighting of a citizen's ballot, no voter exclusion for inability to pay, and no private power to count or alter ballots. Ordinary petition routes remained available. The short campaign was less than sixty days; the lower court found no support for the asserted purpose to drown out opponents. The judgment is on summary judgment, and a genuinely supported material factual dispute could not properly be resolved against Brower. The neutral recitation supplies no such dispute and no new suppressive purpose is found.

There are three distinct federal components:

1. **Access and reimbursement control:** whether conditioning this legislative referral on the beneficiary's reimbursement consent unconstitutionally allocated access by wealth or transferred electoral authority.
2. **Speech and timing:** whether the compressed campaign and resource disparity imposed the asserted constitutional impairment of opposition advocacy.
3. **Federal equal voting weight:** whether the affiliate's reimbursement consent was itself an extra or disproportionately powerful electoral vote.

## Operative law and the limits of the analogies

The operative Standards rule is **Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system**, present operation effective Burdick, June 8, 1992. Anderson–Burdick measures the actual injury against the State's precise interests and the extent to which those interests require the burden. Severe restrictions demand narrow tailoring to a compelling interest; reasonable nondiscriminatory administration receives proportionate review. The particular write-in presumption does not become a categorical presumption for private reimbursement. Alternative petition routes matter to the complete access setting but do not answer a separately established exclusion, discriminatory referral practice or unequal vote.

Reynolds supplies the equal-weight principle asserted. White v. Regester concerns invidious district-based dilution; the absence of districts here distinguishes that setting, but does not place every statewide arrangement beyond equal-vote review. The operative question is whether this consent condition confers governmental electoral choice with unequal weight, rather than whether the case uses familiar district boundaries.

Buckley ACLF distinguishes direct restrictions on political conversation from adequately fitted administration; the statute here does not impose its registration, personal-name-badge or individual-disclosure restrictions. Yoshina requires attention to the actual legal effect of election rules and preserves relief for fundamentally unfair administration; it does not immunize this arrangement merely because state law authorizes it. Reform Party rejects an entitlement to equal campaign resources on its presented theory while preserving different practical suppression and discriminatory administration. Its result is relevant to Brower's resource disparity, not a substitute for deciding his consent-as-vote claim. Gable supplies no merits financing rule. Its dissent provides the published positions of Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer distinguishing competing advocacy from restraints on a speaker's own funds; it is not Court law.

## Alternative-path comparison

### Access and reimbursement control

**Affirmance path.** A legislature may refer a specific proposed public-private arrangement subject to the private participant's undertaking to pay election costs without thereby charging citizens for voting, transferring counting authority or establishing that wealthy persons alone may use ordinary petition procedures. On this record the condition concerns whether that particular financial proposal will proceed. Public election administration, equal voter eligibility and the continued ordinary access routes bear on the actual asserted injury. Requiring the project's beneficiary to bear the special election's expense serves the concrete fiscal function apparent in the reimbursement condition; no invented emergency or team-relocation deadline is needed.

**Strongest contrary path.** Even if public officials count every ballot correctly, the State could confer discriminatory gatekeeping over electoral machinery by making one wealthy beneficiary's assent decisive. A supported exclusive privilege, rejection of comparable proposals because proponents lack money, or practical suppression of opposition would require its own review. The present arrangement does give the affiliate a consequential consent choice, but the legislature independently chose the referral and its financial contingency; ordinary voters were not required to buy voting or petition rights. The claim as supported does not establish the different exclusion needed for that path. Continued petition access is corroborative, not conclusive. No general right to purchase public elections, universal constitutional approval of private funding, or immunity for selective referral is adopted.

### Speech and timing

**Affirmance path.** A short campaign and opponents' inability to match a beneficiary's resources do not, without the supported speech or participation impairment asserted here, establish a constitutional right to postpone this election or equalize advocacy. The State imposed no supported restriction on opponents' own speech, fundraising, spending or organization; the alleged suppressive purpose lacks support in the lower summary-judgment account. This is the supplied application of Anderson–Burdick, ACLF and Reform Party, rather than an abstract proposition that fewer than sixty days always suffices.

**Strongest contrary path.** A facially equal calendar can be deliberately or practically prohibitive, especially where one beneficiary has advance organizational advantages. A proven material inability to organize or communicate caused by the State's calendar would require examination of the actual burden and the precise asserted scheduling justification; public control of counting and absence of a voter fee would not answer it. But neither unequal resources alone nor the duration alone supplies that materially different record. The lower court's finding of no support for suppressive purpose is not a statutory command or a finding that intent is always necessary to a speech claim. No emergency, urgency, alternative calendar, minimum campaign period or newly inferred successful fundraising is supplied. Affirmance rests on the insufficiency of this asserted deprivation, not on a fabricated compelling reason for the date.

### Federal equal voting weight

**Affirmance path.** The affiliate's reimbursement choice determines whether it will assume a financial obligation attached to this legislative proposal. That consent is not an electoral ballot determining the public question. The public alone approves or rejects the referred measure; the consent neither adds weight to a citizen's vote nor diminishes Brower's vote. Reynolds protects equality in governmental electoral choice but does not convert every legally consequential private consent to a proposed transaction into an additional governmental vote.

**Strongest contrary path.** Brower can characterize the affiliate's power to prevent the election by refusing reimbursement as greater electoral power than an ordinary voter possesses. The answer must address that legal effect, not merely note that no districts exist. The private undertaking is a condition on the State's proposed financial arrangement and its referral, not an allocated share of the electorate's approval or rejection power. No corporation receives a ballot, no class's votes are multiplied and no contrary majority is displaced. This defeats the asserted equal-weight characterization. It leaves the distinct access and speech claims to their own analysis and does not authorize a private veto over counted votes or a generally applicable privately controlled electorate.
