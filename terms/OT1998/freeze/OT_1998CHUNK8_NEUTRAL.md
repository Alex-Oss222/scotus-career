## Decisions and reasoning below

**ALJ:** Applied the Commission's Massachusetts Board approach, treated the restraints as inherently suspect without a plausible efficiency justification, and found a violation. The ALJ found that CDA lacked market power but considered such proof unnecessary under that approach.

**Commission:** The principal opinion treated price restrictions as unlawful per se, with abbreviated rule-of-reason analysis as an alternative, and treated nonprice restrictions as unlawful under abbreviated analysis. It disagreed with the ALJ on market power. Commissioner Starek concurred in the result under the Massachusetts Board approach; Commissioner Azcuenaga dissented on the sufficiency of the evidence concerning anticompetitive acts and market power. These positions are reported by the appellate opinion; the complete administrative opinions were not independently read in this preflight.

**Ninth Circuit majority, Hall joined by Choy:** Held that substantial commercial activities furnishing tangible pecuniary benefits to members brought CDA within §44, notwithstanding nonprofit form. It distinguished genuinely charitable activity and relied on the business character of the challenged advertising regulation. It rejected per se treatment of the price rules because the facially antideception rules and professional context warranted inquiry into effects. It nevertheless sustained the alternative quick-look analysis for price and quality restrictions separately.

For price advertising, the majority reasoned that suppressing truthful prices and discounts makes consumer comparison and price competition harder. It accepted that disclosures may be procompetitive but found the required quantity of disclosure practically prohibitive of across-the-board discount advertising and found no evidence that this enforcement increased price transparency. For quality advertising, it regarded the suppression of information about services as a restraint on competition, potentially also reducing demand for nonemergency care, and rejected the claim that possible unverifiability justified barring all quality claims without examining truthfulness. Those are the majority's competitive inferences; they are not findings of measured output reduction.

The majority found substantial evidence of agreement through the association's rules, advertising restraint, CDA responsibility, and enforcement reaching truthful advertising. It regarded dentistry as the product market and local areas as the geographic markets. **Real dissent:** Would find CDA outside FTC jurisdiction because the group-purchasing services do not make the nonprofit association itself a commercial enterprise; benefits arise through members buying from competing outside suppliers. ### Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, No. 96-1866

Decision, June 22, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 25, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit. The question is what showing permits private damages against a school district under Title IX for a teacher's sexual harassment of a student. Review concerns only the appealed Title IX judgment against the district.

In October 1992, parents of two other students complained to the principal about Waldrop's classroom comments. The principal met with him, received an apology and assurance against repetition, cautioned him and informed the guidance counselor, but did not transmit the complaint to the superintendent, the Title IX coordinator. Gebser did not report the relationship herself. Police discovered it in January 1993, and the district terminated Waldrop.

The evidence about grievance procedures is disputed. The superintendent's affidavit described a policy, while deposition testimony described no formal policy; she did not remember whether a handbook notifying students of procedures had been distributed. Gebser testified that she probably would have reported had she known what to do, while describing bewilderment and fear. These are evidentiary accounts, not findings of institutional fault or causation. The District Court granted summary judgment for the district, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed under an actual-supervisory-knowledge requirement.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The reversal affects only the Fifth Circuit's affirmance of the district's Title IX summary judgment. Further proceedings must apply the controlling attribution propositions and determine the unresolved elements, causal facts, damages and defenses. No compensation or compulsory trial is ordered, and no unappealed claim or defendant is restored.

#### Opinions and Joins

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Supports reversal and remand | Five directly reject actual notice to a corrective official as the exclusive attribution route, with the stated limits. |
| Opinion announcing the judgment, Parts II–III | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Supports reversal and remand on the author's grounds | Part II states the narrower attribution ground with the component-specific Marks force explained below. Part III gives his own record application. The four other reversal supporters do not join either part. |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Breyer | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg | Joins Part I and supports reversal and remand | Independent actual-authority/material-aid and negligent-correction grounds; does not join Parts II–III. |
| Separate concurrence | Ginsburg | Souter, Breyer | Supports reversal and remand within their broader framework | Proposed, noncontrolling avoidable-harm defense. Stevens reserves it; Stone-Zsela leaves defenses unresolved. |
| Dissent | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Would affirm | Kinman's exclusive actual-notice rule and its record-specific insufficiency application. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Actual notice to a corrective official is not the exclusive attribution route

**Controlling proposition:** A funding recipient's responsibility for a teacher's intentional discrimination cannot invariably be defeated solely because no official empowered to correct the harassment had actual notice. Kinman is limited insofar as it made that particular notice route exclusive; employment, ordinary teacher access or a technical regulatory breach, standing alone, still does not establish recipient liability.

**Authority:** The five Justices joining Part I directly adopt this limited negative proposition. They do not adopt a common complete damages framework.

**Controlling explanation:** Recipient responsibility remains distinct from the teacher's intentional wrong. That distinction does not make actual notice to a particular corrective official the only possible means of attribution. The categorical rule would exclude other supported grounds before their predicates and causal connection were examined. Franklin preserves compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination while requiring attribution and loss to be established separately. Kinman's actual-notice and deliberate-disregard route remains available, but its former exclusivity does not survive. The Court neither equates teacher employment with recipient responsibility nor treats a violation of grievance regulations as automatic discrimination or damages liability.

##### II. Authority materially enabling discrimination, combined with the recipient's causal fault, supplies a narrower attribution route

**Controlling proposition:** A recipient may be held responsible when school-conferred authority materially enables a teacher's intentional discrimination and the recipient's own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causes that discrimination to continue. This is a sufficient route for attributing the discrimination beyond the actual-corrective-official-notice route; it establishes neither the underlying discrimination nor causal loss, damages or the defeat of a supported defense.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has no other formal joins. Its stated attribution component supplies the narrower controlling ground under Marks v. United States and Cipollone's component-specific application. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer independently support attribution whenever actual delegated educational power materially aids intentional harassment, without Stone-Zsela's additional recipient-fault requirement. School-conferred authority must actually and materially enable the wrong; title or opportunity alone is insufficient under either ground. Every case meeting the narrower conjunction therefore meets that independently sufficient broader attribution route. Those judgment-supporting positions total five, without converting the four Justices into joins in Stone-Zsela's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin's damages remedy does not make every employee wrong the recipient's own wrong. The actual use of school-conferred authority must materially connect the discrimination to the educational relationship, and this narrower route additionally requires the recipient's own causal fault in its continuation. It does not permit the recipient to defeat attribution merely by deliberately disabling transmission of substantial warning information. Ordinary negligence or a technical policy omission alone does not satisfy this route. The underlying discrimination, institutional fault and causal continuation must still be proved.

Marks permits a narrower judgment-supporting ground without whole-opinion joins; Cipollone applies that inquiry to a particular legal component while leaving distinct liability and remedial questions unresolved. Here the four Justices' independent actual-authority ground contains the narrower attribution category. Their additional negligent-correction route is unnecessary to that inclusion. The proposed avoidable-harm limit supported by three of them concerns the extent of recovery; it does not negate their attribution ground. This limited nesting establishes neither their defense nor a complete damages formula. No general circumstantial-awareness presumption, automatic policy-failure inference or pooled-knowledge rule follows.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The authority, recipient-fault and causal-continuation conditions are conjunctive. The alternative involving reporting failure requires willful blindness and deliberate indifference, not constructive notice or negligence alone. The Court does not adopt ordinary agency liability as a complete general rule, the four-Justice negligence route or every Title VII attribution principle. Discrimination, institutional fault, causation, compensable injury, damages and defenses remain unresolved. Official-policy cases, prospective enforcement, individual liability and state-law remedies are outside this holding. The separate regulatory duties to adopt and publish prompt, equitable grievance procedures under 34 C.F.R. §106.8(b) and give nondiscrimination notice under §106.9(a) are not converted into strict private damages liability.

##### III. The appealed Title IX summary judgment is reversed for ordinary further proceedings

**Judgment and operative remedy:** The Fifth Circuit's judgment is reversed only insofar as it affirms summary judgment for the district on the appealed Title IX claim, and the case is remanded. The lower courts must determine whether school-conferred authority materially enabled the discrimination and whether the recipient's own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure caused it to continue, while preserving the actual-notice route and the remaining elements, ordinary procedures and defenses.

**Authority and application limit:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this formal reversal and ordinary remand. Stone-Zsela alone adopts Part III's application of his recipient-fault reasoning to the disputed warning and reporting evidence; the other four support further proceedings on their separately stated grounds. Their judgment agreement is not a five-Justice finding of actual knowledge, deliberate disregard, willful blindness, negligence, material aid, causation or liability. The Court orders neither compensation nor a compulsory trial. The unappealed constitutional, personal and state-law claims are not reopened.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Kinman v. Omaha Public School District:** Limited insofar as actual notice to a corrective official was the exclusive recipient-attribution threshold. Its actual-notice, deliberate and unreasonable disregard and causal-injury route remains available, together with the other Title IX elements. Its rejection of automatic teacher-status attribution and a technical policy breach as sufficient by themselves remains. No earlier sole-Justice awareness inference becomes Court law merely through this decision.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools:** Preserved as allowing appropriate compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination, subject to recipient responsibility, causation and loss. The present attribution holding does not establish a damages award or unintended-effects liability.
- **Cannon v. University of Chicago:** The established private action remains intact; no new regulatory cause of action is recognized.
- **Marks v. United States:** Applied to the particular sufficient attribution ground, without treating every reason or remedy proposed by a judgment supporter as controlling.
- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group:** Its component-specific logical-subset inquiry supports the limited attribution holding despite the absence of whole-opinion joins; separate proof, defenses and damages questions remain distinct.
- **Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association:** Its distinction between a directly shared negative proposition and unadopted complete affirmative frameworks supports keeping the exclusivity holding separate from the limited Marks analysis.
- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson, Andrade and Faragher:** Their employment-attribution principles are not transferred wholesale to Title IX. Faragher's unresolved effective-protection/nonuse defense is not resolved here.
- **Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman:** Its funding-notice principle remains; neither it nor Title IX's administrative enforcement sequence becomes a literal exhaustion requirement for this private action.

#### Law After Decision

Actual notice to a corrective official is an available but no longer exclusive route to recipient responsibility. The narrower authority-plus-recipient-fault attribution route stated in Holding II controls at that component-specific scope under Marks. It supplies no comprehensive damages formula, no general awareness presumption and no rule of automatic liability from employment, nonreporting or a policy defect. The broader agency and negligent-correction theories and the three-Justice avoidable-harm defense remain noncontrolling. Kinman's distinct substantive harassment elements and the ordinary requirements of causation, injury, remedy and defense remain material. This record returns for further proceedings without any liability or compensation finding.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion announcing the judgment, Part III.** The complaint to the principal, the internal warning and the evidence about the absence of a functioning grievance route create, in his view, a factual dispute about the recipient's own fault. He would require the lower courts to examine whether school-conferred authority materially enabled the discrimination and whether actual recipient knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure caused its continuation. A district cannot defeat that inquiry through its own failure to transmit substantial warning information. He does not treat the complaint about classroom remarks as an established report of a sexual relationship, resolve the conflicting policy evidence, or find liability. Employment, ordinary negligence and a technical regulatory violation alone remain insufficient. This record-specific application has no other joins; the attribution component of Part II has only the limited Marks force stated above.

**Justice Breyer, concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by Justices Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.** They join Part I's rejection of exclusivity and support reversal and ordinary remand, but do not join Parts II–III. They renew the two independent attribution routes previously stated in their Kinman position. First, actual delegated educational power materially aiding intentional harassment can attribute the wrong to the recipient without a separate higher-management knowledge requirement. Second, negligent failure to correct after responsible officials knew or reasonably should have known, causing injury or continuation, supplies an independent ground. Teacher title or opportunity alone does not establish actual aid, and a policy breach alone establishes neither negligence nor causation.

Franklin permits compensation for intentional discrimination. These Justices distinguish the teacher's intentional wrong from the agency principles attributing that wrong; their negligence alternative is not compensation for every unintended discriminatory effect. Cannon supplies the established private action, while Title IX's administrative notice and correction process does not require a second intentional wrong by recipient management in every damages case. They acknowledge that Kinman had rejected their broader grounds and would reconsider that exclusivity. Title VII's agency decisions supply a limited analogy, not an automatic transfer of every employment rule.

The actual delegation, material aid, information received, correction, reporting arrangements and causal injury require ordinary further consideration. The affidavit/deposition tension over policy and uncertainty about dissemination are evidence, not conclusive findings. These Justices do not establish liability or compel a trial. Their judgment agreement does not endorse Stone-Zsela's record-specific awareness reasoning. Their broader attribution theories do not become controlling merely because they support the same judgment; only the narrower component identified in Holding II has the stated Marks force.

**Justice Ginsburg, separately concurring, joined by Justices Souter and Breyer.** Within their broader attribution framework, these three would permit a limited avoidable-consequences defense. The district would bear the burden of proving that internal remedies were adequately publicized and likely to provide redress without undue risk, effort or expense. Recovery would be barred only to the extent the plaintiff unreasonably failed to use those measures and consequently suffered avoidable harm. A paper policy, nonreporting alone, an unsafe or ineffective route or eventual correction would not establish the defense. The uncertain policy and dissemination record does not conclusively establish it here. This is a causal limit on avoidable recovery, not automatic complete immunity or an additional Court-adopted attribution condition. Stevens reserves the defense and does not join its terms; Stone-Zsela leaves defenses unresolved.

**Justice O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** They would affirm under Kinman's actual-notice and deliberate-disregard rule. Franklin's availability of compensation does not answer recipient responsibility. Title IX's recipient structure, its administrative correction mechanism and Pennhurst's notice principle support attention to the recipient's own culpable nonresponse rather than ordinary agency attribution or knowledge imputed from the teacher's conduct. They would preserve Kinman's exclusivity and reject the additional attribution route.

Even assuming that the principal had corrective authority, the reported information and response do not, in their view, establish actual knowledge of the relationship or a substantial risk followed by deliberate and unreasonable disregard. The parents complained about classroom comments. The principal met with Waldrop, received an apology and assurance, cautioned him and informed the guidance counselor; termination followed police discovery. These responses are not declared perfect, and a warning does not invariably suffice. But this record does not establish the required culpable refusal to protect.

The possible absence of a functioning complaint procedure is not itself institutional actual notice. The principal's information cannot be pooled with Waldrop's knowledge, and the policy conflict cannot be resolved by assuming that every procedure was absent. Their rule requires neither a written complaint, a victim's personal report, a confession nor proof that a completed sexual relationship was already known. Actual notice of a substantial danger can suffice. Administrative and injunctive enforcement remain distinct, and the district receives no personal qualified immunity. These four oppose both reversal and the limitation of Kinman.

The appealed Title IX judgment is reversed and the claim returns for ordinary further proceedings.
