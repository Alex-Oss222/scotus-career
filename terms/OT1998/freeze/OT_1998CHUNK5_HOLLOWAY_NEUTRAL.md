## Question, record and remedy

The petition asks whether including conditional intent in the amended carjacking statute's specific-intent requirement violates statutory construction and due process. The question is copied in `sources/chunk5_a/HOLLOWAY_QUESTION_AND_ALTERNATIVE_SAFE.md`. It does not independently present every ineffective-assistance, sentencing or constitutional-federal-power contention litigated below.

The complete primary lower majority and Miner dissent have been read in `sources/chunk5_a/HOLLOWAY_LOWER_FULL.txt`. The isolated actual instruction/recharge material in `extract_18_holloway_charge_and_testimony.txt` was also read in full. The Second Circuit reports three charged October 14-15, 1994 carjackings with gun threats, Lennon taking the vehicles and Holloway assisting. Holloway struck Rodriguez when he hesitated. Lennon testified he wished to obtain vehicles without injury but would have used the gun upon resistance. The record includes identifications, testimony and a confession. These are evidence and appellate recitations, not new Supreme Court credibility findings. No victim was killed or seriously injured on this account; absence of that result is evidence about intent, not a conclusive statutory defense.

The jury found Holloway guilty of chop-shop conspiracy (count 1), chop-shop operation (count 2), carjacking (counts 7, 9, 11), and dependent firearm offenses (counts 8, 10, 12). The question concerns the mental element of the carjacking counts and any consequent effect on dependent firearm judgments. The separate chop-shop judgments do not fall automatically. The lower court rejected ineffective assistance and a consecutive-sentencing attack; this Court's review does not independently reopen those issues merely because they were decided there.

The actual charge required proof beyond reasonable doubt, separately for each count, that Holloway at the taking intended death or serious bodily harm. The charge allowed a finding of actual intent to cause that harm if victims refused to surrender their cars, but instructed that intending to frighten with a gun alone was insufficient, and that Lennon's intent alone did not suffice: Holloway must share it. Mere bodily harm was expressly insufficient. The jury requested the law on intent and heard a recharge preserving those distinctions. This is not a charge allowing conviction for foreseeability, recklessness, danger, a bare threat or another person's intent.

The lower majority held actual conditional purpose sufficient, distinguished mere threats and recklessness, and invoked traditional criminal law and the Model Penal Code as interpretive support. Its speculation about a drafting error is not a legislative fact or permission to remove the enacted mental element. Miner would reverse and remand for retrial: the amended text requires specific intent to harm, and the court cannot add conditional intent from state statutes or an unadopted model code. Both positions agree Congress replaced the firearm-possession element with an intent-to-cause-death-or-serious-bodily-harm element applying to the offense, rather than only its death-penalty provision.

## Law and supported paths

Section 2119 as amended in 1994 requires taking or attempting to take the interstate-connected motor vehicle from another's person or presence, by force and violence or intimidation, with intent to cause death or serious bodily harm. The separate graduated consequences for serious bodily injury or death remain. This event resolves intent, not the penalty-element status or every constitutional source of federal authority. *Winship* requires proof of the actual elements beyond reasonable doubt. *Morissette* permits established meaning to inform criminal mental terms while keeping Congress's offense controlling. Lenity resolves genuine residual criminal ambiguity after ordinary construction; it is not an automatic objection to any disagreement.

The mechanically copied A entering-law slice supplies *Bryan* (June 15, 1998): its seven-Justice rule requires knowing unlawful dealing for that offense without knowledge of the particular licensing provision; Scalia and Ginsburg disagree on that statutory question. Its separate six-Justice instruction-scope holding keeps a distinct unpresented error from being decided by resolution of another objection. *Bailey/Robinson* (December 6, 1995) preserves each actual firearm element and separate relation requirement, rather than permitting dangerous association to substitute. Its remedy also separates affected and unaffected counts and rejects automatic conclusions about alternate grounds. *Ratzlaf* and *Staples*, as preserved by those holdings, remain offense-specific rather than universal instructions to narrow all mental elements. Current *Griffin/Langley* (October 29, 1998) requires actual enacted elements while rejecting an additional requested element unsupported by the statute. None decides conditional intent in section 2119.

**Actual conditional-purpose path.** A person who has presently resolved to kill or seriously injure if that is needed to obtain the vehicle has the specified purpose, although he hopes compliance will make harm unnecessary. The condition must not negate the prohibited evil. Compliance with an unlawful demand for the vehicle does not make the threatened injury lawful. Government must still prove actual present conditional intent at the taking, not infer it conclusively from intimidation. The strongest objection is that the completed taking can occur with no harm intended to happen, and importing general model-code provisions risks expanding a federal offense Congress deliberately narrowed. The reply is a construction of the enacted word intent, not elimination of the requirement or adoption of every model-code provision.

**Unconditional-purpose path.** The amended text can instead be read to require an intent that harm actually occur, not readiness to develop or execute such an intention if resisted. This preserves Congress's deliberate narrowing and distinguishes a federal carjacking offense from state robbery. The strongest objection is that genuine settled contingent purpose is still purpose and differs from an idle threat; this interpretation risks adding the word unconditional. An opinion choosing this path must identify a statutory basis, not assume all conditional intent is recklessness. Relief would address the instruction and the dependent judgments, rather than an acquittal on an unadjudicated sufficiency theory or invalidation of the chop-shop convictions.

**Additional probability/belief argument.** Petitioner also argues that the government must prove an objective likelihood of resistance plus his awareness, belief or hope that resistance would occur. The reply invokes the model code's attendant-circumstance and high-probability provisions; government responds that purpose as to conduct/result is the relevant category. This is an argument within statutory construction that can be considered in defining the element. The actual original question is broad enough to permit that rebuttal. It is not established that a distinct instruction with these terms was requested below. The record's general conditional-intent objection does not establish preservation of the distinct requested formulation, and failure to recover a request does not prove forfeiture. A final holding can decide whether the statute contains that extra element without adjudicating an unsupported separate trial-error claim. If a Justice's remedy specifically depends on such a request, its actual source must be established first.
