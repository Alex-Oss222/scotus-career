## Disposition and independent components

All eight Associates would affirm dismissal of the surviving federal constitutional action, with the following grounds and limits. This is a civil challenge; no firearm conviction or sentence exists to affirm or vacate. Settled county and sheriff claims remain dismissed with prejudice. A merits disposition makes further preliminary-injunction relief unnecessary; no reinstatement order or independent damages award follows.

1. **Standing and ripeness:** Hiley's continuing exclusion from armed-deputy work is a concrete employment injury even after reassignment to detention work. The sheriff attributed that exclusion to the statute, and the settlement's express restoration-if-successful term supports redressability. An indirect causal chain is not necessarily speculative. NAGE satisfies Hunt for this legal, prospective challenge through affected members; United Food preserves the distinction between actual member injury and the participation condition. The supported present injury makes this ripe; theoretical pardon or collateral relief possibilities do not eliminate it. No threat of criminal prosecution is invented to support standing.

2. **Preservation and arms:** Footnote 11 establishes abandonment of strict scrutiny, and footnote 16 separately establishes abandonment of procedural due process. Neither becomes a freestanding preserved Second Amendment claim. The Court rejects the district order's collective-only premise insofar as it conflicts with Love/Hale/Rybar, but correction of that premise does not withdraw the recorded abandonment or reopen the settled employment dispute. The surviving equality and substantive-due-process claims receive their properly limited analysis. This case decides no individual private-arms application or universal rule for every misdemeanor.

3. **Commerce:** Reject the presented facial challenge to section 922(g)(9). Its interstate words are relevant but not talismanic. The disqualification operates together with the prohibition on sale or disposition of firearms/ammunition to qualifying domestic-violence misdemeanants in section 922(d)(9) and the federal regulation of their supply. Possession by the excluded recipient is the counterpart of the prohibited supply: allowing the same class to acquire and retain covered arms outside that recipient restriction would permit evasion and materially impair the valid supply regime. Lopez's independently identifiable mechanism, developed in Hale and applied in Kirk, permits necessary inclusion; section 922(g)'s enacted nexus remains a separate offense requirement. No historical movement alone, dangerousness alone, general crime cost or universal firearm police power supplies this ground. No individual weapon's interstate facts are found in this civil case, and no prosecution is excused from proving the actual statutory conditions.

   **Alternative tested:** The commerce words cannot validate every local possession, and Hale's machinegun-stock rule is not automatically a prohibited-person holding. That objection correctly requires identifying the new statute's own mechanism. The fitted supply/recipient relationship supplies it here; the Court does not merely relabel Hale's stock regime. Congress may directly regulate interstate shipment, transportation and receipt, and the presented facial demand cannot eliminate those applications either. No general rule deciding every remote-movement application is necessary.

4. **Equal protection — domestic versus other violent misdemeanors:** On the preserved ordinary-review question, Congress may address domestic violence's risk through a qualifying-conviction firearm rule without disarming every other misdemeanant at once. The statutory force/deadly-weapon and domestic-relationship conditions supply a specific basis, not a bare desire to disadvantage a class. Lee Optical and Beach permit incremental action; Heller/Romer still require a relationship to the actual burden. A narrower or broader reform proposal alone does not defeat this one.

5. **Equal protection — restoration disparity:** Congress may use the convicting jurisdiction's relief arrangements, including expungement, set-aside, pardon and qualified restoration, without guaranteeing identical results across States or between offenses that do and do not carry civil-rights loss. Section 921(a)(33)'s restoration route expressly addresses jurisdictions that provide loss for that offense. The existence of different state consequences does not by itself make the federal selector irrational. The strongest contrary argument is that a less serious offense can have a more persistent consequence; that asymmetry is answered by the rational choice to recognize actual local relief and by the separate relief avenues that remain in the text. No finding is made that every avenue is practically available to Hiley. Caron's state-selector/federal-consequence distinction helps identify the structure; it does not decide whether never-lost rights count as restored, which is unnecessary here.

6. **Equal protection — occupational effect:** The qualifying-conviction restriction applies beyond law-enforcement officers. The supported complaint identifies a greater employment effect for jobs requiring guns, without an allegation that Congress chose the rule to disadvantage officers. Feeney's distinction between uneven effects and discriminatory purpose answers that distinct theory. This does not erase the express official-use classification addressed separately in FOP or make every express classification require hostile intent. FOP now sustains that actual official-use classification on its separate fitted-gap and conduct-based grounds. Hiley's different uneven occupational-effects claim fails for the stated effect/purpose reason; neither theory is substituted for the other.

7. **Substantive due process — employment:** State-created employment interests and a wish to remain in an armed public position do not alone supply a fundamental substantive federal entitlement. The qualifying-conduct firearm rule bears a rational relation to its public-safety objective. Love's state-interest/federal-right distinction and the limited ordinary-review authorities support dismissal of this legislative employment claim. This supplies no ruling that personal arms are never fundamental and no disposition of the abandoned procedural-due-process claim.

8. **Ex Post Facto Clause:** The prohibited firearm conduct occurs after the amendment's effective date. Using an earlier conviction to identify the class does not increase the sentence for that earlier offense or criminalize past firearm possession. The continuing employment consequence does not turn the stated prospective conduct rule into a retrospective offense. No criminal notice or mental-state issue for an uncharged Hiley offense is decided. The clause's retrospective-punishment inquiry is not merged with the separate attainder framework.

9. **Bill of Attainder Clause:** Reject legislative-punishment theory under the historical, functional and purpose inquiries of Nixon and Selective Service System. The general firearm disability follows adjudicated qualifying conduct and regulates future access; it is reasonably fitted to preventing further violence. Its adverse employment effect matters but does not make it a legislatively imposed occupational punishment equivalent to Brown's proscription. No congressional purpose to punish this claimant or occupation is established. A future firearm offense requires ordinary adjudication; no guilt for such an offense is legislatively declared. This does not establish that every disability keyed to a prior conviction is automatically nonpunitive.

10. **Tenth Amendment:** Reject this particular claim independently of the mere existence of commerce authority. The statute regulates possession and supply; it does not direct the sheriff to fire Hiley, enact state law, run a federal background-check system or appropriate funds. Reassignment and lawful staffing choices remain the employer's. New York's prohibition on compelled state regulation and Printz's actual source/function and reserved fiscal rules remain intact; neither supplies immunity from this direct rule governing the relevant conduct.
