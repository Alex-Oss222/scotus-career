## Cleveland v. Policy Management Systems Corp. - May 24, 1999

### Reconciliation result

All eight Associates retain the same disposition and full join at the stated scope. The historical Court unanimously vacated and remanded. Its actual holding rejects both automatic estoppel and the strong negative presumption the Fifth Circuit employed. The lower court recognized possible compatibility, including accommodation and administrative differences, but confined rebuttal to a limited, highly unusual class. Describing its judgment as an exceptionless bar would omit the issue actually decided.

C1 is rejection of that presumption; C2 is the ordinary proof and explanation requirement; C3 is vacatur and remand. The ADA asks about ability to perform essential job functions with or without reasonable accommodation. SSDI applies its own impairment, duration and vocational requirements without the same workplace-accommodation inquiry. Administrative listings, trial-work provisions, different dates and alternative legal theories also prevent a categorical inference that the claims contradict one another. These are possible explanations, not findings that any particular explanation succeeds.

A plaintiff still must produce evidence permitting a reasonable factfinder to find ADA qualification. An apparent inconsistency with an earlier total-disability representation needs an adequate explanation: accepting the earlier statement as true, or accepting that it was made in good faith, a reasonable juror must still be able to find capacity to perform the essential functions at the relevant time with or without reasonable accommodation. Good faith alone does not prove qualification. An explanation based on distinct statutory assumptions does not automatically erase contradictory historical facts. This decision leaves general law governing purely factual contradictions and other statutory interactions undisturbed; it does not adopt every lower-court sham-affidavit formulation cited historically.

### Kennedy: the tested changed premise does not answer his objection

The historical dissent did not overlook the recipient-own-wrong theory. At 526 U.S. 658-663 it expressly addressed the argument that knowing nonresponse subjects a student to discrimination. Kennedy treated agency as necessary but insufficient for teacher attribution, with knowledge and culpable response added; he denied that control of a nonagent peer supplied the same institutional link. At 679-680 he addressed corrective-official notice and regarded the teacher/peer difference as consequential. At 683-684 he preserved possible liability for a school's own gender-discriminatory enforcement, distinguishing it from mere indifference to a student's discrimination. His concerns also included legal limits on school control and the absence of a sufficiently definite funding condition.

In this timeline, Kinman establishes a teacher-harassment actual-notice route to which Kennedy directly joined. That is a concrete prior commitment, but its setting and attribution question remain teacher harassment. Kennedy's public Gebser dissent likewise preserves recipient culpability and rejects broader attribution. The new controlling Gebser conjunction requires school-delegated teacher authority materially enabling discrimination plus the recipient's causal fault. It does not dispense with that authority requirement or place peers within the teacher relationship. Kennedy did not support the new conjunction; its controlling scope nevertheless remains law. Nothing in it decides the peer extension or answers his nonagent objection.

Franklin's rejection of a categorical monetary-remedy objection was also a premise available to the historical dissent; it does not establish recipient attribution. Kinman's known-substantial-danger formulation may affect the knowledge element, but does not turn a peer into a person exercising delegated school authority. NCAA v. Smith separates recipient coverage from attribution and supplies no missing institutional link. Thus the proposed different vote rests on no concrete displaced premise that addresses Kennedy's operative objection. The supported conclusion is affirmance on that objection, without repudiating Kinman's teacher rule, Gebser's controlling teacher-authority rule, or compensation for established recipient discrimination. This is a reconciliation correction, not an automatic instruction to reproduce a historical vote.

### Sufficient peer rule retained by the reversal supporters

These five directly support a recipient's damages responsibility when the following independent conditions are established: receipt of federal assistance; harassment because of sex; actual notice to an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial danger; substantial school control over both the harasser and the relevant educational context; deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that known danger; and a causal contribution to further harassment or vulnerability producing educational deprivation and compensable injury. A peer's acts are not imputed to the recipient merely because the peer attends its school. The recognized wrong is the recipient's own culpable response within its remedial authority.

The educational environment must involve conduct sufficiently severe OR pervasive to be objectively abusive, actually experienced as abusive, and impair educational access or benefit in its full context. This retains Kinman's operative formulation. Age, relationship, setting and actual educational effect are relevant context, not a fixed list of mandatory elements. Sexual subject matter alone does not establish sex-based causation. Neither psychiatric injury, measurable grade loss, physical exclusion nor total loss of schooling is invariably required; none is sufficient by itself. Ordinary teasing or offense without the substantive and institutional elements does not suffice. No universal single-incident rule is decided.

Actual corrective-official notice remains necessary for this sufficient route; generic constructive notice, technical regulatory noncompliance and the harasser's own knowledge do not establish it. Culpability must be more than negligent or unsuccessful correction. A reasonable response is not unlawful merely because harassment continues, and courts do not prescribe a chosen sanction or the victim's preferred remedy. Constitutional and statutory limits on discipline inform the control and response inquiries; substantial control is not assumed over every location, official or student interaction. The plaintiff must establish the causal connection and compensable injury, not only harassment preceding any legally sufficient recipient notice.

This is an express extension of a sufficient recipient-fault route to the school-controlled peer setting, not a claim that Kinman already decided peers. The new route does not transfer Gebser's teacher authority, erase its conjunction, or make ordinary negligence or general agency law controlling. Nor does it purport to reject every distinct ground of direct official discrimination. The earlier teacher rules and the noncontrolling status of broader correction and avoidable-harm proposals are preserved.

### Historical rule difference

**Historical departure:** The reversal supporters retain Kinman's previously adopted severe-or-pervasive, objectively abusive and actually experienced educational-environment rule rather than importing historical Davis's conjunctive severe-and-pervasive formula. Stevens, O'Connor and Souter directly joined the unanimous Kinman substantive rule; Ginsburg and Breyer did too. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also supported broader recipient-attribution grounds in Kinman and Gebser. O'Connor's narrower institutional position is preserved by corrective-official knowledge, culpability, substantial control and causation. For each of these Justices, the concrete changed premise is an already controlling, expressly joined Title IX educational-environment rule that historical Davis did not have. The peer setting is accounted for through the actual relationship, educational deprivation and institutional-control requirements. The earlier rule is neither silently narrowed nor treated as automatic proof that peer conduct is actionable.

The historical majority's own-recipient-wrong, control, response-discretion and causal-exposure reasoning otherwise confirms the supported extension and the pleading disposition. Its harsher conjunctive wording, its teacher-attribution exclusivity descriptions and its use of historical Gebser are not copied into current law. The dissenters do not supply joins to the peer rule merely because they joined Kinman's teacher rule.
