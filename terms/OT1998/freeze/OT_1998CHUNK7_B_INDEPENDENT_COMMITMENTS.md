## Bank of America National Trust & Savings Association v. 203 North LaSalle Street Partnership — May 3

### Threshold, issue and competing paths

Reach the supplied Chapter 11 question after November 2, 1998 argument. The challenged confirmation gives old equity alone the opportunity to contribute new capital and own the reorganized debtor while an impaired senior class objects and is not paid in full. No supported threshold defect prevents review.

**L1 — reverse this exclusive arrangement.** Section 1129(b)(2)(B)(ii) forbids junior holders receiving or retaining property under the plan on account of the junior interest unless the senior unsecured class receives the prescribed full value. The exclusive investment opportunity is itself an economic advantage reserved by reason of old ownership. Payment of capital does not independently account for receiving the exclusive chance to buy. Section 1121 supplies a proposal period, not substantive compliance of every resulting plan with section 1129. This suffices to reject confirmation while leaving survival and lawful scope of a general new-value corollary undecided.

The strongest objection is that new ownership is purchased for present money and judicial valuation, alongside section 1121, can establish equivalence without bidding. That distinguishes investment from gratuitous retention but does not explain why only former owners receive the opportunity. No zero-value finding, market exposure or alternative competitive process is supplied. Ahlers preserves the breadth of property and Code priority while reserving Los Angeles Lumber's actual-money possibility. Noland and Reorganized CF&I preserve statutory exceptions while rejecting judicially preferred ranking; neither already decides this plan question.

**L2 — permit confirmation under a surviving new-value construction.** Necessary, substantial present value could cause the new ownership, rather than old equity; judicial process might protect creditors without an auction. Its strongest difficulty is the additional exclusive opportunity, not demonstrated to have been obtained on an open basis. The plan's exclusivity is not proof of equivalent exchange. These commitments reject L2 here without deciding that all former-owner investment is unlawful.

**L3 — reject every new-value theory.** Enacted priority might support this broader construction, but the exclusive-opportunity issue resolves the confirmation and Ahlers expressly reserved survival. No row requires L3; agreement with L1 is not a vote for L3.

## El Paso Natural Gas Co. v. Neztsosie — May 3

### Threshold, components and alternatives

Reach both components after March 2 argument: **N1**, enlargement of nonappealing plaintiffs' relief by dissolution of existing partial Price-Anderson injunctions; **N2**, deferral of initial statutory coverage to tribal courts under prudential exhaustion. The companies appealed; the plaintiffs did not. Alleged uranium injuries establish neither liability nor coverage.

**N1:** reverse the enlargement. American Railway Express permits an appellee to defend the existing decree on another ground, not obtain a more favorable decree without appeal. The contrary comity argument does not transform defense into an appeal for dissolution. No independent jurisdictional defect requires vacatur regardless of appeal. Do not decide whether every cross-appeal omission is jurisdictional or admits no exception.

**N2:** reverse coverage deferral and have the federal district court itself decide whether the claims qualify as public liability actions. Price-Anderson supplies a federal cause, original jurisdiction, state-court removal and consolidation. National Farmers Union and Iowa Mutual ordinarily favor initial tribal examination, but their judicial comity rule cannot frustrate this specific federal arrangement for covered actions. The federal court must decide the predicate itself.

The strongest competing path requires exhaustion because removal expressly names state courts, not tribal courts. That limit is real: no literal tribal-removal provision should be invented. N2 instead concerns whether judicial comity postpones the already invoked federal court's coverage task. Original federal jurisdiction, the consolidation design and the prudential character of exhaustion support immediate federal coverage determination. Noncovered claims require their own jurisdiction/exhaustion analysis; no universal tribal-tort exception follows.

Strate requires substantive tribal governing authority; completed exhaustion supplies no jurisdictional grant. It does not automatically decide mining torts or their treaty, territorial, consensual and self-government predicates. Mille Lacs preserves instrument-specific rights, not assumed coverage or coverage deferral. Quackenbush distinguishes jurisdiction from prudential surrender and preserves statutory mechanisms.

No public permission to rummage is established. The competing curtilage classifications are not historical facts receiving clear-error protection. The actual layout is known; no missing-facts premise is adopted.

**R1 — affirm a fact-specific no-search conclusion.** Greenwood's exposure/collection rationale applies when cans are deliberately put out for strangers to take in this open, ordinarily used route. Dunn makes neither proximity nor lack of enclosure conclusive. Actual use by both households' visitors is more than hypothetical accessibility. The strongest objection is that passage does not authorize opening closed containers, and rummaging differs from natural observation. R1 answers through the combined actual collection exposure and relinquishment of refuse, not a universal license to search effects or protected residential spaces.

**R2 — hold the inspection a search.** Closed containers adjoining the attached garage remain protected in this immediate residential setting; approach and collection access confer no unrestricted investigative access before collection. Greenwood concerns outside-curtilage exposure. Fields and Carter reject automatic publication from limited sharing and direct attention to the actual vantage. The strongest objection is the established ordinary approach and deliberate collection placement. R2 answers that those facts permit passing or collection, while police physically inspect concealed contents. This protects this home-side intrusion without overruling Greenwood or making every refuse container private.

**R3 — vacate for corrected legal application.** Reject discard intent and bare accessibility as substitutes for examining actual residential protection and police access, while preserving the supported facts and a possible lawful-search conclusion. The strongest objection is that the lower majority already invoked Dunn and considered the layout. R3 identifies the legal correction rather than denying those facts: future transfer and a lawful common approach do not alone establish authority to rummage in protected residential space. It does not infer a record deficiency from absence of the word curtilage or require a new hearing.

## Saenz v. Roe — May 17

### Corrected posture and alternatives

Reach the pure legal challenge on certiorari to Roe v. Anderson, 134 F.3d 1400, affirming a single judge's **preliminary injunction**, after January 13 argument. California limits bona fide new residents' benefits for less than twelve months to prior-State amounts, regardless of previous receipt or migration motive. Section 604(c) permits but does not require this treatment. No permanent decree, three-judge tribunal or direct appeal is assumed.

**S1 — affirm preliminary relief and reject the differential as unconstitutional.** A United States citizen may establish bona fide residence in another State and become its citizen on the same terms as other resident citizens. The Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship and Privileges or Immunities provisions protect that equal state citizenship. The State may not postpone equal ordinary welfare treatment for one year because the prior State paid less. Shapiro supplies the closely related prohibition on durational welfare penalties; Dunn distinguishes bona fide residence from additional duration; Zobel limits allocating ordinary resident benefits by arrival history. Slaughter-House's specific recognition of becoming another State's citizen supplies a narrow textual route without reopening its entire construction. Saving money is legitimate generally, but selecting newly arrived citizens merely restates the disadvantage rather than justifying it.

The strongest objection is that newcomers receive no less than before moving and the period is temporary, unlike Shapiro's complete denial. S1 compares current citizens within their present State, not their former State. Lesser deprivation and eventual equality do not remove current unequal citizenship. This is equality within the chosen program, not an entitlement to a program, fixed amount or national welfare level.

**S2 — sustain the temporary benefit rule.** Immediate entry and continued prior-rate assistance distinguish a transition from denial of a fundamental entitlement. Ordinary fiscal review could sustain that distinction. Its strongest difficulty is direct discrimination among bona fide citizens and Shapiro's welfare holding. S2 requires a narrower travel-penalty rule and refusal to treat every ordinary state benefit as a national citizenship privilege. Congressional permission cannot support it if the Constitution independently forbids the classification.

**S3 — affirm on travel/equal-protection law while reserving P&I.** This is a lawful narrower rationale if historical scope blocks a Clause join. It yields the same practical equality and federal-permission result, but is not a vote for an additional P&I holding. Rows state whether the reservation is necessary.

**S4 — avoid merits because relief is preliminary.** Unnecessary here: the question is legal and the ongoing reduction supports the actual interim posture. A legal holding may accompany affirmance of preliminary relief without converting it into a final decree.
