## Competing institutional paths and concrete discrepancy review

Immediate correction is lawful and supported: the lower judgment presents the federal question, authenticated facts satisfy the settled rule, and Rule 16 permits reasoned summary merits action. Its strongest objection is changing a criminal defendant's favorable judgment without respondent's Supreme Court opposition where counsel's stated bar position is known. A focused opposing presentation could identify a genuinely preserved threshold, scope or remedial objection. That possibility does not prove any such objection exists or require the Court to manufacture one.

Deferring immediate adverse action to obtain opposing presentation is also lawful. Rule 15.1 permits a response request; an actual request would impose an obligation prospectively. Its strongest objection is delaying correction of an explicit settled-law error that is already adequately presented. The deferral position concerns the process before correction, not preservation of the extra-exigency rule or a finding that the lower result is substantively correct. It does not fix the eventual grant-or-deny choice or predict the contents or effect of a response.

The response refresh chose Stevens/Ginsburg for deferral and retained Breyer for immediate action. Full comparison supports Stevens's deferral, but revises the other two. For Ginsburg, the cited Labron/Kilgore dissent concerns actual Pennsylvania state-ground reasoning and the expected utility of federal correction. Those specific concerns are absent from the explicitly federal Maryland judgment; her historical Dyson vote was for immediate action even with the same nonfiling and counsel facts now supplied. Her simulated Houghton passenger-effect position and West's separate preservation remand do not create a categorical opposing-brief prerequisite or alter any present vehicle predicate. The fresh neutral fact is new to the model's knowledge, but is not a changed premise from Ginsburg's historical decision. No concrete Justice-specific alteration supports moving her to deferral after the complete source review.

For Breyer, the historical separate writing directly considers the same nonfiling and counsel circumstances, expressly accepts the federal rule, and still rejects immediate summary reversal without seeking qualified opposing presentation. His earlier Labron/Houghton merits joins therefore do not answer this institutional objection. Neither simulated West nor the dated Rules displaces it: summary merits authority and discretionary opposition were consistent with the historical setting, and permission is not obligation. His provisional view that the settled legal issue eliminates any need for additional presentation lacks a material changed premise. Further source review therefore supports his response-first position. This is not an automatic restoration of history: the amended source facts, actual Labron separate reasoning, Houghton distinction, Rules permissions and alternative remedies were all compared individually.

The historical dissent specifically proposed inviting an attorney to respond as amicus. That verifies the concern about qualified opposing presentation; it does not establish that a Court appointment, admission arrangement or invitation has occurred in this timeline. The reconciled minority commitment is to oppose immediate summary reversal pending an opportunity for such opposing presentation. The supplied Rule 15 response route remains a lawful supported alternative; no particular representation arrangement, compulsory bar membership, new deadline or future noncompliance is ordered or predicted by this handoff. Under the six's immediate disposition, the minority's explanation creates no separate operative response order.
