## The method-election ground

**Operative proposition.** On the supported account of this proceeding, a prisoner who was offered injection as the alternative and default execution method, affirmatively selected gas, and then reaffirmed that choice after an express opportunity to select injection cannot obtain relief here on the objection that execution by the selected gas method is unconstitutional when he makes no present Eighth Amendment challenge to injection. These seven Justices treat the actual repeated selection as waiver of this particular method objection; they do not declare every capital Eighth Amendment protection waivable or every chosen punishment constitutional.

**Authority and scope of agreement.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas adopt this proposition on the reported repeated-choice record. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join at the same operative scope, expressly carrying the qualified present nonchallenge-to-injection premise. For this case those positions directly agree; no logical-subset or Marks construction is needed. None makes injection's universal constitutionality a holding, infers the absence of an injection claim in another proceeding, or treats an unrecovered pleading as a litigant failure. The proposition is not a universal requirement that every method challenger propose an alternative.

**Explanation and contrary argument.** The relevant action is not a failure to use unfamiliar procedures or silence in the face of an available claim. Walter made the affirmative selection and reaffirmed it after the Governor offered reconsideration on March 1. Injection is the statutory default and, on the supported current account, is not challenged in this proceeding. The general intentional-relinquishment principle identified in *Johnson v. Zerbst*, 304 U.S. 458, 464, supports the legal consequence these Justices attach to that conduct; their conclusion is confined to this actual method election. It is not a global finding about counsel advice, mental capacity, every constitutional right or every circumstance in which two methods are offered.

The strongest objection is that the initial Ninth Circuit made election a prerequisite to adjudicating the gas claim, so selecting gas was the act that made the claim ripe rather than an express declaration abandoning it. That history is real and prevents characterizing all the renewed timing as unexplained manipulation. It does not, for these seven, eliminate the consequence of the later opportunity to avoid the particular challenged method through an alternative unchallenged here. A procedural opportunity to assert a ripe claim is not a guarantee of favorable disposition of its merits or equitable remedy. The actual appellate panel proceeded to the method dispute and issued relief; no uncompleted hearing or mandatory additional procedural step has been established that must precede deciding this discrete legal consequence.

Stevens regards that answer as insufficient for summary adjudication. The question whether a prisoner may consent to a punishment that would otherwise be constitutionally unacceptable is important and unresolved in the form presented; he would require full briefing and argument before resolving it. His objection is to the proposed summary legal conclusion, not a finding that injection is challenged here or that no reconsideration offer occurred. He does not adopt the lower court's categorical rule that every Eighth Amendment protection is unwaivable in every capital case.

**Effect on the lower judgment.** This ground defeats the current gas-method objection at the stated scope and is not merely a temporary assessment that the State might eventually prevail. The seven support reversal of the appellate judgment insofar as it grants relief on that objection and vacatur of the gas-specific injunction. Any return is for proceedings consistent with that limited final resolution, not an instruction to reopen the same waived method objection through generic cause reconsideration. The disposition decides no unidentified independent claim, removes no other order, rescinds no certificate by implication, commands no execution, and finds no execution occurred. It does not adjudicate the empirical constitutionality of gas or injection in other settings.

## The separate cause ground and counsel limits

The actual 1983 gas litigation in *Gray v. Lucas* supplied the general pain-and-lingering-death theory with medical and eyewitness support before direct review ended in 1987. ## Other component commitments and lawful alternatives

**Review channel.** All eight preserve *Martinez-Villareal* Part I and *Felker*: a distinct ruling that authorization is unnecessary is reviewable, while an actual §2244(b)(3) authorization decision is not ordinarily reviewable by appeal or certiorari. Review addresses that distinct judgment and injunction; it does not purport to reverse an unquoted authorization order. A petition grant supplies no exemption from the authorization-review bar.

**Successive classification.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain their proposed confined extension of the first-merits principle to a method claim previously presented and withheld as premature until election. O'Connor and Kennedy reserve final classification. Scalia and Thomas retain their contrary view that a genuinely new successive application repeating this claim falls within §2244(b)(1), conditional on the actual application character and reviewable classification. A new claim would require §2244(b)(2)'s different conditions; its exceptions do not rescue a repeated claim under paragraph (1). No reliable new guilt-innocence evidence or existing Supreme Court rule made retroactive is established by a method claim alone. **AEDPA timing and merits predicates.** All eight retain the provision-specific *Lindh*/*Carriger* rule applied in current *Coleman* Holding IV. **Certificate and required process.** The appellate certificate is an actual procedural fact. The State needs no certificate for its own appellate proceeding. *Hohn* supplies review of judicial certificate determinations, not automatic certificate issuance or success. No Justice rescinds the existing certificate as an incidental remedy. *Lonchar* and *Barefoot* preserve actually required meaningful process, lawful screening, procedural disposition and expedition. None supplies a new hearing or indefinite stay from a pending paper alone.

**Independent equity.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain the narrower interim alternative under their *Gomez* and *Lonchar* Parts I–II positions: even assuming an open procedural route, the renewed offer to avoid the challenged gas method through an alternative unchallenged here defeats continued support for this particular restraint.
