## Exact legal propositions and join boundaries

**T1 — Actual functioning with measures actually used.** Section12102(2)(A)’s “substantially limits” and “of such individual” call for assessment of the individual’s actual limitations with the treatment he actually uses. A severe untreated impairment does not by itself establish actual disability if treatment removes substantial limitation. Positive effects, remaining impairments and negative effects of the actual treatment must all count. No hypothetical perfectly treated person, obligation to accept a different drug or dose, or blanket exclusion of controlled chronic disease follows. Treatment burdens can be evidence of disability when they substantially restrict an identified major activity.

**T2 — Underlying impairment without amelioration.** Stevens and Breyer instead assess the individual’s underlying impairment without subtracting the ameliorative effects of medication. The statutory separation of protected status from qualification, accommodation and safety, the longstanding administrative guidance and the reported legislative explanation support that construction. The individual must still have an impairment that would substantially limit a major activity; diagnosis alone is not sufficient. Actual successful treatment does important work in the separate inquiry into ability and permissible employment decisions. T2 does not require stopping medication, prove that this employer discriminated unlawfully, or exempt the claimant from individual evidence.

**T3 — Guidance and interpretive authority.** The six T1 supporters reject the no-mitigation guidance insofar as it substitutes untreated hypothetical limitations for the actual substantial limitation the statute requires. Haggar/NFFE require enforcement of the statute and the agency’s authority before deference. Auer’s considered-own-regulation rule does not authorize a construction inconsistent with the governing statutory requirement. They do not hold that an amicus interpretation is categorically ineligible, that notice and comment never matters, or that the EEOC lacks every relevant delegated power. A categorical resolution of all authority to issue general-definition regulations is unnecessary to their statutory conclusion.

The two T2 supporters find the guidance persuasive and consistent with the statute; their result does not require deciding every delegation dispute. They regard the government’s invocation of Auer as substantial, but join no blanket rule that agency counsel’s statutory position or employer-specific factual assertion is controlling. All eight retain the governing Auer/Haggar boundaries at their actual scope. Bragdon reserved the mitigation question; it supplies no already controlling T1 or T2 answer.

**F1 — Actual-treatment reconsideration.** Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg support T1 but would vacate the actual-disability determination. The circuit moved from the physician’s general daily-function answer to noncoverage without addressing the preserved counterevidence about endurance, lifting and sustained overhead activity, recurring medication-related gout and other reported effects. JA130–138 expressly disputes the supposed absence of limitations. That evidence cannot be erased by describing treatment as control or normal functioning as conceded. Ordinary reconsideration must evaluate the nature, duration and degree of supported limits in the appropriate major activities and distinguish current treatment effects from those of abandoned or stronger regimens. The Court neither finds all symptoms continuously present nor declares them substantially limiting as a matter of law. This is review of the same actual-disability claim and the application of the granted mitigation question, not a new medication-injury claim or an ungranted qualification decision.

**F2 — Treated-state insufficiency.** O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas would instead affirm actual-disability noncoverage under T1. They consider the preserved evidence rather than rely on waiver or concession. In their assessment, restrictions on very heavy exertion, use of levers/carts, moderated exercise, dietary care and occasional sensory symptoms do not demonstrate the required significant restriction compared with the average person; the general statements that gout can incapacitate do not establish the necessary extent of actual substantial limitation at the relevant time. The severe stuttering, memory, sleep and sexual effects associated with stronger attempts at normalization cannot be automatically assigned to the compromise regimen actually used. They do not require continuous incapacity, a numerical threshold, complete inability or exhaustive medical proof in every case. Their conclusion is this record’s insufficiency, not that all medicated hypertension or all episodic impairments fall outside the Act. No finding of absence of hypertension or absence of every symptom follows.

**F3 — Underlying-condition application.** Stevens and Breyer hold the evidence of Murphy’s own longstanding severe untreated hypertension, the actual readings and the medical account of its consequences sufficient to prevent the present summary judgment under T2. No finding of existing heart, kidney or retinal damage is made; the absence of the tests needed to diagnose such damage cannot be converted into either presence or absence. They do not adjudicate each claimed activity, qualification or remedy. Independently, if T1 is adopted, they support F1’s return for proper consideration of residual and treatment limitations rather than affirm under F2.

**W1 — Perceived substantial limitation is separate from motive and legal justification.** Section12102(2)(C) asks whether the employer regarded the individual as having the required substantially limiting impairment. Awareness of hypertension, rejection for a job and an asserted regulatory reason are not individually conclusive. A legitimate motive or good-faith legal judgment does not categorically defeat coverage; an incorrect certification judgment does not automatically establish it. The circuit’s “regulatory reason, therefore no regarded-as disability” inference is too broad. Qualification and direct threat remain distinct from coverage, as Bragdon and Cleveland require within their respective scopes.

**W2 — Working standard.** For the working theory presented, apply§1630.2(j)’s class-of-jobs or broad-range standard, comparing similar training, knowledge, skills and abilities and the relevant accessible labor market. Inability to perform a single particular job is insufficient; complete unemployment is not required. Neither a national certificate count nor subsequent employment alone decides the issue. A perceived limitation can span employers without necessarily constituting a sufficiently broad class, and some remaining work does not necessarily defeat a broad limitation. No mandatory occupational percentage, vacancy survey or requirement of exclusion from every mechanic job is adopted. The Court need not decide every possible challenge to treating working as a major activity to resolve the parties’ theory on this assumed regulatory basis.

**W3 — No sufficient perceived class-wide limitation on this record.** O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg would sustain rejection of the regarded-as route under W1–W2, rather than the circuit’s categorical motive rationale. The evidence supports UPS’s view that Murphy could not perform the particular mechanic position’s commercial-driving component; it does not show UPS perceived him unable to perform a sufficiently broad group of jobs using his mechanical skills. His long mechanic employment and prompt return to ordinary mechanic work support the distinction but are not conclusive admissions or proof of every employer perception. The vocational evidence supports continued access to substantial alternative work without turning its category totals into local vacancies. The geographic reach of the DOT certificate identifies a common requirement, not by itself the relevant breadth of perceived occupational exclusion. The physicians’ own heavy-work opinions are not automatically UPS’s perception. Their conclusion remains limited to this showing and does not announce that commercial driving can never form a class of work.

**W4 — Return the perception/class inquiry.** Stevens, Souter and Breyer instead would vacate the regarded-as determination. The employer invoked a qualification common to commercial-driving employment, not merely a unique local preference. Combined with the stated hypertension ground, that permits further consideration of the breadth of perceived limitation under the actual regulatory standard. The circuit did not conduct that inquiry and its motive rationale is erroneous. These three neither declare Murphy regarded as disabled as a matter of law nor infer all doctors’ opinions were known to UPS. They reject a categorical conclusion that mechanical jobs remaining available necessarily resolve the breadth of the claimed excluded group. Ordinary remand may still yield employer judgment if evidence of the relevant class, geography, abilities or actual perception is insufficient; no new expert proceeding or research-driven production duty is ordered.

**R — Bounded scope and remedy.** A vacatur removes the specified coverage grounds, not every independent district-court ground and not the employer’s eventual ability to prevail. The lower courts address preserved qualification, reasonable accommodation and federal-law defenses only if necessary. Section391.41(b)(6)’s safety language,§391.43(f)’s further-tests language, the advisory criteria, the issued health card and temporary-certification arguments cannot be collapsed into an undisputed absolute160/90 prohibition. Nor do they establish automatic legal qualification. The government’s account of advisory status is considered within its source role; no unseen cover letter or actually issued temporary certificate is invented. No Supreme Court waiver/exhaustion finding is made from incomplete source recovery.

## Competing paths and strongest counterarguments

**T1 versus T2.** T1’s strongest statutory basis is the actual individual limitation language. Its hardest objection is that a person dependent on substantial treatment can lose protection precisely because treatment permits work, contrary to the guidance and the coverage/qualification separation. T1 supporters answer that the statute separately requires disability, that actual burdens and regarded-as/record-of branches retain work, and that coverage cannot be made coextensive with every potentially serious untreated condition. T2 supporters answer the present-tense objection by treating the underlying individual impairment, not a hypothetical generic diagnosis, as the relevant statutory condition and locating successful mitigation in the distinct employment inquiries. Neither side imports same-day Sutton or treats Bragdon’s mitigation reservation as a settled answer.

**F1 versus F2.** F1 is supported by the contemporaneously disputed testimony and the appellate reliance on a general answer. Its strongest objection is that preserved disagreement is not necessarily a genuine material dispute: several effects belong to stronger regimens, restrictions on extraordinary exertion may not be substantial compared with ordinary people, and some symptoms are described conditionally. F1 supporters therefore require reconsideration of the actual relevant evidence, not a finding that each item qualifies or a compulsory trial. F2 supporters decide those limitations on the supplied proof; their strongest objection is that cumulative exertional and episodic limits may significantly alter the condition, manner or duration of ordinary activity even if Murphy works, and that selecting the general doctor answer risks credibility resolution. They maintain insufficiency without denying the testimony or demanding unremitting disability. The difference is substantive application of Rule56 and the definition, not missing research treated as litigant default.

**W3 versus W4.** W3 has support in the ordinary mechanic work, vocational evidence and absence of a supported broad perception beyond certification-dependent work. Its hardest objection is that an industry-wide qualification can exclude a real occupational class even while some mechanically skilled work remains. W4 therefore returns the actual comparison and employer-perception inquiry without declaring every federal certification exclusion substantial. Its hardest objection is that national coverage of a rule is not evidence of the relevant accessible jobs, training comparison or the employer’s perceived inability. The three W4 supporters do not presume those facts; they find the circuit’s categorical legal shortcut insufficient to sustain its disposition on this record. All eight reject both a per-se regulatory-motive defense and automatic coverage from the fact of discharge.

**Ungran​ted alternatives.** The respondent may defend the existing judgment on a properly supported ground, but no final alternative here is established without deciding disputed certification consequences, the role of advisory criteria, essential functions or accommodation. An independent Supreme Court ruling on those matters is unnecessary to the affirming three and unsupported as a short route around the vacating five’s coverage conclusions. On remand, no assumed default, temporary certificate, categorical safety exemption or final employment entitlement is supplied.
