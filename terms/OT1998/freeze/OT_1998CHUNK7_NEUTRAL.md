### Issue 1: Recognition of a protective-function privilege

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Jaffee v. Redmond*, 518 U.S. 1, recognizes a Rule 501 privilege only after examining established confidential relationships, public and private interests served by confidentiality, evidentiary costs, and administrable boundaries. Once recognized, the privilege is not subjected to ad hoc balancing in every case. Swidler & Berlin (June 25, 1998) preserves established attorney-client confidentiality after death and rejects investigative-need balancing within that privilege. It does not recognize a privilege for observations by protective agents; identifying a protected relationship is analytically prior to deciding whether need can overcome it.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Psychotherapist-patient privilege.
* **Other Governing Law:** Federal Rule of Evidence 501; the federal grand jury's evidentiary authority; surviving authority including *United States v. Nixon*, *Trammel v. United States*, and *Branzburg v. Hayes*.
* **Material Other Authority:** Separation-of-powers principles recognize the President's protective needs but do not themselves create an evidentiary privilege for observations by protective personnel.

### Issue 2: Absolute or qualified scope and overcoming need

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Jaffee* rejects routine case-by-case balancing after recognition of a categorical confidential-communications privilege, but it does not support recognition of a privilege lacking the required predicate or a privilege covering ordinary observations rather than protected communications.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Psychotherapist-patient privilege.
* **Other Governing Law:** Rule 501; ordinary grand-jury relevance and law-enforcement need; judicial authority to use sealing, protective orders, and tailored questioning where independently lawful.
* **Open Point:** No current simulated law establishes a distinct overcoming test for a Secret Service privilege because no such privilege has been recognized.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit affirmed the District Court for the District of Columbia's refusal to recognize the asserted privilege and its order compelling Secret Service testimony before a federal grand jury. The public appellate caption remained *In re Sealed Case*.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** The Executive argues that compelled protective-agent testimony threatens presidential trust and security. The prosecutor invokes the grand jury’s entitlement to relevant evidence and disputes the empirical premise and boundaries of the requested privilege. Jaffee supplies a recognition inquiry, not an automatic privilege for every important confidential function.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A new privilege requires a legally supported relationship and administrable scope; if recognized, routine balancing cannot simply erase it. Rejecting this proposed privilege leaves actual security protections and other properly asserted privileges available without inventing a categorical overcoming test.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** A three-judge district court permanently enjoined the one-year durational benefit rule.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** New residents challenge reduced benefits determined by recent migration and their former State’s benefit level. California invokes fiscal interests and federal statutory permission. Bona fide residency and equal treatment of current citizens differ from a legitimate residence-verification rule.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If the classification violates equal citizenship or travel protection, congressional permission cannot save it. A lawful neutral eligibility requirement remains enforceable. Relief concerns the first-year differential rather than a general judicial entitlement to a particular welfare amount.
