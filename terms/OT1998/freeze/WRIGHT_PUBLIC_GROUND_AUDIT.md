## Proposition-level compatibility and operative qualifications

| Proposition | Supported scope and legal basis | Boundary necessary to the confirmed join |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1: coverage | Love's personal keeping/bearing rule and necessary-acquisition principle, Hale's weapon/conduct/restriction separation, and Rybar's preservation of coverage despite commercial setting support an antecedent scope inquiry grounded in text, history and those holdings. | Necessary ammunition and transportation are protected only as genuine incidents of constitutionally protected keeping or bearing. Love uses Andrews as qualified historical evidence; it does not make every ammunition type, transport mode or firearm covered. No public-carry, licensing, proficiency or training application is decided. |
| Stage 2: practical burden | The four may distinguish the degree of actual interference after coverage is established. Burdick provides a concrete analogy for graduated protection; Turner II and Madsen demonstrate the importance of actual operation, fit, meaningful alternatives and different standards for different burdens. | A statutory label, nominal permission or the presence of some alternative arms does not by itself determine the category. The Court must identify what protected activity the actual rule leaves available and what it prevents. No universal numerical threshold, independent empirical-study duty or automatic hearing follows. |
| Core deprivation | A deprivation of the protected core requires a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and no comparably effective materially less restrictive alternative. This is the strongest tier for a genuine core deprivation, not a rule that all arms regulation receives strict scrutiny. | The trigger must concern the practical deprivation of activity already determined to fall within the protected core. The government cannot lower scrutiny merely by calling the restriction a safety measure. Conversely, a burden's existence alone does not establish a core deprivation. Actual supported alternatives matter; no court must imagine every conceivable arrangement or demand a new evidentiary study in every case. |
| Substantial regulation | An important objective, substantial relationship and a restriction that does not unnecessarily or effectively eliminate protected activity provide an intermediate fit inquiry. Turner II is especially relevant to the four's treatment of substantial burdens, legislative latitude and actual alternatives. | This tier does not impose the strict tier's separate no-comparably-effective-materially-less-restrictive-alternative requirement. Alternatives remain evidence of fit. Neither the government's desirable end nor generic dangerousness establishes the necessary relationship. The category does not immunize a practical elimination simply because a statute preserves formal exceptions. |
| Incidental regulation | Deferential reasonableness is compatible with all four's recognition that ordinary administration can coexist with a real constitutional right when it creates no meaningful interference. Their Hale registration joins supply the direct arms example. | The absence of meaningful interference must characterize the actual restriction and preserved claim. Arbitrary administration, discrimination and cumulative destruction remain reviewable. A denial or effective prohibition cannot acquire this status merely from the word registration or from the fact that different arms remain obtainable. |
| Categorical home minimum | A government cannot abolish meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms in the home. The justification is the practical preservation of the settled personal right, not a presumption that every possession restriction fails. Love's necessary-acquisition reasoning rejects formal recognition emptied of practical effect; the four's Ladue joins supply an analogous anti-elimination principle for a distinctive constitutionally protected activity at home. | This is a systemic minimum of protected ordinary defensive possession. It does not resolve every person's eligibility, every weapon's coverage, every method of storage, or every restriction affecting a home. It does not make all arms claimed as defensive ordinary protected arms. Individual disqualification, the actual machineguns and all reserved carrying/training questions require their own legal application. A wholesale abolition cannot be saved merely by satisfying an interest-balancing tier. |

The last row is independent of the severity hierarchy. The strict tier can address serious restrictions that stop short of abolishing meaningful ordinary defensive possession; it is not a permission to abolish that minimum whenever government asserts a compelling interest. Conversely, the categorical boundary is not an automatic holding that Wright's automatic weapons are protected ordinary defensive arms. That question remains within the returned application. The formulation preserves both functions without deciding the machinegun merits through a home-defense label.

## Registration, remand and remaining components

**Registration:** All eight Associates join affirmance of the section 5861(d) pipe-bomb conviction on Hale's independent tax-administration and separate-registration ground. O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg additionally join the incidental-category application to this preserved challenge. Its basis is the administration of lawful making and transfer through identification and registration, without a supported denial, legal impossibility or material burden claim requiring another category. Meaningful ordinary defense remaining available is consistent with that result, but cannot be the sole reason to classify a restriction as incidental. The analysis neither excludes every explosive from coverage nor validates prosecution when lawful registration is unavailable. Section 5841's registration to the transferee through the transferor, section 5812's advance transfer approval and denial for unlawful transfer/receipt/possession, and section 5822's separate making approval and denial for unlawful making/possession remain distinct. No universal duty of a possessor to obtain late registration is created. The four narrower Associates' registration joins do not adopt the incidental tier as a general rule.

**Machinegun remand:** All eight support vacatur of the appellate affirmance of the single machinegun count. The four framework joiners instruct application of both stages; the others support the same disposition on Love, Hale and Rybar's existing ground. One completed .223-caliber automatic weapon and one disassembled .50-caliber machinegun remain within one count. The required statutory-branch predicates remain distinct where material. There is no finding about unreported components, no holding that burial or preparedness establishes an additional criminal purpose, and no transfer of the pipe bombs to this possession count. Existing-record decision is permitted; ordinary further proceedings occur only as needed. No automatic hearing, study, trial, acquittal, release or return of arms follows. The derivative jury-instruction request returns only to the extent procedurally material under the reserved plea; no instruction is directed.

**Commerce and derivative Tenth objection:** All eight retain Hale/Kirk's anti-evasion or material-impairment ground for the actual transfer/stock regime, not generic dangerousness, marketability, remote provenance or crime costs. Section 922(o)(2)(A)'s specified governmental transfer/possession channels remain distinct from section 922(o)(2)(B)'s lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986. Manufacture before that date alone does not establish the latter exception. No new commerce element or independent commandeering claim appears.

**Ninth Amendment:** All eight reject only the unestablished independent exemption for this possession/nonregistration; they do not deny the existence of retained rights or decide a new substantive-due-process theory.
