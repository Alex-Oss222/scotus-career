### Issue 1: Standing and official-use classification

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission and the completed United Food v. Brown Group decision distinguish constitutional member injury from the prudential individual-participation requirement.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The chief members allege operational injury and exposure for supplying guns. That supports review of the asserted official-use discrimination without assuming standing for every individual officer’s private-possession claim.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III; Fifth Amendment equal protection; 18 U.S.C. §§921(a)(33),922(d)(9),922(g)(9),925(a)(1),928.

### Issue 2: Arms and rationality

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Love v. Pepersack (December 11, 1995) recognizes a personal arms right, incorporation and necessary acquisition without militia enrollment. Hale (February 12, 1996) and Rybar (November 24, 1997) reject both automatic military-utility immunity and affiliation-based exclusion. Their arms remands leave ultimate machinegun applications open; no comprehensive scrutiny tier, automatic-weapons exclusion or mandatory evidentiary hearing was adopted.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The government cannot deny a personal right on a collective-militia premise. Nor does personal protection create a right to government employment or a special official-use exemption. Both the firearm disability’s justification and the alleged discrimination require examination.
* **Other Governing Law:** Cleburne; Heller v. Doe (1993); Williamson v. Lee Optical.

### Issue 3: Limits of commerce and federalism

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lopez (April 26, 1995) requires an identified interstate mechanism before aggregation. Hale, Rybar and Kirk (February 14, 1998) sustain §922(o)’s possession component as necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of the national post-1986 machinegun transfer/stock regime. Neither marketability, remote interstate provenance nor generic crime costs alone establishes federal power.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Section 922(g)(9) is a different statute from the machinegun stock prohibition. Its interstate element and relationship to federal restrictions on supply to prohibited persons require their own analysis. New York and Printz distinguish direct regulation from compelled state administration.
* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses; Tenth Amendment.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** D.C. Circuit reversal of the district court’s summary judgment for the government. The selected August 28, 1998 opinion extended the official-use exception by invalidating its exclusion of domestic-violence misdemeanants.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** The association argues that misdemeanants receive a harsher official-use disability than more culpable felons and invokes a personal right. The government argues that the amendment closes a practical enforcement gap left by misdemeanor treatment of domestic abuse and that official employment does not eliminate the danger.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Standing and the proper third-party scope come first. The court may sustain the exclusion, invalidate it with a supported severability remedy, or remand genuinely undecided preserved claims. Invalidating an exception does not automatically justify lifting every possession prohibition.
