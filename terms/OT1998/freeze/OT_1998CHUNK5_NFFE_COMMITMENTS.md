## Common reach and discrete components

All eight Associates participate. Both petitions concern the same Fourth Circuit judgment refusing enforcement. The agency finding against the Survey and dismissal as to Interior are distinct; review supplies no ground to revive the dismissed interference allegation or the uncharged agreement-disapproval claim. No established jurisdiction, mootness or section 7123(c) presentation defect bars this controversy. NFFE expressly presents both general statutory duty and negotiability of the advance clause, and both were administrative grounds. FLRA's decision not to repeat a foreclosed argument in the circuit is not a factual withdrawal of either administrative ground. The ordinary presented-question standard (effective *Lebron*, February 21, 1995) permits deciding the included argument but does not require the broadest answer.

Components: (A) whether sections 7102, 7103 and 7114 establish or leave discretion over a general union-initiated midterm duty; (B) whether bargaining over the actual advance clause is mandatory notwithstanding absence of a general duty; (C) whether the actual two-ground administrative decision should be enforced or reconsidered. The clause concerns negotiable uncovered matters, not unrestricted reopening. Covered-matter, lawful waiver, management-right and federal-law limits remain distinct. Agreed zipper clauses are possible; compulsory negotiation or imposition of a zipper clause is not established. Bargaining does not itself command agreement, but lawful impasse authority remains real and conditional.

## Supported alternative paths

**Enforce on the independent clause ground.** Absence of a statutory entitlement to substantive terms is not itself a prohibition on negotiating them. The actual order expressly adopts this distinction, rejects the contrary circuit rule, and addresses contractual repose by the covered-matter boundary. Sections 7103(a)(14)(C) and 7117(a) perform different work: the former excludes matters specifically settled by federal statute; the latter excludes inconsistent proposals. Neither objection succeeds merely by asserting that a statute which does not command a universal midterm regime has specifically prohibited a contractual one. The strongest counterargument is that a general advance clause, backed by section 7119, defeats a deliberate public institutional choice to confine compulsory negotiations to term agreements and management changes. This requires proving that limiting choice from the enacted structure; the contingent Panel route alone does not prove it. Enforcement would sustain the order to bargain on request with its original ancillary provisions and no retroactivity, not impose the clause. General statutory-duty construction can remain undecided if this independent ground is sufficient.

**Correct the categorical prohibition and return the agency questions.** The text does not directly specify the timing and extent of a general midterm obligation. The broad agreement definition and bargaining rights favor a continuing duty; the agreement-directed obligation, management structure and public impasse mechanism support term stability. Section 7105 entrusts the Authority with administration inside these limits. Rejecting the circuit's categorical prohibition can therefore leave the agency to determine the proper relationship between a general duty, contract provisions, waiver and efficiency. The strongest counterargument is the actual order's separate contractual rationale and its express refusal to return to IRS I: a remand cannot be justified simply by calling that rationale nonexistent. A permissible remand must explain why the existing legal setting affected the scope judgment and require genuine reconsideration rather than mechanically select the same result. The competing enforcement position remains available if the adopted contractual distinction already supplies all necessary reasons.

**Affirm the circuit.** The contrary construction reads section 7114(a)(4)'s agreement-directed duty, section 7106's particular management-change arrangements and section 7101(b)'s efficiency direction together as limiting compulsory bargaining to the prescribed regime. The most substantial objection is the absence of explicit temporal language or an express ban on advance contracts; voluntary particular bargaining and the broad definition cannot be ignored. On this path the distinction is between a permissible particular agreement and a general compulsory future regime, not between all discussion and all prohibition. The opinion would have to identify that structural limit and reject the independent clause ground, rather than rely solely on no general duty. Neither historical Council practice nor disputed legislative-history recitations are an independently established premise here.

## Proposition and remedy boundaries

The provisional positions produce three enforcement votes (Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg), two vacatur/agency-return votes (O'Connor, Breyer), and three affirmance votes (Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas). These are different formal dispositions; no Court majority or equal division is declared before Stone and reconciliation. The five rejecting the circuit's categorical prohibition do not thereby join the same final treatment of the current order. An opinion asserting five votes for enforcement, a universal statutory duty, or a final no-duty holding would exceed these commitments.

For Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, the decisive prohibition is their stated structural reading; section 7103(a)(14)(C) is not treated as a separate automatic sufficient ground. For the enforcement group, independent contract negotiability does not require final acceptance or imply an unlimited general-duty holding. For the remand group, the original order's independent reasoning must be acknowledged and evaluated; no claim of absent agency reasons or forfeited party proof is permitted. A later circulated narrower disposition can be tested against these recorded alternatives but does not silently convert the present commitments.

The existing Survey order's administrative notice and reporting provisions have their original dates and effect; no new Supreme Court clock is selected. The Interior dismissal, uncharged disapproval and unpresented particular impasse remain outside affirmative relief. All source limits in the frozen packet and supplement survive. No principal-source retrieval gap establishes a litigant default.
