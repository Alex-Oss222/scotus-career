## Corrected posture, facts and relief

Kolstad seeks reversal of the decision withholding punitive damages from the jury and further proceedings on that remedy. The Association seeks affirmance. The approved “Relief Requested: Affirm” line describes the respondent’s position only.

The liability verdict is an established procedural fact: on October 26, 1995 the jury found intentional sex discrimination in the selection of Thomas Spangler for the legislative-director vacancy and awarded $52,718; the district court entered judgment on that verdict. The lower opinion identifies the amount as backpay. It is not an existing §1981a compensatory or punitive award. The district judge denied punitive submission, later refused to set aside liability, and denied instatement and fees. The appellate panel sustained liability while reversing punitive dismissal and remanding instatement/fees; rehearing en banc concerned punitive submission alone. The en banc majority affirmed withholding that issue. The underlying liability is not reopened by this review. JA104–110 verifies the requested punitive instruction, renewed objection and verdict; JA111 verifies entry of judgment. A typo in petitioner’s merits opening description of a 1992 verdict must yield to the 1995 judgment itself.

The vacancy arose when Jack O’Donnell announced his retirement in 1992. Kolstad, Director of Federal Agency Relations, and Spangler, Legislative Counsel, applied. Both lawyers had distinguished evaluations. Leonard Wheat headed the Washington office and recommended Spangler; William Allen made the final selection after consulting Wheat. The lower opinion calls Allen executive director; respondent’s brief describes his more specific assistant-executive-director role. His established final selection authority matters here; the title discrepancy is not an adjudicated finding of alter-ego status or the punitive-attribution category.

Evidence, not separate findings of every underlying detail, supports competing accounts of the process. Kolstad points to delayed access to Wheat, grooming and preselection of Spangler, editing the position description using his existing job description, disputed comparisons of qualifications and writing, changing explanations, and Wheat’s alleged sexist jokes and terms. The Association disputes the weight and some content of that testimony, points to the actual legislative emphasis of the job, Allen’s independent familiarity and other recommendations, and notes that preselection is not itself unlawful. The liability verdict survives these competing accounts; punitive state of mind still requires the legally appropriate inquiry. At the instruction/sufficiency stage the court must consider supported evidence and reasonable inferences in Kolstad’s favor rather than deciding contested credibility anew.

The 1984 Resnick decree is not a finding of prior discrimination. It disclaimed liability and expired in 1991. JA91 admits that the quoted preselection provision expired on or about July 6, 1991; JA92 denies that an express anti-preselection policy continued afterwards and states the continuing equal-treatment policy. JA24–25 reproduces a May 1991 equal-opportunity policy reaching promotion and other personnel practices. The decree was excluded from the liability trial; its possible punitive relevance was contested. No current decree violation, universal illegality of preselection, new admission of discrimination or automatic right to introduce it follows. No effective training/compliance program, implementation failure, safe reporting route or specific good-faith factual defense is established merely by a policy’s existence. The lower dissent expressly characterized the Association as not having made a specific good-faith compliance showing; that is a judicial characterization of the record, not a new allocation of the statutory burden.

## Supported competing paths

1. **Require an additional egregious-conduct threshold and affirm.** The en banc majority emphasizes the separate damages subsections, the exceptional punitive function, §908’s outrage language and legislative references to more culpable violations. It finds this promotion record insufficient. Its strongest difficulty is the absence of “egregious” in the enacted text, the disjunctive recklessness term, Smith’s refusal always to demand a higher evidentiary threshold, and Hazen Paper’s rejection of an added outrageousness requirement under a related statute. A legitimate legal sufficiency screen remains available without a separate severity element. The majority expressly allows circumstantial proof and does not categorically prohibit punitive submission in a pretext case; do not replace its actual view with a direct-evidence rule.

2. **Give statutory state of mind independent work and reject an extra severity element.** The Tatel dissent and United States/EEOC amicus emphasize culpability concerning federal rights, with egregious conduct one possible source of evidence rather than an indispensable element. The same evidence can support both liability and punitive state of mind without making them identical; admission to jury consideration does not command an award. The strongest counterargument is that near-universal awareness of sex-discrimination law may collapse the practical distinction and that common-law punitive limits must retain meaningful content. An answer must identify the actual additional mental-state showing and preserve judicial review of evidentiary sufficiency. Petitioner’s ordinary-negligence-adjacent phrasing is not automatically the Court’s standard.

3. **Reject the extra severity rule but separately decide or remand punitive sufficiency.** A jury-permission holding can rest on supported promotion, cover-up/knowledge and biased-influence evidence considered together; Allen’s final signature does not automatically erase Wheat’s role. Alternatively, ordinary reconsideration under the corrected standard may leave factual application and preservation of independent grounds below. Respondent’s strongest alternative is that Allen independently selected Spangler for legitimate reasons and Wheat’s statements show insufficient punitive culpability. The preserved liability verdict cannot be retried through that alternative; the separate punitive inference still must be evaluated. No final punitive sum, fresh compensatory award or mandatory punitive award follows.

4. **Separate punitive attribution from ordinary Title VII liability.** The lower dissent’s good-faith/prevention reasoning, ordinary agency principles, and Ellerth’s explicit reservation support considering whether the employer’s own culpability or operative compliance measures should limit this particular remedy. The competing view treats a discriminatory promotion exercised through entrusted decisionmaking power as the employer’s own act and rejects an unsupported judicial safe harbor. A paper policy alone cannot settle either factual account. A new statutory remedial limitation could be considered without reopening the underlying liability rule, but its predicates, actor level, scope, burden and effect must be separately justified. No current holding supplies them. This packet verifies §908 and the lower opinion’s §219 agency discussion; it does not certify exact §§217C/909 text or attribute those sections to the EEOC brief, which does not invoke them. Any decision dependent on their precise wording requires a bounded date-eligible common-law source addition before commitment, not inference from the historical target decision.

The lawful narrow dispositions include affirming punitive withholding under a defensible standard/application, or reversing/vacating that component for ordinary reconsideration or a properly supported punitive trial. They preserve established liability and the separate status of fees/instatement. A general affirmative defense, individual managerial designation, or affirmative finding of good faith is not silently supplied by a remand.

The full quoted text is appended to the entering-law slice from the controlled source. These reference provisions existed before 1999; they are not newly adopted Title VII law.

Conversely ordinary attribution for compensation does not by itself resolve a separately adopted punitive standard.
