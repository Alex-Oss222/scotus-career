## Inquiries that remain distinct

**Delegation and review.** The frozen supplement identifies 19 U.S.C. sections 1500(b) and 1502(a), TSUS General Headnote 11 and HTSUS General Note 20 as pertinent delegations. Whether regulations within that authority receive Chevron review is distinct from the record on which the Court of International Trade decides a case under 28 U.S.C. section 2640(a)(1). The available exact section 2640 quotation covers only its court-record clause; omitted statutory or delegation text is not certified by this correction.

**Statutory permissibility.** The tariff conditions remain: United States fabricated components exported ready for assembly without further fabrication; retention of their physical identity; and no advancement in value or improvement in condition abroad except through assembly and incidental operations such as cleaning, lubricating and painting. The tariff grants a partial exemption based on qualifying component cost or value, not duty-free entry of the whole article. The supplement identifies HTSUS subheading 9802.00.80 and identically worded earlier TSUS item 807.00. Their conditions must not be replaced by the regulation's more specific categories.

**Regulatory terms.** Section 10.16(b) protects incidental operations before, during or after assembly and includes final pressing and folding. Section 10.16(c) excludes a significant process other than assembly whose primary purpose is fabrication, completion, physical or chemical improvement of a component, or which is unrelated to assembly, whether or not it substantially transforms the article. Paragraph (c)(4) lists chemical treatment imparting new characteristics, including permapressing. Paragraphs (b)(7) and (c)(4) must be considered with their respective introductory terms. Ordinary pressing cannot simply be equated with chemical permapressing; an example cannot silently erase the operative significance, purpose and relationship language. The interaction of the introductory rule and examples remains a legal question, not an assumed conclusion here.

**Actual application.** The frozen facts describe chemically pretreated fabric cut in the United States, sewing and ordinary pressing in Mexico, then 12–15 minutes of baking to activate permanent-press characteristics. Customs treated baking as additional processing. Haggar's account of extra steps and shipment creases if baking were delayed remains its position, not a finding of necessity or incidental character. The significance, primary purpose, assembly relationship and new characteristics of this operation must be addressed under the lawful construction adopted. The regulatory reference to permapressing alone does not establish all application facts or the shipments' other eligibility requirements.

## Competing paths

These are neutral analytical possibilities, not assertions that each was raised or preserved in an unrecovered filing. No position is assigned to any Justice.

**Direct decision on reasonableness.** After resolving the review framework and enforcing statutory boundaries, the Court may decide question 2. A supported argument for permissibility distinguishes assembly and incidental finishing from a significant separate process imparting a lasting chemical characteristic; a regulation may give content to the statutory boundary without denying the statute's specified incidental operations. The contrary argument stresses that the statute permits some value-enhancing or condition-improving operations when incidental to assembly: chemical effect or a named process cannot substitute for that relationship. The statutory examples, protected ordinary pressing, pretreated condition of the exported fabric and actual baking must be examined rather than assumed decisive in either direction.

The Court could sustain the challenged exclusion within the statute or reject it insofar as it crosses the enacted boundary. General authority does not itself establish reasonableness; disagreement with the agency does not itself establish unreasonableness. A direct legal ruling may still leave actual application and relief for remand. A final shipment-level disposition requires supported findings connecting the adopted rule to the refund remedy; this scope correction supplies none.

**Corrected-framework remand.** The Court may decide the general deference question and return the regulation's statutory permissibility, factual application, or both for further consideration under the proper framework. If the reported categorical rejection of Chevron below is rejected, the remand must identify the actual unfinished legal inquiry, relevant agency explanation or necessary factual determination. It may not rest on the former summary omission or describe question 2 as ungranted. Recognition of delegation supplies no automatic validation of the rule or Customs' classification. Conversely, the lower court's mistaken framework alone does not establish that further proceedings are indispensable if the supported record permits direct legal decision. The reason for remand must be stated on its own terms.

**The importer's general-review argument remains.** The packet's contention that the trade-review scheme requires independent tariff construction remains for evaluation. Adding question 2 supplies no answer to question 1. Rejection of deference would not alone establish that the regulation is unreasonable; the disposition would still require an independent statutory construction and a stated consequence for the regulation and reviewed judgment, reserving unsupported application issues as appropriate.
