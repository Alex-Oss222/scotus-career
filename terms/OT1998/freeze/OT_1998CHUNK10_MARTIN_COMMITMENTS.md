## Enhanced comparison of three lawful paths

### A. Divide by date of legal services

Adopt no new ceiling for services completed before April26,1996 and apply the ceiling to services performed from enactment forward. Neither the general action/award language nor the neighboring prospective-relief transition supplies sufficient direction for earlier services. Section802 needed to address existing prospective relief; its differing subject does not create a categorical exemption for every fee in every pending case. Lindh's particular comparison of closely related pending habeas applications does not compel that broader inference.

Completed fee-generating work is the relevant activity. Reducing its recoverable hourly compensation changes the consequences of work already undertaken and performed under the earlier rules and court-approved arrangements. The later award date is not dispositive merely because entitlement or amount still requires adjudication. Bradley remains valid: an intervening fee provision's treatment depends on the concrete consequence and setting; collateral character does not immunize every fee change from the Landgraf inquiry. No assertion of an absolute vested right to an unreduced award is necessary.

For work performed after enactment the statute gives notice of the new defendant-paid ceiling and regulates new services. Undertaking representation earlier and continuing professional obligations are significant practical facts, but do not turn all later legal work into a completed transaction or make the prior rate a perpetual contractual guarantee. The Court need not presume counsel could withdraw freely. The enacted limit governs the future work even within an older action, leaving eligibility, reasonable hours and separate constitutional questions to their proper treatment.

Strongest objection from officials: the text regulates an award made now, and Bradley permits current law in unfinished fee litigation. Response: identifying the actual legal consequence prevents every monetary change from becoming prospective merely because a court enters judgment later; the services have already earned the relevant claim to compensation under earlier standards, even though its final amount remains to be assessed.

Strongest objection from counsel: the filing/representation arrangement and§802 contrast should protect all pending-case fees, especially where counsel cannot simply abandon decree obligations. Response: neither clause guarantees that future compensated activity is insulated from prospective law. The difficulty of ending representation supplies no textual pending-case exception, while preserving earlier services respects the completed activity without immunizing later work.

### B. Exempt these pending cases for both service periods

Affirm the Sixth Circuit's temporal exemption. Ordinary construction of§802's explicit reach to earlier prospective relief alongside§803's omission supports preserving the already-established fee regime of pending remedial actions. Lindh shows that Congress may express temporal distinctions structurally without magic words. The undertaken representation, fee orders and continuing enforcement obligations supply a coherent event boundary at the existing action and representation, not an assumption that every later hour was freely chosen anew. Thus the law can govern newly initiated actions without altering compensation for the continuing representation here.

Strongest objection:§802 changes the handling of previously entered prospective relief, while§803 imposes current limits on fee awards; this is not Lindh's comparison of related habeas chapters. Fee arrangements do not guarantee indefinite rates for future services. Response for B: the provisions form a common prison-litigation reform and Congress expressly addressed preexisting court obligations in one but not the other. Landgraf asks first about legislative temporal choice; the existing monitoring representation makes that structural distinction materially consequential. This route does not rest on a constitutional contract guarantee or an adjudication that counsel cannot lawfully withdraw.

### C. Apply the cap to both service periods in awards not already finally fixed

Reverse the full pending-case exemption: the regulated activity is the court's present statutory award, constrained by “No award ... shall be based” on a higher rate. A statutory fee request is collateral to the completed prison conduct, and the fee law does not create new underlying damages liability. Bradley's actual fee holding and Scalia's Kaiser observation about costs/fees in unfinished litigation support this characterization. Prior arrangements are relevant but not shown to establish a final judgment fixing these unawarded amounts or an immutable private contract binding defendants to future statutory awards.

Strongest objection: the representation and legal services preceded the ceiling, and later adjudication cannot erase the fact that lower recovery changes the consequence of completed compensable work. Response for C: the statutory entitlement at issue is the present fee award, whose level remains subject to congressional control while unresolved; unlike Landgraf's new damages for earlier discrimination, this cap regulates the court's authority to award costs now. This is a characterization of this statute and these unfinished awards, not permission to reopen a final fee judgment or change every substantive claim in pending litigation.
