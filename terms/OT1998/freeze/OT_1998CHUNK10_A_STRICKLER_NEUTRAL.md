## Question, judgment below and relief

The assigned question concerns suppression of impeachment material under Brady, whether cause excuses the state procedural default, and material prejudice at guilt and capital sentencing. Strickler seeks habeas relief from the capital conviction and sentence; Virginia defends dismissal. The separate proportionality-review branch rejected below is not automatically within this grant. A new standalone instruction challenge, withdrawn ineffective-assistance claim or late recantation claim is not added merely because the record mentions it.

The Fourth Circuit rejected cause, reasoning that trial testimony revealing repeated police interviews and a lineup gave state-habeas counsel sufficient grounds to seek the files through Virginia Rule4:1(b)(5). It inferred that a request likely would have produced them. It separately rejected ineffective assistance as cause because open-file practice made a formal trial Brady motion unnecessary. Those propositions do not establish that counsel knew the contents of the missing five documents. Alternatively, the court found no prejudice/materiality at either phase, relying on independent abduction/robbery and killing evidence and describing sentencing as focused on prior crimes for dangerousness and the boulder killing for vileness. Footnote11 expressly makes the same assessment an alternative merits ground. The court did not accept cause or limit its reasoning to mere nonexhaustion.

The district court took a different view. It found unavailable evidence and reliance sufficient for cause, and found the five undisputedly withheld documents material even if the three disputed documents were disclosed. The appellate assessment, not the district court's favorable conclusion, is under review. Neither lower judicial conclusion is an independent underlying fact establishing what a properly informed jury necessarily would have done.

## Material record and competing inferences

Stoltzfus was the only eyewitness to the abduction, not to the killing. Her confident account portrayed Strickler as the dominant aggressive figure and Henderson as the less assertive companion. The five undisputedly withheld documents are Exhibits1,3,4,5,6; disclosure of2,7,8 is disputed and remains unresolved. The actual district opinion at JA392 expressly addressed that dispute, contrary to a respondent-brief characterization that it did not mention the three. The model may evaluate the five without finding that all eight were suppressed; the three remain relevant only at their proven status and lawful contextual uses.

The five documents contain materially different kinds of impeachment. Exhibit1 is a handwritten interview-note transcription whose layout and qualifications matter; it records identification uncertainty and resemblance/photo differences rather than a unitary clear recognition. Exhibit3 is the early observations narrative, including apparently normal behavior as the vehicles separated. Exhibit4 recounts reconstruction aided by the daughter, a purchase and corrected timing, vague uncertainty about the men, and the daughter's lack of memory of seeing the three enter the car. Exhibit5 gives tentative, changing impressions of the vehicle. Exhibit6 describes identification of the victim after several hours viewing current photos with the victim's boyfriend and comparison with a description of another woman. These are not findings that the witness fabricated her account, that police planted information, or that the prosecution intentionally concealed it.

Exhibits2,7,8 provide a typed investigative account and later letters/narratives of increasing detail. Their source-facts table preserves differences and the uncertain date of Exhibit8. Do not silently treat the later narrative as the last document created. The prosecutor's and defense lawyers' recollections of disclosure conflict. The district court accepted co-counsel Roberts's general recollection for summary-judgment purposes but distinguished general knowledge of evolving memory from knowledge of the five documents' particular contents. A newspaper account overlapped some material, including photographs, but the district court found that it did not communicate most missing details. The State contests the extent of novelty. This is a real dispute over the incremental impeachment and preparation value, not an undisputed premise that the defense knew nothing or already knew everything.

Other evidence placed Strickler and Henderson at the mall, identified Strickler driving the victim's car toward the field, connected him with the car, bank card, earrings and other property, and supplied forensic and clothing evidence. Tudor described bloody/dirty clothing and a conversation about a rock killing. The victim suffered lethal head injuries involving a69-pound rock. The State uses these circumstances to prove joint active participation and to contest dependence on the abduction witness. Defense evidence connected Henderson to bloody clothing, the victim's watch/car and a wallet near the body, and included Workman's account of Henderson's admission. The defense challenged Tudor's credibility, chronology, incentives and attribution of statements. These matters support competing inferences; possession or presence must not be redescribed as an eyewitness identification of the person dropping the rock, and Henderson's participation does not by itself exclude Strickler's joint active participation.

The capital instruction required willful, deliberate, premeditated killing during a listed predicate and distinguished mere aiding and abetting from active and immediate participation in the death-causing acts. It expressly allowed capital conviction for joint participation in the fatal beating; it did not require Strickler alone to deliver the fatal blow. The instruction's deadly-weapon definition turns on capacity in the manner and circumstances used. Its predicates included armed robbery and abduction/rape formulations. Both briefs agree that abduction of a person over12 with intent to defile was not a statutory capital predicate at the offense date; they dispute the effect and preservation of that issue. Review of Brady materiality must attend to the actual instruction and valid alternatives without automatically reopening the independently defaulted direct instruction claim.

Defense guilt closing urged first-degree rather than capital murder, acknowledged sufficient aspects of abduction/robbery to focus on active killing participation, and contested the State's witnesses and physical inferences. Those were counsel's arguments, not sworn testimony or an unqualified confession. The parties dispute how the suppressed material could have changed the defense's concessions and preparation. The State also invokes later presentence/allocution statements acknowledging presence while blaming Henderson; those postdated the jury verdicts, and their legal use, accuracy and independence are disputed. This packet does not add them to the evidence actually heard by the jury or resolve their use in a hypothetical retrial.

At sentencing the jury found vileness and future dangerousness and selected death, according to the supported lower/party account. The Fourth Circuit describes criminal history and the manner of killing as principal sentencing bases. Petitioner separately argues that the abduction witness's aggressive-leader portrayal supported the death choice even if guilt survived. Petitioner merits p.36 and reply p.12 n.8 identify that contention; the reply cites Sentencing Transcript p.15 for the prosecution's reliance on leadership. That is an attributed predecision quotation/record citation, not certification of the entire punishment transcript. Respondent p.49 stresses Tudor's attribution and equal capital culpability of active joint perpetrators. Those responses must be assessed against the distinct death-selection question as well as eligibility.

## Default and the actual cause dispute

The Stoltzfus suppression claim was not presented in state proceedings and is now defaulted, subject to an applicable excuse. The parties dispute the precise state rule's operation but accept that a default problem exists. Trial counsel used the prosecutor's open-file procedure without a formal Brady motion. State habeas, filed September1,1992, alleged ineffective assistance concerning a missing Brady request and cross-examination, but did not raise this independent suppression claim. In answering those allegations the Commonwealth represented that the defense had received full disclosure and all Brady material. The representation came after the state petition was filed; petitioner argues it reinforced reasonable reliance while those proceedings continued, and respondent contests its causal significance and breadth.

Petitioner's supported cause argument combines nonpublic police-held evidence, prosecution responsibility for it, actual open-file reliance and the later completeness representation. Its strongest objection is the public/trial information about interviews and changing recollection, the state discovery-with-leave mechanism, and the absence of a state discovery request. Respondent's diligence argument relies on those indications and examples of state-court discovery, but must answer whether general suspicion justified requiring counsel to distrust an affirmative disclosure representation and what showing the actual discovery rule required. Neither the existence of a discretionary avenue nor a later successful federal subpoena proves how a hypothetical state request would have been resolved. A research gap does not itself establish the litigant's diligence or lack of diligence.

Murray v.Carrier,477U.S.478(1986), and Amadeo v.Zant,486U.S.214(1988), supply surviving cause principles concerning an objective external impediment and unavailable factual basis; ordinary attorney error is distinct from governmental suppression. Coleman,501U.S.722(1991), preserves default and its recognized excuses. Those authorities do not create automatic cause for every Brady allegation or a new general right to collateral counsel. Kyles's team-disclosure responsibility and no-open-file-entitlement limit are both relevant. The present case must resolve the actual combination and any separate prejudice requirement, not state that the Fourth Circuit already resolved cause favorably.

## Supported disposition and rationale paths

The independent model must consider cause and phase-specific materiality separately. If cause fails on a legally correct assessment, the default sustains denial within the actual scope, with no unnecessary merits holding. The strongest counterargument is that State suppression plus reasonable reliance, rather than mere failure to ask, supplied the external impediment. If cause is established, that does not itself establish materiality or a writ.

One supported merits path retains confidence at both phases after considering cumulative impeachment and preparation uses against independent guilt, active-participation and penalty evidence. The strongest objection is that the confident apparently disinterested witness tied the narrative together and may have shaped concessions and the portrayal of relative culpability; counting remaining proof or repeating eligibility cannot answer those uses.

A second path finds guilt materiality because disclosure would substantially change how the defense could challenge the abduction/predicate and joint-active-participation narrative. It must answer the independent physical, possession, driving and Tudor evidence and the instruction permitting joint killing; it cannot require the State to identify a sole fatal actor or presume the suppressed documents prove innocence. A supported guilt-level violation can justify a conditional new-trial writ, not acquittal or automatic unconditional release.

A third path distinguishes confidence in capital guilt from confidence in death selection. It may find independent evidence sufficient to retain confidence in the conviction while the witness's aggressive-leader account materially affected the individualized death choice; the opposite evaluation is also supported for testing against the criminal history, manner of killing and other testimony. This path requires an actual penalty-record analysis and may not infer relief simply because an aggravator is disputed. A penalty-only conditional writ must leave guilt intact and identify a lawful new sentencing opportunity or lawful alternative; it cannot silently order immediate release or a specific sentence unsupported by the governing remedial law.

If the correct standard leaves necessary factual disputes unresolved, a bounded remand can require adjudication of the identified issue under existing procedural rules, but a new hearing, new discovery, authenticity finding, resolution of disputed documents2/7/8, or reopening of other claims is not automatic. The five undisputedly withheld documents permit a materiality analysis without an extra suppression finding about the three. Remedy must follow the component actually found defective. Independent preserved defenses keep their lawful scope and cannot be created from unavailable research materials.
