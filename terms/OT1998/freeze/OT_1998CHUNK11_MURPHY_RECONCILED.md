## Final judgment commitments

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg affirm.** They adopt actual-treatment assessment as the answer to granted Question 1. They do **not** decide whether Murphy's residual limitations or medication effects actually satisfy the definition: that distinct application issue is outside the granted question. They also sustain the rejection of regarded-as coverage on the evidentiary ground in P5 below, rather than a categorical defense based on UPS's asserted regulatory motive.

**Stevens and Breyer reverse and remand.** They assess the underlying impairment without subtracting medication's ameliorative effects. Taking Murphy's uncontested evidence of his severe untreated hypertension as true, they regard him as disabled under that construction and would return the case for ordinary proceedings on the unresolved remaining elements and defenses. They decide no separate regarded-as application and do not add an alternative treated-state remand.

These are six affirmance commitments and two reversal/remand commitments among the eight Associates. A final Court judgment, published writing structure and assignment remain for the authorized next stage.

## Exact propositions, joins and limits

**P1 — Measures actually used count in the actual-disability inquiry.** Section 12102(2)(A)'s requirement of an impairment that substantially limits a major life activity “of such individual” requires assessment with reference to the mitigating measures the individual actually employs. A severe untreated impairment does not alone establish actual disability when the individual's treated condition lacks a substantial limitation. This is an individual present-condition inquiry, not a hypothetical perfectly corrected person. The rule does not impute a different drug, increased dosage, successful normalization or an obligation to undertake additional treatment. Actual residual limitations and adverse effects are not erased by the word “controlled”; this statement identifies what actual-condition assessment entails, not a determination of their sufficiency in Murphy's case.

**Joins:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg in full. Stevens and Breyer reject P1's inclusion of ameliorative effects and instead join D1 below.

**P2 — The treated-state sufficiency issue is not within this grant.** Petition Question 1 asks whether Murphy's hypertension must be evaluated in its unmedicated state. It does not separately seek review of whether the evidence establishes disability when assessed in his actual medicated state. The grant limited to Questions 1 and 4 therefore permits an answer to the legal mitigation question without deciding residual-limitation or medication-side-effect sufficiency. Preservation below and renewed argument in the merits reply are acknowledged; neither by itself enlarges the granted question. Having accepted the lower court's legal choice of medicated-state assessment, these six leave its separate application determination undisturbed. They neither find that Murphy conceded normal functioning nor themselves hold that the disputed treated-state evidence fails Rule 56.

**Joins:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg in full, including the resulting affirmance of the actual-disability determination. Stevens and Breyer do not join that affirmance; their contrary answer to the granted legal question independently requires reversal under D1–D2. Their commitments contain no alternative decision or remand of treated-state sufficiency.

**P3 — Statutory construction and guidance.** For the six P1 joiners, the no-mitigation guidance cannot displace the statutory requirement of the individual's actual substantial limitation. The entering Auer, Haggar and NFFE rules require attention to the agency's responsibility, the operative text and statutory boundaries; they supply no automatic authority for an interpretation inconsistent with the adopted statutory construction. This conclusion does not invalidate every EEOC disability regulation, deny all delegated authority, disqualify amicus interpretations as a category, or treat notice and comment as immaterial. The validity of the working regulations is assumed for the analysis in P4–P5. Bragdon expressly reserved the general mitigating-measures question and supplies no previously controlling answer to it.

**Joins:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg in full. Stevens and Breyer preserve the same general authority boundaries but find the no-mitigation guidance persuasive and consistent with their construction under D1; they do not join P3's rejection of that construction. No blanket holding that the government's legal or employer-specific factual assertions control is adopted by anyone.

**P4 — Regarded-as coverage requires the perceived statutory limitation.** Knowledge of hypertension or rejection from employment does not by itself establish that UPS regarded Murphy as substantially limited in working. Conversely, an asserted lawful motive or reliance on a federal qualification does not categorically defeat coverage. On the working theory presented, and assuming the pertinent EEOC regulations valid, the perceived restriction must concern a class of jobs or a broad range of jobs in various classes relative to comparable training, skills and abilities, taking account of the reasonably accessible geographic area as the regulation provides. A single particular job does not suffice. Complete unemployability is unnecessary. A nationwide qualification requirement may matter but does not by itself establish the relevant class or UPS's perception of it.

**Joins:** All eight Associates support these limited coverage/qualification distinctions and the assumed regulatory framework. This shared legal ground does not give Stevens or Breyer a join in P5's application or an independent disposition of Question 4.

**P5 — This record does not show UPS perceived the necessary occupational limitation.** The showing is that UPS regarded Murphy's hypertension as preventing the commercial-driving certification required for its particular mechanic position. In light of Murphy's mechanical skills, his long experience in mechanic work not requiring that certification, the evidence of alternative mechanic occupations and subsequent mechanic employment, that showing does not create a genuine material issue that UPS regarded him as unable to perform a class or broad range of jobs using his skills. The record supplies no sufficient additional employer-perception evidence establishing the required occupational breadth. The six therefore affirm the rejection of this coverage route.

This is a conclusion on the supplied proof, not a requirement that a claimant be excluded from every mechanic job. Remaining employment alone is not conclusive, the vocational profile's category totals are not local vacancy counts, and physicians' heavy-work opinions are not automatically UPS's own perception. Nor does the conclusion hold that commercial driving can never comprise a class of work. Whether UPS was right about certification is unnecessary to this evidentiary result. The lower court's regulatory-motive shortcut is not adopted as the governing rule.

**Joins:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg in full. Stevens and Breyer do not join P5 and do not adopt a contrary triability holding; their reversal rests on actual disability under D1–D2. The independent W4 remand is not retained.

**P6 — Qualification and DOT defenses remain distinct and undecided.** Questions 2 and 3, which concerned DOT justification and qualification/essential functions, appeared in the petition but were not granted. The circuit expressly did not decide those issues. The ability of a respondent to defend its favorable judgment on a supported alternative under X-Citement Video does not establish these disputed alternatives. No Associate uses a categorical DOT defense, an essential-function determination or a qualification finding as an independently sufficient Supreme Court ground here. The binding safety language of §391.41(b)(6), §391.43(f)'s further-tests language and the advisory numerical criteria must remain distinct. Neither an absolute regulatory 160/90 bar, actual entitlement to temporary certification, nor a new waiver/exhaustion ruling follows. The interview-inquiry allegation and a newly presented record-of claim are not reached.

**Joins:** All eight Associates. The affirming six have no remand on these issues because their coverage grounds sustain the judgment. Stevens and Breyer leave necessary, preserved remaining issues for ordinary proceedings under D2. No in-world production order or party default is inferred from a research limit.

**D1 — Underlying impairment without subtracting amelioration.** Stevens and Breyer assess the individual's underlying impairment without subtracting medication's beneficial effects. The separation between protected status and qualification/accommodation, the contemporaneous guidance and legislative explanation support that construction. Individual substantial limitation remains necessary; diagnosis alone does not suffice. On Murphy's uncontested showing, his severe untreated hypertension, approximately 250/160, and the medical account of possible hospitalization establish the requisite disability when that evidence is taken as true. They do not require him to stop treatment, find existing end-organ damage, or infer disability merely from an elevated reading in every case. Successful treatment remains relevant to qualification, accommodation and safety.

**Joins:** Stevens and Breyer in full; the six P1 joiners reject this construction. D1 retains independent T2 and the substance of F3, with the actual-disability conclusion and summary-judgment premise made explicit.

**D2 — Reverse and remand for remaining ordinary proceedings.** Stevens and Breyer would reverse the coverage-based affirmance and remand because the circuit did not decide qualification and ability to perform the essential job functions. Their disposition does not order employment, damages, certification, a particular accommodation, judgment on liability, a new evidentiary proceeding or a mandatory trial. It does not foreclose a properly supported employer judgment on preserved remaining grounds. No treated-state factual determination or independent regarded-as remand is necessary to this disposition.

**Joins:** Stevens and Breyer in full. The other six reject this disposition while sharing P6's substantive reservations.

Murphy's deposition supplies asserted stamina, overhead-work, sensory and gout effects; stronger or abandoned regimens supply other reported effects. **Strongest alternatives tested.** F1's strongest ground is that actual-condition assessment must include the preserved residual evidence. Its decisive obstacle here is that the circuit already used the actual-condition legal rule, while Question 1 challenges that legal choice and does not present independent treated-state sufficiency. No binding authority makes the omission of an extended discussion a different granted legal error. F2's independent application holding has the same scope obstacle. P2 leaves the lower application untouched rather than deciding the merits against either factual account. W4's strongest argument is that common certification can exclude a substantial occupational class and that a regulatory reason cannot substitute for coverage analysis. P4 accepts both legal cautions; P5 evaluates the supported employer perception and available skill-based work and finds this showing insufficient. No new rule requires exclusion from every job. D1 remains a genuine contrary legal answer to the granted mitigation question and supports the two reversal votes without any of these additional routes.
