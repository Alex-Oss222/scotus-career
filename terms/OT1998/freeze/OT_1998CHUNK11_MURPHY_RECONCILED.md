## Exact propositions, joins and limits

**P1 — Underlying impairment without beneficial mitigation.** Under original §12102(2)(A), successful medication does not erase the underlying impairment. Substantial limitation of a major life activity is assessed without treating medication’s beneficial effects as eliminating that limitation. Individual substantiality remains necessary; diagnosis alone and prescription use alone are insufficient. Coverage remains separate from qualification, accommodation and legitimate safety standards. Arline and Bragdon support individual assessment and those separate inquiries; Bragdon’s general mitigation reservation leaves the legal question open.

**Joins:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain T1’s actual-measures-used construction, including residual limitations and adverse treatment effects, without hypothetical better treatment or a duty to undertake it.

**P2 — No separate treated-state sufficiency adjudication.** No controlling proposition resolves treated-state sufficiency or sustains an actual-disability affirmance on that basis. Question 1 presents the legal mitigation choice; P1’s answer requires reassessment under P3, not a separate decision of residual-limit sufficiency under the contrary rule. The disputed medical testimony retains its evidentiary status.

**Joins:** No distinct P2 holding. The eight Associates make no fresh treated-state sufficiency finding; the four actual-measures adherents would leave that application undisturbed, while the four P1 joiners require P3 vacatur on the granted legal question.

**P3 — Statutory construction and individualized reassessment.** The actual-disability judgment is vacated for reassessment without beneficial medication eliminating the underlying limitation. The claimant must prove individual substantial limitation; severe untreated readings and medical accounts are evidence, not an automatic disability finding. Qualification, accommodation and legitimate safety defenses retain their own elements, with no automatic employment, reinstatement, certification, waiver, accommodation or damages award. The statutory construction is the ground; the no-mitigation guidance is not accorded controlling deference. Auer, Haggar and NFFE preserve attention to responsibility, text, delegation and statutory boundaries without invalidating all EEOC regulations or categorically rejecting amicus interpretations. The working regulations are assumed valid for P4–P5.

**Joins:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas oppose vacatur under their T1 construction and find the guidance insufficient to displace that construction; they preserve the same agency-authority boundaries. Stevens and Breyer retain their separate persuasive-guidance point under D1.

**P4 — Regarded-as coverage requires the perceived statutory limitation.** Knowledge of hypertension or rejection from employment does not by itself establish that UPS regarded Murphy as substantially limited in working. Conversely, an asserted lawful motive or reliance on a federal qualification does not categorically defeat coverage. On the working theory presented, and assuming the pertinent EEOC regulations valid, the perceived restriction must concern a class of jobs or a broad range of jobs in various classes relative to comparable training, skills and abilities, taking account of the reasonably accessible geographic area as the regulation provides. A single particular job does not suffice. Complete unemployability is unnecessary. A nationwide qualification requirement may matter but does not by itself establish the relevant class or UPS's perception of it.

**Joins:** All eight Associates support these limited coverage/qualification distinctions and the assumed regulatory framework. This shared legal ground does not give Stevens or Breyer a join in P5's application or an independent disposition of Question 4.

**P5 — This record does not show UPS perceived the necessary occupational limitation.** The showing is that UPS regarded Murphy's hypertension as preventing the commercial-driving certification required for its particular mechanic position. In light of Murphy's mechanical skills, his long experience in mechanic work not requiring that certification, the evidence of alternative mechanic occupations and subsequent mechanic employment, that showing does not create a genuine material issue that UPS regarded him as unable to perform a class or broad range of jobs using his skills. The record supplies no sufficient additional employer-perception evidence establishing the required occupational breadth. The six therefore affirm the rejection of this coverage route.

This is a conclusion on the supplied proof, not a requirement that a claimant be excluded from every mechanic job. Remaining employment alone is not conclusive, the vocational profile's category totals are not local vacancy counts, and physicians' heavy-work opinions are not automatically UPS's own perception. Nor does the conclusion hold that commercial driving can never comprise a class of work. Whether UPS was right about certification is unnecessary to this evidentiary result. The lower court's regulatory-motive shortcut is not adopted as the governing rule.

**Joins:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg in full. Stevens and Breyer do not join P5 and do not adopt a contrary triability holding; their actual-disability vacatur rests on P1/P3. They make no independent regarded-as application decision.

**P6 — Qualification and DOT defenses remain distinct and undecided.** Questions 2 and 3, which concerned DOT justification and qualification/essential functions, appeared in the petition but were not granted. The circuit expressly did not decide those issues. The ability of a respondent to defend its favorable judgment on a supported alternative under X-Citement Video does not establish these disputed alternatives. No Associate uses a categorical DOT defense, an essential-function determination or a qualification finding as an independently sufficient Supreme Court ground here. The binding safety language of §391.41(b)(6), §391.43(f)'s further-tests language and the advisory numerical criteria must remain distinct. Neither an absolute regulatory 160/90 bar, actual entitlement to temporary certification, nor a new waiver/exhaustion ruling follows. The interview-inquiry allegation and a newly presented record-of claim are not reached.

**Joins:** All eight Associates. The four P1/P3 joiners leave necessary preserved remaining issues for ordinary proceedings on actual-disability remand. The four actual-measures adherents would affirm actual-disability noncoverage. The six P5 joiners sustain the regarded-as rejection. No in-world production order or party default is inferred from a research limit.

**D1 — Persuasive guidance supporting the underlying-impairment construction.** Stevens and Breyer find the contemporaneous no-mitigation guidance and legislative explanation persuasive support for P1’s statutory construction and separation of coverage from qualification, accommodation and safety. Their position requires individual substantial limitation and no final disability finding; it does not make agency deference the ground of the Court’s rule. Treatment remains relevant to the separate employment inquiries.

**Joins:** Stevens and Breyer. Souter and Ginsburg accept P1/P3 on their own statutory and individualized-impairment grounds without a separate guidance join.

**D2 — Vacatur for ordinary reassessment.** Stevens and Breyer join P3 vacatur to permit individual assessment of the underlying impairment. Their Bragdon individual-proof commitments and independent T2/F3 vacatur support that remedy without a final finding from the severe untreated-hypertension evidence. No employment, damages, certification, accommodation, liability judgment, new evidentiary proceeding or mandatory trial follows. Necessary preserved remaining elements and defenses remain for the lower courts; no separate regarded-as application or treated-state sufficiency decision is adopted.

**Joins:** Stevens and Breyer, sharing P1/P3 vacatur with Souter and Ginsburg. The four actual-measures adherents reject that disposition while sharing P6’s reservations.
