## Components and common limits

R is the racial political-process basis for preliminary relief; S is the distinct sex/equal-protection theory; T is blanket Title VII preemption; J is the disposition of the preliminary-injunction appeal. These components are not findings that any particular preference is valid. The complete original section 31(a)–(h) is frozen through the neutral supplement and mechanically preserved official text.

All eight reject T as an independently sufficient basis for enjoining the entire initiative merely because Weber and Johnson permit some voluntary affirmative action. A permitted choice is not invariably a federal command or a federally guaranteed power of each subordinate government to disregard its State. Section 703(j) concerns preferences demanded by numerical imbalance; it does not excuse proved discrimination. Sections 708 and 1104 retain their separate unlawful-practice and inconsistency boundaries, and actual federal obligations prevail. Section 31(d), (e) and (h) further prevent treating every federal remedial conflict as present on the face of the initiative. This common conclusion decides no particular future conflict or EEOC-guideline deference question.

All eight preserve the distinction between the ban on discrimination and the ban on preferences. Neither a loss of preferences nor the availability of another amendment by itself resolves the constitutional claim. No Justice treats Adarand as displacing local strict scrutiny or proposes overruling Hunter or Seattle here.

## Competing paths

**Selective political restructuring:** Seattle prohibits relegating a racial remedial issue to a more remote decisionmaker while ordinary decisions in the same field remain available locally. Its measure need not have withdrawn a constitutionally mandatory program. Hunter similarly rejected a special racial political hurdle. Here the racial nature of a previously available measure determines whether ordinary state/local action suffices or a constitutional amendment is needed. The strongest objection is that this converts permission for preferences into a constitutional duty to preserve them. The answer for Justices taking this path is that government may reject a program on its merits or restructure authority neutrally, but may not selectively burden the channel because the claim concerns race. Only otherwise lawful measures qualify; Hopwood and Engineering Contractors prevent validating a defective program under a political-process label.

**Uniform substantive equal-treatment choice:** Crawford preserves democratic authority to withdraw a voluntary policy without freezing every protective enactment in place. This initiative forbids official discrimination and preferences for any listed group, leaves ordinary antidiscrimination protection intact, and preserves federal obligations. The strongest objection is Seattle's refusal to treat statewide adoption as an answer to selective racial relocation. Justices taking this path distinguish a uniform rule governing officials' own classifications from the unequal access to ordinary antidiscrimination law in Hunter and the specially singled-out desegregation machinery in Seattle. They do not claim that every law mentioning equal treatment is immune from political-process review.

**Sex branch:** It cannot borrow racial strict scrutiny automatically. The competing readings are a selective impediment to seeking lawful remedies for sex inequality, requiring the actual important-objective/substantial-relationship inquiry, and a uniform substantive limitation imposing no unequal political franchise on women. Section 31(c)'s genuine operational-qualification exception is not proof of a stereotype or a blanket sex exemption. Justices favoring reconsideration would require a distinct analysis rather than enter final invalidity of every sex-neutrality rule.

## Remedy and coalition boundaries

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the same proposed judgment: vacate the appellate court's categorical constitutional rejection and remand for application of the racial political-process doctrine, separate sex analysis, and ordinary preliminary-relief requirements. They find the racial claim legally substantial under the supported selective-relocation facts; they do not adopt a final judgment that every provision is invalid. Actual threatened injury and the lawful scope of affected measures must determine any interim restraint. The discrimination prohibition and independently lawful operation remain; section 31(h) requires attention to severability. The remand neither compels a new preference nor automatically reinstates the original injunction's potentially overbroad reference to every program the initiative might 'affect.'

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support affirmance of the injunction vacatur on the constitutional and preemption grounds stated. Their disposition is limited to this preliminary facial challenge and does not adjudicate every future program, waive federal law, or immunize discriminatory administration. All eight support rejection of blanket T at the common scope above. No disposition is entered by this handoff and no Stone vote is inferred from the four-four Associate division on J.
