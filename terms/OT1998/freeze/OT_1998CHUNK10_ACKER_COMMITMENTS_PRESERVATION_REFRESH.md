## Enhanced alternatives on the actual corrected posture

**Immediate consideration of the renewed discrimination defense.** The defense seeks precisely the existing favorable judgment, was adjudicated in the district court and is now expressly briefed. The ordinance and stipulated record provide an identified substantive dispute. X-Citement Video defeats any assertion that an adverse reason alone required a separate appeal by prevailing respondents. The Court could elect to assess the source comparison now if the question is properly available and the established record suffices.

Strongest objection: nonpursuit on the intermediate appeal has a distinct prudential effect that the no-cross-appeal rule does not resolve, and the appellate judgment did not evaluate the renewed challenge to the district rejection. The Court should not bypass that history by treating current briefing as automatic restoration. The immediate-review response is that a preserved favorable judgment, a lower adjudication and current presentation distinguish an invented new claim; available review does not depend on another appeal from reasons alone. That response establishes a lawful option, not a mandatory Supreme Court merits ruling. None of the six merits participants adopts immediate substantive disposition on this corrected handoff.

**Definitive present nonreview with no route to reconsideration.** The reported intermediate nonpursuit can support a prudential decision not to address discrimination now. Its strongest basis is orderly review of grounds actually pursued through the appellate process, distinguished from waiver of a substantive right. The objection is that respondents defend a favorable judgment and actually renew a record-grounded alternative; X-Citement Video and Lebron prevent converting earlier nonpursuit into an automatic jurisdictional or intentional-waiver bar. The supplied note does not establish an express surrender, an enlargement of relief or a separate adverse judgment requiring a cross-appeal. The six therefore decline to declare the defense categorically lost or to affirm its substantive rejection simply from that omission.

**Bounded preservation-first remand, adopted by the six reaching merits.** The Court rejects the categorical occupational-license immunity ground but declines to decide discrimination's merits now. The appellate court should first determine whether the renewed alternative remains properly available in light of the actual intermediate nonpursuit, the favorable-judgment rule and the applicable prudential limits. Only if available should it review the discrimination defense on the properly established record. This is not a finding that the claim was never decided, a direction automatically to reopen the district rejection, or a new-evidence entitlement. If the defense is properly unavailable, this mandate supplies no right to reconsider it; if available, the ordinary legal review must identify whether the exemption structure discriminates because of federal compensation source rather than assume the district result correct or incorrect.

Strongest objection to this remand: the existing legal and stipulated record may permit the Supreme Court to decide both availability and merits itself, and returning the issue adds a step. Response: Dandridge, preserved in X-Citement Video, expressly leaves a lawful remand option. The newly established appellate omission is a real procedural question, and the lower appellate court has not addressed this renewed basis in the posture resulting from rejection of its immunity ground. The Court can correct the presented legal error while obtaining that initial appellate assessment. West exemplifies the distinction between a corrected main premise and conditional adjudication of properly presented alternatives; no universal remand rule is derived from it. The remand is justified by this actual procedural history, not merely by the operator's incomplete source archive or a demand to search for new evidence.
