# Schleifer v. City of Charlottesville

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment of the Fourth Circuit and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court shall enter declaratory and prospective relief against enforcement of the ordinance unless a binding state-law construction, legally available on remand and consistent with ordinary saving-construction rules, supplies objective and sufficiently precise boundaries. Charlottesville remains free to enact a narrower curfew with defined exceptions and enforcement standards. The Court does not adjudicate post-judgment applications or damages.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Minors' liberty to remain in public at night

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone does not decide the full substantive scope or level of review because Issue 3 supplies an adequate constitutional ground for reversal.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Minors possess constitutional rights, and the State also possesses special protective authority. Stone declines to define the complete balance in a case that can be resolved through the ordinance's failure to give notice and constrain enforcement.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** No holding is entered on whether minors possess a freestanding fundamental right to remain in public at night or on the scrutiny governing a constitutionally precise juvenile curfew.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Deciding the full liberty question is unnecessary once the penal ordinance cannot lawfully operate under Issue 3. The Court therefore leaves the substantive boundary for a case requiring it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Charlottesville may regulate overnight juvenile activity through a sufficiently precise law supported under the constitutional standard that later proves applicable.

### Issue 2: Fit parent's authority over a minor's lawful activity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone does not decide the parental-authority question because Issue 3 independently requires reversal.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Fit-parent authority is constitutionally significant but not an automatic exemption from every valid law governing minors. The Court should not specify its full application to curfews without necessity.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** No holding is entered on the standard governing a precise juvenile curfew that conflicts with a fit parent's permission for otherwise lawful activity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The parents obtain complete relief from the present ordinance on the vagueness ground. An advisory parental-rights ruling would not alter the judgment or remedy.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A later precise ordinance and concrete record may require separate analysis of the parent's right, the child's right, and the government's protective interest.

### Issue 3: Vagueness, discretion, and protected First Amendment activity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial vagueness and First Amendment-preservation question. The criminal ordinance directly regulates ongoing conduct, and the plaintiffs face present exposure.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies existing fair-notice and anti-discretion principles. The exception for activity protected by the First Amendment cannot make ordinary people and patrol officers decide, at the point of encounter, the full constitutional status of speech, association, assembly, and movement without objective textual guidance. A warning before prosecution does not cure the chill, parental liability, or the officer's power to define which activity counts toward later criminal exposure.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A juvenile curfew is facially invalid when lawful presence depends on an undefined exception for activity “protected by the First Amendment,” and the ordinance supplies neither objective standards nor a binding narrowing construction sufficient to give minors, parents, and police fair notice and to constrain enforcement discretion.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The ordinance delegates the boundary between lawful and criminal presence to an officer's real-time constitutional judgment. Record testimony produced uncertain classifications for political conversation, artistic performance, and comparable activity. The warning system postpones punishment but leaves the initial stop, dispersal order, record of violation, and repeat-offense consequences controlled by the same uncertainty. The defect is therefore structural and directly connected to protected expression.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not create an unrestricted nighttime right for minors, invalidate clear exceptions for work, direct travel, emergencies, parental accompaniment, or specified expressive events, or prevent regulation of demonstrated juvenile crime and victimization through precise means.

# Strickler v. Greene

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the denial of habeas relief from Strickler's conviction, reverse the denial of relief from his death sentence, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court shall issue conditional sentence-only habeas relief unless Virginia conducts a new penalty proceeding within a reasonable period fixed under governing law. The conviction remains intact; Virginia may impose any sentence lawfully available after a constitutionally adequate proceeding.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Procedural default and phase-specific Brady materiality

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches cause and the Brady merits. Suppression and reasonable reliance on the prosecution's open-file representation establish cause; the same cumulative-materiality rule must then be applied separately to guilt and capital selection.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies *Kyles* cumulatively and phase by phase. The withheld impeachment does not undermine confidence in guilt in light of the independent evidence, but it materially weakens the prosecution's dominant-aggressor account and creates a reasonable probability of a different capital sentencing recommendation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Suppression joined with reasonable reliance on the prosecution's open-file representations establishes cause. The withheld impeachment evidence does not undermine confidence in guilt, but its cumulative effect on the dominant-aggressor account creates a reasonable probability of a different capital sentencing recommendation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The prosecutor’s open-file practice, the police’s undisclosed materials and the State’s later representations reasonably concealed the basis for this claim. Counsel had no established reason to know that the apparent file was incomplete. The State cannot defeat cause merely by saying counsel could have sought records whose existence it obscured; police knowledge is attributable to the prosecution under Kyles. Materiality nevertheless remains necessary. Considered together, the five undisputed documents would have permitted a substantially different cross-examination of Stoltzfus and defense investigation into how uncertainty became confident identification. They would also have supported an account in which Henderson, rather than Strickler, dominated the encounter. Stone would reject guilt relief after considering those uses alongside the defense evidence already presented: the car, stolen property, bank-card use, blood and forensic connections, and the account of the killing conversation independently sustain confidence in Strickler’s active joint participation under the instruction actually given. That is a confidence assessment after integrating the impeachment, not a finding that the remaining evidence merely meets a sufficiency threshold. The selection of death asks a different question. Stoltzfus supplied the uniquely vivid portrayal of Strickler directing a relatively passive Henderson, although she did not see the killing. Undermining that portrayal could materially alter relative culpability and the jury’s moral choice even with active joint participation and eligibility established. The five undisputed documents therefore create a reasonable probability of a different penalty recommendation. The disputed suppression of the other three need not be resolved to grant that relief. Tuggle bars treating surviving eligibility as a cure for selection error, and Kyles permits no second harmlessness hurdle once penalty materiality is established.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The conviction remains intact. The five undisputed documents support the penalty remedy without deciding the disclosure dispute over documents 2, 7 and 8. No finding is made about the sentence a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding must produce.

# Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District

* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate in part and remand: reject the categorical sub-fifty-percent bar under the expressly modified rule, while preserving independent evidentiary rulings not shown erroneous.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Determine functional electoral opportunity on admissible evidence and then the remaining §2 conditions and totality inquiry. Do not order a district or invent a completed change to Court law.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: An express modification of the first condition

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Should a genuine crossover opportunity remain categorically excluded?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Retain the majority-population route and recognize a rigorously proved functional alternative for one protected group.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Retain the majority-population route and recognize a rigorously proved functional alternative for one protected group.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The first condition links the challenged electoral structure to a loss of an achievable opportunity to elect. A majority is a useful and predictable way to show that potential, but a categorical percentage can reject a district in which reliable electoral evidence proves the same opportunity. I would modify the rule reflected in Gingles and Growe to permit that demonstration. The departure is limited and express: the claimant must identify a lawful compact district, prove the protected group’s cohesion and demonstrate dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its candidates of choice. A speculative coalition, isolated favorable election or mere influence does not suffice. De Grandy’s rejection of numerical maximization supports the focus on effective opportunity, but does not itself already authorize this modification.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No fixed mathematical crossover formula, right to proportional outcomes or decision on combining distinct minority groups.

### Issue 2: Application to this record

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do the Hispanic population figures establish the alternative?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** They neither establish nor defeat it; vacate the categorical rejection and require proof.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** They neither establish nor defeat it; vacate the categorical rejection and require proof.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A district with 47.9% Hispanic citizen voting-age population may or may not provide durable electoral opportunity. The court must examine cohesion, the electorate’s actual voting behavior, the reliability of crossover support, the challenged system’s bloc voting and the proposed district’s lawful population and geography. If the alternative fails, the claim fails. If it succeeds, plaintiffs must still prove unequal opportunity under the totality of circumstances. Neither maximizing concentrated minority districts nor optimizing broader political influence is the court’s statutory assignment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The State retains lawful choice among effective arrangements; the court does not guarantee a preferred political tradeoff.

### Issue 3: Evidence and remand

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the change erase the trial rulings?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Preserve independently supported demographic and disclosure rulings; allow the trial court to determine whether fair supplemental proceedings are needed under the changed rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Preserve independently supported demographic and disclosure rulings; allow the trial court to determine whether fair supplemental proceedings are needed under the changed rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The current demographic evidence addressed concrete changes in a small district and was not simply an unsupported substitute for census data. The late survey was disclosed on the eve of trial, and the court accommodated plaintiffs’ objection to Rives’s later report by relying on his earlier report. Changing the legal threshold does not make those decisions erroneous. Yet evidence previously immaterial under a categorical threshold may become material to functional opportunity. The trial court should decide whether supplementation is needed and how to provide fair notice and response; reopening is neither automatic nor forbidden.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit's judgment insofar as it protects fees for services completed before the PLRA's effective date, reverse insofar as it exempts services performed after that date, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court shall compensate pre-enactment work under the law then governing and apply the PLRA limits to services performed after the effective date. It shall allocate fees according to when the legal work was actually performed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Temporal reach of the PLRA fee limits

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the temporal question; the record permits the required service-date allocation.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Applying a reduced compensation rule to legal work already completed attaches a new legal consequence to completed conduct. Applying the cap to services undertaken after enactment regulates future work even though the litigation began earlier.

# Jefferson County v. Acker

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the conclusion that removal was proper, hold that the Tax Injunction Act does not bar adjudication of the federal defense in the County's own collection action, reverse the judgment exempting the judges from the tax, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The federal court shall calculate and enforce the nondiscriminatory tax, subject to any remaining calculation or procedural defenses. The judges receive no constitutional exemption from the levy, and the County remains bound by the nondiscrimination and noninterference limits stated here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Federal-officer removal and the Tax Injunction Act

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves removal and the Tax Injunction Act before reaching the tax merits.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The judges may remove because the collection demand is connected to compensation received for official federal service and they assert a colorable federal immunity defense. Their eventual loss on that defense does not defeat § 1442 jurisdiction. Section 1341 does not bar adjudication because Jefferson County itself invoked the judicial forum to collect the tax.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal judge may remove a county tax-collection action when a colorable federal defense bears the required office nexus, and the Tax Injunction Act does not bar that defense in the taxing authority's own suit.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The defendants are federal officers sued for nonpayment of a levy imposed on compensation from their official service. That relation and the nonfrivolous immunity defense satisfy § 1442. Because the County initiated collection litigation rather than the judges seeking anticipatory federal restraint of state taxation, § 1341 does not require dismissal or remand.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize removal by every federal employee, dispense with the office nexus or a colorable federal defense, or permit taxpayer-initiated evasion of the Tax Injunction Act.

### Issue 2: Application of the county occupational tax

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** With jurisdiction established, Stone reaches the immunity defense.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The levy is measured by compensation and applies without discrimination to comparable workers. Its license label does not convert it into a requirement that federal judges obtain local permission to exercise Article III office.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A nondiscriminatory levy measured by compensation is permissible under 4 U.S.C. § 111 and may be collected from federal judges when it neither discriminates against federal officers nor regulates performance of the federal judicial function.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The tax attaches because compensation was earned in the County, not because the County purports to license adjudication. The record shows no federal-source discrimination or operational obstruction. The Eleventh Circuit therefore erred in constitutionalizing the label rather than the levy’s operation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not approve a discriminatory tax, a fee conditioning permission to hold federal office, or a levy uniquely directed at federal judicial functions.

# Jones v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the federal death sentence and remand for a new penalty proceeding; conviction and kidnapping guilt remain intact.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the federal death sentence and remand for a new penalty proceeding; conviction and kidnapping guilt remain intact.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Requested deadlock instruction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the stated question within the verified scope of this proceeding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does every capital jury require a fixed deadlock instruction?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** No universal formula is necessary where the charge accurately conveys the available punishments; Jones’s requests nevertheless identified a material defect in this charge.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** No universal formula is necessary where the charge accurately conveys the available punishments; Jones’s requests nevertheless identified a material defect in this charge.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 1201(a)(2) offered only death or life without release. Section 3593’s general reference to another lesser sentence did not create a third punishment for this offense. Simmons does not dictate a deadlock script, but it illustrates why the jury must not choose death because it has been misled about the lawful alternative. Jones’s requested instructions would have corrected that specific danger. The conclusion rests on the instructions actually given, not an invented question from deliberating jurors.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No universal deadlock instruction is prescribed, and the guilt verdict is unaffected.

### Issue 2: Misleading charge and verdict forms; preservation and plain error

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the stated question within the verified scope of this proceeding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do the actual instructions and forms require a new penalty proceeding?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Vacate the death sentence because the charge presented a materially misleading lesser-than-life alternative. The written requests and objections preserved the substance of that defect; alternatively the error warrants correction under Rule 52(b).
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Vacate the death sentence because the charge presented a materially misleading lesser-than-life alternative. The written requests and objections preserved the substance of that defect; alternatively the error warrants correction under Rule 52(b).
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The combination of Form C’s express unanimity requirement and Form D’s lesser-sentence option could lead a reasonable juror to think that a failure to agree on life as well as death released the judge to impose less than life. General directions to consider all punishments do not cure a legally unavailable alternative. Stone reads the written requests, particularly request 5, as alerting the trial court to this precise problem and would apply preserved-error review. The Government cannot establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt on a record containing substantial mitigation and rejection of several asserted aggravators. If Rule 30 instead makes this forfeiture, Olano requires a separate analysis: the error was clear under the offense’s existing two-punishment statute; no later holding or broad reading of Johnson is needed. Jones’s prejudice showing rests on the charge’s concrete alternative, the contested penalty evidence, the rejected aggravators and the mitigation found, rather than speculation based only on the existence of death. Those circumstances create a reasonable probability that the erroneous prospect of a punishment below life affected at least one juror’s choice. The length of deliberations supplies context, not proof of an internal vote. Finally, allowing a death sentence to rest on that materially false statutory choice seriously affects the fairness and integrity of the proceeding independently of the ordinary prejudice finding. Johnson’s very different forfeited materiality error supplies no automatic overwhelming-evidence answer. Juror affidavits do not enter either analysis.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The alternative plain-error conclusion does not dispense with any of Olano’s four requirements, presume prejudice from every capital error, or decide Johnson’s reservation concerning initially unsettled law.

### Issue 3: Nonstatutory aggravators

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the stated question within the verified scope of this proceeding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Must the additional aggravator claims produce a separate judgment once the sentence is vacated?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reserve an independent disposition of the aggravator and harmlessness claims because the new penalty proceeding already removes this sentence; any retrial must use understandable, legally relevant factors and lawful weighing instructions.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Reserve an independent disposition of the aggravator and harmlessness claims because the new penalty proceeding already removes this sentence; any retrial must use understandable, legally relevant factors and lawful weighing instructions.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The vulnerability factor combined youth, slight stature, background and unfamiliarity with San Angelo, while the second factor addressed personal characteristics and family impact. These are not unconstitutional merely because some facts overlap. Tuilaepa permits relevant individual circumstances in selection, but unexplained background cannot become an invitation to an arbitrary adverse judgment, and the jury cannot be directed to count the same harm twice as if repetition independently increased culpability. Stringer, Sochor and Espinosa require a real cure if a factor or its use is constitutionally invalid, rather than an assumption that other valid factors suffice. Because the misleading-choice error requires a wholly new penalty proceeding, Stone reserves a separate determination whether the first trial’s precise factors were independently invalid and whether their use was harmless. That reservation grants no authority to repeat a vague factor or misleading weighing instruction.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No categorical prohibition on victim-impact evidence or overlapping descriptions is adopted; no independent aggravator error or harmlessness result is invented.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The automobile exception permits warrantless search of a readily mobile car supported by probable cause without a distinct case-specific showing of further exigent circumstances.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** No new exception or balancing is needed. Labron already states the operative condition, and State lower court may not add a separate exigency requirement merely because officers had time to seek a warrant. Constitutionality of any specific container or passenger search is analytically separate and unpresented.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No rule that automobile ownership negates privacy, no categorical permission for search without probable cause, and no ruling on a different passenger-owned container dispute.

# Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** September 28, 1998; no prepared OT1998 position is a completed Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the adverse coverage judgment and remand under the original ADA’s proper mitigation rule.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reassess the actual-disability claim without using beneficial mitigation to defeat coverage; resolve any preserved regarded-as theory, qualification, accommodation and safety defense on its own elements.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Coverage without beneficial mitigation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** How should substantial limitation be assessed?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Evaluate the qualifying impairment without treating beneficial correction or medication as eliminating coverage.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Evaluate the qualifying impairment without treating beneficial correction or medication as eliminating coverage.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The twins allege severe uncorrected myopia. A person does not cease to have an impairment because lenses make ordinary activity possible. Reading coverage to turn on successful correction makes protection disappear precisely when treatment enables work, and improperly merges coverage with the separate question whether a person can perform the job with accommodation. Arline’s functional protection and Bragdon’s individualized approach support examining the underlying visual limitation while requiring proof that it is substantial. This construction rests on the original Act; it is not a categorical rule that every eyeglass wearer is disabled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Beneficial mitigation is disregarded for this coverage inquiry; actual functioning remains relevant to qualification, accommodation and safety.

### Issue 2: Other coverage routes and job qualification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the preserved question within this proceeding; the proposed result is a position, not an entered Court judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What remains after correcting the coverage rule?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Remand the adverse coverage judgment; require the elements of each actual-disability and regarded-as theory.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Remand the adverse coverage judgment; require the elements of each actual-disability and regarded-as theory.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Exclusion from the global-airline-pilot position alone does not show inability to work in a broad class of occupations. The claim concerning seeing is nevertheless independent and must be considered under the correct mitigation rule. The employer may contest qualification and defend a supported safety standard, but cannot use those defenses to erase the separate coverage question.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No automatic hiring, reinstatement or accommodation award.
