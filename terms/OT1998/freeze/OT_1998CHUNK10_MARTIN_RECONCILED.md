# Martin v. Hadix — frozen historical reconciliation

No.98-262; merits event June21,1999. ## Result and precise changes

All eight Associates support nonapplication of the new hourly ceiling to monitoring services performed before April26,1996. O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer support applying the ceiling to services performed after enactment, affirming the Sixth Circuit as to earlier work, reversing its exemption of later work and remanding for fee proceedings consistent with those periods. Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm the appellate exemption for both periods. No final fee amount or constitutional ruling follows.

The original Scalia and Thomas commitments applying the cap to earlier services are not retained. Their general relevant-activity method does not require choosing the fee award rather than the work being compensated as the operative event. Full historical review supplies strong evidence about their application of that method in this very statutory setting; the frozen packet supplies no changed statute, pending fee posture, completed-services fact or controlling simulated rule that would support the provisional contrary result. This is a supported reconciliation change after comparing the competing paths, not an automatic substitution of a vote tally.

Scalia’s distinct method is preserved. Kennedy and Thomas’s published simulated Landgraf concurrence also remains relevant: they may join the concrete service-date holding and its statutory application without being counted as adopting an unqualified general expectations-based framework that their earlier public position rejected. Ginsburg and Stevens’s structural inference supports, but need not conclusively establish, their continuing-representation rationale.

## Historical verification and comparator limits

The Court opinion, Scalia concurrence and Ginsburg opinion (joined by Stevens), including the Court’s joinder footnote, both Scalia footnotes and all three Ginsburg footnotes, were read in full on Cornell’s separate pages. The official Reports copy independently confirms the case, dates, disposition and part-specific lineup. Historical authorship is verification metadata only.

History is more precise than a single “7–2 principal holding” description: all nine historical Justices agreed to protect pre-enactment work, seven applied the cap to post-enactment work, and Scalia’s service-date agreement rested on identification of the activity regulated rather than the Court’s PartII–B expectations reasoning. Stevens and Ginsburg joined the no-clear-command and completed-work portions while opposing the treatment of ongoing representations’ later work. Their separate opinion expressly treats the adjacent statutory contrast as informative rather than dispositive, and identifies the undertaking of representation as the relevant completed event.

The contemporaneous constitutional objections remain undecided. The historical majority and dissent disputed the implications of continuing professional duties; that disagreement is not proof that withdrawal was ordered, available as of right, or factually attempted. The reconciled rule does not depend on inventing any such event. No later PLRA rule, final-judgment reopening power or change to the original monetary-judgment contribution language enters.

## Proposition-level scope for assembly

All eight agree that the general action/award language does not require applying the new ceiling to completed earlier work, and all eight support that work’s exemption. The concrete service-date rationale is supported by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, with the three relevant-activity reservations stated above. Stevens and Ginsburg can join the no-clear-command and earlier-services protection to the extent those propositions are stated independently of rejection of their ongoing-representation rationale. They cannot be counted as agreeing that the earlier undertaking never matters to later work.

The same six support the operative rule that the ceiling applies to post-enactment monitoring services in these pending cases. This is direct support for that temporal proposition; no synthetic logical-subset calculation is needed. No opinion author or final section numbering is selected here. A separate relevant-activity explanation and a separate continuing-representation explanation perform substantive work where the proposed common text does not itself preserve the recorded distinctions. Historical titles or authors do not prescribe their assignment.

The mixed disposition must modify the actual Sixth Circuit judgment, which exempted both periods. It does not affirm a nonexistent appellate service-date split. Subsequent fee computation, compensable hours, other applicable statutory restrictions and the unaddressed constitutional objections remain within ordinary lower proceedings. No categorical entitlement to an evidentiary hearing, fee award or decree alteration is imposed.
