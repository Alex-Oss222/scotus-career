## Cleveland v. Policy Management Systems Corp. — May 24, 1999

**Channel and components.** Merits review of the federal ADA summary judgment, 120 F.3d 513. C1 concerns the rebuttable estoppel presumption arising from an SSDI application or award; C2 concerns the explanation needed for apparently inconsistent representations; C3 concerns judgment and remand. The Fifth Circuit rejected a conclusive bar and must not be described otherwise.

### Lawful alternatives and shared rule

**Retain the presumption.** Sworn inability-to-work representations followed by benefits create a serious integrity concern when the claimant later asserts employment qualification. The lower rule permits credible rebuttal. Its strongest argument is preventing inconsistent advantages without explanation. Its defect is drawing an adverse legal inference across different statutory elements: reasonable accommodation, benefits listings and work provisions, and changes over time can produce compatible conclusions. Describing compatible cases as highly unusual does not cure that mismatch.

**Reject the presumption but require ordinary proof.** ADA qualification under 42 U.S.C. §12111(8) concerns essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation; SSDI under §423(d) does not decide the same accommodation issue. Application or award therefore creates no special adverse presumption. The claimant nevertheless retains the ordinary Rule 56 burden on qualification. An apparently inconsistent earlier disability assertion requires an explanation sufficient for a reasonable factfinder, taking the earlier assertion as true or made in good faith, to reconcile it with ability to perform the essential functions at the relevant time with reasonable accommodation. A bare reference to statutory differences does not answer directly contradictory historical facts. This is ordinary evidentiary assessment, not a special exceptional-case burden or universal immunity from estoppel.

## Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education — May 24, 1999

**Channel and components.** Merits review of dismissal of the district Title IX damages claim, 120 F.3d 1390. D1 concerns recipient responsibility for its own response to peers; D2 concerns knowledge, control, discrimination, educational harm and causation; D3 concerns pleading sufficiency and remedy. Touching, propositions, reports, response and educational interference remain allegations; the guilty-plea recital proves no additional civil element. No liability finding or award is authorized by this posture.

### Lawful alternatives and strongest objections

**Confined recipient-fault route.** A recipient can itself cause sex-based deprivation within its education program when an official empowered to correct a known danger deliberately and unreasonably disregards serious peer harassment within substantial school control, causing further harassment or vulnerability to it and educational injury. This is the recipient's culpable response, not employee-style imputation of a peer's acts. Kinman's actual-notice route supplies an analogy, not an already decided peer rule; Franklin supplies compensation for proved intentional discrimination. Gebser's teacher-power conjunction does not decide this extension.

The strongest objection is that a student cannot bind the institution to funding conditions, and disciplinary control does not turn every inadequate response into discrimination. Actual corrective-official notice, culpable disregard, substantial control over both harasser and relevant educational setting, and independently proved causal deprivation answer that objection. A reasonable response remains lawful even if harassment continues. No particular sanction or responsibility for acts beyond remedial control is prescribed.

**Decline the damages extension.** The competing view finds inadequate funding notice for damages arising from nonresponse to a nonagent's acts. Franklin settles available relief after recipient discrimination is established; Kinman involves a teacher, and Gebser's new route requires materially enabling teacher authority. The strongest counterargument is that the alleged wrong is the recipient's own deliberate action after actual knowledge. The answering position is that disciplinary power plus culpable inaction does not, without a clearer enacted condition, make the third party's discrimination the recipient's intentional wrong. Directly discriminatory official policy or affirmative conduct remains distinct.

### Sufficient rule joined by the reversal supporters

The actual-notice route requires: a funding recipient; harassment because of sex; actual notice of harassment or its substantial danger to an official empowered to correct it; substantial recipient control over the harasser and educational context; deliberate and unreasonable disregard of the known danger; and causal educational deprivation and compensable injury. The harassment must be sufficiently severe **or** pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive and impair educational access or benefit in context. Age, relationship, educational setting and actual effect inform context without becoming a mandatory checklist.

Kinman's severe-or-pervasive formulation is retained, not silently replaced with severe-and-pervasive. No mandatory grade loss, psychiatric injury, complete exclusion or universal rule making every single incident sufficient or insufficient is adopted. Simple teasing or offense does not alone establish educational deprivation; the pleaded repeated touching and propositions over months with educational interference present a different claim. Sexual subject matter alone does not establish discrimination because of sex. Ordinary negligence, generalized constructive notice, technical policy noncompliance and an unsuccessful reasonable response do not satisfy this route. No complete agency or negligence framework is decided.
