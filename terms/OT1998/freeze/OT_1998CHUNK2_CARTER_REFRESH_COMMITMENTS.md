## 1. Governing distinctions and source constraints

### The questions and the proper units of decision

The two federal questions are each respondent's personal Fourth Amendment interest and the legality of the actual unaided observation. Personal interest is substantive Fourth Amendment entitlement, not absence of an Article III case: each respondent has a concrete conviction and suppression controversy. A Justice may assume the personal-interest element to reject the search claim without assuming disputed Article III jurisdiction. Fields itself used that course for Crawley.

The state supreme court's judgments in both cases reversed the intermediate judgments. Johns's B1–B2 opinion adopts Carter's reasoning. Carter's intermediate court had rejected personal interest without deciding observation; Johns's had assumed an interest and sustained the observation. Neither these different intermediate routes nor the companion opinion permits Carter's key proffer to become Johns's evidence. Carter's sentencing issue, Johns's jury issue, and Thompson's conviction are not newly presented federal components. (Neutral §§1–2; X01.)

Long, retained by the seven-Justice jurisdictional ruling in Evans, permits federal review of this substantially federal or interwoven reasoning without a plain, sufficient independent state ground. Citation to Minnesota article I, section 10 does not itself establish independence. No Associate may describe the existing judgment as an established independent state-law disposition. A Justice who preserves Evans's contrary jurisdictional presumption must say so separately; the state courts' freedom to adopt a genuine state ground on remand remains distinct from the present basis.

### Personal interest: the precise Fields rule

Fields's sustained paid access, key, independent entry, and permission to bring guests established personal control; all eight Associates joined that proposition. O'Connor, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg additionally joined protection of Crawley's recurring, expressly permitted sharing of the closed room. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer assumed Crawley's interest to decide the particular observation. Neither branch establishes that every commercial visitor is protected or that every nonovernight invitee is excluded. Recurrence was part of Crawley's supporting combination, not an express universal statutory-like prerequisite. Conversely, removing recurrence from that combination is a further application or extension requiring a reason, not merely a quotation of Fields.

The established grounds here are permission from the sole lessee, approximately two and one-half hours in the apartment, a joint cocaine-packaging task with her, and payment of an eighth of an ounce for use. There is no accepted finding of prior visits, an established personal relationship, stored possessions, continuing control, or an overnight arrangement. That is the evidentiary state, not an affirmative finding that every such fact was impossible. Johns's slippers establish neither residence nor an overnight invitation. Illegality cannot cancel otherwise established privacy; it also cannot itself create a personal entitlement. A short business arrangement and a criminal-disqualification rule are different arguments.

Carter's counsel swore that Carter had apartment keys at arrest and that the sheriff inventoried and released them. The State contests the proffer's support, timing, and reliance below. No adjudication of its procedural status or weight has been recovered. It cannot be silently credited, rejected, or described as nonexistent. Even if a key is proved, the permission and control it actually conferred must be considered; a key alone is not invariably equivalent to Fields's entire relationship. No Justice gives Johns the benefit or burden of Carter's proffer. (X08–X09.)

### Search: what can and cannot be treated as established

The trial findings place Thielen on shared grass approximately 12–18 inches from drawn blinds with gaps, observing for approximately fifteen minutes. The memorandum describes standing and looking down into the garden-level interior, common use, no enclosing fence or vegetation immediately barring access, bicycle storage by the window, and residents and children leaving the walk. Those are findings and supporting observations; its conclusion of noncurtilage and no search remains a constitutional conclusion. It did not establish an ordinary-sidewalk view. (X02–X03.)

The reproduced testimony describes a western grass approach crossing the other building's entrance walk, an initial position by a projecting brick abutment with a view of the table and packaging, and a move right for a closer view of the same activity. The officer denied touching the window or using a flashlight. He allowed that he might at some point have been less than a foot away, though not six inches away. Photographic position marks were approximate; he could not reliably distinguish a between-blind opening from a between-slat opening. (X04–X06.)

Climbing and crouching occur in the state appellate analytical narrative, not in the reproduced trial findings or express testimony. The State contests that account; respondents defend it by invoking state factual finality, photographs, and an asserted failure to seek rehearing. The latter docket fact has not been independently established. Neither advocacy account becomes a new finding here. The photographs did not reproduce the precise night-of blind position and cannot conclusively settle route, posture, or visibility. No view from the public sidewalk is established for the informant either. (X07; X10–X13.)

Fields controls that visibility to cotenants alone is not general publication and that an imperfect blind alone does not negate subjective privacy without the actual vantage. It leaves the ultimate search application open. Its four-Justice opposing application treated an ordinary actual shared view as sufficient, while the four-Justice plurality required a renewed actual-vantage assessment. Those are individual continuity, not competing majority holdings. Pinson and Myers govern acquisition of otherwise concealed domestic information; neither makes all unaided looking lawful nor all targeted looking a search. Ciraolo, Riley, and Dunn retain their actual-vantage and contextual limits as described in Fields.

### Preservation and the later evidence

All commitments below assume subjective desire for privacy for the selected disposition. None grants reversal because a gap disproves subjective privacy. This avoids deciding respondents' express newly-raised-argument objection without the complete lower briefs. No commitment adjudicates an asserted binding curtilage concession: the quoted “may be classified” language and the State's contrary quoted heading do not establish one by themselves. No disposition depends on adopting a forfeited criminal-disqualification theory. The separate objection that the State failed to seek factual correction is immaterial to a ground expressly assuming the appellate additions; it must be resolved through proper state/federal review principles if a later disposition depends on rejecting them. (X12; X14–X16.)

Minnesota's window-observation statute is supporting advocacy, not an adjudicated crime, suppression rule, or verified police exception. This record supplies no independent statutory disposition. State trespass or privacy law can inform the claimed expectation but does not automatically decide the federal question. (X17.)

The search, its justification, and later admissibility are separate. The state supreme court records the State's concession of no probable cause before the observation. Reasonable suspicion and modest intrusiveness do not create a general exception allowing a home search. The later stop, door opening and handgun discovery, arrests, vehicle warrant, apartment warrant, and statements require their own legal and causal treatment. The trial memorandum made the observation central to both the stop and warrants; it did not find a genuinely independent warrant source. Its rejection of the particular Franks attack on misleading investigation/surveillance descriptions is not an independent-source determination. The ninety-minute surveillance gap is not erased. (X19–X20.)

The prosecutor's F2 concession is conditional: if the arrest was invalid, statements and physical evidence would fall. It does not say that any initial observational defect, without the intervening causal and personal-right inquiries, excludes everything for both defendants. Nor does the statement that additional statements were not separately in issue adjudicate all possible statement defenses. All eight Associates previously joined Fields IV: a later warrant is an independent source only if both the decision to seek it and its issuance are genuinely independent of the illegality. Attenuation, inevitable discovery, consent if actually invoked, and other remedies require their actual factual and procedural predicates. Leon does not protect knowing or reckless deception, abandoned neutrality, affidavits wholly lacking reasonable probable-cause indicia, or facially deficient warrants; obtaining and supplying officers and proper execution scope matter. Evans's qualifying court-clerical-error rule supplies no categorical exception for this deliberate police investigation. No new Miranda, Franks, or other claim is manufactured. (X20–X21; Fields IV; Pinson III; Myers III.)

## 3. Individual grounds, alternatives, and join limits

### John Paul Stevens

**Threshold commitment.** Stevens maintains the position he joined and separately explained in Evans: the Court should not presume federal authority over a state suppression judgment invoking both constitutional charters when an independently sufficient state basis has not been affirmatively excluded. He would dismiss, recognizing that current Long/Evans doctrine permits review and that the existing opinion contains no established independent ground. His position is a proposal to retain his contrary jurisdictional rule, not a factual finding that Minnesota already supplied the plain statement. The strongest contrary route is unusually substantial here: the operative discussion expressly applies federal law and identifies a Fourth Amendment violation. Long therefore supplies the governing route for the Court. His contrary vote follows his published presumption, not a fabricated Minnesota holding. (Evans, jurisdiction and Stevens/Ginsburg separate positions; neutral §1; X10, X19.)

**Conditional personal-interest commitment.** If merits are reached, he would recognize each respondent's own interest. The lessee admitted both into the private living interior, participated with them for a substantial interval, and accepted compensation for that permitted use. They were jointly occupying the protected domestic setting, not merely passing through or claiming suppression from the host's ownership. Fields I-B, which Stevens joined, establishes that nonovernight shared privacy can belong personally to a permitted occupant. The present application removes recurrence as indispensable where actual, substantial joint private use is established. Rakas continues to bar mere presence and vicarious claims; Olson supplies the home-sharing premise without making sleep essential. The criminal task supplies no entitlement and forfeits none. The key proffer is unnecessary to his positive holding and is not credited; slippers add no legal status.

**Two paths.** The strongest contrary personal-interest argument is that Crawley's recurrence and respected closed bedroom provide a meaningful boundary absent from a brief paid transaction. Stevens would reject making that difference conclusive: residential sharing is actually exercised here, not inferred solely from a visitor label, and the task's market purpose does not change the place or the host's deliberate sharing. He would not join a rule protecting every delivery worker, public-facing business caller, or person with permission only to enter; those relationships are not this established joint occupancy. Conversely, he would refuse the four reversing Justices' implication that lack of continuing access necessarily defeats these proved interests.

**Conditional search and disposition.** He would hold this observation an unreasonable search even on the trial findings and reproduced standing-position account. A prolonged inspection from approximately a foot away through drawn domestic blinds, reached over grass outside the ordinary entrance route and initially using the brick projection's cover, is not established ordinary public exposure of the living interior. The lawn's shared uses and a bicycle by the window do not establish ordinary exposure to this sustained window inspection. Fields II-A supplies the limited-sharing rule; Katz and the Pinson rationale he joined deny physical-penetration immunity. Pinson's technological facts are an analogy, not the controlling factual classification. He need not find curtilage, climbing, crouching, trespass, a statutory crime, or a precise gap dimension. Accepting the disputed additions would strengthen, not create, his ground. The strongest contrary argument is the admitted unobstructed unaided visibility from common ground; he rejects equating the opportunity for a view with public exposure to the particular examination.

With no pre-observation probable cause, warrant, consent or supported exception, he would affirm the reviewed judgments. He would not join a reasonable-suspicion home-search exception or an automatic all-fruits rule. Ordinary state proceedings must respect each defendant's actual claims and the conditional arrest concession. His maintained threshold dismissal remains the primary vote; these are conditional merits commitments only.

### Sandra Day O'Connor

**Personal interest and formal disposition.** O'Connor would reach and reverse the personal-interest holding for each respondent, remanding as specified in §2. Her own Fields I-A recognizes actual independent control; I-B extends Olson to an established recurring, permitted residential relationship and closed-room sharing. The accepted Carter/Johns grounds do not show either combination. A paid invitation for this one two-and-one-half-hour packaging task establishes permission and an interest in secrecy, but does not by itself establish that either respondent acquired a personal residential privacy interest in the premises. She would state the holding at that factual level; she would not make illegality, short duration, absence of title, or commercial purpose independently disqualifying. Subjective privacy is assumed. (Fields I-A/I-B and O'Connor's published plurality; X08–X09; X14–X16.)

**Carter qualification.** She would not turn the trial/appellate silence about a key into a finding against its existence. Because the state court relied on a legally insufficient combination without deciding that proffer, the remand leaves its procedural availability, evidentiary weight, and actual control implications for lawful state consideration. If a usable finding establishes authorized independent entry/control materially comparable to Fields, she would recognize Carter's personal interest and reach search; a bare key allegation would not do so. That conditional route does not give Johns a key or establish control for him.

**Two paths and join boundary.** The strongest opposite route treats actual permitted joint use of a screened home as enough, with recurrence relevant but unnecessary. She accepts that Fields did not announce a mandatory recurrence checklist. She nevertheless rejects this extension on the accepted transactional record: otherwise the host's permission and any prolonged common activity would do the work Rakas requires the defendant's relationship to perform. A genuinely shared social or residential relationship, temporary rental carrying control, or established independent access would be a different case; this disposition prejudges none. She can join Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas only at this limited supported-facts level, not a broad rule that business visitors or unlawful purposes never receive protection.

**Search if personal interest is established.** Her primary ground does not decide the host's rights or the particular observation. If an adopted controlling personal-interest rule requires a present search disposition, she would vacate and remand that component for a source-accurate actual-vantage application, rather than conclusively choose either factual narrative. Her own Fields plurality and Riley qualification retain the claimant's burden while rejecting automatic publication from limited sharing. The accessible lawn, ordinary standing view, bicycle storage, nonresident users, and lack of enclosure provide a serious lawful-view route; the close sustained observation through drawn blinds from window-side positions outside an ordinary approach provides the strongest search route. The state appellate extraordinary-measures analysis materially invokes climbing and, in one passage, crouching. If rejection of that characterization matters, the state factual-review and rehearing objections must actually be addressed; Ornelas/Fields do not authorize appellate invention of historical facts. If the court determines the same constitutional answer follows even assuming those additions, it should say why. She would order no new hearing or photographic reconstruction and impose no new public-access burden. (X02–X07; X10–X15; Fields II-A and II-B.)

She would join the separate conditional rules on absence of a home-search exception, genuine warrant independence, and evidence-specific remedies stated in §1. She would not turn a later warrant into an automatic cure or the prosecutor's conditional concession into unconditional exclusion. Those rules do not supply a present vote finding the observation unlawful.

### Antonin Scalia

**Personal interest and disposition.** Scalia would reach and reverse both federal personal-interest holdings on the facts accepted below, with Carter's proffer qualification preserved. His Fields I-A join rested on substantive control, while his treatment of Crawley expressly assumed the additional interest rather than adopted the extension. Rakas requires personal rights in the place searched. The current invitation, duration, and common packaging activity do not demonstrate that either respondent possessed the home as a private place or shared a developed residential relationship with its occupant. The payment proves a transaction; without the terms of authorized control it does not establish a temporary tenancy. The conclusion is not that criminal use extinguishes privacy or that lawful work in a home is categorically unprotected. (Fields I-A, I-B's join limits, and Scalia separate Part I; X08–X09; X16.)

**Two paths.** His strongest contrary path is Jones/Olson as developed in Fields: permission can create personally shared privacy without ownership, and payment for use can resemble renting private space. He accepts that principle. He distinguishes this accepted record because there is no found independent-entry authority, right to use a defined private room apart from the present task, or recurring shared residential relation. If Carter lawfully proves a key conferring such authority, Scalia's unanimous Fields I-A commitment makes the personal-interest result different; he cannot reject it just because cocaine was involved. A key whose purpose is unknown is not already that proof. He refuses an all-invitees rationale and also refuses a rule that only one's owned or permanent home can be protected.

**Alternative search commitment if reached.** Scalia could join Breyer's actual-view no-search ground on the established common-use facts, including its assumption in respondents' favor of the disputed climbing/crouching account. His Fields Part I position regarded unaided observation through an actual opening from shared residential ground as outside the retained privacy interest on that record. This ground is stronger here in the absence of a fenced route or enclosure and with use beside the windows by residents and visitors. A private interior does not render every unaided view into it a search. Pinson, which he wrote and joined, protects otherwise concealed domestic information, not an entitlement that officers disregard the actual exposed view.

The strongest objection is that Fields II-A forbids equating cotenant visibility with publication and that climbing/close peering can reveal what ordinary public use would not. He accepts the controlling distinction. His alternative depends on the combined actual accessible setting and unenhanced view, not cotenancy alone, the State's “public” label, or the investigator's purpose. He would assume the appellate additions and conclude that changes of posture or passage over unenclosing shrubs do not themselves create protected concealment where the view is obtained from this ordinarily used common location. He would not join immunity for every common yard, observation obtained by opening blinds, physical entry into the apartment, or technology obtaining otherwise unavailable information. He adopts no finding that the lawn belongs to the police or that Minnesota law permits every such act.

His Ornelas/Fields objection to new independent review of ultimate exigency does not permit factual rewriting here and is not a ground to evade the existing governing review rules. If a later factually supported account establishes a protected, nonpublic viewing precinct or an acquisition beyond ordinary unaided visibility, his alternative no-search application would not control it. His primary personal-interest reversal does not require that additional finding. He retains Fields IV's separate conditional remedy rules and issues no blanket admission order.

### Anthony M. Kennedy

**Personal interest and disposition.** Kennedy would reach and reverse the accepted personal-interest rationale for both, remanding within the identified limits. He joined Fields's actual-control proposition while assuming Crawley's further guest interest. That record did not make every invited person a holder of the host's rights. Here the proved relation is a short, compensated use for a particular transaction, with no accepted continuing authority or established prior relation. He would not equate payment with illegitimacy, but neither would he equate payment with the privacy of a hotel room or independent workspace absent an actual grant of control. A truly shared social or residential setting can carry a different expectation; this holding does not fix a universal minimum visit length or require an overnight stay. (Fields I-A and his join in Scalia Part I.)

**Two paths.** The strongest competing argument is that the host actually admitted the men to share her domestic privacy for hours and participated throughout; the right can be personal even though temporary. Kennedy regards the actual scope of admission as decisive. This record establishes the task-based permission but not a broader personal relation to the home. He would not join a reason declaring the substance handled constitutionally decisive: the same relationship with lawful goods would raise the same issue. Carter's key proffer could materially change the scope inquiry if lawfully accepted and connected to independent permission; its unadjudicated existence prevents a categorical finding that Carter lacked a key. It does not change Johns's proof or compel new proceedings. He may join O'Connor's narrow ground, not an all-commercial-visitors rule.

**Alternative search commitment.** If personal interest is recognized, he would join the bounded actual-view reversal described for Breyer. Kennedy's Pinson/Myers dissent focused on the information actually obtained and resisted deriving a search solely from concern about private domestic activity. His Fields join makes the closer analogy: actual naked-eye visibility through an opening from shared accessible ground can remain unprotected even when the occupant retains a personal interest in the room. He would not treat the exterior location alone as dispositive. The record here supports ordinary ground-level access, no enclosing barrier, an unaided standing view, and no physical manipulation; the fifteen-minute duration does not itself convert the visible information into concealed information.

The strongest objection combines the drawn blinds, very close inspection, route away from the doorway, and appellate climbing/crouching characterization. Kennedy would assume those additions for this alternative and reject a rule that an unusual approach or a change in posture alone creates a search where the actual point is ordinarily accessible and the information is visible without enhancement. A route that crosses an actual protected enclosure or a finding that information was acquired only through an independently intrusive method would present a different case; none is adopted here. He joins no general public-ground immunity, and no finding of statutory compliance. Because the alternative accepts the disputed additions, it does not rest on rejecting a state factual determination or an unproved failure to seek rehearing. Genuine later-warrant independence, the legality of the stop/arrest, and separate claims remain for their own inquiries rather than automatic validation.

### David H. Souter

**Personal interest and disposition.** Souter would exercise jurisdiction under Long and his Evans join, recognize both personal interests, and affirm the reviewed judgments on the unlawful-observation ground. He would treat Fields I-B, which he joined, as rejecting an overnight boundary and protecting actual personal sharing of domestic privacy. Here the host deliberately admitted each respondent for substantial joint activity inside her screened living space and joined the undertaking. That actual occupation provides a personal relationship distinct from mere presence. Recurrence and a respected bedroom door made Crawley's case stronger, but Souter would not turn either into an indispensable condition for temporary sharing of a home's interior. The commercial consideration informs the arrangement without eliminating the privacy that was shared; unlawful activity supplies neither a right nor its forfeiture. Carter's disputed key is unnecessary to this conclusion and Johns's slippers are not used as residence evidence.

**Two paths.** The strongest contrary argument is the short task-specific arrangement, unsupported prior connection, and absence of accepted independent control. He would reject an automatic requirement of control precisely because Fields protected Crawley on a different basis. Rakas still requires a personal relation; an unattended delivery, access confined to a publicly exposed reception area, an incidental entrant, or a claim relying only on someone else's ownership remains outside this holding. He would join Stevens or Ginsburg only in the actual shared-occupancy rationale, not any suggestion that a host's invitation invariably transfers all Fourth Amendment rights.

**Search application.** Fields II-A's limited-sharing rule and the Pinson/Myers holdings he joined require attention to the information and actual acquisition. The trial-level minimum is sufficient for him: sustained inspection of the living interior through drawn blinds from window-side positions roughly a foot away, outside the ordinary entrance route. The record establishes lawn traffic, not general public exposure of the table's activity to the ordinary route. The initial abutment position also required the close window-side approach, so the later movement does not create an independent lawful sidewalk source. An informant's unexplained outside observation supplies none either. No finding of climbing, crouching, curtilage, or a state privacy offense is required. The imperfect screening and common ground are substantial contrary facts, but insufficient to make this precise inspection ordinary public exposure. (X02–X07; X10–X13; Fields II-A.)

The strongest no-search path is Breyer's analogy to the Fields dissent: an unenhanced view from ground actually used by visitors may be observed deliberately. Souter would distinguish opportunity to stand on a shared lawn from ordinary exposure of a substantially screened home interior at this viewing distance and duration. This is an application to the combined circumstances, not a fixed distance limit, a fifteen-minute rule, a rule that all departures from sidewalks are searches, or a declaration that every shared apartment yard is curtilage. The same result follows without either accepting or rejecting the disputed state appellate additions, so the factual-finality/rehearing dispute need not be decided.

**Justification and remedy.** With no pre-view probable cause, warrant, consent, or supported exception, the observation is unreasonable. The informant's tip permits investigation but does not supply a reasonable-suspicion exception for this home information. He would affirm the state judgments while expressly keeping the existing reversals distinct from an acquittal or a new all-evidence suppression command. The prosecutor's invalid-arrest condition must be honored if reached and established. The stop, handgun, arrests, apartment evidence, vehicle evidence and statements retain distinct causation and personal-right questions; the prior Franks ruling supplies no independent source. He joins the full conditional remedy rules in §1, not an automatic warrant cure or an automatic fruit conclusion.

### Clarence Thomas

**Personal interest and disposition.** Thomas would reach and reverse the state court's accepted personal-interest rationale in each case, with a limited remand. His Fields I-A join protects the actual personal control shown there; his decision to assume Crawley's interest withheld assent from that further extension. Rakas distinguishes suppression of a violation of one's rights from a claim derived merely from another's premises or the incriminating character of evidence. These findings establish presence by invitation for a particular joint transaction, not the sustained control or developed shared residential relation recognized in Fields. He would not infer a right to exclude, independent access, or a private-room grant from payment alone. (Fields I-A and Thomas's join in Scalia Part I.)

**Two paths and Carter's different evidence.** The strongest contrary route is that actual permission to occupy the private home for a paid undertaking establishes a temporary legitimate use akin to the protected interests in Jones and Olson as preserved by Fields. He accepts that property formalities and innocence are not prerequisites, but concludes the accepted grounds do not prove the required personal relation at this level of generality. Carter's key affidavit, if procedurally usable and established to confer independent access or control, would require a different application of the unanimous Fields I-A rule. Neither its truth nor its exclusion is determined here. Johns must rely on his own established relation, not that proffer. Thomas can join the narrow common reversal rule but not categorical disqualification based on contraband or all paid visits.

**Alternative search and remedy.** If personal privacy is recognized, Thomas would join the qualified actual-view ground stated by Breyer and the corresponding Scalia analysis. His Pinson/Myers joins show that external acquisition can be a search; they foreclose a physical-entry-only rationale. His Fields substantive join nonetheless distinguishes exposed unaided views from otherwise concealed home information. The supported common ground's open access, use adjacent to the window, absence of an enclosing barrier, and natural-sight observation together sustain the distinction here. He would assume climbing/crouching for that alternative rather than reject an appellate characterization as a factual matter. An unusual route or posture does not by itself change the status of the information where the actual viewing point is ordinarily accessible. He would withhold assent if the rationale rested solely on the word “common,” on illegality of the observed task, or on an unproved precise gap or public-sidewalk view.

He joined Ornelas's majority and Fields's home-exigency review extension; historical factual protection remains separate from independent constitutional judgment. He therefore supplies no approval of replacing the state appellate account with photographs or an inference of officer credibility. His chosen search alternative does not require doing that. Fields IV, which he joined, remains the rule if antecedent illegality is found. Neither rejecting the residential claim nor accepting this alternative decides separate arrest, vehicle, statement or warrant claims, revives a waived objection, or supplies automatic admission.

### Ruth Bader Ginsburg

**Threshold disposition.** Ginsburg would maintain the alternative presumption she stated in Evans and dismiss. The lower court invokes both federal and Minnesota guarantees; she would require affirmative establishment that a federal ruling is indispensable rather than infer it from the absence of a Long plain statement.
