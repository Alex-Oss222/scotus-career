## Wright v. Universal Maritime Service Corp., No. 97-889 — November 16, 1998

### Disposition, sufficient rule and application

Each Associate votes to **vacate the Fourth Circuit's judgment and remand**. The district court's dismissal without prejudice for nonexhaustion cannot stand on the instruments presented. This is not an ADA merits adjudication.

The common sufficient ground is contractual scope: a union-negotiated waiver of an employee's judicial forum for an independent federal discrimination claim must be clear and unmistakable. Broad submission of disputes over the collective agreement does not, without that clarity, surrender the statutory forum. This ground does not determine whether an explicit union-negotiated waiver would ultimately be enforceable. Gardner-Denver supplies the distinction between collectively bargained contractual rights and an individual's statutory discrimination right; Metropolitan Edison supplies the requirement that a union's waiver of protected statutory rights be clear. Gilmer's individual statutory-claim arbitration agreement does not supply language absent from a different collective agreement. AT&T Technologies and First Options preserve antecedent judicial determination of the agreement actually made. First Options' delegation rule is not mislabeled as already deciding statutory-forum waiver; it reinforces the distinct need to establish the relevant assent before relying on arbitral authority.

Clause 15(B)'s grievance machinery concerns disputes within the collective relationship; clause 15(F)'s comprehensive employment coverage and express exclusion of matters outside the agreement do not expressly incorporate the ADA or submit its independent cause of action. Clause 17 prevents contractual provisions from violating law; a savings clause is not an express contractual adoption of every statutory cause or its arbitral submission. The Longshore Seniority Plan independently submits disputes about its terms, interpretation, application and implementing rules; that different instrument also supplies no clear submission of this ADA action. Mastrobuono requires an integrated, instrument-specific reading, not an abstract presumption that the broadest imaginable dispute is submitted.

**Alternative-path test.** The employers' strongest route is that discrimination over qualification for employment concerns a subject comprehensively covered by the CBA, whose grievance process is final and binding. That makes the dispute employment-related, but does not identify a surrender of the statutory judicial forum. Treating the savings clause as an incorporation-and-waiver clause would collapse contractual compliance with law into submission of independent statutory claims. The competing categorical route would hold that no union can ever waive this forum. The present scope defect makes that broader enforceability judgment unnecessary; no Associate's commitment requires adopting it. Contract interpretation is sufficient without imposing arbitration-hostile ordinary rules on genuinely assented-to individual agreements.

## Dodd v. Hood River County, Ninth Circuit No. 97-35124 — November 19, 1998

### Component commitments and operative rules

Each Associate votes to **affirm the Ninth Circuit's judgment**, on narrow issue preclusion plus the independent federal grounds stated here. No Associate adopts the district court's equation of the entire Oregon and federal takings inquiries, a universal acquisition-with-notice bar, a rule excluding residential plans from property protection, or an inference that later permission automatically eliminates a temporary-taking claim.

**D1 — State-compensation proceedings and narrow preclusion.** Section 1738 requires the lawful Oregon effect of the state judicial determination. A reservation of the federal takings claim does not create a general exemption from issue preclusion for a fact actually and necessarily litigated in deciding the distinct state claim. Baker's unanimous recognition component and Allen v. McCurry supply that ordinary effect; Richards requires constitutional authority to bind and a fair opportunity, not an exemption for litigants who personally participated. The Court need not abolish or enlarge Williamson County: Del Monte Dunes preserved its compensation prerequisite outside the established inadequate-remedy setting, and the Dodds completed the state process.

All five Oregon requirements are met for **the availability of profitable timber use**, at that level of generality: (1) the actual retained-use issue is the same fact relevant to the federal total-deprivation claim; (2) the competing evidence was presented and that use finding was necessary to the final state no-taking determination; (3) available evidentiary procedures, including a requested hearing and witness examination, together with actual presentation and judicial review, supplied a full and fair opportunity; (4) the Dodds themselves were parties; and (5) the recorded LUBA adjudication and ensuing judicial review are proceedings Oregon recognizes for this purpose. The reported failure to request the evidentiary hearing is supported procedural history, not an inference from unavailable research. Kremer distinguishes an adequate available opportunity from failure to use every procedure.

This does **not** preclude the entire federal claim, every federal element, a precise residual dollar valuation, or the legal assessment of partial deprivation. Nor does it adopt a free-standing rule that every agency proceeding is fair. England's preservation of federal questions is respected; it supplies no right to contradict the necessary retained-use adjudication in this separate state compensation litigation.

**D2 — Total deprivation.** The established profitable timber use defeats a Lucas claim of complete elimination of economically beneficial use of the relevant parcel. It is unnecessary to find that Goal 4 itself is a background property or nuisance limitation excluding the proposed dwelling from title. Lucas's burden concerning a substantiated background limitation arises only after complete deprivation is shown. Neither prior enactment nor a new description of harm substitutes for that burden.

**D3 — Distinct remaining federal inquiry.** Penn Central's partial-deprivation inquiry remains necessary. On this record its combined considerations support the judgment: the parcel continued to support profitable timber production; the claimed use was a retirement dwelling subject at acquisition to an existing statewide necessary-and-accessory condition; the County's communications did not promise the required permits; the regulation imposed a forest-use restriction serving the actual forestry, fire and chemical-application objectives rather than a physical occupation or demanded conveyance; and the later permit confines any asserted deprivation to the actual earlier period. These considerations are evaluated together. Retained use alone is not dispositive; existing regulation is relevant to the reasonableness of this specific expectation, not a rule that later purchasers lose constitutional protection; legitimate purpose alone is not immunity from compensation. The appellate record establishes no material factual dispute requiring an additional trial to apply these principles. The actual county communications and the continuing forest use distinguish a claim to a reasonable assured dwelling use from a hope to obtain permission inconsistent with the existing condition.

The actual restriction of nonaccessory dwellings also bears the required relationship to the specified forest-management purposes under the received Agins inquiry retained at its bounded scope in Del Monte Dunes. No demanded property conveyance exists, so Dolan's special nexus-and-proportionality burden does not apply. The judgment rests on application to this parcel and record, not on an expansive new formulation of substantial advancement.

**D4 — Time, relief and limits.** The July 1, 1996 building authorization prevents treating the dispute as an ongoing categorical prohibition, but does not itself erase a completed compensable taking. First English, Lucas and Del Monte Dunes preserve compensation for the proved attributable duration of an actual taking. Here the remaining grounds fail to establish that antecedent violation. Affirmance therefore awards no compensation and orders no new permit, jury trial or factfinding. The owners' pace of development is not a constitutional forfeiture rule. The residential purpose receives the same property protection; the conclusion does not depend on calling it noncommercial.

### Enhanced comparison of competing paths

**Path A: narrow preclusion and affirmance on independent federal merits — adopted.** Its strongest objection is that the federal claim was deliberately reserved and its partial-taking elements exceed the state substantial-use inquiry; moreover, summary judgment cannot turn an unresolved magnitude of loss into a finding of trivial impact. The answer is that only profitable use is precluded, while the independent record concerning the legal restriction, actual communications, continuing use and character of the restraint supplies the remaining analysis. No precise loss percentage is invented or deemed precluded. The federal inquiry is decided rather than extinguished by the state claim's disposition.

**Path B: preserve narrow preclusion but return the partial-taking inquiry.** Its strongest foundation is Del Monte Dunes's protection of parcel-specific factual adjudication and its warning that continued value or eventual sale does not conclusively answer a temporary taking. It would be necessary if a material expectation, use, purpose-relationship or economic fact remained genuinely disputed, or if the judgment rested only on continued value. Here the full lower opinion expressly identifies an undisputed record and independently addresses the remaining factors. Its unnecessary rhetoric about residential purpose and delay can be rejected without discarding the supported grounds. The actual land-use constraint predates purchase and was not displaced by a promised permit; the protected timber use and regulatory character remain substantial contextual facts. The record thus permits the limited legal resolution without treating a jury right as a right to retry a precluded fact or to avoid ordinary summary judgment.

**Path C: reservation defeats all preclusion.** Compulsory resort to state remedies is its strongest fairness argument. Neither the supplied statute nor England, Baker or Richards supplies that categorical exemption from a final decision's ordinary factual effect. It would disregard the owners' actual party status, incentive, procedures and judicial review. The preserved federal cause remains independently available, as the D2–D3 adjudication demonstrates.

## Wisconsin Right to Life, Inc. v. Paradise, No. 98-118

**Event and cutoff:** 1998-11-24, authorized post-grant review date. The lower opinion is 138 F.3d 1183, Seventh Circuit No. 97-3668 (March 13, 1998; rehearing denied April 16). No new argument date, updated Wisconsin filing or enforcement event is assumed. All eight Associate Justices participate on the supplied institutional facts; no recusal is invented.

### Issues and lawful dispositions

The actual appellate judgment vacated the District Court's Pullman disposition and directed jurisdictional dismissal. It cannot be described merely as an affirmance. There are two serious paths.

**Return for the correct, claim-specific Article III inquiry.** WRTL brought a facial constitutional challenge seeking invalidity and nonenforcement; its claims cannot be dismissed simply by replacing that requested relief with a command that Wisconsin tribunals interpret every future dispute as the federal judge wishes. Nor can a prediction that Wisconsin courts could save the enactment dispose of a substantial claim as nonjusticiable. Recurring speech, present cessation and the Travis proceedings must be evaluated against the actual official disclaimers. Properly limited relief against officials could redress an official constraint even if it cannot bind every absent private plaintiff. The strongest objection is that this remand could become a vehicle to manufacture a controversy after the parties have agreed that WRTL's materials are exempt. The response supporting this path is bounded: the Court does not find standing, permit amendment as of right, compel discovery, negate an effective disclaimer, or hold that third-party private exposure alone suffices. It requires adjudication of the actual claims and actual remedy before the entire suit is dismissed on the erroneous characterization.

**Affirm jurisdictional dismissal on the existing actual-threat and defendant limits.** WRTL identifies no official threat to its own comparative materials; the official rule and litigation agreement exclude that activity. Travis involves different advertisements and different speakers. The concrete private-suit concerns cannot be enjoined through this action against officials alone. A facial invalidity prayer cannot create a defendant-caused injury, and a hoped-for persuasive effect on nonparties does not by itself furnish likely redress. The strongest objection is that the appellate court replaced actual facial relief with an advisory construction request and did not conclusively resolve credible official injury. The response supporting this path is that the record facts already establish the decisive disconnect, independent of the appellate court's merits comments; a remand is unwarranted without a supported official controversy or a defendant capable of redressing the private one. This position does not declare all facial challenges advisory or require a completed prosecution.

## AT&T Universal Card Services v. Rembert — December 1, 1998

### Proposed result and exact shared propositions

Affirm the Sixth Circuit's judgment rejecting the presented §523(a)(2)(A) actual-fraud exception for the debt under review. The bankruptcy court's nondischargeability finding does not stand on an objective inability-to-pay substitute for contemporaneous fraudulent intent. The appellate judgment rests on the failed intent element; no reliance, causation or debt-extent finding is manufactured, and no new evidentiary hearing is required on this reported record. The judgment concerns the matter brought by AT&T; it does not create an unprovided separate Citibank Supreme Court proceeding.

**R1 — Fraud and the representation.** When a cardholder obtains an advance using the card, the transaction communicates an intention to repay the resulting debt; it is not by itself a representation of present solvency or an objectively reasonable prospect of repayment. On this actual-fraud theory the creditor must prove that the debtor's professed contemporaneous intent was false and that she intended deception, together with the independent statutory elements. Financial distress, accumulated losses, borrowing patterns and other objective circumstances may prove a dishonest state of mind, but inability or unreasonable optimism alone is not a legal substitute for it. Subjective intent is a fact capable of circumstantial proof, not an exemption established merely by the debtor's denial.

**R2 — This record and review.** The quoted bankruptcy explanation moves from the objective unreality of gambling expectations to an absence of intent. The appellate account identifies no contrary contemporaneous-intent evidence and identifies substantial mortgage-funded and later payments corroborating the debtor's intent to repay. Its rejection of the flawed inference is sustained under ordinary factual clear-error review and independent review of the legal standard. This is not a new assessment of witness demeanor, a rule that all gambler testimony must be believed, or an entitlement to another hearing whenever a creditor failed proof at trial.

**R3 — Limits essential to the disposition.** Field's actual and justifiable reliance requirements remain independent. Justifiability is recipient-specific: no universal credit-check or periodic-investigation duty applies, but known or personally obvious falsity and actually encountered warnings retain their force. Card issuance, continued authorization or nonrevocation does not establish automatic actual or justifiable reliance. Subsection (A)'s financial-condition exclusion and subsection (B)'s separate writing, material falsity, reasonable-reliance and intent-to-deceive conditions remain distinct. Grogan's burden is a preponderance. No heightened showing, mandatory twelve-factor test, confession, or additional plan to file bankruptcy is required. The disposition does not adjudicate a different false-pretense or representation-only theory, §523(a)(2)(C)'s distinct recent-debt presumption, or a §523(d) fee claim.

**Authorities and treatment.** Field, November 28, 1995, supplies common-law fraud construction, actual and individualized justifiable reliance, separate statutory elements and correct-standard review. Grogan v. Garner, 498 U.S. 279 (1991), supplies the unchanged proof burden. In re Cohen, June 30, 1997, requires a qualifying fraudulent acquisition before its broader connected-liability rule can operate; it provides no exception for a debt where that predicate fails. The official applicable §523 text supplies the separate (A), (B) and (C) categories. The Sixth Circuit's Rembert opinion supplies the tried facts, the quoted bankruptcy explanation and lower-court judgment; its invocation of Field does not convert the Court's prior reliance holding into a previously decided universal credit-card-intent rule. Judge Krupansky's proposed Ward investigation requirement is not adopted.

## 1. Governing distinctions and source constraints

### The questions and the proper units of decision

The two federal questions are each respondent's personal Fourth Amendment interest and the legality of the actual unaided observation. Personal interest is substantive Fourth Amendment entitlement, not absence of an Article III case: each respondent has a concrete conviction and suppression controversy. A Justice may assume the personal-interest element to reject the search claim without assuming disputed Article III jurisdiction. Fields itself used that course for Crawley.

The state supreme court's judgments in both cases reversed the intermediate judgments. Johns's B1–B2 opinion adopts Carter's reasoning. Carter's intermediate court had rejected personal interest without deciding observation; Johns's had assumed an interest and sustained the observation. Neither these different intermediate routes nor the companion opinion permits Carter's key proffer to become Johns's evidence. Carter's sentencing issue, Johns's jury issue, and Thompson's conviction are not newly presented federal components. (Neutral §§1–2; X01.)

Long, retained by the seven-Justice jurisdictional ruling in Evans, permits federal review of this substantially federal or interwoven reasoning without a plain, sufficient independent state ground. Citation to Minnesota article I, section 10 does not itself establish independence. No Associate may describe the existing judgment as an established independent state-law disposition. A Justice who preserves Evans's contrary jurisdictional presumption must say so separately; the state courts' freedom to adopt a genuine state ground on remand remains distinct from the present basis.

### Personal interest: the precise Fields rule

Fields's sustained paid access, key, independent entry, and permission to bring guests established personal control; all eight Associates joined that proposition. O'Connor, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg additionally joined protection of Crawley's recurring, expressly permitted sharing of the closed room. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer assumed Crawley's interest to decide the particular observation. Neither branch establishes that every commercial visitor is protected or that every nonovernight invitee is excluded. Recurrence was part of Crawley's supporting combination, not an express universal statutory-like prerequisite. Conversely, removing recurrence from that combination is a further application or extension requiring a reason, not merely a quotation of Fields.

The established grounds here are permission from the sole lessee, approximately two and one-half hours in the apartment, a joint cocaine-packaging task with her, and payment of an eighth of an ounce for use. There is no accepted finding of prior visits, an established personal relationship, stored possessions, continuing control, or an overnight arrangement. That is the evidentiary state, not an affirmative finding that every such fact was impossible. Johns's slippers establish neither residence nor an overnight invitation. Illegality cannot cancel otherwise established privacy; it also cannot itself create a personal entitlement. A short business arrangement and a criminal-disqualification rule are different arguments.

Carter's counsel swore that Carter had apartment keys at arrest and that the sheriff inventoried and released them. The State contests the proffer's support, timing, and reliance below. No adjudication of its procedural status or weight has been recovered. It cannot be silently credited, rejected, or described as nonexistent. Even if a key is proved, the permission and control it actually conferred must be considered; a key alone is not invariably equivalent to Fields's entire relationship. No Justice gives Johns the benefit or burden of Carter's proffer. (X08–X09.)

### Search: what can and cannot be treated as established

The trial findings place Thielen on shared grass approximately 12–18 inches from drawn blinds with gaps, observing for approximately fifteen minutes. The memorandum describes standing and looking down into the garden-level interior, common use, no enclosing fence or vegetation immediately barring access, bicycle storage by the window, and residents and children leaving the walk. Those are findings and supporting observations; its conclusion of noncurtilage and no search remains a constitutional conclusion. It did not establish an ordinary-sidewalk view. (X02–X03.)

The reproduced testimony describes a western grass approach crossing the other building's entrance walk, an initial position by a projecting brick abutment with a view of the table and packaging, and a move right for a closer view of the same activity. The officer denied touching the window or using a flashlight. He allowed that he might at some point have been less than a foot away, though not six inches away. Photographic position marks were approximate; he could not reliably distinguish a between-blind opening from a between-slat opening. (X04–X06.)

Climbing and crouching occur in the state appellate analytical narrative, not in the reproduced trial findings or express testimony. The State contests that account; respondents defend it by invoking state factual finality, photographs, and an asserted failure to seek rehearing. The latter docket fact has not been independently established. Neither advocacy account becomes a new finding here. The photographs did not reproduce the precise night-of blind position and cannot conclusively settle route, posture, or visibility. No view from the public sidewalk is established for the informant either. (X07; X10–X13.)

Fields controls that visibility to cotenants alone is not general publication and that an imperfect blind alone does not negate subjective privacy without the actual vantage. It leaves the ultimate search application open. Its four-Justice opposing application treated an ordinary actual shared view as sufficient, while the four-Justice plurality required a renewed actual-vantage assessment. Those are individual continuity, not competing majority holdings. Pinson and Myers govern acquisition of otherwise concealed domestic information; neither makes all unaided looking lawful nor all targeted looking a search. Ciraolo, Riley, and Dunn retain their actual-vantage and contextual limits as described in Fields.

### Preservation and the later evidence

All commitments below assume subjective desire for privacy for the selected disposition. None grants reversal because a gap disproves subjective privacy. This avoids deciding respondents' express newly-raised-argument objection without the complete lower briefs. No commitment adjudicates an asserted binding curtilage concession: the quoted “may be classified” language and the State's contrary quoted heading do not establish one by themselves. No disposition depends on adopting a forfeited criminal-disqualification theory. The separate objection that the State failed to seek factual correction is immaterial to a ground expressly assuming the appellate additions; it must be resolved through proper state/federal review principles if a later disposition depends on rejecting them. (X12; X14–X16.)

Minnesota's window-observation statute is supporting advocacy, not an adjudicated crime, suppression rule, or verified police exception. This record supplies no independent statutory disposition. State trespass or privacy law can inform the claimed expectation but does not automatically decide the federal question. (X17.)

The search, its justification, and later admissibility are separate. The state supreme court records the State's concession of no probable cause before the observation. Reasonable suspicion and modest intrusiveness do not create a general exception allowing a home search. The later stop, door opening and handgun discovery, arrests, vehicle warrant, apartment warrant, and statements require their own legal and causal treatment. The trial memorandum made the observation central to both the stop and warrants; it did not find a genuinely independent warrant source. Its rejection of the particular Franks attack on misleading investigation/surveillance descriptions is not an independent-source determination. The ninety-minute surveillance gap is not erased. (X19–X20.)

The prosecutor's F2 concession is conditional: if the arrest was invalid, statements and physical evidence would fall. It does not say that any initial observational defect, without the intervening causal and personal-right inquiries, excludes everything for both defendants. Nor does the statement that additional statements were not separately in issue adjudicate all possible statement defenses. All eight Associates previously joined Fields IV: a later warrant is an independent source only if both the decision to seek it and its issuance are genuinely independent of the illegality. Attenuation, inevitable discovery, consent if actually invoked, and other remedies require their actual factual and procedural predicates. Leon does not protect knowing or reckless deception, abandoned neutrality, affidavits wholly lacking reasonable probable-cause indicia, or facially deficient warrants; obtaining and supplying officers and proper execution scope matter. Evans's qualifying court-clerical-error rule supplies no categorical exception for this deliberate police investigation. No new Miranda, Franks, or other claim is manufactured. (X20–X21; Fields IV; Pinson III; Myers III.)

## 3. Individual grounds, alternatives, and join limits

### John Paul Stevens

**Threshold commitment.** Stevens maintains the position he joined and separately explained in Evans: the Court should not presume federal authority over a state suppression judgment invoking both constitutional charters when an independently sufficient state basis has not been affirmatively excluded. He would dismiss, recognizing that current Long/Evans doctrine permits review and that the existing opinion contains no established independent ground. His position is a proposal to retain his contrary jurisdictional rule, not a factual finding that Minnesota already supplied the plain statement. The strongest contrary route is unusually substantial here: the operative discussion expressly applies federal law and identifies a Fourth Amendment violation. Long therefore supplies the governing route for the Court. His contrary vote follows his published presumption, not a fabricated Minnesota holding. (Evans, jurisdiction and Stevens/Ginsburg separate positions; neutral §1; X10, X19.)

**Conditional personal-interest commitment.** If merits are reached, he would recognize each respondent's own interest. The lessee admitted both into the private living interior, participated with them for a substantial interval, and accepted compensation for that permitted use. They were jointly occupying the protected domestic setting, not merely passing through or claiming suppression from the host's ownership. Fields I-B, which Stevens joined, establishes that nonovernight shared privacy can belong personally to a permitted occupant. The present application removes recurrence as indispensable where actual, substantial joint private use is established. Rakas continues to bar mere presence and vicarious claims; Olson supplies the home-sharing premise without making sleep essential. The criminal task supplies no entitlement and forfeits none. The key proffer is unnecessary to his positive holding and is not credited; slippers add no legal status.

**Two paths.** The strongest contrary personal-interest argument is that Crawley's recurrence and respected closed bedroom provide a meaningful boundary absent from a brief paid transaction. Stevens would reject making that difference conclusive: residential sharing is actually exercised here, not inferred solely from a visitor label, and the task's market purpose does not change the place or the host's deliberate sharing. He would not join a rule protecting every delivery worker, public-facing business caller, or person with permission only to enter; those relationships are not this established joint occupancy. Conversely, he would refuse the four reversing Justices' implication that lack of continuing access necessarily defeats these proved interests.

**Conditional search and disposition.** He would hold this observation an unreasonable search even on the trial findings and reproduced standing-position account. A prolonged inspection from approximately a foot away through drawn domestic blinds, reached over grass outside the ordinary entrance route and initially using the brick projection's cover, is not established ordinary public exposure of the living interior. The lawn's shared uses and a bicycle by the window do not establish ordinary exposure to this sustained window inspection. Fields II-A supplies the limited-sharing rule; Katz and the Pinson rationale he joined deny physical-penetration immunity. Pinson's technological facts are an analogy, not the controlling factual classification. He need not find curtilage, climbing, crouching, trespass, a statutory crime, or a precise gap dimension. Accepting the disputed additions would strengthen, not create, his ground. The strongest contrary argument is the admitted unobstructed unaided visibility from common ground; he rejects equating the opportunity for a view with public exposure to the particular examination.

With no pre-observation probable cause, warrant, consent or supported exception, he would affirm the reviewed judgments. He would not join a reasonable-suspicion home-search exception or an automatic all-fruits rule. Ordinary state proceedings must respect each defendant's actual claims and the conditional arrest concession. His maintained threshold dismissal remains the primary vote; these are conditional merits commitments only.

## Knowles v. Iowa, No. 97-7597 — December 8, 1998

### Common judgment and sufficient ground

Each Associate votes to **reverse the Iowa Supreme Court's judgment and remand**. A completed citation without custodial arrest does not trigger the categorical search-incident-to-arrest exception. Unexercised authority to arrest cannot supply the custody-related safety and evidence-preservation predicates of a full search that actually follows only a citation. State authorization does not establish Fourth Amendment reasonableness by itself.

Chimel ties arrest-related search to protection of officers and preservation of evidence; Robinson permits the categorical full search incident to a lawful custodial arrest without requiring a new case-by-case showing for each such search. Belton addresses the vehicle setting of an arrest, not a categorical search after every citation. Here the officer issued the speeding citation and then searched without consent, probable cause to search or a preceding arrest. Transport and station custody risks had not arisen. The observed speeding violation supplies no further evidence ordinarily to be preserved by a complete automobile search. No hypothetical subsequent arrest is invented to validate the search retrospectively.

**Alternative-path test.** Iowa's strongest route combines actual authority to arrest with the danger that persists during any roadside encounter. That may support recognized limited safety measures, but it does not justify transferring the full arrest-search exception to a materially different encounter. Whren, Robinette and Maryland v. Wilson expressly preserve independent scope and search limits. An exit direction, Terry frisk or Michigan v. Long protective vehicle search requires its own governing conditions and scope; consent and the automobile exception have their own predicates. None is established for this search. The Court need not hold that every traffic stop is safe or make officers choose the least intrusive conceivable method. Ricci's separate civil-custody rule reinforces the distinction between state enforcement power and the reasonableness of an added intrusion, without deciding whether a custodial arrest for this offense would have been lawful.

## McClellan Federal Credit Union v. Parker

### Common decisional scope and alternatives

**P1 — jurisdiction and reach.** The lender has appellate standing because refusal of the proposed reaffirmation affects the enforceability of the proposed personal car-loan obligation and $1,500 unsecured obligation. The Ninth Circuit correctly reached the statutory issue despite the BAP's standing disposition. The supported economic injury is sufficient; it does not show that approval is legally due.

**P2 — statutory choice.** Section 521(2) does not make surrender, redemption or reaffirmation the exclusive substantive treatments of the collateral merely because the debtor files Chapter 7. It requires a timely statement of retention or surrender and the specified additional election when applicable, and timely performance of the lawful stated intention, with the enacted for-cause extensions intact. Subparagraph (C) prevents those notice/performance provisions from themselves changing substantive rights. A debtor may continue contract payments and retain collateral to the extent existing law and the enforceable agreement permit; this construction neither strips the lien nor invalidates a separately established default. It supplies no installment redemption at a judicially reduced value.

**P3 — approval and judgment.** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's judgment sustaining refusal of this reaffirmation. For this unrepresented debtor, §524(c)(6)(A) requires both no undue hardship and the debtor's best interest; the consumer-real-property exception in (6)(B) does not cover the car and unsecured card debt. The agreement's payment reduction, membership treatment and reduced unsecured amount do not make refusal an abuse of discretion on the reviewed premise. Keeping the lien and contractual payment stream while discharging personal liability is the Code's distinction, not a judicially created discharge exemption. The discharge and ordinary lien remedies remain; no foreclosure, new default ruling or substitute agreement is ordered.

**Competing path tested.** The creditor's strongest construction reads 'if applicable' as selecting which listed retention method applies, gives the performance deadline substantive force and treats continued possession without personal liability as an involuntary new bargain. On that path, the Ninth Circuit's exclusive-options ruling would be reversed and §524 approval reconsidered; it would still not follow that the court must approve an agreement that fails the separate hardship or best-interest tests. The competing difficulty is that the statute does not state a surrender consequence, (C) expressly limits what (A) and (B) alter, and §524(c)(2)(B) requires notice that reaffirmation is not required. Notice and timely performance retain practical work on the nonexclusive reading. Contract-enforcement concerns receive their proper protection through surviving liens and actual lawful defaults rather than an additional forfeiture provision.

The original note is not in the source set. No commitment adjudicates a bankruptcy-triggered default clause, insurance obligation or impairment covenant. Parker's legal challenge presents no independently established breach requiring resolution. A proposed opinion claiming an unconditional right to retain collateral despite any valid default would exceed every commitment below.

## The separate standard-of-review fork

### Chapman extension: O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer

These four would hold that, for the properly available capital-selection misinformation trial error at issue, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt from the whole trial record that the error did not contribute to the jury's actual selection of death. They extend the existing constitutional-habeas categories; they do not announce that Brecht, O'Neal or Roy already decided every trial error. An assumed error can support a methodological remand; a final grant of sentence relief still rests on an available established violation and failure of the applicable harmlessness showing.

O'Connor and Souter adhere to their published O'Neal Part III position favoring Chapman for ordinary preserved constitutional trial errors properly available on habeas. Their reasons include allocation of the risk of a constitutional violation's contribution, verdict reliability and the costs of multiplying categories. Ginsburg and Breyer previously reserved that generalization. They would extend Chapman here on the narrower capital-selection premise: the instruction affects understanding of the sole nondeath alternative and the significance of mitigation in the jury's constitutional choice. That rationale is at least as closely connected to the jury's protected decision as the personal-intent omission they addressed in O'Neal and Roy, and capital-correction doctrine already demands a State-borne noncontribution inquiry in its own defined settings. They continue to reserve the standard for other categories.

**Strongest contrary path.** The present category differs from the guilt-phase personal-intent cases, and collateral finality and comity remain substantial. Those considerations support the alternative proposed by the other four Associates; they cannot be dismissed by calling Kotteakos historically universal law. The Chapman adherents answer that the difference does not justify assigning to this prisoner the risk that a constitutional distortion contributed to a death decision. Ordinary habeas gates and actual harmlessness remain available, and no structural-error rule follows. Capital status alone is not a substitute for this identified relationship to the selection task.

**Method.** Roy's qualified actual-verdict reasoning is useful by analogy: reliable whole-record inferences are permissible, but neither strong aggravation nor an imagined properly instructed jury's likely choice establishes noncontribution. The issue is the effect on this jury's choice, including whether the defect kept it from giving the actual mitigation and nondeath option their proper significance. Necessary findings are not an exclusive universal method for this kind of sentencing error; Ginsburg's separate Roy position about an omitted offense purpose is not converted into a requirement of nonexistent factor-by-factor capital findings.

### Whole-record substantial influence with grave doubt: Stevens and Kennedy

These two would adopt Kotteakos's substantial-and-injurious-effect inquiry for this still-reserved constitutional-habeas category. Their published O'Neal concurrence supplies the allocation: the reviewing court assesses the whole record independently, the State must explain why the established violation did not substantially influence the actual decision, and genuine grave doubt about that effect prevents treating the error as harmless. Coleman need not prove innocence or establish that a properly instructed jury necessarily would have selected life. A demanding actual-effect inquiry is required, not bare sufficiency of untainted aggravation.

Their strongest objection is the Chapman continuity and capital-reliability rationale above. They accept the existing Chapman holdings within their categories and all independent capital-correction rules, but do not treat this commutation misinformation as either a personal-intent omission or an invalid aggravator. The distinct remedial role of collateral review supports the substantial-influence threshold here while the grave-doubt consequence protects against sustaining a sentence when the reviewing court cannot resolve the relevant effect. Their standard is not the State-borne beyond-a-reasonable-doubt rule and cannot be counted as a join in it. Stevens and Kennedy disagree on this record's sufficiency, as set out below.

### Prisoner showing of substantial injury: Scalia and Thomas

These two would require Coleman to establish that this assumed constitutional trial error had a substantial and injurious effect or influence on the selection of death. Their published O'Neal dissent supplies the proposed rule for a category outside the controlling Doyle and personal-intent holdings. In their view, §2254(a)'s custody-in-violation requirement and collateral review's limited remedial role support that prisoner showing; they acknowledge that the statute does not expressly state a tie-breaking rule. An evenly balanced showing does not itself entitle the prisoner to relief under their position.

They accept Brecht, O'Neal and Roy's binding specific holdings without claiming that their Roy join overruled their reserved-category position. Their strongest contrary path is the risk allocation supported by Chapman and the Stevens–Kennedy grave-doubt approach. They would reject both extensions for this category, while preserving structural errors, independent habeas law and properly proved constitutional trial-error claims. Their position requires neither innocence in every habeas case nor a reclassification of the assumed error as nonconstitutional. It is not merged with the Stevens–Kennedy standard merely because both use substantial influence.

## Enhanced comparison of the two actual-disposition paths

### Path A: the existing substantive analysis supports retaining sentence relief

**Adopted by Stevens under his substantial-influence/grave-doubt rule and by Ginsburg under the specified Chapman extension.** Both would deny the State's petition; if the Court grants the question, each votes to affirm the sentence-relief judgment on the concrete merits ground below. This is a completed alternative merits commitment, not a request to defer choosing a standard or application.

The District Court's reasons at App. 149–151 connect the instruction to the actual sentencing choice. The mitigation jury heard explained violent behavior through family trauma, prolonged institutionalization, impaired emotional development and tentative psychiatric evidence. The prosecution attacked those explanations and stressed violence inside and outside prison. An inaccurate suggestion that a single official could open the path to parole could turn explanations of violence into additional reasons to doubt the adequacy of the nondeath option. The prior convictions had actual salience, reflected by the request for their documents, while their legal effect made commutation more constrained than the jury was told. The request does not prove an actual release discussion; it identifies a concrete feature of the sentencing record that made the omitted limitation material.

These two do not treat the prosecutor's remarks as an express commutation argument. They accept the substantial focus on danger to inmates and staff and the absence of an express reference to the Governor. But the District Court's judicial account also records broader danger language; incarceration-based danger was not the only relationship between the evidence and the sentencing alternatives that the jury could consider. The instruction itself supplied the misleading information. Under Ginsburg's rule, the serious aggravation and valid institutional-danger argument fail to establish beyond reasonable doubt that the error made no contribution. Under Stevens's rule, the connection among the inaccurate alternative, the mitigation's explanatory force and the actual emphasis on violent propensity is sufficiently concrete to leave grave doubt about substantial influence. These are their separate sufficient determinations, not an assertion that the District Court expressly used their precise formulas.

**Corrective instruction and other adverse evidence.** The direction not to consider or speculate about commutation, the repeated charge, the extensive violent history and the State's reported short deliberation all favor harmlessness and are assessed. The charge first purported to clarify life without parole through the misleading information and then told the jury to disregard the subject. The District Court specifically explained why it did not regard that direction as curing the distortion. Stevens and Ginsburg find that explanation persuasive in this actual penalty context, not a universal rule that corrective directions never work. Short deliberation is not a finding that a particular consideration was absent. Nor does the absence of an express parole question remove an instruction's influence; unlike Simmons, this case cannot rely on such a question as affirmative evidence. Their conclusions do not rely on New's excluded account or on later-developed mitigation.

**Why they reject remand.** The lower reasoning identifies the particular mechanism and actual record facts, rather than presuming that every inaccurate instruction requires relief. The Ninth Circuit's abbreviated Boyde explanation is incomplete as a general statement of harmlessness, but the District Court's analysis and the present supported record permit the proper application. Each would state the distinct effect inquiry and affirm under it. A remand just to relabel those grounds would not identify a material task necessary to decide the State's narrow petition. This follows Stevens's published Roy distinction between a method stated too broadly and a judgment already supported under the correct substantive inquiry. Ginsburg's personal Roy and Simmons positions likewise resist replacing the jury's decision with an appellate conclusion that strong evidence independently deserved death.

**Limits.** Their preferred denial of certiorari creates no substantive holding and leaves the appellate judgment in place. Their alternative merits affirmance retains sentence-only relief with a reasonable opportunity for lawful state sentencing; it does not reopen guilt, require unconditional release, prescribe a new deadline or decide another penalty claim. They do not convert the State's limited question into a broad concession of every constitutional assertion below. For this petition they accept the unreviewed error determination and decide only the challenge to the sufficiency of the effect assessment.

### Path B: a distinct actual-effect assessment remains unfinished

**Adopted by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.** Each votes to grant review confined to the presented effect question, vacate the Ninth Circuit's sentence-relief disposition on the commutation ground and remand. The existing denial of guilt relief is undisturbed. Their standards and burden directions differ as stated above; a common judgment does not erase those differences.

Their identified unfinished task is to assess what effect the misleading commutation account had on the actual death decision under the selected burden, considering the affirmative reasons that the error may have made no relevant contribution or no substantial contribution. The District Court identified a plausible causal route: more apparent release risk could diminish the mitigating force of background and institutional history. It did not independently resolve that route against the combination of the corrective instruction, the actual institution-focused danger argument, the strength and nature of the aggravation and mitigation the jury heard, and the limits of what the conviction-document request establishes. Its ultimate reasoning treats likely difficulty in considering mitigation and potential diversion as sufficient to establish unconstitutional sentencing. The Ninth Circuit's express response to the effect objection adopts the Boyde reasonable-likelihood formulation as the answer, without identifying the additional effect determination under any of the competing harmlessness burdens.

The defect therefore is not simply that the lower courts omitted Chapman or Kotteakos citations, failed to create another heading or neglected to mention the record. A record-specific explanation can still establish the constitutional error without deciding its legally required consequence. The District Court's discussion of the inconsistent command supplies an important reason against harmlessness; it is not ignored. But showing why a direction did not remove the initial constitutional defect does not necessarily decide whether the defect affected the actual sentence under the selected risk allocation. Likewise, the request for conviction records supports their salience without showing whether the inaccurate release possibility, as distinct from past violent conduct and continuing institutional danger, contributed to the penalty choice. The remand must address that distinction without demanding impossible proof of subjective deliberations.

**Strongest objection and response.** Stevens and Ginsburg can apply a proper standard now and conclude that the State cannot show harmlessness or that genuine grave doubt remains. These six acknowledge that lawful result is available on remand. They nevertheless find that neither lower opinion states or substantively resolves the competing causal inquiry clearly enough to attribute that legal determination to it, and they decline to make the final whole-trial effect judgment on summary review. Their remand corrects the identified relation between error, effect and burden rather than demanding ritual language or a second identical inquiry. No direction to find harmlessness accompanies it.

**Required remand content.** The receiving court must use the existing appropriate trial record and complete charge, separate lawful in-prison danger from the argued broader community danger, consider the actual mitigation and its possible displacement, evaluate the corrective directions and rereading, and give the conviction request only the supported significance. No particular factor is assigned a numeric weight, and no checklist replaces the governing whole-record judgment. Properly supported historical findings retain applicable protection; legal harmlessness is not insulated by relabeling it fact. The excluded juror proffer remains excluded on this submission; no new admission ruling or evidentiary hearing is ordered. The court may reach the same sentence-relief result after the required inquiry.

## Haddle v. Garrison

### Common decisive proposition

**H1.** Reverse the injury-based dismissal and remand. A person who loses at-will employment through acts furthering a conspiracy forbidden by the federal-witness portion of §1985(2) may suffer injury to person or property within the statute's civil-remedy clause even without a constitutional entitlement to continued employment. The employer's lawful ability to end employment does not erase the employee's economic interest against prohibited interference. The rule removes the categorical injury obstacle; it neither establishes the conspiracy nor creates permanent tenure.

**H2.** No damages, reinstatement or final liability follows. The existence and proper object of the conspiracy, statutory witness nexus, qualifying acts, causation, damages and properly presented defenses remain. The Court does not decide whether the particular defendants can conspire as agents of one corporation or whether each is a legally independent third party. The pleaded characterization does not answer those questions.

**Two-path comparison.** The constitutional-entitlement path relies on Roth and the absence of an enforceable promise of future work. Its force is that at-will employment cannot be treated as guaranteed property against termination by the employer. The statutory path answers that Congress prohibited a specified conspiracy and allowed recovery for injury to person or property, separately from deprivation of a right or privilege. Kush requires attention to the actual clause; Truax recognizes that at-will employment has protection from wrongful third-party interference. The statute's specific federal-process object prevents the broader reading from creating a federal action for every firing. No independent constitutional deprivation or class-based equal-protection purpose is added to the first part of §1985(2).

## NYNEX Corp. v. Discon

### Components, lawful alternatives and remedy

**N1.** Reject the proposed per se boycott classification for the pleaded buyer–supplier switch without a horizontal agreement among competitors. A two-firm vertical purchasing arrangement does not become a classic per se group boycott solely because it intentionally harms a rival, serves a fraudulent purpose or lacks an apparent efficiency justification. Klor's involved the coordinated exclusionary structure that gives that category its justification; Business Electronics and Northwest Wholesale Stationers prevent converting every refusal to deal into it. The rule of reason can test harm to competition in an arrangement outside the established category. This does not exempt genuine horizontal arrangements, actual price fixing or conduct satisfying another established category.

**N2.** Regulatory deception, consumer overcharges and destruction of one supplier do not alone establish that the challenged purchasing agreement injured the competitive process. A monopolist's use of an existing regulated market position to charge more through deception is not automatically an anticompetitive reduction in rivalry among removal-service suppliers. Discon must establish the requisite competitive injury and the elements of the theory actually pursued. Neither a harmless-business-purpose finding nor a categorical no-liability finding follows.

**N3.** Vacate the judgment insofar as the surviving §1 claim was allowed to proceed on the availability of per se boycott treatment, and remand for proceedings under the proper rule. Preserve consideration of the remaining §1 competitive-effects theory and the separately reinstated §2 conspiracy-to-monopolize claim, with the latter reconsidered to the extent it relies on the rejected per se or fraud-equals-competitive-harm premises. The Court does not finally adjudicate that §2 claim on a grant framed around §1. The dismissed vertical-price-fixing, monopolization, attempt and RICO components are not revived or newly affirmed on their merits. The District Court's wholesale dismissal is not automatically reinstated. No damages or final injunction is awarded.

**Alternative-path test.** The strongest per se argument is that purchasing from a higher-cost supplier only to punish a lower-priced rival has no conceivable competitive virtue, so Klor's should reach its exclusionary impact regardless of formal vertical structure. The counterargument is that per se classification depends on a reliably anticompetitive class, not a finding of bad motive or moral illegality in one transaction. Without that distinction, a buyer choosing any supplier for a questionable reason could face automatic treble-damages liability without proof of lost competition. The strongest complete-dismissal argument is that the alleged customer injury comes from regulatory fraud and preexisting telephone monopoly, not reduced supplier rivalry. That defeats treating the pleaded fraud alone as sufficient but does not justify deciding every separate preserved theory or factual possibility without the lower court's proper review. The lower decision explicitly preserved §1 rule-of-reason and §2 conspiracy claims, so the remand must state those limits.
