## Reach and judgment

All eight modeled Justices participate and reach the preserved federal qualification question. None finds a supported jurisdictional, participation or vehicle ground for stopping ordinary merits review. They would **reverse the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand for proceedings consistent with restoration of the employer’s summary judgment on the reviewed termination claim**, because the ADA did not require Albertson’s to abandon the operative federal vision criterion in favor of this experimental waiver program. This is independently sufficient regardless of which disability route Kirkingburg can establish.

The judgment does not find that Kirkingburg drove unsafely, that every monocular person is unqualified, or that every federal waiver can be disregarded. It does not adjudicate damages, order employment, settle a newly asserted post-waiver cause of action, or find that the offered alternative job was a reasonable accommodation. The later issued waiver is accepted as part of the record; it is not treated as a nonexistent application or as retroactively in effect at termination. The rule concerns the legal significance of this program, not an unexplained precise issuance date.

## Exact propositions available for joins

**Q1 — Independent statutory questions.** Actual disability under §12102(2)(A), regarded-as coverage under §12102(2)(C), ability to perform driving duties, compliance with a lawful federal qualification, and reasonable accommodation are distinct. Skilled driving does not alone eliminate a substantial limitation in seeing; disability does not itself establish qualification. Cleveland supports the separation of statutory elements, not any SSDI explanation requirement here.

**Q2 — Limited effect of this experimental program.** The ordinary bilateral acuity requirement in 49 C.F.R. §391.41(b)(10) remained unchanged. The 1992 program selected experienced drivers for a study intended to generate evidence for possible revision; it did not embody a contemporaneous determination that the ordinary standard could safely be displaced by its experimental eligibility criteria. On this regulatory record, the ADA did not require an employer to substitute participation in that experiment for compliance with the ordinary federal vision standard. The holding permits this employer’s reliance; it does not assert that federal law compelled rejection of every issued waiver or that a waiver holder was legally forbidden to drive.

**Q3 — Consequence for this claim and proposed accommodation.** Kirkingburg could not meet the ordinary vision standard. His actual driving record and eventual waiver do not defeat Q2. Leave to obtain that experimental waiver would not remove the employer’s lawful ability to require the ordinary qualification, so that proposed route does not preserve the reviewed claim. This conclusion does not rest on the alternative-job offer, its unreported terms, or the employee’s rejection. Reverse the appellate judgment and return the case for implementation of the employer’s entitlement on the reviewed claim; no trial or additional accommodation proceeding is commanded.

**Q4 — Relation to direct threat, agency authority and time.** Reliance permitted by Q2 is not an employer-created medical judgment that must independently prove Kirkingburg poses a significant risk. Bragdon remains applicable within its own scope. The Ninth Circuit also went too far in treating waiver participation as establishing no safety threat and precluding any contrary assertion. An experimental authorization is not a conclusive medical finding. Later evidence of program success cannot supply knowledge at the 1992 termination; conversely, later invalidation is not treated as a reason Albertson’s actually possessed then. The contemporaneous structure and scientific purpose support Q2 without an independent collateral invalidation of the program. Reserve the Hobbs Act question and the legal effect of other waiver systems or an amended federal qualification.

**D1 — Individual substantial limitation.** Section 12102(2)(A) requires a substantial limitation in this individual’s seeing. Seeing with one eye rather than two is not, without the statutory degree of restriction, the complete inquiry. Complete bilateral blindness is not required. The lower court’s diagnosis-and-difference formulation cannot establish a rule covering every monocular person.

**D2 — Natural adaptation.** When assessing this person’s actual seeing, the factfinder must consider what his own compensating mechanisms permit and what substantial restrictions remain. Natural compensation is neither categorically irrelevant nor categorically disqualifying. Depth perception, peripheral vision, permanence and the condition or manner of seeing remain material under the cited regulation. Successful performance of a particular driving job does not answer the whole inquiry. This proposition does not decide the general treatment of medication, devices or every external mitigating measure, which Bragdon reserved.

**D3 — No final disability application.** D1–D2 correct the categorical method; they do not decide that Kirkingburg has failed, or has conclusively established, actual disability on a complete record. The manager’s description remains evidence relevant to the separate regarded-as route; perception of an impairment and perception of a substantial limitation must be distinguished, but no final regarded-as sufficiency ruling is necessary. Neither route independently saves a claim defeated by Q2–Q3. No disability-based judgment against the employee is entered, and no freestanding disability remand is ordered after the qualification ground disposes of the reviewed claim.

## Competing supported paths and strongest objections

**Employer qualification path selected.** The unchanged federal standard, the experimental nature of the program and its contemporaneous absence of the safety determination attributed to it by the Ninth Circuit support Q2. The strongest objection is that an actual waiver permitted lawful driving and the employee had demonstrated ability, making refusal a private screening rule subject to §§12112–12113. The selected answer is limited: legal permission to take part in a study is not itself a command that an employer replace the still-operative criterion with the experimental one. No new finding of unsafe driving is needed. The answer would not establish the same result for an ordinary waiver grounded in a completed substantive determination or for an amended standard.

**Employee remand path considered.** Preserve the appellate reversal because the issued waiver and driving record remove a categorical federal bar, requiring Albertson’s to justify its own exclusion and consider accommodation. Bragdon’s individual objective method and Cleveland’s independent statutory analysis support that argument; the lower majority’s zero-risk and categorical-preclusion language need not be adopted to reach a remand. Its strongest difficulty is the very specific 1992 regulatory posture: the program was gathering the evidence needed to decide whether and how the standard could be changed. It is not enough to show that driving under a waiver was allowed. The selected commitments distinguish permission to experiment from an ADA duty to use the experiment. They do not dismiss the employee’s path because missing research amounts to failure of proof.

**Disability-only employer path rejected as a complete disposition.** The lower categorical rule is vulnerable under Bragdon’s individual method, but successful driving and natural compensation do not establish that the remaining seeing limitations are insubstantial. The record also contains a separate regarded-as basis. A final employee loss solely on actual disability would leave that alternative untreated and would require unsupported record conclusions. Six Justices join D1–D3 as a bounded method correction; none relies on it alone for judgment. Stevens and Breyer preserve the open question because Q2–Q3 already suffice.

**Final liability or mandatory hiring rejected.** Even an employee-favorable remand would establish neither ultimate disability nor every element of qualification, accommodation, defense and relief. No complete accommodation terms, exact waiver instrument or separately preserved later hiring claim is supplied. Those missing sources are reasons to avoid those additional determinations, not facts adverse to either litigant.
