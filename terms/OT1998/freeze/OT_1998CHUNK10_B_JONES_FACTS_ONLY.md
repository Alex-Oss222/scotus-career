## Verified predecision record facts

- The Joint Appendix table of contents lists defendant’s requested sentencing instructions 4 and 5 at printed pages 13–15; preliminary written objections at 16–24; oral charge-conference objections, November 2, 1995, transcript pages 2702–2709, at JA25–32; the charge and verdict forms follow.
- Written requested instruction 4 places the persuasion burden on the Government and says failure to persuade any juror that execution is required should result in life without possible release. Written instruction 5 says unanimity is necessary for death and describes life without release as the consequence of inability to agree; JA13–15, source lines703–765. These are the requested texts, not a ruling about their correctness or preservation effect.
- At the oral charge conference defense counsel asked that references to deciding death be accompanied by alternatives of life without release or a lesser sentence; JA25–26, source lines1151–1220. Counsel later referred specifically to requested instruction 5 and requested an explanation that inability to agree on death would lead to life without release or a lesser sentence; JA30, source lines1359–1385. Preserve this oral wording alongside the written requests; no factual harmonization is supplied.
- The delivered directions said Form B was for unanimous death. They directed use of C or D if the jury was not unanimous on the specified weighing grounds or was otherwise not unanimous in recommending death. They expressly described C as a unanimous life-without-release recommendation, and D as a recommendation of a lesser sentence; JA47–48, source lines2130–2175.
- Form C expressly calls for a unanimous verdict recommending life without possible release. Form D contains a lesser-sentence recommendation and a foreperson line, without the explicit unanimity phrase printed on C; JA58–59, source lines2676–2700. Form B is signed and dated November 3, 1995.
- The appendix prints the Government’s notice, special findings and charge; these permit independent verification of the aggravating-factor wording and verdict. The notice describes victim vulnerability and personal characteristics/family impact; JA11–12. The supplied packet says the jury found kidnapping and heinousness statutory aggravators, found the two described nonstatutory factors, rejected substantial planning/grave risk/future dangerousness, and one or more jurors found multiple mitigation factors. These supplied details should be validated against the printed special findings before adoption; no additional findings are made here.
- The petitioner and respondent briefs disagree about the effect of the oral and written objections. Those positions are preserved in source files, not resolved here. Petitioner merits brief pp.19–25 and respondent pp.15–24 contain the competing review arguments.
