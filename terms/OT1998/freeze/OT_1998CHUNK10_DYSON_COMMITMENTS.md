## Issue-specific alternatives

### Federal authority to review

Path A, adopted by all eight: jurisdiction and useful review under Cox and Long. The highest available state adjudication has made a binding federal determination that the State cannot effectively review later. Its ground is federal, unlike a judgment independently resting on a state constitution. Strongest objection: more proceedings may remain and state courts can still suppress under their own law. Answer: Cox's identified category addresses the first point; hypothetical independent state relief is not an already adequate ground and does not remove the practical utility of correcting this explicit federal error.

Path B: deny review as an exercise of discretion, or decline jurisdiction if an adequate independent state ground actually supports the judgment. The latter factual predicate is absent. Discretionary denial remains institutionally lawful, but the direct conflict with governing Labron in an uncomplicated moving-vehicle setting supplies stronger reasons to review than the speculative prospect of a later independent result. A plenary grant is also lawful but adds no presently identified issue requiring full merits briefing or argument. A request for response is appropriate only to satisfy actual procedure, not a merits substitute.

### Federal automobile rule and application

Path A, adopted by all eight if merits are reached: probable cause and ready mobility authorize this automobile search without a separate requirement that officers lacked time to obtain a warrant. Labron/Kilgore directly resolves prior opportunity, guarding and custody; Carroll, Chambers and Carney supply its surviving foundations, Johns preserves authority despite custody, and Ross confines scope to the object sought. The state court found abundant probable cause after the reliable informant's information and rental-car corroboration. Police encountered a moving rental Toyota; cocaine could be concealed in the trunk duffel bag. The asserted 13–14 hour earlier warrant opportunity adds no federal condition. This applies the rule to the challenged search; it does not merely ask the state court to reconsider whether to retain the same invalid requirement.

Strongest objection: extended advance knowledge permits ordinary judicial warrant review and may avoid unnecessary warrantless intrusions. Answer: that policy objection is inconsistent with Labron's binding holding and does not identify a different legal predicate here. The readily mobile car on the road presents none of Labron's disputed mechanical-status or Kilgore's premises-access considerations. No abandoned claim, new factual finding or reconsideration of Labron is needed.

Path B: vacate only for further examination of mobility, probable cause or scope, or affirm because earlier warrant opportunity defeats exigency. The latter cannot coexist with binding Labron without an expressly justified change in law, which no Associate proposes on this record. A fact-specific vacatur would be lawful if a material predicate were genuinely unsettled; the supplied state findings settle abundant probable cause and actual moving-vehicle mobility, and no special container or person-search issue is established. Additional hearing requirements would manufacture rather than resolve a supported uncertainty. Knowles's citation-only search limit has no work against independently established vehicle probable cause. Houghton's disagreement over a known passenger-owned detached effect does not establish such a claim in Dyson and creates no general separate-exigency exception.
