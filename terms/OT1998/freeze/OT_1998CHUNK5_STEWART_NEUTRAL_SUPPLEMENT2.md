## Earlier Walter counsel proceedings

The initial appellate opinion, 133 F.3d 1253, Walter section III.B, reports that Bruce Burke represented Walter throughout the proceedings then under review. Before appointing Burke in federal habeas, the District Court required him to discuss potential ineffective-assistance claims with Walter and report the result.

Burke reported explaining that ineffective assistance might provide a route to relief from the death sentence, while stating he knew of no instance of his own ineffective assistance. He also told Walter that the court would appoint additional or replacement counsel to examine his performance if Walter wanted it. Walter declined steps that might require Burke's withdrawal and declined seeking new counsel to examine the issue. This is the report reproduced and evaluated by the lower courts; it is not a new independent inquiry into every aspect of Walter's advice, capacity or understanding.

After appointment, Burke learned that Karl's counsel intended to present evidence of ineffective assistance at sentencing. Burke considered that development potentially relevant to his own sentencing performance and sought withdrawal. The District Court denied withdrawal because Walter had waived the offered change-of-counsel opportunity. It separately found no prejudice because the claimed failure did not constitute ineffective assistance. The appellate court affirmed under abuse-of-discretion review on the representation-choice ground. It described the waiver as one of new representation and its potential benefits, rather than a waiver of identified instances of ineffective assistance, and concluded that the particular then-asserted claim fell within that representation waiver. Its explanation does not adopt an independent comprehensive merits judgment on all possible ineffective-assistance claims.

These facts concern Walter, not Karl. They are distinct from the reported Arizona March 2, 1999 procedural bar on the later ineffective-assistance claim.
