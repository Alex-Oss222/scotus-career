# OT1998 chunk 5 - Jackson independent non-Stone commitments

## Scope, chronology, and source validation

Jackson v. Benson, No. 98-376; argument January 11, 1999; decision assignment March 16, 1999. All eight Associates participate. This model uses the frozen C neutral packet, the Jackson focused entering-law copy and its linked public positions, the exact 1995-96 statute and integrated-curriculum/cost findings, the Wisconsin court's complete federal discussion, and the earlier primary writings identified below. No present-case Stone material, historical Supreme Court comparator, or opinion assignment is supplied.

The actual March 3 Sullivan public decision was read in entering-law/american_manufacturers_v_sullivan_merits_1999-03-03.md before this freeze. Its particular private-carrier attribution holding does not attribute every permitted private act to government, and expressly preserves examination of government's own legislation and conduct. Its limited payment-entitlement holding neither grants constitutional immunity to public expenditures nor decides Establishment Clause financing. Jackson challenges Wisconsin's own cost-based expenditure. It does not require declaring every recipient school a state actor. Sullivan thus reinforces the required distinction but supplies no answer to the financing merits. Its actual individual positions, including Ginsburg's and Stevens's refusal to reach additional attribution and Breyer/Souter's continuation qualification, are not converted into Jackson votes.

Control reports completed Minns, Blaze, Holloway and Peguero rules outside the present Establishment Clause issues. No Gable holding is assumed while that matter remains under assembly. Neither Gable's election-finance dispute nor NFFE's pending FLRA statutory-authority question is invoked to decide Jackson. An actual later-supplied rule material to this analysis would require a fresh affected check; neither pending event is treated as law or as a reason to delay this otherwise complete model.

The source facts remain those of the frozen neutral packet. Section 119.23(4) pays the lower of MPS state aid per member and the school's per-pupil educational operating and debt-service cost, in four installments after proof of enrollment. The parent-payable check is sent to the school and restrictively endorsed. Payment is not capped at otherwise charged tuition. Supported findings describe schools with integrated devotional instruction and below-cost tuition supplemented by church support. They establish more than religious identity, but do not establish that every religious provider, every lesson or every funded expense is devotional.

The program retains income and prior-enrollment eligibility, caps, random selection with an optional sibling preference, nondiscrimination, health/safety duties, specified alternative performance criteria, accounting and independent audit. A parent must request the religious-activity exemption in writing; it is not an automatic secular-curriculum or secular-use limitation. Retained financial/academic oversight is not absent, but neither is it a reliable separation of devotional expenses. Paragraph 43 and footnote 16 of the lower opinion recite stipulated broader public, magnet, charter, suburban and other educational choices. That evidence supports genuine choice; it does not guarantee each family a seat at each desired school. The model assumes neither universal compulsion toward religion nor universally effective secular choice.

## Material issues and two lawful paths

### A. Review and object of adjudication

Path A reaches the final adverse federal Establishment Clause disposition under section 1257. The favorable state-law conclusions do not independently sustain conduct forbidden by the federal Constitution. The Court reviews the State's own financing, leaving school-attribution questions unnecessary and independent Wisconsin religious-benefit, local-bill, public-purpose, uniformity and racial-equality issues outside review.

Path B would withhold a federal decision if an actual jurisdictional defect, unresolved necessary claimant predicate or adequate independent ground defeated this review. It cannot be adopted merely by importing the limits on a new federal taxpayer action into review of this final state judgment, or by treating the schools' private status as immunity for Wisconsin's appropriation. No supported defect defeats the present bounded federal review. All eight choose A. Their individual merits disagreements do not create new Article III facts, a damages claimant or a coercion claim.

### B. Expenditure and private-benefit characterization

The governing rule is Agostini's June 23, 1997 modified Lemon inquiry: identify the governmental act and function; apply a controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine when it resolves that act, an established permissible relationship where applicable, and otherwise decide a legally sufficient claim through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. Purpose, effect and institutional relationships are evidence, not three surviving independent prongs. Financing asks what government purchases or performs, without making all recipient activity government speech. Actual devotional financing cannot be justified by a secular objective. Independently identifiable secular work may be purchased with reliable controls against devotional use/diversion. Religious identity and location alone do not establish a violation.

The strongest permissive path is a private educational benefit. Neutral eligibility, independently selected schools, an actual range of public and private alternatives, no State selection of doctrine, and payment controlled by enrollment and educational cost together can characterize the State's activity as enabling a child's independently directed education. Witters upheld a neutral vocational benefit even for ministerial training; Mueller upheld a genuine generally available tax deduction. Neither was overruled. A parent-payable restricted check is relevant, but its form is not conclusive. The strongest contrary evidence is that this particular grant reimburses the provider's educational operating/debt-service cost, can replace church subsidy above ordinary tuition, and contains no separation of the integrated religious instruction shown by the record. Agostini expressly reserved vouchers and says parental choice alone does not answer the expenditure inquiry. The permissive path must decide that objection rather than make it disappear by calling the program neutral.

The strongest restrictive path treats the supported payment for integrated devotional instruction as financing that function. Wisconsin need not direct the theology or become the speaker to be responsible for its own expenditure. The parent endorsement and opt-out do not segregate costs or make the remaining instruction secular. But this path must explain why Witters's genuine individual vocational benefit and Mueller's general tax relationship remain lawful. It cannot prohibit all aid to all religious institutions, treat a religious perspective on an admitted subject as necessarily devotional, or presume indivisibility from a religious name. The narrower corrective disposition sets aside the categorical federal approval and remands to apply the funded-function distinction and determine appropriately limited relief, using the existing record where sufficient. It does not order a new trial or assume no additional fact could matter.

Rosenberger approved an actual printing benefit: secular expressive purpose, broad availability, independent editing/attribution and controlled vendor payments together mattered. Its religious editorial viewpoint is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Bronx Household approved genuinely private rental access, including comparable worship, without deciding public devotional financing. The lower opinion's historical Zobrest aid-merits rationale supplies no authority in this chronology. Agostini displaced Aguilar's categorical location rule and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale, not Ball's distinct Community Education judgment or every particular aid limit.

### C. Remedy and scope

For Justices rejecting categorical approval, the supported consequence is vacatur of the federal Establishment Clause disposition and of the blanket injunction-dissolution direction insofar as dependent on that disposition, with a confined remand. The existing integrated-instruction evidence must be considered; it cannot be replaced by identity alone or ignored because a check names a parent. The lower court must distinguish any demonstrated devotional purchase from lawful independently identifiable secular work and genuine private-benefit relationships and settle state-law severability and appropriate prospective relief. No mandatory new evidentiary proceeding is imposed when the existing record suffices.

The competing remedy is affirmance of the federal judgment because the program is a permissible private educational benefit, reserving a claim about actual official direction, a sham choice mechanism, discriminatory allocation or independently prohibited administration if properly presented later. That path does not confer a constitutional entitlement to public money or command every school to participate.

Neither path redecides the independent state-law holdings or NAACP racial-equality question. The August 25, 1995 educational-status protection for children who entered private schools during litigation is preserved to its lawful scope; income, enrollment caps and other statutory conditions are not waived. A restrictive judgment cannot guarantee continuation of unconstitutional payments, but it does not itself order school closure, pupil transfer, recoupment, damages or an immediate total funding cutoff. The remedy may regulate public funding; it does not command schools to alter their private theology. No court-written replacement program is adopted.

## Individual enhanced commitments

### Stevens

**Result and sufficient ground.** Reach issue A. On B and C, vacate the categorical federal approval and its dependent remedial direction and remand on the limited funded-function ground. The State's actual educational-cost payment cannot be upheld across the challenged integrated devotional instruction merely because a parent chooses the school. Supported integration and the absence of secular-use limits require a real financing analysis, not a presumption that the provider's public and private costs have become independent.

**Individual anchors.** He joined Marshall's Mueller dissent (June 29, 1983), which treated actual tuition assistance and unrestricted instructional subsidies as materially different from secular transportation or supplies; he joined the Witters Court opinion (January 27, 1986), whose particular independent vocational benefit remains an important limit on that position. He joined Souter's substantive and stare-decisis Agostini dissent (June 23, 1997) and Souter's Rosenberger dissent (June 29, 1995). His Bronx Household instruction join (May 11, 1998) accepts private religious perspective within an opened instructional forum; it does not approve the funding here. His actual Sullivan position (March 3, 1999) likewise distinguishes a protected statutory claim and proper governmental process from unnecessary private-attribution adjudication; it supplies no aid immunity.

**Adversarial test.** The best affirmance is Witters plus authentic neutral choice and the public alternatives. Stevens accepts the preserved holdings but rejects an automatic extension to a State payment calculated from the institution's instructional operating/debt costs where the recorded instruction integrates devotion. This is a substantive connection, not a count of religious recipients. The fact that some secular applications are lawful does not defeat the challenge to the religious-function component; conversely, the challenge does not justify invalidating those secular applications.

**Joins, alternatives and remedy.** He will join a narrow prohibition on the supported devotional expenditure and the confined corrective remand without re-adopting Agostini's modified framework as his preferred test. He will not join a private-choice safe harbor, the old three independent prongs as current law, or a new automatic exclusion of every religious provider. His broader institutional-support concerns may be separately stated but are unnecessary to the common disposition. State-law severability and actual permissible relief remain below; the specific enrollment-status protection is not silently erased. He rejects path A's supposed jurisdictional barriers on the supported posture, while preserving any genuinely necessary claimant limit for broader relief.

### O'Connor

**Result and sufficient ground.** Reach issue A. Vacate the blanket federal approval and remand under Agostini's financing distinction; join the proposition that Wisconsin cannot finance the shown devotional instructional function merely through parent-directed provider-cost payments. She does not declare every religious-school payment unconstitutional. Her decisive point is the particular object of this cost-based expenditure and the missing reliable separation for the integrated instructional work, not religion's presence in the recipient institution.

**Individual anchors.** Her Witters separate opinion (January 27, 1986) expressly relied on neutral individual benefit and private decision, and her Mueller Court join (June 29, 1983) supplies the genuine tax-benefit analogue. Her Bowen concurrence (June 29, 1988) distinguishes facially valid cooperation from actual public promotion of religious doctrine and calls for a remedy fitted to demonstrated administration. In the current public chronology she fully joined Agostini's modified framework and its financing qualifications (June 23, 1997), including the reserved voucher question. Her Rosenberger concurrence (June 29, 1995) emphasizes independent expression and the concrete payment safeguards; her Bronx Household join (May 11, 1998) concerns genuinely private access.

**Adversarial test.** Affirmance is a serious lawful alternative for her, not an ideological presumption: the poverty criteria, broader educational choices and parental control closely resemble the reasons she gave in Witters. She nevertheless regards the present cost reimbursement, including replacement of the gap between tuition and instructional cost, plus supported integration of devotional teaching as requiring the expenditure analysis she later adopted in Agostini. Witters's actual individual vocational assistance and Mueller's genuine tax deduction are preserved; neither establishes that every restricted payment of a provider's instructional costs is the same relationship. Parental choice and academic/accounting oversight do not themselves establish secular performance. The written opt-out is protection against required participation upon request, not control over the financed instructional costs.

**Joins, alternatives and remedy.** Her join is confined to the actual expenditure rule, insufficiency of blanket approval on this record, and tailored remand. She refuses an institution-wide presumption, a universal ban on private religious educational benefits, or a finding that no religious provider could identify a lawful secular service. She also refuses a decree inventing secular-use terms and announcing that the present statute already contains them. If the governing opinion confines the remedy as issue C states, she joins; she does not require further evidence as a ritual or erase the supported integration findings. An actual broader claimant or funding remedy would need its own support. Her threshold vote reaches the final federal question, not an original federal taxpayer suit.

### Scalia

**Result and sufficient ground.** Reach issue A and affirm the federal Establishment Clause judgment on B and C. He treats the actual combination of neutral student eligibility, parental selection among supported public/private choices, independently operated schools, and controlled educational-benefit payments as a permissible private-benefit relationship. Religious curriculum selected by a parent does not, on these program facts, become the religious function the State purchases as its own undertaking.

**Individual anchors.** His join of Kennedy's Bowen concurrence (June 29, 1988) insists on actual religious expenditure rather than a recipient-identity presumption. His full Rosenberger Court join (June 29, 1995), full Agostini join (June 23, 1997), and Bronx Household worship/access join (May 11, 1998) require examining the actual function and preserve room for genuinely private choice. His Sullivan Part I join (March 3, 1999) supports precise attribution, while that opinion's reservation of governmental acts prevents using it to avoid the appropriation question.

**Adversarial test.** The strongest challenge is that payment follows institutional cost, including integrated religious instruction, rather than an unrestricted individual grant. He answers that the educational benefit belongs to the eligible child, that independent parental selection determines which school receives it, and that enrollment/cost controls make the benefit verifiable without making school instruction a public curriculum. Reimbursement up to actual educational cost can give institutions incidental financial support without changing that relationship. This is more than neutrality alone: there is no finding that government selects doctrine, directs religious participation, prefers religious recipients or makes the offered choice a sham. Witters expressly permits aid reaching ministerial study; Agostini's voucher reservation leaves room to recognize this analogous private relationship rather than requiring every individual benefit to be rendered secular.

**Joins, alternatives and remedy.** He will join a program-specific affirmance explaining these factors and the strongest cost/integration objection. He will not join a universal rule that neutral funding always passes, a denial of all governmental responsibility for expenditures, or resurrection of historical Zobrest as merits authority. He accepts Agostini's prohibition where the State actually purchases devotional work and would preserve a supported claim of such distinct administration. He would not join O'Connor's characterization here. His affirmance preserves the limited state enrollment-status protection and other actual statutory requirements, creates no money entitlement, and does not decide ungranted issues. If the Court instead remands, he can agree that relief must remain confined without joining its merits ground.

### Kennedy

**Result and sufficient ground.** Reach A; affirm B and C on the particular private-benefit relationship. The program pays for a child's educational opportunity on neutral eligibility and cost rules, with the school selected independently by the family. Its private instructional character does not change solely because the State verifies costs or directs the check to the selected school for endorsement.

**Individual anchors.** His Bowen concurrence joined by Scalia (June 29, 1988) distinguishes actual expenditure from institutional character; his Rosenberger Court opinion (June 29, 1995) combines neutrality, independently attributable expression and real vendor-funded benefit, without making one factor universally sufficient. His full Agostini join (June 23, 1997) preserves the actual private-benefit cases and reserves this category. His Bronx Household join (May 11, 1998) protects genuinely private religious activity; his Sullivan Court opinion (March 3, 1999) distinguishes private choice from the separately reviewable governmental act.

**Adversarial test.** The contrary path has force because the check pays institutional educational cost and no secular-use term excludes the integrated religious instruction. He does not deny those facts or call the payment merely tuition. He concludes that the government's function here is the bounded individual benefit, as shown by eligibility independent of faith, the supported nonreligious alternatives, no government curricular selection and parental direction essential to every payment. Witters's allowance for privately selected ministerial study demonstrates that the beneficiary may use a genuine individual benefit for religious training without making that training a State devotional program. Unlike a State-operated or State-selected instructional service, the cost formula regulates the amount of the benefit rather than adopting the school's religious message. The retained controls matter in identifying this benefit, not because they segregate religion when they do not.

**Joins, alternatives and remedy.** He joins a concrete affirmance and rejects an automatic parental-signature rule or an identity-based exclusion. An opinion must answer Agostini's question about the governmental expenditure; simply finding the schools private would be insufficient. He would reserve actual State direction, religious preference or a sham independent-choice arrangement if supported in a proper claim. He will not join a broad reopening of Ball, a no-coercion-only test, or a universal voucher rule. Affirmance has the bounded consequences in C; no constitutional right to a subsidy, school participation order or adjudication of other state grounds follows. If another majority rejects the program, he accepts only a confined remedy while dissenting from the financing premise.

### Souter

**Result and sufficient ground.** Reach A; vacate the categorical approval and dependent remedial direction on B and C. The actual State payment finances an integrated instructional service whose devotional component is shown, not presumed from affiliation. The device of routing that cost payment through parental endorsement does not answer the constitutional objection.

**Individual anchors.** His Rosenberger dissent (June 29, 1995), joined by Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, distinguishes a genuine separately chosen private benefit from publicly financed religious activity. His Agostini Parts I and II dissent (June 23, 1997) expresses substantive institutional-financing and precedent objections; governing Agostini nevertheless supplies the operative law here. His Bronx Household position (May 11, 1998) accepts genuinely private worship attribution and admitted religious instruction while declining the broader worship-forum application. His actual Sullivan joins and Breyer-concurrence join (March 3, 1999) do not merge governmental financing with private attribution.

**Adversarial test.** The best affirmance is an authentic poverty-based benefit with an independent parental decision and a broad choice context. He accepts that those factors distinguish private activity from government speech. They do not explain why a public payment of educational operating/debt costs containing actual devotional instruction should count as financing only secular opportunity. Witters's individualized vocational benefit and Mueller's tax relationship remain effective at their scope; neither decides this program or transforms all parental routing into insulation. Conversely, he cannot rest current judgment on his rejected categorical institutional-support theory or treat religious editorial perspective alone as impermissible devotion.

**Joins, alternatives and remedy.** He joins the narrow funded-function prohibition and corrective remand as an application of governing law, without joining an unnecessary renewed endorsement of the entire Agostini taxonomy. He would state broader concerns separately if needed. He refuses approval justified solely by no governmental indoctrination or by schools' private identity. The lawful secular service and genuine private-benefit limits must remain explicit; the particular provider/payment scope and state severability remain below. He accepts no automatic new trial, total school exclusion or student transfer. Review under A is proper on this final adverse federal judgment; broader relief retains actual claimant limits.

### Thomas

**Result and sufficient ground.** Reach A; affirm B and C on the concrete neutral private-benefit ground. The eligibility and parental-selection structure, actual diversity of educational choices, independently run curriculum and bounded enrollment/cost payment together leave religious instruction the family's private choice rather than a government religious undertaking.

**Individual anchors.** His Rosenberger concurrence and Court join (June 29, 1995) defend neutral educational benefits while preserving the actual Court's program-specific holding. His full Agostini join (June 23, 1997) adopts the financing prohibition and its private-benefit reservations; his Bronx Household join (May 11, 1998) protects comparable private religious use. His Sullivan joins (March 3, 1999) reinforce precise attribution and the limited consequence of statutory eligibility, not a general funding immunity.

**Adversarial test.** The strongest challenge is that public money pays integrated religious instructional costs with no enforceable secular separation. He answers that a genuine individual education benefit may be spent on religious training without government adoption, as Witters illustrates. Cost verification and restrictive endorsement implement this limited benefit; they do not alone convert it into government's provision of doctrine. The supported secular alternatives and independent selection do material work, while the percentage of religious providers and replacement of former private subsidy do not alone decide the benefit's character. He accepts that a direct governmental purchase of devotional work would present the different Agostini prohibition and therefore does not rely only on neutral recipients or his broader historical view.

**Joins, alternatives and remedy.** He joins Kennedy/Scalia's program-specific permissible-relationship rationale at the stated level, with no need to adopt a broader historical rule or overrule Nyquist categorically. He refuses a categorical religious-provider exclusion or an argument that schools must remove all theology to receive any genuinely individual benefit. He also refuses an automatic constitutional obligation to fund religion in programs not before the Court. Affirmance is bounded by C, with actual statutory exceptions and enrollment protections intact. He can accept remedy limits if the Court remands while withholding its substantive ground. No supported threshold defect defeats A.

### Ginsburg

**Result and sufficient ground.** Reach A; vacate the categorical federal approval and dependent remedial direction on B and C. On the supported integrated-curriculum facts, neutral eligibility and the parent check do not establish that public payment of instructional cost avoids financing devotion. Current Agostini requires that question to be decided, not bypassed by recipient privacy.

**Individual anchors.** Her Rosenberger dissent join (June 29, 1995), Agostini substantive/stare-decisis dissent joins and separate Rule 60 dissent (June 23, 1997), and Bronx Household distinction between private attribution/instruction and the broader worship application (May 11, 1998) supply her public financing/access distinctions. The Rule 60 objection is absent here and supplies no vote. Her actual Sullivan concurrence (March 3, 1999) relies on the narrow payment ground without reaching private attribution, illustrating why an unnecessary recipient-status ruling does not replace the precise legal question.

**Adversarial test.** The most substantial affirmance combines Witters, Mueller and genuine educational alternatives. She accepts their preserved holdings and that private schools do not become governmental speakers. But the State's own cost payment remains reviewable, and actual devotional teaching within that paid work distinguishes this challenge from a bare recipient-identity objection. The school's retained autonomy does not show that the State buys no religious instructional function. The opt-out does not separate funds; neutral audits are not equivalent to secular-use controls. She rejects any invitation to infer all religious instruction or all provider expenses inseparable without support.

**Joins, alternatives and remedy.** She joins the confined funded-function rule and remand without requiring the Court to endorse her broader institutional-support view or old independent Lemon prongs. She refuses a general private-choice exemption, compulsory restructuring of religious schools or blanket termination of lawful secular payments. State severability and any further supported particular relief remain for ordinary resolution. The existing enrollment-status protection retains its lawful limited scope; no monetary or transfer order follows. She reaches the final federal review question without creating a federal taxpayer cause or assuming new claimant facts.

### Breyer

**Result and sufficient ground.** Reach A; vacate blanket federal approval and remand on B and C, joining only the concrete funded-function rule and limited remedy. Supported integration of devotional instruction into the costs reimbursed by the State, combined with no reliable segregation, prevents the parental-choice label from resolving the constitutional question. He does not adopt an institution-wide prohibition or hold that every individual religious-education benefit violates the Clause.

**Individual anchors.** He joined Souter's Rosenberger financing dissent (June 29, 1995). In Agostini (June 23, 1997) he joined Souter's Part II concerning precedent, not Part I's affirmative direct-aid rationale, and joined Ginsburg's procedural dissent. Those unjoined substantive propositions are not attributed to him. His Bronx Household Court opinion (May 11, 1998) protects genuinely private worship/access and makes this expenditure/access distinction especially important. His Sullivan concurrence joined by Souter and his actual Court joins (March 3, 1999) preserve precise state-action and entitlement questions without collapsing distinct public conduct into them.

**Adversarial test.** Authentic choice among supported public/private alternatives and Witters's religious-training benefit are his strongest reasons to affirm. They defeat any theory based only on a school's religious identity or location. They do not supply a sufficient account of this particular restricted reimbursement of integrated instructional costs. Rosenberger's actual printing relationship and Bronx Household's rental forum cannot be enlarged without confronting that function. He accepts actual program facts rather than a speculative inevitable diversion, and he leaves the scope of any demonstrably separable secular service or genuine private benefit to the appropriate existing-record analysis.

**Joins, alternatives and remedy.** He joins O'Connor's narrow application and remand, not Stevens/Souter/Ginsburg's broader institutional critique. An opinion must acknowledge the real parental choice and the preserved specific cases, explain the cost/instruction connection, and avoid commanding religious schools to change their private curriculum. He will not join an automatic all-provider ban, an assumption that missing documents prove the State loses, or a new proceeding merely because the governing test differs. The remedy in C is sufficient and no broader; actual state-law enrollment protection remains within lawful financing. The supported final federal posture warrants A, while new broader relief would require its own predicates.
