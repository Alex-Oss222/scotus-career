## Affected neutral alternatives

**Contextual validity and distinct subjects.** The defense of the factors may now point to an actual trial presentation differentiating 3(B)'s circumstances of vulnerability from 3(C)'s personal and family impact, rather than relying only on an abstract distinction proposed during appellate review. Together with the actual anti-counting instruction, the passages can support an argument that jurors were directed toward understandable, relevant but different selection subjects. The source supports consideration of that argument; it does not establish that every phrase in the factors was adequately defined, that all overlap was removed, or that the Constitution treats an advocate's explanation as an authoritative narrowing construction.

**Continuing objection to breadth or duplication.** Jones's contrary route remains supported by the wording of the charge and findings: personal characteristics may encompass age, stature and background, and the actual factor formulations did not make the prosecutor's suggested distinction legally binding. An instruction against counting factors mechanically addresses counting; it does not conclusively establish that repeated treatment of the same circumstances added no weight. Conversely, overlapping evidence does not by itself establish prohibited double counting. The new passages must be assessed for what guidance they actually furnished, alongside the charge, rather than disregarded because counsel spoke them or promoted into a judicial instruction because they support a narrower reading.

**Permissible impact evidence and relative-worth concerns.** The favorable personal descriptions may be argued to illustrate the victim and the loss to others within §3593(a)'s permitted subject matter and Payne's bounded relevance principle. The packet also preserves Jones's concern about an undefined factor permitting an improper comparison of worth. The extracted passages alone establish neither that jurors made a forbidden comparative-worth assessment nor that the entire argument contained no such appeal. The source contains no finding resolving either inference.

**No-error and cure remain separate.** If the factors are sustained on their actual wording and context, the relevant route is no error on the challenge reached. If one or both are held or assumed invalid, the prosecution's descriptions can inform analysis of the error's actual operation, but do not themselves cure the defect. A court cannot silently replace the delivered instruction with a narrower hypothetical one or infer that jurors applied the proposed narrower definition simply because it appeared in argument. The express special findings establish that the factors were found; they do not reveal their weight in the final selection.

For the cure branch, §3595 and the base packet's constitutional authorities continue to require the applicable showing by the Government. Distinct subject matter and an anti-counting instruction may bear on noncontribution; the existence of two valid statutory aggravators or lawful death eligibility alone remains insufficient. The fact that the prosecutor discussed both challenged factors also does not, by itself, prove harmful contribution. The limited excerpts furnish neither a count nor a comparative measure of emphasis and cannot establish that the factors were dominant, incidental, repeatedly invoked or ignored. Genuine authorized reweighing and harmless-error review remain different legal operations.
