## Validated lower-court procedure

The Ninth Circuit affirmed in part and reversed in part. Its disposition cannot be described as an undifferentiated affirmance of every fair-representation claim. The neutral packet's statements in sections A and C that it affirmed summary judgment on “the fair-representation claim” must be read as referring to the facial challenge to the clause's statutory membership wording, separately from the thirty-day statutory grace-period theory and the following two claims:

1. **Actual enforcement.** The Ninth Circuit reversed summary judgment on Marquez's claim that SAG's enforcement of the union-security clause breached the duty of fair representation. It found genuine issues of material fact remaining for resolution. One identified dispute was whether SAG told Marquez that she had to **join** the union or that she had to **pay** the union. This is a report of the appellate court's conclusion that the evidence conflicted, not a finding that either version was true. The existing proceedings below therefore include this enforcement claim and its unresolved facts. The published recitation cites 124 F.3d 1034, 1041 (1997).

2. **Notice of limited financial obligations.** The Ninth Circuit also reversed summary judgment on the claim that SAG breached the duty of fair representation by failing to notify Marquez of her right under *Communications Workers v. Beck* to pay only the lesser core fees associated with collective bargaining functions. The district court had not addressed this claim, and the Ninth Circuit remanded it for consideration. This procedural disposition does not establish that notice was inadequate or sufficient, or that Marquez prevails on the claim. The published recitation cites 124 F.3d 1034, 1042–1043 (1997).

These are separate components of the existing lower-court remand. Neither component is resolved by a conclusion about the facial permissibility of statutory membership wording. Neither is converted into the separate thirty-day statutory grace-period theory. Both remain unreviewed in the present merits framing. The packet's instruction to preserve the “distinct notice/enforcement remand” refers to these two identified components; it does not authorize a new or expanded remand or an additional granted question.
