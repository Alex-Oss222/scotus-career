**Counts and scope:** Franklyn was convicted by a jury of Count One, machinegun possession under section 922(o) and section 2, and Count Two, felon ammunition possession under section 922(g) and section 2. **Batson and counsel:** The challenged strikes removed three of four African-American panel members using three of the government's six peremptories. Counsel objected after voir dire was completed, the challenged jurors had been dismissed and the court reconvened following lunch. The district court separately found no purposeful discrimination after hearing explanations; the appellate court reviewed that ultimate finding for clear error. The opinion does not reproduce the individual strike explanations, so do not invent them or perform unsupported comparator analysis. The ineffective-assistance disposition rests on the alternative merits finding, not on the mere fact of untimeliness. Published review supports the alternative finding; a proposed departure requiring juror-specific reconstruction needs the actual voir dire record.

**Gonzalez sentencing:** The source distinguishes three decisions. First, section 2K2.1(a)(4)(B), rather than the recommended (a)(6), supplied level 20 based on attributed machinegun possession. The court found both aiding/abetting through matching ammunition and foreseeable possession in joint activity; foreseeability alone is not a new universal rule dispensing with jointly undertaken scope. Second, a three-level section 5K2.6 departure relied on recent discharge, supported by gunshot report, bystanders' directions and the jammed round; the district court also offered a high-density/high-crime-neighborhood rationale, which the appellate court expressly did not reach. Acceptance credit reduced level 23 to 20. Possession versus discharge supplied the lower court's answer to the double-counting objection. Third, category II increased to IV under section 4A1.3 based on inadequately represented history and recidivism. The two old juvenile felonies would support category III; the court treated category IV as a supported total-history assessment, not automatic points for the remaining old misdemeanor. The appellate court rejected a rigid category-by-category ritual where reasons were fully explained. Final sentence: 63 months, at the top of 51–63 months. The published footnote 6 records and rejects Gonzalez's objection to using evidence from Franklyn's trial; do not omit it.
