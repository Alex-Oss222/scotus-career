## Objective facts and operative statutory text

The State enacted a replacement congressional districting plan in response to the district court's decision and order. The new law revised Districts 5, 6, 9, 10, and 12. Under that plan, District 12 excluded Guilford County, included all of Rowan County, and had modified boundaries in Forsyth, Davidson, and Iredell Counties. The State conducted its 1998 congressional elections under the replacement plan with district-court approval. Those facts do not establish that the predecessor plan governed those elections.

Section 1.1 of 1998 North Carolina Session Laws chapter 2 follows, copied mechanically from the supplied exact-section source and checked against the full saved act:

```text
Section 1.1.  The plan adopted by this act is

effective for the elections for the years 1998 and 2000 unless the United

States Supreme Court reverses the decision holding unconstitutional G.S.

163-201(a) as it existed prior to the enactment of this act.
```

Section 2 makes the act effective when it becomes law. The act's ratification text is dated May 21, 1998. The supplied objective recitation establishes actual enactment and use; no separate effective-date assumption is needed for the limited posture assessment.

The express statutory condition concerns Supreme Court reversal of the decision holding unconstitutional G.S. 163-201(a) as it stood before this act. It does not say that every remand, every disagreement with the lower court's reasoning, or every order vacating a judgment necessarily satisfies that condition. The conditional election years are 1998 and 2000. Section 1.1 does not itself spell out a replacement election schedule, invalidate a completed election, identify an officeholder consequence, or expressly direct automatic reinstatement of the predecessor map.
