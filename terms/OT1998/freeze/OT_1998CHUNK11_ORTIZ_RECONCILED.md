## 3. Reconciled operative components

The six in components A–F are **O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg**. Their support is direct and complete at the stated level. Components distinguish construction, financial application, representation and remedy.

### A. Threshold and certification requirements

The six may deny this nonmerits representative device before deciding the particular standing questions generated by inclusion of absent or future claimants. That follows simulated Amchem's exact logically antecedent rule, consistently with Steel Co.; it is not permission to assume Article III power to award damages or bind a person on substantive merits. The objectors intervened, and no general appeal rule for nonintervening class members is needed. Numerosity, commonality, typicality and adequacy remain distinct. Rule 23(e) fairness cannot replace the applicable (a) and (b) requirements. Settlement-sensitive analysis does not import (b)(3) predominance into (b)(1)(B).

Stevens and Breyer accept actual Rule 23 compliance and settlement-sensitive appraisal; they do not dispute the Court's ability to reject certification on an antecedent nonmerits ground. Their opposition to the grounds of rejection is not agreement in an actual certification defect or in removal of the affirmance.

### B. Limited-fund construction

For contested mandatory settlement certification on a limited-fund rationale, proponents must establish a fund limited independently of their own negotiated ceiling and collective treatment consistent with the interests that justify binding absent claimants. Traditional limited-fund actions identify a definitely ascertained maximum fund inadequate to satisfy the aggregate claims, devote the whole qualifying fund to those claims, and equitably treat all claimants sharing the relevant theory of recovery. Those characteristics are **presumptively necessary**; a departure requires justification from its proponent. The Court need not settle every possible permissible departure to reject this arrangement.

The Rule's practical-impairment language is not ignored. Its traditional limited-resource application, illustrated by the preexisting authorities Dickinson, Guffanti, Morrison, Ross and Railroad Co. v. Orr discussed in the historical writing, explains why individual recovery can necessarily prejudice absent claimants. The Enabling Act's prohibition on abridging substantive rights and avoidance of serious jury and nonparty-binding questions support disciplined construction. Neither the textual risk of depletion nor the desirability of settlement establishes that any negotiated figure is the qualifying maximum.

This construction is broader than the independent handoff's cap-cannot-prove-itself proposition, but narrower than the categorical test that handoff declined. It does not demand a physically segregated preexisting res in every case, certainty of insolvency, surrender of every asset in every settlement, mechanical pro rata treatment regardless of valid priorities, or exclusive resort to bankruptcy. Whether mandatory treatment of unliquidated mass-tort claims can ever satisfy the Rule remains open. No probability-of-exhaustion threshold is selected.

### C. Independent financial application

The present showing fails to establish the qualifying limit independently of the bargain. The district court heard evidence, valued noninsurance assets and credited predictions of depletion. The flaw is not an absence of all asset findings. The disputed insurance had no aggregate policy ceiling, and the courts treated the negotiated Trilateral value as establishing its maximum available value without the necessary independent assurance. Insurance value discounted by actual coverage risk may be relevant; the six do not assume coverage victory, defeat or insurer insolvency.

The strongest response is that Fibreboard separately bargained with its insurers in the Trilateral negotiation and would remain exposed to tort claims if its proceeds ran out, giving it an incentive to maximize recovery. The six nevertheless regard that benchmark as insufficiently independent in the combined transaction: the backup arrangement was linked to the Global bargain, and common counsel represented inventory clients whose recoveries depended on resolution while negotiating for the class. Supported vigorous negotiation and later removal of a contingency do not retroactively establish an unconflicted valuation premise. The issue is the adequacy of the asserted maximum under the certification rule, not an inference of wrongdoing or a research-created default.

A reliable evaluation could establish a legally sufficient risk-discounted limited fund or could fail to do so. This judgment does not supply the missing dollar figure. It rejects certification on the present showing, without ordering insurer payments, an insurance merits judgment, or a compulsory new appraisal/hearing. If a lawful new certification is later sought, its actual factual and legal predicates must be established.

### D. Inclusiveness and adequate representation

The mandatory plan also fails the required equitable treatment and Rule 23(a)(4) protection. The class excludes substantial inventory claims still implicated in the settlement negotiations while counsel also represented those clients. This is not a rule requiring every previously satisfied claim to be resurrected or every prior settlement to be reopened. It is the present exclusion of relevant competing claims and interests from a negotiated mandatory resolution that defeats the asserted collective justification.

Independently, the currently ill and later-manifesting groups have materially different bargaining interests in immediate payments, conservation, caps and future protection. Pre-1959 and later exposure produce materially different insurance interests. Uniform nominal treatment resolves those tradeoffs; it does not eliminate them. Individualized later valuation and the possibility of jury proceedings against a capped trust do not restore claims or bargaining protections surrendered to create it.

For these material groups on this record the six require separate, independent representation in negotiating the allocation; independently represented homogeneous subclasses are the supported means identified here. The existing undivided negotiation and later guardian/fairness review do not provide that structural protection. This application is not a universal rule that every difference, future claim or class requires its own counsel. A materially different proposal must be evaluated on its actual protection, without advance approval or a judicially commanded redesign.

The existing findings of common objectives, competent counsel, vigorous bargaining, scarcity of alternative experienced counsel and settlement benefits have been confronted. They do not establish independent responsibility for opposing allocative interests. No fraud, collusion or ethical misconduct is adjudicated. These concrete intraclass conflicts independently defeat adequacy; counsel's inventory interests reinforce them and the fund-valuation concern. The majority's present inadequacy judgment is conclusive as to this representative structure, not a remand leaving its existing adequacy entirely undecided.

### E. Retained assets and reserved issues

Fibreboard's retention of its ordinary value while class claims are restricted raises a serious additional inconsistency with devotion of the qualifying fund. The whole-fund characteristic belongs in the construction, but **retention is not separately held dispositive here**. Whether transaction-cost savings can justify a credit, how close to insolvency a defendant must be brought, and whether retention alone would defeat an otherwise valid arrangement remain open. This preserves the distinction between a presumptive requirement and a decided application.

The six do not decide general notice sufficiency, a universal constitutional opt-out right, each absentee's injury or personal jurisdiction, state choice-of-law questions, individual tort liability, the entire collateral-review boundary, or the Bankruptcy Code's exclusive operation. An alternative unitary insurance-dispute certification theory cannot rescue this Global arrangement because its existing conflicts remain. A differently represented future proposal is not adjudicated.

### F. Reversal and confined remand

The six reverse the reviewed Fifth Circuit affirmance insofar as it sustains the Global mandatory certification and settlement approval, and remand for proceedings consistent with components A–E. Both the present fund demonstration/equitable treatment failure and the concrete inadequacy ground support the rejection; the latter is independently sufficient. This replaces the provisional decision to make only adequacy dispositive.

The Global arrangement cannot bind absent persons through the rejected certification. Its dependent releases and classwide restraints require corresponding treatment within the reviewed judgment's scope. No individual damages are adjudged; no replacement class, settlement, bankruptcy, contribution, distribution or special mandate date is ordered. A remand is not permission to reapprove this unchanged structure by balancing its overall benefits.

**Separate Trilateral/Rudd boundary:** The Global challenge does not vacate the distinct Rudd judgment approving the Trilateral insurance settlement. The supplied neutral facts already separate those proceedings; the historical opinion's note 8 confirms the historical comparison's same nonreview boundary. Nothing here independently reviews, enforces, rewrites or voids that approval or every contractual term. In particular, the described $2 billion backup arrangement does not become a Supreme Court payment command or a direct class entitlement. The actual legal consequences of the distinct agreement remain governed by its own valid proceedings and terms.

### G. Stevens and Breyer's opposing application and disposition

Stevens and Breyer would sustain the challenged limited-fund certification and representation determinations and leave the reviewed affirmance in place on those grounds. They oppose the six's reversal. They do **not** join the independent handoff's vacatur for renewed appellate certification review. Their support is for the actual findings and the flexible Rule application described below, not for dispensing with Rule 23.

For them the significant practical risk of exhaustion, coverage-litigation uncertainty and Fibreboard's limited noninsurance value establish the type of threatened impairment the Rule addresses. Present value may reflect an imminent adverse coverage outcome; perfect precision is neither realistically attainable nor required. They regard the separate Fibreboard-insurer Trilateral bargain as an adequate arm's-length benchmark because it does not release Fibreboard's tort liability and leaves Fibreboard an incentive to maximize insurance proceeds. This is a judgment about the evidence already taken, not about an absent historical document.

They accept the supported lower determinations concerning experienced counsel, phased bargaining, the later secured inventory payments, shared interest in preserving insurance, individually valued distributions, and the practicability of separate representation within the genuine negotiating window. They find no sufficient basis to overturn that circumstance-specific appraisal. They do not hold that common benefit always cures material conflicts or that no class ever needs subclasses. On retained value, they regard the substantial cost savings and continuing fund protection as satisfying the whole-fund purpose rather than demand literal surrender notwithstanding a better actual recovery. Their flexible application opposes components C–D and the use of B to require reversal; it does not join the six's presumptive-traditional framework as the governing rationale.

**Exact reservation:** Their opposition to reversal and preference to sustain certification must preserve the dissent's final qualification. They believe proponents would likely prevail on additional commonality/typicality, notice, Article III, Enabling Act, bankruptcy, jury and due-process objections, but do not definitively decide every additional issue the rejecting Court does not reach. This supplies two votes against reversal on the granted certification grounds; it supplies no conclusive majority disposition of reserved questions and no authorization to assume jurisdiction for affirmative substantive relief. If their alternative became the basis for a final binding Court disposition requiring one of those questions, that necessary issue would need its own supported resolution. The six's sufficient nonmerits rejection needs none of those additional determinations.
