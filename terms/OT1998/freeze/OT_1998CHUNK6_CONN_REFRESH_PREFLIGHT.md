## Lawful merits paths

The existing clean preflight's distinction among temporary investigative interference, exclusion from a profession, and a distinct constitutional injury remains operative. Collins does not itself decide this affirmative-interference claim, and Albright supplies no controlling combined rationale. Graham and Lanier require use of an actually applicable specific guarantee without categorically excluding every independent due-process injury. West Covina supplies a distinct procedural-due-process rule, not a holding on occupational liberty.

One supported path is to conclude that the asserted occupational guarantee does not cover this limited search-related interruption, where the published source reports no evidence of adverse effect on the identified occupational interests. That requires a legal account of the protected interest and of why the actual alleged interruption falls outside it. The addendum alone does not establish a universal duration threshold, a mandatory income-loss element, or a rule that temporary governmental interference can never implicate due process.

Another supported path is to examine whether deliberate obstruction of an identified professional function could independently implicate protected occupational liberty despite the absence of the listed lasting effects. Such a position must identify the right and its predicates under date-eligible law, address the assumed record and the absence-of-evidence recitations, and distinguish the lawyer's own claim from the client's rights. The addendum supplies no affirmative evidence of an additional injury and cannot be repaired by inventing lost clients, fees, reputation, qualifications, a prosecution disadvantage or unreported testimony.

The absence of demonstrated consequential loss is not automatically identical to absence of a constitutional deprivation. Nor does identification of a potential right automatically establish damages, causation, personal responsibility or entitlement to trial. No ruling on nominal damages or other unestablished remedial demands is added to the granted question. The separately pleaded Fourth Amendment claim remains outside the grant and is neither validated nor adjudicated through either occupational-liberty path.

## Lawful immunity path

The preserved qualified-immunity question can be resolved independently if the actual plaintiff-favorable conduct was not clearly prohibited on March 21, 1994. Elder requires consideration of relevant contemporaneous authority; Lanier's later explanation preserves the distinction between particularized legal clarity and a demand for a factual twin, but cannot itself supply earlier notice. The Ninth Circuit's common-sense characterization does not substitute for identifying what established law made apparent in this context. Conversely, lack of a precisely matching precedent is not by itself dispositive.

The addendum may bear on the factual fit between the claimed right and earlier vocation authorities. It does not itself prove that every reasonable official would regard the search or its timing as lawful. A present rejection of the merits theory and a conduct-time immunity ruling remain separate possible grounds with distinct scope. No absolute-immunity ground, mandatory merits-first sequence or finding of lawful warrant execution is introduced.
