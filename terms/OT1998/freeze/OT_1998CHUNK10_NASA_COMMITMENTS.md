Section7114(a)(2)(B) requires opportunity for the exclusive representative at an examination of a unit employee by a representative of the agency in connection with an investigation, upon reasonable belief of possible discipline and a request. NASA is an agency under §7103(a)(3). Section7112(b)(7)'s bargaining-unit exclusion for certain audit/investigation employees is distinct from an exclusion of their examinations of other unit employees. The agency and bargaining definitions are relevant context, not interchangeable texts. The IG Act establishes an independent and objective unit within the establishment, appointment/removal protections, reporting responsibilities, general supervision by the head or next-ranking officer, and protection against preventing or prohibiting the initiation, conduct or completion of investigation. Both enactments must retain operation.

**Coverage path C1.** The investigator acts within NASA on its employee's conduct, develops information usable for NASA discipline and here threatened dismissal for refusal to answer. Independence from management's control of investigative judgments does not make that examiner external to the statutory agency or exempt from independent statutory requirements. The Authority's representative construction is a permissible application of its labor-law responsibility after judicial examination of the IG Act; no deference to FLRA on the meaning of every other statute is assumed. The strongest objection is that representative describes a relationship with the labor-management principal, not every employee of the executive organization, and confidentiality/independence can be impaired by labor-law constraints. C1 answers with the actual examination's agency disciplinary function, the chapter's definition, and the limited assistive role of a representative. It does not equate every employee, contractor or outside investigator with an agency representative.

**Coverage path C2.** In the chapter's agreement-directed context the representative acts for management in the employment relationship. The independent Inspector General has statutory duties to expose management and report to Congress and prosecutorial officials; those duties resist identification with management merely because the office is organizationally within NASA. The strongest objection is that the statutory examination provision protects the unit employee from disciplinary vulnerability, uses any examination and never expressly excludes an OIG; §7112 excludes investigators from bargaining units rather than their interviews from coverage. C2 answers that any modifies examinations satisfying the representative predicate; the IG Act informs the predicate rather than repealing a conceded application. This path must accept that statutory rights can independently bind OIGs when their actual terms do so; independence is no universal immunity from law.

**Headquarters path H1.** Coverage does not establish unlimited respondeat-superior liability. The Authority separately relied on NASA's status as the agency, OIG's internal disciplinary function, its reports and general supervision, and Headquarters' responsibility to ensure the statutory protection. The order requires advice/direction to obey §7114 and associated compliance/notices, not Headquarters control of subjects, evidence, conclusions, investigative termination or negotiated enlargement of representational rights. General supervision can include an instruction to comply with the applicable federal statute while preserving the IG Act's protected functions. The strongest objection is that requiring Headquarters to enforce a contested procedural interpretation can itself control how OIG conducts an inquiry, and §7116 does not expressly establish derivative liability. H1 therefore turns on this limited statutory-compliance order and the established agency relationship, not the proposition that all supervisory omissions automatically violate §7116.

**Headquarters path H2.** Even if OIG must itself respect §7114, the IG Act may deny Headquarters the power whose nonexercise is made an unfair labor practice; §7116's agency language does not by itself establish a second actor's affirmative duty to command an investigation. The strongest objection is the express general-supervision provision and the distinction between an instruction to comply with law and interference with investigatory judgment. H2 regards that distinction as insufficient for a coercive order requiring the parent to direct OIG's procedural conduct, while preserving an enforceable direct OIG obligation if C1 governs. A narrower remand is possible if the Court identifies unsupported attribution or an overbroad order; it cannot be justified merely because NFFE or Haggar remanded different administrative disputes.

**Procedural path.** The lower court found extraordinary circumstances under §7123(c) because the Authority addressed Headquarters sua sponte and sought enforcement that day. This is a case-specific reason to review its statutory objection, not a general dispensation from administrative presentation. The lower account also establishes Headquarters was an original charged party with notice and an opportunity for reconsideration. No supplied source supports a new fact finding of denial of notice or an automatic void order. All eight reach the supported statutory questions on this posture; none uses an invented preservation default to avoid its commitment. A general reconsideration requirement remains unaffected.

## Scope of the right, relief and authority

The request and reasonable fear of discipline are undisputed. The Authority's finding that the observer-only limits unlawfully restricted representation was not appealed on its own terms; affirming coverage does not conduct a new merits review of every interview ground rule. Weingarten permits meaningful assistance while allowing the investigator to insist on the employee's own account, maintain orderly questioning and prevent obstruction. It does not require bargaining at the interview or adversary adjudication.

The record establishes that OIG had determined before the interview that no criminal action would be taken against this employee. The proposed holding decides this administrative-disciplinary setting, not every criminal examination, FBI investigation, outside-agency investigator, negotiated extension of rights or excluded national-security entity. Congress's specified exclusions and the President's actual exclusion authority retain their terms; no exception for this NASA examination is established.

Five direct non-Stone commitments support C1 and, independently, H1: Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Three support C2 and H2: O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas. No source supplies a majority interpretation of a hypothetical enlarged order. On the five-Justice path, affirmance leaves the stated compliance/notices/direction remedy effective; no damages, reinvestigation, new representation regulation, employee discipline determination or expanded management control is ordered. On the three-Justice path, reversing coverage defeats both orders; if C1 otherwise governs, their H2 position would remove the Headquarters component while preserving direct OIG relief.
