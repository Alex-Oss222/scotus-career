## Material comparisons

### AT&T: actual announcement status and actual consequences

All eight Associates joined Scalia's Part XII, which conclusively disposes of the general section 208 challenge as unripe rather than ordering an open-ended threshold inquiry. That completed rule controls this chronology; the earlier independent AT&T modeling handoff is not authority.

The operative distinction is the Court's determination that the announcement itself changes no substantive carrier duty, identifies no operative agreement conflict, revises no state determination, orders no present act and determines no carrier-specific liability. The absence of unconditional protection from future financial penalties is not itself withdrawal of an established protection. Section 208's actual complaint, response and investigation process and review of a covered concluding order permit the authority issue to be resolved in a concrete application. Finality and a predominantly legal disagreement do not alone establish ripeness, and NAACP and WRTL require no additional hypothetical inquiry when the instrument's threshold character is ascertainable.

Reno presents proceedings already instituted and preliminary findings of purposeful association-based selection, disparate treatment and present chill. Those findings do not establish final liability or automatic entitlement to stop proceedings, but they are not a general declaration about a possible future enforcement forum. Reno also presents an identified dispute about legal capability to receive the selection evidence; the proposed section 2347 route has statutory hearing and record conditions absent from a generic promise of later review. AT&T's adequate concrete review path does not establish that different route.

The frozen Reno commitments already reject ordinary defense costs, undifferentiated chill, mere statutory labels and an automatic remand. Ground A rests on an actual review barrier and a substantial present controversy; ground B requires present consideration of the expressly raised constitutional exclusion question instead of certifying a hypothetical later remedy. Neither depends on treating completed agency action as necessarily ripe. AT&T therefore requires an explicit distinction in any relevant Reno explanation, but changes no commitment or rationale scope. It supplies no new rule that every commenced proceeding is immediately reviewable.

### Abate: meaningful opportunity, with the stage and issue identified

Abate's actual-defense holding reinforces the shared Reno requirement to identify what the proposed procedure can lawfully decide and whether it can receive the material evidence. A subsequent hearing on different issues cannot be assumed to cure exclusion of a material issue already resolved elsewhere. In Reno, a statutory deportability defense and an unconstitutional-selection claim likewise must not be collapsed merely because success on the former could prevent removal.

The analogy has limits. Abate concerns a State's common-issue trial and the binding scope of adjudicated findings, not the construction of subsection (g), section 2347 or the constitutional timing of immigration review. It establishes neither ground A's statutory exception nor ground B's constitutional-remand formulation. Its concrete opportunity-to-defend affirmance is consistent with all eight Reno commitments; it does not require all material objections to be heard at the earliest possible stage.

### Your Home: each asserted route requires its own authority

Your Home rejects a section 1331 action whose standing and substantive basis derive from the Medicare reimbursement scheme and which seeks a procedural step toward payment after an available original appeal was not timely used. It separately rejects the APA as an independent jurisdictional grant and mandamus for want of a demonstrated clear duty. It does not treat exhaustion of the later refusal claim as exhaustion or timely use of the original amount appeal, nor does it infer jurisdiction from a potentially meritorious compensation complaint.

The frozen Reno analysis already preserves the same distinctions: subsection (f) is not a grant, the Government cannot create section 2347 power, Kolster does not supply unproved habeas predicates, and jurisdiction to assess a threshold question does not itself authorize the underlying selection merits. Ground B's limited exclusion inquiry particularly must retain that last boundary.

Your Home expressly presents no distinct colorable constitutional claim and reserves independently authorized constitutional channels without guaranteeing one. Reno's competing constructions concern such a substantial claim and an actual alleged inability to obtain its factual adjudication, not an effort to renew a missed appeal of the same statutory payment issue. No corresponding missed deadline or failed timely opportunity to adjudicate selection is established in Reno. The new decision neither validates ground A automatically nor forecloses it; the five supporting Associates must still defend their precise construction of subsection (g). It also does not relieve the three ground-B Associates of addressing the actual constitutional objection to exclusion. The frozen handoff already does both.

### Boring and the remaining decisions

Boring first identifies whether the assigned curricular act supplies the teacher's claimed personal expressive interest. Its holding is specific to institutional instructional production and expressly preserves independent constitutional prohibitions. Reno concerns private association and allegedly discriminatory selection, with disputed legal treatment of the actual conduct; it is not a claim to control government expression. Boring authorizes no categorical conclusion that this selection contention lacks any personal constitutional basis, and its merits resolution does not authorize deciding contested merits on assumed jurisdiction. The frozen Reno handoff already distinguishes substantiality from ultimate protection, retains the Government's conduct and immigration arguments, and adopts no universal alien/citizen equivalence.

Yoshina's already incorporated threshold distinction remains unchanged, including its need for a concrete surviving interest. It supplies no automatic live claim for Barakat or Sharif. NCAA adds a consistent distinction between a disputed statutory coverage element and Article III jurisdiction; subsection (g), by contrast, is an express review restriction requiring its own construction. Wright and Hughes reinforce careful separation of independently preserved claims and grounds at their particular statutory scope. None establishes a constitutional claim, changes an immigration channel or guarantees a new hearing, discovery, amendment or remedy. No material new premise follows from those analogies.
