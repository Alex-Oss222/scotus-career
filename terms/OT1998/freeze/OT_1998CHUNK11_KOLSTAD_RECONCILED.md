## Reconciled dispositions and material corrections

**Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer** would vacate the en banc punitive judgment because §1981a(b)(1) does not impose an additional independently egregious-conduct prerequisite. Their remedies differ: **Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer** would remand the punitive issue for trial; **O’Connor, Scalia and Kennedy** would remand for ordinary application of the mental-state and punitive-agency rules, including the limited good-faith-compliance rule they support. **Thomas** retains his position affirming punitive withholding under the additional aggravated-culpability requirement, while supporting the agency limitation if that statutory position is rejected.

Three corrections to the provisional handoff are necessary:

1. **Scalia:** Replace his provisional M3/affirmance position with M1 and vacatur/ordinary remand. The historical full Part II-A join directly contradicts the provisional prediction. No changed statute, Smith premise, Hazen Paper rule, question, record or remedy explains a different mental-state position for him. His own Hazen Paper join and the statutory text support the historical position; Hicks concerns inference rather than an extra punitive-conduct element, and his BMW dissent concerns constitutional amount review. The correction follows source-specific comparison, not a general presumption of historical replication.
2. **Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer:** Preserve refusal to join a new compliance limitation and their punitive-trial remedy, but replace provisional A3’s categorical merits rejection with **A3R**, the narrower refusal to adopt or make that new limitation dispositive in this case. Their historical writing emphasizes reach, the litigated company promotion decision, supported sufficiency and remand; it does not decide that effective compliance can never limit punitive imputation in another properly presented case. Their simulated prior positions likewise reserve broad defense questions. The separate §1981a(a)(3) argument remains a substantial objection to a new rule, not a newly changed statute or a source of a categorical holding they historically did not adopt.
3. **Compliance burden:** A2R below retains the limited substantive rule supported by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, but **does not adopt the provisional universal employer-preponderance allocation**. The historical opinion did not settle that allocation. Harrison/Ellerth’s plaintiff burden concerns actual-power attribution, and the three-Justice avoidable-loss proposal concerns a different remedial rule; neither decides this new punitive limitation’s burden. The statutory accommodation exception expressly places a demonstration on the covered entity, but its separate context cannot silently supply this rule. The absence of a definitive allocation is an express reservation, not a new plaintiff duty to disprove compliance or permission to presume effective measures.

The liability verdict and $52,718 backpay judgment remain intact for all eight. No punitive sum, §1981a compensatory award, cap determination, new instatement/fees ruling, constitutional amount holding, automatic evidence ruling, or finding of effective compliance follows. ## Exact reconciled propositions

These formulations supersede inconsistent provisional join descriptions only in this separate reconciliation; the independent file is unchanged.

**M1 — Statutory mental state.** Section 1981a(b)(1) requires malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights. Intentional sex discrimination and perceived risk that the conduct violates federal law are distinct inquiries. At minimum, the actor must discriminate in the face of such a perceived risk; mere negligence about legal duties is insufficient. Circumstantial evidence, including obviousness, concealment and knowledgeable implementation, may support the required inference. Egregious conduct can be evidence of that state; it is not a further indispensable, objectively aggravated act. The distinction remains meaningful where the relevant prohibition is unknown, the liability theory novel or poorly recognized, or the actor reasonably believes a statutory exception permits the decision. Those possibilities are not findings about this employer. No direct admission, personal insult, pattern or separate category of proof is required.

**M2 — Submission, award and amount are distinct.** The court assesses whether evidence and reasonable employee-favorable inferences permit the punitive predicates; it does not reweigh the existing discrimination verdict. The same evidence can support liability and punitive culpability without equating their legal elements. Neither liability nor rejected explanations automatically requires punitive submission in every case. If submitted, a punitive award remains discretionary. Caps and ordinary amount review do not substitute for eligibility. No new clear-and-convincing requirement is adopted.

**M3 — Thomas’s additional aggravated-culpability position.** Thomas would preserve an egregiousness/aggravation requirement reserving punitive liability for the worst intentional discrimination, based on the separately enacted punitive tier and traditional punitive character. He regards the supported record as insufficient under that construction and would affirm punitive withholding. This is his statutory disagreement with M1, not a denial of the liability verdict, a direct-evidence rule, a categorical pretext-case exclusion, or an inference from absent research. Scalia no longer joins M3.

**A1R — Punitive agency as a distinct question.** The plaintiff’s proof of ordinary Title VII liability does not automatically resolve punitive imputation. Meritor directs a statutory agency inquiry. Restatement (Second) of Agency §217C and Torts §909 supply four disjunctive reference routes: authorization of the act and manner; reckless employment of an unfit agent; managerial capacity plus scope; or ratification/approval by the principal or a managerial agent. Managerial status concerns actual entrusted authority and discretion, not title alone or an invariant top-management requirement. Delegated promotion work can occur within employment scope despite its discriminatory manner or breach of a prohibition. Allen’s final signature does not erase Wheat’s material role. No authorization, ratification, unfit-hiring, alter-ego or managerial-category finding is invented.

O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join A1R as the affirmative agency framework for this decision. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer accept the general distinction and the significance of managerial company action, but do **not** join a new dispositive agency section that sends this case back to reopen imputation or compliance. Their background agreement must not be counted as a complete join in that section. There is no eight-Justice final adoption of every new agency proposition or remand instruction merely because the reference Restatement is accurately quoted.

**A2R — Limited compliance protection for managerial vicarious punishment.** In the punitive context an employer is not vicariously liable for a managerial agent’s discriminatory employment decision when the decision is contrary to the employer’s actual good-faith efforts to comply with Title VII. O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support this modification of managerial-scope imputation. Its reasons are the special punitive concern about a personally innocent principal, Title VII’s preventive purpose and Meritor’s direction to adapt common-law principles to the statute. It is a proposed new punitive rule here, not an established general defense borrowed from historical Faragher or Ellerth.

A written policy alone, bare employee nonreporting, a good-faith label or a favorable legal rule does not establish actual compliance efforts. Effective enforcement and the relationship of the managerial decision to those efforts remain factual matters. No mandatory training checklist, universal reporting route, employee exhaustion rule or guaranteed program success is imposed. The rule concerns vicarious punishment; it does not erase ordinary liability or compensation or exonerate an employer’s independently established own authorization/ratification of the punitive wrong. No other Restatement route is collapsed into this managerial exception. Its application and any necessary procedural or burden question remain for ordinary resolution; this handoff settles no universal preponderance formula and creates no entitlement to new pleadings, discovery or factual reopening.

**A3R — The four Justices’ refusal to adopt the new limitation here.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer decline to join A2R or a new agency remand that withholds a punitive trial on the present record. The established company promotion decision, supported managerial influence and evidence permitting knowing/reckless violation supply the necessary basis to submit this punitive claim. The remedial question is broad enough to involve agency, but that does not require resolving every new limitation as an alternative route around the punitive trial. Their position does not declare all agency issues jurisdictionally inaccessible, find an unverified forfeiture, or adopt an absolute rule that good-faith efforts can never matter.

They retain substantial objections to the proposed rule: §217C’s managerial alternative already supplies a principal connection; prevention incentives do not themselves override enacted liability; and Congress separately wrote a precise good-faith exception for disability accommodation in §1981a(a)(3). Genuine compliance can bear on mental state, actual agency facts and the jury’s discretionary choice. Those arguments support their nonjoin and trial remedy here without converting their prior reserved questions into a categorical future prohibition.

**R1 — Common corrective disposition.** The seven M1 supporters vacate the en banc punitive judgment, preserve liability/backpay, and return the punitive component under the correct statutory standard. No punitive award is presently entered. Properly preserved independent matters retain their ordinary procedural treatment; their existence, preservation and factual success are not assumed.

**R2 — Trial-directed remand.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer find the cumulative employee-favorable evidence sufficient to permit a jury’s punitive assessment: supported sham-selection/preselection and concealment evidence, shifting explanations, biased managerial participation and evidence of awareness of equal-employment duties. They would remand for trial on punitive damages under M1–M2, without reopening the established liability verdict or ordering an award. Neither the final selector’s title nor isolated treatment of the evidence supplies an adverse factual finding. The expired decree is not a current violation or automatic admissible proof. This remedy does not announce a new categorical exemption from every genuinely preserved independent issue.

**R3 — Ordinary remand under agency limits.** O’Connor, Scalia and Kennedy do not decide that punitive submission must now follow. They would return the case for ordinary application of M1–M2, A1R and A2R, including the relevant actor’s culpability, managerial role and any properly available compliance issue. They find neither that the Association had an effective program nor that it has preserved or proved an independent defense. No compulsory supplementation or new compliance hearing is required. Thomas supports the legal agency limits if M1 is adopted over his disagreement, but his own judgment remains affirmance, not an R3 vacatur vote.

## Record, alternative paths and limits for assembly

The strongest M3 argument is statutory separation and the traditionally exceptional punitive remedy. The seven M1 supporters answer with the text’s focus on culpability toward federal rights, Smith’s disjunctive formulation and Hazen Paper’s explanation that intentional conduct need not be a knowing/reckless legal violation. This leaves real work for judicial sufficiency, punitive attribution and jury discretion. Thomas maintains the competing statutory construction, not a direct-evidence demand or a factual rejection of the existing verdict.

The strongest A2R argument is the difference between punishment and compensation and the statutory reasons for encouraging actual prevention. The strongest contrary argument is that the managerial Restatement route already attributes culpable action and Congress supplied a particular express good-faith exception elsewhere. Section 1981a(a)(3) covers specified disability-accommodation practices and all damages under that section, with consultation and accommodation predicates; it does not directly govern this sex-discrimination promotion claim. The A2R supporters distinguish direct damages immunity from vicarious punitive attribution. A3R supporters decline the new limitation here. Neither camp treats genuine compliance as proved by the mere policy text.

The safe respondent filing’s discussion of good-faith legal mistakes (pp. 34–35), petitioner’s treatment of biased supervisory influence (pp. 46–47), and the United States/EEOC discussion of attenuated punitive attribution (pp. 16–17) were checked. They show the competing theories and preserve the distinction between mistaken-law culpability and a new compliance-imputation limitation. They do not establish an actually implemented program or a particular new defense’s preservation. Historical judicial descriptions of what counsel conceded or failed to argue remain comparison evidence; no transcript-based waiver or default is imported into this frozen record. Removed OCR material is not absence from a filing.

The exact scope of further proceedings differs between R2 and R3 and must survive assembly. R2 is an affirmative trial position grounded in sufficiency, not merely permission to consider remand; R3 does not already adjudicate culpability, managerial status or good faith. Allen’s precise title, the effect of Wheat’s participation, the expired decree’s admissibility and any independent preserved matter must not be silently resolved beyond the frozen record. The court may apply ordinary procedure without mandatory fresh discovery, pleadings or a special compliance hearing. No material neutral correction is needed to support these bounded positions. A proposal to decide actual waiver, new policy facts, a universally allocated compliance burden, a final punitive amount or an enlarged claim requires supported further work before it can replace this handoff.

No final opinion form, author or assignment is selected. Any proposed final join must distinguish the statutory mental-state section, the new agency/compliance section, and the competing remand instructions; judgment agreement alone does not supply a join in the other sections.
