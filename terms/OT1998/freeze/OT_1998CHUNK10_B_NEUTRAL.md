## City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters Association — June18,1999; No.98-966

**Posture and scope.** Simulated post-grant merits review of150F.3d438(5thCir.1998), a final judgment after consolidated actions and an agreed remedies order. The Fifth Circuit affirmed summary judgment invalidating race- and sex-conscious out-of-rank promotions to driver-engineer, lieutenant and captain. The separately reversed deputy-chief disposition, overall plan, TitleVII and Texas statutory merits are outside this grant. The Texas constitutional ground is described below as conforming to federal law, rather than an established independent obstacle. The City seeks reversal of the three-rank promotion judgments; challengers seek affirmance. Argument May4 and decision June18 are simulation-assigned anchors.

**Record and competing evidentiary accounts.** Promotions below battalion chief normally follow rank-eligibility exam scores among qualified incumbent employees. The City used racial/ethnic and sex preferences to advance some candidates ahead of higher-scoring applicants. Review concerns classifications in those particular promotions, not a generalized employment-policy endorsement.

The1972 DOJ letter identifies TitleVII-inconsistent entry practices: no Black hires before1969, twelve Black/Mexican-American uniformed employees among1340, unvalidated entry exams, education requirements and height rules with specified disparate effects. It proposed nondiscriminatory selection/promotion and accelerated minority hiring. The1976 agreement's text addresses recruitment and hiring and effects of earlier discrimination that might have occurred; it contains race and sex commitments, but is not itself an adjudication of every disputed discrimination allegation. The letter's described findings concern race/ethnicity; a sex-discrimination finding must not be supplied by relabeling them. City demographics and the internal promotion pipeline support its argument that earlier entry exclusion persists in upper ranks. Challengers dispute the causal link to current promotions, actual feeder-pool comparisons and the temporal significance of that evidence. The lower court treated the evidence as insufficient; it did not find that numerical absence is irrelevant.

Actual neutral measures include validated exams, minority recruiting, removed seniority points, tutoring and a second paramedic-course opportunity. The City argues persistent disparities demonstrate inadequate progress; challengers answer that slower progress does not establish ineffectiveness. The City invokes qualified beneficiaries, score banding, a50-percent annual cap, five-year continuation periods and cessation when rank imbalance ends. Those features support fit but their significance remains disputed. No exact rank-specific evidentiary finding, equivalence of every candidate or neutral-measure failure is invented. Smith’s separate writing below concerns only the excluded deputy-chief component.

**Issue1 — justification and standards.** Adarand(June12,1995) supplies the common substantial-justification standard for municipal racial classifications, retaining Croson’s evidentiary demands. Engineering Contractors(September21,1998) supplies all-eight-Associate agreement on applying that standard, supported justification and fit. Seven Associates accepted that proven local exclusion can justify fitted repair without invariably requiring an earlier liability judgment; Scalia reserved the full affirmative remedial proposition. Societal inequality, good intentions and imbalance alone are insufficient, but law does not demand egregious resistance or a completed judicial discrimination judgment in every remedial case.

Sex classifications separately require actual important objectives and substantial relationship under Kelley(May22,1995), Klinger and Engineering Contractors; the government's showing is demanding. Do not transfer the racial-classification standard, an exhaustion requirement, or a statistical-only prerequisite to the sex branch. The exact Standards headings are “Race-conscious local public-contracting preferences” (Engineering Contractors effective September21,1998) and the actual-sex-classification component of “Program-specific prison comparisons and sex classifications” (Klinger effective December4,1995). Their program-specific limitations remain.

**Issue2 — actual fit and relief.** Engineering Contractors requires serious consideration of reasonably available responsive neutral measures and a supported explanation of inadequacy; it does not require exhausting every conceivable alternative. Dallas differs from a record with no serious alternatives inquiry because specified measures were actually used. That distinction does not establish success, exhaustion or fit. Qualification limits, score bands, duration, percentages and third-party burdens must connect to the supported remedial need in each rank. A remedial preference is not categorically forbidden, and promotion delay is neither automatically negligible nor identical to a layoff.

**Strongest supported paths and necessary concessions.** The City can argue an identified departmental entry barrier with a closed internal promotion ladder, surviving cohort effects, actual neutral efforts and bounded preferences; no confession or litigation resistance is essential. It must still connect past practices and each classification to the relevant promotion opportunity, justify the sex branch independently, and show fit. Challengers can argue that general demographics and old entry findings do not establish current rank-specific need, the agreement is no admission, responsive alternatives remain and numerical goals exceed proper feeder comparisons. They cannot replace independent constitutional review with a categorical egregious-discrimination prerequisite. Affirmance may rest on supported predicate or fit failures by classification; reversal requires sufficient legal and record support for the actual judgments requested; a limited vacatur/remand may address a wrong legal standard or genuinely unfinished constitutional assessment without finding a lawful program. Remand creates no entitlement to new evidence, no required promotion, and no decision on the deputy-chief or ungranted statutory claims.

**Justice-specific continuity.** In Engineering Contractors, O’Connor/Scalia/Kennedy/Thomas additionally found racial evidence inadequate after independent review; this four-Justice ground is noncontrolling. Stevens/Souter/Ginsburg/Breyer accepted racial tailoring failure while reserving the extra evidence ruling and sought independent reconsideration of the combined sex evidence. Their concerns about controls masking exclusion are relevant analytical considerations, not findings about Dallas. All eight accepted distinct municipal standards. Dallas's actual neutral efforts and internal pipeline must be tested on their own facts.

## Martin v. Hadix — June21,1999; No.98-262

**Posture correction.** The Sixth Circuit,143F.3d246, held the PLRA fee cap inapplicable to both pre- and post-April26,1996 services in these already-pending cases. The district court had drawn the service-date distinction; the appellate court preserved the pre-enactment exemption and reversed application to later work. The supplied SectionI incorrectly assigns the divided rule to the Sixth Circuit. Prison officials seek application of the cap to fees awarded after enactment; prisoners/counsel seek preservation of the pending-litigation exemption. The granted temporal inquiry covers pre- and post-enactment work; the case does not require deciding every PLRA fee issue.

**Record.** The Glover litigation began in1977 and Hadix in1980. Glover’s stipulated fee procedure dates to November1985; Hadix’s monitoring-fee order dates to November1987, following an April4,1985 consent decree. Requests include January1–June30,1996 services, straddling April26. The established rate was$150/hour;150percent of the identified$75 CJA rate produces a$112.50 ceiling. Those earlier orders establish arrangements, not an immutable contractual rate for all future work. Fee rights, reasonable hours and the applicable preexisting rates must be distinguished from whether the newly enacted ceiling applies. No new underlying prison violation, fee amount or performance date is found here.

**Entering-law snapshot.** Original42USC1997e(d), enacted April26,1996, limits defendant-paid §1988 fees and caps the hourly rate at150percent of the applicable §3006A rate. The exact Standards heading “Prisoner civil-rights fee awards under the original PLRA” has present operation effective April26,1996; it expressly reserves pending-case application. Section802's express prospective-relief transition does not automatically govern §803(d)'s fee amendment. The original conditional judgment-payment language must not be replaced with a later gloss.

Landgraf(April26,1994), under “Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct,” asks first for statutory temporal direction and then for new legal consequences of completed acts. Pending litigation or a procedural/collateral label alone does not answer the concrete question. Bradley remains valid in its actual litigation-fee setting. Rivers requires provision-specific direction; Hughes Aircraft(June16,1997) distinguishes expanded liability from a mere forum change. Lindh(June23,1997), with its distinct chapter comparison, supports ordinary construction without a prescribed verbal formula, not immunity for all pending proceedings. Its Standards rule is confined to AEDPA provisions and cannot be generalized into PLRA exemption.

**Strongest paths.** Officials can identify the post-enactment award or continued judicial enforcement as the regulated act, invoke Bradley’s fee setting, and distinguish compensation for litigation from substantive liability for the old prison conduct. Counsel can identify completed legal services and prior compensation consequences as the relevant act; the absence of the adjacent provision's express pending-case language supports a provision-specific inference. For later work, advance notice of the new ceiling supports prospective application, while counsel can argue that the already-established representation and continuing decree obligations make a case-filing distinction more faithful to Congress's arrangement. Each argument requires selecting the legally relevant activity, rather than invoking fairness or vesting abstractly.

Available resolutions include retaining the pending-case exemption, applying the ceiling to both service periods on a supported temporal construction, or dividing by service date. Each needs its own statutory and retrospective-effect reasoning; mixed disposition must accurately reverse the appellate exemption for later work if that is the selected rule. No automatic final fee calculation, new prevailing-party finding, alteration of decree merits or universal decision about all PLRA restrictions follows. Respondents’ constitutional objections were not reached below; their avoidance arguments do not establish invalidity, and rejecting a temporal argument does not decide every constitutional application.

**Justice-specific continuity.** Stevens/O’Connor/Souter/Ginsburg joined Landgraf’s organizing framework. Scalia/Kennedy/Thomas instead identify the regulated activity and require a clear textual statement or necessary implication for application to earlier activity; their published concurrence expressly does not overrule Bradley or decide every fee statute's relevant event. Lindh's Ginsburg opinion was joined by Stevens/O’Connor/Souter/Breyer; Scalia/Kennedy/Thomas viewed its relevant amendment as regulating current judicial authority. Those differences must be applied to the actual fee provision. Breyer's later membership supplies no retroactive Landgraf join. In pre-divergence Kaiser(494US827), O’Connor’s text-based temporal opinion was joined by Stevens, Scalia and Kennedy; Scalia’s concurrence expressly allowed the possibility that a costs/fees provision in unfinished litigation might not be retrospective. ## Jefferson County v. Acker — June21,1999; No.98-10

**Posture and correction.** Review of137F.3d1314(11thCir.1998)(en banc), sustaining the federal forum and holding the occupational levy invalid as applied to the judges. Jefferson County filed collection suits in state court against federal judges William Acker and U.W.Clemon; they removed and defended under federal law. County's primary request contests the federal forum and seeks remand; if jurisdiction exists it seeks rejection of the immunity judgment and collection under the ordinance. Supplied SectionI’s unqualified “affirm removal” requested relief and categorical Tax Injunction Act nonbar are not adopted.

**Threshold issues.** Federal-officer removal under28USC1442(a)(3) requires the applicable office/duty connection and a colorable federal defense; ultimate defense success is not a jurisdictional prerequisite. Mesa and Willingham support that separation. The judges' theory must connect taxation of judicial compensation to performance of official duties; routine personal liability of an officeholder is not automatically an official act. The County contests the federal forum and invokes28USC1341. The United States amicus supports the County but takes a distinct path: §1341 does not bar this defensive collection action, while §1442(a)(3) lacks an official-act nexus because personal tax liability is not an act under color of office merely because its measure is federal pay. It supports §111 tax applicability on the merits. That separate nexus argument must be answered independently of §1341 and colorability. The event-effective §1442(a)(3) does not contain the later “or relating to” expansion.

The Tax Injunction Act question is live: does adjudication of a tax collector’s removed collection action constitute the prohibited federal restraint of assessment, levy or collection, or does the action remain within Congress's distinct protective federal-officer forum? Federal defenses, an affirmative taxpayer injunction and a declaration of nonliability are not simply interchangeable. Arkansas v.Farm Credit Services(June2,1997), exact Standards heading “Private federal instrumentalities and the state-tax forum bar,” separates substantive tax immunity from federal-forum entitlement and preserves questions concerning other federal agencies and special jurisdictional statutes. Its holding about private borrower-owned associations and conceded state remedies does not decide these federal judicial officers' removal. The existence and adequacy of a state remedy cannot be supplied solely from the label of the tax dispute.

**Merits issues if reached.** Ordinance1120, effective January1,1988, imposes one-half of one percent (.005) of compensation attributable to work within the County, with apportionment for work elsewhere; it exempts persons paying specified state privilege/licence/occupational taxes and includes federal/state/local officeholders. It declares working without payment unlawful. No actual county restriction preventing a judge from sitting is established. The stipulated facts and cross-motions preserve legal characterization; they do not establish that every exemption is nondiscriminatory. Public Salary Tax Act,4USC111, consents to state taxation of compensation for federal personal services only without discrimination because of its source. The judges contend the ordinance licenses or burdens performance of federal judicial functions and invokes the Supremacy Clause; County argues the levy operates as compensation taxation despite its occupational label. Graves and Davis require the substantive incidence/operation inquiry; an occupational title neither automatically defeats nor creates consent. The exemption structure also generates a discrimination defense that cannot be erased by calling the tax “nondiscriminatory” at the outset. The district court also relied on the Compensation Clause; the en banc merits disposition did not reach that separate ground. It and the disputed exemption comparison must retain their actual lower-court status. The County invokes Buck Act §§106–110; the judges distinguish federal-area taxation from consent to tax the performance of federal functions.

Blaze(March2,1999) rejects federal-contractor immunity based on indirect financial burden and distinguishes legal incidence from economic effect. It preserves direct federal taxes, actual instrumentalities and discriminatory taxation; it does not equate federal judges with independent construction contractors. The Constitution's ArticleIII compensation protection must not be collapsed into intergovernmental tax immunity.

**Strongest paths and limits.** County can seek a §1341 forum dismissal/remand before the merits; it must account for the collector-initiated posture and §1442’s special purpose. Judges can rely on a colorable official-duty immunity defense for removal regardless of final merits success; they must establish the statutory nexus and answer the tax-administration objection. On the merits, County can emphasize compensation measure and nondiscriminatory operation under §111; judges can emphasize actual occupational conditions, exempt comparator classes, source discrimination and any preserved Compensation Clause defense. A forum ruling must not decide tax validity without jurisdiction. A merits rejection of the particular immunity ground need not establish ultimate tax liability if independent defenses remain undecided. Full affirmance requires a supported barrier to the actual tax; reversal/remand can leave the proper unresolved defenses for lower proceedings. No tax amount, coercive restriction on judicial duties or broad categorical judicial-tax exemption is invented.

**Justice-specific continuity.** Mesa is O’Connor's pre-divergence unanimous federal-defense decision. Kennedy's Davis opinion, joined by O’Connor and Scalia, scrutinizes federal-source discrimination; Stevens’s dissent emphasizes comparison classes and materially different tax arrangements. Farm Credit's unanimous institutional-identity/adequacy rules and eight-Associate declaratory-relief application require genuine forum analysis; they do not prescribe the answer here. Blaze is unanimous only within its independent-contractor setting.

## Sutton v. United Air Lines — June22,1999; No.97-1943

**Posture and requested relief correction.** TenthCircuit130F.3d893 affirmed Rule12(b)(6) dismissal. The twin pilots seek reversal and remand; United seeks affirmance. The supplied “Relief Requested: Affirm” is the respondent's position, not the petitioners'. In this pleading posture factual allegations receive the appropriate favorable treatment; no safety finding is made.

**Record.** The applicants allege severe myopia: uncorrected right-eye vision20/200 or worse and left-eye20/400 or worse, corrected vision20/20, first-class FAA medical certificates and commercial experience. United uses a20/100 uncorrected-acuity hiring cutoff. The complaint alleges disability substantially limiting seeing and that United regarded applicants as substantially limited in working. Below, the court assumed exclusion from all United pilot positions for the appeal, despite United’s asserted narrower position; other pilot employment and the meaning of a class of jobs remain important. FAA qualification does not itself prove that every higher employer standard is unlawful, while an employer safety assertion does not establish actual risk.

**Issue1 — original ADA coverage and mitigating measures.** Original42USC12102(2) separately identifies actual impairment substantially limiting an individual's major life activity, a record of such impairment, and regarded-as impairment. Sections12111(8),12112 and applicable defenses separate coverage from qualification and unlawful discrimination. Bragdon(June25,1998) requires individual limitation and reserves the general mitigating-measures question. Its exact Standards headings, “Actual disability and substantial limitation of reproduction” and “Objective assessment of an ADA direct threat,” both effective June25,1998, retain their activity and TitleIII settings. Seeing is the pleaded activity here; reproduction-specific disagreements cannot be transferred without analysis.

Petitioners argue that impairment and substantial limitation should be assessed without corrective devices, supported by EEOC Appendix guidance, Rehabilitation Act continuity, legislative explanations and the statute's separate accommodation/qualification framework. Otherwise effective self-management can remove protection against impairment-based exclusion. United emphasizes the individualized present substantial-limitation text and actual normal corrected functioning; uncorrected hypothetical inability is distinct from the person's functioning. It does not press the separate contention that myopia cannot be an impairment. A corrected-state inquiry must consider any actual remaining limitations or burdens and cannot be converted into a universal rule that every treatment eliminates coverage. An uncorrected-state inquiry does not establish qualification, liability or damages.

**Issue2 — authority and persuasive weight of agency material.** The regulation’s definition and appended interpretive guidance must be distinguished. Petitioners invoke delegated TitleI authority, notice/comment handling and consistent administrative interpretation; United disputes authority to control the general definition and says guidance contradicts the statute. Chevron and current Haggar(April21,1999) require actual delegation, statutory boundaries and genuine ambiguity before deference; notice/comment and an agency label alone do not supply authority or validity. Haggar's nine-Justice framework remains governing, while its tariff-rule application is not an ADA answer. Guidance may have persuasive force even when not controlling. Do not assume a post-event deference doctrine or silently dismiss the parties’ agency argument.

**Issue3 — regarded-as working and remedial scope.**29CFR1630.2(j),(l) distinguish exclusion from a class or broad range of jobs from a single specialized job. The parties dispute whether exclusion from United’s global-airline-pilot positions shows the broader perception. Applicants point to common training and the exclusion's breadth; United points to other pilot jobs and the specialized position. Perception of an impairment is not automatically perception of substantial limitation. At pleading stage the legal adequacy of alleged scope must be separated from new evidentiary conclusions. Petitioners focus on regarded-as working; no new regarded-as-seeing theory or amended complaint is invented.

**Strongest paths and limits.** Affirmance can rest on a supported corrected-state interpretation and an independently insufficient regarded-as theory; it must answer statutory/guidance and pleading arguments. Reversal/vacatur may follow an uncorrected-state rule, a narrower conclusion that the pleaded correction burdens need examination, or a properly supported regarded-as interpretation. Each route must state which actual theory survives and why; it cannot decide qualification or safety simply from disability or FAA certification. A remand can leave justified business requirements, direct threat, accommodation, causation and relief to ordinary proceedings without ordering hiring or a trial on unsupported new facts.

Ginsburg’s concurrence identifies broader life effects without replacing that sequence. Stevens/Breyer favored the prior lower risk disposition but joined a bounded remand.
