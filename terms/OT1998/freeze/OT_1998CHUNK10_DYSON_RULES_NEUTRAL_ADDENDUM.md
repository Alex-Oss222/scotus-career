## Response requirements and permissions

**Rule 15.1.** A respondent may file a brief in opposition in any case. Such a brief is not mandatory except in a capital case or when the Court orders one. Dyson's supplied drug-prosecution matter is noncapital. The supplied dated text contains no categorical requirement that the Court request or receive an opposition brief before every grant or summary merits disposition. The permission to order a response does not establish that such an order already occurred or that the Court must choose that course here.

**Rule 15.2.** An opposition brief should identify perceived factual or legal misstatements bearing on the questions properly before the Court after a grant. Counsel has the stated obligation to raise those matters there, rather than later. A nonjurisdictional objection to consideration of a question presented based on proceedings below may be deemed waived if not brought to the Court's attention in the opposition. That is a qualified, issue-specific rule, not automatic merits forfeiture from an operator's inability to locate a response. Jurisdictional objections are expressly distinguished.

**Rules 15.3 and 15.4.** Any opposition is due within 30 days after the case is placed on the docket, subject to an authorized extension. Rule 15.3 also requires service under Rule 29. A respondent may not file a separate motion to dismiss the certiorari petition; objections to certiorari jurisdiction belong in the opposition. No actual docketing date, extension, service event or deadline calculation is established by these rules alone. No unexamined service detail from Rule 29 is supplied here.

## Distribution and further papers

**Rule 15.5.** The Clerk distributes the petition upon receipt of an express waiver of the right to oppose, or, if neither waiver nor opposition is filed, upon expiration of the allowed filing time. If an opposition is timely filed, distribution of the petition, opposition and any reply occurs no less than 10 days after the opposition is filed. The period in this dated text is 10 days. The alternative triggers are distinct; an absent research copy establishes none of them.

**Rule 15.6.** A petitioner may reply to new points in an opposition. Distribution and consideration are not deferred pending receipt of that reply. This permission does not establish that Maryland filed a reply or that any reply deadline elapsed.

**Rule 15.7.** A docketed cross-petition defers distribution of both petitions until the cross-petition is due for distribution. No cross-petition is established in the supplied Dyson packet.

**Rule 15.8.** While a petition remains pending, a party may file a supplemental brief limited to new cases, new legislation or other intervening matter unavailable at its previous filing, with the stated form and service requirements. This creates a permissible vehicle for genuinely new matter; it establishes no actual supplemental filing or general reopening of the petition's contents.

## Disposition and supported procedural paths

**Rule 16.1.** After considering the documents distributed under Rule 15, the Court enters an appropriate order. That order may be a summary disposition on the merits. Thus the dated rules permit merits disposition at the petition stage without a mandatory intervening plenary briefing-and-argument cycle. Summary treatment remains permission, not a command or a determination that every jurisdictional, record or substantive condition is satisfied.

**Rule 16.2.** Upon a grant the Clerk prepares, signs and enters the order and notifies counsel and the reviewed court; the ordinary granted case is scheduled for briefing and oral argument. If the record has not been filed, the Clerk requests certification and transmission. A formal writ issues only if specially directed. These provisions preserve the plenary route and its ordinary steps; read with Rule 16.1, they do not extinguish the express summary-merits option. No completed grant, notice, record request, transmission or argument is inferred here.

**Rule 16.3.** A denial likewise requires the Clerk's entered order and notification. A rehearing petition does not itself suspend that denial; suspension requires an order of the Court or a Justice. No denial or rehearing event is established in this matter by that rule.

The supported paths therefore remain: consider an appropriate summary merits disposition on properly distributed documents and the supported jurisdictional/merits record; deny discretionary review; require a response before deciding whether or how to proceed; or grant for ordinary plenary proceedings. A response request becomes mandatory for the respondent if the Court actually orders it. None of these alternatives supplies an already-entered outcome or a preferred Justice position.
