The statutory routes are distinct. Section 301 governs retention of qualified-party primary status, together with municipal caucus, convention and certification conditions. ## Cleveland — May 24

The actual Fifth Circuit rule is more specific than automatic estoppel. The full opinion at 120 F.3d 513 expressly rejects a per se bar, creates a rebuttable presumption of judicial estoppel from application for or receipt of SSDI, and permits credible admissible evidence showing compatibility in the particular circumstances. It recognizes listed impairments, the absence of a reasonable-accommodation inquiry at the SSA and trial-work provisions, but describes compatible cases as limited and highly unusual. It concludes Cleveland's repeated sworn statements did not create a genuine issue rebutting the presumption. The question therefore includes the validity and application of that presumption, not merely a universally conclusive bar the lower court did not adopt.

The lower opinion supplies the actual sequence. Cleveland suffered a January 1994 stroke, applied for benefits, returned to work in April, requested accommodations and was terminated in July. She asserts that she notified the SSA and withdrew the application when she returned; the employer concedes notification of return but disputes withdrawal or abandonment of total-disability representations. Later reconsideration papers again asserted disability; the September 1995 administrative award treated disability as continuous from January 1994. Cleveland asserts worsening after termination and ability to work with accommodation during the intervening employment. These are the record's positions, not new Supreme Court factual findings. The district court dismissed the Texas claim without prejudice; review of the federal rule does not revive or decide it automatically.

The ADA's qualified-individual inquiry asks whether the person can perform the position's essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation. SSDI asks about substantial gainful activity under its distinct impairment, duration and vocational provisions. Statutory differences may explain a legal conclusion of disability without resolving every contradictory statement of historical fact. Supported paths include retaining the lower presumption, rejecting the presumption while requiring an adequate explanation under ordinary summary-judgment rules, or resolving a genuinely preserved narrower evidentiary question. None is current Court law merely because the original packet's Lawful Paths paragraph states a preferred rule. There is no adjudicated finding here that Cleveland was qualified, an accommodation reasonable, the employer liable or a trial inevitable.
