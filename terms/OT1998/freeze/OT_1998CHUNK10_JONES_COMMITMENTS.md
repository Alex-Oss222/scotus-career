### Common reach and record limits

The reviewed issues concern sentencing. No Justice here reopens guilt, all eighteen appellate issues, every statutory aggravator, the whole FDPA, or a general capital-elements rule reserved by the March Jones decision. No jury acquittal of death or actual deadlock is established; the jury returned a unanimous death recommendation. The mitigation counts record individual factor findings, not votes for life, proof of deadlock or a quantified moral balance.

The district court required all jurors to agree on aggravation, allowed each to consider individual mitigation, told them death was never required, prohibited surrender of an honest belief just to agree, and said weighing was not mechanical counting. It also identified a legally unavailable lesser-sentence option, specifically directed C or D when the jury was not unanimous for death, made C explicitly unanimous for life, and gave D only a lesser-sentence recommendation with a foreperson signature. The earlier general unanimity language applied to all recommendations; that text must be weighed against the specific later routing. Foreperson-only signatures also accompanied unanimous findings, so format alone proves no contrary understanding. Neither the death form, juror poll, nondiscrimination certification nor absence of a question settles the claimed misunderstanding.

The two challenged nonstatutory factors were expressly found. The jury rejected future dangerousness, grave risk to another and substantial planning; it found kidnapping-death and heinous/cruel/depraved conduct involving torture or serious physical abuse. One or more jurors found ten listed mitigators, plus the separate former-wife entry. No finding establishes that the two challenged factors had no influence. Conversely, their affirmative findings do not quantify their contribution.

All eight would exclude the proffered internal juror accounts as proof of misunderstanding or compromise under Rule 606(b). Section 3593(c)'s relaxed receipt of sentencing information does not silently repeal the distinct post-verdict rule. No external-information or outside-influence exception is established, and no later exception is imported. That exclusion leaves objective whole-charge review available; it does not prove the charge was understood correctly.

## Shared alternative paths, with their different consequences

**D1 — Nondeath after genuine deadlock:** Section 3594's unqualified “Otherwise, the court shall impose any lesser sentence that is authorized by law” governs absence of a unanimous death/life recommendation. Because this offense allows only death or life, a completed sentencing proceeding genuinely ending without a unanimous death recommendation leads to lawful life without release. Section 3593(e) governs a jury's recommendation, not whether the judge's otherwise duty can operate. Section 3593(b)'s good-cause provision allows replacement of a guilt jury unable properly to conduct sentencing and reconsideration after an imposed sentence; it need not permit evading the otherwise direction after completed deadlock. The strongest response is that no unanimous recommendation means no verdict and good-cause discharge permits a second sentencing jury under the ordinary manifest-necessity model. The absence of an actual deadlock permits a Justice to leave that disputed statutory question open rather than adopt either outcome.

**D2 — No mandatory general deadlock instruction:** Even assuming D1's eventual consequence, an otherwise accurate capital charge can direct jurors to decide punishment individually and conscientiously without advertising the procedural consequence of failing to reach any verdict. Nothing in §§3593–3594 expressly prescribes that explanation. Shannon is an analogy about allocation, not a controlling capital instruction rule; Simmons protects accurate information responsive to the State's future-danger case, not a general invitation to stop deliberating. The strongest response is that a sentencing jury differs from Shannon's guilt jury and must not be misled about the lawful nondeath alternative. D2 therefore cannot excuse an affirmative or reasonably likely misleading instruction, and does not itself answer the C/D problem.

**I1 — Objective charge requires correction:** Read together, routing nonunanimity to C/D, making C expressly unanimous for life and describing D as a lesser sentence can reasonably imply that a juror refusing death risks a sentence below life unless the jury unanimously agrees on life. A capital sentencing choice cannot rest on that legally false premise. General unanimity, individual conscience, optional death and an instruction not to consider the judge's eventual sentence are substantial counterweights, but may fail to correct the specific apparent dilemma. This is a contextual Boyde/Simmons-informed determination, not a claim that every omitted deadlock instruction violates the Constitution or that the returned death verdict proves compromise.

**I2 — No reasonable likelihood of the combined inference:** The whole charge expressly requires unanimous recommendations, identifies life without release, makes death optional and protects individual judgment; the C/D directions can be read as identifying available verdict forms after a decision against death, not compelling a divided jury to recommend a sentence nobody favors. A juror-only signature difference cannot alone negate the general unanimity instruction. The strongest objection is the specific direction to choose C or D precisely upon nonunanimity, not merely an omission of the word in D. I2 must answer that direction directly, not rely on a presumption as an irrebuttable cure. The isolated insertion of an unavailable lesser option remains statutory error even for a Justice rejecting I1's alleged constitutional consequence.

**P1 — Preserved core, narrower forfeitures:** The written instruction and repeated oral requests specifically sought explanation of what nonunanimity means and linked it to the sentencing alternatives, alerting the court to that core before deliberations. Counsel's own lesser-sentence requests seriously undermine a separate complaint that merely including that option was error, but do not necessarily abandon the core request for an accurate explanation. The alternative P2 distinguishes a preserved refusal-of-request claim from an unpreserved constitutional attack on the instructions actually delivered and on signature formats. Post-verdict motions and appellate consideration do not automatically satisfy Rule 30. Section 3595 does not eliminate its express preservation provision. Neither view turns the entire record into intentional waiver under Olano.

**F1 — Factors valid in context:** Factor 3(B)'s age, stature and unfamiliarity identify victim vulnerability/circumstances; 3(C)'s personal characteristics and impact on her family identify the loss caused by the crime. Tuilaepa permits understandable relevant selection subjects without exclusive labels or mechanical weights, and §3593(a)/Payne allow victim-impact information. Overlap does not alone show that jurors counted the same weight twice, particularly under the anti-counting instruction. The strongest objection is that the actual charge did not use the limiting term vulnerability, left background/personal characteristics undefined and directed separate aggravating findings. Permissible evidence does not automatically validate every factor formulation; forbidden relative-worth or race-based aggravation remains excluded.

**F2 — Assume invalidity and require a genuine cure:** On the Fifth Circuit's determination, these factors must be treated as errors for the cure inquiry. Its statement that reweighing and harmlessness are interchangeable, that the statute fixes no standard, and that remaining statutory aggravators support death cannot replace §3595(c)(2)'s express Government burden. Its reference to prosecutorial emphasis is relevant but does not itself demonstrate noncontribution. Stringer/Sochor/Espinosa distinguish valid eligibility from the actual selection balance, and Neder supplies a compatible warning against hypothetical-verdict substitution within its own scope. A federal appellate court's power to make a fresh sentencing choice cannot be inferred from a State's authority in Clemons. Section 3595 authorizes harmlessness review; if the required showing fails, it directs remand for §3593 reconsideration or nondeath sentencing. It does not authorize replacing that failed showing with appellate preference for death.

These are alternative grounds, not cumulative votes. No-error F1 does not join a harmlessness opinion assuming error. F2 permits a limited remand without declaring every victim-impact factor unconstitutional. None of the commitments invents a full penalty transcript, a number of closing references, an unreported curative instruction, or an evidentiary finding from an internal juror account.
