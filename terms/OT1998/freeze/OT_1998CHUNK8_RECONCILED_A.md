## Florida v. White — No. 98-223

### Reconciled rule and result

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer support reversal and remand. They join the rule that officers with probable cause that a readily movable automobile itself is forfeitable may seize it from a public place without a warrant or an additional case-specific emergency. The rule rests on the automobile's mobility and the public-place seizure, not on a legislature's choice to call property contraband. Accepted probable cause concerns the vehicle's earlier facilitating use; no present probable cause of drugs inside is invented. The later inventory cannot supply retroactive authority to seize.

Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm the reviewed seizure-based judgment. They join the proposed rule requiring prior judicial warrant process for this planned forfeiture seizure based on past use where no current evidentiary search object or independently justified need to dispense with process is established. Their position protects the possessory interest as well as privacy; it does not restore an additional emergency requirement to Labron's evidentiary vehicle-search rule or extend Good's civil real-property hearing rule automatically to every car.
