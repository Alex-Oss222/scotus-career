## Discrete components and common limits

The presented question is the statute's specific-intent requirement and associated due process. All eight Associates reach that question through section 1254(1). No basis for nonparticipation is established. All distinguish (1) actual present purpose to cause death or serious injury if resistance makes that necessary to take the vehicle; (2) a mere frightening threat or willingness to reconsider later; and (3) recklessness or foreseeable danger. No commitment treats those as interchangeable. The actual charge requires the first and Holloway's own shared intent beyond reasonable doubt, separately on each count.

The likelihood-of-resistance argument is considered as a statutory rebuttal. The record does not establish a distinct probability instruction requested below. No Justice infers its absence or forfeiture merely from an unrecovered request. A rejection of the claimed extra statutory element does not decide every possible separate instructional error. Unrelated ineffective-assistance and consecutive-sentence issues are not independently reopened. Carjacking counts 7, 9 and 11 and dependent firearm counts 8, 10 and 12 must be separated from chop-shop counts 1 and 2.

## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Actual conditional purpose satisfies the element.** Intent to cause the prohibited harm can exist now even when its execution depends on resistance and the actor hopes that resistance will not occur. The condition here seeks compliance with an unlawful vehicle demand; it does not negate the prohibited harm. Congress's intent amendment remains consequential because bluffing, intimidation alone, mere foresight and intent to inflict only minor injury fail. This construction affirms the actual conditional-purpose instruction, not the lower court's drafting-error speculation. The strongest competing argument is that the completed taking occurs with an overriding object of obtaining a vehicle and no wish that injury occur; federal criminal terms cannot be enlarged by model-code or state-law policy. The response rests on the ordinary and traditional meaning of purpose as applied to a presently settled conditional plan, with the actual harm element preserved.

**Only intent that harm occur suffices.** The contrary reading treats the specific intent to cause death or serious injury as a distinct object accompanying the taking, rather than an intention to do so only if a later condition materializes. The amendment narrowed the federal crime, and state robbery remains available. The strongest counterargument is that this adds an unconditionality requirement absent from the text and excludes a genuine resolved purpose merely because the victim's compliance prevents its execution. The contrary position must answer that ordinary-meaning point; it cannot label every conditional plan reckless or assume the amendment was a mistake. Lenity, on this view, independently favors the more confined construction only after the competing meanings remain unresolved.

**Probability/belief alternative.** A present conditional purpose as to the prohibited result is different from knowledge of an independently specified attendant circumstance. The statute does not separately require proof that victim resistance is objectively probable or that the defendant hopes, believes or knows resistance will occur. The probability of resistance can bear evidentially on whether a professed purpose is real; it is not automatically a new element. The strongest contrary argument invokes the model code's attendant-circumstance and knowledge provisions and implausible conditions. The ordinary resistance condition here is part of the plan to obtain the vehicle, not an independently specified statutory circumstance or a fanciful condition. The Court need not adopt a universal rule for every conditional threat or an abstract attempted-murder hypothetical.

## Arithmetic and remedial precision

Six Associates (Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer) support affirmance on the actual conditional-purpose rule and reject a separate probability/belief element as statutory construction. Scalia and Thomas support reversal of the reviewed carjacking affirmance and return of the dependent firearm judgments under their contrary construction. Neither group reopens chop-shop counts or independently decides ineffective assistance, cumulative punishment or constitutional congressional power.

The reversal position is instructional, not a finding that evidence is legally insufficient under every lawful theory. Further proceedings must respect applicable preservation, harmless-error, sufficiency and retrial rules as actually presented; these commitments do not invent their facts or a particular ultimate conviction. The affirmance position approves the pertinent actual intent instructions against the presented objection, not every sentence in the trial charge.
