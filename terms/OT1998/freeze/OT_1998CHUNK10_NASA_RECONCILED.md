## Final component commitments

Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm coverage of this examination under §7114(a)(2)(B) and, on a separate ground, affirm the limited Headquarters statutory-compliance order. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas reverse coverage and the dependent enforcement order; if coverage otherwise controls, they independently reject the Headquarters component to the extent it imposes the disputed managerial direction duty, while preserving direct OIG compliance. All eight reach these statutory questions on the supplied preservation posture.

The five coverage commitments rest on the actual examiner's agency work and disciplinary function within NASA, statutory text and permissible Authority construction after independent judicial examination of the IG Act. They do not equate every employee or outside examiner with an agency representative. The separate five-Justice Headquarters ground permits this instruction to comply with the limited statutory right under retained general supervision; it does not authorize management to determine investigative subjects, evidence, conclusions or termination, or to bargain for enlarged control of OIG procedures.

The three opposing commitments construe representative in the labor-management relationship with the Inspector General's independent duties intact. Their conclusion concerns this investigation, not immunity from every federal employment statute or a categorical finding that no OIG examiner could ever represent management under exceptional facts. No automatic remand is adopted from NFFE or Haggar.

## Entering-law comparison

Simulated NFFE/FLRA v. Interior differs from any broad reading that administrative responsibility alone settles interpretive validity. It requires actual enacted authority, genuine statutory discretion and a reasoned agency choice; its remand turned on an unexercised choice obscured by a mistaken compulsory-duty premise. Haggar separates general authority, rule validity and factual application. Aguirre-Aguirre likewise permits a supported application without a universal rehearing command. Zurko concerns factual review and does not govern this statutory meaning question.

Those rules are already built into each frozen NASA row. The five affirmers independently harmonize the IG Act and find the existing coverage and parent-compliance rationales adequate; the three opponents find a contrary statutory boundary. No Justice relies on a prediction of automatic agency deference, automatic enforcement or mandatory remand. The difference in simulated law therefore changes the required explanation but does not supply a Justice-specific reason to reverse the modeled judgment alignment. History remains strong corroboration of the preserved actual statutory premises, not an instruction to reproduce its opinion wording.
