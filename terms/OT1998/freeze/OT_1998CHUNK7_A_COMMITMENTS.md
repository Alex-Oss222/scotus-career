## 1. In re Sealed Case — No. 98-93 — April 13, 1999

### Shared decisional propositions and remedy

Reach the preserved Rule 501 question through the accepted third-party privilege appeal. Affirm the order compelling testimony insofar as challenged by the asserted protective-function privilege. Rule 501 does not create the proposed privilege for information protective personnel acquire by their proximity to the President on this record. Presidential protection is an exceptionally important public function; that importance does not establish either a confidential communications relationship or a sufficient reason-and-experience basis to withhold this broad class of observations from a federal grand jury.

The proposed exception for an observation or statement that, at the time made, gave reasonable grounds to believe a felony had been, was being, or would be committed does not cure the recognition problem. It would make the privilege turn on an agent's contemporaneous legal and factual perception, while excluding other probative observations; agents and the President could not know in advance that all relevant observations would remain protected. This is an objection to the proposal's justification and boundaries, not a finding about any particular agent's knowledge or criminal conduct. Jaffee authorizes principled common-law development and does not make historical recognition an indispensable prerequisite. Its demonstrated treatment relationship and widespread recognition materially differ from the supported premises here. Swidler preserves an established communication privilege and likewise does not create protection for independently observed facts.

The strongest opposing path recognizes a qualified protection because anticipated compulsion could cause a President to limit protective access, then demands extraordinary investigative need. Director Merletti's declaration and former President Bush's April 15, 1998 letter affirmatively support the confidence-and-proximity prediction. Those submissions deserve consideration; they are not adjudicated findings of impaired access, and the supported prediction and proposed scope together do not establish a sufficient basis to outweigh the evidentiary cost of this proposed class. Actual impairment is not made a mandatory prerequisite for recognition of every privilege. Neither a presumption that Presidents will compromise their own security nor a presumption that disclosure can never affect security is adopted. An absolute privilege is still less supportable. Once recognition fails, no special overcoming test should be announced or applied. Actual independently supported security accommodation and other properly asserted privileges remain available under their own law; this judgment creates neither an automatic protective order nor a special necessity threshold.

## 2. UNUM Life Insurance Co. of America v. Ward — No. 97-1868 — April 20, 1999

### Notice-prejudice component: common decisional ground

California's insurance-specific notice-prejudice rule falls within ERISA §514(b)(2)(A)'s saving clause as applied to this insured disability policy. It limits the insurer's contractual forfeiture defense: late notice does not relieve the insurer without proof of actual prejudice, and neither lateness itself nor possible prejudice suffices. It is part of the substantive insurer-insured bargain, not merely a state-created damages remedy for administering an ERISA claim. Its application in Ward's §502(a)(1)(B) suit leaves the federal enforcement channel intact. The self-funded-plan question and the deemer clause's distinct protection are not decided.

The common-sense insurance inquiry and the McCarran-Ferguson guideposts address actual operation. The rule specifically governs insurance, directly regulates the policy relationship and preserves the insurer's assumed coverage absent actual prejudice. The parties may dispute whether that last feature independently transfers or spreads risk in a technical sense; these guideposts do not require transforming every consideration into an indispensable conjunctive element. The decision need not invent a replacement saving-clause test or decide every timing rule.

The strongest opposing argument is that permitting state variation in claim timing defeats uniform administration and rewrites an express plan deadline. Metropolitan Life preserves substantive insurance terms despite their effect on insured plans; applying §502(a)(1)(B) to those terms does not create Pilot Life's competing remedy. Travelers and De Buono preserve uniform administration but do not repeal the insurance saving clause. John Hancock preserves both compatible insurance regulation and a concrete-conflict inquiry; no contradictory ERISA command requiring this absolute forfeiture has been identified.

Affirm reversal of the insurer's summary judgment on the notice-prejudice ground and remand for the actual-prejudice inquiry. No finding that prejudice is absent, no award of benefits, and no automatic finding of employer agency follows.

### Employer-as-agent component: corrected legal premise and bounded remand

The primary contemporaneous United States merits filing confirms that the agency question was litigated as a second question; the short neutral grant statement was incomplete. Reach the question at the scope the actual dispute permits. A state rule assigning an employer the insurer's agency role because the employer performs specified benefit-plan administration has a connection with that administration under §514(a). It directly reallocates notice and claim-processing responsibility; it is not merely an external cost of buying insurance. The Ninth Circuit's unconditional no-relation rationale cannot support its contingent Elfstrom instruction on that understanding.

Ward disputes that characterization and describes ordinary agency principles applied to actual circumstances. That distinction matters. The Court cannot turn the disputed doctrine into a universal irrebuttable employer-agency rule, declare every ordinary agency principle preempted, or find a voluntary insurer designation absent or present. Ward did not press insurance saving for this agency theory; the notice-prejudice holding does not silently save it. The actual policy's written-authorization clause and the legal source of any asserted agency must receive their proper effect without treating the employer's administrative participation alone as conclusive.

Vacate the contingent agency direction insofar as it rests on the erroneous premise that the described mandatory allocation does not relate to a plan. If UNUM proves actual prejudice and the agency issue therefore remains material, the lower courts must identify and consider the actual rule invoked under the corrected relation standard, preserving any properly presented voluntary or fact-specific agency theory. This is not an automatic ruling that notice to MAC was ineffective, and no broad federal agency doctrine is created. The notice-prejudice remand remains independently necessary and is affirmed; the complete disposition is **affirm in part, vacate in part, and remand**.

## 3. United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — No. 97-2044 — April 21, 1999

### Shared decisional ground and remedy

Reverse the Federal Circuit's categorical exclusion of Chevron review for the noticed Customs regulation and remand for the proper legal and factual inquiries. Congress's delegation to Treasury/Customs to implement tariff classifications can coexist with the Court of International Trade's decision on its own trial record under 28 U.S.C. §2640(a)(1). The latter provision establishes the court's evidentiary-review function; it does not itself withdraw the delegated interpretive authority or instruct the court to disregard a valid regulation.

The court must first determine the statute's meaning and actual bounds; clear statutory terms control. Where those terms leave the delegated issue open, the formally promulgated construction receives Chevron treatment only within that lawful discretion. Section 10.16(c)'s significant nonassembly-processing rule and (c)(4)'s chemical-treatment/permapressing example must be tested against the partial exemption for assembly and incidental operations. The record must then support application to the actual 12–15 minute baking process. Recognizing the review framework does not itself find the regulation valid, find the factual predicates satisfied, or direct a final duty amount.

The exemption retains all three component conditions: the United States fabricated component was exported ready for assembly without further fabrication, retained physical identity, and was not advanced in value or improved abroad except through assembly or incidental operations. The amount is a partial duty reduction for qualifying United States component cost/value, with the enacted proportional treatment for specific or compound rates; the Court computes none. Section 10.16(b) permits incidental operations before, during or after assembly, including ordinary final pressing and folding; that permission is distinct from paragraph (c)'s significant improvement and chemical-treatment exclusions. Timing after sewing alone therefore establishes neither inclusion nor exclusion, and ordinary pressing must not be equated with resin-activating permapressing.

The strongest contrary path relies on de novo trade review and §10.11's assurance of judicial review to demand wholly independent tariff construction. Judicial review remains effective precisely because the court determines statutory boundaries and permissible interpretation; preserving that right does not negate the promulgation and delegation. Conversely, the Government's strongest broader request could sustain the exclusion at once because permapressing is named. The proper remand preserves the difference between regulatory authority, statutory validity and factual application. The importer's commercial sequencing explanation is an argument bearing on incidental processing, not a judicial finding that the resin activation is necessary assembly. No generalized rule about informal classification letters, no later administrative doctrine, and no conclusory equation of every notice-and-comment rule with lawful authority is adopted.

## 4. Brooks v. Barnes — No. 98-1521 — April 22, 1999

### Components and lawful alternatives

The supplied neutral handoff authorizes ordinary post-grant merits submission and circulation on the existing record by April 22; it does not establish an oral argument. Reach the Section 2 results claim and the Fourteenth/Fifteenth Amendment purpose claims. The reviewed primary law includes county offices, legislative seats, specified judges and district attorneys. The separate general-election rule, multimember offices and an additional First Amendment claim are outside the supplied grant.

**Section 2, six-Associate commitment:** Vacate the statutory judgment and remand under the proper electoral-opportunity standard. Section 2 protects equal participation in processes leading to nomination or election; an otherwise supported claim against a primary barrier does not require proof that every additional minority-preferred nominee would win the general election. Political cohesion and majority bloc voting must be tested in the electoral settings actually affected by the challenged runoff requirement, using voters' preferred candidates rather than candidate race as the controlling proxy. Neither the rarity of runoffs among all primaries nor a statewide net of Black-versus-white winners conclusively answers whether the challenged rule denies equal local opportunity. Relevant candidate strength and crossover evidence remain part of the inquiry. Necessary causal conditions and the independent totality inquiry remain the plaintiffs' burden; no racial-motive requirement, automatic liability from polarization or quota is adopted.

The complete lower opinion establishes the legal errors directly. Paragraph 48 uses the small fraction of all primaries proceeding to runoffs and the statewide net candidate-race statistic to reject the third Gingles condition. Paragraphs 50–52 require proof that additional plurality nominees would actually win general elections. The court's proposed-comparison analysis in paragraph 43 likewise requires a net increase in Black officeholders and balances a hypothetical one-percent plurality winner against the aggregate net gain. These are not merely unfortunate phrases outside an otherwise independent, correctly framed inquiry. They affect the necessary-conditions and totality determinations; the bare finding that polarization was not proved cannot be detached confidently from those stated legal premises. The Court neither makes its own polarization finding nor rejects supported subsidiary facts.

The strongest affirmance path relies on the trial court's credited evidence about relative candidate strength, insufficient proof of deterrence and failure to establish polarization, with Rule 52(a) deference. The six supporting Associates find a correction/remand necessary because the primary/general-election requirement and denominators were used to evaluate those same claims, not because an appellate court prefers another statistical account. Gingles protects supported findings but permits correction of legally infected mixed determinations; SCLC supplies the same independent-ground discipline. The 56 adverse and 29 favorable candidate-race reversals establish neither 27 unlawfully lost offices nor negligible opportunity harm. No loss of a particular protected nomination is found here.

A comparison must have independent legal and practical roots and fit the office and electoral function. The State's desire for a broadly supported party nominee and resistance to vote-splitting manipulation deserve concrete consideration, but do not immunize the runoff mechanism or make a hypothetical extreme plurality outcome conclusive. The existence of a workable alternative is separate from proof of unequal opportunity and from choice of a remedy. The Court requires neither statewide pure plurality nor the 45-percent alternative, no specific new statistical study and no new liability trial as an automatic step. The lower courts must reconsider under the corrected law on the existing record and any supplementation ordinary procedure independently permits. Relief follows only if a violation is proved.

**Constitutional component, all eight Associates:** Affirm rejection of the purpose claims. Under Arlington Heights, Hunter and Mt. Healthy, a substantial or motivating discriminatory purpose triggers the defenders' burden to prove the same enactment without that factor. The supported findings both distinguish the failed racially motivated 1963 Groover bill from the 1964 proponents and independently establish enactment for the credited anti-manipulation and election-administration reasons even without discriminatory motivation. The Court need not decide the full extent of Fifteenth Amendment dilution coverage because the claim fails on that assumed coverage. De Grandy's statutory results rule does not supply this constitutional standard.

The strongest contrary argument relies on the discriminatory historical sequence, the earlier committees and literacy proposals, Sanders's earlier racial motives, and possible unreliability of retrospective testimony. The trial court considered the contemporaneous committee record and credited witnesses with firsthand roles, including the third committee's membership and Sanders's power over the enacted package. The general racial climate is relevant but does not require imputing Groover's failed bill's purpose to every supporter of the later enactment. The absence of a direct admission is not a legal prerequisite for intent, and contemporaneous evidence does not become categorically inferior to testimony. On the actual findings and the independent same-enactment ground, the record supplies no clear-error basis for reversal. Upholding that ground does not establish that harmless present effects cure a purpose violation or that mixed motives are universally lawful.

## 5. United States v. Sun-Diamond Growers of California — No. 98-131 — April 27, 1999

### Statutory construction commitment

Section 201(c)(1)(A) requires a connection between the thing of value and an identifiable official act performed or to be performed by the official. The words requiring that connection do not permit conviction solely because the recipient holds public office, because the giver has matters before the agency, or because a gift is intended generally to improve the relationship. The Government need not prove bribery's corrupt bargain or intent to influence in order to establish a gratuity; the required act-linked reason can be a reward for an act already done. The legal distinction between the provisions does not erase the gratuity provision's own official-act language.

The strongest contrary construction invokes the comprehensive anti-corruption purpose and the difference between bribery and gratuities: gifts given for status may help purchase access without a demonstrable bargain. That concern cannot change the enacted object from an official act into officeholding. The definition in §201(a)(3) identifies decisions or actions on a legally cognizable question or matter; the phrase covering future official acts expands timing, not liability to no act at all. Other prohibitions may address gifts beyond this provision. The ruling should not supply a comprehensive narrowing definition of official act beyond the question presented, a quid-pro-quo requirement, or immunity for a sequence of gratuities linked to identifiable acts.

The instruction expressly authorized conviction without any identifiable-act link and was erroneous. The court's initial reading of the statutory words did not correct its later explicit permission to convict on the broader office-based ground. All eight Associates join this statutory construction and instructional-error conclusion.

Affirm the D.C. Circuit's Count One reversal and new-trial remand. The Government's harmless-error alternative cannot be sustained on its assertion that pending regulatory interests and the charge's repeated statutory words necessarily supplied the missing jury determination. The repeated explicit direction permitted the jury to rely on cultivating goodwill or celebrating official status; the vice-president's business role, company reimbursement and two regulatory interests support possible act-linked proof, but do not make the legally required inference unavoidable. His college friendship with Espy underscores that the motive for the gifts was a real question, without establishing a friendship defense. The error changes what the jury had to find and is not a technical misstatement cured by quoting the statute. This is a rejection of the supported harmlessness claim on the actual instruction and evidence account, not a universal structural-error rule or a declaration that the Government possesses no further evidence. The Court does not undertake a new exhaustive sufficiency determination. The previously ordered new trial remains available; no acquittal is ordered and no final finding of adequate proof under the correct rule is made. Indictment sufficiency was not sought or granted and is not reopened; the separate probation/reporting and resentencing matters remain outside review.

## 6. Russell v. Burris — Nos. 98-397 and 98-399 — April 28, 1999

### Actual components and record

Reach the $300 per-election ordinary candidate limits for the specified statewide offices, the $100 per-election limits for other offices (with the separately established judicial-office burden), the $200 annual receipt limit for contributions to any one ordinary PAC, and the small-donor PAC's $2,500 candidate-contribution allowance. The latter is available only to a qualifying committee funded by individuals giving no more than $25 annually each; it is not a $2,500 intake limit and does not permit unlimited repeated $25 gifts. The separate $500 independent-expenditure-committee limit and local-authorizing provision are outside the grants. The companion petitions concern the same reviewed judgment without inventing a historical consolidation order.

Buckley and Kentucky Right to Life preserve the distinction between a candidate contribution and the quantity of independent or candidate expression. Contribution limits require a sufficiently important anti-corruption or appearance interest and a closely drawn fit; equality of political influence is not an independent justification. Day also requires meaningful collective association and fit to an identified corruption/circumvention mechanism for intermediary limits. No dollar figure is a permanent floor or safe harbor. A completed bribe or demonstrated quid pro quo is not required, and neither the existence of some successful candidates nor the possibility of self-financing automatically answers a prohibitory burden.

### Candidate limits: office-specific commitments

**All eight Associates:** Affirm invalidation of the $300 caps for the enumerated statewide offices and the $100 caps for judicial offices, with the existing severability-based declaratory/injunctive proceedings at that scope. This is an application to the established scale, fundraising burdens and evidence, not a universal minimum. The approximately $900,000 gubernatorial campaign scale and the absence of a correspondingly supported reason for reducing ordinary gifts to $300 distinguish the legislative-race evidence; a $1,000 gift represented about one-tenth of one percent of those campaign costs. The statewide conclusion does not require a rule that a small percentage can never appear corrupt, but the cited legislative examples do not justify every office's sharply reduced ceiling by transposition.

Judicial candidates face the separate $100 cap combined with no personal solicitation or acceptance and the limited committee fundraising period (180 days before the primary through 45 days after the last contested election), while the statewide and appellate electoral areas exceed legislative districts. Suster recognizes judicial impartiality as important but requires fit to the actual burden. The judicial judgment concerns this combination, not a holding that judges receive less anti-corruption regulation or that the other ethical constraints are invalid.

**Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer:** Reverse invalidation of the $100 cap for the remaining non-statewide, nonjudicial offices and reject the presented challenge at this record's scope. The trial evidence supplied an appearance-of-influence concern tied to contributions' proportion of these smaller campaigns: the Maddox fundraiser, tobacco contributions and other financial concentration were actual evidence, even though the court credited denials of improper access and found no actual undue influence. Candidate agreement with a donor is not itself corruption. The credible testimony about feasible funding, actual special-election receipts above historical open-seat averages, the $24,000 and $40,000 Senate campaigns, and resetting limits for each election support preserving meaningful campaign support on this record. The theoretical 320/780-donor calculation is not treated as proof that candidates obtained those donors. Neither access to wealthy self-financing nor the favorable small-donor category alone carries the judgment.

The strongest opposing evidence is that up to eighty percent of past legislative contributions would be affected and challengers' professional fundraisers disputed effective fundraising. The five distinguish affected contributions from a demonstrated inability to raise sufficient campaign resources, while recognizing that the burden is material and that existing officeholder experience is not automatically representative. The trial's actual operational evidence, rather than mathematical possibility alone, supports this bounded result. No successful-party, incumbent or donor-size exemption, universal rule for small States, or new burden of proving campaign impossibility is adopted.

**Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas:** Affirm invalidation of all reviewed $100/$300 candidate caps, including the nonjudicial $100 cap. The eighty-percent impact and sharp fundraising reorganization impose substantial practical burdens; the State's appearance evidence principally identifies donor interests and aggregate shares, without adequately explaining this unusually restrictive individual ceiling. The two special elections and officeholders' fundraising do not answer the burden on ordinary challengers or show the needed fit. Their ground accepts the governing contribution distinction and does not require proof of completed bribery. They do not turn an inflation comparison with Buckley into a constitutional floor or invalidate every lower amount as such.

### Ordinary PAC receipt restriction

**Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer:** Reverse invalidation of the $200 annual per-person receipt limit for each ordinary PAC. The trial's years-of-operation evidence and robust candidate-directed PAC activity supply a materially different burden record from Day. The lower court's account of no significant impairment is considered as a finding about the tried case, not an invented waiver. Each donor may support multiple committees; this is not Kentucky Right to Life's single aggregate pool across all recipients. Ordinary PACs can channel candidate funds without the federal multi-donor/multi-candidate qualifications, making circumvention a concrete regulated route. The State may limit that route without first proving an actual corrupt transaction. The sum, annual duration and actual recipient function are considered together; $200 alone does not answer constitutionality.

The strongest contrary path treats the small cap as materially impairing political pooling and finds the conduit argument insufficiently specific. The five accept that Day requires a real inquiry, but distinguish its established substantial pooling burden and the combined-recipient breadth of Kentucky Right to Life. This record does not establish a comparable burden, while the unrestricted intermediary channel gives the anti-circumvention interest actual work. No holding permits every cap on an independently operating committee; a materially different recipient-function or operational burden remains open.

**Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas:** Affirm invalidation of this cap. Day requires more than a low cap plus a generally possible candidate conduit, and the annual ceiling reaches all covered ordinary PAC support rather than tracing the particular candidate-funded transfer. Aggregate committee activity does not establish a meaningful opportunity for each donor to associate through the chosen committee. The lack of federal-style qualifying conditions may explain a circumvention concern, but does not establish why this across-the-board $200 receipt restraint is closely fitted. They preserve realistic earmark, coordination and conduit controls and reject an inference that contributions to PACs are categorically equivalent to independent expenditures.

### Small-donor committee differential

**Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer:** Reverse the appellate invalidation of the small-donor differential. A committee genuinely pooling only annual individual gifts of $25 or less can receive a larger candidate-giving allowance because its financing is dispersed and individual financial control attenuated. The $2,500 allowance concerns the committee's contribution and does not itself guarantee absence of committee-level influence; the constitutionally relevant conclusion is that a carefully defined diffuse-donor class may be treated differently without requiring identical caps for every source. Ordinary PACs retain broader donor types and greater individual receipts, and their members' ability to form a qualifying committee is supporting context, not an answer to unequal treatment by itself. The distinction is financial structure, not favored viewpoints or equalization. The judgment concerns the challenged differential on these qualifications, not unlimited candidate contributions by any entity labeling itself small-donor.

**Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas:** Affirm invalidation of the differential. Even diffuse donors can act through a committee that makes a $2,500 candidate transfer; the asserted candidate obligation is to that committee, and the State has not sufficiently fitted its preferential allowance to that separate risk. The disparity burdens other associations' candidate support on the basis of financing form without adequately answering the risk at the recipient end. They do not hold that all multi-donor qualifications or all differentiated ceilings are invalid. The ability to organize a different committee does not cure the insufficient justification for limiting the existing association.
