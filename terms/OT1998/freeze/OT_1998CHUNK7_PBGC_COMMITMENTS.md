## P1 — tax priority: sufficient ground, alternatives and limits

Section 412(n)(1)(B) supplies a lien when the covered unpaid minimum contributions exceed $1 million; §412(n)(4)(B) supplies the 60-day onset rule. Section 412(n)(4)(C) directs tax treatment for an amount **with respect to which the specified lien is imposed** and imports rules similar to ERISA §4068(c), (d) and (e). Section 4068(c), 29 U.S.C. §1368(c), expressly addresses a Title 11 or insolvency case and treatment as a tax for Title 11 purposes. A court must interpret that combined direction, including its conditions and scope. It may not disregard it solely because the cross-reference runs from the pension statute toward Title 11 rather than the reverse.

That legal error is sufficient to vacate the Tenth Circuit's categorical rejection of the incorporation argument. The functional character of a payment cannot substitute for examining an actual statutory direction. Conversely, recognizing a statutory connection does not establish that its condition is met, that every funding obligation receives tax treatment, or that every applicable §507 requirement is satisfied. The source's private-plan-purpose rationale cannot decide the effect of a special congressional direction before the court determines whether that direction applies. No universal classification of all pension contributions is necessary.

**Actual alternative, not a missing-record invention:** Source note 4 reports that the bankruptcy and district courts denied tax priority because the installment became due fewer than 60 days before the petition; on their view no lien had arisen and the automatic stay prevented its creation. The Tenth Circuit expressly did not decide that ground. Those lower determinations and the debtor's argument remain available. They are not converted into a new Supreme Court finding that the lien attached, never could attach, or is legally unnecessary. Remand permits the appellate court to resolve the preserved lien condition and bankruptcy consequences on the existing record, including the effect of the stay, and to affirm denial on a legally sufficient alternative if appropriate. It commands no new evidence or new lien proceeding.

The strongest affirmance path is that the statutory tax direction is conditional on a lien and the lower courts' reported timing/stay ground already defeats that condition. It is materially stronger than the Tenth Circuit's one-way-cross-reference premise. The selected remedy leaves that distinct legal issue to the court that declined it, rather than silently treating its reported conclusion as an independently reviewed Supreme Court holding. The strongest broader PBGC path is that Congress expressly prescribed tax treatment, so functional private-purpose analysis is beside the point. That succeeds in requiring consideration of the linkage; it does not eliminate the linkage's own conditions.

## P2 — administrative priority: sufficient ground, alternatives and limits

The disputed additional claim derives from pension compensation earned through prepetition labor. A due date after the petition, continuation of the plan, statutory minimum-funding duties and later PBGC termination do not by themselves change the origin of that obligation into a new postpetition debt. Section 503(b)(1)(A)'s application therefore supports the denial of additional priority, while contributions attributable to actual postpetition employee services retain the priority already allowed.

**Reading is a real alternative.** Reading allowed postpetition negligence liability administrative treatment without a benefit to the estate. The decision here does not adopt a rule that every administrative claim must purchase a measurable estate benefit. Even assuming that a qualifying statutory liability can fall within that principle, this claim's additional portion remains compensation grounded in prepetition services, not a new postpetition injury or liability of the kind on which that alternative depends. Statutory enforcement of an earlier obligation does not alone settle when the debt arose.

The strongest PBGC path treats operating the company and retaining an unterminated plan while withholding legally required funding as a postpetition cost or wrong; continuing statutory duties cannot simply be ignored by a debtor in possession. That establishes the importance and continued enforceability of the duty, not priority for every dollar attributable to earlier labor. Reading prevents a benefit-only denial but does not erase the prepetition/postpetition distinction. The holding does not pronounce every postpetition statutory violation ineligible or every pension liability merely contractual. It does not depend on categorically excluding the claim from all statutory obligations.

The Coal Act comparison supplies a competing argument about duties triggered by continued corporate existence, but its different statutory trigger and tax treatment do not establish that this contribution debt arose after filing. Section 1114's express treatment of retiree medical payments is distinct; it is not a rule that Congress can grant priority only by printing an amendment in Title 11. Because P2 rests on the origin of the additional debt and preserves Reading, it remains sufficient even though P1's tax inquiry returns to the lower court.

## P3 — valuation: sufficient ground, alternatives and limits

Section 1362(b)(1)(A) measures termination liability by total unfunded benefit liabilities as of termination. Section 1301(a)(18) defines that amount by benefit liabilities, using assumptions prescribed by PBGC for §1344 purposes, less current plan assets. A bankruptcy claim based on that federal liability does not lose its creating statute's measure merely because §1301 introduces definitions for ERISA purposes. The court must first identify the obligation that law creates, then apply the Bankruptcy Code's allowance and petition-date commands.

Section 502(b)'s valuation as of the petition date remains operative. Section 1123(a)(4)'s equal treatment of claims within a class does not itself require every creditor's legally different underlying obligation to be generated with the same assumptions. Section 1144(d) preserves other federal law; it requires an actual incompatibility rather than presuming that any ERISA measure yields whenever a claim enters bankruptcy. Harmonizing the statutory liability measure and the petition-date allowance inquiry preserves each provision's work.

Haggar supplies the governing delegation framework. The express reference to PBGC-prescribed assumptions is an actual source of authority, not deference inferred from the agency's identity. Courts must enforce its limits and any applicable unambiguous Bankruptcy Code command. Within a lawful delegation and statutory ambiguity, a reasonable prescribed assumption is not displaced simply by a judge's preferred discount method. The lower court's categorical reasons do not establish an actual conflict or an invalid assumption; they therefore cannot sustain the prudent-investor valuation.

The strongest estate path is that §502 places petition-date valuation with the bankruptcy court, that annuity-purchase assumptions calculate termination insurance liabilities for another statutory purpose, and that differential discount rates can materially alter distributions to equal-ranked creditors. Those are serious questions about the measure's scope and application. They do not support an automatic rule that the source of the liability ceases to govern in bankruptcy. The strongest broader PBGC path would direct its comparison figure immediately because the statute incorporates its assumptions. That goes beyond the supported disposition: the Court has not fixed the reconciliation of the statutory dates or the final allowed amount.

On remand the courts must use the lawful statutory liability measure and apply the Code's actual petition-date and allowance requirements. They may address any properly presented challenge to a particular assumption's statutory fit, reasonableness or application; no new challenge, evidentiary hearing or invalidity finding is ordered. No double discount is authorized by the mere existence of different statutory dates. The decision does not transplant Rash's replacement-value method or LaSalle's separate investment-opportunity analysis into this claim, establish a universal discount rate, validate every PBGC methodology or award $222,866,000.
