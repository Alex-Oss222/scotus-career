## Reconciliation of the material forks

### Statutory deadlock and the separate duty to instruct

**All eight Associates adopt the same statutory consequence.** After a completed penalty deliberation reports inability to return a unanimous recommendation, §3594's “Otherwise” clause transfers sentencing to the judge for a lawful nondeath sentence. For this §1201 death-resulting conviction, the lawful alternative is life without release. Section 3593(b)(2)(C)'s good-cause discharge covers circumstances such as disqualification; it does not authorize another attempt to obtain death merely because the jury deadlocked. The Government's manifest-necessity/new-jury argument is rejected as inconsistent with that operative command, not treated as conceded away by its separate death-or-life concession. New sentencing after reversible error under §3593(b)(2)(D) remains a different event. No actual deadlock occurred here.

The historical Court II-A and dissent III-B agree on this construction. The enacted text, Government argument, posture and requested instruction are unchanged. Simmons, Shannon, Neder and the new public decisions change no premise of this statutory question. The original O'Connor/Scalia/Kennedy/Thomas reservation therefore lacks a concrete basis and is corrected. This conclusion is not drawn mechanically from unanimity in history: the “Otherwise” command supplies the legal reason, while the replacement-jury provision has substantial independent work without swallowing it.

**All eight reject a universal Eighth Amendment or supervisory requirement to explain deadlock consequences in every capital case.** Jurors ordinarily must deliberate and make their own punishment decision; the Government's legitimate interest in a unanimous recommendation does not require advertising failure of deliberation. Congressional silence supplies no mandatory script. But the absence of a universal duty does not excuse an affirmatively misleading description of available punishment. The four affirming Associates find no such misinformation in context; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer find a corrective instruction necessary here. Dissent note 20 expressly separates those propositions. No Associate is committed to the imperfect request verbatim, a nonunanimous jury life verdict, or a beyond-reasonable-doubt burden on the ultimate moral choice.

### Delivered instructions, preservation and juror accounts

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain the distinction between the preserved refusal claim and forfeited attacks on the delivered charge/forms. Rule 30's timely, distinct objection is not supplied automatically by later motions, appellate merits consideration or §3595(c)(2)(A)'s “arbitrary factor” language; reading that phrase to erase (c)(2)(C)'s preservation condition would deprive the latter of effect. Counsel's lesser-sentence requests do not establish intentional waiver of every related objection.

These four find no reasonable likelihood of the asserted below-life/deadlock inference under Boyde. The specific direction toward C or D on nonunanimity is the strongest counterargument, not merely the missing unanimity word or signature format. Read with the initial requirement that every recommendation be unanimous, the explicit life-without-release option, optional death, and protection of individual conscience, C/D provide forms for a recommendation rather than compel a genuinely divided jury to endorse a punishment it does not want. Foreperson-only signatures also recorded expressly unanimous factor findings. The instruction not to concern themselves with the court's sentence reinforces the allocated task; it is not an irrebuttable cure for every contradiction.

The isolated lesser option is unauthorized by §1201, but it is not pressed as an independent preserved basis for reversal. On the forfeited combined theory these four additionally assume plain error and find no demonstrated substantial-rights effect: the stated alternatives and safeguards do not establish that the ambiguity altered this choice. They need not decide initially unsettled-law timing or the fourth condition once prejudice fails. That is a defendant-burden determination, not Chapman harmlessness or a guilt-based exception.

Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg retain preservation of the core corrective request: the written request and repeated reference to it alerted the judge to the relationship between nonunanimity and the legal minimum, notwithstanding counsel's imperfect lesser-sentence wording. They also reject the original handoff's blanket fallback that a forfeiture ruling would necessarily defeat instruction relief. Their historical III-A and notes 16/18 treat the false minimum as clear at trial and the charge's concrete distortion of the life/death choice as a substantial reliability injury. On that alternative they find error, plainness, a substantial-rights effect in this actual selection framework, and independently a serious effect on fairness and integrity warranting correction. The conclusion rests on the specific false third outcome and routing, not on capital status alone or an assertion that any ambiguous word proves prejudice. The fact that pressure could cause either faction to yield does not restore confidence in a choice distorted in this manner. Johnson's reserved initially unsettled-law question is unnecessary because these three find the offense's minimum clear from the enacted text; its limited fourth-condition refusal does not compel refusal here. Their preserved route independently suffices.

Breyer's commitment is narrower and different. He finds abuse of discretion in the preserved refusal of instruction 5's legally accurate core: the trial court's contrary legal premise and the remaining charge's false impression required correction. He does not find the unobjected delivered instructions to amount to plain error. This is the distinction expressly recorded in historical dissent note 15. The original generalized P1/Chapman-remand characterization did not adequately preserve it.

No new unanimous Supreme Court merits ruling on Rule 606(b) is assembled. The majority historically declined the independent affidavit-admissibility issue as outside the presented questions (Court note 11); it did not decide exclusion anew. The neutral packet establishes no outside influence and does not authorize treating internal accounts as proved juror mental states. All present operative instruction grounds can be resolved objectively. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg acknowledge the exclusion precedent while retaining their historical reluctance to discount the accounts altogether (dissent note 19), an ancillary caution that supplies no admissibility exception or necessary factual premise. Breyer's preserved-refusal ground likewise needs no affidavit. The original categorical all-eight exclusion attribution is narrowed accordingly. No disposition depends on an unverified juror switch.

### Factor validity and a distinct assumed-error cure

**Validity:** O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas adopt contextual no error on the presented vagueness, breadth and overlap challenges. Factors 3(B) and 3(C) address the victim's physical/situational vulnerability and the individual/family loss. The actual trial descriptions now validated in the addendum support these distinct functions. Tuilaepa's understandable relevant selection subjects, §3593(a), Payne and the anti-counting charge support that conclusion. Shared evidence is not necessarily a duplicated aggravating circumstance, and a subject's frequent relevance across murders does not prevent individualized selection. They do not adopt an unrestricted comparative-worth rule, convert favorable advocacy into findings, or say eligibility cures a defective selection factor.

Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg instead find these delivered, separately submitted factors duplicative and inadequately definite. “Personal characteristics” encompasses the listed age/stature/background/unfamiliarity; neither the charge nor findings made the vulnerability/loss distinction obligatory, and an anti-counting instruction does not remove double weight assigned to an overlapping consideration. The actual argument is relevant and strengthens the contrary contextual reading, but does not overcome their objection to the instruction's task. They accept lawful victim-impact evidence and meaningful contextual factors; their ruling concerns this formulation, not every overlap or victim characteristic. Historical dissent IV supplies the precise position and objection. The closing passages were already before the historical Court, so newly making them available to the model is not a changed historical factual premise.

Tuilaepa is expressly considered rather than ignored. Stevens and Ginsburg's simulated bounded joins accept relevant subjects without fixed weights; Ginsburg's position there differs from history. But its protection of actual weighing-error claims and its distinction between relevant age and automatic aggravation remain. Jones separately labels the overlapping personal subjects as aggravators to be found and weighed. That concrete difference allows their Tuilaepa joins and Jones objection to coexist. Souter's historical Tuilaepa join already coexisted with his Jones IV join. His recent Strickler selection dissent reinforces attention to the real moral choice, without deciding this different claim. No changed premise supports his refreshed affirmative validity vote. Stevens/Ginsburg's reservation and Souter's no-error selection are corrected to the specific substantive objection, with the historical waiver objection separately treated below.

Scalia reserves validity and does not join either definitive validity ground. His historical III-A nonjoin has no published explanation; it cannot be converted into invalidity or guessed to rest only on waiver. His Tuilaepa join permits a no-error alternative but does not establish this application, and its wording and closing context are unchanged. His current Neder position addresses omitted materiality, not these factors. His model's affirmative validity join therefore lacks a concrete premise and is withdrawn.

Breyer also reserves validity, with no join in the three-Justice invalidity ground or the three-Justice no-error ground. Historical nonjoinder in dissent IV is not an affirmative no-error vote. Neither his Neder/Lilly remedial positions nor his Strickler selection assessment decides the present formulation, and the actual closing was historically present. The instruction ground disposes of his sentence vote; no independent factor merits or factor-remedy vote is needed.

**Independent assumed-error harmlessness:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas adopt a direct, bounded §3595/Chapman harmlessness ground for the asserted loose-drafting defects even if invalidity is assumed. This corrects their universal conditional-remand rows. It is an alternative ground for O'Connor/Kennedy/Thomas and the selected factor ground for Scalia. It does not depend on accepting the Fifth Circuit's equivalence of reweighing and harmlessness or its emphasis on eligibility.

Their determination concerns the actual meaning communicated to this jury. The listed physical/situational circumstances, the separate family-impact clause, the prosecutor's concrete vulnerability and personal-loss explanations, the express findings of both subjects, and the direction to assign weight rather than count headings provide the connection. They infer beyond a reasonable doubt that the jury understood the distinct permissible subjects it was weighing; a precise written description would have made explicit that understanding rather than changed the sentencing consideration. The assumed defect of loose formulation therefore made no contribution to the actual recommendation. That is a judicial inference in applying the Government's burden, not a new neutral fact or proof of jurors' private thoughts. It neither subtracts all victim-impact evidence from the record nor independently chooses death because statutory aggravation outweighs mitigation.

The strongest contrary argument is substantial: counsel could not bind the jury, the headings overlap, the findings do not reveal weight, and the excerpts establish no exclusive use or comparative emphasis. The four nevertheless find the known actual presentation and instructions sufficient for this narrowly defined defect. They do not require an inference that every other closing passage was favorable, that these were the only references, or that any particular number of references occurred. The known contextual connection supplies their proof; absence of a complete local transcript does not. An independently established forbidden use, materially conflicting argument or different invalidity would require its own analysis and is not declared harmless by this ground.

Neder is a real changed legal premise compared with historical Neder, but does not displace this precise inference. Its actual-verdict method rejects hypothetical sufficiency while allowing supported contextual inference beyond necessary equivalent findings. Historical Jones III-B itself states that the jury actually understood the subjects; it does not rest solely on overwhelming proof or eligibility. Here the sentencer actually considered the lawful vulnerability/loss evidence, rather than being forbidden to determine an omitted element. O'Connor/Kennedy/Thomas's Neder method and tax application therefore support the availability of that inference. Scalia's narrower omitted-materiality approach does not become a universal capital-factor rule: precise expression of an actually considered permissible selection subject does not necessarily replace a never-made element finding. His Sochor/Espinosa constitutional reservations cannot erase the enacted §3595 burden, which he applies here. Clemons permits this precise-definition route; Stringer and Sochor require substantive correction, not a prescribed formula or automatic remand whenever the court below reasoned inadequately.

Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg reject that direct cure. Their validity objection means that the separately authorized overlapping weight, rather than mere imprecise description of an actually distinct lawful subject, is the error. The prosecutor's explanation and anti-counting instruction do not establish noncontribution of that authorization beyond a reasonable doubt. Remaining statutory eligibility supplies none. Their Stringer/Sochor/Simmons positions and current Neder approaches support this distinction; Ginsburg's narrower method is additional personal evidence, not a new universal equivalent-findings requirement. Their instruction ground already requires a new hearing. If it were unavailable, their factor ground would at least require vacatur of the appellate death affirmance and proper §3595 correction, with §3593 reconsideration or lawful nondeath sentencing if no adequate cure is established. They do not decide that an appellate court may impose death anew by reweighing or that life automatically follows.

Breyer makes no necessary assumed-factor-error determination. His agreement that §3595 requires a lawful actual inquiry is not a vote that these factors were harmless or harmful, or for an independent factor remand.

### Remedy and scope

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas affirm the death-sustaining judgment on their specified grounds. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the sentencing decision and death sentence and remand for a new, accurately instructed penalty hearing if the Government pursues death; a lawful nondeath disposition remains available. All preserve the conviction and unrelated claims. No execution date, release, automatic life sentence, or unrestricted power to convene successive juries after deadlock is supplied.

Breyer's original first-appellate-harmlessness-only instruction remedy is corrected. Historical dissent V, which he joined, requires a new sentencing hearing. His simulated Neder fraud remand answered a materially different omitted-element review left unfinished by an antecedent no-element ruling. It does not displace his judgment that this preserved erroneous refusal left the actual life/death choice unreliable and requires a properly informed sentencer. Lilly's separate remedial rule likewise supplies no reason to change the remedy. Incomplete local penalty material alone could not justify either new-hearing or harmlessness-remand relief; the established legal misdirection supplies his reason. No concrete changed premise supports the original departure.
