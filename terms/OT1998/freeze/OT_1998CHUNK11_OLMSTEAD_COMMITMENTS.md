## 1. Review, common premises, and proposition definitions

The authorized question is petition question 1: whether Title II compels community treatment and habilitation when appropriate treatment and habilitation can also be provided in a state institution. The scope certificate confirms question-1-only review. Each Associate reaches that statutory question. None adjudicates Congress's Fourteenth Amendment §5 power, damages, independent immunity, or the unadjudicated constitutional claims.

The continuing injunction and the officials' request to overturn their obligations present an ongoing controversy notwithstanding the actual community placements. The lower opinion's recurring-admission and unstable-service account reinforces that conclusion; no new admission, present medical deterioration, or assumption that every similar case evades review is required. The model does not rest exclusively on a categorical capable-of-repetition exception. No further vehicle or participation defect is established. All eight Associates were seated at argument and decision; no recusal is supplied.

The following definitions fix the operative propositions so that a shared disposition cannot silently become a shared rationale. They are candidate legal propositions, not an already adopted Court test.

- **Coverage:** Title II reaches the State's mental-health services, including institutional programs. *Yeskey* forecloses a categorical institutional exception, while leaving qualification, causation, modification and relief distinct.
- **Integration duty:** In administering services it provides, a State can subject a qualified person to disability discrimination by requiring institutional segregation that is unnecessary for that person's appropriately supported treatment. Where the State's treatment professionals have found community care appropriate, the individual seeks that care, and a reasonable modification can provide it without fundamental alteration, Title II and the valid integration regulation require the integrated setting. The absence of a nondisabled person needing the identical disability service does not defeat this claim. This proposition does not declare every hospitalization discriminatory, equate disability-caused need with discrimination, or require creation of every desired health benefit.
- **Statutory-regulatory ground:** Sections 12131–12134, read together with the enacted identification of segregation and institutionalization, authorize the integration rule. Section 12134(b)'s reference to the Part 41 coordination regulations is specific legal support; it is not an unlimited delegation to redesign benefits. Under *Haggar* the court determines lawful authority and textual limits; under *Auer* the responsible agency's considered, text-consistent construction receives its prescribed weight. The United States' position establishes no disputed Georgia fact.
- **Individual limit:** This record permits leaving intact the lower court's conclusion about suitably supported community care at the relevant times. Earlier contrary assessments, failed inadequately supported placements and intervening physical illness do not establish a present contrary finding. No opinion here adjudicates a case in which the State's treating professionals continue to find community placement inappropriate. The ADA does not compel an individual to accept an accommodation, and the choice rule gives guardians no authority to refuse medical necessities. Appropriate institutional treatment remains available as needs change.
- **Resource defense:** Section 35.130(b)(7) places on the public entity the demonstration that the otherwise necessary modification would fundamentally alter its service, program or activity. Relevant evidence can concern fixed and transition costs, available funding and transfer authority, actual institutional needs, and the effect of the requested modification on the State's services to other eligible people. Neither a bare shortage assertion nor a favorable average per-person cost comparison decides the issue. The duty is not erased by labeling existing arrangements a program; the defense is not erased by viewing each claimant's expense in isolation.
- **Plan example:** A demonstrated plan for systematic, prompt movement into appropriate community care may bear on the resource defense. Its existence is not independently dispositive: the State must show why the request, considered with comparable requests, would disrupt lawful priorities or implementation sufficiently to meet the regulation's actual limitation. An inert list, an unsupported promise, or a plan designed merely to prolong segregation does not demonstrate that limitation. No Justice below finds that Georgia has established such a plan, any particular place in a queue, or any prescribed rate of movement.

**Three distinct disposition positions used below:**

1. **Preserve the reviewed remand:** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit's integration determination and its existing fundamental-alteration remand, with the qualifications stated in the opinion. The current injunction remains subject to lawful lower-court administration and the already pending defense proceedings. Do not independently decide or vacate the January 1999 judgment.
2. **Correct the defense-related component:** Affirm the availability of the bounded integration claim and preserve the relevant individual-suitability determination; vacate the reviewed judgment to the extent its treatment of the defense makes the additional expenditure for these two claimants, measured against the whole budget, the controlling measure. Remand through the Eleventh Circuit for the defense and consequent prospective relief to be assessed under the full program inquiry. This does not vacate an independently reviewed January order, direct readmission, terminate services automatically, or establish Georgia's defense.
3. **Reject the statutory basis:** Reverse the integration-liability determination on the statutory theory presented, vacate the dependent affirmance of the statutory injunction, and remand for proceedings on any preserved unresolved claims and appropriate treatment of existing relief. Do not order final dismissal of the unadjudicated constitutional claims, authorize unlawful confinement, or find either respondent presently unsuitable for community care.

No position commands discharge, closure of an institution, a particular appropriation, provider, placement deadline, new medical examination, evidentiary hearing, or new administrative proceeding.

## 2. Stevens

**Commitment and reach:** Reach question 1; preserve the reviewed remand. Affirm the statutory integration determination as bounded above and the circuit's existing opportunity for the State to demonstrate fundamental alteration. Do not decide the separately appealed January ruling or prescribe a new plan defense.

**Sufficient ground and support:** The operative prohibition, Congress's specific direction respecting Part 41, and the integrated-setting regulation govern how existing services are administered. They permit a discrimination finding based on unnecessary segregated delivery without requiring a nondisabled comparator for the disability-specific benefit. *Choate*'s meaningful-access and neglect principles support this construction while its benefit-adequacy limitation remains. Stevens's joins in *Yeskey*, *Auer* and *Bragdon*, and his service/notice opinion in *Cedar Rapids*, support enforcing the stated obligation and the actual regulatory limitation together. His *Heller* join addresses the actual disadvantage associated with confinement rather than treating a disability classification as self-justifying. That constitutional decision does not itself create a placement entitlement.

**Application and remedy:** The existing service system, supported professional determinations and respondents' requested placement supply this case's statutory application. Lower opinion paragraphs 40–48 already recognize fixed overhead, budget demands, funding alternatives and nonexclusive relevant factors. A better description of that remand can acknowledge actual effects on others without vacating it. The October in-limine order may have interpreted the mandate too narrowly, but it is not proof that the reviewed appellate judgment compelled that interpretation. The later appeal is the proper setting for deciding that application's legality on its own record.

**Exact joins and barriers:** Joins Coverage, Integration duty, Statutory-regulatory ground, Individual limit and Resource defense, including relevance of established effects on other recipients. Does not join a new rule granting a defense merely because a State presents a placement plan, a rule requiring these respondents to await all similarly situated patients, or vacatur of the original remand on the strength of the later district order. He can join discussion that a real plan is relevant evidence; he does not adopt the Plan example as an additional holding resolving when a plaintiff must wait. No requirement of discriminatory animus, proof of a particular stereotype, or equal nondisabled treatment is added.

**Supported alternative and strongest counterargument:** Correcting the defense component could ensure that the right remains compatible with obligations to the whole population; the United States' pages 19–22 support that substantive concern. The limiting fact is the reviewed judgment's expressly nonexclusive inquiry and respondents' supported scope objection. Stevens's *Bragdon* concurrence favored affirmance where the lower court had already performed the appropriate inquiry, though it accepted a supported limited remand; his *Haggar* position likewise rejected unnecessary return when the legal and factual predicate was sufficient. Here the original resource remand is already available. The alternative statutory rejection fails for him because it makes the specifically directed integration regulation largely ineffective as applied to the mode of delivering an existing service.

## 3. O'Connor

**Commitment and reach:** Reach question 1, including the minimum defense standard needed to answer what Title II compels. Correct the defense-related component while sustaining the bounded integration claim. Do not adjudicate §5 or determine the January judgment on independent review.

**Sufficient ground and support:** Sections 12132 and 12134 authorize an integrated mode of providing existing services, subject to qualification and the express fundamental-alteration limit. O'Connor's *Yeskey* opinion rejects an institutional exception but emphatically separates coverage from individual entitlement. Her *Cedar Rapids* joins enforce a specified service duty without adding a cost exception absent from that statute; here the operative regulation expressly contains the defense. Her *Heller* joins distinguish the actual confinement disadvantage from legitimate professional and family participation. *Auer* and her *Haggar* joins require examination of real delegated authority and application, not automatic acceptance of a policy preference.

**Application and remedy:** Professional agreement and consent permit the bounded integration determination without reexamining every earlier medical episode. The defense must allow demonstrated effects on the State's entire relevant service responsibility, including patients who still require institutions and legitimate community-placement priorities. Paragraphs 46–48 repeatedly measure the two plaintiffs' additional expenditures against the total mental-health budget. Although the circuit permits other relevant factors, that formulation does not adequately state that implementing the requested modification may affect the distribution and nature of services independently of the size of its isolated bill. The correction targets that legal formulation. The October order demonstrates the live interpretive dispute but supplies neither appellate error by itself nor an independently reviewed judgment.

**Exact joins and barriers:** Joins Coverage, Integration duty, Statutory-regulatory ground, Individual limit and Resource defense. Joins the Plan example only in its fully conditional form: a genuine implementation plan plus a demonstrated fundamental alteration from disturbing its lawful implementation. No blanket waiting-list immunity, automatic priority for institutions, exhaustion duty, numerical threshold or compulsory plan-making procedure. Will not join reasoning that clinical suitability alone makes placement immediately due, that community cost savings necessarily become available upon transferring these two patients, or that all appropriate institutional care carries a legal presumption of wrongdoing.

**Supported alternatives and strongest counterarguments:** Preserving the existing remand is substantial because its factors are nonexclusive and defense application is separately appealed. O'Connor nevertheless treats the content of a statutory compulsion as inseparable from its express exception and limits the correction to that content. Her statutory-boundary dissent in *NFFE* gives weight to what a regulatory regime actually requires, rather than resolving its consequences by labels. Petitioners' no-discrimination construction would protect allocation choice more completely, but the directed integration regulation and the specific circumstances here supply the legal obligation missing from the broader regime she rejected in *NFFE*. Her *Bragdon* disability dissent supplies no basis to deny the accepted disability premise here or to replace objective medical assessment with administrative preference.

## 4. Scalia

**Commitment and reach:** Reach question 1 and reject the statutory basis. Reverse the integration-liability ruling on the theory that medical appropriateness of another setting establishes the required discrimination; vacate the dependent statutory remedial affirmance and remand with the unresolved claims preserved. The resource-defense application is unnecessary under this ground.

**Sufficient ground and support:** Section 12132 requires exclusion, denial of benefits or discrimination by reason of disability. It does not turn every appropriate alternative way of supplying disability services into a benefit that must be supplied. *Choate* and *Traynor* preserve the distinction between nondiscriminatory access and an asserted entitlement to a different or expanded benefit. Scalia accepts *Yeskey*'s coverage holding and its separate liability requirements. His *NFFE* join and *Haggar* joins require identification of the statutory duty before deference, while his *Bragdon* writing insists on keeping separate statutory elements separate even where impairment and significant personal interests are acknowledged.

**Treatment of the hardest contrary authority:** Section 12134(b) and the Part 41 integration language are substantial legal text, not merely aspirational history. For Scalia they direct administration of services consistently with the nondiscrimination obligation; they do not remove that predicate or unambiguously require the State to expand residential community capacity whenever another setting could work. The integration regulation remains operative against discriminatory segregation in administering an available service, including discrimination within a program serving only disabled people. Its use here to derive liability simply from medically unnecessary institutional residence exceeds that implementing function. *Auer*'s rule for a permissible interpretation cannot supply authority beyond the statute. The objection is substantive statutory fit, not the United States' amicus format.

**Application and remedy:** The appellate inference in paragraph 4—that disability explains confinement and therefore establishes the disputed discrimination element—is insufficient. The later professional agreement establishes suitability; it does not itself establish the different statutory proposition. Scalia does not find Georgia's allocation actually evenhanded, its list legitimate, or its resources insufficient: those contested descriptions are unnecessary to rejecting the asserted sufficient ground. Nominally voluntary status alone also does not defeat a claim. His disposition removes the integration theory as the basis for this judgment without deciding a different preserved claim or the legality of confinement under other law.

**Exact joins and barriers:** Joins Coverage and the common threshold/scope reservations. Accepts the choice regulation and that medically necessary institutional care is not categorically prohibited, but does not join those propositions as elements of a newly adopted affirmative integration duty. Does not join Integration duty, its affirmative Statutory-regulatory ground, or either integration-supporting judgment. No categorical nondisabled-comparator requirement: differential treatment among persons with disabilities can violate Title II. No holding that only animus counts. Discussion of systemwide cost or a plan would be conditional analysis, not a join in the Court's affirmative defense framework or its remand ground.

**Supported alternative and strongest counterargument:** Enforce the integration regulation under Congress's express consistency instruction, confining it to existing services and protecting resource allocation through fundamental alteration. This is the strongest competing path and answers much of the benefit-expansion concern. Scalia's *Cedar Rapids* join shows that he will enforce a costly expressly defined service duty with adequate notice. He distinguishes that affirmative IDEA obligation, the precise nursing category and *Tatro* from an ADA discrimination prohibition whose liability predicate he finds unsatisfied. The statutory rejection cannot rest simply on State sovereignty, general expense, or disagreement with deinstitutionalization as a policy. No constitutional invalidity is decided.

## 5. Kennedy

**Commitment and reach:** Reach the statutory question; correct the defense-related component. Sustain only the bounded existing-service integration rule and the relevant suitability determination, with a distinct explanation of the statutory predicate and resource limit if the lead reasoning goes beyond them.

**Sufficient ground and support:** The existing program supplies both institutional and community services. For persons whom the State's professionals find appropriately served in the community and who seek that setting, withholding a reasonable means of integrated access may violate Title II under the authorized regulation. Kennedy's *Yeskey* join separates coverage from entitlement; his *Auer* opinion, *NFFE* join and *Haggar* joins recognize actual delegated implementing authority within judicially enforced limits. His *Bragdon* opinion requires actual individual proof and objective professional assessment. These support a legal judgment about the demonstrated service arrangement, not a universal inference from disability to unlawful segregation.

**Application and remedy:** The minimum resource-defense rule is fairly included in deciding whether the State must provide community services even when appropriate institutional treatment is possible. A prima facie integration ruling cannot leave the legally relevant program undefined. Correct the reviewed additional-expenditure formulation to permit proof about maintaining a workable range of services and the actual effect of the modification on other recipients; decide no ultimate defense fact. The State's responsibilities to others do not create an unreviewable professional or budgetary veto. Kennedy's *Cedar Rapids* dissent preserves the need for an actual statutory obligation and clear notice; here the specific integration direction supports a qualified obligation, not every level of community care on demand.

**Exact joins and barriers:** Joins Coverage, the Integration duty as expressly limited in section 1, the Statutory-regulatory ground, Individual limit and Resource defense. Does not join a sufficient-ground proposition that all avoidable institutionalization inherently expresses a stereotype or that disability-caused confinement alone proves discrimination. Social isolation may explain the statute's purpose and the concrete disadvantage without becoming an irrebuttable factual characterization of every institution. Will join the proposition that an actual plan and priorities can be relevant proof; reserves the Plan example as a freestanding safe harbor or complete test for what all waiting persons must accept. A lead opinion adopting the example only as an illustration of the existing demonstration requirement is compatible with his join. No heightened constitutional scrutiny or constitutional right to the preferred treatment setting.

**Supported alternatives and strongest counterarguments:** Statutory rejection avoids imposing new benefits and accords with the notice concern in his *Cedar Rapids* dissent. It gives insufficient effect here to Congress's explicit integration-regulation instruction and the State's existing integrated service program. Preserving the existing remand better respects the separate appeal and the circuit's nonexclusive factors; Kennedy's objection is that the original operative expenditure comparison needs correction before it is reaffirmed as the governing legal measure. The remand is confined to that question, not a general retrial of professional suitability. His *Heller* positions support respect for legitimate differences and professional functions without importing rational-basis constitutional review as the ADA liability standard.

## 6. Souter

**Commitment and reach:** Reach question 1; preserve the reviewed remand. Affirm the bounded integration determination and existing defense remand. Reserve precise plan sufficiency and the adjudication of later remedial proceedings.

**Sufficient ground and support:** Title II's enacted structure and §12134's specified regulatory baseline make unnecessary segregated delivery of existing disability services a cognizable discrimination claim. *Choate* distinguishes absence of animus from absence of discrimination and preserves meaningful access without promising adequate medical provision generally. Souter's *Heller* opinion focuses on the concrete disadvantage of confinement and the actual justification offered, while preserving lawful State choices on remedy. His *Yeskey*, *Auer*, *Bragdon*, *Cedar Rapids*, *NFFE* and *Haggar* joins support the distinctions between coverage, statutory obligation, delegated implementation, individual proof and appropriate judicial remedy.

**Application and remedy:** The qualified, supported placement finding is sufficient for the statutory question; no need exists to choose between the parties' complete historical accounts of treatment administration. The original appellate judgment already rejected a simplistic average-cost finding and remanded for budget demands, overhead, transfer authority and other relevant factors. Its refusal to decide a classwide deinstitutionalization suit is a scope reservation, not necessarily an instruction to disregard every proved effect on others. Preserve that legal remand and leave the actual later restriction and defense ruling for their proper appeal.

**Exact joins and barriers:** Joins Coverage, Integration duty, Statutory-regulatory ground, Individual limit and Resource defense. Will join an explanation that genuinely supported effects on other eligible people are relevant under the existing defense. Does not join vacatur based on an inference that the later district order was required by the appellate mandate. Does not adopt the Plan example as a newly controlling defense rule; may recognize a particular plan as evidence without determining its sufficiency in advance. Rejects indefinite postponement based solely on assertions that many others have comparable claims, and rejects an automatic immediate-placement rule based solely on professional approval.

**Supported alternatives and strongest counterarguments:** A corrected defense remand could prevent cumulative obligations from overwhelming a service system even if each individual cost appears small. That is a valid objection to an isolated-cost rule. Souter's answer is that the judgment actually reviewed permits consideration of the agency's operations and other relevant factors; the sharper evidentiary exclusion belongs to a later order. His *Bragdon* join demonstrates willingness to correct an actual legal-source error but not to reopen an ungranted whole-record question. Statutory rejection, meanwhile, fails to explain the distinct work done by Congress's selected integration regulation and mistakes a change in the mode of providing an existing service for an unrestricted adequacy claim.

## 7. Thomas

**Commitment and reach:** Reach question 1 and reject the statutory basis. Reverse the statutory integration determination, vacate its dependent remedial affirmance, and remand without deciding the unresolved constitutional claims. No adjudication of the resource defense is necessary to that result.

**Sufficient ground and support:** The discrimination prohibition must supply a legal reason for treating a service allocation as unlawful. Neither the fact that only disabled people need the program nor the clinical possibility of another setting alone establishes that reason. *Choate*, *Traynor* and *Davis* distinguish access and reasonable accommodation from a duty to provide a different substantive benefit. Thomas's *Cedar Rapids* dissent addresses the character of the statutory service and clear notice before any question of affordability. His *NFFE*, *Haggar*, *Yeskey* and *Bragdon* positions likewise maintain the separation of enacted scope, individual statutory elements and implementing discretion.

**Regulation and constitutional avoidance:** Section 12134's instruction is binding within Title II, but consistency with prior coordination regulations does not authorize the Attorney General to dispense with §12132's discrimination requirement. Thomas would reject the proposed application of the integration rule insofar as it makes suitable alternative treatment, without the required discrimination, sufficient for liability. That does not invalidate every operation of the regulation or eliminate discrimination claims in programs serving only disabled persons. *Auer* supplies no power to enlarge the statute, and *Haggar* demands judicial enforcement of that boundary. *Gregory* and *Pennhurst* reinforce caution about converting this language into a new State treatment entitlement; the statutory construction is sufficient without a holding that Title II is unconstitutional, that it is solely Spending Clause legislation, or that any particular immunity applies.

**Application and remedy:** No finding of a sufficient State plan or inadequate resources is required. The State's concession about the two plaintiffs' incremental expenditures therefore does not resolve his statutory objection. He accepts the record's professionally appropriate placement premise for purposes of the legal question and does not order reexamination of current medical conditions. The statutory ground falls; other legal bases and the proper handling of ongoing relief remain for ordinary lower proceedings.

**Exact joins and barriers:** Joins Coverage and the threshold/scope reservations. Accepts the choice provision and the absence of an absolute discharge requirement. Does not join Integration duty, the affirmative Statutory-regulatory ground, either integration-supporting disposition, or the affirmative defense framework as a holding. Shares Scalia's rejection of the particular statutory inference and of a mandatory nondisabled-comparator premise. Their common judgment does not require Thomas to adopt a separate factual finding of neutral allocation or Scalia to adopt a freestanding clear-notice holding.

**Supported alternative and strongest counterargument:** Congress expressly identified segregation and directed regulations consistent with a pre-existing integrated-setting command; enforcing that command subject to fundamental alteration can be viewed as administering existing services rather than creating benefits. That is the principal difficulty for his construction. Thomas reads the direction together with, and subordinate to, the enacted discrimination predicate. His *Cedar Rapids* dissent supplies a concrete commitment to examining the nature and clarity of the claimed service duty instead of treating manageable cost as proof of authorization. The affirmative alternative is rejected on that legal ground, not because reasonable accommodation must never cost money or because institutional patients lack statutory protection.

## 8. Ginsburg

**Commitment and reach:** Reach question 1; correct the defense-related component while affirming the bounded integration claim and preserving the relevant suitability determination. Reach only the legal dimensions necessary to make the statutory duty and its express limitation operate together.

**Sufficient ground and support:** The integration regulation implements an express congressional direction against segregated delivery. Equal access can require reasonable change in how a public service is supplied; an identical nondisabled comparator is not indispensable to this claim. Ginsburg's *Yeskey*, *Auer*, *Cedar Rapids* and *Bragdon* joins support that conclusion. Her *Bragdon* concurrence recognizes the effects of disability on ordinary participation while declining to treat a diagnosis or general observation as proof of every individual element. The present rule therefore rests on supported professional suitability, choice and an existing service program, not generalized assumptions about all institutions.

**Application and remedy:** Reject petitioners' complete statutory exemption for the asserted mode of care. Also reject treating the modest cost of two placements as a sufficient answer to every programwide defense. The regulation asks whether the modification fundamentally alters the service, and that inquiry can involve obligations to other recipients even without a class action. Paragraphs 46–48's expenditure-centered formulation should be corrected to state that relation expressly. The remand determines whether the State can demonstrate it and what follows for prospective relief. It neither accepts every asserted aggregate demand nor erases an individual's right because other people also have it.

**Exact joins and barriers:** Joins Coverage, Integration duty, Statutory-regulatory ground, Individual limit and Resource defense. Joins the Plan example as a bounded illustration requiring a proved relationship between actual implementation, lawful priorities and fundamental alteration; does not join a categorical plan safe harbor, list-based exhaustion, an obligation to wait indefinitely, or a presumption that the State has acted reasonably. No finding of animus or proof of a stereotype is an additional element. No agency statement is allowed to establish disputed clinical or financial facts by deference.

**Supported alternatives and strongest counterarguments:** Preserving the remand is supported by its nonexclusive list and by respondents' express scope objection. Ginsburg's *Haggar* separate join with Stevens favored an immediate result when the actual regulatory application was sufficiently established; that position weighs against remand merely for caution. Here, however, the legal significance of effects on other eligible persons remains disputed and no accepted programwide determination resolves it. Her *Bragdon* join supports a limited legal-method correction that leaves application open without commanding further evidence. The statutory duty and its limitation are both fairly encompassed by what question 1 asks the State to do; the later January order remains outside the judgment under review. The no-duty alternative gives inadequate effect to the specific integration direction.

## 9. Breyer

**Commitment and reach:** Reach question 1; correct the defense-related component. Affirm the availability of the bounded integration claim and preserve the supported individual-suitability premise, with a remand allowing a demonstrated programwide fundamental-alteration defense and consequent adjustment of relief.

**Sufficient ground and support:** Congress prescribed nondiscrimination in the administration of public services and specifically directed integration-consistent regulations. The Attorney General's construction gives that scheme practical effect within the reasonable-modification limitation. Breyer's *Yeskey*, *Auer*, *Cedar Rapids*, *NFFE* and *Haggar* joins support enforcing actual authority, respecting a permissible administrative construction and separately examining application. His full *Bragdon* join requires supported individual assessment rather than diagnosis, general risk, or a professional title alone.

**Application and remedy:** Clinical approval and an individual's choice permit this claim, but implementing integrated services can require decisions about transition costs, the availability of suitable providers, continuing institutional needs and priorities among eligible persons. The United States' pages 19–22 articulate those effects without denying the statutory obligation. They belong in the legal meaning of the defense, which cannot be reduced to the two plaintiffs' additional expenditure divided by the total budget. Correct that formulation in the reviewed judgment; do not decide Georgia's proof, convert asserted demand into demonstrated cost, or treat a federal waiver authorization as already funded State capacity.

**Exact joins and barriers:** Joins Coverage, Integration duty, Statutory-regulatory ground, Individual limit, Resource defense and the fully conditional Plan example. The plan illustration is an application of the regulation, not a new administrative procedure or categorical exception. It must be supported, directed toward systematic prompt integration and actually impaired by the proposed departure to the degree the regulation requires. An assertion of planning or an inert queue is insufficient. No categorical demand for immediate placement, universal new program, hospital closure, permanent institutional preference or fixed numerical cost standard.

**Supported alternatives and strongest counterarguments:** Preserving the original remand is a serious possibility because it already allows other relevant factors; Breyer's *Bragdon* concurrence preferred affirmance after a thorough lower-court review. Here the operating defense standard, and hence the content of the affirmative obligation, remains in dispute. The requested correction supplies a substantive limitation missing from an isolated-expenditure account without reaching the later judgment. The statutory-rejection path preserves administrative flexibility but discounts Congress's particular integration command. The defense makes that command workable while retaining actual proof; administrative inconvenience alone cannot establish the exception.

## 10. Cross-cutting adversarial limits and handoff instructions

**Scope fork remains real.** Stevens and Souter preserve the appellate resource remand because its express nonexclusive factors can accommodate lawful programwide proof and because the later restriction is separately appealed. O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer instead correct the original expenditure formulation as part of defining what question 1 compels. The latter position may not be implemented by saying the appellate court expressly excluded all evidence about others: it did not. Its ground is the inadequacy of the operative expenditure comparison, read with paragraphs 43, 46–48 and footnote 10, despite the nonexclusive factors. The October order illustrates the dispute; it is not a substitute for identifying that appellate legal issue. The statutory-rejection commitments rest on a different ground and are not affirmative joins in either resource remedy.

**No imported medical or resource finding.** The lower court's supported relevant-time suitability determination is not a finding of uninterrupted agreement, suitability for any placement, or current clinical condition. Its funding figures retain their dates. JA159's concession concerns the plaintiffs viewed alone; it neither waives the wider defense nor proves that defense. The broader State affidavit remains evidence and estimate, not a Supreme Court finding. The provider's cost account is not complete marginal accounting. No commitment needs a finding that Georgia maintained a legally sufficient placement plan, moved a queue at a particular pace, or had exhausted all lawful resources.

**Legal theory is distinct from factual characterization.** The affirmative integration commitments identify the imposed segregated mode of receiving an existing service as the statutory disadvantage; they do not rely on disability's causal role in needing treatment alone. The statutory-rejection commitments do not adopt the State's disputed claim that all allocation here was neutral and voluntary. Neither side may convert an advocate's characterization into a found fact. *Youngberg*'s constitutional professional-judgment rule does not by itself decide the statutory integration duty, and *Heller* supplies no general placement entitlement or new scrutiny tier.

**Joins and future handling.** Each stated disposition and proposition boundary is frozen independently. Compatible legal language may be assembled without fictional exchanges. A different disposition, a plan-based conclusive defense, a new burden, a medical veto, or a separate constitutional holding requires an actual supported change through the authorized stages. No author, opinion form, final coalition, Court count, or historical comparison is selected here. These commitments do not authorize aggregating dissents' conditional resource observations with affirmative integration reasoning to create a Court holding.
