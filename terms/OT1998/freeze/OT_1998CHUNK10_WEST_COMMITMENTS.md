## Commitments at a glance

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse the Seventh Circuit's no-administrative-power premise and remand the compensatory-damages component for consideration of properly presented actual-exhaustion and other remaining arguments. They do not direct reinstatement of the district court's dismissal. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm that component on the ground that Congress did not authorize an administrative compensatory award detached from the judicial-stage conditions of §1981a. No Justice awards damages or decides Gibson's disputed compliance, counseling, injury or amount.

## Governing components and alternatives

**Power and consent.** Path A reads §717(b)'s authority to enforce federal nondiscrimination through appropriate remedies together with §1981a's express addition of compensatory recovery for intentional §717 discrimination. The existing administrative delegation carries the newly enacted category; enumerated hiring and backpay are illustrative. This is enacted consent, not an inference from discrimination's wrongfulness, Franklin's default remedies, or administrative convenience. The strongest objection is that §1981a(a)(1) says action and §1981a(c) permits any party a jury; the agency cannot insist on judicial review of an adverse administrative award. Path A answers that §717 distinguishes the administrative enforcement stage from the employee's civil action, while the jury provision regulates an available judicial trial. The action-or-proceeding definition supports the coordinated statutory setting but does not alone confer power. A federal employing agency has no free-standing jury right at every administrative step merely because either litigating party may elect a jury in a civil action.

Path B reads the action language and any-party jury condition as limits on the newly authorized Treasury remedy, which the general earlier delegation does not unmistakably displace. The strongest objection is that this creates a remedial split despite an expressly compensable federal employment wrong and an existing broad administrative remedial delegation. Path B responds that compensation remains available in court; matching administrative and judicial relief is not mandatory when Congress conditions new liability. It does not deny §717 coverage or judicial damages consent. Lane supplies a rule requiring particular-remedy clarity, not an already-decided answer about these words.

**Jury, damages and precedent.** Both paths preserve Landgraf's linkage between available §102 damages and the jury election, its prohibition on disclosure of caps, and its separate treatment of compensation and §706(g) relief. Neither treats this 1992 conduct as preenactment. Both preserve intentional discrimination, the §1981 condition, statutory caps, proof of compensable injury, and the governmental punitive-damages exclusion. Path A construes the election's institutional setting; Path B regards that setting as a restriction on administrative power. Their agreement on the statutory conditions does not erase this disagreement.

**Exhaustion, alternate grounds and remedy.** If A prevails, McCarthy's compensation-incapable-process premise cannot by itself sustain the Seventh Circuit's judgment. That does not establish nonexhaustion. A remand may address Gibson's contention that factual presentation and cooperation exhausted the discrimination claim, his reported cash-award request, counseling obligations, and any properly preserved equitable response. The alternative of affirming immediately on those grounds faces missing operative regulatory verification and contested factual/preservation predicates. The alternative of directing dismissal faces the same defects from the other direction. Love cautions against inappropriate technicalities; Zipes and Irwin do not establish every claimed estoppel exception; Christianson does not prove preservation. A remand may leave each issue open without adopting the brief's regulatory account, shifting a burden, or deciding government estoppel.

If B prevails, the no-power ground defeats the asserted requirement to obtain this unavailable administrative award; it does not make all federal employment exhaustion optional. The reinstated damages claim proceeds subject to otherwise applicable law, proof and defenses. Gibson still receives no award from this decision. Frontpay, transfer, completed promotion/backpay enforcement and the retained fee matter are outside the reviewed component.

## Proposed proposition coalitions and authority limits

- Five non-Stone direct rationale commitments support administrative power under the coordinated statutes: Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. They also support rejecting the no-power premise and bounded remand. This establishes no final opinion assignment or Stone join.
- Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support the contrary power construction and affirmance of the reviewed component. Their agreement that preserved alternatives should not be extinguished if A governs is a conditional remedial agreement, not a reversal vote or a join in A.
- No eight-Justice merits coalition on actual exhaustion, estoppel, regulatory breach or damages entitlement is asserted. All eight decline to decide those unproved predicates in this handoff, for the component-specific reasons above.
- Lane and Blue Fox remain intact. Landgraf's substantive conditions remain intact; the proposed administrative-power holding resolves a distinct institutional question. Neither Franklin nor McCarthy supplies an additional waiver or abolishes statutory exhaustion.
