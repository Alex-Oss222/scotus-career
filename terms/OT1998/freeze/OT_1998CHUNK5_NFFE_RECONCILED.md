## Reconciled dispositions

Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would **vacate the Fourth Circuit judgment in both dockets and remand through that court to the Authority for reconsideration**, without directing present enforcement of the Survey order. They conclude that the statute leaves the timing and extent of the relevant midterm bargaining duties, and the related duty to negotiate an advance clause, to the Authority's reasonable construction within the chapter's limits. Neither the Fourth Circuit's categorical prohibition nor a judicially compelled universal statutory duty supplies the governing premise.

O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would **affirm in both dockets**. They read the agreement-directed bargaining provisions, the separately structured continuing obligations and the public impasse system as foreclosing compulsory bargaining over this general advance commitment. They preserve narrower clauses and voluntary particular bargaining; they do not infer that every statutory omission is a prohibition.

These five/three Associate positions replace the provisional three-enforcement/two-return/three-affirm distribution. The changes are source-supported corrections of provisional inferences after comparison, not a fictional circulation, a forced arithmetic compromise or a new historical fact. Each Justice's ground is explained below. No complete-Court disposition or tally is declared here.

## Common five-Associate rule and remand justification

The statutory definition of an agreement is not confined expressly to a basic term contract, and a midterm agreement can be an agreement reached through collective bargaining. The direction to negotiate toward an agreement and the reasonable-times/frequency requirements do not unambiguously prescribe a comprehensive continuing duty either. Section 7106 defines management rights and exceptions principally by subject; its limited midterm implications do not necessarily establish a prohibition on all other midterm subjects. In particular, the agency-election condition in subsection (b)(1) is not transferred to (b)(2) or (3). Section 7105's actual administrative responsibilities provide authority to resolve the remaining question within the enacted scheme.

The efficiency instruction and conditional Panel powers matter to that choice. They do not make perpetual bargaining, strategic withholding or imposition of this clause established facts. The contrary concerns about responding to newly arising workplace needs likewise do not prove a universal duty. The Authority must explain its statutory construction and policy application within the actual bargaining, management, waiver and consistency limits. Judicial review remains under section 7123(c) and section 706. No free-standing judicial cost test, universal borrowed private-sector rule, mandatory factor checklist or required new factfinding procedure is created.

**Classification of the remand:** This is a return for administrative reconsideration after correction of a governing legal premise. It is not an evidentiary remand, a finding of absent agency reasons, punishment for changing interpretation, or a rule that every ambiguity requires remand. The independent alternative remains genuine and may be adopted again through a reasoned application of the corrected framework. But the current alternative mostly says that contractual rights may exceed statutory entitlements and that SSA did not decide negotiability. That explains why a no-entitlement premise is not a complete legal prohibition; it does not settle the distinct discretionary relationship between a general duty, an advance clause, agreement repose, waiver and the public impasse system once statutory compulsion is removed. The five would let the agency exercise that responsibility before directing enforcement.

MCI preserves actual statutory boundaries and a discernible agency ground; it does not make every discernible alternative automatically sufficient after a material legal premise is corrected. NationsBank sustained a reasoned approval within actual statutory discretion; it does not require a reviewing court to treat an assumption of statutory necessity as an independent discretionary choice. Current AT&T distinguishes implementing power from validity of the particular duty and returns unresolved rules under the correct allocation. Your Home separately establishes statutory meaning before additional regulatory support. Those rules leave the historically material remand premise intact for each of the five; none mandates either enforcement or a remand merely by analogy.

The no-entitlement/negotiability distinction is not rejected as a logical proposition. The five reject the circuit's conclusion that no statutory entitlement automatically forecloses the contractual undertaking, while preserving Authority consideration of all actual constraints. The three dissenters accept that abstract distinction but conclude that this comprehensive commitment violates a structural limit, rather than filling ordinary lawful agency discretion. That is their sufficient section 7117 inconsistency ground. They do not treat section 7103(a)(14)(C)'s specifically-provided-for exclusion as an automatic additional ground based on statutory silence.

**O'Connor's additional deference position:** The exact current [Good Samaritan entry](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK5_NFFE_GOOD_SAMARITAN.md), which O'Connor joined, permits a reduction in the weight of a changed agency view while forbidding automatic disqualification of a considered, otherwise permissible construction. That rule leaves room for her additional objection: the court-induced reversal, coupled with the present order's continued reliance on an asserted statutory mandate and its limited alternative explanation, does not warrant treating the current interpretation as the Authority's independently reasoned choice. She would examine the statutory text and structure without assuming the judicially prompted change supplies its own justification and would again reject the general compulsory regime and this clause. Her conclusion rests on the reasons and asserted statutory setting, not change alone. This is an additional reason against deference to the present construction, not a new rule requiring courts always to choose their preferred reading whenever an agency changes position, and not a displacement of Chevron. Her statutory ground remains independently sufficient. Scalia and Thomas do not join this separate deference position; their Part I-equivalent statutory ground supplies their affirmance. No material departure from the historical scope of their agreement is asserted.

## Exact scope, remedy and compatibility

Both statutory questions are presented and were actual agency grounds. Not renewing a foreclosed argument before the Fourth Circuit does not turn the administrative ground into a concession. The five's examination supplies the premise necessary for meaningful review of the clause; it imposes neither a universal duty nor universal immunity from midterm bargaining. The reported renegotiation does not establish mootness, replacement contract terms, settlement or waiver. No section 7123(c) agency-presentation defect is found.

On the five-Associate disposition, the Fourth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the cases return through it for Authority reconsideration. The Court does **not** order enforcement of the existing Survey order, impose a clause, require agreement or concessions, award retrospective bargaining effect, direct the Panel to act, or adjudicate an actual impasse award. No Supreme Court mandate newly starts the original administrative posting/reporting periods. Their disposition is not an unexplained blanket stay of any order: existing and future procedural effect is handled in the statutory channel under the vacatur/remand, without inventing an unrecorded stay or new deadline. The Authority may reach the same negotiability conclusion under the correct framework if lawfully reasoned; the Court does not compel that outcome.

On the three-Associate disposition, the Fourth Circuit's refusal of enforcement stands under their structural interpretation. They do not reopen Interior's dismissal, the uncharged agency-head disapproval or unrelated complaints. All eight preserve those exclusions on either disposition.

Any adopted future midterm duty remains subject to negotiable-subject restrictions, matters covered by the agreement, applicable lawful waiver, management rights and federal-law consistency. The clause's reference to uncovered negotiable matters does not silently adjudicate every waiver question. The Authority's reported covered-matter branches and fully explored-subject rules remain descriptions of its existing framework; no hypothetical proposal is found covered, waived or outside management authority. Agreed zipper clauses, compulsory negotiation of a zipper clause and compulsory acceptance at impasse remain distinct. Universal availability of an employer-imposed zipper clause cannot be used as an established cure.

All five can join the complete ambiguity/delegation and corrected-framework remand propositions. The three do not join those propositions merely because they agree that bargaining is not acceptance. Common disapproval of compelled acceptance supplies no majority rationale for immediate enforcement. No separate author is selected, no opinion-form requirement is imported from history, and no Court majority is manufactured by summing the former incompatible dispositions.

**Historical departure:** None remains in the reconciled Associate judgment positions. The five revisions correct unsupported provisional departures because no material statutory, factual, procedural, remedial or current-law premise distinguishes the positions historically taken. The original-order recovery is improved evidence of the same premise, not a changed premise. Fuller protection of the Interior dismissal and the exact ancillary-remedy limits preserves the record rather than altering the historical statutory question.
