## Separable issues and enhanced alternatives

### 1. Federal-officer removal under§1442(a)(3)

The statute permits removal by an officer of the federal courts “for any act under color of office or in the performance of his duties.” Mesa requires a colorable federal defense as well as the office/duty nexus. Willingham's protective-forum purpose permits deciding that defense federally without demanding that it ultimately prevail. The Government's separate nexus objection must be answered even if§1341 does not bar the action.

Path A: removal is authorized. The collection demand expressly taxes compensation for the judges' performance of judicial work within the County, and the ordinance describes doing that work without payment as unlawful. Their federal defense claims that the County thereby taxes or conditions their judicial functions. That is a colorable official-duty immunity claim connected to the activity generating this collection suit; it is not federal employment alone. The defense may fail when the levy is characterized on the merits as compensation taxation, without undoing jurisdiction. The County cannot compel an officer to defeat the very asserted federal immunity before obtaining the federal forum designed to determine it.

Strongest objection: the immediate wrong sued upon is personal nonpayment of tax, not adjudication or any official act; nearly every federal employee's ordinary personal liability can be traced to salary, and colorability cannot erase the independent nexus. Response for A: the particular occupational levy and its asserted condition on performing official work create the needed connection here. This is not a categorical rule that all salary-funded purchases, property taxes or personal debts of federal officers are removable. The connection is to the performance the ordinance directly selects as its taxable occupation, not simply the source of funds available to satisfy a debt.

Path B: no statutory nexus; vacate the federal judgment and return the collection suits to state court without tax merits. Judges may have a colorable federal defense, but refusing a personal tax payment is not an act under color of judicial office. The statutory pre-2011 language does not say “related to”; protective purpose cannot replace “for any act.” The Government's separate objection is therefore sufficient even though it agrees that§1341 does not itself bar a defensive collection suit.

Strongest objection: the County's own ordinance targets working in the judicial office, so the official acts are the very taxable activity. Response for B: tax liability's compensation measure does not convert payment into official adjudicative duty. The relevant defendant conduct remains a personal refusal to satisfy a generally imposed levy. This rule need not require success on the immunity defense, but it insists on the separate causal statutory nexus. An actual effort to prevent a judge from sitting could present a different case; none is established.

### 2. Tax Injunction Act

Path A, adopted by the six Associates reaching federal merits:§1341 does not bar adjudication of this collector-initiated action defensively removed under§1442. The County seeks a collection judgment; federal adjudication of the defendants' federal defense is not the judges' independent suit to enjoin, suspend or restrain tax administration. Removal changes the tribunal for that action. Farm Credit's affirmative injunction/declaration and borrowed sovereign exception do not compel the result here. A protective federal-officer forum and ordinary adjudication of the collector's own claim can coexist with§1341's prohibition on taxpayer-initiated federal restraints.

Strongest objection: a judgment of nonliability prevents collection as effectively as an injunction, and Farm Credit extends the bar beyond formal labels to declarations; state courts can hear federal defenses. Response for A: the relief and posture remain materially distinct. Treating any defense that defeats a collector as prohibited “restraint” would undermine the expressly protective removal setting without textual direction. This does not grant every federal worker a freestanding tax injunction or declare all state remedies inadequate. No general sovereign exception for judges is necessary, and no automatic inference follows from the United States' amicus appearance.

Path B:§1341 bars a federal outcome restraining collection even when a collector initially chose the state court. That would require proper treatment of the special removal statute, the actual relief requested and the plain/speedy/efficient state-remedy condition. It cannot follow merely from the word “tax” or from a conceded remedy in a different case. The six reaching the issue reject B for the stated statutory/posture reasons, without inventing a present Alabama-remedy adequacy finding. Scalia/Thomas resolve removal under issue1 first and reserve a holding on§1341 rather than supply an advisory second jurisdictional ground.

### 3. Occupational label, actual operation and federal salary-tax consent

Path A: reject the categorical intergovernmental-immunity ruling based on labeling this levy a license on federal judicial functions. Graves and§111 permit a duly authorized, nondiscriminatory tax on federal pay/compensation; Davis preserves the source-discrimination condition. Ordinance1120 imposes a .005 charge measured by compensation for work performed within the County, with apportionment outside. It includes federal, state and local officeholders. The record establishes a revenue collection demand, not a county permission system actually preventing federal adjudication. An occupational label or provision declaring unpaid work unlawful cannot by itself determine the federal characterization. In substance the challenged demand operates as taxation of compensation within§111's subject matter, subject to the independent nondiscrimination and constitutional qualifications.

Strongest objection: the ordinance expressly makes performance of work unlawful without payment, taxes entitlement to compensation and fully exempts holders of certain state licenses. A State cannot condition federal judicial acts on county permission merely by calculating the price as salary. Response for A: the Court does not approve an actual licensing veto. It decides the compensation levy in the present collection suit by substance and operation. The exemption comparison may defeat consent if it is discriminatory, but its existence does not make every occupation-based compensation tax a forbidden license on government. The present conclusion does not rely on treating federal judges as independent contractors under Blaze; Blaze supplies the distinction between legal operation and indirect economic burden, with its actual-instrumentality reservation intact.

Path B: affirm the categorical immunity judgment because the tax legally conditions performance of federal judicial duties rather than taxing salary received. Strongest objection: this would allow state-law nomenclature and ordinary nonpayment language to override Congress's compensation-tax consent regardless of the actual revenue operation. The six merits participants reject B on this record, while preserving any genuinely coercive restriction on judicial sitting for a case that establishes it. The Buck Act's federal-area provisions supply no necessary independent permission to regulate judicial functions;§111 is the relevant consent provision and its conditions remain.

### 4. Source discrimination, Compensation Clause and remand

Rejecting the asserted occupational-license immunity does not establish ultimate liability. Section111 expressly conditions consent on no discrimination because of federal source. The ordinance exempts persons paying specified state privilege/license/occupational taxes. The relevant classes, actual other tax burdens and significant differences require examination; inclusion of state judges and payment by other federal employees do not conclusively answer every exemption comparison.

Path A, adopted by all six merits participants: reverse/vacate the categorical intergovernmental-immunity basis and remand for proper resolution of preserved independent defenses, including source discrimination and the district court's distinct ArticleIII Compensation Clause ground not reached by the en banc appellate court. No finding that every exemption is nondiscriminatory, that a judge actually holds a state license, or that alternative burdens are equivalent is supplied. Davis gives the necessary significant-differences inquiry; Stevens's different comparison-class analysis remains available as a separate view, but it does not turn a disputed comparison into an established fact. ArticleIII's compensation protection must be examined separately from ordinary federal tax immunity;§111's general consent alone does not adjudicate that constitutional ground.

Strongest objection: existing stipulations and nationwide salary-tax principles may permit final resolution now, sparing further proceedings. Response: the appealed categorical ground does not settle the distinct defenses and the clean source packet does not establish the necessary exemption comparison or a complete adjudicated Compensation Clause rationale. The lawful narrow disposition corrects the legal characterization while leaving those actually preserved questions for proper lower consideration. It requires no new evidence unless ordinary procedure permits it and predicts no defense's success.

Path B: affirm on a demonstrated discriminatory exemption or independently sufficient Compensation Clause barrier, or reverse outright and order collection after rejecting all defenses. Those are lawful possibilities on a sufficient record and legal determination, but neither is supported here as an entered merits resolution. The Court does not infer universal invalidity from the existence of exemptions or universal validity from their state-law title. No tax amount, apportionment calculation, coercive work restraint or liability judgment is directed.

## Coalition, formal judgment and separate function

Six Associates—Stevens/O’Connor/Kennedy/Souter/Ginsburg/Breyer—support federal-officer removal, no§1341 bar in this posture, rejection of the categorical occupational-license immunity rationale, and a remand preserving the unresolved discrimination and Compensation Clause defenses. Scalia/Thomas would vacate the federal judgment for lack of§1442(a)(3) nexus and return the collection cases to state court, reaching no tax merits. These distinct reasons for disturbing the lower judgment cannot be combined into eight merits votes.

The six's disposition leaves the federal forum available, reverses the categorical immunity ground under review and remands for further proceedings on the preserved independent grounds. It does not direct entry of a county collection judgment, calculate taxes, find any remedy inadequate, require an evidentiary hearing or permit the County to stop the judges from performing duties. The two's proposed jurisdictional vacatur leaves every tax defense for the proper state proceeding. No opinion author is assigned.

A separate Scalia/Thomas position has the material function of explaining the official-act-nexus disagreement and refusal to reach merits. Within the six, different Davis comparison approaches do not require a separate writing where the opinion reserves application. If later assembly purports to decide the exemption comparison, a fresh supported affected model is necessary; this freeze contains no provisional concession that the tax is nondiscriminatory. No separate constitutional holding is implied by the remand.
