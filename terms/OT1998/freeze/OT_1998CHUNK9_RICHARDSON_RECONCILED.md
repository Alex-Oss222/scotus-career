## Ginsburg: substantive reconciliation of the unmatched statutory vote

The provisional opinion classified the individual violations as elements using Ginsburg's Gaudin, Rutledge and Jones joins, then invoked her Roy agreement with Scalia against appellate replacement of actual jury findings. These authorities support important jury protections. They do not, without a further premise, resolve whether the CCE statute makes the constituent violations elements or means of establishing an enterprise's continuing course of criminal activity.

Her historical Richardson dissent join addresses that antecedent question directly. Its sufficient ground is that Congress defined the continuing series as a characteristic of the criminal enterprise; the supervisory, concert and substantial-income requirements channel the jury's inquiry toward that same enterprise. Jurors must agree on the required series and other elements, but the transactions may supply alternative means of proving its continuing character. This construction preserves Gaudin's protection of actual elements. It does not rest on a judge finding an element or on an appellate court supplying a missing finding after instructional error. The dissent's reliance on the relation among the statutory requirements is present in the unchanged statute and neutral record.

The materially different simulated authorities were considered rather than treating history as conclusive. Ginsburg's Almendarez-Torres join now treats the prior aggravated-felony fact as an aggravated reentry element; Jones applies that statutory analogue and contains her separate constitutional agreement concerning a fact indispensable to a greater statutory maximum. But those decisions distinguish identification of a punishment-authorizing element from its subsequent jury protections. They do not establish that every possible evidentiary route to a concededly necessary element is another element. A CCE series found unanimously as an enterprise characteristic can remain an element under that distinction. Rutledge separately identifies the series and agreement requirements while expressly reserving substantive-predicate questions. Her Monge position addresses an additional personal-conduct qualification used for recidivist punishment, not alternative evidence of a single ongoing enterprise element. None displaces the specific statutory relationship on which her Richardson dissent rests.

Roy changes the remedial baseline and Ginsburg's published remedial position must be retained. In Roy she joined the rule that the personal-purpose omission is reviewable, while insisting that the actual verdict must effectively embrace the omitted purpose. She did not hold that every disputed statutory fact is an element. Mitchell likewise separates impermissible proof from the classification of a sentencing fact. These differences could matter if a predicate-unanimity error has first been established; they do not supply the missing bridge to a different interpretation of section 848.

After that comparison, the proposed departure lacks a concrete changed premise sufficiently connected to Ginsburg's element/means choice. Her reconciled position is therefore **no-error affirmance**, on the bounded enterprise construction also supported independently in the frozen alternative-path analysis. This is a correction of an unsupported provisional inference, not an automatic command to reproduce history. Her conditional remedial position remains compatible with a remand reserving both harmlessness questions; it supplies no vote for the vacatur and does not abandon her Roy actual-findings limit if review is available.

The five Associates supporting vacatur also directly join the same statutory rule: in a section 848 prosecution, the jury must unanimously agree that the defendant committed each particular violation necessary to compose the continuing series. The instruction expressly allowing disagreement about the particular violations is erroneous. This does not require agreement about every evidentiary detail of an agreed violation. It does not decide the identity-of-supervisees question, every managerial relationship, the form of substantial resources, or a new universal constitutional element rule. The number three remains the charge's assumed minimum for this question, not a separately decided statutory construction; the March 24, 1989 condition is a charge condition, not part of the quoted statutory definition.

These five support an opinion explaining the distinction between elements and means through the enacted violation language, ordinary jury protection for independently unlawful acts, the breadth of disparate violations that otherwise could support different jurors' verdicts, and the limits recognized in Schad. The enterprise focus does not erase the constituent violations. The contrary path remains substantive: O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg regard continuity, size, leadership and income as coordinated enterprise characteristics and find the series-wide agreement adequate. Their shared dissent ground need not invoke the displaced historical Almendarez-Torres majority or claim that Gaudin itself determines the antecedent classification.

## Exact remand and opinion compatibility

**Formal relief supported by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer:** Vacate the Seventh Circuit judgment insofar as it sustained the challenged Count Two CCE conviction on the erroneous instruction, and remand for proceedings consistent with the statutory unanimity requirement. The Seventh Circuit is to consider in the first instance whether this error is subject to harmless-error review and, if so, whether it is harmless. No present classification or harmlessness finding follows.

The frozen references to reversing the statutory ruling identify rejection of the lower court's legal conclusion. Vacatur is the precise formal treatment of the appellate judgment because the ultimate effect of the error remains unresolved. This clarification preserves the frozen remedy's substance rather than ordering a new trial by implication. It is supported by the reserved questions and the untouched appellate task, independently confirmed by the historical Court's identical reservation.

Roy does not require a different mandate for any of the five. Stevens's actual prior position rested on an already completed and adequate Chapman determination. Scalia's actual prior position rested on the failure of the claimed findings to establish the required connection and the absence of an unfinished material finding question in Roy. Neither proposition decides every preserved nonunanimity case or prohibits reserving a genuinely undecided availability question here. Souter, Thomas and Breyer retain Roy's qualified inference approach for its own category, without prematurely extending it. A shared remand paragraph can preserve all those positions without inventing a common answer to the reserved methodology question.

If constitutional harmless-error review is available, the Government retains the applicable beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden; evidence sufficient to convict is not by itself proof that this error did not contribute to the actual verdict. The decision does not decide that the constitutional inquiry applies, that its showing necessarily fails, or that Roy's particular method resolves the present defect. The section 846 agreement does not itself establish the requisite completed predicate transactions. Richardson's asserted absence of an equivalent finding remains an argument, not a new exhaustive record finding.

There is no automatic new trial, acquittal, release, fixed sentence, required new evidentiary proceeding, or separate Count One remedy. The remand may lead to relief on Count Two if the governing inquiry requires it; this Court does not select that result now. Rutledge's dual-conviction law is preserved but not independently applied to an unpresented remedy here.

The three affirming Associates join neither the predicate-unanimity holding nor the actual vacatur. Their conditional willingness to accept this mandate under the contrary construction does not enlarge any judgment or rationale coalition. No separate writing is forced for the unresolved harmlessness differences, and no author is assigned.
