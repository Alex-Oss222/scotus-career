## Entering law and the actual restraint

Morales requires independent legal guidance and rejects a warning as a cure for a missing common predicate. PRO distinguishes absence of a legal criterion from difficulty applying one. Here age, hours and remaining are criteria; protected First Amendment activity refers to governing law rather than an officer's unrestricted judgment about worthwhile purposes. No scienter is imported from adults' subsections into the minor's presence offense. The first-warning procedure still permits immediate removal and temporary care and therefore does not eliminate a present liberty burden.

The precise ordinance governs. It covers unemancipated children under17, 12:01–5 a.m. Monday–Friday and 1–5 a.m. Saturday–Sunday. Its separate exceptions cover parent/in-loco-parentis accompaniment; defined emergencies; employment and direct travel; the sidewalk abutting the home where the child resides with a parent; specified adult-supervised organized activities and direct travel; a parent-directed errand with the specified signed writing; interstate travel; and constitutionally protected expression. **The sidewalk exception does not require a parent to stand beside the child.** This follows the exact enacted text; the packet's compressed reference is not an added accompaniment condition. Neither that exception nor the errand exception is a general parental-permission exemption.

Prince and Bellotti support attention to children's vulnerability and the State's protective function while preserving children's constitutional rights. Meyer and Pierce establish parental authority without an absolute veto over public protective laws. Stanglin does not make all social association expressive or intimate association. These surviving authorities perform different work: ordinary liberty and parental judgment remain at issue even when socializing is not itself a First Amendment association. Glucksberg does not impose an exclusive most-specific-history rule for all liberty, and Saenz decides no local nighttime right.

## Enhanced alternatives by separable issue

**Notice, enforcement and facial expression.** Path V1 sustains the presented facial challenge disposition because the ordinance has objective central criteria, contextual emergency/civic terms and an exception referring to independently enforceable constitutional law. The chief's uncertainty about a musician and political discussion is relevant but not an arrest, authoritative limiting construction or conclusive demonstration that every application lacks a standard. Path V1's strongest objection is that criminal exposure depends on an uncertain protected/unprotected distinction and benign enforcement promises cannot cure it. Its answer must be the enacted exception and judicially ascertainable constitutional rules, not those promises; protected music or political speech cannot be declared outside the exception simply because it occurs in a restaurant.

Path V2 would invalidate a demonstrated uncertain or substantially overbroad expressive boundary under Grayned, Kolender and Reno, without deciding the entire liberty question. Its strongest objection is this record's objective core and the distinction between uncertain applications and Morales's missing common criterion. There is no supported basis to hold that ordinary recreation becomes protected expression in every instance or to delete the protective exception. On this record no eight-Justice commitment treats V2 as a sufficient facial-invalidation ground. The constitutional-exception construction applies its express terms; it does not add a new parental permission or errand definition. Ordinary as-applied litigation remains possible.

**Juvenile liberty and the age classification.** Path L1 sustains a narrowly timed restriction responsive to juvenile vulnerability, with substantial lawful activities expressly excepted. Crime/victimization evidence supports a protective connection; the City need not prove every child dangerous or a completed empirical reduction in crime. Its strongest objection is that the record's timing, causation and underinclusiveness leave substantial harmless activity restricted. The affirmative answer must address hours, ages and exceptions, not merely identify an important interest. Excluding seventeen-year-olds is a permissible boundary for the younger group; it does not demonstrate that younger children face no relevant risk. No holding treats adult public presence as subject to the same rule.

Path L2 demands a closer justification for a recurring restraint on lawful presence that includes immediate removal. It finds the asserted protective evidence insufficient to support displacement of competent parental decisions about otherwise lawful activity across all covered locations. Its strongest objection is that the rule operates only during limited overnight hours and preserves work, organized events, emergencies, travel and expression; parental choice cannot immunize exposure to public risks. The reversal positions accept those legitimate interests but find that the unexcepted parent-authorized class cannot be sustained merely by general juvenile statistics. This does not make individual dangerousness an element of every protective law or mandate a universal strict-scrutiny test.

**Parental authority.** Path P1 recognizes an actual burden but regards it as bounded public protection compatible with Meyer, Pierce and Prince, rather than a custody or education displacement. Accompaniment includes an authorized adult in loco parentis, and the specific errand exception preserves a category of parental direction. Its strongest objection is that the City criminalizes knowing parental permission even when a fit parent has assessed an ordinary lawful activity; the rule cannot be justified simply as strengthening that same parent's responsibility. P1 therefore rests on independent child/public protection, not a claim that the ordinance only implements every parent's wishes.

Path P2 regards the unexcepted class as an unjustified displacement of the parent's decision coupled with the child's ordinary liberty. Its strongest objection is the State's independent protective power, which P2 preserves for adequately justified rules and particular dangers. P2 supplies neither an absolute parental veto nor a statutory exemption by judicial redrafting. A.St.P.C. and Baby Richard inform the separateness and seriousness of interests; their custody-specific rules are not imported.

**Relief.** Affirmance preserves future genuinely different applications and decides no withdrawn Fourth Amendment claim. Reversal must identify the substantive application class and return to the lower court for relief fitted to established plaintiffs and challenged provisions. No source supplies a general severability finding. The remand therefore requires ordinary consideration of appropriate declaratory/injunctive scope and any necessary state-law severability question; it does not order excision of an exception, broaden criminal liability, or rewrite the ordinance. Independent false-identification liability is not summarily adjudicated. Relief from unsupported parent-authorized applications is a proposed constitutional limit, not an established record fact that every desired activity is unexcepted.
