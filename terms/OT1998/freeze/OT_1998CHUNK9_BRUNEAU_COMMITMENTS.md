## Shared legal conclusions

**Independent constitutional claim:** Title IX does not displace section 1983 enforcement of an independently established Equal Protection right merely because the constitutional claim and a Title IX action arise from the same harassment. The constitutional claim retains its own intentional discrimination, state action, personal responsibility or municipal attribution, causation and remedial requirements. Overlapping evidence is not a comprehensive incompatible remedial scheme.

**Statutory claim:** The individual right not to be subjected to sex discrimination under a covered educational program satisfies the particular-right inquiry for the claim presented. Title IX's administrative funding process and established implied private action do not expressly exclude section 1983 and are not incompatible with section 1983 enforcement of that same right. This conclusion does not create an action for aggregate regulatory compliance or supply a Title IX duty for a defendant whom the statute does not bind. Any statutory section 1983 claim must establish the actual Title IX violation, including the recipient-responsibility conditions applicable to it; changing the enforcement label does not expand the underlying right.

Blessing unanimously separates individual rights from general program duties and treats an actual individual right as presumptively enforceable absent exclusion or incompatibility. Cannon establishes the private Title IX action, and Franklin recognizes appropriate compensatory relief within it. The administrative funding process does not give this student an exclusive personal remedial forum, and the implied private action has no elaborate claim-processing structure that a parallel constitutional action would evade. Sea Clammers and Smith v. Robinson preserve their actual incompatible-scheme holdings; neither establishes that any private action automatically erases section 1983.

Kinman independently adjudicated Title IX and constitutional supervisory/municipal theories, without deciding the present preclusion issue. Gebser and Davis refine recipient attribution, not constitutional extinction. Davis's express distinction between peer conduct and an official's own discriminatory enforcement is particularly important to the scope of the constitutional claim. Those decisions neither establish liability here nor change what was clearly established for the individuals in 1993.

## Adversarial paths

**Displacement path:** The combination of agency oversight, termination of funds and a direct damages action may be read as Congress's selected enforcement arrangement. Allowing section 1983 suits could permit defendants or substantive conditions omitted from the Title IX action to reenter through a different caption, undermining the funding bargain. Its strongest application concerns a nominal statutory claim against a nonrecipient.

**Non-displacement path:** Congressional foreclosure requires actual remedial incompatibility. Different defendants and constitutional duties demonstrate that the claims are not interchangeable; the absence of an individual administrative adjudication or obligatory exhaustion path distinguishes Smith and Sea Clammers. Statutory section 1983 enforcement need not evade Title IX limits because the claimed statutory right retains them.

All eight Associates reject categorical displacement on the second path. The strong evasion objection is answered by enforcing the underlying right and defendant-specific duties, not by extinguishing a separately protected constitutional right or declaring the administrative process comprehensive. Conversely, the non-displacement position cannot treat the general jury verdict as establishing an official's discriminatory intent or a municipal policy. A verdict for the recipient may have a properly demonstrated issue-preclusive effect, but its general form does not identify an issue necessarily decided.

## Remedy, present force and limits

Reverse only the portion affirming the District/Board section 1983 dismissal on Title IX-displacement grounds; remand those claims for ordinary proceedings. Leave the Title IX trial judgment and Race/Parker immunity affirmance undisturbed. This limited grant supplies no basis for a new Title IX trial, renewed individual claims, student merits judgment, damages or mandatory trial.

The remand preserves pleading, proper defendant, state action, intentional discrimination, statutory recipient status and attribution where relevant, municipal policy/custom or final-policymaker attribution, causation, compensable injury, supported defenses and any properly presented effect of the existing verdict. Kinman's rejection of institutional liability from rank or teacher status alone remains controlling. Gebser's conjunctive teacher route and Davis's independent peer conditions remain unchanged. A general defense verdict establishes no newly specified factual finding. Current law does not retrospectively establish the clear notice needed for 1993 qualified immunity.
