# Neder - frozen neutral record-validation addendum

Purpose: validate the actual tax-count charge, defense arguments, and verdict linkage before independent modeling. This addendum makes no harmlessness determination, predicts no Justice's vote, and does not replace the existing frozen neutral packet. It supplies positive predecision trial facts and narrows the earlier record uncertainty.

## Answer to the factual question

The tax charge did not submit an unrestricted choice of false statements. Its second element specifically required falsity of the reported annual total income: $53,625 for 1985 and minus $980,377 for 1986. The copied indictment counts likewise identify omitted additional income as the charged falsity. The judgment affirmatively records guilty findings on counts 86 and 87. Thus the actual charge supplies a link between those verdicts and the knowing, purposeful falsity of the reported annual income totals. An account treating these verdicts as potentially resting on an unspecified unrelated false statement must confront the express second element and the actual counts, rather than assume a generic section 7206(1) charge.

That link is narrower than a special finding of the Government's exact understatement amounts. The indictment alleges approximately $1,361,361.79 of additional 1985 income and approximately $4,268,767.83 of additional 1986 income. The final four-element tax instruction identifies the reported totals but does not make those exact additional amounts separately specified findings required by its enumerated elements. The amounts remain allegations and evidence-based propositions unless an identified verdict inference establishes more. Neither the general judgment nor the indictment should be relabeled a special factual verdict. Whether the actual findings and trial record suffice under the governing legal standard is reserved entirely for modeling.

## Final instructions and verdict linkage

The complete final charge, JA238-265, was read. JA238-240 assigns the specific elements to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt, directs it to follow the whole charge, states that the indictment is not evidence, and distinguishes lawyers' arguments from evidence. JA243-245 directs separate consideration of each offense and defendant, says the jury will receive the indictment, and identifies counts 86/87 as the section 7206(1) income-tax counts.

JA255-256 supplies four tax elements: subscription of a return declared under penalties of perjury; the identified false annual total-income statements; knowledge of falsity; and purposeful statement rather than accident, negligence, or inadvertence. It then expressly instructs jurors not to decide materiality even though the indictment uses that term. The introductory lines on JA255 are partly corrupted in OCR; the second through fourth elements and express materiality withdrawal on JA256 are readable. No missing text has been supplied by conjecture.

JA263 includes a general instruction that one proven statutory alternative may suffice when several are charged conjunctively, provided jurors unanimously agree on at least one. JA264 requires a unanimous verdict, and JA264-265 describes filling, dating, and signing the verdict form. That general alternatives passage must be read together with the particular four-element tax instruction; it is not a printed tax-specific instruction identifying another false statement. The source provided here contains the final charge, not a separately completed verdict form. The affirmative tax guilty-count linkage comes from the separately recovered judgment entry.

The charge conference at JA165-166 confirms defense objection to removing materiality and the judge's stated reason for telling jurors to disregard the indictment's materiality language. The bench ruling at JA166-167 finds materiality outside the jury's presence and expressly extends the same ruling to the tax returns. This is the judge's finding; it is not a finding made by the jury.

## What the defense actually argued

The relevant opening and closing blocks were read in context and copied exactly. Their content prevents treating taxable character, knowing falsity, or good-faith reliance as conceded merely because a separate materiality argument was not the defense's focus.

At JA147-150 (opening; transcript volume 1, pages 88-92), counsel described nominee corporations, advice from CPA Tom Brooks, a proposed deferral of tax until completed-project sales, and an analogy to a second mortgage: proceeds were said to be owed back rather than profit. These are counsel's preview of expected proof, not adjudicated facts.

At JA207-212 (closing; volume 35, pages 220-227), counsel argued that Neder intended to repay the loans, personally guaranteed them, and reinvested proceeds; disputed that excess loan proceeds were current profit; and attacked IRS agent John Gay's opinion and its asserted factual basis. Counsel acknowledged a hypothetical in which borrowing with an original intent not to repay would be taxable but argued that hypothetical did not describe this case. This is a dispute about the character of the receipts and criminal state of mind, not a stipulation that the charged additional income was received as taxable income.

At JA216-224 (closing; volume 35, pages 232-242), counsel argued that lawyer Tom Turner had given transactional and tax advice, that billing records and corporate/closing documents contradicted his testimony, and that good-faith reliance negated criminal intent. At JA234-235 (closing; volume 35, pages 255-257), counsel returned to good faith and asserted that Neder consulted tax attorney Hans Tanzler before signing the returns and relied on the advice. These passages establish arguments made to the jury; they do not establish that the jury credited the described advice or resolve every subsidiary factual dispute about it.

At JA237 the court expressly cautioned that its instructions would control over counsel's statements of law. The validation therefore does not substitute counsel's claim that the Government had to prove an intent never to repay for the court's actual final elements.

A tax guilty verdict under the specified charge cannot be described as acceptance of a defense that would leave the identified annual income statements truthful or innocently mistaken. It also does not tell us, by a separate special finding, precisely which pieces of the professional-advice narrative were disbelieved or what exact taxable amount each juror accepted. These are the supported boundaries; the addendum decides no actual-verdict harmlessness inference.

## Statutory text and chronological dependency

The recovered official 1994 [section 1341](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK9_A_sources/statute-1341.txt), [section 1343](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK9_A_sources/statute-1343.txt), and [section 1344](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK9_A_sources/statute-1344.txt) files were read in full and copied byte-identically. Their amendment notes must be observed: the 1994 carrier language and changed penalties are not silently applied to earlier conduct; the common fraud language is distinct from those amendments. Section 1344's notes reproduce the pre-1989 formulation. No sentencing consequence is decided here.

The file initially named statute-7206.txt is a GovInfo error page and is not used as authority. A valid [official section 7206 source](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK9_A_sources/statute-7206-official.txt) was recovered from the [2023 U.S. Code publication](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title26/html/USCODE-2023-title26-subtitleF-chap75-subchapA-partI-sec7206.htm), read in full, and saved mechanically. Its source and amendment notes show enactment in 1954 and a 1982 fine amendment only; the unchanged section 7206(1) operative text is used as a historical-text verification, not as later law. Cornell's identical text and amendment history were cross-checked. Section 7206(1) concerns willfully subscribing a document declared under penalties of perjury that the signer does not believe true and correct as to every material matter. The charged return statements and actual jury instruction, rather than a general description of every possible section 7206 offense, control this factual validation.

The effective June 1 [Richardson public law](../entering-law/richardson_v_united_states_merits_1999-06-01.md) was read in full. It establishes constituent-violation unanimity for section 848 and expressly reserves availability and application of harmless-error review for that defect. This satisfies the previously identified chronological refresh for Neder; it supplies no predetermined Neder result. Existing Gaudin, Roy, Sullivan, Johnson, Jones, and Mitchell retain their own scopes.

## Reading and boundary receipt

This validation read only the specifically authorized trial-only text, exact tax counts, exact trial-judgment count entry, safe statutory files, the identified current public Richardson law, and the statutory websites listed above. No historical Neder Supreme Court disposition, merits reasoning, votes, or petition treatment was encountered. No comparator, Stone material, commitments, raw current Record, general control intake, or locked directory was read. No Git command ran. Original briefs and prior frozen packets were not edited.

The complete supplied final charge and the supplied charge conference/bench ruling were read. The complete relevant tax, advice, and good-faith argument blocks identified above were read; other portions of the 204,826-character trial-only text were searched as location aids or left unread. This is not a claim to have read the full underlying trial, all original filings, or every page of the complete appendix. The addendum draws its conclusions from affirmative count and charge text, not from a supposed absence of evidence in unread material.
