## Material qualifications for reconciliation

- **Goldsmith:** The Court adopted two grounds: no statutory review jurisdiction over the independent executive drop-from-rolls action, and alternatively no necessary or appropriate writ given adequate other avenues. Preserve BCMR review, available APA review for nonmonetary relief, and Tucker/Little Tucker routes where a monetary claim can be framed. Footnote 12 reserves the BCMR constitutional-correction question rather than deciding it. The Court distinguishes a genuine alteration of an affirmed court-martial judgment, where All Writs enforcement may be available, from perpetual supervision of everything related to a sentence. Ex post facto and double-jeopardy merits were not reached. The actual sentence was confinement and forfeitures, not dismissal.
- **Hunt:** All Justices rejected summary judgment against the State, but only five joined Thomas's explanation. The majority allows plaintiff summary judgment in an appropriate circumstantial-evidence case; it does not prohibit that route categorically. The dispute concerns motivation and reasonable competing inferences, not truth of the raw district shape and demographic data. Footnote 1 preserves a live controversy because the State would revert to the 1997 plan after a favorable ruling. Footnote 6 distinguishes new reply-brief data from evidence actually before the district court. Stevens's concurrence treats shape as equally consistent with politics and race, stresses actual voting rather than registration, and expressly leaves denial of the State's own summary-judgment motion outside review. The opinion does not supply an independent injury beyond the historical Shaw theory; any different entering-law threshold requires separate analysis.
- **White:** Preserve a readily mobile vehicle, a public place, and probable cause that the vehicle itself was forfeitable. Probable cause to believe it presently contained drugs was absent. This is suppression review in a possession prosecution, not a forfeiture judgment. Footnote 4 expressly reserves whether excessive delay can make probable cause stale. Souter/Breyer reject treating any legislative contraband label as permission for warrantless seizure; their concurrence is not a general rule that all publicly located forfeitable property may be taken. Stevens/Ginsburg distinguish current contraband searches from permanent seizure for past use, emphasize possessory interests and a neutral warrant determination, and distinguish the prior tax assessment in G. M. Leasing. Possible state-law protection is not adjudicated.
- **Ruhrgas:** Permission to choose personal jurisdiction first is not an unqualified sequencing preference. Ordinarily easy subject-matter questions go first; state-law difficulty and comity matter. Straightforward personal jurisdiction with difficult novel subject-matter issues permits the alternative order. No merits power is assumed. Crucially footnote 8 leaves correctness of the district court's personal-jurisdiction dismissal to the Fifth Circuit on remand because that issue is outside the granted question. Do not affirm that dismissal as an independently reviewed merits component. Diversity, federal-common-law international relations, and arbitration-removal grounds are separate asserted bases.
- **New Jersey:** Six operative parts preserve the Compact boundary, New York's original island to low water and 1834 pier, the December 1, 1998 map and all 182 NAD83-described courses, retained jurisdiction, and equally shared Special Master expenses. A decree is not a new merits division. Copying its instrument for an authorized prospective reconstruction does not prove actual simulated filing, adoption, consent, or default.
- **Maine Green Party:** The majority's waiver analysis concerns a material asserted factual shift from practical inability to establish national organization to philosophical local-only organization, not simply different authorities or improved advocacy. The earlier theory was not developed on appeal. The district court's adoption order did not expressly invoke waiver. Campbell disputes the shift, emphasizes preservation and the absence of a waiver argument from Maine, and would affirm on the merits given the broader ballot system. His merits position does not become the lower court majority holding. There is no Supreme Court merits vote to match or depart from.
- **Cleveland:** Application or receipt of SSDI creates neither automatic estoppel nor a special strong negative presumption. An apparent contradiction nevertheless requires an explanation sufficient for a reasonable juror assuming the earlier statement's truth or the plaintiff's good-faith belief in it. Accommodation, administrative presumptions, trial work, changed condition, and alternate pleading explain potential compatibility. Pure factual contradictions and other statutory interactions are left untouched; cited sham-affidavit cases are not wholesale endorsed. Remand allows both sides to present or contest explanations, in sworn form where appropriate; the Court does not itself find Cleveland qualified or decide accommodation merits. The Fifth Circuit used a rebuttable presumption with a limited/highly unusual exception, not a literally exceptionless bar.
- **Wilson:** Separate unanimous constitutional determination from 8–1 qualified-immunity disposition. The rule addresses third parties whose presence does not aid warrant execution; legitimate assistance remains distinct. Footnote 2 says lawful police presence is not itself invalidated by the media violation and reserves exclusion of evidence obtained by media. Formal ride-along policies matter only against an undeveloped legal background, not as authority to override settled law. Stevens relies on clear home/warrant limits, Lanier, common-law trespass, section 3105, and the weakness of contrary precedents/policy. His discussion distinguishes the merits consensus from an immunity split. The Fourth Circuit below declined the constitutional question.
- **Davis:** Liability is for the recipient's own deliberate indifference, with actual knowledge, substantial control over harasser and context, and harassment so severe, pervasive, and objectively offensive that educational access is effectively denied. Indifference must cause exposure or vulnerability. A clearly unreasonable response is required, not ordinary negligence, guarantee of cessation, specified discipline, or compliance with a victim's preferred remedy. Age and setting matter; schools retain disciplinary discretion and constitutional/statutory constraints. Mere teasing, a grade decline alone, and the dissent's easier hypotheticals are not sufficient. The Court discusses the theoretical possibility but practical improbability of a single severe peer incident having systemic effect; do not turn this into an unqualified absolute rule. The complaint survives; facts and liability remain unproved. Kennedy's dissent rests on Spending Clause notice, statutory attribution, constrained control, and uncertain standards; he distinguishes deliberately gender-discriminatory enforcement by the institution from simple failure to stop a student's conduct.
- **California Dental:** Unanimous FTC coverage of this nonprofit with concrete economic benefit to for-profit members is separate from the 5–4 antitrust analysis. Part II declines to decide deference to the Commission's jurisdictional interpretation; coverage rests on ordinary construction. Footnote 6 reserves other nonprofit/profit paradigms and merely presumed educational benefits. Part III holds the abbreviated analysis inadequate, not that the restraints are lawful. Both price and quality restrictions require attention to professional information asymmetry and plausible effects. No full-market study is categorically mandated; analysis must fit the restraint. Footnote 8 leaves the Ninth Circuit to decide whether to use the existing record or return to the Commission for further development. Breyer's four-part approach accepts the agency's operative findings, finds anticompetitive tendencies and market power, and places the burden of proven justification on the association; that disagreement is substantive and must not be reduced to a preference for a quick label.
- **Hanlon:** The per curiam recognizes that the complaint alleges a violation under Wilson and grants the officials qualified immunity because no intervening authority changed the legal landscape from 1992 to the 1993 search. Stevens disagrees only on immunity for his Wilson reasons. The warrant excludes the residence. The opinion's short recitation does not establish that the entire ranch is protected space, decide curtilage, decide CNN's liability, or establish all details of joint media participation. Its disposition is vacatur and remand, not affirmance of the Ninth Circuit's immunity denial.
- **City:** The prior June 16, 1997 public decision affirms the same single $1,450,000 award, gives effect to the owners' conditional additional-damages waiver, preserves taking/exaction limits, and resolves the constitutional jury question. Historical 1999 merits review cannot be imported as a new event merely because it appears in this chunk. No newly supported postmandate proceeding was established by this verification.
