## Exact scope of the common proposition

The district court's findings about lost opportunities and the special amendment hurdle are accepted record findings, not commands recited in section 31. On the preliminary record, they establish a present burden on the ordinary political channels for seeking otherwise lawful racial measures. Calling that burden a present structural injury does not require proof that every claimant would have obtained a particular benefit. The four agree that the racial character of the foreclosed measure cannot itself justify categorically ending Hunter/Seattle review. They do not adopt a general right to favorable legislation at every governmental level or a prohibition on ordinary substantive policy withdrawal.

Thus the proposed statement that the findings establish a present structural injury and support confined protection against selective racial foreclosure is compatible with all four only if support means a legal basis for confined preliminary protection subject to the ordinary prerequisites. It cannot mean that this Court has settled the precise restraint, ordered automatic continuation of every part of the district injunction, found every threatened loss cognizable regardless of the underlying measure's validity, or eliminated the remand's actual-injury inquiry.

**Supported public formulation:**

On this preliminary record, the district court's supported findings of lost opportunities and the special constitutional-amendment hurdle establish a present structural burden on ordinary political access to otherwise lawful racial measures. That burden supports the racial political-process claim and confined preliminary protection when the ordinary requirements for such relief are met. The lower court must match any restraint to the actual threatened injury, preserve independently lawful operation, and separately examine the sex-based theory. The Court does not automatically reinstate the entire injunction or validate any particular affirmative-action program.

This wording clarifies the already frozen distinction between a supported structural injury and the scope and prerequisites of relief. It changes no component disposition or vote. If stronger language is proposed establishing final invalidity, compelling an identified injunction without the remaining equitable inquiry, or validating programs, these four joins cannot be inferred from this receipt.
