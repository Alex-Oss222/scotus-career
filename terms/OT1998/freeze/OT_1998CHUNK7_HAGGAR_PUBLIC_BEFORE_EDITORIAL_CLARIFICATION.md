
## Event

United States v. Haggar Apparel Co., No. 97-2044. Argued January 11, 1999; decided April 21, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Federal Circuit, 127 F.3d 1460. The questions are whether the promulgated Customs regulation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation and whether its exclusion of permapressing reasonably implements the statutory allowance for operations incidental to assembly. The dispute concerns baking resin-treated fabric after assembly abroad to activate permanent-press characteristics.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the consideration and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is vacated and the case remanded under the correct statutory and administrative-review framework.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing that disposition:** Stevens and Ginsburg, who would reverse outright and sustain the challenged Customs denial.

All nine reject categorical exclusion of Chevron review. The Court leaves the regulation's reasonableness and application unresolved; no refund, duty amount or new hearing is ordered.

## Holdings

### Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation

**Holding and operative rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing Congress's actual delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation: courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and the delegation's boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The Court of International Trade's duty to decide on its own record does not repeal that legal framework; agency labels and notice and comment alone do not establish authority or validity.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Parts I–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer and control this complete framework.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes governing law from judicial determination of facts. Sections 1500(b) and 1502(a) and the tariff delegation provisions authorize implementing rules within their scope. Section 2640(a)(1)'s own-record review does not assign all substantive tariff lawmaking exclusively to courts, and §10.11's preservation of judicial review does not require disregarding a lawful regulation. Chevron supplies the division of responsibility; NationsBank permits reasonable gap filling within actual authority. MCI and NCUA require courts to enforce enacted limits before deferring. The importer correctly insists that review remain independent on those limits and effective on the facts. That does not justify categorically withholding the weight due a sufficiently authoritative delegated rule. Conversely, recognizing that framework does not make every Customs interpretation lawful. Informal classification letters and later administrative doctrines are not decided.

### This regulation's statutory fit and actual application require the corrected inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment must be vacated because categorical nondeference left unresolved whether the regulation reasonably respects the tariff's incidental-assembly allowance and lawfully applies to this process. On remand the courts must enforce the enacted conditions, examine the regulation's actual introductory terms and examples, and resolve relevant application questions under the proper review standards; neither regulatory validity nor the claimed exemption is established in advance.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part IV is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, seven votes. Stevens and Ginsburg do not join this holding or remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The tariff requires United States fabricated components exported ready for assembly without further fabrication, retention of physical identity, and no advancement in value or improvement abroad except through assembly and incidental operations, including the statutory examples. The allowance reduces duties by qualifying component cost or value, with proportional treatment for specific or compound rates; it does not exempt the whole article. Regulation §10.16(b) permits incidental operations before, during or after assembly, including ordinary final pressing and folding. Section 10.16(c) addresses a significant nonassembly process whose primary purpose is fabrication, completion or physical or chemical improvement, or a significant process unrelated to assembly. Its chemical-treatment examples, including permapressing, do not erase those introductory alternatives. Timing after sewing alone settles nothing, and ordinary pressing is not necessarily resin-activating permapressing. The significance, purpose, relationship to assembly and new characteristics of the actual process bear on the statutory-regulatory boundary, not merely on duty arithmetic. NationsBank's framework does not answer those unfinished questions. AT&T distinguishes general authority from particular validity; NFFE distinguishes available discretion from its actual lawful exercise without requiring a new agency proceeding here. Both questions were granted, but that scope does not make this intertwined inquiry complete. The lower courts must undertake it without the erroneous categorical premise. The Court commands no new evidentiary hearing, burden, deadline or finding that existing litigation papers are missing.

## Precedent Treatment

- Chevron supplies review within actual delegated authority and statutory ambiguity.
- NationsBank of North Carolina v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co. permits reasonable administrative classification within statutory limits.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T and National Credit Union Administration v. First National Bank require enforcement of enacted boundaries before deference.
- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board separates general authority, particular rule validity and unresolved applications; it creates no automatic validation or universal remand rule.
- NFFE/FLRA v. Department of the Interior distinguishes legally available discretion from its lawful exercise; its distinct remedy does not compel a new Customs proceeding.

## Law After Decision

The Federal Circuit may not categorically exclude this promulgated tariff regulation from Chevron review. Judicial enforcement of statutory limits and trade-court factual review remain intact. The regulation's ultimate reasonableness and its application to Haggar's processing remain for the corrected inquiry. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens, joined by Justice Ginsburg, agrees with Parts I–III but would reverse outright. Chevron and NationsBank permit the agency to distinguish assembly and ordinary finishing from significant chemical treatment that imparts a new characteristic. In their view the actual ovenbaking activates a lasting permanent-press property and reasonably falls on the excluded side of that line. They preserve the regulation's introductory terms and do not treat every chemical effect or use of heat as disqualifying. Because review includes reasonableness and the supported process facts establish the application, they see no remaining reason to return validity or the challenged denial for decision. They would sustain that denial without calculating a new duty amount or deciding unrelated shipment issues.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Federal Circuit for consideration of regulatory reasonableness and application, with further trade-court proceedings as lawfully needed under the correct framework. The Court neither upholds the denial nor grants the exemption. No mandatory hearing, new agency proceeding or collateral shipment adjudication is imposed.
