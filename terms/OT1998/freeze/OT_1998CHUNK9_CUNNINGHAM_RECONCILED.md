## Cunningham v. Hamilton County — No. 98-727 — June 14, 1999

**Threshold and actual order.** All eight review the Sixth Circuit's dismissal and accept that the underlying estate civil-rights case remained pending. The order at issue is the personal $1,494 Rule 37(a)(4) discovery sanction, not the separately settled $2,432 sanction. There was no contempt hearing or finding; a contempt appellate route cannot be substituted. Ending Cunningham's representation through disqualification does not make the client's pending action final. The sanction's substantive validity, amount and the disqualification merits are not adjudicated.

**Common rule and independently sufficient grounds.** A monetary Rule 37 discovery sanction against an attorney is not immediately appealable under §1291's collateral-order doctrine while the underlying case remains pending, even when the attorney's representation has ended. Assuming a conclusive sanction determination, the category does not satisfy complete separation from the merits: reviewing discovery relevance, responses and compliance can require examination of the case's substantive issues and litigation record. Independently, the sanction remains effectively reviewable after final judgment: the attorney personally bound by it may then seek review of her own sanction notwithstanding former-counsel status, with reversal capable of correcting the monetary obligation. Ordinary payment, delay and professional burden do not establish the kind of important immunity from litigation irretrievably lost without immediate review. Neither assertion makes every attorney order unappealable or purports to decide a different sanctions regime.

**Authority.** Cohen and Coopers & Lybrand impose cumulative conditions. Private settlement rights and collateral-order appeals, effective Digital Equipment, June 6, 1994, preserves category discipline and adequate final review despite irretrievable litigation costs. All then-seated Associates joined both grounds of Scalia's opinion. Swint, March 1, 1995, rejects convenience as jurisdiction and distinguishes ordinary liability from immunity. Behrens, February 21, 1996, preserves qualifying legal-immunity appeals, not immediate review of every personal burden. Ginsburg authored Swint and Behrens. None makes disqualification a new finality exception.

**Alternative-path check.** Immediate review has its strongest case in the conclusive, personal obligation against a lawyer who is no longer conducting the litigation. That status makes her interest distinct from the client's but does not establish that assessing discovery compliance is completely separate from the merits, nor that she loses standing to challenge her own sanction after final judgment. The alternative cannot survive merely by calling the sanction collateral or pointing to a completed representation. Conversely, deferred review must actually remain available to the affected attorney; the common conclusion expressly preserves it instead of assuming the client will appeal. This legal conclusion does not falsely attribute an effective-unreviewability holding to the Sixth Circuit, which expressly reserved that question. No special hardship fact is found or categorically adjudicated beyond the ordinary monetary-sanction category presented.

**Joins and remedy.** All eight directly join the rule, both independent grounds and affirmance of the interlocutory dismissal. The underlying action proceeds; the sanction is neither affirmed on its merits nor vacated. Section 1292 certification, mandamus and a genuinely applicable contempt appeal retain their independent requirements; none is found satisfied, granted or ordered. The decision creates no new alternative appeal, payment stay, fee reduction or immunity from discovery obligations.

**Kennedy's retained qualification:** An attorney is not remediless in every exceptional case. If the trial court declines to stay enforcement and the resulting exceptional hardship is itself likely to cause injustice, mandamus might permit review of the sanction or denial of a stay under that remedy's own requirements. A genuine contempt adjudication against a person lacking congruent interests with a litigant may present a different appealability question. No such fact, contempt finding, mandamus petition, stay entitlement or alternative appeal is found here. Kennedy can retain this published reservation while fully joining the common opinion; the final decision must not transform it into an automatic Court-authorized route or claim that he dissents from either ground.

**Scope/mandate audit:** The sanction is $1,494 under the then-operative Rule 37(a)(4); the settled $2,432 order is not reviewed. Disqualification ended representation; it did not conclude the underlying civil action. The Sixth Circuit reserved effective unreviewability, so adequate final review is the Court's legal conclusion rather than a lower-court factual finding. The attorney may seek review of her own sanction after final judgment; this cannot depend solely on whether her former client elects to appeal. The category's ordinary delay/payment/professional burden supplies no right to avoid litigation comparable to immunity. A case-by-case showing that one discovery question looks especially separate does not remove the category requirement. Affirming dismissal neither upholds the sanction on its merits nor vacates it, decides the disqualification, stays payment or resolves the civil-rights claim.
