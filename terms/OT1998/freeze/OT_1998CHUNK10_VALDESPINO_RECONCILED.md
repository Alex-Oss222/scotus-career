## Governing law and the three paths

The exact dated Standard retains the conventional district-majority, compactness, cohesion and majority-bloc prerequisites, with the special-circumstances qualification and independently necessary totality inquiry. Growe and Voinovich preserve the stated population-denominator, coalition and influence reservations. De Grandy rejects maximization and numerical safe harbors but does not establish a below-majority district entitlement; its Senate crossover example is relevant totality evidence, not a second majority district or a right to compel one. Brooks concerns a nomination/runoff barrier. It supports examining the actual causal mechanism, but does not by itself repeal the district-capacity prerequisite in this at-large district-formation claim.

**N — conventional numerical capacity.** O'Connor and Kennedy retain the prerequisite for this claim and select citizen voting-age population as the relevant electoral-capacity denominator. Accepted 47.9% and 48.3% figures do not meet it. This is distinct from the total-population inquiry into an equally populated, workable district. N decides this single-group claim without categorically disposing of every influence claim, distinct-group coalition claim, or lawful voluntary crossover district.

**F — expressly modified capacity condition.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would permit a sufficiently compact single group to establish a workable district's actual ability to elect through dependable crossover support, while retaining cohesion, the relevant majority-bloc showing, causation and totality. Near-fifty arithmetic, officeholders' race, influence or speculation cannot establish that capacity. Any discrepancy between majority-bloc defeat in the challenged at-large arrangement and effective crossover within a proposed district must be proved in the relevant settings, not assumed. This is a bounded proposed modification of the first condition, not existing law or a judicial finding that these plaintiffs meet it.

**X — statutory noncoverage.** Scalia and Thomas maintain their published De Grandy and current Brooks position that §2 covers access, casting and counting rather than districting dilution. They acknowledge the contrary controlling precedents and propose to reject the incompatible coverage rule. Their affirmance of a judgment already denying relief is a separate formal choice, not an adoption of N or an automatic repetition of their different De Grandy vacatur-and-dismissal mandate. No two extra numerical-threshold joins arise from their common judgment consequence.
