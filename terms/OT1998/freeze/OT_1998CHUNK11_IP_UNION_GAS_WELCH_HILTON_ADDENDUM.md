## 1. Union Gas: actual private claim, actual authority, and Stevens's distinct account

The official [491 U.S.1 case unit](../sources/chunk11-root-scope/union_gas_491_us_1_official.pdf), with [full extracted text](../sources/chunk11-root-scope/union_gas_491_us_1_official.txt), establishes the following:

- **Actual posture, pp.5–6:** The United States sued Union Gas to recover cleanup expenditures. Union Gas brought a private third-party complaint against Pennsylvania, alleging owner/operator responsibility and negligent contribution to the release. The immunity question concerns that private contribution/cost claim, not a suit by the United States against Pennsylvania. The Court affirmed the appellate judgment removing the immunity bar and remanded; it did not fix Pennsylvania's liability or a damages amount.
- **Statutory holding, pp.7–13:** Brennan's Parts I–II, joined by Marshall, Blackmun, Stevens and Scalia, supply five actual votes for clear authorization of State liability in federal court under CERCLA as amended by SARA. The Court read the enactments together, including State inclusion, the qualified involuntary-ownership exclusion, and liability to the same extent as nongovernmental entities. It explicitly rejected an interpretation permitting only United States suits (pp.10–12). Thus the private third-party posture is real, but it is not a basis for recasting the statutory holding as a United-States-only exception.
- **Constitutional conclusion, pp.13–23,57:** Brennan, Marshall, Blackmun and Stevens support the Commerce Clause abrogation analysis in Part III. White separately accepts the constitutional conclusion while expressly declining much of the reasoning. Those five establish the decided Commerce-power conclusion; Brennan's entire rationale has only four joins. White opposed the statutory clarity ruling, accepted that ruling once it prevailed, and then supplied the fifth constitutional vote and affirmance. Scalia's statutory join does not supply a constitutional join.
- **No Parden waiver holding, p.23 n.5:** The opinion states that Union Gas itself “eschews reliance” on Parden waiver. It therefore does not discuss that theory. Brennan's constitutional account describes consent in the plan of the Convention, not a factual determination that Pennsylvania knowingly elected a newly conditioned commercial activity. Do not convert this abrogation decision into an actual-voluntary-waiver holding or a notice-and-exit test.

**Stevens's complete concurrence, pp.23–29, is especially material.** He distinguishes the Eleventh Amendment's actual constitutional withdrawal of jurisdiction from judicially created immunity under Hans. Congress cannot amend the Constitution by statute; Congress may displace the latter prudential immunity when federal judicial power otherwise exists. His textual reading expressly concerns the State–citizen and alien–State diversity grants and says the Amendment does not affect the other ArticleIII grants. He therefore treats a congressionally created federal-question claim as within federal judicial power and joins Brennan's opinion. It would be inaccurate to describe this concurrence simply as a rule barring all suits by citizens of another State regardless of the jurisdictional basis.

Stevens also invokes the Court's willingness to recognize State waiver, and the distinction between prospective and retrospective relief, as evidence that much immunity doctrine concerns prudential federalism rather than an absolute lack of ArticleIII power. Those observations support his constitutional/common-law distinction. They do not supply an additional Parden-waiver ground for Union Gas's judgment, especially given the express disclaimer in the opinion he joins. He does not decide a federal-benefit bargain or the constitutionality of every future commercial-condition statute.

**Current-force qualification:** The simulated Seminole public writings and the exact current Standards control how these antecedents enter 1999. They retain Union Gas's damages posture, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion at their decided scope; they do not adopt every four-Justice proposition or resolve every ArticleI statute. Stevens's later simulated Seminole writing expressly leaves the textual out-of-state/foreign-plaintiff issue open. Souter/Ginsburg/Breyer's common-law/ArticleI account remains an unused conditional alternative because their officer route succeeds. Neither reservation may be inferred away from earlier opinions. The original Union Gas concurrence gives a genuine jurisdictional-basis distinction for analysis, not authority to rewrite a later reserved position into an actual commitment.

**Narrow factual limit:** The inspected Supreme Court factual recital does not establish Union Gas's corporate citizenship, and control has no separately verified contemporaneous source for it. This addendum does not call Union Gas a same-State citizen; the Pennsylvania site and case caption prove no such thing. No conclusion here requires that biographical assumption. If a proposed rule depends specifically on the claimant's citizenship, that exact fact requires targeted support. The verified posture is a private third-party federal statutory claim following a United States action.

## 2. Welch: the result and the limited extent of its reasoning

The official [483 U.S.468 case unit](../sources/chunk11-root-scope/welch_483_us_468_official.pdf), with [full extracted text](../sources/chunk11-root-scope/welch_483_us_468_official.txt), must be read at its fractured scope.

| Component | Precise support and limit |
|---|---|
| Actual result | The State employee's federal Jones Act action against Texas and its Highway Department could not proceed. Powell, Rehnquist, White, O'Connor and Scalia support affirmance. |
| Powell's opinion | Four Justices join. It distinguishes State consent, clear congressional intent and Congress's constitutional power. The Jones Act's general “any seaman” language is insufficient to express unmistakable intent to subject States to federal suit. |
| Express Texas waiver | Not decided on review: pp.474–75 treat the state-law express-waiver issue as outside the petition and not fairly included; the petitioner's merits discussion did not expand the grant. This is not a holding that an actually clear State waiver would fail. |
| Extent of Parden disapproval | At pp.477–78 Powell rejects Parden's inference of congressional intent from broad statutory language and overrules it **to the extent** inconsistent with unmistakably clear enacted abrogation. Page478 n.8 expressly leaves the additional Commerce-power question undecided. |
| Clear prospective condition language | Page477 quotes White's Parden dissent: only when Congress expressly declares that undertaking specified regulated conduct will be deemed waiver should immunity be disallowed. This passage gives a source-supported distinction between Parden's general-language inference and an express condition. It is a four-Justice discussion, not an adjudication upholding a newly enacted condition or a complete controlling voluntariness test. |
| Scalia's separate concurrence, pp.495–96 | He declines reconsideration of Hans on the limited presentation and reads the Jones Act/FELA against the historically assumed immunity background. He would not read them as covering States and agrees Parden should be overruled. He does not join Powell's constitutional/history analysis or announce that all clearly conditioned future conduct constitutes consent. |
| White's concurrence, p.495 | He emphasizes that the Court does not displace Petty's retained Jones Act coverage construction for state-employed seamen. This underscores the difference between substantive statutory coverage and federal-forum immunity. |

The safe lesson is not “Welch upheld every clear prospective condition,” and not “Welch decided the validity of every clear prospective condition.” It rejects the inference used to make general FELA/Jones Act coverage consent to federal suit. The separately assumed ArticleI power question and the unpresented Texas express-waiver issue remain separate. Union Gas subsequently supplies its actual Commerce-power conclusion. A new express-condition argument must still identify which statute imposes what condition, the source of congressional power, what State choice supplies consent, and why that choice is meaningfully voluntary; the evidence here does not itself decide those questions.

## 3. The exact simulated Hilton public holding controls the retained FELA setting

Control recovered the canonical public entry from `terms/OT1991/output/OT_1991CHUNK3.md`; its exact [public copy](../sources/chunk11-root-scope/OT1991_CHUNK3_CANONICAL_PUBLIC.md) contains Hilton at lines68–90. The [source receipt](../sources/chunk11-root-scope/OT1991_CHUNK3_PUBLIC_RECEIPT.json) supplies byte/hash provenance. It matches the canonical Holdings entry already in the baseline.

Kennedy's controlling December16,1991 opinion is joined by Stone, White, Stevens and Souter: five of eight participants. Blackmun supplies a sixth judgment vote on a broader view of congressional power. O'Connor, joined by Scalia, dissents; Thomas did not participate. Ginsburg and Breyer were not on that Court and receive no inferred Hilton position.

The holding preserves FELA liability for a state-owned interstate railroad in a competent state court. It separates Parden's retained FELA coverage construction from the displaced inference that railroad operation waives federal-forum immunity. Congress kept the relevant language, and States/employees relied on the specialized railroad arrangement; South Carolina excluded FELA-covered employees from workers' compensation. Howlett supplies state-court enforcement of valid federal liability. The federal dismissal is not reopened, and entering commerce is not pronounced a general waiver.

This has two real implications. First, Souter's actual Hilton join supports neither resurrection of the original general-language Parden waiver nor a conclusion that statutory coverage alone establishes federal-forum consent. Second, Hilton did not adjudicate the later 1992 Patent/Trademark Acts' express language or adopt a general rule for every ArticleI damages action. A proposed distinction based on later unmistakable wording and a genuinely prospective, voluntary choice must be defended as a distinction; it is not already Hilton's holding. Conversely, the source cannot be reduced to an unexplained neutral reservation: the specific original FELA-operation waiver remains displaced and the state-court statutory coverage remains controlling.

## 4. What the cited Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer public positions do establish

Exact supporting passages are in the new [additional public excerpt file](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK11_IP_ADDITIONAL_PUBLIC_EXCERPTS.md). These are bounded analogies and actual prior positions, not target commitments.

- **Hess, November14,1994:** All three join Stevens's opinion that PATH is not an immune arm under its actual compact, finances and institutional structure. The opinion expressly preserves Hilton and distinguishes Feeney's federal-forum consent from arm status. It says no abrogation or waiver rule is enlarged. A separate commercial enterprise's lack of arm status is not a holding that an admitted State arm waives immunity by selling a product. Florida Prepaid's admitted arm status therefore cannot be erased with a market-activity label.
- **Seminole, March27,1996:** Souter, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, actually supports the constrained connected-officer route and avoidance of direct-State amenability. The conditional common-law/ArticleI explanation is not an operative consent ruling. Neither negotiation nor a generally regulated activity is declared waiver. The existing public text and Standards expressly retain the broader reservations.
- **Lane, June20,1996:** Souter writes, with Ginsburg joining, that federal program coverage does not itself establish clear consent to the particular Treasury damages remedy. Breyer joins Stevens's dissent finding sufficient consent in the coordinated enactments; the dissent accepts that consent must come from enacted law and Franklin alone cannot waive immunity. All three distinguish legal duty, forum/remedy authorization and actual entitlement, although they disagree on that statute's construction. None decides a State's commercial consent.
- **Schacht, June22,1998:** All three join the claim-specific immunity/removal holding. They accept that State immunity can be waived while expressly leaving whether removal itself waives it unresolved. That case cannot supply an already-decided litigation-waiver answer or a general voluntary-conduct test.
- **Cedar Rapids, March3,1999:** All three directly join the independent funding-notice holding: the express IDEA duty, existing regulation and Tatro construction give clear advance notice of the particular service obligation. The recipient cannot create surprise merely by emphasizing continuity or expense. This is genuine support for treating clearly stated prospective funding obligations as legally consequential; it involves accepted federal assistance and a specified service obligation, not compelled waiver from continued ordinary commerce. The opinion keeps distinct enforcement/remedy doctrines separate.
- **West, June14,1999:** Souter writes and Ginsburg/Breyer join the holding that the EEOC may administer compensation Congress expressly authorized through the coordinated TitleVII provisions. Clear remedy-specific federal consent is satisfied there; administrative convenience supplies no waiver. The result shows that the clarity requirement is applied to actual enacted language and can be satisfied. It does not establish Congress's power to abrogate State immunity or equate a State's commercial conduct with consent.

The cited materials thus support genuine distinctions among an entity that is not the State, an actual State waiver, express federal consent, a conditioned federal benefit, clear prospective substantive regulation, direct ArticleI abrogation, and the original displaced Parden inference. They do not establish that Souter, Ginsburg or Breyer has already adopted or already rejected the exact 1992 express-commercial-condition theory. That issue remains for independent modeling from the actual text, record, scope and constitutional source. The absence of such an earlier holding is not a party's failure to preserve or prove its present claim.
