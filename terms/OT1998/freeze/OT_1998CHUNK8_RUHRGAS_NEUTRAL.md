## Posture and supported record

Marathon Oil Company, Marathon International Oil Company and Marathon Petroleum Norge sued Ruhrgas AG in Texas state court on July 6, 1995, asserting state-law fraud and related business torts arising from a Norwegian North Sea gas venture. Ruhrgas is German and Norge Norwegian. The claims allege inducements to invest in the venture, injury to the value of a production license, three Houston meetings, and correspondence directed to Texas. These remain allegations, not findings adopted here. MPCN's gas-sales agreement selected Norwegian law and Stockholm arbitration; whether the plaintiffs' claims sufficiently relate to that agreement is disputed.

Ruhrgas removed and asserted three subject-matter grounds: diversity on the theory that Norge was fraudulently joined; federal-question jurisdiction based on the asserted foreign-relations character of the claims; and removal under 9 U.S.C. §205 for an action relating to a Convention arbitration agreement. Ruhrgas sought dismissal for lack of personal jurisdiction; Marathon sought remand for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction. This corrects the neutral brief's shorthand suggesting multiple Ruhrgas defendants or that the defendants challenged their own asserted subject-matter jurisdiction.

After jurisdictional discovery, the District Court dismissed for lack of personal jurisdiction without deciding subject-matter jurisdiction. It treated Texas's long-arm authorization as extending to constitutional limits and found the contacts insufficient. Its conclusions about the Houston meetings, absence of tortious Texas conduct, and lack of systematic and continuous Texas contacts are lower-court determinations. Their correctness is not independently established by this validation.

The panel vacated and directed remand to state court after rejecting all three asserted removal grounds. It found difficult Norwegian-law questions prevented the proposed fraudulent-joinder showing, rejected the proposed foreign-sovereignty basis, and regarded the claims as independent of the gas-sales agreement for §205 purposes. Rehearing en banc vacated that panel decision. Its conclusions are therefore not an operative adjudication of federal jurisdiction.

The en banc court required subject-matter jurisdiction to be determined first in removed cases and remanded to the District Court for that inquiry. It expressly withheld an opinion on the panel's resolution of those grounds. Its majority invoked jurisdictional restraint and protection of the state forum. Judge Higginbotham's dissent agreed that subject-matter jurisdiction ordinarily comes first but would permit an easier personal-jurisdiction disposition when it presents no complex state-law issue and subject-matter jurisdiction is difficult; it regarded this case as suitable. These competing lower-court positions are both available for analysis, without transferring either to the Court.

## Review question and strongest paths

The supplied question, stated in substance rather than certified grant language, asks whether subject-matter jurisdiction must invariably precede a personal-jurisdiction dismissal in a removed case. Ruhrgas seeks reversal of the mandatory sequence and permission for the personal-jurisdiction route. Marathon supports deciding whether the federal forum has subject-matter authority before it disposes of the removed action.

- **Retain the required sequence:** Federal judicial authority and the State's interest in deciding a case removed without jurisdiction may justify resolving subject-matter jurisdiction first. A personal-jurisdiction adjudication can affect later proceedings even though it does not decide liability. The strongest objection is that this treats distinct nonmerits thresholds as hierarchically ordered despite the absence of such a general rule in current Steel Co. The corresponding relief would retain the en banc direction for a subject-matter determination, not itself find jurisdiction absent or order state-court remand.
- **Permit a confined choice of threshold:** Dismissal for lack of personal jurisdiction denies adjudicatory authority over the defendant and grants no merits relief. A readily resolved personal-jurisdiction question without a difficult state-law issue may avoid an unnecessary and difficult federal-jurisdiction determination. The strongest objection is that convenience cannot authorize a federal merits judgment or disregard an actual statutory remand duty. The corresponding relief can reject the categorical sequencing rule and return the case for application or review under the lawful standard, without deciding any removal ground or independently adopting every district-court contact finding.

These are analytical paths grounded in the supported record, not invented party submissions. Ordinary subject-matter-first treatment remains a distinct possibility where that question is readily answered, personal jurisdiction is difficult, or important state-law issues favor initial treatment by the proper forum. Permission to choose a threshold would not create a command to use personal jurisdiction first.

## Entering law and statutory limits

The operative register provision is **Article III power before disputed statutory merits**, effective under *Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.*, June 2, 1997. It prohibits assuming disputed Article III power to decide contested statutory merits. Its express limits leave nonmerits sequencing unexhausted and preserve the distinct already-controlled-merits settings of *Norton* and *Avrech*. The holding does not itself decide the question here. The en banc court's characterization of historical Steel Co. cannot replace the current rule.

*NAACP, Los Angeles Branch v. Jones*, November 5, 1998, likewise forbids assumed standing before contested constitutional merits and expressly declines an exhaustive order among nonmerits issues. *Murphy Brothers*, April 5, 1999, separates removal timing, formal process and actual subject-matter jurisdiction; a timely removal establishes no jurisdiction. Its preserved-remand-ground instruction is case-specific. *El Paso Natural Gas*, May 3, 1999, gives a particular statutory coverage task priority over prudential tribal exhaustion; it does not impose a universal jurisdictional hierarchy.

Sections 1331, 1332 and 205 remain separate possible jurisdictional sources. The objective extract establishes the Convention-agreement-related condition invoked under §205, not a holding that these tort claims satisfy it. Neither fraudulent joinder nor foreign-relations jurisdiction is established. No full construction of those provisions, the Texas long-arm statute, or §§1447(c)–(d) is necessary for the bounded sequencing question. Any proposed sequencing permission must preserve an applicable statutory remand command and covered-remand review bar; the present appeal concerns a federal dismissal followed by an appellate remand to the District Court, not an established district-court order remanding to state court.
