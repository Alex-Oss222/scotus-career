## Reconciled operative rule and alternatives

**L1 — seven Associates.** When an impaired senior unsecured class objects and is not paid in full, a plan cannot reserve to old equity alone the opportunity to contribute new capital and acquire the reorganized debtor when old ownership supplies that exclusive eligibility. The opportunity is property received under the plan on account of the old interest within section 1129(b)(2)(B)(ii), even though the investment purchases the new ownership and the underlying property and contribution valuations are not challenged.

The statutory question is the cause of the preferential opportunity, not whether the judge made a numerical valuation error. Section 1121's lawful proposal-exclusivity period does not itself convey a confirmable ownership preference under section 1129. The two provisions do different work. No additional valuation of the opportunity is needed to recognize the economic right; no finding that a particular outsider would bid more supplies this decision. Ahlers supports recognizing economic ownership rights despite arguments minimizing their value and confines equity to the Code. Noland and Reorganized CF&I preserve the distinction between genuine statutory authority and an extra-statutory alteration of priority. Nobelman offers an analogy separating valuation from protected legal rights; its mortgage holding is not extended to decide this Chapter 11 issue by itself.

The strongest contrary argument is that statutory exclusivity and unchallenged confirmation findings already establish a permissible new-capital exchange, so an added competition requirement changes Congress's scheme. The seven answer that the old interest still determines who alone may obtain the opportunity. An accurate value for the asset and new contribution does not remove that separate legal preference. They do not reverse the unappealed exclusivity order or assert that lawful proposal exclusivity is itself forbidden property.

**L2 — Stevens.** The preserved absolute-priority objection does not defeat confirmation merely because the plan's new ownership goes to old holders who supply the specified new capital. In his construction, the new contribution accounts for receipt of the ownership; the lawful plan-making arrangement's eligibility is not an additional distributed asset requiring an extra exchange or mandatory market process. The unchallenged valuation and other confirmation findings remain operative on this appeal.

Stevens recognizes that proposal exclusivity does not immunize every plan and that preferential investment eligibility can have economic significance. He rejects the seven's conclusion that the opportunity here is a separate old-interest distribution notwithstanding the existing findings. Los Angeles Lumber's capital distinction and Ahlers's express reservation of its survival support his reading; his Noland/CF&I positions reject judicial replacement of enacted conditions, rather than compel a new competitive procedure. He does not certify unrecited findings, deem the installment promise immediately paid, find the opportunity valueless, excuse inadequate capital generally or declare that every proposed new-value plan is valid.

**L3 — categorical elimination of new value.** No reconciled commitment requires a holding that every former-owner investment is forbidden whenever a senior class remains unpaid. L1 resolves the exclusive opportunity without deciding the survival or full requirements of a general new-value corollary. Stevens rejects the proposed categorical bar through L2. Neither position converts the statutory phrase on account of into a redundant reference to any eventual former-owner participation.
