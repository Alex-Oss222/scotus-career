### Issue 1: Constitutional connection

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lopez (April 26, 1995) requires an identified interstate mechanism before aggregation. Hale, Rybar and Kirk (February 14, 1998) sustain §922(o)’s possession component as necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of the national post-1986 machinegun transfer/stock regime. Neither marketability, remote interstate provenance nor generic crime costs alone establishes federal power.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The §922(o) holdings do not decide this separate prohibited-person statute. A felon-possession restriction must be connected to the valid national restrictions on firearm supply to felons; a bare recital of past travel is not a substitute.
* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses; 18 U.S.C. §§922(d)(1),922(g)(1).

### Issue 2: Statutory nexus and proof

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Bass and Scarborough supply the statutory background for firearm nexus language. Lopez does not hold every jurisdictional element sufficient, but also does not require each regulated instance itself substantially to affect commerce.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** If the instruction sought a new contemporaneous or individually substantial effect, it demanded more than the statutory language and retained nexus construction. The actual requested instruction must be checked before a precise instructional mandate.
* **Other Governing Law:** Jackson v. Virginia; ordinary preservation and sufficiency standards.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** Fourth Circuit affirmance of the Middle District of North Carolina judgments, Nos. CR-96-93 and CR-96-95, Chief Judge Frank W. Bullock, Jr.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Defendants argue that past manufacture outside North Carolina is insufficient after Lopez and seek an instruction requiring more. The government invokes the express statutory nexus and the firearm-access restrictions on convicted felons.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Separate statutory proof from constitutional adequacy. Connecticut manufacture can prove past interstate movement without deciding why Congress may regulate the statutory class; the latter requires the identified regulatory mechanism.
