## 2. Completed June 22 increment

### Albertson’s

Kennedy’s unanimously supported Part I makes the ordinary federal vision qualification dispositive for the reviewed claim despite an actual later experimental-study waiver. The program did not require the employer to replace the ordinary qualification with study participation or justify the Government’s standard anew. This is a bounded regulatory-program holding, not a conclusion that waiver recipients could never drive lawfully, that the employee was unsafe, or that every exemption lacks effect. Other exemption systems and a separately enlarged post-waiver claim remain reserved. The individual-disability discussions in Part II are expressly noncontrolling and unnecessary to the judgment.

The case supplies no sovereign-consent rule, commercial-waiver test, FLSA defense, or class-certification standard. Its actual waiver must not be erased, but its legal treatment cannot be transferred to a State’s consent to suit. Stone’s adaptation disagreement, Thomas’s additional qualification rationale, and the differing Part II joins remain published positions in that statutory setting, not target commitments. Albertson’s own unresolved mitigation discussion does not undo the cumulative controlling rules supplied by completed Sutton and Murphy for law entering June 23.

### Kolstad

The controlling culpability rule concerns the employer’s perceived risk of violating federal rights; an independently egregious act is not required. The separately supported agency portion requires managerial authority and scope and recognizes a meaningful, implemented good-faith-compliance limit on managerial vicarious punitive liability. It does not establish a defense to ordinary liability, decide every allocation of proof, or adopt the four-Justice complete Restatement framework. The existing liability verdict and backpay remain; common vacatur does not supply a five-Justice command for a new punitive trial.

Employer attribution is expressly treated as part of the granted punitive-remedy inquiry. That matters to IP question scope, but it does not make every constitutional source or alternative defense fairly included in every grant. The statutory good-faith accommodation provision is distinct from the adopted agency limit. Neither provides a State-immunity defense, an FLSA liquidated-damages rule, a constitutional patent-deprivation fault standard, or approval of a class settlement’s punitive-damages restriction. The four Justices opposing the compliance limit disagree within statutory and agency doctrine; their position is not general opposition to remedial limits. Thomas’s culpability view likewise supplies no new constitutional due-process requirement.

### Murphy

The six-Justice actual-disability rule considers mitigation actually used, including residual and adverse effects; it imposes no hypothetical perfect-treatment or additional-treatment duty. The treated-state evidentiary application was not separately within granted question 1. The unanimous perceived-class framework does not make an employer’s regulatory motive dispositive; the six-Justice application separately sustains summary judgment. Those disability rules do not alter any June 23 core.

The scope holdings are material to both IP matters. Review was expressly restricted to questions 1 and 4. Preservation and reply briefing did not enlarge that grant to a distinct application, and all nine declined the expressly ungranted DOT-qualification and essential-function questions. That agreement is not common support for a different ultimate judgment or a freestanding merits rule about those defenses. Murphy expressly preserves X-Citement Video’s properly supported respondent alternative for an unchanged favorable judgment. It neither establishes the disputed DOT grounds nor categorically excludes every Article I argument in a different case. A model must compare the actual grant, party posture, rights affected, and factual predicate.

### Olmstead

The six-Justice integration holding reaches unjustified isolation in administering existing Title II services, subject to supported professional suitability, individual choice, and reasonable accommodation without fundamental alteration. Its **five-Justice Part II** requires consideration of the relevant service system, resources, other recipients, and equitable administration. The State bears the regulatory demonstration; isolated additional cost against the whole mental-health budget is insufficient accounting. No Georgia defense, plan compliance, current medical condition, or lawful queue priority is found.

The **four-Justice O’Connor plan concurrence**, joined by Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, treats its specified effectively working plan and reasonably moving queue as conditionally sufficient without an additional disruption showing. It is **not controlling**. Stone’s separate requirement connecting plan evidence to fundamental alteration is also **not an additional Court holding**. Kennedy adopts only the narrower rejection of isolated-cost accounting, not full Part II or the plan rule; Stevens joins the confined defense-remand judgment without Part II. These agreements cannot be aggregated into majority plan sufficiency. Breyer’s join in Kennedy’s clinical cautions leaves his full Court and plan-concurrence joins intact. Kennedy’s separate discrimination inquiry and Thomas/Scalia’s statutory disagreement do not displace the majority integration rule.

Olmstead expressly treats the duty’s fundamental-alteration limit as fairly included in its granted statutory question. Its separate §5 question is outside the grant; independent immunity, damages, constitutional claims, and classwide closure are not decided. The two individual plaintiffs’ prospective-relief action supplies no Article I abrogation or Rule 23 holding. It neither turns budget scarcity into a general sovereign defense nor makes actual constitutional injury unnecessary to every §5 enactment. The separately reported later district judgment is not independently vacated, and no hearing, new evidence, plan procedure, or medical examination is commanded. These boundaries reinforce, rather than change, the June 23 packets’ separation of proceedings and supported remedial limits.

## 4. Exact prior-public continuity: Hilton and the neutral IP addendum

The recovered **December 16, 1991 Hilton** entry is now copied in full in the dated compilation. Kennedy’s controlling opinion was joined by Stone, White, Stevens, and Souter. Blackmun supplied a sixth judgment vote on a broader congressional-power view; O’Connor dissented with Scalia. Thomas did not participate, and Ginsburg and Breyer were not on the Court. No position is imputed to the latter three from Hilton.

The actual majority retained FELA coverage and competent-state-court enforcement for the specialized state-owned interstate railroad setting: longstanding statutory construction, Congress’s retained text, and compensation reliance, including South Carolina’s workers’-compensation exclusion. Howlett supplies enforcement of valid federal liability. The holding separates that retained coverage from displaced constructive federal-forum waiver; it neither reopens the federal action nor announces that entering commerce generally waives immunity.

For **Alden**, Kennedy, Stevens, and Souter therefore have actual published support for that bounded state-forum result and rationale. A model cannot reduce Hilton to an unexplained reservation or assume its reasoning automatically controls every Article I remedy. O’Connor and Scalia’s clearer-statement/inseparability dissent is an actual contrary position, but not an already adopted universal constitutional bar in a State’s own courts. The FLSA extension or distinction still needs independent reasoning. This is additional precision about existing prior law and continuity, not a changed Hilton holding or a predicted Alden vote.

For the IP matters, the authorized [Union Gas/Welch/Hilton neutral addendum](OT_1998CHUNK11_IP_UNION_GAS_WELCH_HILTON_ADDENDUM.md), its [public excerpts](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK11_IP_ADDITIONAL_PUBLIC_EXCERPTS.md), and [exact Standards](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK11_IP_ADDENDUM_STANDARDS.md) remain incorporated. Union Gas concerns an actual private third-party claim and retains its five-vote constitutional conclusion without converting Brennan’s four-Justice rationale into a complete majority test; no unsupported corporate citizenship is assumed. Welch’s fractured reasoning does not already validate every express commercial condition. Hilton does not revive original constructive waiver. Hess arm status, Lane federal damages consent, Schacht claim-specific removal, Cedar Rapids accepted funding obligations, and West express federal remedial consent retain their different predicates. None establishes a target Justice’s vote on the 1992 Acts.

The exact relevant Standards retain their stated effective authorities: **“State immunity of bistate compact entities,” Hess, November 14, 1994; “Rehabilitation Act federal-program coverage and damages consent,” Lane, June 20, 1996; “Removal of federal claims alongside an immune state claim,” Schacht, June 22, 1998; and “IGRA State-action authorization and unresolved amenability,” Seminole, March 27, 1996.** No separately later wording authority is stated for those entries.
