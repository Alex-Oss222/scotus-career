## Article III: the same committee's recurring policy controversy

Every Associate's threshold commitment is sustained on the same bounded proposition: the Natural Law Party committee's challenge to the candidate-selection policy is capable of repetition yet evading review. The claimant is the existing committee formed generally to promote its candidates, distinguished in the stipulated background from the separately named committee formed for Marcus's 1996 campaign. Its participation alongside its excluded candidates across congressional districts connects its electoral function to this broadcaster's actual challenged selection. The general editorial policy and the established recurring Iowa Press program complete the basis for a reasonable expectation of another short electoral-access controversy involving this committee.

That is an inference from the frozen facts. It is not an additional finding that a particular candidate ran again, that another debate was scheduled, that an exclusion occurred after 1996, that the policy was affirmatively proved unchanged in 1999, or that the committee performed any particular later campaign activity. The occurrence of several challenged candidate exclusions within the established electoral programming gives the committee's general purpose concrete application. Purpose or litigation persistence alone would not suffice. Nor does the absence of evidence of dissolution or policy change shift the jurisdictional burden to IPTV. The affirmative organizational relationship and actual pattern carry the inference.

The strongest contrary path is substantial: every identified candidacy and broadcast belongs to 1996; the next nominee's identity and support are unknown; a weekly interview program need not hold a particular future debate. Nonetheless, a reasonable recurrence finding does not require a scheduled repetition or a guarantee that every future candidate will be excluded. The continuing committee's own expected interaction with the contested selection practice distinguishes the case from a conjecture about unrelated future plaintiffs. The pre-election period is too short for ordinary complete appellate review. Both elements of the exception are expressly resolved; short duration alone does no jurisdictional work on the same-claimant element.

Norman supplies the party-and-election comparison, but its actual continuing party-qualification consequences are absent here and are not imported. Chandler demonstrates recurrence through an individual's stated future candidacy; it does not make that particular proof indispensable for an ongoing organizational claimant. Spencer retains its requirement of a concrete continuing controversy and the same claimant's reasonably recurring injury. No general election exception, presumed future injury, relaxed post-grant jurisdiction or reputational consequence is adopted.

The independent handoff also invokes the current neutral projection's NAACP and Paradise propositions. This reconciliation has not reopened that projection or their Records. Their stated functions are consistent with the narrower analysis here: jurisdiction must be decided rather than assumed, and the defendant must actually be connected to the claimant's challenged injury. The present result independently rests on the supplied Norman, Chandler and Spencer law and frozen Marcus facts; those references supply no missing factual premise.

## Merits: standards, application and findings

The affirmance commitments independently assess constitutional reasonableness; they do not treat the advisory jury's answers as the constitutional rule. The district judge adopted the answers independently, assessed credibility and credited Miller. The findings include failures to prove departure from policy, selection solely by Democratic or Republican nomination, newsworthiness or exclusion for disagreement with political opinions, as well as affirmative findings of genuine editorial judgment and selective invitations. Failure of proof is not an affirmative finding of every contrary fact. The Eighth Circuit's no-clear-error treatment of the viewpoint finding does not eliminate judicial review of reasonableness.

On the supported affirmance ground, the actual selective format, informing purpose, editorial-integrity policies and testimony concerning electoral engagement and public interest establish a reasonable relationship between selection and the program. Miller's considerations remain evidence of professional judgment, not a binding checklist, numerical threshold or a finding that every candidate lacked every characteristic. The short audience-interest definition in Instruction 15 is not considered in isolation. The general professional norms are not a license to exclude viewpoints.

The specific contrary case is preserved. Petitioners asserted Newell lacked material candidate information, sought some information only after suit, failed to conduct meaningful individual review, and excluded Marcus despite asserted publicity and invitations from opponents. The fundraising comparisons and Professor Mack objections remain litigant contentions supported by identified record citations in the filings, not newly adjudicated facts. Their force is not reduced to ballot eligibility. The affirmance commitments conclude that the supplied claims do not establish clear error in the credited factual findings or compel a different constitutional reasonableness judgment. The figures do not establish an adopted dollars rule, the candidates' equivalence across district or office, or equal electoral significance. This is not a finding that every allegation is false, nor a rule that subjective hostility is the only possible defect.

Regan's objection to governmental content-based restrictions on private publication does not invalidate every content-sensitive editorial selection in a nonpublic forum. Forbes supplies the operative forum distinction. A campaign's political ideas cannot themselves justify exclusion because officials dislike them; Miller's ideas-versus-election testimony is not approved as a categorical disqualification. Offers of other programming do not cure discrimination or independently prove the exclusion lawful.

Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg maintain the distinct standards objection of their published Forbes position. Iowa provides more descriptive detail than a bare label, but Instruction 15 and the illustrative considerations still leave officials to decide how interesting competing candidacies are without sufficiently definite restraints under that position. The asserted information and comparison problems explain why reviewability matters; they are not findings of improper motive. Their contrary result preserves the Forbes disagreement rather than claiming that the controlling Forbes opinion already requires it. This is a finite standards objection to the allocation of a government-provided candidate platform, not a categorical prohibition on newsworthiness judgments or an entitlement for all ballot-qualified candidates.

## Equitable relief and exact join boundaries

The actual prayer includes correction or retraction of program-guide language describing the Republican and Democratic nominees as the major candidates. That demand is preserved as a separate remedial question; the neutral packet supplies no current dissemination, reputational consequence, damages claim or independent guide cause of action.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer support denial of guide correction insofar as it is requested as relief for the reviewed forum violation they reject. They make no independent merits adjudication of the guide's language and no finding that the guide has continuing effects. Their affirmance concerns the common selection-policy claim and its derivative relief; it does not silently decide an independently live individual claim on an unestablished basis.

Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would return the guide request for consideration of its relation to permissible equitable relief and any continuing injury that relief could redress. They establish no correction entitlement. Their remand of the live policy claim does not itself establish jurisdiction over an independent guide claim. The past broadcasts cannot be restored, and no future broadcast, inclusion, damages, statutory access remedy, or declaration of actual viewpoint hostility is ordered by their position.
