## 2. What the historical writings establish

The historical judgment reversed the Ninth Circuit. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer joined the Court's opinion; Stevens concurred in the judgment. The seven Associates in the historical Court opinion agreed that the search-related interruption did not violate Gabbert's occupational liberty, accepted the specific-source discussion concerning an unreasonable search, and expressly left the clearly-established-law question unreached after the merits conclusion. They did not decide the separately pleaded Fourth Amendment claim. Neither the syllabus nor the Court opinion supplies a historical vote on a separately adopted conduct-time immunity holding.

The opinion distinguished complete professional exclusion from the brief interruption before it. Its final formulation included a search calculated to annoy or prevent consultation. That language must be read with its asserted occupational interest and actual search-related interruption; it does not establish that a warrant defeats every independently protected right or that every imaginable temporary interference is constitutionally harmless. The source paragraph addresses the objection to execution of a search as unreasonable. Reading it as universal displacement would go beyond its identified subject and would disregard the distinct merits analysis.

Stevens independently found no deprivation of Gabbert's own liberty or property on the described record. He relied on the absence of evidence of adverse income, reputation, clientele or qualification effects and, separately, the absence of real evidence or allegation of substantial prejudice to the client. He did not require permanent exclusion from practice, announce economic loss as an element of every constitutional injury, or decide Baker's rights. His objection was to treating the possible existence of another constitutional protection as sufficient to extinguish a due-process violation. His historical writing therefore strongly confirms M and the independent-protection reservation; it does not support an immunity-only account of his historical judgment.

There is no historical judgment departure among the eight Associates. The important reconciliation questions concern the breadth of M, the exact function of S and whether Q is actually reached. History is evidence for those choices, not governing law in this proceeding.

## 3. Final proposition levels and publication limits

### M. Adopt the record-limited occupational-liberty merits ground

**Operative rule:** Liberty to pursue a profession does not guarantee that every occasion for providing professional services will be free from government interruption. The search-related interruption of consultation shown here, with no prohibition on practice, impairment of professional qualifications or supported consequential interference with Gabbert's legal practice, does not establish deprivation of his Fourteenth Amendment liberty to pursue his profession, even assuming the prosecutors deliberately arranged the timing to obstruct consultation.

This is a conclusion about the protected interest, the nature of the interference and this record. Duration alone does not decide the claim. Neither permanent professional exclusion, lost fees, damaged reputation nor substantial client prejudice becomes a compulsory element in every occupational-liberty case. The distinct absence-of-evidence recitations explain this case's failure to establish the asserted professional deprivation; they are not a damages checklist. No finding of lawful search execution, benign motive, waiver, conceded client rights or absence of every possible constitutional injury follows.

Roth and the vocation authorities do not identify a personal guarantee against each interrupted service. Collins requires an actual underlying constitutional deprivation and cannot be expanded from its voluntary-workplace omissions into a rule against all affirmative-interference claims. Albright supplies no controlling synthesized due-process rule and no palpable-consequence prerequisite. Soldal and James Daniel Good preserve genuinely independent protections; West Covina applies that distinction without deciding this occupational question. Haddle addresses injury under a different statute, not a constitutional entitlement to uninterrupted employment or practice. Lewis's emergency-pursuit culpability standard does not govern this planned investigation.

The strongest opposing argument remains that deliberate obstruction at the very time counsel is needed can injure professional activity without lasting economic harm. M takes that argument seriously but rejects the claim on the presented interest and evidence. It does not convert an unproved additional professional injury into a fact, treat interruption as nonoccurrence, or decide a separate client's claim. The absence of substantial client prejudice is separately reported and corroborative of the limited episode; it is not the definition of the lawyer's own protected liberty.

**Direct joins:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No aggregation of distinct rationales or Marks claim is needed. M is the adopted sufficient merits ground supported by every Associate.

### S. Keep the specific-source explanation confined to the search objection

**Exact supported proposition:** To the extent Gabbert objects that the search itself was unreasonable because of its execution or timing, the Fourth Amendment supplies the governing standard for that objection. That classification does not dispose of a genuinely independent occupational deprivation merely because a search also occurred. The independent occupational claim is resolved by M; the merits of the separate Fourth Amendment claim are not before the Court.

Graham supplies this actual-coverage rule. Lanier preserves it while rejecting categorical exclusion of every due-process claim; Albright supplies no controlling broader displacement rationale. The existing public Albright writings and West Covina preserve independently applicable protections. S is therefore a source distinction and explanation of scope, not a second independently sufficient ground for rejecting every occupational claim. It cannot be stated as a holding that the pleaded Fourth Amendment claim absorbs the entire interference allegation, or that overlap itself defeats due process.

**Direct joins if this additional explanatory paragraph is included:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens accepts the general actual-coverage principle and the reservation of the Fourth Amendment claim but does not join this additional case-specific classification. His refusal is not a vote for a constitutional violation or against M.
