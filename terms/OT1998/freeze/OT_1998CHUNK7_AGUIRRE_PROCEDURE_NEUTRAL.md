## Conditional burden and distinct standards

The quoted 1995 regulation has a trigger: evidence must indicate that one or more withholding-denial grounds apply. When that trigger is satisfied, the applicant bears the burden of proving by a preponderance of the evidence that the indicated grounds do not apply. An analysis invoking that allocation must identify the evidence indicating the relevant ground, determine the legal trigger, and evaluate the actual record under the resulting burden. Absence of a BIA brief is not itself the indicated substantive ground and cannot substitute for that sequence.

The reported bus burnings and assaults may be invoked as evidence relevant to the statutory serious-nonpolitical-crime ground. Their legal significance still depends on the adopted construction and the actual evidentiary record. Political purpose and context remain relevant to that inquiry under the supported agency framework; a missing appellate submission does not delete existing testimony or automatically prove that the applicant failed to carry the conditional burden.

Former section 1253(h)(2)(C)'s serious-reasons inquiry is distinct from the regulation's preponderance allocation once triggered. Neither is a criminal conviction requirement. They also remain distinct from the persecution-risk requirement for withholding and the standard for judicial review of agency findings. The BIA did not decide the persecution-risk question; the supplement reports that it applied the exclusion while assuming that showing might be made. The separate discretionary asylum decision must not be merged with withholding's statutory bar or treated as resolved by this procedural correction.

## Strongest supported review and remand paths

The following are analytical alternatives, not claims that every formulation appeared in an unrecovered filing.

**Resolve the presented legal question on the existing review record.** The Court may assess statutory boundaries, permissible agency interpretation and the Ninth Circuit's review without deciding the newly asserted translation allegation. The strongest basis for separating them is that the allegation was not presented below and is now the subject of an existing agency motion. That route must not declare the allegation false or preordain the motion's result. If the adopted law and supported findings permit a direct disposition, its merits ground must be stated independently of the missing BIA brief. Neither the pending motion nor the conditional burden automatically requires another hearing.

**Remand under a corrected legal-review framework.** If the Ninth Circuit displaced a permissible agency construction or failed to apply the proper review sequence, a remand may identify the legal and record questions remaining for lawful review. Conversely, deference cannot cure an interpretation outside the statute or an actual failure of reasoned administration. Any remand must identify the defect that makes further consideration necessary; general recognition of agency authority does not itself establish either correctness or insufficiency of the present Board decision. A return through the Court of Appeals is not automatically an order to the BIA for new factfinding, and the regulation does not authorize resolving an application by default.

**Further consideration of the alleged record error.** The strongest concern supporting additional attention is that a material translation or transcription error, if established, could affect the conduct attributed to the applicant or the regulatory trigger and showing. But the allegation's newness, materiality, proper forum and available procedural authority must be addressed before it supplies a judicial ground for relief. The existing BIA motion provides an actual procedure in which the claim has been presented; its filing proves neither entitlement nor futility. A court selecting a translation-based remand or other procedural accommodation must state the lawful basis and supported need despite the claim's history, not infer error from the allegation or from an unavailable original transcript. No generic order for a new hearing, automatic stay or jurisdictional dismissal follows from this extraction.
