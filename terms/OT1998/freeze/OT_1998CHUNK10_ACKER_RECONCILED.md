### Characterization and statutory consent

The provisional six uniformly treated the charge as compensation taxation. Further review changes O’Connor and Breyer to their historical contrary conclusion. Their objection does not rest on an occupational label alone or on an actual county injunction. It concerns the combined operation of the unlawful-work provision, liability triggered by engaging in work and entitlement to pay, allocation according to work within the County, and exemption of occupations subject to other state licensing charges. All are already in the clean source materials. The absence of a proved restriction on sitting was also consistent with the historical dissent and is not a new premise defeating it.

Their common rule is that the county cannot impose a direct charge on performing federal judicial functions; §111 consents to compensation taxation, not a fee on those functions merely measured by compensation. The Buck Act addresses the federal-area jurisdiction objection and does not independently consent to a direct federal-function tax. Their comparison is functional and cumulative, not an enumeration of mandatory factors for every levy. O’Connor's Davis join and Breyer's Farm Credit opinion do not reject this characterization; Blaze expressly preserves direct federal burdens and actual instrumentalities. Thus no changed prior simulated position justifies converting either into a compensation-tax vote here.

Stevens, Kennedy and Ginsburg retain the compensation-tax position. Scalia, Souter and Thomas adopt it conditionally. The levy sought in these collection actions operates as a tax on compensation within §111, which is not restricted to a conventional income tax bearing a particular state-law name. A compensation measure alone would not save every federal-function license, but the presented revenue demand, absence of an established restriction on performance, and enacted operation support the statutory characterization. No power to license federal judges is approved. The Buck Act need not be decided by these six. No hypothetical withholding burden, unadmitted numerical workforce assertion, new collection practice or actual coercive order enters this determination.

### Discrimination, preservation and remedy

The preservation refresh correctly withdraws the premise that discrimination was never adjudicated and correctly distinguishes permission to defend a favorable judgment from compulsory review. It develops a lawful preservation-first remand. That option has been tested independently against immediate consideration of the actually renewed defense on the established record.

The concrete timeline comparison does not support the new remand as the likely choice of the six who sustain the compensation tax. X-Citement Video's favorable-judgment rule and Lebron's bounded review discretion permit reach. Their limits do not prohibit resolving this presented alternative. AT&T refused to invent an unadvanced independent theory; this defense was decided in the district court and actually renewed here. West preserves initial appellate attention to properly raised alternatives after correction of a distinct exhaustion premise, but creates no mandatory remand rule and adjudicates neither the availability nor the tax substance of this defense. The historical decision itself knew about intermediate nonpursuit and nevertheless reached the renewed same-judgment alternative. The clean refresh supplies that same fact, not a changed omission, new question, new relief request or express surrender. No Justice-specific change explains why these six would now refuse present consideration solely because a lawful remand exists.

The six therefore elect to reach the renewed discrimination defense and reject it on the supplied record. The actual comparison is between similarly situated federal and state officeholders subject to the compensation levy; the clean source confirms state judges' payment and the ordinance's inclusion of state, local and federal officers. The exemption for occupations subject to other state or county licensing charges is not on this record a classification favoring state compensation over federal compensation. The assertion that federal judges cannot personally fall within that exemption does not itself establish the forbidden source distinction. Davis continues to forbid source discrimination and requires attention to relevant comparison; it does not demand new findings or a categorical equivalence among every licensed occupation before this presented argument can be rejected. Stevens's broader comparison approach also supports rejection without adopting a new exemption from nondiscrimination law. The result neither treats the lower rejection as automatically correct nor finds intentional waiver from appellate nonpursuit.

This is a confined §111 decision on the present tax and record. Future exemptions favoring state officials, an actually demonstrated source distinction, or a genuinely coercive restriction would present different premises. None is invented. No proposition relies on the historical oral-argument percentages, which were outside the record, or on unstated facts about any individual judge's license.

O’Connor and Breyer do not join the §111-validity disposition: their direct-function characterization independently sustains the existing judgment. Their original conditional preservation-first remand drops out of their operative disposition. They need not separately decide whether the exemption structure violates the source-discrimination condition of a consent provision they find inapplicable.

The Compensation Clause remains distinct and undecided. The six's judgment reverses the intergovernmental-immunity basis and remands for proceedings consistent with the compensation-tax and nondiscrimination holdings, leaving proper treatment of that separate, unreviewed constitutional basis to the lower court. It does not direct unconditional tax collection, quantify liability, automatically vacate the district discrimination rejection, reopen the discrimination issue, or award new evidence. The same remand word must not conceal the material difference from the independent model's preservation-first discrimination remand.
