## Component commitments

Seven Justices—Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—find cause for the omitted suppression claim in the actual combination of nonpublic police-held material, reasonable open-file reliance and the Commonwealth's later complete-disclosure representation while state proceedings continued. All eight retain confidence in capital guilt under the correct cumulative Brady standard on the established five-document suppression, taking account of defense preparation and the actual joint-active-participation instruction. Their agreement does not approve the appellate court's characterization of all predicate facts as undisputed or turn counsel's closing into a confession.

## Governing standards and preserved boundaries

**Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility**, present operation effective Kyles,April19,1995, governs all lawful combined uses: impeachment, preparation, corroboration and investigation. Reasonable probability means confidence undermined, not probable acquittal, per-document materiality or insufficiency of the remaining proof. Police working on the prosecution's case are within its disclosure responsibility. No finding of deliberate suppression is necessary or made. A completed material violation receives no second duplicative harmlessness inquiry. Independent habeas conditions remain separate.

**Independent psychiatric-assistance injury after surviving capital eligibility**, effective Tuggle,October30,1995, preserves the distinction between eligibility and selection. Its unanimous rule prevents an intact aggravator from answering a different penalty injury by itself; it supplies no new Brady burden, assumed psychiatric violation here or automatic sentence relief. The relevant wrong remains suppression assessed under Bagley/Kyles.

**Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments**, effective for the identified capital extension in Carriger,April16,1998, retaining Lindh's June23,1997 component, places this pre-April24,1996 application under former law for amended §2254(d) and replacement §2254(e)(1). No Chapter154 qualification is established, and capital status alone supplies none. No blanket exemption from every AEDPA provision, §2254(e)(2) ruling or automatic hearing follows. All eight apply this controlling temporal law irrespective of earlier positions.

Carrier, Amadeo and Coleman preserve external impediment, reasonable availability, attorney-error distinctions and cause/prejudice. O'Sullivan,June7, distinguishes default from an available unexhausted route and retains the existing gateways; it does not decide this suppression excuse. Calderon v.Coleman's category-specific capital-instruction standard, Neder's preserved omitted-element review and Lilly's confrontation analysis do not replace Brady's built-in materiality inquiry. Neither Chapman nor another harmlessness burden is appended to a completed Brady claim.

## Enhanced issue comparisons

### Cause and the default gateway

**C1: external impediment established here.** The missing five were nonpublic case-police documents; counsel actually used the open file; the Commonwealth later asserted full disclosure and delivery of Brady material while state habeas remained pending. That representation did not cause the original trial omission or precede the state petition, but reinforced reliance during the remaining opportunity to pursue the independent claim. General knowledge that a witness had interviews, a photo procedure and evolving recollection is not knowledge of these documents' specific contradictions. The Fourth Circuit's inference that an unmade discretionary request would likely have succeeded does not itself establish reasonable factual availability.

The strongest objection is that counsel had reason to investigate and in fact alleged deficient Brady practice and cross-examination in state habeas, yet never requested available discovery. C1 does not disregard that conduct or establish a right to open files. It distinguishes reasonable use of the State's chosen disclosure arrangement plus a continuing completeness representation from ordinary failure to pursue known favorable evidence. A Brady label alone, prosecutorial silence alone, or any possible undisclosed record would not establish cause.

**C2: reasonable availability and unpursued discovery defeat cause.** Trial information and existing state-habeas allegations identified a concrete line of inquiry before the later representation; discretionary discovery did not require possession of the hidden documents. Kyles expressly preserves diligence concerning known evidence. Its strongest objection is that it can make defendants distrust an apparently complete disclosure and turns hypothetical access into actual availability. C2 must rest on the specific earlier warning signs and available procedural avenue, not a universal duty to search every police file or an unsupported prediction that the state judge would certainly grant access. Thomas adopts this narrower diligence assessment; the other seven find those signs insufficient in the actual represented-disclosure setting.

Cause does not establish prejudice. For a phase at which cumulative suppression is materially prejudicial under Brady, the same demonstrated injury supplies the relevant prejudice here; no separate duplicative harmlessness showing is imposed. The positions finding no materiality therefore deny relief even after accepting cause. No independent actual-innocence gateway is established or newly decided.

### Favorability, established suppression and guilt

All eight treat the five established documents—1,3,4,5,6—as favorable impeachment material withheld from the defense within the prosecution's case-team responsibility under controlling Kyles. The finding does not decide disclosure of2,7,8. Those three can be considered only at their proven status and for lawful contextual use; they are not added to the suppressed set. The five reveal identification uncertainty, changes in vehicle impressions, reconstruction through the daughter and purchase/timing evidence, normal-appearing behavior as vehicles separated, and photo-assisted victim identification. Those are different mutually reinforcing uses, not five interchangeable credibility attacks. They do not prove fabrication, planted information or police coercion.

**G1: retain confidence in capital guilt.** Full disclosure would materially strengthen cross-examination and investigation of the mall account, including preparation to contest the apparent independence and confidence of that witness. The known general memory evolution and newspaper account do not make those specifics cumulative in every respect. Nevertheless the witness did not observe the killing; other witnesses, car driving/possession, victim property, forensic/clothing evidence and Tudor's account supply a distinct evidentiary chain. The defense's supported Henderson evidence and attacks on Tudor remain part of that assessment. The instruction allowed joint active participation in the fatal acts and did not require identifying Strickler as the sole person who dropped the rock. Impeachment of mall dominance consequently does not create a reasonable probability of a different capital verdict on this record.

G1's strongest objection is that the apparently disinterested eyewitness organized the narrative, supported the predicates and shaped defense concessions; her collapse could alter how jurors assessed the remaining circumstantial proof. G1 answers that concern by considering a defense without those concessions and with stronger impeachment, not by treating closing as sworn proof. The established missing evidence does not expose a common contaminating source for the independent killing/possession evidence in the way Kyles's provenance and alternative-suspect material affected its record. This is a relationship-of-evidence judgment, not an arithmetic tally or sufficiency test.

**G2: guilt-level materiality and a new-trial writ.** The alternative views the changed mall narrative as sufficiently undermining the abduction/robbery and active-participation theory to produce a reasonable probability of a lesser verdict. Its strongest objection is that Henderson's active role remains compatible with Strickler's joint active killing and that the documents do not directly undermine every independent link. All eight reject G2 on that ground here while acknowledging its lawful test. They do not require proof of factual innocence or sole-perpetrator identification. No post-verdict presentence or allocution assertion is added to what this jury heard; the dispute about later retrial admissibility is unnecessary to G1.

The actual instruction's invalid abduction-with-intent-to-defile alternative is not ignored, but this grant does not independently reopen the defaulted instruction claim. G1 examines the actual active-participation charge and the supported valid robbery alternative in evaluating the claimed suppression injury. It does not adjudicate a new general-verdict challenge or pretend every instructed alternative was legally valid.

### Capital selection, independent from eligibility

**P1: death-selection materiality.** The eyewitness's portrayal of Strickler as the aggressive instigator and Henderson as subordinate supplied a basis for evaluating their relative culpability beyond the minimum of joint capital participation. The identified predecision sentencing submission specifically locates prosecution use of the leadership portrayal at Sentencing Transcript p.15. It is used as a supported attributed recitation, not a claim that the complete transcript was independently read or that leadership was the sole sentencing argument. The five documents together would permit the defense to challenge the reliability and reconstruction of that portrait, weaken its extension from mall conduct to the killing, and argue for a different individualized punishment even while capital guilt remains secure.

P1's strongest objection is the independent prior-crime/future-danger case, the brutal rock killing/vileness evidence and Tudor's attribution, which the Fourth Circuit relied on at sentencing. P1 considers those aggravating bases and does not erase them. Their survival establishes substantial support for death, but does not restore confidence in the selection when the principal disinterested narrative assigning relative leadership would be materially different. No single document must carry that inference alone; no finding that the jury necessarily relied on leadership is made.

**P2: no reasonable probability of a different punishment.** The witness described earlier events, while the jury had independent evidence of active fatal conduct, the manner of killing and the criminal-history danger case. Additional challenge to mall leadership, though useful, does not sufficiently change the penalty picture to undermine confidence in selection. Its strongest objection is that this repeats eligibility and treats powerful aggravation as conclusive. P2 expressly addresses the distinct life/death choice and gives the leadership use weight; it finds that use insufficiently central in the whole supported penalty account. It does not claim that one surviving aggravator automatically resolves Brady.

The source record permits this bounded disagreement. Neither side uses the unverified Tudor recantation, assumes contents of an unread presentence report, invents additional mitigation, or states a complete-transcript certification. An assessment requiring the exact full penalty argument, a factual dispute about the quoted leadership use, or new authenticity findings would need that particular source or a separately justified remand. No such new finding is necessary for the commitments recorded here.

- **Cause:** seven direct C1 commitments; Thomas adopts C2. The seven do not announce automatic cause for every suppression allegation. Thomas's alternative merits views remain alternatives, not joins in their threshold ground.
- **Conviction-level materiality:** all eight support G1 under the controlling cumulative standard. Thomas's merits ground is expressly alternative to default. This does not decide whether a future opinion obtains his unconditional join in a merits holding after a contrary cause ruling; his current merits assessment itself is specified.
- **Penalty:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support P1 and conditional sentence relief. O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy support P2 after cause; Thomas supports denial on default and alternatively P2. The four P1 and four P2/default commitments are not aggregated into a Court result before Stone enters.
- **P1 remedy:** reverse the judgment directing denial of the penalty Brady component and remand for the district court to issue a conditional writ requiring Virginia, within a reasonable time fixed by that court, to provide a lawful new sentencing proceeding or lawful nondeath disposition. Capital guilt and the other convictions are not vacated by this sentence-only relief. Failure to comply requires vacatur of the death sentence under the writ, not release from independently valid imprisonment. No number of days, unconditional release, adjudicated innocence, mandated life term, new guilt trial or automatic evidentiary hearing is ordered.
- **P2/default remedy:** affirm denial of the reviewed suppression claim without reopening proportionality review, standalone instruction issues, withdrawn ineffective assistance or alleged recantation. No execution order or new scheduling act is inferred.
