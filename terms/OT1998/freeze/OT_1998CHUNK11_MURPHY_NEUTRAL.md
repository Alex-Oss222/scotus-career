## Corrected posture, record and relief

Murphy seeks reversal of the coverage-based summary judgment and further proceedings; UPS seeks affirmance. The approved “Relief Requested: Affirm” line supplies only the respondent’s request. The circuit resolved actual disability by applying its mitigating-measures rule and quoting the treating physician’s statement that medicated Murphy functions normally in everyday activity. On regarded-as coverage it reasoned that UPS acted on perceived DOT noncompliance rather than unsubstantiated fears of stroke or heart attack. It did **not** conduct the detailed class-of-jobs inquiry stated in the approved synopsis; that inquiry appears in the district court and parties’ competing arguments. Paragraph14 expressly declined qualification/accommodation and the federal-regulation defense. The district court had also addressed those independent grounds. Do not describe them as appellate holdings or automatic grounds of affirmance.

Murphy had longstanding severe hypertension, approximately250/160 without medication according to the circuit’s recitation, and took medication. He worked as a mechanic for about22 years before UPS. UPS hired him in August1994 after an examiner issued a DOT health card although measuring186/124. A UPS nurse later flagged that reading. September26 readings were160/102 and164/104, as the filings/JA recite. UPS discharged him on October5,1994, explaining that he did not meet DOT requirements. UPS mechanics performed road calls and road tests in commercial vehicles; Murphy sometimes was the only mechanic on duty. Murphy disputed whether driving was an essential rather than peripheral function, not that he ever drove. He obtained another mechanic job within two to three weeks according to the district-court recitation quoted by UPS. These facts matter to the breadth of working limitations; no subsequent successful job automatically establishes the employer’s entire earlier perception.

**The medication record is disputed and must not be converted into a concession.** Dr.Debra Doubek testified that Murphy, medicated, “functions normally doing everyday activity that an everyday person does” (JA63–64), and discussed a restriction on repeated lifting of200 pounds. She described possible end-organ injury from severe hypertension but lacked the tests necessary to determine existing injury at the September28 visit (JA64–65). Possible long-term injury is not a finding that Murphy already had heart, kidney or retinal damage. Her answers about reaching160/90 and functioning were qualified and contested in the summary-judgment response.

Murphy’s deposition describes limited stamina, diet restrictions, ringing ears and visual “runners” when pressure rises, and gout from some medication that could leave him bedridden (JA51,55–60). More intensive medication bringing pressure to normal levels caused reported stuttering, memory problems, impotence, sleep loss and irritability. The evidence distinguishes the regimen actually used from stronger doses or earlier attempts; it does not establish that every listed side effect operated continuously at termination. JA130–138 expressly contests UPS’s assertions of normal daily functioning and inability to reach160/90 without disabling side effects. JA137–138 distinguishes his roughly160/100 compromise from140/80 episodes and cites a possibility of further adjustment. Thus the brief’s “generally controlled” shorthand neither establishes normal pressure nor concedes absence of substantial residual or treatment-related limitations. Conversely the existence of symptoms does not by itself prove their statutory substantiality, duration or effect on a properly identified activity.

Doubek and examiner David Couch discussed heavy/very-heavy work restrictions; these opinions are not automatically UPS’s perceived limitations. UPS’s vocational expert Lewis Vierling opined that hypertension generally did not restrict Murphy’s access to the open labor market, treated very-heavy work as a small category, and identified alternative occupations using his skills (JA112–116). His profile’s742 retraining alternatives and8,144 closely related occupations are categories in that evaluation, not local vacancies or adjudicated counts. Murphy relies on the breadth of commercial-driving certification and related mechanical work; UPS emphasizes ordinary mechanic jobs not needing that certificate. No national certificate count by itself establishes the relevant geographic/class comparison or the employer’s actual perception.

**DOT text and advice are distinct.** Section391.41(b)(6) asks whether there is a current clinical diagnosis of high blood pressure “likely to interfere with ... ability to operate a commercial motor vehicle safely.” The reproduced §391.43(f) examination language states that consistently above160/90 “further tests may be necessary.” It is not an absolute160/90 disqualification in the regulatory text. JA97–99 authenticates the employer’s1988 “Medical Regulatory Criteria” document: it distinguishes moderate/severe initial readings above180 systolic and/or104 diastolic from mild hypertension, describes reduction below181/105 before temporary certification, and describes a three-month period to reduce pressure to160/90. The initial186/124 and later160/102 or164/104 therefore raise different questions even under that advice.

The United States/EEOC amicus pp.23–25 n.10 explains that DOT regarded those criteria as nonbinding recommendations at the time of the events, reproduces the advisory disclaimer, and attributes its omission from the record subdocument to the usual separate cover-letter practice. This is an official litigation representation of the agency position, not an independently recovered Murphy cover letter or a factual finding that one was delivered. It is substantial support for distinguishing the regulation from advice; it supplies no finding that Murphy was qualified or that a temporary certificate was actually issued. UPS disputes the legal and record consequences, asserts initial certification error, relies on the second examiner’s failure to issue a temporary card, and raises waiver/exhaustion/scope arguments. Murphy invokes the already issued card, possible temporary qualification and §391.47 dispute procedure. A research gap about certification does not establish waiver, exhaustion failure or party default.

Original ADA §12102(2)(A) requires an impairment substantially limiting one or more major life activities of the individual; (B) is record-of; (C) is regarded-as such an impairment. The requested review concerns(A)/(C), not a newly established(B) claim. Section12112(a) separately protects a *qualified* individual, and §12111(8) defines qualification by essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation. Sections12112(b)(5),12113 and29C.F.R.§1630.15(e) preserve appropriate accommodation, hardship, job-related/business-necessity, direct-threat and other-federal-law issues on their actual predicates. Coverage does not establish qualification or invalidate a lawful federal safety requirement.

The parties reproduce 29C.F.R.§1630.2(h)–(j),(l): a physiological condition affecting the cardiovascular system is an impairment; major activities include seeing/hearing/breathing/working; substantial limitation is inability or significant restriction in condition, manner or duration compared with the average person. The working rule asks for a class or broad range of jobs relative to comparable training, skills and abilities, with geographic access and similar/dissimilar work considered under its provisions; inability to perform a single particular job does not suffice. Apply the regulation’s language rather than invent a required percentage, proof of total unemployability or automatic exclusion of all commercial-driving categories.

The contemporaneous interpretive guidance in the JA directs case-by-case assessment without mitigating measures such as medication or prostheses. Its controlled-hypertension example describes unsubstantiated fears causing reassignment, not a universal rule that an employer relying on a genuine legal qualification cannot perceive substantial limitation. Petitioner invokes statutory structure, consistent agency interpretation and committee reports. The government relies on Auer/own-regulation interpretation; UPS contests delegated authority, whether guidance interprets the statute rather than a regulation and the strength of the claimed procedure/consistency. Guidance status and statutory meaning must be analyzed separately. ## Strongest supported decisional alternatives

**Mitigating measures.** A present-function reading emphasizes “substantially limits” and “of such individual”: actual correction must count because the statute differentiates impairment from disability and requires individualized rather than hypothetical effects. Its strongest answer to broad protection is that record-of/regarded-as branches perform separate work and an impairment is not statutory disability merely because untreated effects would be grave. Its hardest objections are the guidance and legislative reports, the separation between coverage and qualification, and the possibility of losing protection precisely because treatment permits work. If adopted, the rule must address both positive and negative effects of the measures actually used; it cannot erase proved treatment burdens or assume perfect correction.

The opposite reading evaluates the underlying impairment without mitigation, locating medication’s effectiveness principally in qualification, accommodation and safety. It relies on the guidance, statutory structure and consistent committee reports and avoids making coverage fluctuate with treatment. Its strongest difficulty is the enacted individualized substantial-limitation wording and a potentially much wider category than persons presently limited; deference cannot override unambiguous text or unsupported delegation. Even this reading does not make every impairment a disability: Murphy must have a supported substantially limiting underlying condition in his own case. On this record severe untreated hypertension and the physicians’ accounts supply substantially more than a bare diagnosis, without automatically establishing every activity or every element.

A narrower path can answer the legal mitigation question while leaving residual/treatment-burden application for ordinary reconsideration if necessary and fairly within review. Another can affirm on supported treated-state insufficiency, but must explain why the cited residual evidence does not create a genuine material issue rather than treating it as conceded absent. Bragdon’s accepted treated/unmedicated transmission risk did not decide this issue and supplies neither automatic rule. Any treatment-burden-only disposition must address granted scope and actual preservation; the JA response establishes that the factual contention was made below, not that every later theory is fairly included or must prevail.

**Regarded-as working limitation.** One path holds that the record shows only perceived inability to satisfy the combined UPS mechanic/commercial-driving requirements, with ordinary mechanic opportunities remaining. Recognition of an impairment or inability to meet one specialized requirement does not itself establish a class-wide substantial limitation. The strongest supporting evidence is Murphy’s long mechanic career, subsequent mechanic employment and the vocational evidence. Its strongest objection is that UPS invoked an industry-wide certificate, not a uniquely local employer rule, and the circuit substituted legitimate motive for the statutory perceived-limitation inquiry. A supported affirmance must address the actual breadth and awareness evidence, not announce that any regulatory rationale defeats coverage.

The other path regards the claimed exclusion from commercial driving and related work as a sufficiently broad class, or at least finds a triable issue under the actual working standard and remands. The United States/EEOC advocates that approach and argues that good motive or accurate safety judgment cannot collapse coverage into qualification. Its strongest counterargument is the need to compare work fitting Murphy’s training, abilities and accessible market: national totals of certificates or theoretical lost opportunities may not establish that UPS perceived substantial limitation. Neither the doctors’ own heavy-work views nor the DOT’s national reach automatically proves the employer’s actual perception. A factual remand can preserve that distinction rather than declare every DOT certification exclusion a disability.

**Remedy and reach.** Because either actual disability or regarded-as coverage may satisfy the definition, failure of one route alone does not sustain the entire judgment if the other requires correction. Conversely coverage on either route does not warrant judgment for Murphy on the whole ADA claim. Available dispositions include affirmance on supported coverage insufficiency; limited reversal/vacatur for correction of either coverage route; and ordinary consideration below of preserved independent qualification/accommodation/defense grounds if coverage survives. Grant Questions2/3 are excluded, and circuit¶14 reserved their substance. A respondent’s alternative affirmance under that record must pass the actual scope/record rule and address disputed regulatory consequences; no categorical DOT defense, temporary certificate, essential-function determination, waiver/default, new accommodation duty, reinstatement or damages award is preordained. The circuit also rejected an unlinked interview-inquiry allegation; no standalone new claim is presented here.
