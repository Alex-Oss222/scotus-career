## Corrected neutral premises

**Findings were made and are not independently challenged.** The source says the Bank does not challenge the Bankruptcy Judge's property valuation or other section 1129 findings apart from the plan's compliance with section 1129(b)(2)(B)(ii). The neutral packet must not treat this appeal as presenting an independently disputed dollar valuation or assume confirmation findings were never made. The present-value figure of about $4.1 million describes the proposed $6.125 million contribution over five years; it is not $6.125 million paid immediately. This extraction does not enumerate every section 1129 finding, so it cannot support invented findings, a new valuation amount for the property, or a claim that each conceivable new-value criterion was separately adjudicated in specified terms.

Those unchallenged findings do not alone resolve the legal meaning of receiving or retaining property on account of an old interest. Conversely, a legal objection to exclusive eligibility must not be rewritten as a concession that the Bankruptcy Court's valuation was wrong. The supplied materials do not independently price the exclusive opportunity, establish a completed auction, identify an actual superior bid, or establish the absence of a feasible competitor. The supplement's reference to an unestablished opportunity value concerns that particular issue; it is not a statement that no property valuation or confirmation findings exist.

**Plan-proposal exclusivity and exclusive investment eligibility are distinct.** The Bankruptcy Court denied the Bank's motion to terminate the debtor's exclusive proposal period and instead extended it for cause under section 1121(d). The Bank neither appealed that denial nor raised it in the supplied appeal. The contemplated Bank plan would liquidate the property; the extraction does not establish that the plan was filed, approved, economically superior or presently available for confirmation. Separately, the proposed plan limited eligibility to contribute the new capital to old equity holders. That plan condition is the subject of the preserved absolute-priority objection. The unappealed section 1121 ruling is not an independent appellate ground for reversal; it does not by itself adjudicate compliance with section 1129(b)(2)(B)(ii).

**Classification is outside the supplied review.** The source's classification sentence concerns separate classification of the Bank's unsecured deficiency from other general unsecured claims. The Bank sought no review of that issue. Do not supply a classification holding, reopen it as an additional ground, or infer that the classification was unlawful merely because the absolute-priority objection remains live. That preservation limit does not eliminate the Bank's reported objection as an impaired senior creditor or add an unreported concession on the plan's exclusive eligibility.

## Competing legal paths

These are neutral analytical possibilities, not assertions about unprovided party submissions or commitments.

**A confined absolute-priority objection.** The Court could determine that reserving the opportunity to acquire the reorganized debtor to old equity itself gives property on account of the old interest, notwithstanding new capital and unchallenged property valuation. On that legal view, a judicial valuation of underlying property or other confirmation findings would not necessarily answer whether the exclusive eligibility right was allocated consistently with the priority statute. The analysis would have to explain why the opportunity is protected property and why old ownership causes its receipt; it cannot simply assert a higher outside bid, a low valuation or an available competing plan. A confined decision could address the exclusive plan without deciding every proposed new-value arrangement.

**A permissible new-capital construction.** The opposing path treats receipt of the reorganized interest as consideration for the new contribution rather than the old interest and gives appropriate effect to the findings the Bank has not challenged. It may also distinguish the Code's proposal-exclusivity mechanism from a forbidden old-interest distribution. That path must establish the permissible statutory construction, explain why the exclusive eligibility condition does not defeat it, and identify which actual findings support the conclusion. It cannot treat the absence of a separate appeal from the section 1121 ruling as waiver of the section 1129(b)(2)(B)(ii) objection or assume that findings about property value conclusively establish the price of every plan opportunity.

**A broader statutory answer or bounded remand.** Whether any new-value corollary survives under the Code remains a distinct legal question. A broader answer requires its own statutory and precedential analysis; the extraction neither compels it nor supplies a result. Alternatively, if a corrected legal standard requires additional consideration, the Court may remand the confirmation question while specifying what remains to be decided. A remand must not be justified by invented absence of existing valuation findings or by treating their dollar accuracy as a preserved dispute. If an adopted legal rule makes a competitive opportunity, market process or further finding relevant, the need follows from that rule and must be stated; it is not a neutral fact already established here. No particular auction, termination of exclusivity or competing plan is presumed available or mandatory.

A remedy must address the reviewed confirmation judgment and preserve the distinction between its legality and the unreviewed exclusivity/classification rulings. The Court may explain the consequences of its adopted priority rule for further lawful plan proceedings without pretending it directly reviewed and reversed an unappealed order. The correction itself orders no new valuation, hearing, liquidation, auction or opportunity to bid.
