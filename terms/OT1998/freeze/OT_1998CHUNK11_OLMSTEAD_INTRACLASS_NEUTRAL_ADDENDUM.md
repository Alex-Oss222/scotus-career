## Exact controlling propositions in their own statutory settings

### O’Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., No. 95-354

The canonical Holdings entry states:

> An ADEA plaintiff need not show replacement by someone under forty to establish the initial inference of age discrimination. A replacement's membership in the protected group does not itself defeat that inference.

O’Connor’s Part I is unanimous. Its published explanation distinguishes §631(a)’s protected population, persons at least 40, from §623(a)(1)’s prohibited reason, the individual’s age. A protected 56-year-old can face age discrimination when replaced by a protected 40-year-old. An under-forty condition therefore lacks the necessary causal relevance to be an indispensable initial-proof element.

The companion controlling proposition states:

> Under the assumed framework, prima facie evidence and each required element must have a logical connection to an inference of age discrimination. A substantially younger replacement may support that inference even if at least forty; an insignificant difference, standing alone, does not supply it.

Part II has all eight Associates’ complete joins; the Chief joins the causal-inference requirement, positive relative-age proposition, unchanged burdens and reservations, but not the general insignificant-gap-alone proposition. No numerical minimum is prescribed. The public opinion expressly says a substantially younger replacement is not indispensable when other evidence supplies the inference or no replacement sensibly can be identified. A sixteen-year difference does not establish all elements or ultimate discriminatory motive. The Court assumes McDonnell Douglas applicability for this question, takes the case as outside reduction in force, and retains ordinary employer production, employee persuasion and summary-judgment rules. Its reversal removes the categorical barrier without establishing liability, compelling a trial or prescribing relief.

**Exact operative Standards title:** “Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness.” **Present operation effective:** “O’Connor, April 1, 1996, for the replacement inference; Hazen Paper’s causation and willfulness rules survive.” The provision prints no separate Current wording authority field. Its full current wording and component authorities are copied in the supplemental slice; no different effective date is invented.

### Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc., Fifth Circuit No. 95-30510

The canonical Holdings entry states:

> Title VII's protection against discriminatory employment conditions includes same-sex harassment. The employee must still plead and ultimately prove discrimination because of sex and every other element of the asserted claim; common sex supplies neither immunity nor liability.

The separate causal-proof holding states:

> Competent direct or circumstantial evidence can establish discrimination because of sex without proof of desire, a particular sexual orientation, or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual content, contact, or the gravity of conduct does not alone conclusively establish that causal element.

Thomas’s opinion is unanimous at both levels. The public explanation says the enacted protection concerns the individual employee’s treatment rather than an unstated requirement that offender and employee belong to different sex categories. It separately rejects a mandatory actual opposite-sex coworker as an evidentiary condition. Those are different propositions: common identity of offender and claimant is not the same question as the presence of a beneficiary/comparator outside the group. The latter express holding makes Oncale pertinent beyond the former relationship.

Sex-directed hostility, selective conduct, statements, threats and circumstances are illustrative evidence, not exclusive routes or a compulsory checklist. The actual inference and competing explanations remain for adjudication. An all-male workforce supplies context rather than immunity. Harris’s objectively and subjectively abusive severe-or-pervasive requirement and Andrade’s distinct attribution rule survive. The decision rejects a freestanding dignity claim, finds no participant’s motive or orientation, makes no independent sexual-orientation coverage holding, and establishes neither factual sufficiency nor final liability or remedy.

**Exact operative Standards title:** “Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite.” **Present operation effective:** “Oncale, 1997-03-19, for same-sex coverage and causation boundaries; Harris's environment threshold retains 1993-11-09 authority.” The provision prints no separate Current wording authority field. Its full current wording and component authorities are copied in the supplemental slice.

## Material relevance to the Olmstead alternatives

These existing holdings materially qualify a universal premise that discrimination statutes necessarily require an actual comparator outside the statutorily protected population. O’Connor rejects that condition for its ADEA inference; Oncale expressly permits its Title VII causal showing without an actual opposite-sex coworker. They are controlling in those settings, and are date-eligible interpretive and evidentiary analogies here. They do **not**, by themselves, establish a controlling Title II rule or decide whether avoidable institutional segregation meets §12132.

The existing Olmstead packet explains competing positions about discrimination in a disability-only service program but does not identify these two public decisions. Adding them is material to testing a categorical comparator rationale and preserving the difference between a proof prerequisite and the substantive definition of unlawful discrimination. It supplies no new fact, claim, burden or disposition.

**Strongest argument drawing on the cases.** The protected-person definition and the statute’s prohibited treatment are distinct questions. A rule categorically excluding a claim because all people receiving the relevant mental-health services have disabilities could prevent proof of treatment prohibited by Title II for reasons unrelated to the statutory words. Oncale makes especially clear that competent proof of forbidden causation need not take the form of an actual outside-group coworker. Respondents can therefore argue that the absence of a nondisabled person needing the identical disability service does not, alone, end the inquiry. Their integration theory still must explain why the challenged service arrangement constitutes exclusion, denied benefits or discrimination *by reason of disability* under §12132, read with §12134, the integration regulation and the qualification/modification provisions. The two analogies do not perform that substantive work automatically.

**Strongest scope distinction.** O’Connor concerns relative age within a threshold-defined protected population, under an assumed employment-inference framework; an actual younger replacement existed there even though that person was protected. Its decision does not hold that all services or benefits must be identical within every protected population. Oncale concerns adverse employment conditions allegedly imposed because of sex, and removes unsupported categorical barriers while retaining independent causal proof and the hostile-environment standard. It does not supply a public entity’s duty to offer a particular service, require expansion of a benefit program, classify a resource allocation as discrimination or define reasonable modification.

Petitioners therefore retain the narrower argument that this actual placement/allocation practice reflects clinical suitability, distinct services, limited community capacity or legitimate demands from other eligible people rather than prohibited disability discrimination. The existing neutral records that their reply accepts Title II coverage of disability-only programs in some circumstances, such as exclusion due to an additional disability; the argument need not be restated as an absolute immunity for all such programs. O’Connor and Oncale require attention to the asserted causal/statutory theory rather than determining whether that narrower account succeeds here. Conversely, resource labels or the mere fact that disability creates the need for services do not independently prove that the challenged treatment is lawful. The actual statutory/integration question remains.

**Questions neither analogy resolves.** They do not decide the validity or interpretation of §35.130(d); whether the challenged isolation, rather than some different service distinction, is discrimination by reason of disability; whether a requested modification is necessary and reasonable; what makes the service/program fundamentally different; how State responsibilities to others and transition costs enter the actual defense; medical suitability, individual choice, waiting priorities or an effective plan; or the scope and remedy for this two-person suit. No ADEA prima-facie formula, Title VII severe-or-pervasive threshold, employment attribution rule, numerical comparator requirement or new burden allocation is transplanted into Title II. The State’s existing regulatory burden on fundamental alteration remains as stated in the original neutral, without expansion or reduction by analogy.

**Remedial discipline.** Both prior decisions separate elimination of an invalid categorical gate from established liability and an entitlement to relief. If an outside-class comparator premise is rejected in Olmstead, remaining statutory, causal, qualification, resource and scope questions retain their independent roles. The two authorities do not require a particular judgment, community placement, trial, new evidence or remand.
