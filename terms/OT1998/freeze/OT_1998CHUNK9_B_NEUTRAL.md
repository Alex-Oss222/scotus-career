## City of Chicago v. Morales — No. 97-1121

**Event:** Merits, June 10, 1999; argued December 9, 1998. Review of 177 Ill. 2d 440, 687 N.E.2d 53 (1997). Illinois held the ordinance facially vague and an arbitrary restriction on personal liberties, and reversed the convictions. Chicago seeks reversal; respondents seek preservation of the judgment. The Illinois court did not reach status-offense, probable-cause or overbreadth objections.

**Question:** Whether the gang-loitering ordinance's conditions for mandatory dispersal and punishment for disobedience provide constitutionally sufficient notice and enforcement standards. Facial-review scope, the actual authoritative state construction and the relation of an individual warning to the initial dispersal decision are separable analytical questions.

**Record:** Chicago's council found serious gang-related violence and intimidation, territorial control through public presence, and intimidation when gang members committed no separately punishable offense in police presence. Those are legislative findings, not proof that every person reached by the ordinance threatens anyone. The ordinance triggers when an officer reasonably believes a criminal street gang member is loitering in a public place with one or more other persons. Loitering means remaining in one place with no apparent purpose. The officer must order all such persons to disperse and remove themselves from the area; anyone who does not promptly obey violates the section. The absence of an actual gang member is an affirmative defense. Public place includes a public way and any publicly accessible location, public or private. The statute authorizes a $100–$500 fine, imprisonment up to six months, or both; up to 120 hours of community service may be required in addition or instead. Community service is permissive, not mandatory.

General Order 92-4 limits enforcement to designated officers and designated areas, supplies gang-identification criteria and states an antiarbitrariness objective. The City does not disclose designated areas to the public. The supplied record reports over 89,000 dispersal orders and 42,000 arrests during three years. Those totals do not establish how each encounter occurred. The ordinance has no express obstruction, threat, unlawful-purpose or minimum-duration predicate. The approved neutral material states that Illinois rejected the City's proposed narrowing constructions; no federal insertion of an intimidation-purpose element is assumed. The supplied statutory quotation omits some detailed gang-definition subdivisions; no challenge to those omitted enumerated-offense details is resolved here.

**Entering law:** Grayned supplies intelligible notice and enforcement standards; Papachristou addresses ordinary public activity and discretionary vagrancy enforcement; Coates rejects an offense depending on others' variable annoyance. Kolender, 461 U.S. 352 (1983), treats minimum enforcement guidance independently from actual notice and considers authoritative constructions. Its criminal-penalty/protected-activity discussion limits simplistic transplantation of Hoffman Estates's business-regulation analysis. Hoffman Estates, 455 U.S. 489 (1982), preserves the distinction between an intelligible core applied to the challenger and uncertain marginal applications, with context, scienter and protected activity relevant.

The important current-term addition is **Peoples Rights Organization v. Columbus, October 15, 1998**. Thomas's Court opinion, joined on the relevant parts by all seven other Associates, distinguished difficult factual proof from a missing legal criterion, preserved independent fault requirements, and invalidated both undefined modification branches as written because they failed to identify the relationship making a handgun prohibited. Kolender's enforcement-guidance requirement did substantive work even though physical conversion could be a clear example. The rule is confined to the actual central classification defect and does not make uncertain margins, every criminal ordinance or every claim involving a protected right facially invalid. Its rule rejects both automatic wholesale invalidation and an automatic scienter cure for an undefined offense. Read particularly Holdings II–VIII and XI in the linked public copy. No current holding establishes a general fundamental right to all loitering.

**Supported paths and opposing arguments:** Chicago can argue that gang association, objective officer belief, an opportunity to comply, designated enforcement personnel/areas and clear refusal to disperse give a sufficiently definite core and distinguish Papachristou. Respondents can argue that none supplies a standard separating innocent from targeted presence when the order is issued, and that a warning cannot retroactively supply the missing criterion. The strongest facial-relief objection is that individual disobedience is clear and some threatening gang assemblages are properly regulable; the answer must explain whether the challenged defect lies in every use of the same undefined trigger rather than rely on hypothetical unfair arrests alone. The strongest defense of invalidity must address the actual narrowing rules and state construction, not omit them.

A narrow enforcement-guidance disposition need not establish a comprehensive substantive liberty to loiter, decide every Fourth Amendment theory or forbid a more definite anti-intimidation ordinance. A result sustaining the law need not approve unconstitutional individual applications. Relief follows the actual Illinois judgment and its federal grounds; no replacement ordinance, guilt findings, damages or new prosecution rule is supplied.

**Justice-specific source directions:** O'Connor authored Kolender; its majority reasoning and all writings are saved in `../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK9_B_sources/kolender.txt`. Current PRO gives every Associate's actual join to the relevant vagueness propositions, including Thomas as author. Distinguish that adopted law from his or any other's potential application to a different ordinance; no vote is inferred by label.

## Lilly v. Virginia — No. 98-5881

**Event:** Merits, June 10, 1999; argued March 29, 1999. Review of 255 Va. 558, 499 S.E.2d 522 (1998). Virginia affirmed Benjamin Lilly's convictions and sentences, including capital murder. Lilly seeks relief from admission of his brother's custodial statements; Virginia seeks affirmance. The original neutral line describing affirmance as the unqualified relief request is the respondent's position, not petitioner's.

**Record and preservation:** Benjamin, Mark Lilly and Gary Barker committed the described burglary, robberies and abduction; DeFilippis was killed. Police separately questioned the three. Benjamin attributed coercion in the robberies to the others and did not discuss the murder. Mark and Barker identified Benjamin as the instigator and killer. Mark's two recorded interviews occurred shortly after arrest. He emphasized intoxication, admitted stealing liquor/beer, handling a gun and presence, while assigning the principal violence to others and denying involvement in the shooting. Police warned of armed-robbery charges and a possible life sentence unless he broke family ties. These circumstances do not establish an express immunity bargain or a recovered promise of leniency.

Mark invoked the privilege against self-incrimination when called at Benjamin's separate trial. The Commonwealth offered his statements as an unavailable witness's declarations against penal interest. Lilly objected both that the accusations shifted responsibility rather than genuinely disserved Mark and that admission violated confrontation. The trial court admitted the recordings and transcripts in their entirety. Virginia held the exception firmly rooted in Virginia law, relied on corroborating evidence for reliability, and treated the self-serving allocation as weight rather than admissibility. The packet contains no independently reached finding that every assertion was trustworthy, no prior cross-examination, and no harmless-error determination on the whole trial record.

**Entering law:** Ohio v. Roberts's received exception-sensitive confrontation framework remains. **White, January 15, 1992**, permits the accepted firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and treatment exceptions without a separate production/unavailability requirement; it neither validates every police interview nor adopts a formal-accusation-only boundary. The register's exact title is **Confrontation and firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions**, present operation effective **Hale, February 12, 1996**, preserving White's components and limiting Hale to independently maintained public-record nonentry certifications.

**Williamson, June 27, 1994**, and **Assertion-specific declarations against penal interest**, effective that date, require genuinely penal-adverse assertions assessed in context under federal Rule 804(b)(3). A general confession does not automatically carry blame-shifting material; apparently neutral details can be self-inculpatory in context. This is a federal evidentiary rule, not a direct rule of Virginia evidence or a resolved confrontation claim. Its constitutional question and prosecution-side corroboration issue remained open.

**Lee v. Illinois, 476 U.S. 530 (1986)** supplies presumptive unreliability of accomplice accusations and the particular risk of shifting or spreading blame. **Idaho v. Wright, 497 U.S. 805 (1990)** requires particularized constitutional trustworthiness to arise from circumstances surrounding the statement; independent trial corroboration cannot itself establish the inherent reliability that makes cross-examination of marginal use. It rejects a mechanical checklist of interview procedures and categorical exclusion merely from later testimonial incapacity. External corroboration can matter to harmlessness, which is distinct. Wright's actual words and Kennedy's opposing corroboration analysis are in the saved full source; O'Connor authored the majority, joined by Stevens and Scalia among current Justices; Kennedy dissented.

**Gray v. Maryland, March 9, 1998**, preserves the distinction between constitutional admissibility and remedy. Its majority remand permits only properly available Chapman review, with the State required to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that error did not contribute to the verdict; it awards neither automatic retrial nor acquittal. Lilly is not a Bruton limiting-instruction/redaction case because the confession was introduced substantively at a separate trial.

**Supported paths:** Virginia can argue a genuinely self-inculpatory context and sufficiently trustworthy statements despite custody, and can seek a narrower application or initial lower-court assessment rather than a categorical exclusion of all penal-interest statements. Lilly can distinguish the precise accusatory subclass from genuinely self-inculpatory declarations and rely on minimization, intoxication, interrogation and pressure as reasons cross-examination mattered. State-law acceptance alone cannot establish federal constitutional firm-rootedness. A Justice favoring a broader reconstruction must identify a date-eligible basis and confront White's retained framework; no later testimonial/primary-purpose doctrine enters.

The Court may resolve firm-rootedness at the scope actually offered, assess the supported intrinsic circumstances, or remand genuinely unresolved application under a corrected constitutional standard. An error finding and the appropriate mandate are separate. Any harmlessness disposition must use the trial record and preserved arguments; this packet supports a preservation-qualified remand but no present finding of harmlessness, automatic new trial, retrial immunity or acquittal.

## Dickinson v. Zurko — No. 98-377

**Event:** Merits, June 10, 1999; argued March 24, 1999. Review of en banc **In re Zurko, 142 F.3d 1447 (Fed. Cir. May 4, 1998)**, adopting the reversal stated in 111 F.3d 887. The complete lower primary text is [zurko-lower-cap.txt](../runtime/chunk9-control/zurko-lower-cap.txt). Applicants sought a patent for improving computer security; the examiner found obviousness under §103 and the Board sustained rejection. The panel found the prior-art factual premise clearly erroneous. The en banc court expressly stated that the outcome turns on the review standard. The PTO seeks APA review; applicants defend clear-error review.

**Question and lower rationale:** Whether §559 preserves a pre-APA common-law patent-review requirement more searching than the ordinary §706 standards. The lower court did not simply overlook the APA. It relied on §559's preservation of additional requirements imposed by statute or otherwise recognized by law, early manifest-error/clear-conviction review, Morgan v. Daniels, the specialized appellate tradition, legislative materials and statutory stare decisis. It conceded that historical terminology was not uniform and that no patent statute expressly specifies clear-error review. It regarded earlier express proposals to exempt the PTO, their removal and the separate civil-action remedy as compatible with implicit preservation through §559. Its asserted clear-error tradition permits a court's own assessment of the record; it contrasted agency-reasoning review. Those are lower-court reasons, not controlling conclusions here.

The Commissioner advocated §706(2)(E) substantial-evidence review, alternatively §706(2)(A) arbitrary/capricious review. The lower opinion describes informal Board practice, no live testimony and a separate possibility of civil-action review; those features must not be transformed into an automatic statutory classification or universal review standard for every PTO action.

**Entering law:** Official 1994 Code text of **§559 and §706**, including all exceptions and the whole-record/prejudicial-error instruction, is mechanically copied in the statutory appendix. Section 706(2)(E) applies to §§556–557 cases or other review on the record of a statutorily provided agency hearing; (A) and (F) have separate terms. The proposition that the APA applies is distinct from selection between its standards and from the patentability merits.

**Allentown Mack, January 26, 1998**, requires whole-record substantial-evidence review under the actual announced substantive standard, including contrary evidence while preserving agency choice between fairly conflicting views. It forbids silent conversion of reasonable doubt to proof of actual minority status. Scalia's Parts III–IV, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas among Associates, control that rule/application; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined the separate permissible-policy holding but not that application. The register **Whole-record agency review under the announced substantive standard**, effective January 26, 1998, supplies no de novo review and no patent exception. **APA persuasion burdens and statutory departures**, effective **Greenwich Collieries, June 20, 1994**, separates statutory persuasion allocation from appellate review and requires the actually invoked departure to be established, preserving its express reservations.

Current **Haggar, April 21, 1999**, unanimously rejects categorical exclusion of lawful delegated interpretation merely because a trade court determines facts on its own record; its seven-Justice remand leaves particular validity/application unresolved. That legal-interpretation holding is not itself a standard for direct review of PTO factual findings. Universal Camera preserves substantial whole-record scrutiny and deference among fairly conflicting views. Morgan, 153 U.S. 120 (1894), addressed priority between contesting parties in a later equity proceeding; its thorough-conviction language and scope require examination before treating it as a preserved §141 direct-review standard.

**Supported paths:** One path holds that the APA's generally applicable review governs because historical terminology and the separate judicial action establish no sufficiently identifiable preserved additional requirement for this direct agency appeal. The competing path reads §559 to preserve an established more searching tradition without requiring a single modern label, treating continuity and the specialized statutory structure as material. Neither path can be rejected by calling the PTO a court or an agency alone; both must explain §559 and the actual historical review. Efficiency or expertise is no independent textual exemption. If APA review governs, a remand for the correct inquiry may leave the (A)/(E) choice unresolved where unnecessary, and must leave patentability undecided absent support for final application. If clear-error review is preserved, affirmance remains subject to the actual granted question; this packet does not independently adjudicate the invention's obviousness.

## American Airlines, Inc. v. Tice — No. 98-1704

**Event:** Already-granted merits, June 10, 1999; minimum full-briefing submission premise above, no invented argument date. Review of **Tice v. American Airlines, Inc., 162 F.3d 966 (7th Cir. December 17, 1998)**; rehearing denied January 26, 1999. The full lower opinion is [tice-cap.txt](../runtime/chunk9-control/tice-cap.txt). It reversed judgment on the pleadings dismissing twelve pilots' ADEA claims and separately affirmed denial of transfer to the Northern District of Texas.

**Question and record:** Whether virtual representation binds these nonparties to earlier age-discrimination judgments. American's up-or-out rule requires flight officers to be eligible to progress to pilot; the FAA age-60 pilot/copilot limit does not itself bar flight-officer service. These incumbent pilots claim the rule unlawfully prevented downbidding upon turning 60. They were too young and excluded from the earlier Johnson plaintiff group, and their earlier employment interests could differ from the interests of older pilots seeking to remain. The earlier Murnane and EEOC actions concerned hiring; EEOC represented 57 identified persons, none these plaintiffs. American did not claim these plaintiffs gave written consent to an earlier ADEA group action. The Seventh Circuit found no evidence of manipulative practice, participation, acquiescence or relationship beyond common employment; later retaining the same counsel did not supply it. Attribute those assessments to the lower record rather than inventing exhaustive findings about every possible relationship.

**Actual arguments below:** American invoked common interests, the same policy, common counsel, finality and alternatively issue preclusion/EEOC representation. The pilots and EEOC emphasized individual statutory rights, lack of representation and differing interests. Section 626(b) incorporates §216(b)'s written-consent requirement: no employee becomes a party plaintiff without written consent. ADEA collective actions are opt-in, unlike the ordinary Rule 23 opt-out comparison; a generic class-action label cannot evade that rule.

**Entering law:** **Richards, June 10, 1996**, and **Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants**, effective that date with Matsushita's retained representation component, require constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate authorized representation or a recognized special arrangement. Identical interests, resources, awareness or advocacy alone do not suffice. Notice alone creates no intervention duty; notice/opt-out cannot cure inadequate representation. The Court did not constitutionalize all federal Rule 23 details or foreclose every representative judgment lacking individualized notice.

Current **South Central Bell, March 23, 1999**, unanimously applies the prerequisite to both claim and issue preclusion against absent taxpayers. Current **Dodd, November 19, 1998**, unanimously gives a necessary fairly litigated Oregon factual finding its lawful effect against the same actual owners, while preserving the distinct federal questions; it supplies no absent-party exception. Hansberry, Martin v. Wilks and Phillips Petroleum v. Shutts retain their respective adequate-representation, nonparty-hearing and class-procedure limits. These holdings distinguish precedential force from authority to extinguish a stranger's claim. Current Merrill Lynch v. Kravitz is a securities-fraud/execution holding and supplies no Tice preclusion rule.

**Supported paths and limits:** American's strongest lawful position is a demonstrated recognized representative or control relationship, not shared policy interests alone; it may argue that the actual facts establish such a relationship, while confronting the findings and opt-in statute. The competing path rejects the asserted nonparty bar and leaves ADEA merits, BFOQ defenses and actual-party judgments intact. A narrower issue-preclusion formulation still requires authority to bind these pilots. The source establishes no entitlement to a new exploratory hearing to construct a different preclusion relationship.

**Venue:** Below, American relied on Texas witnesses/evidence, operations and a flight simulator. The pilots relied on nearly equal O'Hare/DFW employment, produced documents and extensive Illinois operations. The district court found convenience relatively balanced and questioned the simulator's trial relevance. The Seventh Circuit reviewed for clear abuse of discretion and affirmed. The supplied grant scope is nonparty preclusion; that separate venue ruling remains undisturbed unless independently established granted/preserved scope brings it in. No automatic plaintiff-forum presumption, merits BFOQ decision or disability-accommodation claim is created.

## Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association v. United States — No. 98-387

**Event:** Merits, June 14, 1999; argued April 27, 1999. Review of **149 F.3d 334 (5th Cir. 1998)**. Broadcasters seek declaratory and injunctive relief against §1304 and the companion FCC restriction as applied to truthful promotions of lawful private casino gambling. Louisiana and Mississippi allow regulated private casinos. New Orleans stations wish to carry their advertising; Louisiana signals can under appropriate conditions reach Texas and Arkansas, where private casino gambling is unlawful. That potential reach is an FCC official's record statement, not a new finding about every broadcast's audience.

The district court decided cross-motions for summary judgment, sustaining interests in reducing gambling harms and accommodating nonlottery States. It noted the distinction between advertising casino amenities and gaming, and existing advertising. The Fifth Circuit adhered to approval on the supplied remand posture, acknowledging a more demanding commercial-speech inquiry. This packet imports no unrecorded Supreme Court remand action or result.

**Statute:** The exact §1304 broadcast prohibition, §1307 exceptions and §2720 tribal-gaming exception appear in the appendix. Keep their conditions distinct. Section 1307(a)(1) permits information about a State-conducted lottery broadcast by a station licensed in that State or another State conducting a lottery. Subsection (a)(2) requires the lottery/scheme to be authorized or not otherwise prohibited where conducted, and to be conducted by a nonprofit or governmental organization, or as a clearly occasional promotional activity ancillary to a commercial organization's primary business. Section 2720 exempts tribal gaming conducted pursuant to its chapter consistently with that chapter's requirements. Section 1307(b)'s transportation/mailing exceptions do not mention §1304; its special lottery definition is for subsection (b), not automatically the broadcast prohibition. The §501 tax-exempt definition concerns nonprofit organizations. Do not turn these conditioned exceptions into a statement that all gambling advertising is allowed except private casinos.

**Entering law:** Central Hudson first asks whether speech concerns lawful activity and is not misleading, then requires a substantial interest, direct material advancement and reasonable fit. Fox does not require the least restrictive means. **Edenfield (April 26, 1993)** requires demonstrated real harms and material alleviation in the relevant setting without invariably demanding studies. **Rubin (April 19, 1995)** has eight direct votes for independent advancement and fit failures where the actual scheme leaves unexplained contradictions; Stevens joined judgment only. Its state-accommodation ruling distinguishes unsupported assertion from a demonstrated mechanism. **Ibanez (June 13, 1994)** requires justification responsive to the actual truthful-information restriction.

**44 Liquormart (May 13, 1996)** leaves ordinary Central Hudson/Fox review in force: five adopt the record-specific material-advancement failure; seven, including O'Connor and Breyer assuming advancement, adopt independent unreasonable fit. Stevens's joins are conditional; Scalia and Thomas add no balancing votes. No majority adopted heightened scrutiny, a categorical anti-ignorance rule, numerical-study demand or least-restrictive alternative. **Edge Broadcasting (June 25, 1993)** preserves the record-specific meaningful-reduction and state-policy accommodation holding; it neither approved every gambling-advertising restriction nor foreclosed an as-applied challenge or supplied a categorical vice exception. The exact standards are **Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions**, present operation **Lanphere & Urbaniak, April 18, 1995**, and **Alcohol regulation and freedom of speech**, effective **44 Liquormart, May 13, 1996**; the latter does not create a gambling exception or new tier.

**Supported paths:** The Government can defend incremental reduction and accommodation of neighboring States despite leakage and distinct federal policies for tribal, charitable and state gaming. It need not regulate all sources at once or prove experimental certainty. Broadcasters can argue that permitted promotion of materially comparable gambling undercuts this mechanism, that amenity advertising and cross-border exceptions matter, and that direct regulation of gambling can expose disproportionate suppression. Exceptions are evidence requiring explanation, not an automatic constitutional formula. Neither categorical vice deference nor categorical least-restrictive-means review governs. A lawful result must test the actual coherent mechanism and scope, and distinguish Edge concretely. As-applied relief can address these lawful casino advertisements without erasing §1304, the FCC rule, deceptive advertising rules, gaming restrictions or other applications wholesale. No damages or replacement gambling policy is at issue.

## Cunningham v. Hamilton County — No. 98-727

**Event:** Merits, June 14, 1999; argued April 19, 1999. Correct judgment below: **Starcher v. Correctional Medical Systems, Inc., 144 F.3d 418 (6th Cir. 1998)**. The neutral split's 128 F.3d 376 citation is a derived factual error and is not used. The Sixth Circuit dismissed this interlocutory appeal over dissent. Cunningham seeks immediate review of a personal discovery sanction before judgment in her client's civil-rights action.

**Record:** Counsel represented the estate's civil-rights action following an inmate suicide. Discovery requests and orders required full responses and controlled specified depositions. The magistrate found violations, imposed **$1,494** in costs and fees under the then-current **Rule 37(a)(4)** payable to the county treasurer, and expressly stated there had been no contempt hearing or finding of contempt. A separate **$2,432** sanction was settled and is not in this appeal. The district court affirmed the sanction and also disqualified counsel as a material witness. The civil action remained ongoing when she appealed. These recitations establish what the courts ordered and found, not this Court's endorsement of sanctions or a fresh conclusion that the discovery conduct was culpable.

The Sixth Circuit found the appeal insufficiently separate from the merits and held that ending representation did not itself justify immediate appeal. It expressly avoided deciding effective unreviewability, observing that postjudgment appeal appeared available. The broader source split's statement that later review was conclusively adequate is accordingly an argument/legal question, not an independently established holding of the Sixth Circuit. No civil-contempt route may be substituted for this noncontempt order.

**Entering law:** Section 1291 ordinarily requires a final decision. Cohen and Coopers & Lybrand require conclusiveness, an important question completely separate from the merits, and effective unreviewability after final judgment. **Digital Equipment, June 6, 1994**, unanimously requires category-level public importance; its eight-Associate independent ground preserves adequate final review despite irretrievable litigation expense. The exact register **Private settlement rights and collateral-order appeals**, effective June 6, 1994, retains both grounds and distinct authorized routes. **Swint, March 1, 1995**, rejects convenience-based jurisdiction and distinguishes legal immunity from ordinary liability. **Behrens, February 21, 1996**, preserves otherwise qualifying legal-immunity appeals and excludes factual-sufficiency review; it does not establish a right to appeal every personally burdensome order.

**Supported paths:** Counsel can stress a conclusive personal monetary obligation, departure from representation, and an asserted separation between professional misconduct and the client's ultimate liability. The county can stress the category's connection to discovery relevance/compliance and merits litigation, adequate later correction and the disruption from routine appeals. The latter position must actually explain counsel's ability to obtain eventual review; former-counsel status cannot simply be ignored. A conclusiveness finding alone does not satisfy the remaining conditions, and inconvenience alone does not establish an important right lost without review. If collateral review fails, affirmance of dismissal decides no merits validity of the sanction, amount or underlying civil claim. Certification, mandamus and a genuine contempt route retain distinct requirements; none is established or ordered merely to solve this appeal.

§1304. Broadcasting lottery information



Whoever broadcasts by means of any radio or television station for which a license is required by any law of the United States, or whoever, operating any such station, knowingly permits the broadcasting of, any advertisement of or information concerning any lottery, gift enterprise, or similar scheme, offering prizes dependent in whole or in part upon lot or chance, or any list of the prizes drawn or awarded by means of any such lottery, gift enterprise, or scheme, whether said list contains any part or all of such prizes, shall be fined under this title or imprisoned not more than one year, or both.

Each day's broadcasting shall constitute a separate offense.

(B) broadcast by a radio or television station licensed to a location in that State or a State which conducts such a lottery; or



(2) an advertisement, list of prizes, or other information concerning a lottery, gift enterprise, or similar scheme, other than one described in paragraph (1), that is authorized or not otherwise prohibited by the State in which it is conducted and which is—

(A) conducted by a not-for-profit organization or a governmental organization; or

(B) conducted as a promotional activity by a commercial organization and is clearly occasional and ancillary to the primary business of that organization.



(b) The provisions of sections 1301, 1302, and 1303 shall not apply to the transportation or mailing—

(1) to addresses within a State of equipment, tickets, or material concerning a lottery which is conducted by that State acting under the authority of State law; or

(2) to an addressee within a foreign country of equipment, tickets, or material designed to be used within that foreign country in a lottery which is authorized by the law of that foreign country.



(c) For the purposes of this section (1) “State” means a State of the United States, the District of Columbia, the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, or any territory or possession of the United States; and (2) “foreign country” means any empire, country, dominion, colony, or protectorate, or any subdivision thereof (other than the United States, its territories or possessions).

(d) For the purposes of subsection (b) of this section “lottery” means the pooling of proceeds derived from the sale of tickets or chances and allotting those proceeds or parts thereof by chance to one or more chance takers or ticket purchasers. “Lottery” does not include the placing or accepting of bets or wagers on sporting events or contests. For purposes of this section, the term a “not-for-profit organization” means any organization that would qualify as tax exempt under section 501 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986.

§706. Scope of review



To the extent necessary to decision and when presented, the reviewing court shall decide all relevant questions of law, interpret constitutional and statutory provisions, and determine the meaning or applicability of the terms of an agency action. The reviewing court shall—

(1) compel agency action unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed; and

(2) hold unlawful and set aside agency action, findings, and conclusions found to be—

(A) arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of discretion, or otherwise not in accordance with law;

(B) contrary to constitutional right, power, privilege, or immunity;

(C) in excess of statutory jurisdiction, authority, or limitations, or short of statutory right;

(D) without observance of procedure required by law;

(E) unsupported by substantial evidence in a case subject to sections 556 and 557 of this title or otherwise reviewed on the record of an agency hearing provided by statute; or

(F) unwarranted by the facts to the extent that the facts are subject to trial de novo by the reviewing court.



In making the foregoing determinations, the court shall review the whole record or those parts of it cited by a party, and due account shall be taken of the rule of prejudicial error.



(Pub. L. 89–554, Sept. 6, 1966, 80 Stat. 393.)

§559. Effect on other laws; effect of subsequent statute
