## McClellan v. Cablevision of Connecticut, Inc. — January 7, 1999

### Components and sufficient ground

**M1 — Asserted damages action.** Section 531(e), read with the Cable Act's distinct enforcement provisions and the demonstrated amendment history, does not authorize the asserted implied private damages action against this operator. Protection of public-access programming supplies a substantive command and evidence of intended beneficiaries; it does not establish Congress's intention to create this judicial damages remedy. Cort, Touche Ross and Transamerica require that separate inquiry. Suter confirms it. Franklin's appropriate-relief rule presupposes a cognizable action, and Central Bank and Musick, Peeler concern incidents of an already received securities action.

**M2 — Judgment and relief.** Reverse the Second Circuit insofar as its judgment permits damages through that implied action, and remand. The supplied grant concerns damages although the complaint and lower decision also concern injunctions. The holding does not decide whether any independently supported prospective, franchise or state-law route survives. The lower courts must identify any remaining route by its actual source and ordinary procedural requirements. This neither recognizes an equitable §531(e) action nor directs dismissal of every request for relief. CUTPA and supplemental-jurisdiction consequences remain for lawful consideration.

**M3 — Limits.** No finding approves the blanket exclusion, determines whether a program fit a lawful exclusion category, resolves the viewers' reserved cognizable-claim question, establishes operator state action or public-forum status, recognizes an unpleaded §1983 claim, or awards damages. Section 531(e) remains subject to §544(d); its separate public-access permission concerns a program or portion containing obscenity, indecency or nudity. Permission is not a command, and public-access wording does not automatically cover all educational or governmental uses. Denver Area's judgment concerning 1992 §10(c) neither extinguishes the separate 1996 provision nor adjudicates its constitutionality or application.

All eight Associates support M1–M3 at these scopes, including the same sufficient intent ground. Their individual assessments below do not convert judgment agreement into joins in broader propositions.

### Two lawful paths

**Recognize the action:** A direct anti-editorial command protects identifiable programmers. Franchise enforcement under §531(c) need not enforce every independent statutory right or provide personal compensation. Cannon establishes that express remedies elsewhere do not alone preclude an otherwise supported implied action; if that action exists, Franklin supports appropriate compensation. The 1992 and 1996 amendments may be examined against their contemporary legal context.

**Strongest objection:** This would be a new action. Section 532(d) expressly supplies a particular leased-capacity action; it is not a general public-access damages remedy. Three district-court recognitions, one later abandoned, do not establish the settled judicial remedy examined in Curran or Central Bank. Amendment of the underlying restriction does not itself adopt the proposed action. Cannon requires actual support for the inference, not an automatic remedy for every protected interest.

**Reject the asserted damages action:** The text, differentiated enforcement choices and limited judicial background together fail to establish intended private damages enforcement. Its strongest objection is the remedial gap for the direct beneficiary. The answer is not that franchise enforcement is comprehensive or adequate: a gap does not establish the congressional authorization needed for this damages action. The bounded grant permits a final answer without resolving every independent prospective source.

## City of West Covina v. Perkins — January 13, 1999

### Components and sufficient ground

**W1 — Published procedures.** When an owner has notice of the seizure and knows the responsible agency, due process does not additionally require officials to give individualized instructions about adequate return procedures reasonably ascertainable from publicly available statutes and judicial decisions. Meaningful notice of governmental action does not impose a general duty to supply a personalized guide to generally available law. This proposition concerns the stated setting, not every inaccessible recovery procedure.

**W2 — Application and judgment.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's contrary notice ruling and its direction to enter judgment for Perkins on that ground; remand consistently with W1. The receipt identified the agency, warrant date, issuing judge and court, search date, contacts and inventory. The District Court found that respondents had not established that the omitted warrant number was necessary to bring the return motion. Reported difficulties and disputed officer statements do not authorize an affirmative obstruction finding or replacement of that finding. The Ninth Circuit's conclusion that California's procedures were adequate is the premise for this legal-instruction issue, not a finding that every practical implementation was adequate.

**W3 — Limits.** Mullane's meaningful-notice requirement and Good's independent due-process protection remain. Memphis Light's notice of an operative utility-dispute procedure does not automatically require restating generally available judicial law. Withholding case-specific information actually necessary to invoke a remedy would present a different issue requiring its own support and preservation. No fresh hearing, discovery or reopened claim is ordered. Return of property does not itself defeat a supported earlier-deprivation claim. Individual qualified immunity is not reopened. No warrant-invalidity, damages or entitlement-to-the-reported-stolen-pistol ruling follows.

All eight Associates support W1–W3 and the stated reversal and remand.

### Adversarial alternatives

The instruction path invokes Mullane, Memphis Light and Mathews: the property interest is substantial and adding concise directions to a receipt costs little. Its strongest setting is an obscure or inaccessible procedure or an indispensable undisclosed identifier. Its objection is that adequate, publicly ascertainable judicial remedies and notice of the custodian already permit response without a government legal guide.

The contrary path distinguishes notice of the act and custodian from instruction in law. Its strongest objection is that nominal publication does not establish meaningful access. W1–W3 answer that objection by preserving the actual-access distinction while refusing to find an indispensable missing identifier contrary to the supported lower finding. An assertion that warrant compliance alone exhausts due process is neither necessary nor adopted.

## Roberts v. Galen of Virginia, Inc. — January 13, 1999

### Common components and remedy

**R1 — No added motive.** EMTALA's stabilization requirement does not require proof of improper financial or other invidious motive. Section 1395dd(b) supplies the hospital's determination of an emergency medical condition and ensuing stabilization-or-lawful-transfer duty; subsection (c) supplies the conditions for transfer of an unstabilized individual. Neither supplies an additional improper-motive element. Anti-dumping purpose cannot add one.

**R2 — Judgment.** Reverse the Sixth Circuit insofar as it affirmed summary judgment for the hospital on the appealed stabilization claim for failure to prove improper motive, and remand. The recognized genuine stabilization dispute cannot become an undisputed finding of compliance or lack of actual knowledge. The alternative knowledge and completed-screening defenses remain for lawful consideration on their actual predicates and supported record; neither is rejected categorically or accepted on unknown facts. The separate state ostensible-agency ruling is not reopened.

**R3 — Conditions and limits.** The Court decides neither the required emergency condition, hospital determination, duration of any duty, stabilization, lawful transfer, causal injury nor ultimate liability. No subsection (a) screening-motive rule, general federal malpractice action, personal liability, trial order or damages award follows. Subsection (d)(2)(A)'s express hospital action retains direct personal harm, state-law personal-injury damages and appropriate equitable relief, and its separate two-year limitation.

The duty remains examination and treatment within available staff and facilities or transfer consistent with subsection (c). Treatment refusal under (b)(2), transfer refusal under (b)(3), and (c)(1)'s informed written transfer request or qualifying medical certification together with appropriate transfer are distinct. Their risk/benefit information and reasonable written-refusal efforts, certification and applicable countersignature, risk-minimizing care within capacity, receiving acceptance and resources, medical records, qualified transport and any further regulatory conditions remain; none is found satisfied or violated here. A motive ruling replaces none of them.

## California Public Employees' Retirement System v. Felzen — January 20, 1999

### Components and sufficient ground

**F1 — Appellate status.** A shareholder who merely objects to a Rule 23.1 derivative settlement, without becoming a party through intervention, does not acquire the status needed to appeal approval. Financial injury, an objection opportunity and entitlement to conduct an appeal are distinct. The derivative claim belongs to the corporation; the notice-and-objection procedure does not itself make every shareholder a party prosecuting that claim on appeal.

**F2 — Application and reliance.** Affirm dismissal of CalPERS's attempted settlement appeal. The supported fact is a deliberate choice not to seek intervention in reliance on circuit practice, not a denied or neglected application. Marino already supplied the general party/intervention principle, including for persons who objected at the approval hearing. Contrary circuit practice does not itself confer the asserted status. This conclusion does not require declaring that Harper eliminated every prospective disposition or every party-status restriction jurisdictional.

**F3 — Limits and routes.** Intervention and review of its denial remain distinct routes subject to their actual requirements. United Airlines v. McDonald does not convert an unfiled motion into permitted intervention or guarantee a late motion here. No tolling, reopening, excusable-default ruling, new filing period or mandatory intervention grant is ordered. Richards preserves constitutional adequacy of representation before binding persons; it does not grant every affected person an appeal or decide actual representation here. No settlement-fairness, attorney-fee, direct-shareholder-claim or comprehensive collateral-preclusion ruling follows. Dismissal of the appeal is not approval of the bargain's merits.

All eight Associates support F1–F3 and affirmance. The presented threshold question permits review of the appellate refusal itself; the nonparty rule is not used to prevent deciding whether that refusal was correct. The Florida board's participation below does not establish an additional Supreme Court petitioner or docket.

### Two lawful paths and remedial alternative

**Party-equivalent objector:** Rule 23.1 expressly invites objections and a representative settlement can affect the shareholder. Allowing a participating objector to appeal protects against a bargain accepted by all named parties and avoids forfeiting review after reliance on circuit practice. The strongest distinction concerns the representative relationship, unlike Marino's separate employment-right claimants or Izumi's distinct unreviewed intervention denial.

**Strongest objection:** Marino's appellants themselves objected at the approval hearing without intervening. Participation and adverse effect therefore are insufficient without an additional source of status. Kamen identifies the corporate cause and allocation of authority over it. Notice, objection and intervention perform distinct jobs. Richards protects the prerequisite to binding; it does not equate a lawful representative relationship with a personal right to conduct the corporate appeal.

**Require intervention:** Apply Marino's existing rule with the corporate relationship identified by Kamen; Izumi reinforces the distinction but does not already decide this §1291/Rule 23.1 question. The strongest objection is that settling representatives rarely appeal their own bargain and another motion can frustrate meaningful review. Intervention is the established route to obtain the needed status and its denial is reviewable. This is neither a finding that an application certainly would succeed nor a duty to intervene whenever another case affects personal rights.

**Reliance/remedy:** A genuine request or independently authorized remaining opportunity could be assessed under its own requirements. No such motion or established reopening entitlement exists in this record. Harper cannot be expanded into a universal abolition of prospectivity, but the general party principle predates the attempted appeal. Reliance on contrary circuit practice does not create appellate status. A remand to manufacture a missing intervention record is unwarranted.
