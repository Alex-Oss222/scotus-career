## 1. Added authority and exact analogical proposition

The [neutral addendum](OT_1998CHUNK11_OLMSTEAD_INTRACLASS_NEUTRAL_ADDENDUM.md) and [supplemental entering-law slice](../entering-law/OT_1998CHUNK11_OLMSTEAD_INTRACLASS.md) reproduce the following existing simulated public law:

- ***O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp.*, April 1, 1996:** Part I rejects an indispensable under-forty replacement condition under the ADEA. Part II, joined fully by all eight Associates, requires a logical connection between the initial evidence and age discrimination; a substantially younger replacement may support the inference despite protected status, and other evidence can supply it without an indispensable replacement. An insignificant age difference alone is insufficient, without a numerical minimum. Parts II–III preserve production, persuasion, evidentiary rules, alternative grounds and the separation of removal of an invalid gate from liability or relief. Justice O'Connor authored these propositions.
- ***Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.*, March 19, 1997:** Thomas's unanimous opinion separately rejects exclusion because claimant and harasser share a sex and a mandatory actual opposite-sex coworker as a route of causal proof. Direct or circumstantial evidence can establish discrimination because of sex; common sex, sexual content, contact or severity alone supplies neither immunity nor liability. The independent hostile-environment, attribution and remedial predicates remain.

These are controlling holdings in their employment settings and date-eligible analogical support here. The added inference is confined to this proposition: **membership of all relevant service recipients in the protected disability population does not itself establish an evidentiary or definitional immunity; absence of an actual nondisabled person receiving the identical service does not alone defeat a properly supported Title II theory.** Protected status and the treatment prohibited by the statute must be analyzed separately. No universal rule for every discrimination statute is inferred.

*Oncale*'s express comparator-proof holding matters independently of its same-sex-actor holding. *O'Connor*'s actual younger replacement was inside the protected group, so that case alone does not demonstrate the absence of every possible factual comparator; its express reservation concerning other evidence also matters. Neither case resolves whether the challenged segregated mode of providing these existing services is discrimination under §§12132 and 12134. That remains the issue answered differently by the original commitments.

## 3. Competing paths tested and remaining limits

**Strongest change argument:** Because all eight Associates have expressly rejected outside-group barriers, a statutory objection built on the absence of a nondisabled recipient cannot survive by relabeling that absence a failure of discrimination. In particular, Thomas's authored *Oncale* comparator holding is stronger evidence than a generic assumption about his interpretive approach. This argument rules out that categorical rationale for every Associate. The original commitments already excluded it, so it does not displace their actual sufficient grounds.

**Strongest distinction:** Removing a group-boundary gate does not itself classify a service allocation or mode of treatment as unlawful discrimination. *O'Connor* concerns age causation under an assumed ADEA inference framework; *Oncale* concerns conditions already recognized as potentially discriminatory under Title VII, with independent sex causation and hostile-environment requirements. Neither defines Title II's benefit or integration obligation. The affirmative commitments still need §§12132–12134 and the integration/modification provisions; the statutory objections still must engage those provisions substantively rather than require an outside-class comparator. The prior disagreement on that legal work therefore remains supported.

The analogous remedy lesson is also limited: correcting an invalid categorical exclusion does not establish liability or automatically require a trial, new evidence, placement or remand. Neither new source resolves Olmstead's actual scope/resource-defense fork, the legal significance of the later district-court order, or a disputed clinical or financial fact. No ADEA burden sequence, Title VII severe-or-pervasive threshold, employment-attribution rule, intent-only limitation, numerical comparator condition or new Title II burden is imported. The State's existing regulatory demonstration burden remains as originally stated. No §5 determination or same-day *Sutton* authority enters.
