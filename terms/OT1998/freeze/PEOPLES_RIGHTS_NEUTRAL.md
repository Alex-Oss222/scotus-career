### Issue 1: Concrete pre-enforcement injury

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lujan requires actual threatened injury, causation and redressability; declaratory relief does not require inviting criminal prosecution where intended possession and a credible enforcement threat are established. Hunt governs associational standing.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** These owners identify existing guns and the City admits it intends enforcement. Their choice between surrendering local possession and risking prosecution is concrete.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III; Declaratory Judgment Act; Abbott Laboratories.

### Issue 2: Notice and governing personal protection

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Love v. Pepersack (December 11, 1995) recognizes a personal arms right, incorporation and necessary acquisition without militia enrollment. Hale (February 12, 1996) and Rybar (November 24, 1997) reject both automatic military-utility immunity and affiliation-based exclusion. Their arms remands leave ultimate machinegun applications open; no comprehensive scrutiny tier, automatic-weapons exclusion or mandatory evidentiary hearing was adopted.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Love makes it incorrect to dismiss all constitutional significance of personal keeping. Kolender and Hoffman Estates require attention to notice, enforcement discretion, criminal sanctions and scienter. A prosecutor’s inconsistent construction is not an authoritative limiting construction.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; Columbus City Code §§2323.11(G),2323.31,2323.32.

### Issue 3: Grandfather classifications

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Romer, Cleburne and ordinary equal-protection principles require a legitimate relationship between a classification and its asserted purpose; preserving genuine reliance can be legitimate without proving every grandfather distinction valid.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The invalid 1989 registration scheme creates an arbitrary dividing line between similarly situated possessors. The separate federal-registration exception can rationally protect reliance and lawful use even if it extends to magazines not individually registered; ordinary rationality does not require a perfect device-by-device fit.
* **Other Governing Law:** City of New Orleans v. Dukes; §2323.32(B)(2).

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed in part and reversed in part the district court’s judgment, 925 F. Supp. 1254. It invalidated all five assault-weapon definitions and the 1989-registration distinctions, while upholding the remaining federal-registration magazine exception.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Owners contend that strict criminal liability turns on inaccessible design history, hypothetical magazine availability and an expired registration window under an invalid law. The City invokes objective capacity, a default recklessness provision and protection of reliance interests.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Assess concrete threatened possession and each distinct definition and exception. Fair-notice relief does not itself establish that a sufficiently clear restriction would survive substantive arms review; uphold separable provisions where their own predicates remain valid.
