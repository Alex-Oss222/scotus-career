### Issue 1: Machinegun commerce authority

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lopez (April 26, 1995) requires an identified interstate mechanism before aggregation. Hale, Rybar and Kirk (February 14, 1998) sustain §922(o)’s possession component as necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of the national post-1986 machinegun transfer/stock regime. Neither marketability, remote interstate provenance nor generic crime costs alone establishes federal power.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The completed automatic weapon falls within the same transfer/stock regime. Interstate ammunition proof is not needed to add an element to §922(o), and generalized aggregation cannot replace the controlling mechanism.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §922(o).

### Issue 2: Peremptory challenges and counsel

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Batson requires a genuine determination of discriminatory purpose; Hernandez respects supported credibility findings, while Purkett’s facial-neutrality step does not complete the ultimate inquiry. Strickland requires deficient performance and prejudice.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Timeliness serves the ability to correct jury selection. An alternative credibility ruling must still be checked as an ultimate discrimination ruling; an untimely objection alone does not defeat every ineffective-assistance claim.
* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; Sixth Amendment.

### Issue 3: Departures

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Koon requires review for abuse of discretion while enforcing legal limits on departure grounds; Watts does not dispense with reliable factual support or authorize double counting.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Discharge and underrepresented criminal history must be tied to the actual findings and guideline structure. Gonzalez’s ammunition conviction does not automatically authorize punishment for any nearby weapon.
* **Other Governing Law:** U.S.S.G. §§1B1.3,4A1.3,5K2.6.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** Second Circuit affirmance of the Southern District of New York judgments in the consolidated appeals of Robert Franklyn (also known as Franklin) and Ralph Gonzalez (also known as Raphael Quinones).
* **Material Positions and Limits:** Franklyn disputes federal power and the treatment of allegedly discriminatory strikes. Gonzalez contests whether the stated circumstances justify departures beyond the ordinary guideline range.
