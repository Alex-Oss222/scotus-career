## Enhanced issue comparison

### 1. Actual disability and mitigating measures

Path A: assess substantial limitation in the person's actual state, including measures actually used and their beneficial or adverse effects. The statute asks what substantially limits this individual, not what would limit an imagined untreated version. A device does not erase an impairment or invariably eliminate disability, but it can remove the substantial functional limitation. This interpretation preserves the record-of and regarded-as branches and does not confuse disability with qualification. On these allegations vision is20/20 or better with lenses, and amended complaint¶37(e) expressly states identical functioning with correction. Paragraph37(d)'s constant dependence and inability without lenses are accepted; they do not allege an additional substantial limitation while corrected. The actual-seeing theory therefore fails under this interpretation.

Strongest objection: this permits the very successful mitigation that makes someone qualified to exclude the person from protection against impairment-based discrimination; the accommodation/qualification structure, Rehabilitation Act continuity, legislative explanations and agency guidance support measuring the underlying impairment before mitigation. Response for A: Congress separately required a substantial individual limitation, rather than prohibiting every disadvantage based on any physical impairment. Recognizing present function gives that enacted threshold work. Qualification remains a later distinct inquiry for people who satisfy one of the coverage branches. The Court may not invent current adverse effects or turn the undisputed constant use of ordinary lenses into a substantial limitation inconsistent with the complaint's identified normal functioning. Other treatments and actually burdensome corrections require their own individual assessment.

Path B: assess this underlying substantial impairment without the mitigating lenses, then consider actual corrected performance under qualification and any defense. Severe uncorrected acuity and the specifically pleaded inability to drive, shop or watch television suffice to plead substantial limitation of seeing under this construction. The statutory reference to an impairment that substantially limits supports identifying the condition's effects, while the separate reasonable-accommodation structure avoids denying protection to people whose management permits ordinary participation. Individualized assessment still distinguishes trivial impairment from this severe uncorrected limitation; it is not diagnosis-only automatic coverage.

Strongest objection: the statutory present-tense, individual-specific wording describes actual limits, and a universal disregard rule can protect many people whose actual lives face no substantial restriction. Response for B: the condition is real and permanent, and lenses are an ongoing means of overcoming its effect, not an absence of the effect underlying the exclusion. A sufficiently substantial underlying limitation remains required. The remedial design and consistent explanatory materials resolve the interpretive choice without converting protected status into hiring entitlement. This construction does not decide every medication/device question or whether every correctable visual condition qualifies.

A narrower remand to discover unpleaded burdens of these lenses is not adopted by either group. The complaint affirmatively describes identical corrected function; ordinary favorable pleading treatment does not create a different asserted injury. Path B's remand instead rests on the pleaded uncorrected limitation and a different legal reading.

### 2. EEOC authority and Appendix weight

Path A: enforce the statutory individual present-limitation requirement and decline to give dispositive force to contrary Appendix guidance. The appended no-mitigation instruction is distinguishable from the promulgated regulatory definitions, which do not themselves settle mitigation. Even assuming the fullest relevant TitleI delegated authority, Chevron/Haggar does not permit an interpretation contrary to the governing statutory boundary. The Appendix's consistency, expertise, notice/comment history and Rehabilitation Act connection merit consideration; they do not establish legislative force or overcome that boundary. It is unnecessary to resolve the entire disputed extent of EEOC power over§12102, which lies outside TitleI's particular definitions.

Strongest objection: the statute is genuinely ambiguous, Congress delegated implementation of TitleI, and a longstanding considered interpretation should influence the coverage inquiry. Response: the adopted statutory reading supplies the sufficient ground, with persuasive arguments expressly considered. Do not hold that every Appendix statement is powerless, that all EEOC regulations lack authority, or that a label alone resolves delegation.

Path B: the no-mitigation guidance is persuasive because it coherently reconciles protection, accommodation and qualification and accords with the statute's inherited setting and legislative explanations. Haggar permits appropriate administrative interpretation only within actual delegated authority; nothing requires assigning this Appendix binding legislative force to adopt its sound reading independently. The strongest objection is the distinction between a regulation and guidance and the disputed delegation over the general definition. Response: those questions foreclose automatic deference, but do not erase persuasive force or independent judicial adoption. Neither Stevens nor Breyer needs a categorical declaration that this guidance controls every title. No separate affirmative delegation holding is supported by a necessary decisional coalition.

### 3. Regarded-as working

Path A: on the assumption that working can supply the relevant activity, the employer must regard the applicant as substantially restricted in a class or broad range of jobs, rather than a particular specialized employment opportunity. Perceiving an impairment or rejecting an applicant under a demanding standard does not alone establish that perception. Even accepting the appellate assumption of rejection from all United pilot positions and the complaint's breadth across aircraft/routes, the asserted category of global airline pilot remains specialized in relation to other piloting employment using the applicants' training. The pleading does not allege United regards them as unable to undertake the wider range. The number of vacancies at one or several global carriers does not itself change the occupational scope. Affirm dismissal of this presented theory.

Strongest objection: all United flying positions, across models and conditions, can constitute an occupational class; regarded-as addresses United's perception, so actual work for other employers cannot defeat it. Response for A: actual employment elsewhere is not conclusive proof of United's beliefs, and neither universal exclusion by all employers nor proof of actual disability is demanded. The legal deficiency is the scope of the perception alleged, not a finding that every other airline would hire them or that United's safety rationale is true. The asserted global-carrier specialization does not encompass the broader piloting work; thousands of similar opportunities do not automatically create a statutory class.

Path B: treat the breadth of the allegation and common skills as sufficient at the pleading stage and remand to examine the employer's perceived occupational exclusion; a bare “single job” label should not resolve a fact-dependent class boundary. Strongest objection: the definition's substantiality requirement would become any large employer's refusal to hire for its characteristic job. Response for B: a properly occupational class inquiry could prevent that result without a categorical employer-size rule. The two actual-disability dissenters need not adopt this independent route to obtain reversal and reserve its application rather than supply a conditional merits vote. They do not endorse converting actual successful work for others into a conclusive answer to regarded-as perception.

No general holding that working always is, or never is, a major life activity is necessary. No new claim, evidence about United's actual subjective reasoning, or safety finding is made.

## Coalition, remedy and separate function

Six Associates—O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg—affirm the dismissal on the corrected-state actual-disability rule plus the independently insufficient regarded-as-working claim. Their same sufficient grounds can form a common rationale at that scope. Stevens and Breyer reverse/remand on actual disability; their reservation on regarded-as is not agreement with its dismissal and supplies no affirmative contrary application holding. All eight keep coverage separate from qualification, discrimination and defenses and retain Haggar's real-authority framework. No binding EEOC-delegation holding or general working-activity classification is manufactured.

Affirmance leaves the pleaded claims dismissed; it neither validates every aspect of United's policy nor decides liability, damages or safety. The reversal position reinstates the actual-disability claim for ordinary proceedings under its interpretation, leaving essential functions, lawful standards, direct threat, accommodation, causation and relief to resolution on an appropriate record. FAA certificates are relevant facts but do not themselves establish every employer qualification rule unlawful. No hiring order, automatic trial or damages follows.

A Stevens/Breyer separate position serves the substantive function of explaining the competing construction, the persuasive agency/legislative sources, and the practical coverage/qualification interaction. Any narrower Ginsburg qualification must preserve the individual-proof limit but need not become a separate writing when the shared opinion states it. Authorship is unassigned. No vote or rule is inferred from a historical Sutton, Murphy or Albertsons decision.
