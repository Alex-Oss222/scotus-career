# OT1998 chunk 5 - Sullivan independent commitments

**Case and cutoff:** American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan, No. 97-2000; March 3, 1999. All eight Associates participate. Stone is neither modeled nor counted. These conclusions follow the separately frozen [Sullivan neutral packet](OT_1998CHUNK5_SULLIVAN_NEUTRAL.md). No historical comparator or present Stone material was received. No opinion author is assigned.

## Components and proposed operative propositions

**A - Private attribution.** Reverse the Third Circuit insofar as it makes these private carriers state actors merely by electing the described utilization-review withholding procedure. Statutory permission, compulsory insurance coverage, facial Bureau screening and assignment, enforcement of compensation obligations, and later review do not, on this record, make the insurer's independently chosen initial withholding the State's own act. The Bureau does not substantively approve the initial medical objection or order the suspension. Payment of private employers' obligations is not an exclusively governmental function, and no Lebron institutional-status predicate is established. This is a decision about the supported attribution theory, not an immunity for every private actor or a rule that a legislature's own action escapes review.

**B - Included withholding question and its limit.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas would also reach the petition's second question and reject an unconditional constitutional rule requiring payment of every disputed medical bill before the particular service's reasonable-and-necessary character has been established. General eligibility for compensation is not by itself an unconditional entitlement to payment for every proposed or supplied service. That distinction does not eliminate a worker's substantive state-law entitlement to reasonable and necessary care, establish that any particular challenged service was unnecessary, or decide process for interrupting a service already covered by an operative agreement or adjudication. The insurer's assertion of a dispute alone does not conclusively establish that the claimed service falls outside an existing entitlement.

Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer would decide A and decline B in this petition after that disposition. They would not convert the private-carrier appeal into a final adjudication of every public-defendant deprivation. They do not adopt an unconditional constitutional advance-payment rule; their objection is to this Court's additional merits determination and its remedial reach on the present presentation.

**C - Disposition and residual proceedings.** All eight reverse the private-carrier attribution holding and remand for entry of judgment rejecting that section 1983 theory against those petitioners. They do not award damages or certify a class. The five reaching B additionally would vacate the categorical advance-payment/severance disposition only to the extent it necessarily treats general compensation eligibility as an unconditional right to pay every disputed service pending its initial determination. They would remand the public-defendant proceedings for application of that distinction, rather than direct wholesale dismissal. The independent notice/personal-statement features expressly excluded from review are not newly invalidated or approved. Any remaining remedy must rest on a surviving, preserved violation and fit that violation. The three declining B would leave the public-defendant merits and their remedial consequences undisturbed by this appeal, subject to ordinary remand proceedings already required below. The two scope dispositions must not be recorded as eight votes for a complete vacatur of the public claims.

## Adversarial paths common to the issue-specific rows

**Attribution path A1:** The initial withholding remains private. Its strongest objection is that Bureau approval is legally necessary, workers cannot opt out of coverage, and the system delegates interruption of an existing public entitlement. The supported answer is functional: screening establishes a procedurally proper request but supplies neither a substantive medical decision nor execution of an official seizure. The benefit obligation originates in private employment injury; later state adjudication of its scope does not attribute every earlier payment dispute. Alleged loss of care increases the importance of protection but does not alone supply the missing attribution.

**Attribution path A2:** The combination constitutes joint governmental deprivation or significant encouragement. Its strongest objection is Blum's distinction between the State's assistance and the particular private decision, plus Lugar's separate attribution element. A2 could govern materially different supported official involvement or an actual delegated state function; the recovered record does not establish that narrower premise here. Denver Area's content-selected legislative permission positions permit direct scrutiny of government's law; they do not supply a majority rule turning every permitted act into private-party liability. Lebron is institutional, not a general public-benefit test.

**Withholding path B1:** Reach the included legal question and distinguish conditional service entitlement from a universal advance-payment right. Its strongest objection is Loudermill: a State cannot destroy a substantive benefit simply by requiring an adverse recipient to prove it again, especially where continuing treatment or prior orders are alleged. B1 survives only at the expressed level of generality. It makes no finding that an already established service entitlement has disappeared, gives no conclusive effect to an insurer's objection, and requires actor-, treatment-, procedure-, and remedy-specific treatment of surviving public claims. No finding of the adequacy of all existing procedures follows.

**Withholding path B2:** Decline the additional question after private attribution is resolved, retaining the public-defendant issues for their proper presentation. Its strongest objection is that withholding is expressly presented, could independently support reversal, and the lower severance affects the system in which petitioners operate. That makes B1 legally available rather than automatically beyond the grant. B2 rests on the separability of the private relief, the actual unchallenged process features, unresolved public-party defenses and live-relief issues, and materially different care/entitlement applications. Petitioners' asserted assessment exposure does not prove every condition necessary to decide the public claims finally.

**Process-sufficiency alternative B3:** Assume a protected deprivation and uphold temporary withholding under Mathews. Its strongest support is professional review, potential payment with interest, insurer review costs, and the cost of irrecoverable payments. The strongest objection is that completed bills and interrupted ongoing care differ, delays are disputed, and the earlier regime provided no personal statement or warning of suspension. No Associate adopts a complete B3 merits holding on this record. It would exceed petitioners' retained issue if used to undo expressly unchallenged process requirements. This does not decide that every withholding procedure is deficient.
