Reed allows cause when a constitutional claim's legal basis was not reasonably available, preserving its actual novelty categories and the contextual inquiry for arguably sanctioned practice. Engle distinguishes reasonable availability from futility in an unsympathetic court; other counsel's actual litigation is material to availability without requiring every lawyer to anticipate every future doctrine. Carrier requires an objective external impediment in the ordinary case; failure to perceive or raise an available claim is not alone cause. Constitutionally ineffective assistance is a distinct potential cause ground that generally must first be presented as an independent claim in state court; ordinary lawyer error, exhaustion and actual procedural default remain different matters.

Lucas's 1983 gas section shows that counsel then presented an Eighth Amendment cyanide-gas claim based on unnecessary pain and lingering death, supported by eyewitness accounts and medical evidence. Its earlier adverse result does not itself establish cause under Reed or Engle.
