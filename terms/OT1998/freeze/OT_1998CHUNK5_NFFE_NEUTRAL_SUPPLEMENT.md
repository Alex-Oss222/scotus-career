The Survey sought Interior's advice and independently followed it; the Authority found a violation by the Survey under section 7116(a)(1) and (5), but dismissed the allegations against Interior because advice was not proved direction or interference. That dismissal is not automatically reopened by review of the Survey bargaining obligation.

Interior disapproved that agreement under section 7114(c). The complaint did not allege a violation based on disapproval. This reservation is not adoption of the disputed clause and supplies no new attack on the disapproval.
- Part IV.A.1 says the clause reiterates a statutory right under IRS II/MSPB. Part IV.A.2 expressly rejects requests to revert to IRS I, adheres to the Authority's midterm position, distinguishes covered-matter repose from denial of any midterm duty, and adopts Energy's independent ground that even agreement with SSA would not change negotiability because contractual proposals can be negotiable without a statutory entitlement to their substance. It expressly makes no judgment on the proposal's merits.
- The existing order therefore contains an actual independent negotiability rationale. A reviewing court must evaluate that rationale; it cannot describe the entire order as silence or a counsel-created ground. Whether those reasons constitute a sufficient independent administrative choice, or instead still require reconsideration after correction of the governing legal framework, remains a legal question.

The order directs bargaining on request, notice posting for sixty consecutive days and a compliance report within thirty days of the Authority order. It imposes no contract term, retroactive agreement or actual Panel resolution. These original administrative periods are not newly started Supreme Court deadlines.
