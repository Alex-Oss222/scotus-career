## Common operative commitments

### 1. The reviewing court must consider the duly promulgated regulation under Chevron

Reverse the Federal Circuit's categorical exclusion of Chevron review. Congress's tariff-implementation delegation to Treasury and Customs is compatible with the Court of International Trade's duty to make determinations on the record made before it under §2640(a)(1). That record requirement does not itself instruct the court to disregard an authorized regulation or withdraw delegated interpretive discretion. Section 10.11's assurance of judicial review likewise preserves review without turning it into authority to ignore a lawful regulation.

The reviewing court first determines statutory meaning and the actual delegation. Clear statutory limits govern; where Congress leaves a delegated choice open, the court accepts a reasonable regulatory construction. Neither the agency's identity nor notice and comment alone establishes statutory authority or substantive validity. This holding concerns the promulgated tariff regulation here, not every informal classification letter or every Customs decision. No later administrative-law doctrine is imported.

### 2. The challenged exclusion is a reasonable construction at its stated scope

Answer the second granted question directly: §10.16(c), including paragraph (c)(4), reasonably treats a significant separate process whose primary purpose is to give clothing a new permanent chemical characteristic through permapressing as outside operations incidental to assembly. The rule is read with its introductory significance, purpose and relationship terms and the statute's actual allowance for incidental operations. Merely naming a step permapressing, finding a chemical effect, or noting that it occurred after sewing does not independently establish every regulatory predicate or decide the statute's other conditions.

The statutory exemption applies to United States fabricated components exported ready for assembly without further fabrication, retaining their physical identity, and not advanced in value or improved abroad except by assembly or operations incidental to assembly, such as cleaning, lubricating and painting. Those examples show that some improvement is compatible with incidental character. They do not require treating a significant process directed principally to adding a lasting garment quality as assembly merely because performing it abroad is convenient or economically efficient. The regulation supplies a permissible distinction between ancillary assembly/finishing operations and significant production of additional characteristics; it does not replace the statute with a rule against every value-enhancing treatment.

The introductory paragraph has distinct alternatives: a significant nonassembly process whose primary purpose is fabrication, completion, or physical or chemical improvement, **or** a significant process unrelated to assembly. Those alternatives are not collapsed into a requirement that every covered improvement be wholly unrelated to assembly. Section 10.16(b), in turn, permits incidental operations before, during or after assembly and includes final pressing and folding. Ordinary pressing that completes assembled clothing and the chemically activated permanent-press characteristic described by (c)(4) are not the same legal category merely because both use heat. Preservative coating and other statutory incidental examples retain their actual protection; chemical effect alone cannot defeat it.

The Court need not find that this is the only permissible construction or that every possible permapressing operation necessarily meets the rule's terms. The reasonableness question can be resolved from the enacted phrase, its examples and the regulation's articulated classifications. No missing administrative motive, concession or extra agency explanation is invented: the operative construction under review appears in the rule itself. Its permissible boundary can be decided while remaining application questions are returned.

### 3. Return the classification and refund controversy for application of the lawful rule

Reverse and remand. The lower courts must apply the regulation under the foregoing legal conclusions, rather than exclude Chevron or reopen the challenged exclusion's categorical permissibility. They must determine its application to the actual processing and resolve the refund claim under the established record and ordinary procedure. The Court does not direct collection of a particular amount, reject every refund claim, or award the exemption.

The supplied account establishes chemically pretreated United States fabric, sewing and ordinary pressing in Mexico, followed by 12–15 minutes of baking to activate permanent-press qualities. It does not establish every shipment-level eligibility determination or require this Court to resolve every significance, primary-purpose and assembly-relationship application. Haggar's assertion that later baking would require additional steps and risk unwanted shipment creases is its argument, not an adjudicated finding of statutory necessity. That argument may bear on application; convenience alone cannot turn the named significant chemical-improvement category into a statutorily compelled incidental operation.

The exemption is a partial duty reduction based on qualifying United States component cost or value, not duty-free entry of the entire article. The enacted proportional treatment for specific or compound rates remains. No additional filing, evidentiary hearing, agency proceeding, deadline or burden of proof is ordered. Ordinary proceedings determine what application work remains under the corrected law; source unavailability is not a party default.

## Adversarial path assessment

**Independent construction without Chevron.** Its strongest ground is the court's special trade jurisdiction and own-record review coupled with §10.11's promise of review. It fails because neither provision withdraws the pertinent statutory delegation. Judicial control of statutory bounds and agency compliance remains real under Chevron. O'Connor's and Thomas's particular-allocation concerns in AT&T require examining this allocation; they do not make all judicial review incompatible with delegated interpretation.

**Invalidate the permapressing exclusion.** Its strongest ground is that incidental operations can improve an article and that paragraph (b)(7) expressly permits final pressing. The chemically pretreated fabric and production sequence also undermine any simplistic claim that all fabrication first occurred abroad. Those points preclude a chemical-effect-only or after-sewing-only rule. They do not make the adopted distinction unreasonable: new permanent characteristics supplied through a significant process can be distinguished from the statute's ancillary operations, even where practical manufacturing considerations connect the processes. The ruling sustains the rule with its actual introductory terms, not an unlimited named-process prohibition detached from them.

**Remand both reasonableness and application.** Its strongest ground is that the courts below excluded the proper framework and trade-specific factual considerations might illuminate the statute's boundary. A remand cannot be rejected merely because review includes question 2. Here, however, the supported legal texts permit a direct answer to the challenged category's reasonableness without making the disputed application findings. Neither an unprovided finding nor a missing agency explanation is made necessary to that bounded legal determination. Returning that same legal question without identifying a distinct unresolved premise would duplicate work rather than preserve an actual factual issue. Application and refund relief do require return and remain distinct.

**Sustain the rule and immediately deny all refund relief.** Its strongest ground is that the process description includes baking to activate permanent-press characteristics expressly mentioned by the rule. That makes the rule materially relevant but does not supply every application and eligibility finding. A legal classification cannot be substituted for the lower courts' unfinished application on the complete trial record. The Court therefore does not enter final shipment-level liability.
