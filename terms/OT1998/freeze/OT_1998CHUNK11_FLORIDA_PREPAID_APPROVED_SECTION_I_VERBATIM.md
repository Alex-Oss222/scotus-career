* **Question Presented:** Whether Congress validly subjected nonconsenting States to private patent-infringement damages actions through the Patent and Plant Variety Protection Remedy Clarification Act.
* **Question Granted:** Whether Congress validly subjected nonconsenting States to private patent-infringement damages actions through the Patent and Plant Variety Protection Remedy Clarification Act.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit affirmed denial of Florida Prepaid’s sovereign-immunity defense and allowed College Savings Bank’s patent action to proceed.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse and dismiss the damages action as barred by state sovereign immunity.
* **Material Record:** College Savings alleged that Florida Prepaid, a state entity marketing prepaid tuition plans, infringed its patent for an investment method. ### Issue 1: Express patent remedy and Article I power

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Pennsylvania v. Union Gas retains its decided force. Seminole Tribe does not adopt a categorical Article I prohibition; its officer-remedy holding and statutory direct-State analysis do not decide constitutional amenability in every federal action.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The Patent Remedy Act expressly includes States. The unresolved constitutional question is whether the surviving Article I principle extends from the commerce setting to nationally uniform patent enforcement.
* **Other Governing Law:** Patent Clause; 35 U.S.C. §296; 28 U.S.C. §1338.

### Issue 2: Separate Fourteenth Amendment theory

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** City of Boerne requires congruence and proportionality between an identified constitutional injury and §5 legislation. Fitzpatrick recognizes distinct Reconstruction-amendment authority.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Infringement of a patent is not automatically deprivation without due process. State remedies, fault and the legislative predicate matter to this independent theory; statutory clarity does not prove §5 validity.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment §§1,5.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The Federal Circuit affirmed denial of Florida Prepaid’s sovereign-immunity defense and allowed College Savings Bank’s patent action to proceed.
* **Material Positions and Limits:** The patent owner invokes explicit abrogation and constitutional protection of its patent; Florida Prepaid disputes Congress’s remedial power and the required constitutional injury. Boerne permits preventive as well as direct remedial enforcement, and Seminole supplies no general prohibition on Article I damages.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A valid direct-violation application, a properly fitted preventive rule and any preserved alternative abrogation source require separate treatment. Statutory infringement alone proves none of them. A remand may preserve the willful-infringement claim without declaring universal immunity or universal liability.
