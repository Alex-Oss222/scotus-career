## Threshold commitment before the power question

**Selected threshold proposition T:** The controversy over legal authority for the December 23–April 17 restraint remains live on this record. GMD identifies a distinct claimed injury during a period when respondents had neither judgment-creditor status nor a proprietary interest. A ruling on the power to impose that restraint can affect whether it was wrongful for Rule 65(c) purposes. The subsequent money judgment and acceptance of a postjudgment injunction do not, without more, establish authority to restrain before judgment. This accepts neither bond liability nor the assertion that reversal guarantees damages. It permits adjudication of the challenged legal predicate, with the actual bond claim, causal loss, amount, defenses and proper further procedure left below.

**Strongest alternative T-alt:** The interlocutory appeal is moot because the preliminary order merged into an unchallenged permanent injunction; Camenisch generally sends surviving bond issues to trial on the merits and subsequent appeal. The Court could vacate the reviewed interlocutory judgment and remand for treatment of that appeal as moot, without an equity-power holding or disposition of the separate turnover appeal. The source-supported distinction is the difference between an expired provisional merits assessment and a legal prohibition confined to prejudgment time. Camenisch's warning that the case can remain alive while an issue becomes moot must be answered directly. Here the challenged legal authority is itself a surviving predicate of the asserted past-restraint injury; final success on the contract does not retrospectively resolve it. The final judgment is already entered, and the legal question does not depend on predicting contract liability. Consequently T is selected. No reliance is placed on the ambiguous May 4 caption as a conclusive resolution of Article III.

## Equity power, judgment and scope — independent commitments

### Stevens

**Judgment:** Affirm the reviewed preliminary-injunction judgment on the bounded preservation ground below, after rejecting mootness under T. His position concerns the original order's lawfulness, not current dissolution or affirmance of the later turnover and permanent orders.

**Ground and reach:** General equity can restrain disposition in this setting to prevent imminent insolvency and depletion from rendering an otherwise available judgment ineffectual. Injunction is a traditional equitable remedial form; its permissible adaptation is not exhausted by an historical catalogue of each commercial use. Deckert's threatened-depletion rationale and First National City Bank's preservation principle support the application by analogy, with their equitable and statutory distinctions acknowledged. De Beers's prohibition on interim relief unrelated to the eventual decree does not compel excluding preservation of assets available for the money recovery actually sought. His Mertens dissent join is evidence of attending to the actual traditional remedial function; his Varity join supports equity's operation within an established cause. Neither makes necessity a freestanding legal source.

**Strongest contrary path and response:** Reverse because an unsecured creditor has no equitable interest and historically had to obtain a judgment before a creditor's bill; Rule 64 supplies prejudgment-security routes that a Rule 65 injunction cannot bypass. This presents a serious structural objection. Stevens would distinguish temporary in-personam preservation from an adjudication of security, ownership or distribution priority: Rule 64's availability does not, on the supplied law, expressly extinguish general equitable injunction authority. The independent general-equity source, not Rule 65 alone, bears the weight. Blue Fox is not displaced; no sovereign payment or substituted obligation is imposed here.

**Application, remedy and barriers:** The district court's concrete threatened-noncollection findings, almost-certain success finding, notice/hearings and bond support affirmance under this ground. No rule makes mere financial difficulty, an ordinary debt or every lawful creditor preference sufficient. The merits brief leaves likely success and irreparable harm unchallenged in this review; he would not independently adopt the circuit's serious-questions alternative or convert its comments about duplicity into a fraud finding. No separate mandatory intent element is necessary on this record. He will not join a categorical bar to all such private preservation; he can join a formulation preserving genuine statutory/property remedies and denying automatic liens or priorities. No new injunction, bond award or disposition of final orders follows.

### O'Connor

**Judgment:** Reverse the Second Circuit's approval of the preliminary restraint and remand for proceedings consistent with the absence of general-equity authority on these predicates; T permits reaching that issue.

**Ground and reach:** A general creditor seeking only damages, without judgment or an equitable proprietary claim, cannot obtain this prejudgment security in unrelated assets solely through general federal equity. De Beers's final-relief relationship and the traditional creditor's-bill limitation supply the relevant boundary. Rule 65 administers available injunctions and does not supply the missing substantive entitlement; Rule 64's state-law security structure is not displaced simply because its requirements cannot be met. Her Mertens dissent supported an identified traditional trust-restoration remedy and expressly distinguished remedies arising only after merger. It does not support inventing the absent trust/property relation here. Her Blue Fox join confirms the need to establish entitlement rather than infer it from a remedial label.

**Strongest contrary path and response:** Affirm a restrained preservation power where findings and a bond show that eventual recovery otherwise fails. Deckert and First National City Bank support preserving effective relief, and a freeze need not formally establish priority. O'Connor would nevertheless require the underlying legal footing of those cases: equitable rescission/fund relief in Deckert and federal statutory tax enforcement in First National City Bank. The real restraint of competing uses and creditors' payments cannot be justified only by calling it temporary. Her position does not require all equity to be static; it rejects this change in the general creditor's substantive remedial position.

**Application, remedy and barriers:** Accept likely success and threatened noncollection as supplied; they do not supply power. No ruling on an additional intent requirement is necessary, and no inference that every transfer was lawful or fraudulent is made. She will not join a general assertion that equity can never grant monetary relief or affect foreign assets. A genuine lien, trust, fraudulent-transfer claim, statute, available attachment or postjudgment enforcement would present its own legal predicates, absent here. Remand may address an actual bond claim; it awards nothing and does not dissolve the permanent injunction or decide turnover.

### Scalia

**Judgment:** Reverse the reviewed preliminary-order ruling and remand on the absence of the asserted general-equity power, after T.

**Ground and reach:** The general jurisdictional grant carries historically recognized equitable authority, not an unrestricted power to create security for legal claimants. Where the plaintiff has neither judgment nor an equitable interest in the assets and requests only contract damages, the requested freeze crosses the traditional creditor limitation. De Beers supports requiring preliminary relief within the permissible substantive remedial relation. Mertens, which he authored, does not directly govern but demonstrates why the content of equitable authority cannot be replaced by the proposition that courts of equity can do what effective relief requires. His Blue Fox opinion likewise separates legal entitlement from labels and need. Rule 65 does not enlarge jurisdiction or substantive remedies; Rule 64's express provision for state-law security confirms that unavailable attachment is not a reason to create its equivalent through a new general rule.

**Strongest contrary path and response:** Affirm because the injunction is a traditional equitable form and general equity develops to prevent frustration of judgments. The strongest version preserves assets temporarily without conferring title and invokes Deckert/First National City Bank. Scalia would reject classification by form alone: the actual operation is prejudgment security for an unsecured money claim, while those authorities rested on materially different equitable or statutory entitlements. Modern English experience and lower-circuit practice cannot establish the historical content of the federal grant. Congress may supply additional authority; desirability alone is no substitute.

**Application, remedy and barriers:** The legal-power defect is dispositive even accepting the adverse district-court findings. No additional intent inquiry, alternate irreparable-injury rule, attachment adjudication or fraud remand is needed. He can join an express reservation of genuine property, statutory, rescission and postjudgment cases; will not join a universal ban on all monetary equity. Reverse the legal approval, preserve proper bond proceedings without finding liability, and leave the independent final orders outside the mandate.

### Kennedy

**Judgment:** Reverse and remand the preliminary-injunction judgment after T, with a rule confined to the asserted general-creditor power.

**Ground and reach:** The unsecured damages claim lacks the judgment or proprietary equitable entitlement that would support this restraint under general equity. De Beers and the historical creditor limitation distinguish preservation of an available equitable remedy from judicial creation of security. His Mertens join preserves the substantive content of equitable categories; his Varity join permits meaningful individual equitable redress for an actual authorized violation, not an unlimited necessity exception. Blue Fox's actual-entitlement analysis reinforces the distinction, while its sovereign-immunity scope is expressly preserved. Rule 64 cannot be supplanted merely because this case's state-law route is unavailing; Rule 65 alone is insufficient.

**Strongest contrary path and response:** Affirm a narrowly constrained freeze under the preservation principle of Deckert and First National City Bank, because near-certain liability and actual dissipation make legal recovery inadequate. Kennedy would recognize the practical concern but treat the power question as anterior to discretion. Those authorities do not eliminate their fund/equitable and statutory predicates. A general preservation rule risks affecting the unsecured-creditor relation without its traditional or enacted source; a bond protects some losses but does not supply that source.

**Application, remedy and barriers:** No remand to add an intent finding or refine hardship balances can cure this legal defect on the existing claim. He accepts the supplied findings for this appeal and does not adjudicate their collateral characterization. He can join reservations for independent lawfully pleaded causes or authorized remedies, including appropriate in-personam decrees affecting foreign assets; cannot join an assertion that personal jurisdiction itself authorizes the freeze. Reverse only the reviewed approval and leave bond causation, damages and independent defenses below, and the money judgment/final restraints outside review.

### Souter

**Judgment:** Affirm the preliminary-injunction judgment on bounded general-equity preservation, after T.

**Ground and reach:** The Court may preserve the practical efficacy of the contract judgment in the demonstrated insolvency/depletion circumstances without awarding compensation in equity or creating a lien. Souter's Mertens join limits the substantive compensatory category selected by ERISA; it does not remove an injunction from general equity merely because the underlying final award will be legal damages. His Varity rule rejects an absolute remedial exclusion when an actual violation calls for appropriate individual equity, while preserving qualified adequacy constraints. The analogy favors keeping the source and function distinct here: a valid contract cause supports the final remedy; general equity supplies temporary preservation. Deckert and First National City Bank furnish the preservation rationale but not mechanically controlling facts. De Beers remains a limit against restraints with no connection to permissible final relief.

**Strongest contrary path and response:** Reverse because a historical creditor's bill required judgment and no equitable interest exists, with Rule 64 as the selected security route. Souter would not dismiss the objection by invoking necessity alone. He would distinguish a final property entitlement from provisional control of a defendant already subject to the court's jurisdiction, where the court is preserving assets for a permissible judgment and no statute expressly excludes that equitable power. The fact that Mertens enforced a statutory remedial limit does not make every historical remedial circumstance immutable. His Blue Fox join remains consistent because no sovereign waiver or claimant ownership is invented.

**Application, remedy and barriers:** The accepted findings support this injunction; no intent-to-frustrate element is required in addition to the demonstrated threat. The Court need not adopt the circuit's full general preliminary-injunction formula, its serious-questions alternative, or its characterization of all preferences. He would refuse a rule authorizing any freeze whenever a creditor predicts collection difficulty. He can join a preservation holding explicitly withholding any new lien, priority or decision about absent creditors, and reserving ordinary debts without the demonstrated danger. Affirmance determines no bond claim and neither approves nor vacates the separate final orders.

### Thomas

**Judgment:** Reverse and remand the preliminary-injunction judgment after T.

**Ground and reach:** No historical or enacted source authorizes the particular private, prejudgment freeze on these predicates. The general creditor has no judgment or proprietary equitable right; the traditional creditor's-bill requirement and De Beers's remedy relation supply the limit. His Mertens join and Peacock opinion require the particular remedy/obligation to arise from legal authority rather than a useful enforcement device. Peacock is not directly applied as ERISA law here; it supports the refusal to substitute remedial convenience for a source. His Blue Fox join likewise gives no independent substantive entitlement to a claimant who merely seeks assets sufficient to pay a debt. Rule 65 is procedural, and unavailable Rule 64 relief does not establish a separate equitable right.

**Strongest contrary path and response:** Affirm because preservation is a traditional injunction function and the record shows imminent frustration. Thomas would distinguish Deckert's equitable rescission/fund relation and First National City Bank's statutory tax power. Calling the measure an injunction does not answer whether general equity had authority over this debtor-creditor relation. The equitable remedy cannot obtain its missing source from the outcome it is meant to secure.

**Application, remedy and barriers:** Even accepting likely success, threatened depletion and the bond, the legal defect requires reversal; the lower court's alternative intent discussion is unnecessary. He will not join an invented fraudulent-transfer exception applied without a claim and its predicates, nor a ruling on foreign property solely from its location. The holding may reserve independently established liens, property remedies, enacted authority, attachment and postjudgment collection. Remand leaves any actual bond claim and proof below; no damages, new claim or dissolution of the permanent restraint is ordered.

### Ginsburg

**Judgment:** Affirm the reviewed preliminary injunction under bounded general-equity preservation after T.

**Ground and reach:** General equity permits provisional action to prevent the established judicial remedy from becoming ineffectual where the findings show imminent insolvency and depletion, without treating the underlying contract claim as itself equitable or conferring a proprietary interest. Deckert and First National City Bank support the function of preserving relief, while their distinct predicates must be acknowledged. De Beers bars disconnected preliminary security; the present restraint preserves property for the monetary recovery the action itself seeks. Her Varity join supports appropriate individual equity where the actual claim warrants it and does not make an abstract alternative remedy invariably adequate. Her Peacock and Blue Fox joins require a real obligation and respect distinct remedial limits; the contract obligation here exists independently of the injunction, and neither a sovereign waiver nor a new compensatory cause is being created.

**Strongest contrary path and response:** Reverse because the historical absence of a general creditor's prejudgment interest defines equity's boundary, and the proposed injunction displaces Rule 64 attachment conditions. Ginsburg would treat historical equity as supplying principles and remedial functions rather than an exclusive list of applications frozen before contemporary commercial transactions. Rule 64 does not expressly abolish distinct general injunctive authority; Rule 65 supplies procedure, not the substantive source. The freeze's temporary function matters, but cannot be used to decide priority among absent creditors. Blue Fox therefore limits careless entitlement language without dictating reversal in this private action.

**Application, remedy and barriers:** The district court's actual findings and procedural protections support affirmance; insolvency in the abstract and ordinary preferences do not receive blanket approval as sufficient. No new intent element is required, and the Court should not adopt the circuit's entire preliminary-injunction test when the legal-power question and accepted findings suffice. She would reject an opinion that declares the investors equitable owners, adjudicates fraudulent transfers, or treats the pari-passu covenant as a lien. The proper consequence is affirmance of the preliminary-order judgment only; no final-order or bond disposition is imported.

### Breyer

**Judgment:** Affirm the preliminary-injunction judgment on the bounded preservation ground after T.

**Ground and reach:** The general equitable power to protect the efficacy of judicial relief includes a temporary restraint under the concrete findings of imminent noncollection here. The available final judgment would otherwise be practically frustrated, and the temporary injunction operates in aid of that judgment without itself awarding the debt or changing ownership. Deckert and First National City Bank provide an analogous preservation function; De Beers limits unrelated measures, rather than foreclosing every preservation injunction when damages are sought. His Varity join supports asking whether the alternative remedy is actually adequate in the case; his Peacock and Blue Fox joins preserve source, actual entitlement and sovereign-waiver limits. Those sources do not establish a universal private-creditor bar.

**Strongest contrary path and response:** Reverse under the creditor's-bill boundary and Rule 64's architecture; the injunction may prejudice other creditors and induce routine freezes. Breyer would require the independent general-equity foundation and concrete findings, rather than treating collection difficulty as authorization. He would accept the demonstrated combination of imminent depletion and established likely success as a proper equitable application, while recognizing that a bond and discretion do not themselves create power. The historical and statutory distinctions in the cited precedents limit their direct holdings but do not foreclose the analogous traditional preservation function.

**Application, remedy and barriers:** The legal question can be resolved on the supplied findings without inventing a new numerical likelihood, fraud presumption or mandatory multi-part test. He will not join an unlimited rule for ordinary debt litigation or one making foreign location alone dispositive. Neither actual fraudulent intent nor a new lien is found; no general rule approves every creditor preference. Affirmance reaches the original preliminary restraint, leaves any different bond issues to proper proceedings, and determines nothing about the separate final injunction or turnover appeal.
