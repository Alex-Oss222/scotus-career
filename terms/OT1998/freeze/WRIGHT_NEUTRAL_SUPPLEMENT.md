## Material lower-court supplement

The official January 28, 1998 rehearing opinion was downloaded and read in full (nine pages). Its majority supplies two independent reasons for upholding denial of acceptance credit. First, it construes the sentencing judge's remarks at R.3:23 as a factual conclusion that Wright lacked remorse and did not believe his conduct wrong. Second, it states that denial would remain permissible even if based exclusively on his constitutional challenges. Both grounds require review; the first is a characterization of the sentencing remarks, not an independently established additional trial-court finding.

The rehearing dissent disputes both grounds. It reads the fuller remarks at R3:5-6 and R3:23 as rejection of counsel's constitutional theory, rather than a finding of false testimony, factual denial or lack of remorse. It distinguishes circuit decisions involving factual denials or frivolous objections from legal challenges unrelated to admitted factual guilt. The published record supports immediate possession admissions, cooperation, consent to a home search, a timely guilty plea, and an assurance that Wright possessed no weapons after arrest. The legal significance and weight of those facts remain questions for adjudication; they do not automatically entitle him to the adjustment.

The sentencing judge's quoted discussion concerned Wright's asserted militia purpose, including whether the group existed to carry out Georgia law or resist perceived threats. Under entering personal-arms law, no result may rest solely on the premise that a private possessor lacks rights without organized state-militia membership. That does not establish whether a supported independent finding of lack of acceptance remains or dictate the sentencing remedy.

## Separate counts and limits

The machinegun count includes a completed .223-caliber Olympic Arms automatic weapon and a disassembled .50-caliber machinegun. The section 5861(d) count concerns three pipe bombs. The published record supplies no machinegun-registration-impossibility defense to the destructive-device count. A guilty plea with reserved constitutional objections does not create new preserved statutory issues. Keep commerce authority, each arms application, the Ninth Amendment contention, and the sentencing adjustment distinct. No factual finding about the components of the disassembled weapon, capability of each bomb, or a government's refusal of a registration application is invented.

## Confirmed additional scope details from the original opinion

The original opinion's footnote 1 records Wright's alternative request for a Second Amendment jury instruction and rejects it solely because the court concluded he could claim no Second Amendment protection. This is a derivative request within the arms disposition, not a basis to invent a conducted jury trial following his guilty plea. If the governing arms premise changes, address the instruction request consistently with the preserved plea and lawful remand; no entitlement to a particular charge follows automatically.

Footnote 4 identifies a derivative Tenth Amendment objection to section 922(o), rejected through the lower court's Commerce Clause conclusion. It supplies no allegation of federal commandeering of state officers and no independently described state-administration claim.

Footnote 18 leaves open both the required showing of a connection to a well-regulated militia and what governmental interests could justify infringement if protected activity existed. Consequently the lower court made no alternative holding adjudicating the government's justification for restricting these particular arms under a personal-right premise. The source's disposition cannot be recharacterized as an already-performed individual arms application.

These scope details are verified from the published [original opinion](https://openjurist.org/117/f3d/1265), footnotes 1, 4 and 18. They do not restore its superseded sentencing holding or import any historical Supreme Court treatment.
