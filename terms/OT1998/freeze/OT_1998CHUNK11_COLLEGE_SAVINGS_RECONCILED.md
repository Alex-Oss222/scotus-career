## 2. Section 5: seven negative joins and Stevens's distinct affirmative ground

**Seven direct negative adopters:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The alleged misdescription of Florida Prepaid's own product, and resulting lost custom, do not establish a constitutional entitlement to truthful competitive self-advertising or prospective customers. Existing assets, goodwill and properly established legal claims can be property; the statutory tort and economic injury alone do not establish their constitutional deprivation. The negative proposition is confined to the own-product advertising theory presented, not trademark appropriation, palming off, trade secrets, statements about CSB's product or every §1122 application. It imposes no universal exclusion-right definition of constitutional property.

The seven preserve Boerne's real prevention principle: Congress may reach conduct that is not itself a constitutional violation. The absence of an individual violation is therefore not by itself enough. Their objection is that the asserted own-product-advertising entitlement supplies no identified constitutional deprivation to which this preventive remedy is fitted. They do not decide a further numerical legislative-record requirement, demand formal findings, require a violation in every State or require every statutory plaintiff to prove a constitutional violation. Paul, Roth and the precise statutory/constitutional distinction in Haddle support that confined conclusion. Sullivan preserves the separate entitlement to fair adjudication and established individual property; it supplies no categorical no-property rule.

**Stevens's independent affirmative position:** An existing going business, its economic activity and goodwill are property; deliberate State destruction of that business can be a deprivation. Congress may protect against that category of constitutional injury through the TRCA's defined unfair-competition remedy without requiring the pleaded advertisement itself to be a completed constitutional deprivation. He finds a reasonable preventive relationship between protection of that existing business interest and the stated private remedy, with the presumption of legislative validity; the distinction between prevention and constitutional redefinition remains. This is his application of the governing congruence/proportionality standard, not a new general rational-basis replacement for it. He would sustain the challenged application and reverse without finding the allegations true or awarding a remedy.

**Correction of unsupported departure:** Stevens's historical solo dissent, 527 U.S. 691–693, expressly adopted those property and category-prevention premises and independently upheld §5. The provisional row instead rejected §5 by citing his Boerne join, Haddle, and Sullivan. His full Boerne join was also part of the historical background; the supplied simulated Boerne expressly protects category prevention and disclaims an individual-violation prerequisite. Haddle still distinguishes statutory economic harm from constitutional property and does not deny the going-business/goodwill proposition. His simulated Sullivan writing affirmatively treats the substantive claim as property and declines the Court's entitlement rationale; it supplies no new rejection of his historical business-property premise. No changed advertisement, claim, statute, remedy or factual record is established. These sources therefore do not justify his provisional departure. His affirmative §5 ground is restored in reconciliation; the independent source freeze is not rewritten.

For Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, the historical record establishes neither joining Stevens's §5 ground nor adopting Scalia's negative ground. Their present explicit rejection is retained as an independently supported commitment, with that historical limit disclosed. A dissenting judgment does not imply disagreement with every proposition of the majority. Conversely, these seven present negative joins must never be described as eight, as historical unanimity, or as seven votes to affirm.

## 3. Prospective commercial condition, actual consent and Parden scope

**Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join:** When Congress unmistakably and prospectively makes specified State commercial conduct subject to private federal-court enforcement, a State continuing a commercial undertaking it can realistically choose not to operate accepts the stated federal-forum condition for violations arising from covered post-enactment activity. Here the undertaking is competitive marketing of investment products, an optional method of pursuing education, rather than education itself. The relevant choice is operating that undertaking under known terms, not electing to violate federal law. No federal subsidy, signed State promise, profit figure, exit-cost finding or invented withdrawal event is presumed.

Their strongest objection is the difference between Congress imposing liability and the State consenting. They answer at this confined scope through express advance notice, continued covered activity and meaningful practical choice. The original Parden inference from general statutory coverage remains displaced by Welch and the actual simulated Hilton holding. The later explicit text and prospective commercial choice supply the present distinction. Breyer's full historical dissent, joined by the other three, confirms the importance of real withdrawal and equality of enforcement; it does not license waiver from all regulated activity. The historical dissent's broader characterization that original Parden survived cannot rewrite the simulated Hilton boundary. Hess does not eliminate the admitted arm-of-State status. Lane, Cedar Rapids and West are limited analogies about clear obligations and remedies, not existing holdings of State commercial consent.

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join:** An unmistakable congressional statement that a State shall be liable establishes legislative intent, not the State's unequivocal decision to submit to federal jurisdiction. Continuing otherwise lawful commerce, even realistically optional commerce, is not that submission. A threat to exclude the State from that activity is not equivalent to accepting a specifically offered federal benefit or voluntarily invoking a court. The four reject the residual Parden commercial-waiver theory needed here without adopting the Third Circuit's government-function classification. They preserve genuinely voluntary waiver and distinguish properly conditioned grants and interstate compacts, including the possibility that a funding condition can become coercive. They do not decide a particular funding condition.

Their historical consent ground remains independently supported by Great Northern, Beers, Atascadero and the public simulated Hilton/Seminole distinctions even though historical Seminole's anti-circumvention rationale cannot be cited as governing law. This reconciliation does not silently overrule Union Gas or declare Parden's retained substantive FELA construction extinct. Any statement about Parden's present treatment must expressly preserve Hilton's state-court coverage and reliance holding; the actual negative commitment reaches imputed federal-forum consent from ordinary commercial participation. No additional sweeping overruling proposition is needed or authorized by these joins.

**Actual consent and litigation scope:** No Associate here rests judgment on express State consent, acceptance of a federal grant, removal, a live State counterclaim or reciprocal consent. The unappealed dismissed counterclaim and appearance do not establish an adjudicated litigation waiver. That distinct lower issue is outside the verified questions and is not reached. The displaced historical explanation for delayed immunity assertion does not itself enlarge the grant or decide waiver. Schacht reserves its different removal-waiver question.
