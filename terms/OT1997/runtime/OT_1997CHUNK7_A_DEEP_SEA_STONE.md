## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: State immunity in an in rem admiralty action

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves sovereign immunity before ownership. He reaches no final conclusion concerning abandonment, statutory coverage, title, or salvage.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Federal arrest of a res outside a State’s possession neither compels the State nor seizes state-held property. A State’s assertion of title therefore does not defeat in rem jurisdiction, but it also does not establish the factual and statutory predicates of ownership.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Eleventh Amendment does not bar federal in rem admiralty jurisdiction over property outside a State’s possession merely because the State asserts title. Abandonment, statutory coverage, ownership, and salvage remain for source-specific adjudication.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The wreck was not in California’s possession when the federal court took jurisdiction. The lower courts nevertheless moved too quickly from jurisdiction to merits conclusions about the Abandoned Shipwreck Act and title on an incomplete record, requiring vacatur and remand.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not authorize seizure of property actually possessed by a State, personal or treasury relief against California, or a determination that California lacks title.
