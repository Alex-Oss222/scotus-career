## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Punishment classification of the OCC sanctions

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the punishment-classification question because it is necessary to the Double Jeopardy claim.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Ursery and Hendricks without alteration. Congress designated the money penalties as civil, and occupational debarment is administered by banking regulators to protect the integrity of a regulated industry. That designation is not conclusive, but the petitioners must provide the clearest proof that the sanctions’ combined design and actual operation are so punitive as to override it. Deterrence, severity, and connection to criminal conduct are relevant but insufficient by themselves.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The OCC monetary assessments and occupational debarment are civil on this record because their combined design and operation do not supply the clearest proof of criminal punishment. The later criminal prosecution therefore is not barred on a multiple-punishment theory.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The sanctions were imposed through civil banking authority and serve conventional regulatory functions: monetary accountability for unsafe banking conduct and exclusion from positions of trust in the banking system. Unlike the extraordinary tax in Kurth Ranch, they are not structured as a post-conviction exaction whose defining operation is a second punishment. Unlike the application invalidated in simulated Hendricks, the record does not show prisonlike custody or a failed civil treatment-and-release structure whose operation defeats the statutory label. The record therefore does not satisfy Ursery’s demanding override requirement.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not classify every banking sanction as civil, preclude a different result on materially different design or operational facts, resolve an Excessive Fines claim, or immunize a punitive measure merely because Congress labels it civil.

### Issue 2: Same-offense and successive-prosecution question

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone does not reach whether the OCC predicates and criminal charges constitute the same offense because Issue 1 establishes that the OCC measures were not criminal punishment. A Blockburger ruling would therefore be unnecessary.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** No separate holding.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Double Jeopardy’s same-offense inquiry becomes necessary only after the first proceeding qualifies as criminal punishment. That predicate fails here, so the Court stops.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The same-offense relationship among the administrative predicates and charged crimes remains unresolved.
