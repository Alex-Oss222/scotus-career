## Distinct questions and procedural ground

The Ninth Circuit did not issue a freestanding innocence writ. It rejected that claim, found the Schlup gateway satisfied, found material Brady suppression and ordered a new-trial writ. Each step retains its own burden and predicates. The District Court had rejected this petition as abusive and denied the claims; the petition was filed in August 1995. The en banc court left mitigation, trial/sentencing competence and resentencing-counsel claims unaddressed. A disposition of the gateway or the particular Brady claim cannot silently decide those claims or their individual procedural posture.

**Temporal provisions:** Lindh's existing holding is expressly limited to the ordinary chapter 153 amendments in a pre-enactment noncapital application. Five Associates who joined that decision—Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—would apply its chapter-specific reasoning to this pre-enactment application under ordinary chapter 153 rather than infer that capital status alone activates chapter 154. Section 107(c) makes chapter 154 applicable to pending cases only within that chapter's qualifying conditions; no Arizona qualification predicate is established in the present source. This would be an express application of Lindh's statutory rationale to the capital posture, not a quotation of an already decided universal rule. Its scope is the ordinary adjudication/deference provisions actually at issue. It does not exempt all pending proceedings from every AEDPA provision.

The section 106 authorization requirement addresses filing a later application. The August 1995 application was already filed and adjudicated by the District Court before enactment; the supplied matter is not a post-enactment refusal of advance authorization. Felker and Triestman therefore do not bar ordinary Supreme Court review of this appellate writ judgment. No independent original-writ route is needed. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain the specific present-authority objection expressed in their Lindh dissent but can resolve the actual writ under the more favorable former-law gateway and deference rules without deciding the additional temporal question. This is not a demand for retroactive authorization or a finding of chapter 154 eligibility.

Former §2254(d)(8) requires attention to the state finding and whether the pertinent record as a whole fairly supports it. The state judge heard the 1987 confession and recantation, although he was not the 1978 trial judge. Absence from the original trial does not erase the later judge's factfinding role. A permissible contrary inference is not automatically a statutory exception. Conversely, the statutory record-as-a-whole qualification remains genuine, and later reliable evidence is not automatically excluded from Schlup's independent whole-record probability inquiry.

## Gateway: two supported paths, with the whole record retained

**Path supporting the gateway:** Dunbar's confession was detailed, given without immunity and accompanied by exposure to capital prosecution. Its store and body-position details, other reports of his admissions and framing boasts, the evidence of his methods of shifting blame and disposing of evidence, the false alibi and the collapse of his claim that counsel supplied his knowledge offer meaningful support. The changed record can alter the apparent independence of physical corroboration. His later recantation was accompanied by a reason to avoid prosecution and explanations contradicted by counsel. New impeachment and prisoner accounts cannot be excluded categorically under the current Schlup rule.

**Strongest objection:** Dunbar's participation does not negate Carriger's participation in felony murder. Joyce's later account implicated both men; Larry understood Carriger to have participated; Patience recounted Dunbar describing the victim pleading with Carriger; Stephanie placed Joyce at home. Dunbar's accusation of Joyce is therefore a material conflict, not a harmless detail. Planning-like conduct, handling the cases and stolen property, the key, boots and tape print remain inculpatory. The later expert offered a 21.5-inch print location; the offer was not trial evidence, but trial admissibility does not exclude it from the gateway. The dissent explains that its limited status followed withdrawal of the defense challenge and a relevance ruling, not a judicial finding that the science was unreliable. The majority's inability-to-match-ends and lack-of-independent-testing objections also remain in the record. No expert finding or guilt fact is supplied here.

**Path defeating the gateway:** The state credibility finding has record support, the inculpatory accomplice evidence survives even if Dunbar's trial testimony is discarded, and the partial physical corroboration permits a reasonable juror to find Carriger participated. Ordinary doubt about the actual verdict therefore does not meet Schlup's more-likely-than-not no-reasonable-properly-instructed-juror standard. The principal contrary argument is that treating each surviving item as independently sufficient would fail to consider how the reliable framing evidence changes its apparent provenance and significance. A gateway rejection must confront that cumulative change; it cannot require affirmative conclusive innocence, DNA, an alibi or a categorical ban on recantations.

**Remand path:** The en banc majority states the exact demanding formula in its principal text, but its footnote substitutes confidence in the actual verdict for the reasonable-juror inquiry, and its treatment of the accomplice record denies evidence the dissent specifically identifies. The problem is the application as a whole, not an isolated infelicitous sentence. A fresh application can require consideration of both exculpatory and inculpatory evidence, the actual significance and reliability of the tape offer, and the statutory treatment of the particular state findings. This is identified legal work, not a direction to hold an innocence hearing merely because the case is troubling. No new testimony is automatic, and no finding establishes the truth or falsity of Dunbar's competing accounts.

## Brady: favorability and materiality do not establish attribution

### What the file adds

The file's violence, armed offenses, repeated blame-shifting and systematic false accounts differ materially from the six burglary convictions and false gun-purchase form known at trial. The prosecution presented Dunbar as a nonviolent, truthful burglar and relied heavily on him. The undisclosed contents have combined potential uses for contradiction, cross-examination, investigation, defense preparation and assessment of the evidence's provenance. Those uses must be considered with the entire remaining case under Kyles and Bagley. Materiality neither requires innocence nor disappears because the prosecution has legally sufficient evidence. Once material suppression is established, no second duplicative harmlessness screen remains.

### What the present source does not establish

The lower majority calls the individual prosecutors' possession of the corrections file inconclusive. DOC's possession alone is not the current Kyles rule. No conclusive finding identifies police acting for this prosecution as knowing the materially different file contents. A detective's knowledge that Dunbar was an informant cannot be treated as suppression of an unknown fact without addressing Carriger's April 18, 1978 letter showing particular knowledge of his earlier informant role and anticipating retrieval of the prison file. Knowledge of a file's existence is not knowledge of every material item, but that distinction does not itself put the file on the prosecution team.

**No independently sufficient existing-Kyles ground is established in this neutral record.** The materially different DOC information is the core of the identified Brady theory. Existing information independently known to the defense cannot be silently substituted as sufficient suppression; inconclusive prosecutor possession cannot be silently resolved in either direction. A new-trial writ on this theory therefore requires either (a) supported findings tying material favorable contents to actual prosecution/case-team responsibility, or (b) an expressly justified legal extension imposing a duty to obtain and review this file despite the absence of that established tie. The latter is not merely Kyles applied and must receive its own compatibility and applicable habeas treatment. This analysis adopts neither an all-government knowledge rule nor categorical immunity for unknown information in another agency.

### Competing legal paths on the duty

**Bounded acquisition-duty path:** Brady/Giglio's protection of a fair trial, Kyles's rejection of evasion through personal ignorance, the government's choice to use a central immunized career-criminal witness, the identifiable existing prison file and the importance of predictable impeachment supply the strongest argument for a specific duty to obtain that file before relying on the witness. The proposed duty is narrower than an obligation to search every agency. Its strongest objection is that Kyles's actual case-team rule does not make potential access equivalent to responsibility, and it could shift general witness investigation to the prosecutor without an established statutory or constitutional limit. A constitutional extension cannot avoid the separately applicable rules governing a later habeas claim merely by calling itself clarification. No unasserted retroactivity defense, waiver or exception is found here.

**Existing-case-team path:** Apply Kyles to evidence actually within the case team's responsibility, determine what relevant contents were known there, and separately determine what the defense independently knew. If neither team responsibility nor an independently adopted acquisition duty is established, DOC possession and materiality alone do not prove suppression attributable to the prosecution. Its strongest objection is that requiring the prisoner to prove actual personal awareness would defeat Kyles. The answer is organizational case-team responsibility, not personal-prosecutor knowledge; a factual inquiry into that responsibility cannot be replaced with a presumption that every state repository belongs to the team.

**Provisional selected scope:** No Associate commitment here supports silently adopting the lower majority's acquisition extension. The five remand-supporting Associates below reserve that extension because the current writ must first be set aside for the identified gateway defects, and the record can be addressed under the actual attribution rule. Reservation is not a vote against every possible bounded duty or a vote for its eventual adoption. The three gateway-rejection Associates would not reach a new acquisition rule after their sufficient gateway disposition. They reject automatic constructive possession and find no already established alternative Brady ground. No record-wide finding that the prosecution committed no Brady violation is made by this analysis.

## Provisional remedy boundaries

Five Associates currently favor vacating the new-trial writ direction and remanding for a correct, bounded assessment. Three favor reversal on the gateway's failure on the present submission, rather than reopening that gateway simply to apply a different wording. All eight reject treating the existing appellate direction as an acquittal or as a freestanding-innocence writ. No fixed retrial deadline, unconditional release, immediate execution, new execution date, automatic evidentiary hearing or automatic stay follows from either path.

The common legal propositions include the separation of the standards; the need to retain inculpatory as well as exculpatory evidence; the actual former-law state-finding qualification; no categorical rejection of prisoner or impeachment evidence; no automatic all-agency possession; and no second duplicative harmlessness test after an established material Brady violation. The remedy agreement extends only to setting aside the present writ direction, not to agreement about whether the gateway has conclusively failed. The freestanding claim need not be reopened to adjudicate the State's challenge to the actual Brady writ. No general federal innocence threshold is supplied by the current Herrera holding.
