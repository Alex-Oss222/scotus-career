## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Constitutional taxation — nonresidents and alimony

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** May New York deny nonresidents an alimony deduction that it allows residents when calculating tax on income earned in New York?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Article IV, §2 Privileges and Immunities Clause; substantial justification and the relationship between the discrimination and that justification.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** REVERSE and REMAND for nondiscriminatory treatment and appropriate tax relief.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would require a concrete account of why nonresidence justifies categorical denial of this deduction. The narrower nonresident tax base does not by itself establish that a personal expense recognized for residents becomes irrelevant whenever the payer lives elsewhere. The State may use a properly supported allocation rule to avoid deducting expenses against income it does not tax, but complete denial must have a substantial justification linked to the disparity. This record, as reconstructed, supplies no such sufficient fit. The proposed result addresses the actual deduction scheme rather than requiring identical tax codes for residents and nonresidents in all respects.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Fulton and Oregon Waste concern interstate commerce, not the identical constitutional clause, but reject using a different tax burden as a conclusory justification for discrimination. Here the State must explain the actual treatment of resident and nonresident income and deductions. Reich governs any withdrawal of a clearly offered refund remedy after payment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Commerce Clause cases are analogies; they do not replace the Article IV test.
