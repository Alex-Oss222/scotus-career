## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Meaning of willful and malicious injury

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only whether negligent or reckless malpractice satisfies the statutory willfulness requirement. He reserves substantial certainty and the independent meaning of “malicious.”
- **Stone's Legal Position:** “Willful” modifies “injury.” The creditor therefore must establish a deliberate or intentional injury, not merely a voluntary act that negligently or recklessly produces harm.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A debt arising from negligently or recklessly inflicted injury does not satisfy § 523(a)(6)’s willfulness requirement. This malpractice debt is dischargeable under that provision.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The judgment establishes malpractice rather than injury-directed intent. Reading willful to describe only the underlying treatment decision would collapse the statutory distinction between intentional injury and negligent or reckless harm.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not decide subjective knowledge of substantial certainty, define “malicious,” discharge a debt based on proved injury-directed intent, or foreclose another independently pleaded discharge exception.
