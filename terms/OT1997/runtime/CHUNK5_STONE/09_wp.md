## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Civil classification and process for community notification

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches both the civil-punitive classification and the required process.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Treat the scheme as civil in primary purpose and structure, while requiring strong predeprivation safeguards for broad individualized notification. The State carries clear and convincing persuasion because an erroneous high-tier classification produces severe, difficult-to-reverse consequences and turns on contested predictive facts.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** New Jersey’s notification scheme is civil rather than punishment, but Tier Two and Tier Three notification requires a pre-notification hearing at which the State proves the factual basis and risk classification by clear and convincing evidence.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Registration and notification seek public safety and do not automatically constitute a second criminal sentence. Yet broad disclosure can irreversibly alter housing, work, safety, and status. The State controls the evidence and proposes the classification, while a prompt adversarial hearing can materially reduce error without defeating the program.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not decide every internet registry, lifetime classification without review, confidential law-enforcement registration, or a scheme whose operation is affirmatively punitive.
