* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** GRANT Glendora’s present motion for leave to proceed in forma pauperis (permission to file without prepaying the docketing fee); DECLINE the separate prospective fee-and-form restriction on future noncriminal certiorari petitions. This is a fee-access disposition, not a grant of certiorari.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone grants Glendora’s present fee-waiver motion and separately declines the future noncriminal filing restriction. The alleged false service, judicial acceptance of it, and federal dismissal do not themselves establish state action by private counsel, conspiracy, or an available collateral challenge to the state judgment. Failure to confront the dismissal grounds is a serious obstacle to certiorari, and Martin preserves discretion to deny financial leave on a supported finding of particular frivolousness. Stone nevertheless maintains his recorded personal position favoring acceptance for ordinary petition screening; he does not convert his disagreement with Martin’s companion fee denial into binding Court law. The fee grant neither excuses a jurisdictional defect nor revives the Second Circuit appeal. The history of fourteen petitions supports scrutiny but supplies no independent showing that individualized treatment of future petitions is inadequate. He therefore rejects the prospective restriction without disabling supported sanctions or targeted controls in a different proceeding.

* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Glendora’s failure to address the district court’s dismissal grounds and the Second Circuit’s frivolousness determination are relevant to a particular-submission assessment under Martin and Zatko. Fourteen prior denials cannot substitute for that assessment. Her false-service and judicial-complicity allegations do not establish state action, conspiracy, or a right to reopen the underlying judgment. Demos and Anderson distinguish an arguable access allegation from success on an underlying claim. The separate proposed future restriction must satisfy Martin, Day, and Sassower on its own record; present deficiencies do not establish the content of unidentified future petitions.
