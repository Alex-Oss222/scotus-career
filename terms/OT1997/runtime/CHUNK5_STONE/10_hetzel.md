## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Civil procedure — remittitur and jury trial

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** May a federal appellate mandate reduce a jury’s damages award without permitting the plaintiff to choose a new trial?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Seventh Amendment; Rule 59 remittitur; distinguish excessiveness review from judicial substitution of a damages verdict.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** REVERSE the mandate insofar as it forecloses the new-trial option, and REMAND.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would enforce the jury-trial boundary already stated in Gasperini. A court may find an award excessive and condition retention of judgment on acceptance of a lawful reduction. It may not convert that condition into a forced replacement verdict. Protecting the choice does not restore an excessive award as a matter of right; it requires a procedurally lawful way to correct it. The scope of a new trial, including whether damages can fairly be separated from liability, must follow the actual record.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Revised Gasperini expressly preserves the plaintiff’s choice between remittitur and a new jury trial and disallows appellate first-instance substitution of value. BMW’s review of punitive excessiveness does not eliminate that protection for this compensatory verdict.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The original award is not thereby held reasonable; damages-only retrial depends on separability.
