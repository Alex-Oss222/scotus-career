### Issue 1: Justiciability of negligence claims arising from live military exercises

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves only the categorical political-question dismissal. He does not decide the merits or the justiciability of any specifically identified high-level training-policy challenge.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Gilligan rejected continuing judicial supervision of military training, not every retrospective claim arising from a completed military accident. Political-question analysis is claim specific and bars only a claim whose resolution actually requires selection of constitutionally committed military policy or supplies no judicially manageable standard.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A military-training accident is not categorically nonjusticiable. The political-question doctrine bars only claims whose resolution actually requires selection of constitutionally committed military policy or for which no judicially manageable standard exists.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The lower court treated military context as categorical immunity and did not separate ordinary operational allegations from claims that might require the court to design training, command, or weapons policy. A claim-specific remand is therefore required.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not decide negligence, causation, damages, statutory coverage, sovereign immunity, Feres, or the justiciability of any specifically identified high-level training-policy challenge.
