## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Criminal law — Assimilative Crimes Act

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** May the Assimilative Crimes Act import Louisiana’s first-degree-murder offense when federal murder law already punishes the killing?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** 18 U.S.C. §§13 and 1111; identify the conduct punished by federal law and whether assimilation would fill a gap or interfere with Congress’s offense scheme.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** REVERSE the assimilation ruling and REMAND for proceedings under the proper federal offense.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would read the Assimilative Crimes Act as a gap-filling statute. The existence of a more particular state label does not establish a federal gap if Congress already addressed this killing through its graded murder provisions. Assimilation that displaces that grading changes Congress’s chosen criminal scheme. At the same time, the mere existence of some federal criminal law cannot categorically foreclose assimilation of genuinely different conduct. The correct comparison is concrete: what acts and distinctions do the two laws regulate, and would adding the state offense complement or supplant federal law?
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Wells requires enforcing the enacted federal offense rather than supplying an additional element or route by preference. Here the inquiry is statutory occupation of the homicide field at issue, not whether state law better describes the victim’s age. Gaudin preserves jury determination of any elements that remain material after identifying the proper offense.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No acquittal of all homicide liability is implied. Verify the actual verdict before selecting the replacement judgment.
