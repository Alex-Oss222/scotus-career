## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Hobbs Act application to local commercial robbery

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The constitutional claim is preserved and presents a clean application of the post-*Lopez* Commerce framework. Stone reaches only the granted Commerce Clause challenge to the Hobbs Act convictions; the vulnerable-victim sentencing issue is outside the grant.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress may apply the Hobbs Act to robbery that directly depletes the operating assets of a business participating in interstate commerce when the statute requires proof of an actual effect on commerce.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Lopez* without changing it. Stone clarifies, rather than changes, the architecture: Robinson is the first case in which he makes explicit how aggregation operates after a genuine economic-market connection has been established. The robbery is directed at an operating commercial business and directly depletes its commercial assets, and the Hobbs Act contains an enacted commerce requirement. Stone therefore does not need the generalized proposition that crime costs the national economy money; the relevant commercial mechanism exists before aggregation begins.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may regulate robbery that directly depletes the operating assets of an active commercial business participating in interstate commerce when the Hobbs Act’s commerce element is actually proved. The effect of one robbery may be slight because the regulated class is economic and directly operates on commercial assets; aggregation measures magnitude only after that commercial connection exists.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory class is robbery affecting commerce, not robbery in the abstract. The victim business is an operating commercial enterprise. The taking directly removes assets from that enterprise and therefore acts on the business’s commercial capacity rather than merely producing generalized social costs. This supplies the connection *Lopez* requires before substantiality and aggregation. *Wickard* and *Perez* therefore remain available for class-wide magnitude. The Government still must prove the Hobbs Act’s enacted commerce element in the particular prosecution. Stone rejects any theory that all local robbery is federally regulable merely because crime has aggregate economic consequences. He also rejects treating the business label alone as sufficient if the record fails to establish the enacted commerce effect. On the supplied record, Robinson committed a series of robberies of operating commercial establishments, the robberies directly depleted business assets, and the jury necessarily found that the resulting disruption of businesses engaged in interstate commerce was an effect on interstate commerce.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide robbery of a purely personal victim, robbery of property with only remote historical interstate movement, de minimis statutory construction questions outside the constitutional issue, or whether Congress could omit the Hobbs Act’s enacted commerce connection entirely.
