## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: First sale and reimported domestic copies

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory question only for copies made in the United States, sold abroad with the copyright owner’s authorization, and later reimported by their lawful owner.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 602(a) treats unauthorized importation as infringement of the § 106(3) distribution right; § 106 makes that right subject to § 109(a). An authorized first sale therefore exhausts the distribution right, and territorial-pricing policy cannot recreate it.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 602(a) does not revive an exhausted § 106(3) distribution right when a copy made in the United States is sold abroad with the copyright owner’s authorization and later reimported by its lawful owner.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory provisions operate together. Once L’anza authorized the foreign sale of these domestically made copies, § 109(a) permitted the owner to dispose of them. Their return across the border did not restore a distribution right that the first sale exhausted.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not decide foreign-manufactured copies, unauthorized reproductions, counterfeits, reproduction rights, trademark confusion, labeling or consumer-protection law, or contractual duties enforceable against a contracting party.
