## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Statutory and constitutional no-knock entry with property damage

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory § 3109 ground and the Fourth Amendment question. He does not decide the remedy for materially different destruction.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 3109 incorporates the traditional exigency exceptions. Under Richards, the suspected presence of a violent fugitive with access to weapons supplied case-specific reasonable suspicion. Property damage does not increase that entry threshold, but its nature and extent remain part of overall execution reasonableness.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Property damage does not increase the reasonable-suspicion showing required for a no-knock entry, although it remains subject to overall Fourth Amendment reasonableness. Section 3109 incorporates the traditional exigency exceptions, which were satisfied here.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reliable information placed a violent escaped prisoner with access to firearms in Ramirez’s home. Breaking one garage window to prevent access to the reported weapons was proportionate to that danger on this record. The single act of damage therefore did not make the otherwise justified entry unreasonable.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not authorize gratuitous, punitive, disproportionate, or objective-unrelated destruction, validate an entry lacking case-specific suspicion, or decide the remedy for materially different destruction.
