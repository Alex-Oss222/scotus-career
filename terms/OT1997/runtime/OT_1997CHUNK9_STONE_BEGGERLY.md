### Issue 1: Federal courts — independent equitable action and land title

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Do later-discovered land-title materials justify an independent action reopening the parties’ earlier settlement, and can the Quiet Title Act limitation be avoided?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Rule 60’s preserved independent action; extraordinary equitable relief to prevent a grave miscarriage of justice; Quiet Title Act limitation and notice provisions.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** REVERSE, preserving the latest supplied Stone position.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone’s supplied position recognizes the independent action but refuses to make it a substitute for ordinary litigation diligence or an unlimited discovery remedy. The later archive discovery may be important to the parties without proving that leaving the settlement intact causes a grave miscarriage of justice. The Government’s allegedly incomplete search must be assessed on the actual duty and conduct; it is not automatically the kind of fraud that defeats finality. The Quiet Title Act question remains a separate statutory limit, not something displaced by invoking equity. This reasoning is fully compatible with the simulation’s broader respect for Congress’s express reinstatement authority.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Revised Morgan Stanley and Plaut uphold a specifically bounded congressional reinstatement rule. They do not authorize courts to reopen any final settlement on a broad fairness balance. Kokkonen separately distinguishes enforcement of a settlement from jurisdiction over an independent dispute. The action here must satisfy its own narrow equitable and statutory route.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No absolute constitutional prohibition on legislative reopening is imported from historical Plaut; no general archive-search immunity is created.
