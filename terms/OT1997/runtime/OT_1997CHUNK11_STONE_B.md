* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment on the pleadings.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The notice-based claims remain dismissed. Any independently enforceable promise or claim not dependent on treating AOL as publisher of third-party information remains governed by its own elements.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Notice-based distributor liability under § 230(c)(1)

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory classification of the pleaded duties.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Notice changes the fault standard historically applicable to a distributor but not the legal character of distributor liability as participation in publication. Duties to remove, retract, investigate, or screen third-party material therefore treat the service as publisher within § 230(c)(1).
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Notice-based distributor liability treats an interactive computer service as the publisher of information supplied by another content provider and is barred by § 230(c)(1).
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Every duty Zeran alleged concerned AOL’s editorial treatment of information supplied by another content provider. Notice did not make AOL the information’s creator or materially develop its content.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not immunize the original author, information AOL created or materially developed, conduct outside the statutory definitions, or an independently enforceable promise not dependent on publisher status.

### Issue 2: Temporal reach of § 230

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches temporal operation because the provider conduct preceded enactment while the complaint and potential liability followed it.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Then-§ 230(d)(3)’s commands that no inconsistent cause “may be brought” and no liability “may be imposed” clearly govern post-effective-date actions and judgments. Hughes Aircraft is distinguishable because its amendment contained no comparable temporal language.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Then-§ 230(d)(3) governs actions brought and liabilities imposed after its effective date, including claims based on earlier provider conduct.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Landgraf step one controls because Congress directly addressed when inconsistent causes could be brought and liabilities imposed. Zeran filed after the effective date and sought a later judgment, so applying § 230 follows the enacted temporal command rather than attaching an inferred new consequence.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** AOL’s recordkeeping practices remain unresolved, as do claims outside the statutory definitions and exceptions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Criminal law — restored rights and firearm restrictions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** When state law restores civil rights but retains a restriction on some firearm possession, does §921(a)(20) preserve the federal consequences of the conviction for all firearms?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** 18 U.S.C. §§921(a)(20), 922(g), and the charged sentencing provisions; identify both the restoration and surviving state-law restriction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AFFIRM the federal effect of the partially restricted restoration under the statute as presented.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would read the restoration clause as determining the status of the prior conviction for federal law rather than importing a shifting weapon-by-weapon state license. When the State expressly retains a firearm disability, Congress’s unless clause prevents the conviction from being disregarded. The federal prohibition then supplies its own scope. This interpretation must rest on the actual state law at the relevant time, not a vague claim that Caron remained dangerous. Love remains governing constitutional law for a properly presented arms challenge, but does not authorize rewriting the statutory exception in this case.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Love recognizes a personal and incorporated arms right but does not decide every convicted-person restriction. It cannot automatically invalidate or validate the statute here. The presented question is how Congress used state restoration to classify a prior conviction; a separate constitutional challenge requires its own preserved basis.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Verify the restoration mechanism and exact remaining prohibition. No general holding that every firearms disability is constitutional.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Recipient fault for Title IX damages

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the governing recipient-fault rule and the sufficiency of the record to require further proceedings. He does not find liability.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Title IX does not impose respondeat superior for every employee wrong. A recipient may nevertheless be liable when school-conferred authority materially enables discrimination and the recipient’s own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causes the discrimination to continue. Actual notice to an official with corrective authority remains sufficient but is not exclusive.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title IX does not impose respondeat superior for every employee wrong. A recipient may nevertheless be liable when school-conferred authority materially enables discrimination and the recipient’s own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causes that discrimination to continue; actual notice to an official with corrective authority is sufficient but not exclusive.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The complaint to the principal, internal warning, and alleged absence of a functioning grievance route create a factual dispute about the recipient’s own fault. They do not establish liability, but they prevent affirmance under a rule that allows the district to defeat attribution through its own failure to transmit substantial warning information.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Employment alone, ordinary negligence, or a technical regulatory violation alone is insufficient. This position does not establish discrimination, institutional fault, causation, or damages, and it leaves ordinary agency liability, official-policy cases, prospective enforcement, individual liability, and state-law remedies unresolved.
