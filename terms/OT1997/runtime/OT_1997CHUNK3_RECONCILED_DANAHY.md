## Controlling-law application and the strongest alternative

**Legal ground accepted by all eight.** Elrod–Branti, refined before the conduct and restated in Rutan, protects public employees from impermissible affiliation conditions, including nonreappointment. The employer's constitutional justification turns on whether affiliation is appropriate for effective performance of the particular work. Harlow and Anderson retain a separate personal damages defense where the particular unlawfulness was not apparent under the pre-existing law. Elder requires all relevant conduct-time authorities. Lanier and the actual Smith decision confirm that obvious coverage requires no factual twin, while an abstract right does not resolve an uncertain application. No mandatory merits-first sequence requires a final Branti holding before this defense can be sustained.

The source gives an affirmative common function: direct and close work with the special assistant attorneys general on confidential investigations, auditing and case preparation relating to the exercise of prosecutorial discretion (¶¶42, 46–47). Its importance is the relationship to the public prosecutor's actual decision process, not the bare fact that information was private. The pre-1995 Branti distinction, Regan's inherent-function approach, Vezzetti's expressly nonexclusive indicators, Savage's treatment of particular confidential offices and the contemporary prosecutor authorities allowed reasonable error about that relationship. They did not establish that every confidential employee could be removed for politics. Their availability is described and quoted in the primary lower opinion; no unverified detailed holding from an unrecovered earlier case is added.

Reasonable error on these known functions is sufficient even accepting patronage motive and nonpolicymaker status. There is no need to prove that every employee actually exercised prosecutorial authority or that shared party beliefs in fact improved the work. Such a merits conclusion is not established. Conversely, an official cannot manufacture uncertainty merely by naming an exception. The affirmative duties and direct relationship, evaluated against the substantive rule, do the work here.

**Partial remand is the strongest competing path.** Henesey, Matthews and Angiletta have no identified full descriptions, and their concrete work can be understood as technical collection of reliable information rather than a role requiring partisan loyalty. Branti itself protected employees in a law-enforcement office and rejected ordinary trust as enough; its deputy-prosecutor reservation does not answer support-staff cases. A lawful partial disposition could affirm the sufficiently supported components while vacating the others for functional application on proper assumptions, without finding liability, compelling a trial or ordering document production.

The reason to reject that path here is substantive, not the panel's preference to dispose of all claims at once. The source already supplies the direct confidential prosecutorial relationship for each of the three; Henesey's additional activities make a merely clerical account inaccurate. The required immunity conclusion is reasonable uncertainty about the constitutional significance of that relationship in 1995, not final proof that party affiliation was appropriate. The administrative/statutory-description divide does not itself establish a corresponding difference in that relationship. It would be equally unsound to presume a fact favorable to officials from a missing description or presume the opposite fact solely to secure a remand. On the supported common assumptions, each Associate accepts the legal defense for the three closer roles as well as the senior roles, for the Justice-specific reasons below.

**Other routes.** Vacatur of all seven components on Branti's obvious-coverage theory is lawful if the supported ground amounts only to ordinary confidentiality or generalized employer loyalty; the reconciled ground expressly requires more and cannot be converted into that theory. Dismissal of the entire interlocutory appeal is inappropriate because the legal notice question accepts the adverse assumptions; an actually identified factual-sufficiency challenge would remain unavailable. A final merits judgment approving all seven dismissals is unsupported by the limited functional record and unnecessary to this independently dispositive defense. No description-focused post-Gordon duty, guessed missing evidence or new procedural checklist supplies an alternative disposition.
