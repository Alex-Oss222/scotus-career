## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE the Commerce Clause determinations against both the flow-control requirement and the diversion fee and REMAND for the lower courts to apply the public-facility rule. Stone writes the Opinion of the Court adopting that rule: routing locally generated waste to a genuinely public facility does not facially discriminate against interstate commerce when local private processors and out-of-state processors are treated alike; whether a facility is genuinely public turns on its actual ownership, control, financing, public-service responsibility and revenues and on the absence of concealed private favoritism, and public title alone does not settle the question; a diversion fee survives only insofar as its incidence and amount are genuinely tied to diversion-related costs rather than interstate origin. Stone joins the Justices who would remand so that the Court has a complete judgment. He writes separately that he would reverse outright: routing locally generated waste to the actual public facility does not facially discriminate merely by excluding both local and out-of-state private alternatives, and a diversion charge is valid if its incidence is nondiscriminatory and it genuinely corresponds to diversion-related costs; public ownership cannot disguise private favoritism, and a protectionist or origin-based fee remains invalid.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Neither measure is held valid or invalid by this Court; the reviewed injunction determinations are vacated for renewed adjudication of both Commerce Clause claims. The takings and ripeness rulings outside the grant are not disturbed, and no takings question is decided.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Municipal flow control and diversion fee

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches both granted Commerce Clause questions and no takings issue.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A requirement directing locally generated waste to a municipally owned facility does not facially discriminate against interstate commerce when local private processors and out-of-state processors are treated alike. A fee imposed on diversion from that municipal system is permissible when it functions as a charge tied to costs of diverted waste rather than as an interstate-origin surcharge.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The municipal flow-control provision does not facially discriminate against interstate commerce merely by directing local waste to a municipally owned facility, and the challenged diversion fee is not discriminatory when it applies without regard to interstate origin and reasonably corresponds to costs imposed by waste sent outside the municipal system.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** *Carbone* condemned coercive favoritism toward a private enterprise, while the public-facility line preserves room for a municipality to provide a traditional public service. Here the relevant distinction is participation in the municipal disposal system, not the destination State. The fee therefore survives only insofar as its incidence and amount are genuinely tied to diversion-related costs rather than protection of local commerce.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Public ownership cannot be used as a label for private favoritism, and an origin-based or protectionist fee would remain invalid. Property and takings claims are outside the simulated grant.
