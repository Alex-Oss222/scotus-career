## Validated material facts and their precise scope

1. The United States intervened as trustee in June 1983, before the January 1984 trial. The original packet's placement after the November 1987 gross-proceeds deposit order is incorrect. The corrected date establishes earlier federal participation; it does not establish that Westmoreland timely protested its taxes or that every refund condition was met.

2. In the 1988 registry proceedings, the district court relied on the appellate characterization of the ceded-strip minerals as part of the reservation. It concluded that Interior's reservation-tax approval covered Westmoreland's mining of those minerals and that the contrary refusal rested on an erroneous interpretation. The prior neutral description of an unapproved early ceded-strip tax cannot therefore be used, without qualification, as a conclusive absence of lawful tribal taxing authority.

3. The same 1988 ruling treated the 1982 agreement as governing the registry funds and distributed those funds to the United States for the Tribe. The later district clarification confined that ruling to this distribution; it did not establish a Westmoreland tax obligation to the Tribe before the agreement. The legal-coverage premise, the scope of the adjudicated payment duty, and the existence of an earlier enforceable debt must remain separate.

4. The earlier refusals actually occurred. The district court separately found that Westmoreland would not have paid the tribal taxes before the required approval permitted customer pass-through. That is a finding about the company's prospective conduct and commercial arrangements. It is not itself a holding that the Tribe lacked legal authority, that the entire State collection belonged to the Tribe, or that the State's retention was equitable.

5. The already supported limits remain: the current amended complaints seek restitution and interest; they do not establish a separately available compensatory-damages claim. Market-effect evidence was conflicting and produced no finding. The 1982 agreement absolved Westmoreland of the earlier tribal obligation, and registry funds were distributed for the Tribe's benefit. The precise status of the proposed 1993 amendment remains unverified and supplies no assumed relief. Crow II's adjudication of the State taxes' invalidity is not reopened.

## Materiality and lawful alternatives

The supplement is material to the equitable treatment of Interior's refusal and to any account portraying the federal trustee as appearing only after the final deposit order. It removes a potential argument that lawful tribal taxation was simply absent during the earlier period. It does not resolve the separate restitution controversy by law of the case: the 1988 adjudication established distribution of registry funds under the agreement, not a judgment for all preregistry payments.

Two lawful approaches remain. One rejects mandatory full disgorgement because the Tribe must establish why all taxes paid by a distinct taxpayer constitute a benefit unjustly retained at its expense; actual refusal, commercial pass-through, the agreement, the taxpayer's refund position and the unmeasured market effects remain relevant, without making an erroneous refusal a legal disability. The other treats the Tribe's direct trust-mineral and federal preemption interest, clarified lawful tax coverage, and the State's extraction of invalid taxes from that resource as sufficient for benefit-based restitution. Under that approach the claim is neither Westmoreland's assigned refund nor compensation measured by lost royalties; absence of a finding of equal financial loss is not dispositive.

The distinction matters to the remedy. Rejection of automatic entitlement does not itself establish that the Tribe can never recover. Conversely, accepting this particular restitution claim does not decide a pre-1982 debt owed by Westmoreland, revive the taxpayer's lapsed refund claim, or establish that every entity economically affected by an invalid tax owns the proceeds. The new commitments must state which independent equitable theory each Justice adopts and why the corrected approval premise affects, or does not affect, that choice.
