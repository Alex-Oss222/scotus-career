* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Under the 1834 interstate compact, which State has sovereignty over portions of Ellis Island created by later artificial fill?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Article III original jurisdiction; the congressionally approved 1834 compact; boundary principles distinguishing artificial additions from changes that lawfully alter a boundary; prescription and acquiescence if established.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** SUSTAIN New Jersey’s sovereignty claim to the added fill, while preserving New York’s compact entitlement to the original island.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would treat the compact as the controlling allocation rather than let later federal construction unilaterally enlarge a State’s territory. An island’s ordinary name may continue to describe its expanded physical area without transferring the underlying sovereign boundary. New York could still prevail on a legally sufficient later adjustment or acquiescence theory, but scattered administration or public assumptions should not substitute for the required proof. The decree should locate the boundary through the factual work necessary to implement the compact, not award the entire island by a slogan about historical identity.

Its immunity holding does not decide sovereignty here. The compact’s allocation of the original island and surrounding territory must be applied to the physical history of the fill and any proven later agreement.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Sovereignty does not itself resolve federal ownership, every regulatory issue, or any unstated municipal boundary.
