## Component commitments

**U1 — announcement standard.** Property damage during warrant execution does not create a categorical higher degree of exigency than the particularized reasonable suspicion that Richards requires for dispensing with announcement. Officers must have facts known when the challenged force begins supporting reasonable suspicion that ordinary announcement would create danger, be futile or impede the investigation in a recognized way. A no-knock warrant label and the offense category do not independently meet that requirement. Destructive means remain subject to separate Fourth Amendment reasonableness scrutiny.

**U2 — constitutional application and force.** The credited pre-entry information supplies particularized reasonable suspicion of danger: a reliable informant reported seeing the dangerous escaped prisoner at the house and possible weapons in its garage, and an agent corroborated the presence of a similar-looking man. The single-window break and pointing a firearm through that opening were directed at preventing access to the reported weapons during entry. On these supplied facts that limited destruction was reasonable. Neither the number of officers outside nor the possibility that the believed fugitive was absent eliminates the particular indoor danger known beforehand. The subsequent gunfire does not supply the justification; the later failure to find Shelby does not retroactively remove it. This application authorizes neither gratuitous damage nor any amount of destruction merely because announcement can be excused.

**U3 — independent §3109 construction and application.** The statute's ordinary command requires notice of authority and purpose and refusal of admittance before breaking, subject to its express liberation alternative. Simultaneous announcement and breaking do not establish ordinary compliance, and no trapped officer or assistant establishes the liberation alternative. Nevertheless, the common-law announcement rule codified by §3109 includes a narrowly supported danger exception; particularized reasonable suspicion that following the ordinary process would expose officers or occupants to danger is sufficient for this exception. The statute includes opening a closed unlocked door within breaking, as Sabbath holds, and does not create a separate announcement-exigency tier when entry also damages property. The same pre-entry violent-fugitive and garage-weapons information satisfies this narrowly adopted statutory danger exception here. No statutory rule concerning every futility, escape or evidence-destruction situation is necessary to the decision.

## A3. Lewis v. United States, No. 96-7151

### Components and supported alternatives

**L1 — assimilation.** Do not assimilate Louisiana's child-victim first-degree murder provision for this killing. Section 1111 already punishes unlawful killing with malice and allocates every murder between its first-degree categories and its residual second-degree category. Using the State's additional age circumstance to make this second-degree killing first-degree murder with a mandatory life penalty would alter that existing congressional grading and punishment choice. A difference in elements or punishment does not itself establish a gap under §13(a).

**L2 — conviction.** Preserve the retained federal second-degree murder conviction. The reported jury findings necessarily include that offense's elements, and Lewis does not contest the legal correctness of retention. This is a bounded disposition on that accepted posture, not a general power to reform convictions despite disputed elements, notice, preservation or jury findings. *Gaudin* requires an actual element to be found by a jury; it does not manufacture an unraised defect in these accepted findings. *Wells* keeps statutory elements and remaining conviction/sentence questions separate.

**L3 — sentence/remedy.** Vacate the sentence portion of the appellate judgment and remand for federal resentencing; leave the federal conviction intact. The statutory availability of life does not establish that the sentencing court exercised the federal discretion or applied the Guidelines, whose reported range is 168–210 months. The Government concedes resentencing on this federal-conviction premise. Any upward departure, including a legally justified sentence of life, remains initially for the sentencing court. No acquittal, retrial or mandatory sentence reduction is ordered.

**Enhanced alternatives.** The competing assimilation path treats child murder as a distinct offense protecting children from a wrong not specifically identified by §1111. Its strongest support is the ACA's local-law gap-filling function: a broad federal prohibition need not eliminate every distinct state offense merely because conduct overlaps. The strongest objection is decisive here: §1111 is a comprehensive grading of the very offense of murder and expressly places all remaining murders in the second degree. Importing the different state grade would revise that scheme. *Williams*, 327 U.S. at 717–724, rejects both enlarging a federally defined offense through a different state definition and using the ACA to change its punishment; its analysis addresses both precise acts and the generic offense, rather than authorizing mechanical element comparison. Neither a universal same-transaction exclusion nor a universal different-elements permission follows.

Two nonassimilation rationales remain distinct. A statutory-purpose/displacement inquiry can ask whether the federal enactment leaves room for the particular state prohibition, considering the federal definition and punishment scheme. A narrower generic-offense construction reads §13's act-or-omission language through *Williams* and resolves this ordinary murder case without an open-ended inquiry into every conceivable congressional policy. Both support L1; agreement on L1 alone does not establish assent to every formulation of the broader inquiry. No new mandatory multi-factor test is prescribed.

## A5. Quality King Distributors, Inc. v. L'anza Research International, Inc., No. 96-1470

**Q1 — statutory rule.** Section 109(a) applies to the owner of these qualifying copies lawfully made in the United States and sold abroad before reimportation. Section 602(a) defines unauthorized importation as infringement of the §106 distribution right, and §106 makes that right subject to §109. Section 602(a) does not create a freestanding right exempt from that limitation. A foreign first sale of these domestically made copies does not itself remove the limitation. Ownership and lawful manufacture remain necessary; no unauthorized copying is alleged.

**Q2 — judgment and scope.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's categorical legal ruling and remand for proceedings consistent with Q1. No finding identifies which distributor first obtained title, the precise place of title transfer or the actual importer. Those details are not invented, and no new damages or contract disposition is ordered. Foreign-manufactured copies, independently enforceable contract obligations and unauthorized reproductions are outside the holding.

**Alternative test.** The separate-importation reading gives §602 an independent territorial right to prevent foreign price arbitrage. Its strongest point is the provision's evident additional work and its express exceptions. But the enacted linkage to §106 defeats treating it as categorically exempt from §109. The import provision retains work where the imported possessor is not the owner of a qualifying lawfully made copy, including unauthorized copies or possession without ownership; the expressly enumerated government, personal-use and nonprofit import exceptions operate on their own terms. This case need not determine every surviving application or construe foreign manufacture to reject the assertion that §602 becomes meaningless. *Bobbs-Merrill* supplies the statutory first-sale background and rejects expanding copyright through a resale notice; it does not decide foreign manufacture or contract enforceability. *Campbell* and *Fogerty* preserve the actual enacted limits rather than treating the owner's market interests as dispositive.

## A6. Gray v. Maryland, No. 96-8653

### Components and enhanced alternatives

**R1 — preserved redaction issue, Bruton application.** A confession redacted by conspicuous blanks or spoken deletion in place of names can remain a direct accusation of the codefendant sitting beside the declarant. On this joint-trial record the obvious substitutions preserve the accusatory structure and invite immediate identification; they do not remove reference to the other accused's existence as in *Richardson*. The two omitted names and testimony about six participants do not turn this conspicuous accusation into the otherwise neutral narrative considered there. Admission without an opportunity to cross-examine Bell violates confrontation notwithstanding the limiting instruction. This is not a categorical rule that every neutral pronoun or every later evidentiary linkage violates *Bruton*.

**R2 — competing Richardson application.** The confession names neither Gray nor a unique identifying description. It contains two deletions and the other testimony involves six participants; assigning a blank to Gray therefore requires an inference from other evidence or trial context. The limiting instruction should control under *Richardson*'s distinction between facial accusation and inferential linkage. Obvious editing alone does not establish the overwhelming inability to follow instructions that *Bruton* requires. This rationale affirms on the preserved confession-admission issue, without overruling *Bruton*.

**Strongest objections.** R1's principal difficulty is that a blank does not linguistically identify Gray and several other participants exist. Its answer confines the rule to the conspicuous name-deletion device and its direct accusatory function in the joint trial, not all inferences. R2's difficulty is that *Richardson* expressly reserved symbols and neutral pronouns and removed every reference to the defendant's existence; a conspicuous omission may communicate the same accusation as an actual name. Its answer treats semantic identification, rather than the salience of editing, as the administrable constitutional boundary. *Zafiro* preserves a specific confrontation right independently of general joinder discretion; it neither settles this boundary nor makes instructions universally effective.

**R3 — remedy and separate prosecutorial question.** For Justices finding R1 error, reverse the Maryland Court of Appeals' reinstatement and remand for proceedings consistent with that constitutional determination. Chapman harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt remains available if properly presented; the packet does not permit a present harmlessness finding or an unconditional new-trial mandate. No acquittal follows. All eight keep the follow-up question about Bell's information and Gray's arrest distinct. The State's prior-warrant account is a submission, not a new finding, and no contemporaneous objection to that question is identified. It is unnecessary to rest the R1 conclusion independently on that question; R2 supporters do not silently use it to evade preservation. Any independently preserved prosecutorial-use claim needs its own review.
