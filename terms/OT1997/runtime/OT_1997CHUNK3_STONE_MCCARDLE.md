### Issue 1: Preservation of an affirmative defense after trial

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preservation question can dispose of immunity without announcing a new merits or immunity rule.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Treat qualified immunity as an affirmative defense subject to applicable preservation rules, not a jurisdictional obligation a court must invent for the officer. Stone’s proposed substantive replacement need not be adopted to resolve this procedural question.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An official cannot ordinarily use an unpreserved affirmative immunity defense after trial to undo a verdict where the applicable procedural requirements were not met.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would preserve the supplied affirmance. The jury could credit the evidence of a glove-compartment search while rejecting compensation for the alleged emotional injury; nominal damages recognize the violation without inventing loss. Haddad’s failure to preserve the defense at the required stages prevents an appellate immunity theory from displacing that verdict. Punitive damages require their own culpability proof, and the finding of a search does not compel that finding. The extremely limited success, absence of prospective relief, and lack of a newly established rule permit the historically reviewed nominal fee result under the applicable fee standard. Stone’s contextual defense proposal is not an excuse to bypass ordinary litigation obligations or retry credibility.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Failure of immunity does not itself prove the constitutional claim, causation, damages, supervisory liability, or a right to trial.
