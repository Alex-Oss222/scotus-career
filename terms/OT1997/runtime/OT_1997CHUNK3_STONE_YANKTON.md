## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Reservation diminishment

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the reservation-status merits for the ceded lands.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Hagen and the enacted cession arrangement. The agreement’s surrender language, fixed-sum compensation, and treatment of surplus lands establish diminishment, but the judgment should track the land actually ceded rather than declare the entire historic reservation disestablished.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The 1894 Act diminished the Yankton Sioux Reservation by removing the ceded surplus lands from reservation status; it did not on this record erase every retained tribal land or authority.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress paid a fixed amount for surrender of the surplus territory, a structure materially stronger than a mere opening for settlement with proceeds held for the Tribe. Later jurisdictional treatment confirms but does not create the result. A parcel-focused remedy avoids converting diminishment into total disestablishment.
