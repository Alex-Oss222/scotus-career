* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** May prisoners obtain a standalone declaration resolving whether California qualifies for AEDPA’s special capital-habeas procedures before litigating their habeas cases?

Prisoners’ legitimate need to know deadlines is significant, but does not by itself create a freestanding cause and controversy for a separated procedural issue. The court hearing an actual habeas case can decide which statutory regime applies with the relevant predicates before it.

It therefore forecloses dismissing this case merely because relief is declaratory. Raines requires the actual dispute and injury, not an abstract disagreement about legal rules.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No categorical prohibition on declaratory judgments or pre-enforcement challenges; the rule concerns this fragmented habeas issue.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Labor law — compulsory fees and arbitration

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Must nonmember employees exhaust a union’s arbitration procedure before challenging compulsory fees in court?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Railway Labor Act union-security framework; Hudson procedural safeguards; arbitration ordinarily depends on agreement or a statute imposing it.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AFFIRM the absence of mandatory arbitration exhaustion for these nonconsenting objectors.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would preserve the distinction between providing a safeguard and compelling its use. The procedure responds to the union’s power to exact money from nonmembers; it is not a contract voluntarily accepted by those nonmembers. Requiring exhaustion would turn that protection into a procedural condition Congress did not impose. Courts can manage genuine duplication and consider what issues remain after a voluntary arbitration, but they should not manufacture consent from the union’s unilateral adoption of a procedure.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** A requirement that the union offer a prompt impartial means to resolve objections differs from a rule that objectors must surrender direct judicial access. The source record identifies no completed simulated decision creating mandatory exhaustion for nonconsenting fee objectors. Efficiency cannot itself supply that obligation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Consenting parties and express statutory exhaustion provisions present different questions.

### Issue 1: Tribal immunity for off-reservation commerce

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves immunity and does not reach breach or damages.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing immunity rule even though Stone regards broad commercial immunity as policy contestable. The legal source places revision primarily with Congress and permits parties to demand waiver, security, or nontribal counterparties. Judicially inventing an off-reservation exception would alter settled sovereign status without a source-bound boundary.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An Indian tribe retains sovereign immunity from suit on an off-reservation commercial contract unless the tribe clearly waives immunity or Congress unequivocally abrogates it.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Immunity attaches to the sovereign, not only to governmental acts on reservation land. The proposed commercial exception raises line-drawing and reliance questions Congress can address comprehensively. The note contains no waiver, so the state court lacked authority to adjudicate the claim against the Tribe.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not immunize individual agents automatically, bar suits under a clear waiver, prevent Congress from narrowing immunity, or decide enforcement against property not protected by immunity.

**523 U.S. 833 (1998) | May 26, 1998**

**Proposed Stone disposition:** REVERSE the Ninth Circuit's substantive due process ruling.

**Reasoning:** Constitutional culpability depends partly on the actual opportunity for deliberation. An officer responding to a rapidly developing pursuit cannot automatically be judged by the standard applicable to an official who has time to evaluate alternatives. In the high-speed pursuit presented, the record does not establish a purpose to cause harm unrelated to legitimate law enforcement, as required for the substantive due process claim.

Stone would emphasize that the merits inquiry and qualified-immunity inquiry are distinct. Contextual analysis cannot use information learned after the incident to manufacture a constitutional violation, but neither does an official's profession excuse deliberate misconduct when time and opportunity for responsible action exist.

**Doctrinal contribution:** Context-sensitive constitutional culpability; protection against hindsight; distinction between negligence, deliberate indifference, and intentional abuse.
