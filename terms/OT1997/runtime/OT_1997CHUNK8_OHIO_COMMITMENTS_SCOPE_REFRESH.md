## Judgment and additional operative limits

**Unchanged provisional judgment:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer support vacating the Sixth Circuit judgment and remanding for dismissal without prejudice as unripe, with the District Court's substantive summary judgment displaced. The judgment accounts for all three described substantive claims: the NFMA/NEPA/APA challenge to the plan and below-cost clearcutting; the public-trust challenge to below-cost sales; and the NFMA/APA challenge to the timber-suitability regulations' treatment of economically unsuitable land.

The additional legal labels do not themselves identify a new current injury, final project authorization or loss of later review. The regulation challenge has the strongest claim to immediate review as a completed general rule, but *Toilet Goods*, *Abbott* and current *Texas* require consideration of actual effects and fitness beyond finality. The alleged substantive flaw becomes materially concrete in its application to proposed harvesting. The shared ground is the present record's absence of a demonstrated need to decide this substantive dispute before that reviewable application, not a universal prohibition on direct regulatory challenges.

All eight join these additional limits:

- **NEPA is expressly pleaded.** No particular omitted statement, denied comment or other specific procedural deprivation is established by the supplied complaint recitation. The Court neither invents one nor rules that a genuine procedural claim must await project approval. The existing *Lujan* concrete-interest procedural protection is retained.
- **Public-trust law is not decided.** The Court neither recognizes nor rejects the pleaded duty or a cause of action. The timing ground disposes of the present substantive challenge without reaching that question.
- **The regulation count is included, not silently lost.** Its completed-rule character is considered; the disposition leaves direct review of an independently consequential regulation possible on an appropriate record. It creates no forest-plan or regulation immunity.
- **Later recreational assertions are not established injury findings.** The brief's motorcycle/heavy-machinery, road-closure and hiking-trail assertions could concern effects different from future cutting. No prior presentation or actual present effect is established by the clean materials. They supply no established basis for deciding the complaint's reviewed dispute differently, but receive no merits or ripeness adjudication as an independent claim. No intentional waiver, categorical forfeiture rule, or inexistence of recreational harm is found.
- **No remedy extension follows from requested relief.** A prayer to stop timbering does not prove present logging authorization, and requests for costs or fees do not supply the missing present controversy. The Court orders no injunction, plan revision or fee award. It does not decide an independent fee-entitlement question.
