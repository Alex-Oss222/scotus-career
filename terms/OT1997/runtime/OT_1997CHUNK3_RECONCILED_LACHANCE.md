## Historical evidence and operative scope

The full historical report, **522 U.S. 262–268**, confirms that both Federal Circuit judgments were reversed unanimously, with one Court opinion and no separate writings or nonparticipation. That establishes strong premise-sensitive evidence for each Associate; it does not replace the independent statutory and constitutional analysis.

The historical Court rejected both sources of asserted immunity: §7513's service-efficiency cause standard and enumerated procedural protections contained no right to knowingly give false denials, and meaningful process did not require protecting such deception. Its comparisons to truthful testimony and perjury answered the claimed oath distinction, because these were employment false-statement charges, not perjury prosecutions. Its discussion of valid self-incrimination and possible civil adverse inferences did not adjudicate a respondent-specific privilege, compel an immunity waiver, or restore a particular sanction. The final formal disposition was “Reversed”; the text did not find every administrative element anew or order reinstatement of each original penalty.

The materially altered simulated employee-process and civil/criminal cases leave those premises intact. Jeffries requires actual legally disciplinable conduct and a supported agency account; Richenberg distinguishes genuine rebuttal from compulsory acceptance of every denial; Valot protects lawful nonsham process use while preserving anti-fraud measures and independent employment action. None immunizes proved deliberate falsehood. Gaudin's criminal element-allocation rule, Lanier's criminal fair-warning rule and Hudson's actual civil/criminal classification remain at their stated scope. They do not create a criminal jury or burden here, and the historical report's citation to postdivergence Dunnigan does not supply an unprovided in-world holding. The shared sufficient grounds are the actual statutory provisions, surviving Bryson and Loudermill principles, and the supplied simulated law at its proper level of generality.

## Final components and exact join limits

**C1 — procedural due process.** All eight reject the categorical proposition that a meaningful opportunity to answer a misconduct charge immunizes a knowingly false factual denial from an otherwise lawful employment falsification charge or penalty consideration. The assumed protected employment interest remains assumed; Loudermill's notice, explanation of evidence, meaningful response and adequate later process retain their force. The grammatical form of a denial does not turn established deliberate deception into necessary protected participation.

Every join requires the distinction between a failed defense and proved knowing falsity. Good-faith factual contest, mistake, uncertain recollection, a dispute over legal characterization, a procedural nonadmission and insistence that the agency prove its charge are not automatically falsification. The actual charge and its requisite culpability must be supported under the governing administrative burden. A separate supported determination does **not** require evidence from a source different from the evidence of underlying misconduct, mandatory corroboration, a criminal jury, proof beyond a reasonable doubt, or a newly universal materiality element. The absence of an oath does not create the categorical immunity at issue, but no identical oath status is found across all employee files.

**C2 — statutory claim.** All eight reject categorical denial immunity inferred from §§7513 and 7701. Section 7513(a) still requires cause promoting service efficiency. Its notice, answer, evidence-submission, representation, specific-decision, record-maintenance and appeal rights are effective without sheltering proved knowing falsehood. The thirty-day advance-notice rule retains its statutory reasonable-cause-to-believe-a-punishable-crime exception, with no finding that the exception applies here; the answer period remains at least seven days. Agency provision of a hearing under §7513(c) is permissive, while the applicable Board hearing/representation rights under §7701 remain distinct. Ordinary misconduct requires preponderance proof; specified performance/SES matters have the separate substantial-evidence rule. Harmful procedural error, a prohibited personnel practice and legal invalidity remain independent grounds against sustaining otherwise supported action. Section 7703(c)'s appellate substantial-evidence/legality review is not the agency's proof burden. The OPM review route and its actual prerequisites are preserved; missing research copies establish no failure to satisfy them.

The Constitution and the statute are separately answered asserted sources of immunity. Rejecting one does not decide the other by assumption; all eight join both analyses. The result creates no new reporting duty, regulatory charge, final service nexus, universal materiality requirement, or chosen penalty.

**C3 — penalty and impeachment.** All eight remove the categorical bar to ordinary lawful consideration of a proved knowing false denial in penalty selection or credibility assessment, including the identified McManus impeachment bar. This permits, but does not compel, an otherwise lawful enhancement or evidentiary use. It does not make an adverse underlying finding automatically prove a lie, compel an adverse inference, or displace relevance, actual charge proof, procedural fairness and remaining defenses. The statutory decisionmaker determines the lawful consequence on the actual record.

**C4 — reporting and self-incrimination.** All eight set aside the Barrett/Roberts reporting disposition only to the extent it depends on the invalid categorical premise that self-implication alone immunizes the employee from any information duty. Remand preserves the actual reporting rule and every supported defense. Garrity's protection against criminal use of answers compelled by threatened employment loss and Gardner's prohibition on discharge solely for refusing to waive protection against criminal use and fruits survive. Gardner's different setting of questions specifically, directly and narrowly concerning official duties without a required waiver remains distinct. The reproduced §5.4's limitation for information whose disclosure law or regulation prohibits also survives.

No respondent-specific compulsion, criminal-use threat, immunity, refusal or waiver is established. Preservation of valid self-incrimination protection is not a new universal bar on lawful civil adverse inferences: the historical opinion's Baxter discussion addressed that distinct possibility. No actual inference or silence penalty is selected in these commitments. Neither the absence of a right to lie nor the presence of a possible privilege resolves an unproved individual reporting claim. No independent holding about every invocation of silence, general duty to confess, or criminal immunity adjudication follows.

The reporting branch's dependent disposition is set aside only within C4, without a new reporting or privilege merits determination.
