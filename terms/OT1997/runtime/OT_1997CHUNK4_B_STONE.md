## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Competitor standing and zone of interests

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds standing and proceeds. The banks allege direct competitive effects from agency expansion of a rival’s customer base.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The banks’ competitive injury is concrete and lies sufficiently close to the common-bond limitation, which structures the market Congress authorized federal credit unions to serve.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Competing banks may seek APA review of an agency interpretation that expands federal credit-union membership beyond the statutory common-bond limit.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The banks need not be the statute’s primary beneficiaries. Their asserted injury is the market consequence of the exact membership rule they challenge, and invalidating the approvals would redress it.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not make every competitor challenge cognizable or convert the zone inquiry into a guarantee of success.

### Issue 2: Meaning of the occupational common bond

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory meaning after finding reviewability.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The Act permits a credit union organized around one occupational common bond, not a portfolio of unrelated groups whose only commonality is joining the same credit union. Agency convenience and policy cannot supply the missing bond.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Act’s occupational common-bond provision does not authorize one federal credit union to aggregate multiple unrelated occupational groups under a single charter.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The text links membership to a common occupational bond. The NCUA reading makes “common” perform no work because membership itself becomes the only connection. That is elimination of a statutory boundary rather than permissible gap filling.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Congress may amend the statute, and the decision does not restrict associational or geographic charters governed by different text.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Dependent Indian community status

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory land-status question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A dependent Indian community requires land set aside by the Federal Government for Indian use under federal superintendence. ANCSA intentionally replaced reservation and trust status with corporate fee ownership and broad alienability, so both predicates are absent.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Land conveyed in fee under ANCSA, after revocation of the reservation and without federal superintendence, is not a dependent Indian community under § 1151(b).
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The settlement act sought economic autonomy through fee ownership rather than continuing reservation status. Federal programs benefiting residents do not amount to territorial superintendence, and Native ownership alone does not create a federal set-aside.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not question tribal identity, federal benefits, trust parcels, or authority arising from a source other than territorial Indian-country status.

- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not bar consensual trial before the transferee judge, later transfer by the originating court, or statutory amendment.

**Proposed Stone disposition:** REVERSE the judgment denying absolute legislative immunity.
