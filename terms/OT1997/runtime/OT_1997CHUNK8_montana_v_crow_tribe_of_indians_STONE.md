## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Indian law — restitution of invalid state taxes

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Does invalidation of state coal taxes entitle the Tribe to recover all taxes collected by Montana from a nontribal mining company?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Federal Indian tax preemption and equitable restitution; identify the claimant’s entitlement separately from the invalidity of the tax.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** REVERSE an automatic full-restitution award and REMAND for the proper equitable analysis.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would not dilute the prior determination that the State lacked taxing authority. But invalidity and ownership of the resulting recovery are different questions. An award to the Tribe must account for who paid, who bore the loss, any settlements or waivers, and the risk of duplicative or windfall recovery. Equitable analysis cannot become an excuse for the State to keep an unlawful gain without examination; it must explain why this claimant is or is not entitled to the particular fund. The remedy follows proven entitlement rather than punishment of the State.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Reich and Fulton require adequate correction for unlawful taxation in their proper claimant and procedural settings. They do not direct payment of every invalid tax to any entity injured economically. The Tribe’s restitution theory requires its own connection to the collected funds and consideration of the actual taxpayer’s position.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Do not reopen the established tax-invalidity holding or assume that the Tribe and mining company have identical claims.
