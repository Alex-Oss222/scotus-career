## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Capital sentencing — mitigation instructions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Must a capital sentencing jury receive an additional instruction defining mitigation or listing particular mitigating evidence when the charge otherwise permits consideration of that evidence?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Eighth Amendment mitigation principles under Lockett, Eddings, and Boyde; assess the charge as a whole and whether there is a reasonable likelihood of forbidden application.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AFFIRM rejection of the instructional claim on the reconstructed unrestricted-charge premise.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would examine what this jury was legally allowed to do, not insist on preferred wording. If the instructions and argument left the jury free to consider the defense evidence and choose life despite aggravation, absence of a separate definition does not establish an unconstitutional barrier. The gravity of the sentence requires close examination, but does not justify converting helpful instructions into automatic constitutional elements. Conversely, an instruction reasonably understood as foreclosing mitigation would require a different result; the proposal depends on the whole charge actually permitting effect to be given to it. The decisive distinction from the simulated Graham and Johnson reversals is therefore functional: this proposed affirmance is justified only if Buchanan’s jury could express the mitigating judgment through its authorized sentencing choice, rather than needing to answer a factual special issue falsely. If the actual charge lacks that route, Stone must follow those reversals; a preference against detailed instructions cannot overcome them.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Revised Graham and Johnson v. Texas require an effective route for the jury to give material mitigation its independent culpability significance; admission of evidence and partial relevance to dangerousness alone are insufficient. Delo allows a supporting-evidence requirement for a particular mitigating instruction, and Tuilaepa rejects mandatory numerical weights or labels. The question here is whether the whole charge already supplies the effective route that was absent in Graham and Johnson. If an actual constitutional error is found, revised Brecht retains the State’s Chapman burden on the properly presented collateral claim; historical Brecht’s more lenient standard is not simulated law.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual transcript must support the unrestricted-consideration premise; the jury cannot be barred from giving effect to relevant mitigation.
