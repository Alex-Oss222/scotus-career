## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Path A — reverse the retention-based ADEA bar.** The statutory conditions are minimum conditions for legally effective waiver, rather than a nonbinding description of informed assent. Retention cannot retroactively furnish the missing ADEA reference, preexecution consideration opportunity or contractual revocation protection. Calling retention ratification does not avoid the prohibition when its asserted consequence is to enforce the same relinquishment of the ADEA claim. Nor does treating retention as a distinct waiver satisfy the actual requirements or the employer's burden. A tender condition that bars the action until consideration is returned gives this defective waiver the same prohibited effect. Estoppel based only on the same retention presents the same problem.

The strongest objection is that Congress regulated initial formation without expressly abolishing traditional ratification or rescission rules: a later informed election can affirm a voidable bargain, and allowing repudiation while keeping its price may be inequitable. That argument has force and does not require claiming the original execution complied. It nevertheless fails for these provisional commitments because the requested consequence still makes this noncompliant waiver dispositive of an ADEA claim; neither a compliant later agreement nor a distinct statutory basis is established. Congress need not enumerate every common-law label for enforcing a prohibited waiver. The conclusion is limited to retention's asserted claim-barring operation; it does not invalidate every contractual term or eliminate every lawful monetary remedy.

**Path B — affirm on later ratification or prior tender.** This path would preserve the original instrument as voidable, treat informed retention after awareness as affirmance, and require restoration before repudiation. Mezzanatto supplies a date-eligible reason to consider lawful consent and background waiver principles; actual attorney consultation and months of payments make this a stronger version than simple reliance on a signature. The path would need a legal explanation why the resulting ADEA relinquishment escapes §626(f)'s minimum conditions and express burden. It would also have to use the reported retention-after-awareness rationale without inventing the date or content of later advice, an additional promise, or a new agreement.

Its central defect is legal even crediting the lower court's reported awareness premise: later informed retention is insufficient to supply the absent statutory safeguards, and an otherwise common-law remedy cannot operate as the very release defense Congress conditioned. None of the supplied precedents authorizes ignoring an express statutory minimum because actual understanding is alleged. On these sources the eight provisional commitments reject Path B. That convergence does not make every ratification doctrine unlawful; different rights, an actual compliant agreement or genuinely distinct equitable conduct would require its own inquiry.

**Path C — permit the action but adjudicate repayment immediately.** Removing the waiver bar need not assure Oubre that she may keep all consideration. Conversely, the amount paid alone does not establish an employer's right to repayment or deduction. The full agreement may cover other rights, its allocation is unknown, and no restitution judgment is before the Court. McKennon's separation of liability and relief supports preserving, rather than deciding, a legally available accounting issue. A mandate can leave lawful restitution, recoupment or setoff questions open without ordering $6,258 returned, creating a new debt or conditioning the action on tender. This is the remedy position of all eight Associates.

**Scope and compatibility.** The common sufficient rule concerns this conceded statutory noncompliance and retention alone. Agreement on reversal is not agreement that the entire contract is void, that no employee can ever ratify any transaction, that all equitable defenses are displaced, or that the employer has an affirmative recovery right. All eight can join the narrower common rule and its bounded mandate. More categorical language would require the distinct joins described below and cannot be inferred from the judgment votes.

## Individual commitments

### John Paul Stevens

**Reach and judgment:** Reach the waiver, ratification, prior-tender and retention-based estoppel questions. Reverse the Fifth Circuit's affirmance insofar as it sustains summary judgment barring the ADEA action on those grounds, and remand for ordinary further proceedings. Preserve separate state-law dispositions and other rights in their existing posture unless a proper ground independently requires reconsideration.

**Sufficient ground and own authority:** Section 626(f)'s express minimum conditions control the asserted release. His full McKennon join refuses a later defense's categorical elimination of the ADEA claim while preserving legally established remedial limits; his Bates join requires the enacted predicates. Neither analogue substitutes for the waiver text. Later retention cannot provide the omitted statutory protections. Stevens did not join Mezzanatto's six-Justice rationale; the supplied record gives no separate reasoning from him, and none is inferred.

**Counterargument, remedy and joins:** The employer's strongest concern is retention of the bargain's benefit after knowledge of the defect. That concern can support an independently lawful accounting claim, not cure the statute through a condition that prevents suit. He does not rely on inability to repay or assume discrimination from the poor evaluation or severance choice. Can join the common statutory rule and bounded reversal. Cannot join automatic repayment, universal recovery immunity, a liability finding, or revival of every state-law claim. A narrower holding about retention alone is sufficient; a broader holding voiding the whole agreement is unnecessary. No separate writing is required on the compatible formulation.

### Sandra Day O'Connor

**Reach and judgment:** Reach the preserved defense questions and reverse the ADEA release-based summary judgment component, with remand. Do not adjudicate the employer's possible independent recovery or the discrimination merits.

**Sufficient ground and own authority:** Her unanimous McKennon opinion separates the existence of an ADEA violation from the scope of appropriate relief without treating equitable concern as wholesale claim forfeiture. Her Mezzanatto join recognizes knowing and voluntary consent where the law permits it, while preserving particular statutory constraints. Here Congress itself defined necessary conditions and assigned the validity burden; the admitted deficiencies prevent this release from barring the action. Ratification by retention would enforce the same prohibited waiver, and prior tender cannot be its substitute.

**Counterargument, remedy and joins:** Actual legal consultation and reported later awareness make the common-law election argument substantial, but statutory compliance cannot be replaced with a generalized assessment of sophistication. McKennon does not grant a restitution remedy or import its misconduct proof test. She can join a rule preserving any otherwise lawful accounting claim subject to its own predicates and defenses. She cannot join a pronouncement that retention is consequence-free in every setting, a specific repayment direction, or an invented showing of inability to repay. A narrower retention-based reversal reaches the necessary issue; broader rules about unrelated fraud or enforceability of other rights remain open.

## Provisional disposition and source limits
