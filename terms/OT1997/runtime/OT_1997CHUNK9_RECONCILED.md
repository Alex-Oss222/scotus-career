## Six components: grounds, counts and operative limits

### 1. Compact interpretation and original entitlement

**Six support NJ original entitlement:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Article First supplies the midwater sovereignty boundary; Article Second preserves New York's original island jurisdiction. Article Third's commerce/navigation jurisdiction does not restore New York's underlying sovereignty west of that line. The Compact does not displace the ordinary rule that artificial filling leaves the interstate boundary unchanged. Foreseeability of reclamation and omission of metes and bounds do not establish an agreement transferring the submerged sovereign territory whenever fill is added.

This is the whole-Compact/background-law ground, not a claim that the word “present,” by itself, gives metes and bounds. The Master understood that word partly in terms of retained jurisdiction after the federal cession; the geographic conclusion does not require treating his explanation as the only possible meaning. Central Railroad, 209 U.S. 473, 478–479, remains precedent distinguishing sovereignty from Article Third jurisdiction, without becoming a prior judgment binding these States on the precise present island. Artificial enlargement is distinct from natural accretion, and no universal proposition about every federal dredging operation or every subsequently connected feature is adopted.

**Scalia and Thomas support NY interpretation.** The Compact's integrated structure and its commerce/navigation arrangement make the scope of the retained island sufficiently ambiguous for practical construction to matter. Article Third informs the meaning of Articles First and Second even though the State did not advance an independent Article Third title claim. They regard the decades of state and federal administration as establishing the Compact's meaning: New York governed the enlarged island as a single jurisdiction, while New Jersey did not supply comparable island administration. They do not treat Article Second as an automatic transfer clause for every imaginable future fill or treat prescription as proved merely by relabeling it interpretation.

Their source ground is the historical Scalia dissent, joined by Thomas, 523 U.S. 829–832, including its distinction between interpreting an ambiguous agreement and ousting an otherwise undoubted owner. Its date-eligible interpretive support includes Air France v. Saks, 470 U.S. 392, 396, Choctaw Nation, 318 U.S. 423, 431–432, and Vermont v. New Hampshire, 289 U.S. 593, 619. The supplied Master already discusses Vermont's practical-construction principle, and the New York exceptions invoke the same boundary practice; the neutral packet supplies the relevant structural argument and competing conduct. This is no newly reconstructed neutral fact. Scalia and Thomas would also consider the City's argument rather than characterize it as the State's preserved independent claim. Their sufficient ground here remains the integrated Compact and practical construction; no separate independent Article Third holding is supplied.

### 2. Prescription and acquiescence

The five and Stevens apply the preponderance standard to New York's affirmative claim of long, continuous possession/assertion of sovereignty over the actual disputed fill and New Jersey's acquiescence. Acquiescence requires knowledge of the adverse claim or sufficiently open, notorious, visible and uninterrupted adverse acts to support that inference. No fixed universal number of years, mandatory municipal-service checklist, requirement of a formal State protest, or rule that federal occupation categorically precludes prescription is adopted. The latter general question remains open.

The five acknowledge specifically located fill-resident electoral and hospital records. They do not dismiss them as wholly unspecified references to “Ellis Island.” They nevertheless find the cumulative adverse claim and notice inadequate: the identified civil acts are intermittent, often performed elsewhere, and coexist with mixed federal understandings and episodes recognizing New Jersey's interests. Original-island New York jurisdiction makes generic references equivocal. Federal control constrains both the acts available to New York and the notice or protest reasonably expected of New Jersey. These are contextual reasons why the affirmative burden is unmet, not a new elevated proof quantum.

The 1904 deed is not an unqualified sovereign concession, the 1933 permit's object must be respected, and federal or local conduct is not automatically State conduct. ## Common operative rule and explanation

Count III charges a monetary transaction in criminally derived property exceeding $10,000 under §1957. The January 1991 Florida deposit and withdrawals are the transactions charged. Missouri supplied the antecedent drug proceeds, not a laundering act by Cabrales alleged in these counts. The Government does not allege that she transported the funds from Missouri to Florida or performed any constituting laundering act in Missouri.

The strongest contrary construction treats the predicate crime as the laundering offense's beginning because illicit origin is indispensable. It fails here because an indispensable attribute of the transaction's object need not be prohibited conduct by this defendant at the place where that attribute arose. All statutory elements retain their force; the ruling does not excise knowledge or unlawful source. Neither a functional description of a laundering scheme nor the practical value of Missouri evidence adds charged acts.

Actual Thomas illustrates a different application: an authorized interstate delivery completes a charged transportation at its destination. ## J — Limited jurisdiction to consider correction of the same decree

The United States invoked the original court's jurisdiction and obtained its quiet-title judgment there. An independent action brought in that same court to obtain relief from that judgment does not require a second independent jurisdictional grant merely because it is procedurally a new action. The traditional ancillary character, preserved by Rule 60(b)'s savings language and illustrated by Pacific Railroad, treats the corrective request as a continuation for jurisdictional purposes.

This decides actual authority to entertain the request; jurisdiction is not assumed to reach merits. The Rule preserves an existing equitable procedure, not general subject-matter jurisdiction or a universal waiver for new claims. The Government's original invocation supports correction of its own decree, not unlimited affirmative liability. Kokkonen's separate settlement-contract enforcement holding expressly reserved reopening. Peacock's new-debtor case distinguishes decree effectuation from imposing a different substantive obligation. Neither case requires the Government's proposed blanket independent-jurisdiction rule here.

## E — The independent action fails the extraordinary standard

An independent action may relieve a completed judgment only to prevent a **grave miscarriage of justice**. The traditional equitable requirements must establish that exceptional need; they do not create a routine second opportunity whenever favorable evidence later appears.
