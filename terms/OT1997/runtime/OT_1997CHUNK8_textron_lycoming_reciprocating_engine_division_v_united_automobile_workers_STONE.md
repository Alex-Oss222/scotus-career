## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Labor law — §301 jurisdiction and contract validity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Does LMRA §301(a) confer jurisdiction over a union suit seeking to invalidate a collective-bargaining agreement without alleging its violation?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** 29 U.S.C. §185(a); distinguish jurisdiction over a suit alleging breach from adjudication of contract validity within such a suit.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** REVERSE the jurisdictional ruling and REMAND.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would apply the statutory gateway before constructing federal common law. Congress’s choice of “suits for violation” identifies the dispute that opens §301 jurisdiction. The union’s theory, as reconstructed, asserts invalidity without breach. Jurisdiction cannot be manufactured by noting that a different plaintiff might later sue to enforce the agreement. Once a genuine violation suit exists, however, a court may need to decide formation or invalidity defenses; the proposed holding does not require enforcing a fraudulent contract.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Kokkonen rejects enlarging federal jurisdiction merely because a contract relates to federal litigation. Similarly, federal labor policy cannot replace §301’s specified category. Contract validity may be a necessary issue in an actual violation suit, but that does not make every validity suit a violation action.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No categorical rule bars validity defenses in a properly brought §301 violation action.
