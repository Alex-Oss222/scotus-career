## A. Case, record, and posture

- **Case:** *EEOC v. Johnson & Higgins, Inc.*, 91 F.3d 1529 (2d Cir. 1996).
- **Supreme Court petition:** No. 96-1743, captioned *Johnson & Higgins, Inc. v. EEOC*. **Term:** October Term 1997. **Simulation:** petition proposed for grant; merits date unassigned.
- **Question presented, reconstructed:** Whether the Age Discrimination in Employment Act applies to a corporation's owner-directors who remain full-time managers, whether the EEOC may sue without an individual employee's charge or consent, and whether a fixed retirement age is defensible as a reasonable factor other than age.
- **Judgment below:** Second Circuit affirmed judgment for the EEOC, including declaratory and injunctive relief against the compulsory retirement of employee-directors.
- **Record:** New Jersey-incorporated Johnson & Higgins operated an international insurance and employee-benefits business. Its board of owner-directors consisted of senior officials who continued full-time operational employment, reported to senior board members, and had performance-based reviews. A policy ended their combined status at age 60 or 62. No former director supported the enforcement suit. EEOC separately investigated and sought conciliation before suing.
- **Relief sought:** Company sought invalidation of EEOC enforcement and injunction; EEOC sought elimination of the age-triggered employment practice.
- **Threshold:** Agency independent public enforcement authority, statutory conciliation, employee-versus-owner status under enacted ADEA, jurisdiction and injunction specificity.
- **Date:** Second Circuit decided August 8, 1996.

## B. Law entering the case

The Age Discrimination in Employment Act, its incorporated enforcement provisions, and the Court's governing employment-relationship decisions control. The Court's *Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden* (1992) ruling employs ordinary common-law agency criteria for statutory employee status when no contrary definition is supplied. Later amendments and decisions are not imported.

### Issue 1: EEOC authority independent of an employee complainant

- **Governing law:** 29 U.S.C. § 626(b), (f), incorporating enforcement authority; incorporated FLSA provisions, 29 U.S.C. §§ 216(c), 217; *General Telephone Co. of Northwest v. EEOC*, 446 U.S. 318 (1980), recognizes independent federal enforcement of discrimination law. OWBPA, 29 U.S.C. § 626(f)(4), protects the Commission's enforcement role against private waivers.
- **Open point:** Whether conciliation was reasonable in the face of company's categorical denial and whether personal waiver could affect public relief.

### Issue 2: ADEA employee or corporate proprietor

- **Governing law:** ADEA, 29 U.S.C. §§ 623, 630(f), 631(c); *Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden*, 503 U.S. 318 (1992), uses common-law agency principles to identify employees when Congress supplies no contrary definition. Corporate director status alone is not dispositive of an individual's separate employment duties.
- **Open point:** How actual control, compensation, supervision, and continued job duties characterize owner-directors with overlapping roles.

### Issue 3: Age-specific retirement and relief

- **Governing law:** 29 U.S.C. § 623(f)(1) permits differentiation based on reasonable **factors other than age**; § 631(c) states an exceptional retirement regime for certain bona fide executives or high policymakers at specified conditions. Statutory injunctive relief must address unlawful employment practices.
- **Open point:** Whether age 60/62 is a valid alternative factor merely because succession planning is its underlying rationale.

## C. Decision-material litigation positions

- **Below:** EEOC tried appropriate conciliation; no individual consent was necessary. These directors' continued duties, evaluations and own description demonstrated employment notwithstanding equity ownership and governance. The retirement rule facially used age rather than another factor and stopped employment at a prohibited age.
- **Company:** The directors are collectively the owners and highest governance body, comparable in substance to partners or employers. They wanted and accepted succession terms; coercive EEOC action threatens owner autonomy and settlement of private arrangements.
- **EEOC:** The business chose a corporation, the ADEA covers its actual employees, and public enforcement cannot be disabled by the preferences of potentially affected workers. An age-based rule remains age-based even if adopted for succession planning.
- **Limit:** Government cannot invoke general equality aspirations to alter the precise statutory coverage or order directors to retain board seats solely because they retain a separate employment relationship.

## D. Lawful paths and uncertainty

1. Affirm under employee/common-law facts, independent EEOC enforcement power, and the age-specific prohibition.
2. Reverse if all covered persons were truly proprietor-employers without employment relationships, under an established legally valid characterization.
3. Limit an injunction so the company remains free to change ownership or directorship independently from protected employment, rather than locking in its chosen board indefinitely.
