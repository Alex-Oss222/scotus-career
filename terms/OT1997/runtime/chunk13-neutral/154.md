## A. Case, record, and posture

- **Case:** *Engineering Contractors Ass'n of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County*, 122 F.3d 895 (11th Cir. 1997).
- **Supreme Court petitions:** Nos. **97-1115** (Metropolitan Dade County) and **97-1160** (Allied Minority Contractors Association). **Term:** October Term 1997. **Simulation:** both petitions proposed and approved for review; merits date not fixed.
- **Question presented, reconstructed:** What evidentiary showing and fit justify local-government race-, ethnicity-, and sex-conscious construction contracting preferences under equal protection, and did Dade County's evidentiary record satisfy those requirements?
- **Judgment below:** Eleventh Circuit affirmed district court's declaration that three county programs were unconstitutional and permanent injunction against their operation.
- **Record:** County programs for Black-owned (BBE), Hispanic-owned (HBE), and women-owned (WBE) construction businesses used contracting goals of 15, 19, and 11 percent respectively for construction work over $25,000. Eligibility required local operation, covered ownership and control, and normally small-business size. Programs authorized several preferential contract measures. The County offered bidder, award, contract-dollar, and marketplace studies, including Wainwright and Brimmer analyses and evidence of discrimination. The district court found these insufficient, stressing firm-size explanations and inconsistent regression results. The appellate court found no clear error in those factual judgments.
- **Relief sought:** County and intervenors seek reversal of program invalidation and the permanent injunction; private contractor associations seek affirmance.
- **Threshold:** Plaintiffs suffered unequal opportunity to compete for public contracts; review of factual findings for clear error differs from independent review of the legal level of scrutiny. The record's distinctions among Black, Hispanic, and women contractors must not collapse into one assumption.
- **Dates:** Eleventh Circuit opinion September 2, 1997, amended October 8, 1997.
- **Participation:** No identified Court recusal.

## B. Law entering the case

The governing distinction in this Court's decisions is between state and local racial preferences under *City of Richmond v. J.A. Croson Co.* and the separate federal remedial arrangement in *Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña* (1995). The latter was decided under the retained *Metro Broadcasting* federal important-objective and substantial-relationship standard, not a new federal strict-scrutiny rule. The *Northeastern Florida Chapter of Associated General Contractors v. Jacksonville* (1993) decision recognizes unequal competitive opportunity as a constitutional injury without proving the contract would otherwise have been awarded.

### Issue 1: Racial and ethnic contracting preferences

- **Governing law:** *City of Richmond v. J.A. Croson Co.*, 488 U.S. 469 (1989), subjects local race-conscious contracting to strict scrutiny, requiring identified remedial injury and narrow fit. In this Court's *Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña* (1995), a separate federal optional subcontracting incentive was sustained under *Metro Broadcasting*'s important-government-objective and substantial-relationship standard. *Adarand* expressly retained the distinct *Croson* rule for state and local measures; it did not extend federal strict scrutiny to every remedial classification. Local Dade County remains governed by *Croson* unless that rule is expressly reconsidered.
- **Open point:** Statistical evidence both reflects the operation of an existing long-running BBE program and can be distorted by firm-size composition; whether available studies demonstrate sufficient discrimination in the relevant procurement market is contested.

### Issue 2: Sex-conscious contracting preferences

- **Governing law:** *United States v. Virginia*, 518 U.S. 515 (1996), requires an exceedingly persuasive justification for sex classifications within the intermediate-scrutiny framework. The Eleventh Circuit treated the WBE program under intermediate scrutiny.
- **Open point:** Whether mixed industry data and acknowledged gaps establish an important remedial objective substantially related to the preference actually deployed.

### Issue 3: Remedy

- **Governing law:** 42 U.S.C. § 1983 where properly invoked, the Fourteenth Amendment, traditional equitable requirements, and standards of review for factual findings following trial.
- **Open point:** Whether different outcomes for BBE, HBE, and WBE measures require correspondingly separate injunctive boundaries.

## C. Decision-material litigation positions

- **Below:** No strong evidentiary foundation for the race and ethnic programs. Bidder-to-award rates did not consistently disfavor covered firms; many contract-dollar disparities diminished when firm size was accounted for. Women's program also lacked sufficiently persuasive substantiation. The permanent injunction stood.
- **Petitioners:** Statistical comparisons, income disparities, contracting history, and testimonial evidence show barriers that naive bidder comparisons miss, particularly because preferences already in effect could make remaining disparities appear small. County government can correct exclusion in its own procurement market.
- **Respondents:** The government must prove more than historical unfairness or national disparity; size-adjusted data do not show general County discrimination sufficient to give legally consequential preferences to every favored contractor. Individual nonbeneficiaries lose contract opportunities.
- **Limit:** A numerical goal may operate as a real set-aside, and a neutral-sounding label does not determine burden. Conversely, reducing observed discrimination through years of a corrective program can affect what current raw statistics show.

## D. Lawful paths and uncertainty

1. Affirm all programs' invalidation under *Croson*, *Adarand*, and intermediate sex scrutiny, accepting trial findings absent clear error.
2. Reconsider *Croson*'s rule for a properly supported local remedial classification, openly stating and defending any doctrinal change; if a modified rule is adopted, vacate racial components and remand for actual evidence, corrective fit and nonbeneficiaries' opportunities. This would not follow automatically from the distinct federal *Adarand* holding.
3. Any remand must avoid presuming findings absent from the record, automatically reinstating the County's entire scheme, or treating evidence sufficient for one target group as sufficient for all.
