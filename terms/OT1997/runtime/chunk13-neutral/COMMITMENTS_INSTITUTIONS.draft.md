## 154. Engineering Contractors Association v. Metropolitan Dade County

### Components and shared legal premises

**E1 — jurisdiction, standing and review.** Exercise §1254(1) review of the final appellate judgment. The six associations' regularly bidding members and the stipulated likely future unequal exclusion establish equal-competition injury for each of the BBE, HBE and WBE programs, germaneness and the suitability of common prospective relief. A stipulation supplies facts, not jurisdiction. No lost award need be proved. The common-predicate challenge reaches each program without proving injury from every possible contract measure; it does not authorize every distinct hypothetical operational challenge. Subsidiary findings receive clear-error review; constitutional sufficiency, scrutiny and fit are independently reviewed. All eight Associates accept E1.

**E2 — racial and ethnic standards.** Current Adarand's common substantial-justification standard governs local race/ethnicity classifications: an important objective within lawful authority, a substantial, evidence-supported relationship, justified coverage and burdens, and a justified response to concrete, practicable alternatives. Croson's retained evidentiary demands and current Hopwood's fitted-repair principle apply within it. General social inequality, numerical imbalance or good intention alone is insufficient; a supported local system of exclusion in which the County participates may justify fitted repair without a fresh prior judgment finding liability. All eight apply that standard here as governing precedent; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas maintain their preference for strict scrutiny, which these programs would also fail. Scalia reserves his narrower view of permissible racial remediation beyond the grounds necessary to reject these ordinances.

**E3 — BBE/HBE application.** The selected common ground is independent failure of the alternatives requirement: the County did not seriously assess reasonably available neutral measures addressing its identified procurement, information, financing, bonding, payment and discriminatory-treatment problems. Limited technical/financial assistance and conclusory dismissal of neutral measures do not establish considered inadequacy; the HBE record expressly contains no such consideration. This requires genuine consideration and a supported explanation, not an obligation to try and exhaust every conceivable neutral alternative. Flexibility, waivers and review periods do not supply that missing inquiry. All eight affirm invalidation and the injunction against the challenged BBE/HBE programs on this ground. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas additionally find the evidence inadequate after independent constitutional review. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reserve that additional issue, including the weight of existing-program effects and combined local testimony, because the tailoring ground is sufficient.

**E4 — WBE standard and application.** Actual important objective and substantial relationship govern, with the government's demanding burden; intermediate scrutiny does not become the racial-classification standard, a least-restrictive-means test, or a compulsory neutral-alternatives exhaustion rule. Neither exact numerical parity nor a fixed statistical-significance threshold is constitutional law. Relevant anecdotal proof may establish actual disadvantage without a categorical numerical prerequisite. All eight accept this framework. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas independently find this record insufficient to justify the full three-sector program and affirm. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the WBE affirmance and remand for independent intermediate-scrutiny assessment of the combined evidence, retaining supported subsidiary findings. Their remand neither finds the program valid nor directs its immediate reinstatement.

**E5 — remedy and preserved questions.** Affirmance of BBE/HBE concerns these programs on this record, not a ban on all future corrective measures. The operative programs and stipulated likely future exclusion, not past injury alone, support confined prospective relief. Current Williams's separate preventive-remedy requirement is therefore satisfied without importing its FECA limitations holding. No absolute ban on local affirmative action, mathematical-parity requirement or change to Adarand's standard follows. The four WBE affirmers leave its existing injunction in place on the inadequate-predicate ground; they do not revive the district court's rejected strict-tailoring analysis. The four WBE vacaters would return the WBE declaration and permanent injunction for prompt reconsideration under the corrected legal inquiry, with any necessary interim restraint separately assessed below; they do not automatically authorize operation during remand. No damages, identified contract award, individual-discrimination liability, new program or unreported procedure is adjudicated.

### Individual conclusions

**Stevens.** Accept E1–E2; affirm BBE and HBE on E3's neutral-alternatives defect, reserving the independent evidence issue; vacate and remand WBE under E4; accept E5 in the corresponding scope. His join in the current Adarand opinion adopts the common standard for federal, state and local classifications, retaining Croson's demand for relevant evidence; his joins in Hopwood I and III accept that standard's supported wrong and fitted repair. Those positions foreclose treating a remedial aim alone as justification. His joins in Kelley II, Klinger and Virginia adopt independent intermediate scrutiny directed to actual opportunity, not an automatic statistical entitlement. For the WBE question he gives weight to the significant entry disparity, surviving SIC15 disparity and accounts of refusing to deal with female owners; the appellate court's explicit reliance on whether a factfinder's ultimate constitutional view was merely permissible does not resolve their combined legal sufficiency. The strongest contrary path is affirmance because the trial judge did assess the whole record and most size-adjusted comparisons favored or did not disadvantage women. He accepts those subsidiary findings but regards the legal conclusion as requiring a distinct intermediate assessment; he does not find present constitutional sufficiency himself. On race, the same contextual objection cannot cure the independent failure seriously to assess neutral responses. Join barriers: no categorical invalidity of benign local classifications, no general adverse inference from absence of numerical disparity, and no approval of the entire WBE scheme or automatic reinstatement.

**O'Connor.** Accept E1–E2; affirm BBE/HBE on both E3 grounds; affirm WBE under E4; accept E5. Her current Hopwood opinion requires a demonstrated remedial link and fit, while her Kelley, Klinger and Virginia opinions require an actual important purpose and meaningful relationship for a sex classification without converting the tier. She independently considers the combined statistical and testimonial record: size controls largely explain award-dollar differences, the surviving results vary across sector and year, the subcontract denominator and general marketplace samples are not reliable procurement comparisons, and limited WBE incidents plus an entry disparity do not adequately explain this program's three-sector allocation. She does not defer to the ultimate legal conclusion merely because it is plausible. The strongest remand path invokes possible masking by existing BBE preferences and the lower evidentiary demand for WBE; she accepts both considerations in principle but finds no supported inference sufficient for these programs after considering the actual evidence as a whole. Her racial conclusion is additionally and independently secured by the unexamined neutral remedies; her WBE conclusion is not. Join barriers: no universal statistical minimum, no requirement of a formal prior discrimination judgment, no strict scrutiny for sex, no categorical duty to exhaust alternatives, and no rejection of the WBE fit determination merely because it permits preferences. A narrower racial-tailoring-only opinion would also receive her join.

**Scalia.** Accept E1 and the applicable E2 standard, maintaining his preference for strict scrutiny; affirm both racial programs on E3's independently sufficient grounds; affirm WBE under E4; accept E5. His actual joins in current Hopwood I, Kelley II and Klinger bind his analysis to the separate racial and sex standards. His refusal to join Hopwood III prevents attributing to him that opinion's entire affirmative remedial account; rejection of these unsupported and insufficiently fitted classifications requires no such extension. His join in current Cohen's independent sex-classification review likewise requires actual need, rather than preference justified by the beneficiary category alone. The strongest opposing path is a remand correcting the appellate court's legal-review terminology. He independently evaluates the accepted findings and finds the same result: scattered residual disparities, ambiguous comparisons and relatively little WBE anecdotal evidence do not substantiate this program-wide allocation, and the racial alternatives defect remains independently fatal. He would join a disposition confined to that racial defect; he would not demand a holding forbidding every remedial race classification as the price of doing so. Join barriers: no entitlement from aggregate imbalance, no equation of sex and race scrutiny, no adoption of all lower-court rhetoric about preferences as medicine, and no blanket validity determination for hypothetical future programs.

**Kennedy.** Accept E1–E2; affirm BBE/HBE on both E3 grounds and WBE on E4; accept E5. His current Hopwood I join insists on actual individual competitive equality, and his III join permits supported, causally limited repair while refusing general social-discrimination justification. His judgment-only position on Hopwood II is not an affirmative diversity-interest endorsement and supplies no contracting interest here. His joins in Kelley, Klinger and Virginia retain a real intermediate inquiry for WBE. The compelling counterpath is to vacate for independent evaluation of the cumulative evidence, especially where firm size might partly reflect earlier exclusion. He regards that possibility as relevant but not a demonstrated correction to the actual regressions or broad enough evidence to justify present three-sector preferences; he reaches that constitutional assessment independently. The lack of serious neutral-option consideration separately resolves both racial programs. He would accept a narrower E3 opinion leaving the racial evidentiary dispute open, and an E4 opinion that emphasizes the absence of an adequate combined showing rather than numerical benchmarks. Join barriers: no general racial-balance interest, no inference that remedial intent removes the individual burden, no heightened sex tier or least-restrictive-means duty, and no injunction purporting to preapprove or prohibit unexamined future schemes.

**Souter.** Accept E1–E2; affirm BBE/HBE on the alternatives ground alone under E3, reserving evidentiary sufficiency; vacate/remand WBE under E4; accept E5 with that remedy. His Brown Group opinion and Northeastern Florida join support concrete competitive injury and lawful associational enforcement. His joins in current Adarand and Hopwood apply one standard that still weighs each setting's authority and evidence, while accepting a supported local remedial objective. His Kelley, Klinger and Virginia joins require review at the level of the actual opportunity and purpose. He would not resolve the disputed racial evidentiary question unnecessarily where a distinct conceded omission defeats the alternatives requirement. For WBE, accepted regression findings do not themselves answer whether entry barriers plus actual reports of sex exclusion establish an important remedial objective; the appellate opinion's permissibility-of-factfinding formulation leaves that legal assessment insufficiently performed. The strongest affirmance argument is that mixed statistics and a small number of women's accounts are too weak even under intermediate scrutiny. His response is a bounded remand, not a finding of sufficient evidence or permission to assume every account true. Join barriers: no judicial invention of counterfactual no-program data, no independent constitutional cutoff at two standard deviations or an 80% ratio, no inference of invalidity from the failure to meet racial proof demands, and no reopening of supported subsidiary findings merely to reach a desired result.

**Thomas.** Accept E1–E2's applicable standard, maintaining his preference for strict scrutiny; affirm BBE/HBE on both E3 grounds; affirm WBE under E4; accept E5. His current Northeastern Florida opinion makes equal competitive opportunity an individual injury; his Hopwood I and III joins require concrete justification and fitted repair rather than a generalized societal premise. His Kelley and Klinger joins adopt intermediate scrutiny for an actual sex classification; his nonparticipation in Virginia is not treated as a vote either way. He finds the adjusted data and accepted evidentiary weaknesses insufficient to demonstrate the justification for these operative preferences, with genuine consideration of neutral methods independently absent for race/ethnicity. The strongest opposing path gives cumulative force to business-entry disparity, contractor testimony and possible masking. He accepts that those forms of evidence can matter, but will not translate a possibility, beneficiary perception or broad entry imbalance into the necessary showing for every covered sector. He independently reaches the legal result, rather than adopting clear-error review of constitutionality. Join barriers: no categorical statistical-only rule, no equation of private discrimination with proven County responsibility without an actual remedial connection, no sex strict scrutiny, no change to Adarand's common standard, and no requirement that a numerical goal precisely equal availability. Narrower racial-tailoring reasoning is joinable.

**Ginsburg.** Accept E1–E2; affirm BBE/HBE on the E3 tailoring ground while reserving the disputed evidence; vacate/remand WBE under E4; accept E5. Her current Adarand and Hopwood I/III joins permit meaningful remedial action but require supported local fit. Her joins in Kelley, Klinger and Virginia treat actual sex-linked opportunity as the constitutional subject and distinguish intermediate scrutiny from a demand for the least restrictive device. She takes seriously that firm size is itself potentially affected by entry barriers; a regression controlling size answers a different question from whether women have been excluded from acquiring the capacity to compete. The actual record, however, does not prove every causal step or a right to the entire scheme. Thus the strongest contrary argument—women's favorable results in some sectors and the trial court's actual consideration of the whole record—limits her to remand for independent assessment of the combined predicate and program scope. It does not support a judicial finding that all three sectors are justified. On race she accepts the independent defect that the County bypassed practical neutral responses without serious analysis. Join barriers: no treatment of women as presumptively incapable or uniformly disadvantaged, no use of strict racial evidentiary demands as the sex standard, no categorical numerical prerequisite, and no automatic restoration of WBE preferences while lawful relief is reconsidered.

**Breyer.** Accept E1–E2; affirm BBE/HBE on the E3 tailoring ground, reserving evidentiary sufficiency; vacate/remand WBE under E4; accept E5. His joins in current Adarand, Hopwood, Kelley, Klinger and Virginia require context-sensitive but actual proof, preserving the different scrutiny standards. He accepts the expert-supported reasons to discount unqualified denominators and account for firm size; he does not infer that the remaining data prove either no discrimination or complete justification. The entry study, remaining WBE SIC15 disparity, reported discriminatory dealings and differing sectors call for an independent assessment of what limited remedial objective and scope the combined record can support. The strongest affirmance path stresses that the district judge already heard all of it and found it weak. He preserves those subsidiary findings but rejects treating the ultimate constitutional choice as conclusive if merely plausible. A remand is his limited response, with no invented statistical adjustment or commanded preference. Serious assessment of neutral procurement reforms remains independently missing for BBE/HBE, so he does not extend that remand to the racial judgments. Join barriers: no exact-parity or statistical-threshold mandate, no assumption that every general market disparity identifies County discrimination, and no finding that the women's program is valid in advance of the corrected inquiry.

## 155. EEOC v. Johnson & Higgins, Inc.

### Components and shared legal premises

**J1 — independent enforcement and actual conciliation.** Section 626(b), with its incorporated public-enforcement provisions, authorizes EEOC action without an individual charge, consent or desired private recovery. Section 626(d)'s charge/wait requirement concerns an individual's action; §216(b)'s party-plaintiff consent does not govern the Commission. Section 626(f)(4) expressly prevents a private waiver from disabling EEOC enforcement even if the waiver is valid. The actual meetings, determination, invitations and renewed information requests satisfy the preaction attempt at informal conciliation, conference and persuasion in this case. Company resistance informs that assessment but categorical disagreement alone is not a general excuse to skip the attempt. All eight accept J1. The question does not decide individual damages, release validity, double recovery or an automatic duty to award monetary relief to unwilling persons.

**J2 — classification rule.** Section 630(f)'s circular employee term calls for ordinary common-law agency and the whole actual relationship, consistently with current Darden. Corporate form, a party's label, ownership, director title, profit-based payment or the lower opinion's three selected factors alone is not decisive. Actual control, ability to direct/remove the worker, operational duties, supervision, governance, profit and loss, and the other relevant incidents must be considered. A person may be both a director/owner and an employee; a genuine proprietor without an employment relationship does not become protected merely by working full time. All eight accept this rule. Importing the FLSA's broader suffer-or-permit definition from remedial incorporation would be error.

**J3 — application and judgment.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm employee status on the complete undisputed relationship, rejecting any exclusive corporate-form or three-factor rationale: these individuals continue ordinary operational employment under actual evaluation, compensation and removal controls exercised by other senior actors, while their collective ownership and governance do not eliminate that relationship. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate the classification/liability ruling and resulting injunction for full common-law assessment that gives substantial ownership and collective control their proper weight. They do not direct judgment that all directors are proprietors or hold that owner-employees are categorically outside the Act. No new record fact or automatic evidentiary hearing is ordered by either path.

**J4 — age trigger and defenses.** For a covered employee, compulsory retirement at age 62, or at age 60 with fifteen board years, is age-based even if succession planning is a reasonable aim. The latter alternative's corporate-need exception permits only year-by-year extension through the end of the year age 62 is attained; it does not remove age as the operative trigger. Hazen Paper distinguishes years of service actually independent of age; it supplies no defense to these explicit age conditions. Section 623(f)(1)'s reasonable-factor-other-than-age defense is unavailable for the age trigger itself. No bona fide occupational qualification, foreign-law conflict or qualifying benefit/seniority-plan defense is established. Section 631(c) requires age 65 or older, bona fide executive/high-policy service for the two immediately preceding years, and an immediate nonforfeitable aggregate annual benefit of at least $44,000 computed with the prescribed annuity-equivalence exclusions and adjustment; it is not a general executive exemption. This policy fails the age requirement regardless of unresolved benefit amounts. All eight accept those statutory propositions. The five coverage affirmers apply them to affirm liability; the three coverage vacaters join the conditional rule but withhold an affirmative liability holding until J3 is resolved.

**J5 — equitable scope and posture.** Supreme Court review under §1254(1) concerns a §1292(a)(1) appellate injunction judgment following partial summary judgment on liability, not a final award of damages. The five affirmers sustain the described injunction against this unlawful employment-retirement policy, incoming employee agreements to it, and future unlawful age retirement, including rescission and submission of a compliant policy on a date to be set below. The maintained policy and the company's asserted entitlement to continue it supply the concrete preventive basis; completed past acts alone would not. That application is consistent with current Williams's independent prospective-remedy requirement, without adopting any FECA tolling rule for the ADEA. The order does not require continued directorship or equity ownership if separately and lawfully arranged, and it leaves monetary relief for further proceedings. They find no established Rule 65(d) defect in the described prohibitions and identified policy; a generalized duty to obey all employment law is not adopted. The three coverage vacaters would vacate those dependent commands pending lawful classification, not invalidate the Commission's authority or direct a monetary remedy. All eight preserve claim-specific compensation, willfulness, permissible waiver effects on individual relief, and any separate lawful governance decisions. No fixed new deadline, damages amount or reinstatement follows here.

### Individual conclusions

**Stevens.** Join J1–J2; affirm status and liability under J3–J4 and the bounded J5 injunction. His actual Darden join accepts common-law classification; his Town & Country, Walters and Robinson joins reject labels as substitutes for the actual relationship and preserve statutory context. Here continued managerial jobs, annual review by six senior directors, compensation decisions and removal by other board members demonstrate an employment relationship alongside ownership. The strongest opposite path is Jacobs's proprietor analysis: collective owners choose their own succession arrangement, receive profits and possess ultimate corporate authority. Stevens gives those facts weight, but does not treat collective governance rights as eliminating the distinct daily relationship or private assent as repealing the ADEA. His Gilmer dissent supports the public judicial enforcement function, although it opposed the arbitration disposition; he did not join General Telephone's majority, whose governing public-enforcement proposition remains independently relevant. His current Oubre majority join reinforces the statutory distinction between private bargains and express safeguards; J1 rests more narrowly on §626(f)(4), so no waiver-invalidity holding is needed. Join barriers: no corporate-form per se rule, no conversion of every partner into an employee, no automatic damages for unwilling individuals, and no order guaranteeing board tenure or equity.

**O'Connor.** Join J1–J2; affirm status/liability under J3–J4 and J5's limited injunction. Her Darden join requires all incidents, and her own Hazen Paper opinion separates actual age motivation from genuinely independent service criteria. These retirement alternatives expressly use age; the benign aim and unanimously adopted policy do not make age a different factor. Her current Oubre opinion treats Congress's waiver conditions as operative, while expressly keeping Commission enforcement and private monetary consequences distinct. The strongest remand argument is that the lower court gave too little significance to entrepreneurial attributes. She rejects its categorical phrasing but finds the undisputed operational service, review, compensation and removal structure sufficient when governance and equity are actually weighed, making a further classification remand unnecessary. Her Gilmer majority join recognizes independent EEOC enforcement notwithstanding an individual's arbitration arrangement, consistent with the present express text. Join barriers: no exclusive three-factor test, no compelled board membership, no inference of willfulness from facial illegality alone, no automatic damages or repayment, and no rule that mere company denial excuses required conciliation. A full common-law explanation, not corporate incorporation alone, is required for her merits join.

**Scalia.** Join J1–J2; vacate/remand J3's classification and dependent liability/injunction; join J4's conditional statutory rule and J5's remedial limits. His Darden, Town & Country and Walters joins require a real employment relationship and forbid replacing the whole inquiry with selected proxies. The directors' voting, merger/dissolution and appointment authority, ownership, profit compensation and mutually adopted departure terms are substantial incidents; they cannot be set aside because the enterprise incorporated or continued using employment terminology. The strongest affirmance argument is that each individual still works under senior managers' actual review and compensation control. He agrees those facts could establish dual status but finds the omission of meaningful proprietor analysis sufficient to require a corrected inquiry rather than classification by this Court. His Gilmer join supports independent EEOC authority. His Oubre dissent's tender requirement for a private plaintiff does not overcome §626(f)(4)'s express reservation of Commission power; no abandonment of that dissent is inferred. Join barriers: no categorical corporate-owner coverage, no blanket proprietor exclusion, no private-consent veto over enforcement, and no unconditional liability holding before status. He would join statutory rejection of the succession/RFOA argument expressly conditioned on coverage, and leaves all individual accounting questions open.

**Kennedy.** Join J1–J2; vacate/remand J3 and the dependent J5 injunction; join J4 conditionally. His Darden and Walters joins demand actual agency status, and Town & Country permits compatible roles without requiring every claimed dual role to count as employment. He regards ownership and the power collectively to control corporate life, officers, compensation and continued membership as facts the lower court must assess as part of the actual relationship, not merely as a formal label. The best affirmance path stresses the working hierarchy: these directors are individually evaluated, paid and removable by others. He does not dismiss that showing and therefore rejects outright judgment for the company; the conflict between employee supervision and substantial governing authority requires a full legal classification below. His Gilmer majority join and current Oubre majority join preserve public enforcement independently of private contractual preferences, but neither establishes employee status. Hazen Paper's rule, which he joined, defeats calling the actual age trigger a factor other than age if coverage is established. Join barriers: no inference of proprietorship solely from board title or equity, no omission of actual supervision, no general corporate self-governance immunity, and no injunction compelling ownership retention. His remand does not command a new trial where the existing record permits lawful assessment.

**Souter.** Join J1–J2; affirm J3–J4 and the bounded J5 injunction. His own Darden opinion supplies the all-incidents rule; his Walters and Robinson opinions insist on a genuine relationship and statutory context rather than a payroll or present-tense shortcut. He expressly weighs substantial equity and governing powers but finds the continued full-time operational work under performance review, individualized compensation and removal controls establishes employment alongside collective governance on this undisputed record. The strongest opposing path says those controls are the proprietors' internal organization and the appellate court's entrepreneur dismissal masks the decisive issue. He rejects that dismissal as a general rule, but finds the complete described relationship sufficient without directing a second hearing merely to restate undisputed incidents. His Gilmer join and Oubre majority join support the separate agency/private-remedy distinction, while §626(b) and (f)(4) provide the immediate statutory authority. Join barriers: no three-factor exclusive test, no corporate-form conclusion, no assumption that all working owners qualify, no displacement of actual conciliation, and no automatic relief to every person covered by the challenged policy. J5's employment-only limitation is essential.

**Thomas.** Join J1–J2; vacate/remand J3 and dependent liability/injunction; join J4's conditional rule and J5's limits. His Darden join applies the ordinary common-law meaning despite a broad remedial purpose, and his Town & Country and Walters joins preserve actual control and enacted limits. Substantial owner control of governance and mutual succession terms cannot be made legally insignificant because the workers also perform managerial tasks. The strongest affirmance position correctly notes real evaluation, performance-based compensation and individual removal power. He would permit a finding of employment based on that complete relationship but will not adopt it through the lower court's narrowed approach; nor would he declare proprietorship conclusively. His separate current Oubre dissent preserves background contract doctrines where Congress has not displaced them; §626(f)(4) is the express displacement as to Commission enforcement, so he joins J1 without repudiating his private-ratification/tender position. The statute's age-65 condition and words excluding age from the RFOA defense require J4 once employee status exists. Join barriers: no substitution of remedial purpose for statutory coverage, no categorical owner immunity, no waiver adjudication unnecessary to public enforcement, and no damages/board-retention order from the interlocutory record. Any later remedy must follow a properly established employment violation.

**Ginsburg.** Join J1–J2; affirm J3–J4 and the bounded J5 injunction. Her own current Town & Country opinion treats compatible roles as consistent with actual employee service while independently testing statutory boundaries; it does not categorically answer proprietor status. Her Walters and Robinson joins likewise focus on the real relationship. Applied here, meaningful senior review, compensation decisions, required operational duties and removal by other directors coexist with equity and voting rights; the former are sufficient to preserve protected employment rather than disappearing upon elevation to the board. The strongest contrary argument is that profit-sharing owners govern themselves collectively and voluntarily accept the succession term. She treats that as relevant but not a basis to extinguish the actual employment relation or public prohibition. Her Oubre majority join supports fidelity to express statutory safeguards and the separate public-enforcement clause, without presuming a particular private damages entitlement. Join barriers: no assumption that all partners or shareholders are employees, no FLSA-definition transplant, no mandatory owner consent, no automatic damages, and no injunction maintaining board membership or shares. The full common-law explanation and employment-only scope answer her principal concerns.

**Breyer.** Join J1–J2; affirm J3–J4 and J5 in its employment-only scope. His actual Town & Country, Walters and Robinson joins support an assessment of how work and control function, not a title-driven exemption. He weighs the size and structure of the board, senior performance review, compensation machinery, full-time operational duties and actual removal power together with equity and governance rights. The resulting dual relationship supports the present coverage determination. The strongest contrary path would remand because the lower majority dismissed entrepreneurial features too readily; he rejects its dismissive formulation but finds the necessary actual incidents undisputed and adequate for decision, so remand would not identify an unresolved material fact. His current Oubre concurrence distinguishes a protected claim from possible independently supported accounting and reciprocal-contract consequences; correspondingly, J1 does not prejudge monetary relief or all effects of private agreements. Join barriers: no corporate-form per se rule, no fixed hierarchy factor that decides every closely held enterprise, no equation of a reasonable succession aim with an other-than-age criterion, and no lifetime governance or automatic compensation order. The actual conciliation effort, not a blanket futility exception, is required for his enforcement join.
