## Material source additions and corrections

The challenged use is confined to construction SIC 15 (general building), 16 (heavy construction), and 17 (specialty trades), on County-funded contracts over $25,000. The 15%, 19% and 11% goals concern BBE, HBE and WBE respectively. A program participant ordinarily must qualify as a small business, but a larger participant may retain certification upon showing it continues to experience the discrimination addressed. Actual local business, covered ownership and control remain required.

Measures differ: set-asides ordinarily require three available covered firms; competitive bidding can be waived with two if neither received a like County contract in three years and price analysis establishes a competitive price. Subcontract goals vary by contract; a waiver is available for unavailability at a competitive price, but inability to obtain bonding is not a waiver basis. Project goals use a pool of covered subcontractors. Bid preferences reduce the comparison price by up to 10% without reducing the actual paid price; selection factors can boost nonprice evaluation by up to 10%. Annual efficacy review and five-year continuation review are reported. These qualifications must not be replaced by a generic quota description.

The associations' regular competing members and stipulated likely future exclusion cover all three programs. Parties stipulated relevant facts, not jurisdiction. Northeastern Florida and current Adarand supply unequal-opportunity injury without proving a lost award. A challenge to the programs' common justification differs from an unsupported challenge to a discrete measure's particular operation.

The complete opinion separately assesses five statistical categories and anecdotal proof. The main County-contract studies compare 1989–91 and 1993; 1992 was omitted because Hurricane Andrew distorted expenditure. Firm-size regressions using both total value bid and largest contract awarded generally remove disparities. An adverse BBE SIC15 disparity remains for 1989–91 but not 1993; HBE SIC17 (and under one method SIC15) remains for 1989–91 but not 1993; WBE SIC15 remains for 1993. The aggregate BBE 1989–91 result remains adverse, while 1993 does not. Sector disaggregation has expert support, and the trial court discounted rather than prohibited aggregate evidence.

Subcontract statistics include firms' total receipts from outside the County, undermining their denominator. Marketplace survey data include licensed firms beyond the willing/able/qualified procurement pool; after firm-size control no statistically significant BBE/WBE disparities remain, but HBE disparities remain in SIC15, SIC17 and the aggregate. Wainwright finds lower business-entry rates for all groups, statistically significant for Black owners and women, and a significant Black-owner income disparity of 72.2%; the latter study lacks a direct firm-size control and conflicts with other regressions. Brimmer finds Black-business receipt disparities in 1977 and 1987, but not 1982; County attributes 1982 to Metrorail preferences, without data removing that influence. The existing BBE program may mask discrimination; the trial court acknowledged that possibility rather than ignoring it. Its significance cannot be supplied by inventing a counterfactual data set.

Anecdotal evidence includes two County administrators, 23 contractors (most through sworn declarations), and interviews of 78 certified Black-owned firms. It is chiefly BBE, some WBE and none HBE. It describes alleged discriminatory treatment, financing/bonding barriers, disparate punch lists, refusal to deal with Black/female owners, bid shopping, late invitations and replacement. It is not a finding that every reported incident occurred or that no discrimination exists. The appellate court leaves open an exceptional case in which nonstatistical evidence alone suffices; it creates no categorical statistical prerequisite.

The racial programs have an independent tailoring ground below: no serious good-faith assessment of available neutral alternatives. The HBE record expressly lacks consideration; the BBE consideration is conclusory, with limited assistance unevaluated and no reported responsive discipline or local anti-discrimination measures. Available procurement, information, bonding, credit and payment reforms are identified. The lower opinion does not require mathematical parity: it rejects the district court's criticism of a 19% Hispanic goal below estimated Hispanic availability.

**Critical WBE distinction:** the Eleventh Circuit rejected the district court's importation of strict racial tailoring into the women's program. It found the WBE program sufficiently flexible, including waivers, to satisfy substantial relationship if the evidentiary predicate existed. WBE invalidation rests on inadequate evidence, not failure to consider neutral alternatives or an excessive 11% goal. The women's component must be separately decided.

## Governing law and unresolved modeling choices

Current Adarand adopts a common substantial-justification standard for federal, state and local racial classifications in Stone's opinion for the Court, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in its vacatur and remand, preferring strict scrutiny. It displaces Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation while retaining Croson's demand for relevant evidence. Current Hopwood Part I applies that standard to state racial classifications with all nine joining, while Part III requires supported wrong and fitted repair without invariably demanding a prior formal adjudication. The standard governs Dade County's programs directly; its application to this record remains for decision.

Current Kelley and Klinger require a genuine important objective and substantial relationship for actual sex classifications. Virginia expressly preserves demanding intermediate scrutiny, with exceedingly persuasive justification describing the burden rather than strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. No numerical minimum, fixed significance threshold or automatic decisive statistical proxy has been adopted by the Supreme Court in these materials.

Modeling must distinguish ordinary clear-error review of subsidiary findings from independent review of constitutional sufficiency and fit. Supported paths are affirmance on evidentiary and/or independent racial tailoring grounds, a bounded remand for legally proper totality assessment, and separate treatment of WBE where strict-tailoring grounds cannot sustain the injunction. A remand would neither reinstall the entire scheme nor establish discrimination. The strongest counterargument to affirmance is that fragmented statistical assessment and excessive deference to the ultimate constitutional conclusion undervalue combined local evidence and existing-program effects. The strongest counterargument to remand is that the trial court did consider the whole record and the race/ethnicity alternative ground independently supports its judgment.
