## Material source additions and corrections

**Posture correction:** this is review of an interlocutory injunction under §1292(a)(1), following partial summary judgment on liability. The district court reserved monetary relief. The Second Circuit affirmed and returned the cause for determination of additional relief, if any. Section 1254(1) permits Supreme Court review; no final damages judgment or damages entitlement is established. Do not preserve the imported final-judgment label.

The corporation's board contains 20–44 directors, with 35 when the described investigation began. Nomination by the chairman and election by two thirds of the board select existing officers/senior managers. Each new director tenders a resignation effective at two-thirds-board pleasure. Directors continue full-time managerial duties, receive annual reviews by six senior board members, and receive a profit percentage determined annually through the compensation committee and performance reviews. Their equity and governance powers are substantial, not incidental; no corporate-form label alone decides employment status.

Each director must own at least 500 shares; cessation terminates the directorship. Departure requires sale of shares, with the Ten-Year Contract promising dividend-equivalent payments for ten years in return for consulting availability and noncompetition. Stock is predominantly owned by directors; a small fraction has been issued to other key employees since 1993. These terms support the company's proprietor argument and do not erase actual employee work by stipulation.

**Exact retirement alternatives:** retirement occurs at the end of the year in which age 62 is attained, or, if earlier, the end of the year in which age 60 and fifteen years' board service are attained. In the latter case only, corporate need found by the chairman and approved by the executive committee permits year-by-year continuation up to, but not beyond, the end of the year age 62 is attained. Retirement ends directorship, stock ownership, officer status and employment together. The policy was unanimously adopted in 1983. The service condition does not make the age trigger age-neutral.

EEOC learned the policy during investigation of a former director's separate charge. No current/former director complains of this policy; affidavits oppose the suit. The source's preliminary description loosely dates the present action to 1992, but its detailed chronology identifies investigation notice May 21, 1992 and **filing August 5, 1993**. Use the detailed filing chronology; no missing 1992 suit is invented. Determination, July 1992 meeting/invitation, September/December information requests and company refusals establish the actual prefiling conciliation efforts. Categorical denial alone should not become a general excuse to omit statutory conciliation: the actual series must support sufficiency.

The claimed waivers lack stated ADEA-specific and seven-day-revocation terms in the reported account. More basically, §626(f)(4) expressly preserves EEOC enforcement even assuming valid private waivers. Monetary consequences of a particular individual's waiver are not decided by that independent-enforcement rule.

Jacobs agrees the EEOC can bring an unrequested enforcement action. His disagreement concerns classification: broad ownership, voting, dissolution/merger, appointment and compensation authority may make these directors proprietor-employers regarding their inter se succession agreement. He argues the majority's three indicia (traditional duties, no other employment, reporting upwards) cannot displace the whole relationship. He would reverse, alternatively finding a material issue against summary judgment. His view is not a rejection of public enforcement or an RFOA holding.

The injunction bars enforcing the unlawful employee-retirement rule, requiring incoming employee-directors to agree to an unlawful age retirement, and retiring covered employees because of age; it requires rescission and submission of a compliant policy on a date to be set below. Reasons and detail are reported. The employment/directorship link may be severed; no continued board seat or equity ownership is compelled merely by employee status. No precise new deadline, compensation amount or reinstatement award follows from affirmance.

## Governing law and unresolved modeling choices

Current Darden adopts ordinary general common-law agency and all relevant relationship incidents, with no single decisive factor. The Court cannot adopt corporate form, a party's employee label, or an exclusive three-factor test as conclusive. Current Town & Country (Ginsburg for all nine), Walters (Souter for all nine), and Robinson (Souter for all nine) preserve actual relationship, compatible dual roles, statutory setting and express exclusions. The relevant distinction is genuine protected employment versus a proprietor's self-governance, not the abstract possibility of a director also being an employee.

Current Hazen Paper (O'Connor for all then participating Justices) distinguishes a real non-age factor from age motivation and an age proxy. A facial age trigger does not become a reasonable factor other than age because succession planning is reasonable. Section 631(c) requires age at least 65, two years immediately before retirement in a bona fide executive/high-policymaking position, and an immediate nonforfeitable annual retirement benefit of at least $44,000, subject to the statutory annuity-equivalence adjustment. It is not a blanket executive exemption, and the policy's ages 60/62 cannot satisfy its age condition.

Current Oubre, January 26, 1998, was read from the neutral projection. Its seven-vote mandatory-waiver rule and Scalia/Thomas's different common-law reservations must be preserved. This case's independent Commission power follows express §626(f)(4), even on a compliant-waiver assumption; no imported Oubre disagreement defeats that text. Sections 626(b), 216(c), 217 and predivergence General Telephone and Gilmer supply independent public enforcement, with the actual conciliation condition retained. Further verification of the latter primary texts is available if needed.
