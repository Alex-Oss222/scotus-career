# Air Line Pilots Association v. Miller — independent non-Stone commitments

**Event:** No. 97-428; merits; May 26, 1998. Argument March 23, 1998. Review of the D.C. Circuit, 108 F.3d 1415, through 28 U.S.C. §1254(1).

## Questions and common limits

The question is compulsory exhaustion of the offered impartial fee arbitration by nonmembers who did not agree to arbitrate. It is not the ultimate chargeability of an expenditure, the audit's professional adequacy, discovery scope, fee amount, or whether Hudson governs every NLRA arrangement. The litigants either declined arbitration or participated under protest. Their appearance does not supply contractual assent. The 67 arbitration participants who did not sue are not added to this judgment.

All eight accept that the presently asserted obligation has no express statutory exhaustion command; an exhaustion obligation, if justified, would be judicial rather than a term assented to by these pilots. They distinguish binding contractual arbitration from nonbinding preliminary exhaustion. No Justice treats the arbitrator's award as entitled to contractual finality against these plaintiffs. Hudson's requirement is a prompt opportunity before an impartial decisionmaker, not arbitration in every case; its footnote 21 rejects preclusion in subsequent §1983 litigation. The applicability of Hudson's protections to this RLA arrangement is the presented premise rather than a newly adjudicated universal private-union rule.

## Competing lawful paths

**Independent judicial access:** Affirm the rejection of compulsory arbitration. Hudson protects objectors and does not itself require them to use the offered procedure. First Options' antecedent-assent principle excludes a contractual basis, while McCarthy requires a separate assessment of any judicial exhaustion ground. Here an outsider adjudicates rather than completing a statutory agency or plan's authoritative internal decision; no congressional allocation gives that decision deference. Useful expertise and possible settlement do not justify a mandatory additional adjudication before the independent judicial proceeding these employees retain. The strongest objection is that useful nonbinding exhaustion need neither contractual assent nor preclusion. This path answers that objection by the particular source, institutional fit, and duplication at issue, not by declaring every nonconsensual exhaustion requirement unlawful.

**Protective procedure first:** Reverse the categorical rejection of exhaustion, while preserving independent judicial adjudication afterward. Hudson itself calls for a prompt impartial process; a reasonable requirement to use a compliant process can identify disputed expenses, permit error correction and avoid unnecessary judicial disputes even when its result has no preclusive force. McCarthy permits sound judicial discretion without a statutory command. The strongest objection is that an option established to protect unwilling payers becomes an additional burden on their access to court, without an authoritative administrative decision to complete. This path answers that the initial process is confined to the particular prompt, impartial fee procedure and may eliminate or narrow disputes; it does not authorize a union to impose any private procedure it chooses, make arbitration binding, or evade judicial review. Actual compliance and practical adequacy remain indispensable.

# Calderon v. Ashmus — independent non-Stone commitments

**Event:** No. 97-391; May 26, 1998; argued March 24, 1998. Section 1254(1) review of the Ninth Circuit's §1292(a)(1) preliminary-injunction judgment, 123 F.3d 1199.

## Disposition and scope

All eight Associates would reverse the appellate judgment sustaining the injunction and remand with directions to remove the declaration and injunction resting on this standalone chapter-154-qualification claim and dismiss that claim for want of an Article III controversy. This is a jurisdictional disposition, not a determination that California qualifies. The remaining complaint claims were not resolved in the interlocutory appeal; their lawful jurisdictional treatment is for the district court, without an invented wholesale merits dismissal. The jurisdictional defect bars the challenged anti-assertion injunction and its declaratory premise. Neither the granted Eleventh Amendment issue nor the First Amendment objection needs decision.

The sufficient common rule is limited: prisoners may not use a standalone declaratory action to obtain an advance adjudication of whether the State may invoke a particular procedural regime in their eventual federal habeas actions, when the action seeks neither habeas relief nor another conclusive adjudication of the substantive legal relation at issue in those proceedings. That isolated procedural controversy must be addressed in a concrete habeas proceeding with its actual claims, relevant dates and requested relief. This does not make declaratory relief generally nonjudicial, require an executable coercive decree in every case, or hold that every anticipatory suit must await completed injury.

## Enhanced alternatives

**A. Jurisdictional dismissal.** Aetna requires a definite controversy susceptible to conclusive judicial relief; Coffman and Wycoff distinguish a concrete legal relation from an isolated issue whose significance lies in another proceeding. Chapter 154 qualification here is sought as a preliminary rule for future custody litigation, without those custody claims or a standalone legal entitlement conclusively presented. Declaratory Judgment Act §§2201–2202 do not expand Article III. Steel Co. requires deciding the disputed constitutional power before contested qualification merits.

The strongest objection is present hardship: the State's position and delays in appointing counsel can pressure prisoners to file deficient petitions or risk an asserted shorter limit. That is a serious practical problem, not imaginary injury. It nevertheless does not transform every disputed potential defense into a separate controversy suitable for advance adjudication. Qualification, the applicable limitations regime and appropriate protection can be contested within the actual habeas proceeding; no particular clock, denied amendment, default or lost claim is proved by the class descriptions. The holding does not rely on the lower opinion's mistaken suggestion that every late initial petition becomes successive.

**B. Present statutory-burden declaration.** An alternative would recognize a concrete relation between identified prisoners and officials asserting an immediate shorter filing regime; an effective declaration could remove that uncertainty. Seminole Part I confirms that a conclusive declaration may be judicial despite further proceedings, while Texas and Ohio Forestry preserve review of actual present burdens. This alternative fails here because it treats the procedural predicate of the habeas dispute as the complete legal relation, using the practical importance of a litigation issue to supply the missing controversy. Seminole adjudicated an existing statutory negotiation duty and activated defined consequences; the chapter-154 dispute asks which procedures another court will apply to custody claims not adjudicated here. No immediate-execution criterion is used.

**C. Reach immunity or speech.** If an independent actual controversy existed, the ongoing-violation and connected-officer predicates would still require separate analysis. Seminole's actual IGRA officer holding and Coeur d'Alene do not turn a disputed procedural litigation position automatically into an ongoing federal violation. Conversely, the State's characterization of the injunction as speech regulation would not automatically defeat a lawful decree. Neither issue supplies Article III power, and no Associate needs to decide either once path A is adopted.

# County of Sacramento v. Lewis — independent non-Stone commitments

**Event:** No. 96-1337; May 26, 1998; argued December 9, 1997. Section 1254(1) review of the Ninth Circuit, 98 F.3d 434; the underlying circuit appeal was from final summary judgment under §1291.

**Boundary:** Frozen independent commitments, no assignment or completed Court action. ## Record and reach

The presented claim concerns Smith's own pursuit and unintended collision, not a constitutional duty to protect the victim from private violence. Plaintiffs assert no Fourth or Eighth Amendment claim. Under Brower the absence of termination by intentionally applied means matters; an unsuccessful command to stop and an unintended collision do not establish a seizure. Graham and the actual Lanier specific-provision rule remain applicable when a specific provision does cover the conduct. Albright's actual seven-vote judgment supplies no new general displacement rationale.

The plaintiff-favorable account is retained: roughly seventy-five seconds over 1.3 miles, exceptional speed and inadequate following distance, residential roads and intersections, unhelmeted minors, danger to other travelers, no established dangerous-felony predicate, and alleged or conceded pursuit-policy violations. Smith did not intend the collision. No invented benevolent finding, purpose to injure, hearing of Stapp's command or known violent offender changes that account. Policy breaches may be evidence of disregard but do not themselves enact a federal constitutional rule or prove particularized federal notice.

The municipal judgments were affirmed below and no cross-petition reopens them; state dispositions were not appealed. The present result addresses only the reviewed federal individual claim. It orders no damages, trial, municipal retrial or state-law disposition.

## Tested paths

**A. Emergency-sensitive constitutional disposition.** The Due Process Clause protects against an official's arbitrary abuse of power, not every grave error causing bodily injury. For a rapidly evolving high-speed pursuit requiring decisions without a practical opportunity for considered deliberation, the conscience-shocking culpability necessary for this substantive-due-process claim is a purpose to cause harm unrelated to a legitimate law-enforcement objective. Recklessness or deliberate indifference to the pursuit's dangers does not alone meet that standard. Whitley's competing-obligation analysis supplies the emergency analogy; it did not itself decide civilian pursuit liability. Farmer's actual-awareness rule and Doe's supervisory rule address settings with a different duty and meaningful opportunity to respond. Their distinct operation is preserved.

The strongest objection is that this pursuit was discretionary and allegedly unnecessary from its beginning, and that declining or stopping it could have removed the danger. A law-enforcement label cannot establish good faith or categorically excuse deliberate official injury. Nevertheless, deciding whether to pursue and how to respond during this continuous seventy-five-second flight involves contemporaneous competing demands, rather than the practical opportunity for unhurried consideration that makes deliberate indifference adequate elsewhere. The absence of a serious original offense and the policy violations support an allegation of very bad judgment; without evidence of the requisite harmful purpose they do not supply this constitutional wrong. This is a classification of the supported encounter, not a factual finding that the officer correctly weighed its risks.

The rule does not cover a contrived encounter used to injure, a purposeful ramming, or every action that an official calls an emergency. Nor does it decide the precise culpability required for materially different conduct allowing actual deliberation. No exhaustive seconds-based test, universal intent requirement for all executive action, or universal sufficiency of deliberate indifference outside emergencies follows.

**B. Deliberate-indifference merits path.** The direct creation of an extreme risk, its apparent lack of necessity, and the opportunity to desist could support a constitutional inference under a serious actual-awareness rule. The direct-deprivation distinction in Pinder and Lanier prevents rejecting this path solely for lack of custody. But Farmer and Doe do not erase context: custodial safety and a supervisor's opportunity to address deliberate sexual abuse cannot simply furnish the culpability rule for an evolving chase. Canton concerns municipal attribution, not the underlying individual right. This path's possible validity for a deliberative encounter is reserved; it does not establish that this supported seventy-five-second pursuit satisfies the needed constitutional culpability.

**C. Immunity-only disposition.** Independently, the law on May 22, 1990 did not make this unintended pursuit collision's substantive constitutional unlawfulness sufficiently apparent. All relevant authorities must be considered, without demanding an identical pursuit case. Garner and Brower concern intentional force or intentionally applied stopping means; they do not supply an obvious rule for this nonseizure. Wood II concerns abandonment after taking control of a driver's vehicle and passenger's protective circumstances; Fargo's gross-negligence formulation was questioned and modified. Checki addresses intentional misuse of a police car to terrorize, materially different from the established pursuit purpose here. Roach and Jones rejected the actual claims before them; their recognition that some sufficiently egregious pursuit might be actionable does not identify this particular constitutional boundary. Daniels expressly reserved recklessness questions. Later Farmer, Doe, Collins and current decisions cannot retroactively furnish notice.

The strongest objection is that Wood II plus the prohibition on unjustified deadly force gave sufficiently particular warning when Smith knowingly created extreme danger for no established serious offense. The objection is substantial but does not remove the distinction between intentionally using lethal means and an unintended collision during a pursuit, nor the unsettled due-process culpability in that context. The contrary notice judgment cannot stand merely because injuries are severe or a departmental directive is violated. Uhlrig, Elder, Lanier and Smith v. Mattox supply the current method, not retroactive substantive rules. No exact-case or numerical-consensus requirement is imposed.

## Common legal premises

All eight accept the Compact's federal-law character and its distinct allocations. Article Third's wharf, fastened-vessel, quarantine/health/passenger-law and fisheries provisions retain their actual separate terms. The present land additions are artificial. No Justice converts the record into gradual natural accretion merely because filling took years or was federally undertaken. Georgia's application to mixed dredging and sedimentation was factual; Scalia and Kennedy's disagreement there did not reject the governing law. Property ownership, federal enclave jurisdiction, residual state functions and interstate sovereignty remain distinct. Under Illinois and Georgia, New York bears the preponderance burden on long, continuous possession and assertion of sovereignty over the actual disputed area and New Jersey's long acquiescence. No fixed universal number of years, formal-protest requirement, mandatory state-service checklist or categorical federal-enclave exemption is adopted. ## Enhanced component comparison and provisional conclusions

### 1. Compact: retain the original island, not an automatically expanding artificial enclave

**Supported rule and result:** All eight reject New York's claim that Article Second alone carries sovereignty to these artificial additions. Read together, Articles First–Third leave the underwater territory west of the midwater boundary in New Jersey while preserving New York's existing island enclave. The Compact supplies no agreement that adding fill to that enclave transfers the other State's territory. Ordinary artificial-change principles therefore operate; federal filling changes the physical shore without itself changing interstate sovereignty.

The shared sufficient ground is the whole Compact and the absence of a bargain displacing that consequence, not the assertion that the adjective “present” alone gives metes and bounds. That word supports retention of an existing jurisdictional object, but can also describe the jurisdiction's quality after the federal cession. The Master's federal-enclave explanation of it is not silently adopted as the only reading. The two routes cannot be merged into a fictitious common textual holding.

**Strongest competing path:** New York invokes the named island as an enduring entity, foreseeable reclamation, the lack of a size limit, the 1827 negotiations, existing filling powers and the Compact's navigational purpose. A reservation may sometimes encompass later change; artificial formation does not answer interpretation before the agreement is construed. But foreseeable filling elsewhere establishes no reciprocal agreement to relinquish sovereignty over this substrate. Article Second has real work preserving original islands on New Jersey's side of Article First, unlike the redundancy Kennedy identified in Georgia's differently worded natural-island reservation. The 1889 map and 1921 silence provide no express enlargement provision, and the 1921 agreement preserves the earlier allocation except where actually modified.

Whether the later hospital tracts were temporarily distinct islands need not independently decide the compact question. Pilings, gangways and submerged cribbing are not interchangeable with continuous dry land, but even attachment would not establish a transfer. Conversely, the case establishes no universal rule that every later connected feature is legally a new island. The continuing-island theory is rejected for this artificial enlargement under this Compact.

**Exception:** Overrule New York's compact-construction exception. This decides original entitlement only; prescription remains independent.

### 2. Prescription: a genuine disagreement over the complete record

**Five-Justice provisional conclusion:** O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg find New York has not carried the burden and would overrule its prescription exception. New York has real evidence of state functions directed to fill residents and facilities. The hospital-specific birth/death certificates, electoral records identifying residents of Islands 2 and 3, compensation treatment and particular construction records cannot be dismissed as merely generic island labels. Federal control also means ordinary police, taxation and service patterns cannot be mechanically demanded. Those points make this a close cumulative-evidence question.

The evidence nevertheless does not establish the requisite continuous adverse dominion and acquiescence over the additions. The identified electoral years are intermittent; registration and an actual vote are distinct, and the record's old map outlines complicate what territory the administration openly claimed. Specific hospital records prove genuine acts at identified locations, but sparse vital records are not themselves a continuing, unmistakable territorial assertion throughout the enlarged tract. Federal choice of wage scales and construction standards has varying significance; statutory or obligatory compensation treatment is stronger than a convenient benchmark, yet it cannot turn every federal choice into New York's exercise of sovereignty.

The contrary evidence must also be weighed precisely. The 1904 deed and recordation were assertions of New Jersey's proprietary interest with territorial implications under the construed Compact, not a federal admission deciding all sovereignty. The permits sometimes concerned submerged property, and exempt county rolls are weakened by whole-island descriptions and uncertain notice. Neither is treated as conclusive state possession of the dry fill. The 1934–35 controversy nevertheless publicly challenged a New York-only geographic understanding; the later federal decision favoring New York is considered, not omitted. The 1947–49 New Jersey wage episode was reversed in 1949 and no fictional State communication is added, but that oscillation and the conflicting federal map descriptions undermine a claim of uniformly recognized New York territorial dominion. Norton, Hague, federal labor officials and the States are not treated as interchangeable actors. The significance of an episode may be uncertainty about the openness or exclusivity of the asserted sovereign claim, even where it does not prove a formal New Jersey protest.

The five do not require New Jersey to have issued a particular legal notice or filed this suit earlier. Nor do they hold that a 1955 protest alone would defeat a title already acquired. They find the earlier cumulative evidence insufficient to establish the necessary acquisition before that controversy. The post-1955 record then supplies ongoing disagreement, rather than an unbroken later period curing the deficiency. This is an evidentiary conclusion under the preponderance standard, not an increase in the burden to certainty or a numerical count of governmental acts.

**Three-Justice provisional conclusion:** Stevens, Scalia and Breyer would sustain New York's prescription exception and recognize New York sovereignty over the enlarged island on that independent ground. They accept the same legal test but find its burden met by 1954. Their conclusion rests on decades of residual sovereign administration suited to this federally controlled territory: repeated election registration of identified fill residents, hospital vital records from fill facilities, marriage-law administration to the extent supported, compensation and construction arrangements specifically linked to the additions, and limited actual police/fire activity considered at its proper weight. They do not infer that every listed registrant voted, every marriage occurred on fill, every wage reference was mandatory, or every fireboat response asserted land sovereignty.

Their decisive distinction from Illinois is that the strongest acts here are affirmatively tied to the disputed fill and its resident community, not simply to an acknowledged central sailing channel or undisputed original tract. “Ellis Island” usage alone would be insufficient, but the identification of fill-resident and hospital activity makes the usage more than a label. Public administration of the resident community under New York law across decades supports notice of a practical claim covering the enlarged federal installation. Neither a new quantified yearly frequency nor a finding of personal knowledge by a named New Jersey officer is invented.

They find the competing episodes insufficient to defeat that inference or establish timely nonacquiescence. The 1904 transaction primarily concerns New Jersey's submerged property, which Article Third can preserve separately from administration of the enlarged island. Waterfront permits and water-main work likewise have an independent submerged-land explanation. Undistributed or obscure exempt rolls covering concededly New York land are weak notice of an adverse fill boundary. The 1934–35 jobs dispute contains local and federal positions rather than a decisive State territorial assertion and ended in a federal return to New York jurisdiction; the two-year wage experiment also ended in restoration of New York treatment. The maps are conflicting, sometimes derivative and not equivalent to competing state administration. These matters prevent treating the history as unanimous but, in their assessment, do not defeat the preponderance of long practical New York dominion and acquiescence before the later sustained public contest.

These three do not declare that only a governor or formal original complaint can preserve sovereignty. They reject the rival evidence for its particular object, provenance and circumstances. Nor do they assume federal ownership bars prescription or that possession of the original island automatically possesses every later addition. Michigan's described-tract analogy is supporting context only: the fill-directed activity and asserted coverage of the enlarged installation must do the actual work. Their judgment is an acquisition established by 1954 on the cumulative record, not a newly announced fixed prescriptive term or an invented earlier acquisition date. Under this path later protest does not undo the acquired sovereignty.

**Separate-writing function:** The disagreement must be explained as a disagreement over the territorial significance, continuity and notice of concrete acts. A dissent supporting New York must confront the deed, permits, maps and both federal reversals; a controlling rejection must confront the specific fill-resident and hospital evidence. Neither account may substitute “every immigrant thought New York,” unsupported motives or an inference about destroyed records for the admitted proof.

### 3. Laches: reject the independent defense without inventing lost evidence

All eight support rejecting the pleaded independent laches bar if it is necessary to reach it. Their common sufficient ground is that, assuming a distinct compact-enforcement laches defense could be available here, New York has not established material litigation prejudice attributable to New Jersey's delay sufficiently to bar this sovereignty claim. The claim concerns lost archives, expired retention periods, vandalism, leaking roofs, absent guards and deceased adult witnesses, and both affirmative proof and rebuttal. It is not rejected on the false premise that New York offered no evidence.

The strongest contrary path is that waiting until the immigration-era records and witnesses disappeared materially impaired a defense even if New Jersey had voiced disagreement earlier. That is a legally distinct claim from acquiescence and deserves its own consideration. But the circumstances of loss do not establish that the missing material would supply the disputed adverse dominion or notice, or defeat particular contrary acts. No precise missing document or witness account is required as an impossible universal prerequisite; the problem is that this record does not connect the asserted loss to a sufficiently supported material impairment and its attribution to the delay. The Court may not presume favorable contents merely because archival conditions were poor.

Illinois supplies an additional reason for caution: interstate boundary repose ordinarily operates through prescription and acquiescence. O'Connor and Souter are willing to apply that boundary treatment as an additional reason here. The common opinion need not turn it into a categorical rule for every compact case, which Kansas expressly left open. The common assumed-availability ground does not decide whether New Jersey's delay was inexcusable, choose a different laches remedy, or silently establish any prescriptive element. Stevens, Scalia and Breyer need no laches bar for their preferred prescription disposition, but authorize joining this bounded alternative rejection if the Court resolves the component.

The traditional requirements—an inequitable judgment, a good defense, fraud/accident/mistake preventing its use, absence of claimant fault or negligence and absence of an adequate legal remedy—must establish that exceptional need together. They do not create a broad second opportunity whenever a better document is later found. The operative limit is prevention of a grave miscarriage of justice, not a categorical prohibition on correcting settlements or a requirement that only intentional fraud could ever qualify.

The Government's reported archive search failed and its representation that the island had never been privately granted was inaccurate. Accepting the claimants' sustained search and the representation's influence on settlement, those facts do not establish extraordinary interference with their ability to present their title claim of the kind necessary for this independent action. The later discovery of a material translation does not by itself establish a corrupted adjudicative process, a guaranteed good chain of title or an exceptional equitable right to reopen. No finding of intentional concealment or obstruction is supplied; none is inferred simply from the document's presence in federal archives. The claimants are not blamed for failing to locate it sooner, and the Court does not find that the Government searched adequately. The asserted search/disclosure failure and later evidence, taken at their supported level, are insufficient for the extraordinary remedy demanded.

Actual Morgan Stanley and Plaut permit expressly bounded legislative reinstatement with independent judicial adjudication; they do not establish historical Plaut's categorical Article III bar, and no such bar is used here.
