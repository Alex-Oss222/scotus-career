## 2. Governing distinctions accepted in the reconsideration

Shipp recognizes authority to preserve the subject of a substantial jurisdictional dispute while the Court determines its power. Mine Workers applies that principle in a civil setting where a substantial question concerned statutory authority for ancillary restraint. Neither case permits a merits judgment on assumed Article III jurisdiction, excuses a frivolous jurisdictional theory, waives immunity or authorizes restraint after the relevant want of power is determined. Shipp's then-operative automatic habeas-stay statute is not 1998 law. Section 2251 remains discretionary and leaves state proceedings valid when no stay has issued.

Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction makes §1651 residual, not a means of avoiding a statute that specifically governs the proposed order. Stevens's dissent emphasizes the traditional relationship between federal courts and their marshals; it does not establish general judicial control over state execution officers or an exception to §2283. O'Connor joined the Court's limiting opinion. The source supplies both personal evidence and controlling law at their proper levels.

Hill and Atlantic Coast Line bring enforcement of a state judgment within §2283. Directing an order to execution officials, rather than the state court, changes neither its substantive effect nor this statutory inquiry. A short restraint is still a restraint. Section 1651 alone is not an exception, foreign sovereignty is not the United States-plaintiff exception, and neither the ICJ measure nor an ungranted leave motion is an existing judgment of this Court to protect or effectuate.

Hale and County of Imperial preserve a genuinely independent nonparty route. The issue concerns each applicant's binding relationship to the Virginia criminal proceeding, including actual privity, not merely the federal caption or assertion of separate interests. That relationship is not found here. Stevens's County of Imperial dissent relied on the record and determinations in that case; it supplies no missing determination in this one. The Associates neither assume that Paraguay and the consul are bound nor presume that they are strangers. A disposition resting on an independent sufficient ground can leave this factual/legal predicate unresolved.

Mitchum establishes that §1983 is within the expressly-authorized statutory exception. Both civil actions actually plead a Consul General §1983 theory; neither is treated as County of Imperial's complaint that did not invoke the statute. The exception's classification does not have to be proved afresh for every alleged right. But the actual claim, a right enforceable through that channel, the proper defendant and the relevant interim and equitable showing still require attention. Blessing's separation of right, cause and remedy remains material. An official-capacity claim concerning Paraguay's international entitlement is not automatically an adjudicated individual right. Conversely, an unresolved but pleaded theory is not treated as a nonexistent filing. Paraguay's separate treaty and comity claims do not automatically inherit another claimant's statutory exception.

Atlantic Coast Line requires more than a connection between a requested injunction and pending federal litigation. The necessary-aid exception concerns interference that seriously impairs the issuing court's ability and authority to consider or decide its own case. Ordinary concurrent litigation, an asserted federal right and a preference to preserve the status quo do not suffice. Its reference to this Court's broader potential appellate review of state decisions is not attributed to A-738's review of a federal civil judgment or to A-771's original channel. No general Supreme Court exemption from §2283 is asserted.

## 3. A-738 — revised common denial

**Formal position, all eight Associates:** Deny A-738's request for a stay or injunction. No restraint of execution issues through this application. No final decision on civil petition No. 97-1390 follows.

**Common sufficient ground, 738-S:** The Court can assess this application now under the applicants' favorable review/reversal/irreparability formulation without deciding the full-Court injunction-standard dispute. Paraguay asserts its own rights, not Breard's defaulted personal claim. Nevertheless, consular access has been available since 1996, and the present showing does not supply a significant prospect that the older notice and access duties require the requested restraint as relief for a current violation. The 1859 most-favored-consul theory may change the notification trigger; it does not itself resolve that continuing-duty and remedy problem. Current Seminole and Coeur d'Alene forbid categorical officer-immunity shortcuts but retain actual-duty and prospective-remedy limits. The new ICJ duty is separately pleaded in A-771 and cannot be silently inserted into this older cause.

This ground remains sufficient even if an otherwise proper §2283 route is assumed in the applicants' favor solely for assessing the independent interim showing. That is not a finding of nonparty status, a successful §1983 right or constitutional jurisdiction to enter a final merits judgment. The Court denies only the extraordinary interim request, without deciding those unknown predicates against applicants.

**Why the proposed administrative alternative is withdrawn:** The initial 738-P purpose was additional coordinated consideration of treaty rights and remedies after the international order. Shipp and Mine Workers instead require identification of an actual substantial jurisdictional or injunctive-power question for which additional preservation is necessary. Here appellate review of the federal civil judgment is already identified, the older claim's duty/remedy problem can receive the bounded interim assessment above, and the new original claim has its own route. Nothing in the supported old-action posture requires a further jurisdictional interval before this application can be decided. The proposed pause cannot be sustained merely by renaming the desired additional merits consideration jurisdictional preservation.

The strongest affirmative alternatives remain genuine: a proven independent nonparty status could place an applicant within Hale; a legally sufficient Consul General §1983 action could invoke Mitchum; and a truly necessary protection of the Court's appellate authority could invoke the necessary-aid exception. No contrary party relationship or categorical inability to use those routes is found. But the first two alternatives do not themselves create an entitlement to an injunction or additional consideration, and the third is not established merely by the risk that the requested execution-specific relief will become unavailable. On this older claim and its presently assessable continuing-duty objection, the Court's ability to resolve the application is not shown to require restraint. The serious international interests and irreversibility receive weight without becoming substitutes for that inquiry.

Thus the former preservation supporters do not deny because a research source fails to establish privity or because a label has not been accompanied by an invented required filing. They independently conclude that even favorable resolution of those unresolved routes would not justify the requested relief or the proposed administrative interval on this presentation. No Rooker-Feldman, political-question, litigant-default or automatic Monaco ground is added.

## 5. A-771 — necessary preservation versus present denial

### The four preservation positions

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain P-771: a temporary **administrative injunction** against execution, ending **at 5 p.m. Eastern Daylight Time on April 16, 1998, or an earlier further order of the Court**. The remainder of A-771 and the leave motion remain for consideration. If the Court determines before that endpoint that it lacks the relevant jurisdiction or power, Mine Workers requires vacation; the scheduled endpoint supplies no entitlement to continue a restraint after that determination.

**Identified domestic object and authority question.** No. 125 Original was actually presented with a consular party claiming an official federal right and connected Virginia officials threatening the act the complaint seeks to restrain. The concrete object is the Court's ability to decide its own power to entertain and grant prospective protection of the newly asserted April 9 compliance duty before the particular execution occurs. It is not simply preservation of the ICJ's calendar, the power to retry guilt or a request for general diplomatic supervision.

The unresolved jurisdictional and injunctive-power inquiry is specific: whether this official-capacity consular claim fits Article III and §1251(b)(1)'s original class, and whether the current Young route permits prospective restraint against these execution-connected officials on the alleged new federal duty rather than making the suit an impermissible action against the State under Monaco. The alleged Charter/Statute duty's domestic force bears on the officer route; a right to ultimate relief is not assumed. The current Seminole and Coeur d'Alene holdings supply a substantial basis for the officer inquiry without supplying its answer. Docketing and the consul's title alone do not decide the questions.

**Case-specific necessity and §2283.** These four find the original jurisdiction/power question substantial rather than a manufactured pretense. The pleading asserts a new threatened duty after the April 9 order; the original complaint is dated April 13 and execution is scheduled for April 14. They determine that this distinct party, immunity and new-duty interaction requires the specified brief period of judicial consideration and cannot responsibly be answered by importing the earlier civil action's completed-notice characterization. This is their assessment of the actual judicial inquiry and emergency, not a finding that papers were unavailable, filed late, unserved or unanswered, or that every original application requires two days.

Execution would irreversibly destroy the specific pre-execution protection whose jurisdictional availability the Court must assess. In this confined setting, they conclude that it would impair the Court's authority to consider that domestic jurisdictional question itself, not merely make ordinary parallel litigation less useful. They apply Shipp and Mine Workers's preservation principle to the original setting and find the corresponding **necessary-in-aid-of-jurisdiction exception under §2283** satisfied for this short inquiry. This is an expressly reasoned application by these Justices, not a claim that either precedent already adjudicated this foreign-consul action or that every irreversible injury satisfies the exception.

They assume §2283 applies for this analysis and invoke its identified exception. They do not need, and do not make, a finding that the applicants are strangers to the criminal judgment. They do not rely on Mitchum automatically validating the Consul General's disputed new-duty right, on Paraguay inheriting his §1983 exception, on a federal-government-plaintiff exemption or on protection of an existing domestic judgment. Section 1651 supplies auxiliary authority in aid of the identified original proceeding; it supplies no fourth exception. There is a substantial officer-based route for examination, not a waiver of State immunity.

**The strongest objection and the adopted limit.** Mere loss of requested relief cannot authorize a civil injunction whenever a plaintiff asks a federal court to prevent a state act. Shipp's habeas appeal and Mine Workers's properly presented civil parties differ from an ungranted original complaint, and uncertain merits cannot be converted into uncertainty about jurisdiction. These four accept those limits. Their narrower conclusion rests on an actually pending original action, a new and substantial question about the Court's power to grant the particular prospective restraint under its actual officer cases, imminent irreversible destruction of that action's precise object, and the bounded period necessary to decide that power. The older civil claim in A-738 does not need that same initial jurisdictional consideration, and the prisoner invokes a different statutory route in A-767.

No final Article III, sovereign-immunity, treaty-bindingness, individual-right or remedial holding follows. No particular party response, filing, evidentiary hearing or favorable ruling is required. A further order would be necessary for longer restraint; any such request must meet its own authority and interim requirements. This protection gives the Court time to decide whether it may act, not authority to grant final relief on assumed jurisdiction.

### The four denial positions

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain 771-D and deny the present TRO/preliminary-injunction request. They acknowledge Shipp/Mine Workers preservation power but find the proposed original-case application insufficient. The Court can make the present interim determination without a further protected jurisdictional interval. The new claim must be distinguished from old notice failures, yet the steps from interstate provisional measure to a domestically enforceable duty and proper officer action remain insufficient to support the requested injunction. They make no final original-action or merits disposition on hypothetical jurisdiction.

They regard §2283 as an additional reason to reject the initial general preservation formulation. Serious legal questions, imminent harm and a request for execution-specific relief do not themselves establish the exceptional impairment of domestic judicial authority required by Atlantic Coast Line. For them, the four proponents have identified a serious claim and preferred additional consideration, but not necessity to protect the Court's power from interference distinct from loss of the relief requested. The distinction between the asserted original channel and Shipp's habeas appeal or Mine Workers's otherwise properly presented civil action matters; analogy alone does not compel a new injunction.

They do not hold that the nonparty or Mitchum routes are categorically unavailable. Their denial is independently sufficient even if a proper statutory route could be established: they find no adequate interim merits showing or need for an administrative interval. They neither infer privity nor decide the personal §1983 issue against the claimant merely because it remains unresolved. They retain the initial rejection of blanket political-question doctrine and historical categorical immunity rules. Their disagreement with the four preserving Justices is substantive and case-specific; it cannot be erased by describing everyone as supporting respectful consideration of the international proceeding.
