## Rice v. Paladin Enterprises — May 14, 1998

**Shared disposition:** Affirm the reversal of defendants' summary judgment and remand for the reserved proceedings. On the motion-only stipulations, the First Amendment supplies no absolute immunity from civil liability for purposeful, concrete assistance actually used to commit the murders. All tort elements, actual intent/assistance/causation outside the motion's assumed facts, defenses and damages remain for ordinary proceedings. No instruction manual is banned and no final liability is entered.

**Shared operative scope:** Intentional participation through operational expression in independently unlawful conduct is distinct from advocacy of lawlessness. The necessary safeguards here are the publisher's admitted purpose to assist murder for hire, the instructions' concrete assisting role, and stipulated assistance to these completed crimes, together with the applicable tort's substantive and causal requirements. Mere publication, offensiveness, abstract advocacy, knowledge of possible misuse, foreseeability or someone else's imitation does not establish that combination. The absence of knowledge of Perry's specific plot does not create an absolute defense when purposeful assistance to criminal readers and actual assistance to these crimes are stipulated. The Court does not decide the constitutional sufficiency of lesser mental states, impose a general immediacy exception to Brandenburg, or adopt the lower opinion's broad claims about a publication having no legitimate value.
