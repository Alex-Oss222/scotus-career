## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Standing to challenge racial discrimination in grand-jury selection

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves Campbell’s direct due-process standing and does not reach third-party equal-protection standing, the unpreserved Sixth Amendment theory, or the discrimination merits.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Campbell was personally subjected to the charging authority of a grand jury whose composition allegedly depended on race. Hobby is distinguishable because Louisiana selected the foreperson before completing the grand jury and gave that person a full voting seat rather than assigning ministerial duties afterward.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A defendant has standing to pursue his own due-process objection when alleged racial discrimination affected the composition of the grand jury exercising charging authority over him. Campbell’s race does not defeat that direct injury.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The alleged defect concerns the body that indicted Campbell, and lawful correction can redress that personal injury. This direct route fully answers the standing judgment and makes it unnecessary to decide whether Campbell also may assert excluded jurors’ rights.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not find purposeful discrimination, treat statistical disparity as conclusive, define the substantive due-process rule, authorize third-party assertion of excluded jurors’ rights, decide the fair-cross-section theory, or prescribe relief if a violation is proved.
