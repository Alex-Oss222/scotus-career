### Issue 1: Nonrenewal and retaliation for lawful benefit claims

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Petition Clause claim and the separate no-entitlement and alternative constitutional theories.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A lawful, non-sham invocation of an established governmental process to claim benefits is protected petitioning and need not address a matter of public concern. A public employer may not refuse reemployment solely because the claim imposed the benefit cost prescribed by law. These plaintiffs possessed no property entitlement to rehire and established no independent substantive-due-process or equal-protection claim.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Petition Clause expressly protects petitions for redress of grievances. The drivers used the benefit process the State established and sought payments allegedly due. Annual contracts and retained hiring discretion defeat a property entitlement, while the specific Petition Clause source makes substantive due process unnecessary.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Government may reject fraudulent or ineligible claims, enforce neutral filing rules, contest eligibility, change prospective employment terms lawfully, and decline rehire for independent job-related reasons. Qualified immunity, damages, and the precise remedy remain unresolved.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Prospective termination of institutional relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory and Article III questions needed to review termination; reserve factual compliance issues not resolved in the supplied summary.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply revised Morgan Stanley/Plaut and Rufo as distinct holdings. Neither absolute finality nor an unrestricted congressional reopening power describes the received law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may change statutory conditions for prospective prison relief while leaving the court to apply the required current-violation and remedial findings.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would affirm termination of the unsupported continuing obligations and modify the judgment to end the decree’s future operation, while refusing retroactive vacatur. The difference follows from the enacted word “terminate,” the inclusion of consent decrees within prospective relief, and Congress’s power to change future remedial standards. It does not depend on the historically different Plaut rule. The parties cannot preserve federal coercive supervision simply by calling the same prospective obligations a contract; neither can officials use termination to erase completed adjudications, payments, or other settled historical effects. On this monitoring record, the inmates have not supported the findings needed for continuation. Their fear of later double bunking or abuse cannot itself establish a present federal violation. An actual later violation remains actionable. Due process creates no vested entitlement to unchanged future injunction terms; equal protection permits rules aimed at the administration of prospective prison remedies while leaving enforceable constitutional rights intact. The remand implements the modified termination judgment; it is not an instruction to relitigate every condition or invent a current constitutional violation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The remedy ends future operation, not the decree’s historical existence. The Commissioner set aside the separate Rule 60(b) vacatur issue if termination was obtained. At the simulated 1998 event, the amended §3626(b)(3) requires a current AND ongoing violation; the historical 1997 opinion quoted the earlier current OR ongoing wording.
