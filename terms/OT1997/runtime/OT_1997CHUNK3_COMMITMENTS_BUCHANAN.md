## Components and common operative conclusion

**C1 — reviewed Eighth/Fourteenth Amendment instruction claim:** Reach the presented constitutional question and affirm denial of habeas relief on the sufficient ground that this complete charge, evidence and trial context disclose no reasonable likelihood that the jury was prevented from giving effect to supported mitigation. This conclusion includes the requested general direction and the asserted entitlement to the four factor-specific instructions. It does not adopt the lower court's proposition that admissibility alone is always sufficient.

**C2 — derivative due-process claim based on the identified statutory mitigation factors:** Affirm rejection insofar as the claim asserts that the supplied statutory factors and admissibility provision themselves require these particular additional instructions as a matter of federal due process. No independent state-law entitlement to specified wording, or separately preserved arbitrary refusal to follow such an entitlement, is established by the packet. No new adjudication of a different due-process claim follows.

**C3 — collateral gates and correction:** Because C1 establishes no constitutional error under the ordinary constitutional standard, do not decide AEDPA's application, a new Teague bar, harmlessness, or entitlement to a writ. Do not impose a procedural-default defense. The missing federal filing date and capital statutory predicates prevent an unconditional grant premised on assumed pre-AEDPA law; they do not prevent this no-error disposition. Preserve every unrelated habeas and guilt component outside the reviewed question.

**Operative level of generality:** After lawful death eligibility, the Constitution requires an effective route to give supported mitigation its material culpability significance. It does not prescribe a general definition, a list of factors, fixed weight, or particular wording when the submitted decision itself gives that route. Here the jury was expressly choosing between death and life; even after a vileness finding it only *may* choose death and must choose life if all the evidence makes death unjustified. Nothing requires a false aggravator answer or suppresses independent background or emotional-disturbance significance. The two-day sentencing presentation, both sides' express mitigation arguments and the verdict form's mitigation reference confirm that reading of the judicial instruction. Those features do not make counsel a substitute for an adequate judicial route, establish any juror's mental state, or harmlessly cure a contrary charge.

No finding is made that every requested factor had adequate evidentiary support. Evidence of background and emotional disturbance is present; the absence of a need for factor-specific wording is sufficient without deciding the evidentiary predicate for each remaining factor. The instruction's unanimity direction concerns the death sentence. This commitment neither imposes unanimity on recognition of mitigation nor decides an unpresented unanimity challenge.

### John Paul Stevens

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Affirm on C1 and the dependent C2 theory, reaching the whole-charge constitutional question. His joins in Souter's Graham Parts I–III and V (January 25, 1993), O'Connor's Johnson independent-youth holding (June 24, 1993), and Tuilaepa Parts II–V (June 30, 1994) support effective independent mitigation without guaranteed weight or fixed labels. The first two require a genuine route; the last permits contextually understandable selection. The actual life alternative here supplies what the Texas special issues lacked.

**Two paths and strongest objection:** A finding of instructional error could rest on the absence of any judicial explanation of mitigation and the conditional phrase requiring belief that death is unjustified. Its strongest support is the substantial background/emotional evidence and the rule that admission and advocacy alone cannot secure its legal effect. Against that path, the express discretionary death/life choice and reference to all evidence authorize the relevant moral judgment without any contradictory factual answer. That contrast is sufficient under Boyde's reasonable-likelihood threshold; it is not a conclusion that evidence alone cures restriction. A remand merely to have the same complete charge read again is unnecessary on the supplied record.

**Own additional public commitment and join barriers:** His Tuilaepa join excluded Part I's additional framework discussion; he does not join a categorical freedom-from-guidance theory or any assertion that eligibility completes the constitutional inquiry. His January 14 Rogers join concerned a vehicle construction and establishes no merits instruction or harmlessness rule here. He joins only the record-specific no-error ground; no favorable-weight requirement, automatic Chapman extension, or unconditional writ follows. If the opinion instead finds a genuine prohibited use, his present affirmance cannot be transferred to harmlessness without further analysis.

### Sandra Day O'Connor

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Affirm C1–C2 on the functional adequacy of this charge. Her Tuilaepa opinion (June 30, 1994) permits relevant, understandable individualized selection without mandatory labels or weights. Here a truthful vileness finding leaves an unrestricted choice of life for mitigation found persuasive, unlike the required factual answers in Johnson.

**Two paths and strongest objection:** The contrary error path correctly insists that the court, rather than counsel, must leave an effective route; it also fairly identifies the asymmetric “may death / shall life if unjustified” phrasing. The whole instruction nevertheless makes death discretionary and links the alternative to all evidence; it does not demand additional factual proof, assign a separate burden of persuasion to mitigation, or make an aggravator dispositive. The trial context confirms rather than creates that permission. Her Delo Part I and Part II joins (March 8, 1993) reject automatic instruction entitlements while preserving context and evidentiary predicates. No general mitigation-instruction mandate is needed to enforce her Johnson rule on these facts.

**Qualification and join barriers:** Her Brecht Chapman join and her O'Neal opinion concern the supplied categories and, in O'Neal Part III, a proposed broader rule lacking a majority. **Qualification and join barriers:** His recorded Graham position would have used Teague for the requested relief there; it does not establish Buchanan's finality facts or a new-rule bar here.
