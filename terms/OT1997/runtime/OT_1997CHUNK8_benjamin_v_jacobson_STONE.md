## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: What the PLRA terminates

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory and Article III questions needed to review termination; reserve factual compliance issues not resolved in the supplied summary.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply revised Morgan Stanley/Plaut and Rufo as distinct holdings. Neither absolute finality nor an unrestricted congressional reopening power describes the received law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A restriction on future enforcement is not necessarily retrospective legislative adjudication of the original dispute; the statute determines which prospective obligations terminate.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would uphold the termination provision and remand with the boundary between future enforcement and completed effects made explicit. The ninety-plus orders cannot survive wholesale merely because the parties once consented, but they also cannot be annulled retroactively without statutory language doing that work. The prisoners must have a meaningful opportunity to produce evidence supporting the findings the statute itself makes decisive. The court should identify current violations, test each retained obligation for necessity and scope, and terminate the remainder. Stone would not constitutionalize the panel’s suggestion that every decree remains automatically enforceable in a state contract action: any such action depends on the agreement, applicable state law, and §3626(d), not a fictional continuing federal decree. Due process protects adjudication under the new rule, not permanent immunity from changes to prospective remedies; the prisoner-specific remedial classification is rationally related to limiting unsupported federal prison administration. This preserves the supplied remand without inventing findings about current New York jail conditions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Only the termination provision is presented; do not decide the automatic-stay provision or the entire PLRA. The 1998 simulated event uses the amended current-AND-ongoing text. Later en banc historical developments are not new-run holdings.
