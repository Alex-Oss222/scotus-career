## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** **AFFIRM.**
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Affirm Kirk’s § 922(o) possession conviction against the preserved Commerce Clause challenge. No Commerce-based vacatur, evidentiary remand, new trial, or transaction-specific interstate allegation is required.

Stone does not affirm because the machinegun is dangerous, because firearms have national markets generally, or because gun crime affects national productivity.

He affirms because *Hale* already established a narrower constitutional mechanism, which *Rybar* applied: **possession of newly prohibited machineguns may be included as a necessary anti-evasion component of Congress’s valid prohibition on additions to the post-1986 civilian machinegun stock.**

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Whether § 922(o) constitutionally reaches possession without transaction-specific interstate proof

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Kirk preserved the constitutional challenge through his conditional plea. Stone reaches the issue directly.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether *Hale*, as applied in *Rybar*, permits Congress to prohibit possession of a machinegun within the post-1986 prohibited stock even when the Government does not prove that Kirk’s particular possession arose from an interstate or commercial transaction.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** **Apply *Hale* and *Rybar*. Clarify *Hale*’s necessary meaning but create no new Commerce route.**
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:**

> **Section 922(o)’s possession prohibition may constitutionally reach possession within the prohibited post-1986 civilian machinegun stock without proof of a defendant-specific interstate transaction because the possession component is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion and material impairment of Congress’s valid national prohibition on additions to that market. The Commerce Clause does not require a transaction-specific jurisdictional element where the regulated possession class is validly included through the Necessary and Proper implementation of the interstate regime.**

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:**

#### 1. Kirk and Rybar test the same rule from opposite factual directions

Stone expressly recognizes the relationship between the two cases.

*Rybar* asked whether *Hale* works where the possession occurs at the obvious core of commercial market activity. The Court held that it does, and that the possession component’s validity does not depend on adding a transaction-specific interstate-crossing element.

*Kirk* asks whether *Hale* still works when the individual prosecution contains no proved transaction-specific interstate event and the conviction does not rest on a proved transfer.

The second question is actually more important doctrinally.

If *Hale* only applies when the Government proves an individual commercial transfer, then its anti-evasion rationale does almost no independent work.

The Government could simply regulate the proved transaction.

The reason possession matters is that possession persists **after the transaction becomes difficult or impossible to prove**.

#### 2. Lopez requires an interstate mechanism, not necessarily an interstate element in every prosecution

Stone begins where *Lopez* requires:

**What did Congress regulate?**

The defined class is possession of machineguns outside the exceptions preserved by § 922(o).

**What interstate mechanism connects that class to commerce?**

The answer is not:

machineguns cause crime;

machineguns are dangerous;

machineguns are expensive;

firearms generally move interstate.

The mechanism is the one *Hale* identified and *Rybar* applied:

Congress prohibited additions to the post-1986 civilian machinegun stock and included possession because a transfer-only prohibition would be vulnerable to evasion and material enforcement failure.

That is a constitutionally identified mechanism.

#### 3. Hale should mean what its reasoning necessarily implies

Stone refuses to make *Hale* meaningless by requiring proof of an individual transaction in every possession case.

The possession rule exists because:

a prohibited transfer may be hidden;

the seller may disappear;

records may not exist;

the acquisition may be informal;

the machinegun may be manufactured or converted after the cutoff;

or the Government may discover the prohibited stock only after the transaction has ended.

If the federal prohibition ended whenever the transfer itself could no longer be proved, Congress could not effectively maintain the stock restriction.

That is precisely the kind of evasion problem Necessary and Proper authority permits Congress to address.

#### 4. Kirk’s intrastate-conversion argument does not defeat the federal rule

Suppose a machinegun is created entirely within one State by converting another firearm.

That does not demonstrate why the resulting possession must fall outside the national stock prohibition.

It demonstrates the opposite.

If Congress validly closes the interstate civilian market to post-1986 machineguns, permitting unlimited local production followed by possession would materially defeat that regime by creating substitutable new prohibited stock.

Stone therefore distinguishes:

**local production or possession as an independent basis of Commerce power**

from

**local production or possession included because exclusion would materially undermine a valid interstate market prohibition.**

Only the second theory is necessary here.

#### 5. No universal manufacture theory is adopted

Stone is nevertheless careful.

The fact that someone can manufacture an object locally cannot become a general rule allowing Congress to prohibit possession of every locally made object merely because similar objects are sold nationally.

The constitutional force comes from the **specific structure and subject of the regulated regime**:

a defined machinegun market;

a national prohibition on post-cutoff additions;

a grandfathered lawful stock;

and a possession rule fitted to preventing circumvention of that stock boundary.

The fit must do actual work.

#### 6. Rybar confirms the core; Kirk defines the edge

Stone treats the two cases as complementary, with *Rybar* as the precedent.

In *Rybar*, actual gun-show sales made the connection between possession and the regulated market unmistakable.

In *Kirk*, the absence of transaction-specific interstate proof, in a conviction resting on possession alone, demonstrates why possession is itself part of the anti-evasion mechanism.

Thus:

> **Rybar shows the rule at the market core. Kirk shows why the rule cannot constitutionally depend on reconstructing the market transaction in every possession prosecution.**

Neither case needs to create a new Commerce category.

#### 7. Aggregation is secondary here

Stone does not make *Kirk* depend primarily on aggregating every machinegun possession and asking whether their combined economic effect is substantial.

That would unnecessarily blur *Lopez*.

The stronger reasoning is structural:

valid interstate regime  
→ identifiable circumvention/enforcement problem  
→ fitted possession rule  
→ Necessary and Proper inclusion.

Aggregation may support the magnitude of the market problem, but it does not create the constitutional connection.

#### 8. The absence of a jurisdictional element is not fatal

*Lopez* never established that every Commerce statute must contain an offense-specific interstate element.

Such an element can be useful when the statute depends on showing that the individual offense falls within interstate commerce.

But another valid route can establish federal authority at the class/regime level.

*Hale* supplies that route here. *Rybar* has already said as much for § 922(o): the possession component’s validity does not depend on adding a transaction-specific interstate-crossing element.

Stone therefore rejects two opposite mistakes:

**Mistake one:** The gun once moved interstate; therefore federal power automatically exists.

**Mistake two:** The statute lacks an interstate-crossing element; therefore federal power automatically fails.

The actual question is whether the regulated class possesses a constitutionally sufficient mechanism.

It does here.

#### 9. Sparse findings do not defeat § 922(o)

Stone maintains *Lopez*’s findings rule.

Congressional findings can support a disputed factual mechanism.

They are not constitutionally mandatory in every statute.

Nor can findings create power by recitation.

Here the anti-evasion mechanism is apparent from the enacted regulatory structure and has already been recognized in *Hale* and applied in *Rybar*.

Stone therefore does not manufacture crime statistics or national productivity findings to save the statute.

They are unnecessary.

#### 10. Federal and State police authority remain separate

States retain broad authority over weapons and crime.

That does not create an exclusive State domain.

Likewise, federal authority over this machinegun class does not establish a general national firearms police power.

Stone’s federalism remains source-specific.

#### 11. The Fifth Circuit split does not change the governing source

The equal division below is significant because it identifies a genuine unresolved disagreement among lower-court judges.

But it does not produce controlling en banc doctrine.

Stone therefore does not adopt either lower-court camp wholesale.

He applies the simulation’s governing Supreme Court law.

That law is *Lopez* plus *Hale*, as applied in *Rybar*.

Under that law, Kirk loses.

* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Apply *Lopez*’s connection-before-magnitude architecture and its Necessary and Proper route; apply *Hale* Part I; apply *Rybar* Part I, including its statement that no transaction-specific interstate-crossing element is required, without enlarging either decision. *Wickard* and *Perez* keep their economic-class scope; *Parker*’s payment-obligation mechanism is distinct.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does **not** hold:
  * that Congress may prohibit possession of every object traded in interstate commerce;
  * that every federal product-market restriction automatically supports a possession ban;
  * that dangerousness itself creates Commerce authority;
  * that remote historical movement suffices;
  * that a jurisdictional element is constitutionally irrelevant;
  * that legislative findings never matter;
  * that Congress may regulate ordinary firearm possession generally;
  * that machineguns are unprotected under the Second Amendment;
  * or that § 922(o) is valid under every independent constitutional provision.

  The holding depends upon:

  1. the valid national prohibition on additions to the post-1986 civilian machinegun market;
  2. the defined stock boundary;
  3. the practical relationship between possession and enforcement of that prohibition; and
  4. the conclusion, already established in *Hale* and applied in *Rybar*, that excluding possession would materially permit evasion or impairment of the regime.
* **Approved Conditional Position or Fallback:** None. Stone does not need a remote-movement theory, generalized substantial effects, or an alternative defendant-specific interstate nexus. *Rybar* is applied as entering precedent, not as a fallback; Stone’s result rests on the mechanism *Hale* established, which *Rybar* confirms.

### Relationship to Rybar for simulation purposes

*Rybar* (decided November 24, 1997) and *Kirk* are related Commerce Clause matters decided in sequence. *Rybar* is precedent for *Kirk*; neither independently expands the Commerce power beyond *Lopez* and *Hale*.

* **Rybar:** possession tied directly to two completed gun-show sales. Court Commerce result: challenge rejected, 9–0, with Stone joining Part I. Doctrinal significance: core application of *Hale*.
* **Kirk:** preserved possession conviction without transaction-specific interstate proof. Stone’s Commerce result: uphold. Doctrinal significance: confirms what *Hale* necessarily means — anti-evasion possession authority does not disappear merely because the Government cannot reconstruct an individual interstate transaction.

> **Hale regulates the defined possession class because possession is necessary to make the machinegun stock-and-transfer prohibition effective. Rybar demonstrates the rule where the market transaction is visible; Kirk demonstrates why the rule exists when the transaction is not.**

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Parole files, liberty, and equal protection

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the due-process and equal-protection theories concerning confidential protest material.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Texas creates no parole liberty interest. Without that entitlement, unreliable information does not activate procedural due process. Unpredictable receipt of protest letters establishes neither purposeful classification of an identifiable group nor an irrational parole criterion.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The protest-letter system establishes no due-process or equal-protection violation on this record.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Board retains broad release discretion, and the letters do not define a protected class or formal rule of decision. Federal due process therefore does not become a general accuracy code for the confidential file.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This creates no right to parole, adversarial parole hearing, disclosure of confidential letters, or federal correction of every inaccurate file.

### Issue 2: Retaliation for protected litigation and classwide relief

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the legal retaliation standard but remands because the record does not separate protected activity, establish Johnson’s injury, or prove an authorized Board practice.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Denial of discretionary parole because of protected, nonfrivolous litigation is itself adverse injury. Once the claimant proves protected activity and that retaliation substantially motivated the denial, the Board may establish that it would have made the same decision for lawful reasons. Systemwide relief additionally requires named-plaintiff standing and an ongoing official policy or widespread authorized practice.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A parole board may not punish protected, nonfrivolous litigation: once the claimant proves that such activity substantially motivated an adverse parole decision, the Board may establish that it would have made the same decision for lawful reasons. Classwide prospective relief requires named-plaintiff standing, an ongoing official policy or widespread authorized practice, corresponding class injury, and relief fitted to that violation.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Nonfrivolous direct appeals, habeas claims, and conditions-of-confinement litigation are protected, but not every filing or unrestricted jailhouse lawyering. Isolated references to litigiousness do not justify a systemwide injunction. The remand must identify the protected activity, causal role, same-decision proof, and lawful remedial scope.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not protect frivolous litigation or permit systemwide relief based only on generalized references to litigiousness or isolated employee conduct.

### Issue 1: Dependent Indian community status

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory land-status question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A dependent Indian community requires land set aside by the Federal Government for Indian use under federal superintendence. ANCSA intentionally replaced reservation and trust status with corporate fee ownership and broad alienability, so both predicates are absent.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Land conveyed in fee under ANCSA, after revocation of the reservation and without federal superintendence, is not a dependent Indian community under § 1151(b).
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The settlement act sought economic autonomy through fee ownership rather than continuing reservation status. Federal programs benefiting residents do not amount to territorial superintendence, and Native ownership alone does not create a federal set-aside.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not question tribal identity, federal benefits, trust parcels, or authority arising from a source other than territorial Indian-country status.

### Issue 1: Mandatory remand after MDL pretrial proceedings

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory authority question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1407’s direction that transferred actions “shall” be remanded is mandatory. Section 1404 does not authorize the transferee court to defeat that command by transferring the case from itself to itself.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An MDL transferee court lacks authority to retain a transferred action for trial through self-transfer under § 1404(a); the action must be remanded when coordinated pretrial proceedings conclude.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The provisions can coexist: § 1407 governs temporary pretrial transfer and mandatory return, while § 1404 remains available to a court possessing the action through an authorized venue posture. Efficiency cannot erase the sequence Congress chose.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not bar consensual trial before the transferee judge, later transfer by the originating court, or statutory amendment.

**Proposed Stone disposition:** REVERSE the judgment denying absolute legislative immunity.

**Reasoning:** Introducing a municipal budget, voting on legislation, and signing an ordinance are genuine legislative functions. **Doctrinal contribution:** Preserves historically established absolute legislative immunity while preventing its expansion through official titles.
