## 1. Reach, record and components

All eight Associates participate: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The supplied packet establishes no recusal, question-scope obstacle or separate jurisdictional defect. Each reaches both constitutional questions. This §1983 action challenges clemency procedure; it does not present a new adjudication of guilt, innocence, punishment eligibility or execution competency.

The District Court granted judgment on the pleadings. The Sixth Circuit rejected an independent state or federal entitlement to clemency, accepted a second due-process theory treating clemency as part of the overall criminal adjudicatory system, and remanded to determine process. It also accepted a possible unconstitutional-condition objection to the interview. The State seeks reversal of those adverse directions. It is inaccurate to describe the Sixth Circuit as having rejected the Fifth Amendment theory.

The Governor's ultimate discretion is complete and the Authority's recommendation advisory. The Authority must conduct its review and recommendation even after a stay; another hearing or changed recommendation on new information remains discretionary. The prisoner may request an interview and did not request one here. His objections concern three days' interview notice, ten days' hearing notice, inability adequately to prepare while postconviction matters were pending, absence of counsel at the interview, counsel's hearing participation at the chair's discretion, and inability to testify or submit documentary evidence at the hearing. Those allegations receive their proper pleadings posture; they are not newly found facts of arbitrary exclusion. No confidential or immunized interview is promised. No actual use of an answer, particular coercive question, automatic ineligibility upon silence or specific punishment for silence is established. The record does not describe the board's use of silence. No equal-protection claim is presented.

Separate components are: (A) a state-created entitlement to clemency or its procedural incidents; (B) the Sixth Circuit's Evitts theory; (C) any independently retained constitutional protection against arbitrary deprivation of life or obstruction of access; (D) the adequacy of the challenged safeguards on the pleadings; and (E) the Fifth Amendment condition and associated remedy. The affirmative retained-life proposition is distinct from entitlement to a favorable discretionary decision.

## 3. Common alternative-path comparison

**State entitlement.** One possible path treats Ohio's mandatory hearing and recommendation as protected procedural interests. Its strongest support is the substantive importance of clemency and the State's affirmative establishment of a regular review process. Its decisive obstacle is that the required steps do not constrain the Governor to grant relief or the Authority to recommend it upon particular substantive facts. Sandin, Doe and Johnson forbid converting mandatory administration alone into the requested entitlement. All eight reject a state-created right to a favorable result or a full adjudicatory hearing. Stevens's independent-life ground is not an exception fabricated from mandatory words.

**Evitts and retained life.** The Sixth Circuit's strongest argument is that a State must administer fairly the procedures it chooses to make part of its criminal system. The strongest objection is that first appellate adjudication and subsequent executive mercy perform different functions: clemency does not adjudicate an existing right to freedom from this lawful sentence. Seven Associates reject the lower court's general integral-stage rationale; Stevens also would not make protection depend upon an open-ended measurement of distance from trial, but preserves the remand through independently retained life and liberty. The competing retained-life path preserves a narrow constitutional floor against entirely arbitrary obstruction or exclusion without creating entitlement to mercy. Its strongest objection is that it might re-create the procedural entitlement Dumschat denied. The answer of its supporters is to confine the floor to an independently retained interest and to keep access distinct from a new merits tribunal. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas decline to adopt that additional procedural-life rationale for this discretionary process, while preserving independently applicable constitutional prohibitions.

**Actual safeguards and remedy.** The strongest reversal path is that Ohio offers a real opportunity to request personal consideration, gives advance notice and conducts a required advisory review. The requested counsel, hearing-participation and document rules seek more than protection against arbitrary denial of access. The strongest remand path is the pleadings posture: the combination of short preparation, exclusion of counsel from the interview and limits on personal or documentary participation may defeat meaningful access even if every restriction considered alone is permissible. Seven Associates conclude that those stated objections do not establish arbitrary exclusion from the mercy process and require no new federal adjudicatory procedure. Stevens would preserve a bounded remand to determine meaningful access in the actual process. Neither side assumes unmentioned written-submission channels or factual board practices. No one finds Woodard entitled to clemency, a new criminal trial or a judicial merits assessment of his clemency grounds.

**Fifth Amendment.** The strongest invalid-condition path is that the sole personal interview is offered without counsel, confidentiality or immunity to a person whose truthful answers might expose him to criminal consequences. Johnson forbids dismissing such a claim solely because the benefit is discretionary, and LaChance preserves actual compulsion analysis. The strongest answer is the absence of a legal demand to attend or speak or an identified sanction attached to a valid refusal. Voluntary presentation of favorable information may involve a difficult strategic choice without compelling testimony. The lack of immunity does not itself compel speech. All eight reverse the present Fifth Amendment remand on that limited basis. None adopts a categorical rule approving every adverse inference, a compelled waiver, or later criminal use of answers actually coerced. Baxter permits only its bounded inference practice; it does not decide the legal effect of an unspecified Ohio practice. Stevens's agreement is limited to the optional procedure and absence of a supported command or penalty, not a finding that every future interview answer would be voluntary.

## 4. Justice-specific commitments

### Stevens

**A–D:** Reject an entitlement to receive clemency and do not convert mandatory procedures alone into one. Affirm the availability of the due-process remand on an independently retained-life ground, while replacing the Sixth Circuit's unnecessary general integral-stage theory. Dumschat, 452 U.S. at 468–472, is his own direct account of continuing liberty and protection against arbitrary use of an established release process; his Sandin join and concurrence with Ginsburg preserve intrinsic liberty independently of code drafting. His full Ford join supports an opportunity to present material where life is at stake. He would require further examination of whether the combination of the asserted limitations leaves a meaningful opportunity to seek mercy, not declare each limitation separately unconstitutional.

**Strongest contrary path and limit:** Johnson, which he joined in full, forecloses a general accuracy or adversarial-parole code simply because initial release is important. He follows that rule here for the state-entitlement claim. His distinction is the retained life interest and the alleged exclusion from meaningful personal or documentary presentation before execution, not mere unreliability in a discretionary file. Ford's competency adjudication was legally constrained; it does not itself prescribe clemency procedure. His commitment therefore reaches a bounded inquiry into meaningful access, without importing Ford's federal hearing remedy, an immunity requirement or every trial safeguard.

**E:** Reverse the Fifth Amendment remand insofar as it rests on the optional interview/nonconfidentiality combination alone. His full Murphy dissent join is the strongest argument for retaining further proceedings: an implicit sanction and official ambiguity may coerce even without a formally worded penalty. But Murphy involved an affirmative compulsory probation command and incriminating answers; Ohio's invitation contains neither a supported obligation to speak nor a specified consequence for asserting a valid privilege. LaChance, which he joined, requires the actual compulsion predicate. He will not join a finding that no coercive practice could exist or that voluntary participation waives all future objections.

**Joins and remedy:** Joins the common no-state-entitlement rule, an affirmative minimum retained-life/access proposition, and the limited Fifth Amendment reversal. Refuses the due-process application sustaining judgment on the pleadings and any declaration that life has lost all constitutional protection. Would leave a due-process remand concerning effective notice and meaningful submission opportunities; no particular procedural entitlement or ultimate violation is found. His separate-position function is to explain this remand and the distinction from ordinary discretionary initial parole.

### O'Connor

**A–D:** Reverse the due-process remand. Dumschat and the Sandin/Johnson rules yield no state-created mercy entitlement. Her Ford writing, 477 U.S. at 427–431, separates an actual substantive protection from the procedures that safeguard it and accepts flexible minimal process after a valid conviction. Preserve a minimal retained-life protection against arbitrary denial of access, but hold the pleaded Ohio restrictions insufficient to violate it. The requested interview, actual advance notice and mandatory review distinguish complete exclusion. A counsel-attended interview, personal hearing testimony, documentary submission at that hearing, or postponement until collateral proceedings end is not automatically constitutionally required for discretionary mercy.

**Strongest contrary path and limit:** Her Ford insistence on consideration of written submissions strongly supports remand if the process is effectively closed to the prisoner. But Ford involved a substantive restriction on execution upon proof of incompetence, not unconfined mercy. Here an offered personal interview supplies an actual access opportunity. She does not infer an additional written channel, approve intentional interference with an application, or decide the adequacy of a materially different record. Her narrow direct-life reservation also does not revive the Sixth Circuit's general appellate analogy.

**E:** Reverse. Her full Murphy majority join supports distinguishing a demand to be truthful from a compelled waiver and requiring an actual threat before treating silence as legally penalized. This case presents still less compulsion: no attendance obligation, compulsory question or supported silence sanction. LaChance preserves future protection where the required predicate exists. Nonconfidentiality and the absence of immunity do not create that predicate.

**Joins and remedy:** Joins common no-entitlement, rejection of Evitts extension, minimal retained-life floor, adequacy on these objections and limited Fifth Amendment grounds. Refuses categorical language excluding all due process from clemency and refuses an absolute permission to penalize protected silence. Reverse adverse Sixth Circuit directions and remand for entry of a disposition consistent with sustaining the District Court on the presented claims; no additional process ordered.

### Scalia

**A–D:** Reverse. His Sandin dissent join distinguishes ordinary sentence-authorized restraint from a separately protected deprivation, while the actual Sandin rule and his full Johnson join require a substantive limit on denial of initial release. The Governor's unconfined mercy power supplies none. The lawful sentence is not newly imposed by denying clemency. Evitts's appellate adjudication premise does not create an entitlement to another adjudicatory stage. He does not adopt a general procedural-life test for this discretion, and the particular notice, counsel and hearing objections accordingly do not require constitutional relief.

**Strongest contrary path and limit:** Sandin's nonexclusive rule preserves direct constitutional protections; Dumschat cannot be read to license every independently prohibited act. Johnson expressly preserves retaliation claims despite discretion. He retains those constraints, but the presented due-process objection must identify a protected deprivation independent of the desired clemency procedure itself. The alternative broad retained-life rationale is not sufficient for him to prescribe further procedure for a validly imposed sentence. No equality or retaliation claim is decided here.

**E:** Reverse under his LaChance Part IV and Johnson joins: an independent privilege cannot be extinguished by labeling mercy discretionary, but the optional interview creates no actual duty or supported penalty. Baxter's distinction between an evidentiary choice and compelled testimony supports the limited conclusion without supplying a ruling on unspecified Ohio inferences. No privilege to lie, general duty to confess or all-purpose immunity follows.

**Joins and remedy:** Joins no entitlement, rejection of Evitts, failure of the pleaded procedural claim, and limited noncompulsion. Refuses the affirmative retained-life procedural floor as a new sufficient basis for reviewing mercy, and refuses a hypothetical ruling on every distinct constitutional prohibition. Sustain the District Court's judgment on both claims through reversal of the contrary appellate directions.

### Kennedy

**A–D:** Reverse on the entitlement/adjudication ground. His own Johnson opinion is the closest current-term commitment: conditions regulating the collection and consideration of information do not create a right to discretionary initial release, and an accuracy or adversarial-review claim cannot be derived from such a right. His Sandin dissent join likewise does not treat continuation of the valid sentence as a fresh deprivation merely because relief was sought. Ohio's mandatory review does not constrain the Governor's ultimate choice. He rejects the generalized Evitts extension and does not join an affirmative retained-life procedural floor for this advisory mercy process.

**Strongest contrary path and limit:** His Johnson opinion independently protects constitutional conduct even in discretionary decisionmaking; it is therefore unavailable to establish that an absence of entitlement excuses every unconstitutional condition. The distinction is between an independently identified prohibition and a procedural claim derived from the ungranted benefit. He preserves the former while finding no latter entitlement here. The capital setting and genuine retained-life concerns do not justify importing trial or appeal machinery without identifying the new deprivation and its legal constraint. His Herrera plurality join supplies no exclusive-clemency rule and no new ground against review of actual innocence.

**E:** Reverse. His LaChance join preserves an actual privilege and rejects compulsory waiver; his Johnson analysis requires examination of the protected-right penalty rather than termination of inquiry at discretion. Here neither a command to answer nor a supported sanction is part of the optional interview procedure. The claimant's strategic desire to improve the application does not supply the missing coercive state act. A different actual threat remains open.

**Joins and remedy:** Joins common no-entitlement, rejection of the appellate analogy and the limited Fifth Amendment rule; he can join the conclusion that none of these procedural objections establishes constitutional injury. He withholds an additional affirmative retained-life procedure rationale, without endorsing a proposition that no independent constitutional limit ever applies. Reverse the adverse appellate directions; no new interview, hearing or immunity order.

### Souter

**A–D:** Reverse, while recognizing a minimal retained-life/access floor. His Sandin majority join and concurrence with Breyer require examination of the whole substantive scheme; the same inquiry, as applied in his full Johnson join, finds no substantive Ohio entitlement. The life interest nonetheless remains distinct from that state-created route. A nonexclusive constitutional account does not permit the State arbitrarily to shut a condemned person out of the existing mercy process. The notice and offered interview here do not show that kind of denial, and hearing restrictions do not by themselves require a triallike proceeding.

**Strongest contrary path and limit:** The strongest remand argument is the cumulative force of the alleged submission limitations at the pleadings stage. Sandin permitted remand where genuine legal constraints and significant punishment required further application. Here the substantive mercy choice is already established as unconstrained, and the actual access offered permits a bounded conclusion without assuming missing submission channels or ordering adjudication of the clemency merits. He refuses categorical extinction of direct constitutional protection and leaves an actually closed or deliberately obstructed process outside this ruling.

**E:** Reverse. His own LaChance Part IV expressly separated the reporting duty, valid privilege and coercive use rather than declaring all truthful responses compulsory. Murphy supplies the distinction between a real penalty and a difficult choice. The present invitation does not demand an answer or waive protection from actually compelled criminal use. No general license to draw adverse inferences is needed or joined.

**Joins and remedy:** Joins the no-entitlement rule, rejection of the Evitts extension, minimal access floor and its satisfaction here, and the limited Fifth Amendment reversal. Will not join broader procedural immunity for executive mercy or an unqualified silence-use holding. Reverse the remands on these claims and leave the ordinary final disposition to the lower courts.

### Thomas

**A–D:** Reverse. His Sandin dissent join preserves direct unexpected-restraint and sentence-duration interests but distinguishes them from ordinary lawful confinement; his full Johnson join rejects an initial-release process claim where substantive law does not constrain denial. Dumschat and Doe reinforce that Ohio's mandatory administration creates no right to mercy. Evitts does not transform a discretionary executive recommendation into an appellate guilt adjudication. He declines to derive a new procedural entitlement from the fact that execution remains ahead under a valid sentence.

**Strongest contrary path and limit:** The strongest retained-life argument correctly notes that conviction does not remove every constitutional protection. He accepts Sandin's nonexclusivity and Johnson's independent-right limitation as governing law, but finds neither a state entitlement nor an independently identified unlawful life deprivation in the pleaded hearing-format objections. The fact of death alone does not establish which additional clemency procedure is required. He would not decide unasserted equality, retaliation or execution-competency claims.

**E:** Reverse. His LaChance and Johnson joins preserve protection against actual coerced testimony and a proved prohibited penalty, but neither expands privilege into a right to give voluntary information with an immunity guarantee. The absence of an obligation to interview or a supported sanction resolves this challenge. Baxter supplies no blanket approval of unexamined practices, and no such approval is necessary.

**Joins and remedy:** Joins no state entitlement, rejection of Evitts, failure of the pleaded procedure claim and the bounded Fifth Amendment ground. Withholds the affirmative retained-life procedural floor for discretionary clemency. Reverse adverse appellate directions; the District Court's rejection of these claims should stand.

### Ginsburg

**A–D:** Reverse on the presented objections, preserving a minimal retained-life floor. Her own Sandin concurrence, joined by Stevens, rejects the premise that liberty is wholly a creature of state codification; her full majority and Johnson joins nonetheless keep substantive constraints distinct from mandatory administration. She therefore denies a state-created mercy entitlement while preserving review of arbitrary exclusion from a proceeding concerning the petitioner's remaining life. The optional personal interview and actual notice prevent treating these allegations as total exclusion. The Constitution does not on these facts require each requested hearing or counsel safeguard.

**Strongest contrary path and limit:** Her Sandin opinion found an actual protected deprivation on a punitive-isolation record and supports examining the cumulative effect of restraints. It does not require the same remedy whenever a prisoner requests relief: Ohio imposes no new segregation or other punishment through the contested interview arrangement. Stevens's proposed remand is a plausible retained-life application, but the allegations here concern the means of seeking discretion rather than an established denial of meaningful access. She joins no finding of unmentioned evidence channels and no declaration that arbitrary interference is immune from review.

**E:** Reverse with express limits. LaChance's compelled-incrimination reservation and her Johnson join prevent equating discretionary benefit with unrestricted authority to condition it. The present invitation requires no incriminating response; refusal of immunity and absence of confidentiality do not alone impose a penalty. A materially supported forced-waiver or silence-penalty claim would require its own analysis. No categorical ruling about every criminal use or civil inference is adopted.

**Joins and remedy:** Joins common no-entitlement, rejection of an automatic Evitts extension, minimal retained-life protection, adequacy here and bounded Fifth Amendment reversal. Rejects extinction of life protection or unnecessary approval of adverse silence practices. Reverse the appellate remands on the presented grounds; order no new substantive outcome or procedure.

### Breyer

**A–D:** Reverse, with a minimal retained-life/access qualification. His own Sandin majority opinion requires examining real substantive constraints in the full scheme and a meaningful deprivation, not extracting an entitlement from a procedural word. His separate concurrence with Souter found Hawaii's actual defined-offense and evidence scheme consequential; his full Johnson join found Texas's discretionary initial-release scheme different. Ohio likewise provides no substantive release constraint. At the same time Sandin is nonexclusive, so the State may not render the prisoner's existing access to a life-preserving plea wholly arbitrary. The actual offer of an interview and notice satisfies that modest requirement on the objections presented.

**Strongest contrary path and limit:** The practical interaction of the asserted restrictions may seem to justify further development. But the alleged three-day/ten-day preparation intervals and hearing-format limits do not themselves establish exclusion from the entire process, and no record finding that clemency must be deferred until collateral review ends can be invented. The absence of a substantive adjudicatory standard makes a new evidence hearing inappropriate. He preserves a remedy for an actually obstructed or meaningless opportunity without prescribing a multifactored constitutional clemency code.

**E:** Reverse. His LaChance and Johnson joins require real analysis of whether the State exacts a protected-right penalty. The prospect that truthful answers may be useful outside clemency identifies possible incrimination, but not compulsion; the optional interview supplies no established demand or sanction. The distinction prevents making immunity a prerequisite to every voluntary request for favorable discretion.

**Joins and remedy:** Joins no state entitlement, rejection of the integral-appeal theory, a minimal retained-life/access floor satisfied here and the limited Fifth Amendment disposition. Refuses categorical abolition of direct protection and a hypothetical broad adverse-inference rule. Reverse the adverse appellate directions and remand for a disposition consistent with rejection of the presented claims.
