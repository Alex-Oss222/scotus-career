## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Administrative review — appeal from Social Security remand

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** May a benefits claimant appeal a sentence-four remand when she sought an immediate award and received only further agency proceedings?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** 28 U.S.C. §1291 and sentence four of 42 U.S.C. §405(g); finality and aggrievement are separate questions.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** REVERSE and REMAND for the court of appeals to consider the claimant’s challenge.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would apply Schaefer’s finality rule consistently to both sides. A final remand that refuses an immediate award leaves the claimant with more proceedings and without the benefit entitlement she requested. Calling her a prevailing party for another statute does not erase that concrete disagreement. Allowing an appeal does not decide whether the record permits an immediate award; it ensures that the appellate court reviews the remedy actually chosen rather than denying jurisdiction because the claimant won something.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Schaefer holds that a sentence-four remand ends the district-court civil action and requires a judgment; it is not a sentence-six remand retaining jurisdiction. That supplies finality. A claimant can prevail enough for some purposes while still being adversely affected by denial of the larger requested relief.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Sentence-six remands and cases granting all requested relief present different finality or aggrievement questions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Ownership of interest generated by client funds

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the property-ownership question and reserves every constitutional consequence and remedy.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Texas law recognizes the clients’ ownership of the principal, and the traditional rule assigns interest generated by that principal to the same owner unless a valid background property rule provides otherwise. Pooling, administrative cost, and inability to realize positive net interest individually may affect taking and compensation but do not erase ownership.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Interest generated by client funds in an IOLTA account is private property of the principal’s owner unless a valid background property rule assigns it elsewhere. The holding determines ownership only.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The interest exists because the pooled client funds generated it. Public use of the proceeds and the clients’ inability to earn net interest outside the program concern the later takings inquiry, not title to the increment.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not decide whether Texas has taken the interest, whether just compensation exceeds zero, whether the program serves a public use, or whether declaratory, injunctive, or monetary relief is available.

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only whether Title II categorically covers state prisons and their programs.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 12131 includes every state department, agency, or instrumentality within “public entity,” and § 12132 covers its services, programs, and activities without a custodial exception.
