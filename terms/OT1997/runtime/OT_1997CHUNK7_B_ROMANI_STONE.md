## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Federal tax liens — estate priority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Does the federal priority statute displace the protection Congress gave a previously perfected judgment lien against a later federal tax lien?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** 31 U.S.C. §3713 and 26 U.S.C. §6323; coordinate a general priority rule with a specific tax-lien scheme.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AFFIRM the judgment creditor’s priority on the stated perfected-lien facts.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would read the specific tax-lien protections as substantive limits on the Government’s resort to the general priority statute in this setting. Otherwise the protection Congress carefully gave judgment-lien creditors would disappear whenever the debtor’s estate was insolvent, precisely when priority matters most. The Government remains entitled to collect from available assets according to the governing scheme. The result depends on the creditor actually qualifying under §6323, not merely asserting an earlier debt.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Holywell requires payment of taxes imposed on a fiduciary but does not rank every federal tax claim ahead of every lien. Hill cautions against collapsing distinct statutory classifications. The actual question is priority of these competing interests, not whether the tax debt exists.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Verify perfection, notice filing, and property coverage; an unsecured earlier claim is not equivalent to the lien here.
