## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Criminal sentencing — mixed drug conspiracy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** May a sentencing judge determine the types and quantities of drugs attributable to a conspiracy after a general verdict referring to cocaine and cocaine base?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The then-operative drug statutes and Sentencing Guidelines; jury determination of offense elements; distinguish statutory authorization from selection within the authorized range.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AFFIRM the judge’s Guidelines factfinding authority within the statutory range supported by the conviction.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would reject the idea that every sentencing fact must be treated as an element under the law then governing, while equally rejecting the claim that Guidelines findings can enlarge the offense of conviction. If the imposed sentences remain within the range supported even by the defendants’ reading of the verdict, the judge can determine the drugs actually involved for the Guidelines calculation. A different statutory-maximum problem would require separate resolution. This reasoning uses the simulation’s Witte and Gaudin boundaries without importing later sentencing decisions into 1998.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Revised Witte permits relevant-conduct consideration within the authorized statutory range but does not authorize punishment beyond it. Gaudin and Johnson preserve jury determination of actual offense elements. These holdings require checking the maximum supported by the verdict before using judge-found drug conduct to select a sentence.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No later Apprendi or Booker rule is imported; actual offense elements and the authorized ceiling remain separate constraints.
