## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Consumer credit — expiration of rescission

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Does the Truth in Lending Act’s three-year period extinguish the federal rescission right even when asserted defensively in foreclosure?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** 15 U.S.C. §1635(f), contrasted with the separate damages provision and its defensive-use language; distinguish extinguishment of a right from a time limit on suit.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AFFIRM expiration of the federal rescission right.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would enforce the distinction between a right’s duration and a procedural deadline. If Congress provides that the right itself expires after three years, recasting rescission as a defense cannot recreate it. The contrast with Congress’s express treatment of defensive damages strengthens that reading. This is not a ruling that every TILA remedy or state-law defense disappears; each has its own text. No equitable exception should be invented merely because foreclosure is the event at which the borrowers most need relief.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Reich protects access to a remedy the government held out; it does not extend a private substantive right beyond Congress’s stated duration. Here the decisive inquiry is whether §1635(f) says the right expires, not whether courts generally favor defensive recoupment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Do not import later litigation about the form of timely rescission notice or decide remedies not presented.
