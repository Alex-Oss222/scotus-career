## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Fourth Amendment — custodial arrest for fine-only licensing violation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Whether full custodial arrest pursuant to an automatic municipal policy is reasonable for the fine-only offense of operating a business without a license.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Fourth Amendment; 42 U.S.C. §1983; United States v. Watson, 423 U.S. 411 (1976); Tennessee v. Garner, 471 U.S. 1 (1985). Neither later Atwater nor later decisions about state-law arrest limitations are available OT1997 authority.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** NEWLY PROPOSED: REVERSE the categorical Fourth Amendment approval of automatic custodial arrest on this record and REMAND the remaining liability and remedy issues.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would decide the actual licensing case rather than announce that all minor offenses either require or forbid arrest. The officer had probable cause, but probable cause identifies a legal violation; it does not answer every question about the permissible seizure. The record identifies an established business operator, a license obtainable immediately, and a fine-only regulatory obligation. It identifies no failure to provide identity, attempt to flee, danger, or other reason to remove Ricci from his business. An automatic policy treating custody as the inevitable consequence of this civil licensing defect lacks a sufficient connection to securing compliance or appearance. Whren forecloses recovery merely because officers disliked the fundraising business; the objective mismatch between this policy and the seizure supplies the ground instead. Stone would reject the Village’s categorical defense while leaving its liability to the ordinary policy-and-causation requirements. Individual immunity must be assessed against 1994 notice, not retroactively defeated by this newly proposed clarification. The settled search claim and abandoned probable-cause claim remain outside the judgment.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Whren makes objective legal justification, rather than a police officer’s hidden purpose, central to the traffic-stop inquiry. It does not by itself resolve whether every civil fine violation permits custody. Wilson recognizes that historical arrest and search practices inform reasonableness without eliminating exceptions. Riverside regulates the timing of judicial probable-cause review after arrest; it does not establish that this initial custody was authorized. Lanier’s fair-warning analysis matters separately to individual damages for 1994 conduct.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This proposed merits position differs from the actual unexplained DIG. The Supreme Court’s historical order supplies no adopted reason for dismissal and no Fourth Amendment holding. Civil licensing violations and criminal misdemeanors must not be conflated.
