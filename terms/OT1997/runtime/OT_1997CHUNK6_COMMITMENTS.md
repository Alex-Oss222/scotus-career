## Almendarez-Torres v. United States — March 24, 1998

### Reach, components and common limits

All eight Associates reach the preserved statutory-classification and necessary constitutional questions through certiorari to the Fifth Circuit. The indictment omitted the prior aggravated felonies; the plea admitted them and their relation to the deportation; the sentence was 85 months. The question is whether subsection (b)(2) permits that punishment without charging the prior convictions, not whether the admitted facts were true. The 1994 text supplies the applicable twenty-year maximum; the original 1988 fifteen-year maximum must not be attributed to the 1988 enactment as twenty years. No exact found-in date or unpresented temporal challenge is supplied.

**Penalty path (P).** Section 1326(a) defines unlawful return, expressly subject to (b); (b) addresses the described alien and substitutes punishment based on prior adjudicated conviction status. Read together with the traditional recidivist sentencing distinction, that structure makes (b)(2) a penalty provision. The Constitution permits this recidivist classification, although neither McMillan nor Watts independently authorizes every statutory-ceiling increase. The preserved indictment objection fails because the prior conviction is not an element of this offense. The admission supports the bounded application, but is not used as an automatic waiver or a substitute for the classification holding.

**Element/avoidance path (E).** The structure also reasonably permits an aggravated offense: the additional fact is necessary to replace the two-year ceiling with a twenty-year ceiling. The absence of an express sentencing-factor designation and the serious constitutional question raised by that increase support the element construction. A reciprocal cross-reference can coordinate aggravated and basic offenses; it does not settle allocation. On that construction, Hamling's indictment requirement and Gaudin's actual-element rule apply. Plea-stage factual admission does not by itself cure the preserved omission from the grand jury charge. The proposed remedy is reversal of the sentence affirmance and remand for lawful sentencing on the offense charged, without selecting a replacement term or disturbing the basic guilty plea.

**The principal competing considerations.** P must answer the tenfold ceiling change and the limitation of McMillan and Watts to their particular punishment settings. It answers through the distinctive prior-adjudication and recidivism category, not a universal legislative-label rule. E must answer the genuine tradition of considering recidivism at sentencing and the statutory cross-references. It finds those relevant but insufficient to eliminate the available element reading or the substantial constitutional doubt. Old Chief shows that prejudice from prior-conviction evidence can be managed while retaining a status element; it does not decide the classification here. Custis restricts a collateral-review forum, not charging requirements. Rogers establishes neither an admission exception nor harmlessness for an omitted indictment allegation. No position adopts later law or a comprehensive rule for every sentence-enhancing fact.

## United States v. Scheffer — March 31, 1998

### Components and supported alternative paths

All eight Associates reach the constitutional question through the stated §1259 channel. Article 36(a)'s distinct statutory-validity issue is not independently decided without supported scope and preservation. The actual rule excludes results, examiner opinions and references to taking, offering or refusing an examination; **Rule 707(b) retains otherwise admissible statements made during the examination**. The accused testified to innocent ingestion. No Justice finds his testimony true or false, validates the urinalysis, or treats the examiner's conclusion as established truth.

**Sustain-and-reverse path (S).** The Constitution permits a uniform military evidentiary choice excluding this form of scientific credibility opinion. The actual first-person account remains available, and the rule preserves otherwise admissible examination statements. The reliability judgment underlying the rule and the lower military decisions, considered in the distinct setting of expert certification of truthfulness, gives the rule a sufficient justification without an individualized hearing on every tender. That conclusion accepts the reasonableness of the evidentiary choice; it is not an independent finding about scientific consensus, error rates or the accuracy of Scheffer's examination. The military judge's categorical ruling is not recharacterized as a completed reliability finding. The holding concerns the constitutional validity of applying Rule 707 to the proffer; it does not hold every polygraph inaccurate or forbidden by the Constitution.

**Foundation-review path (F).** The complete prohibition can exclude a significantly probative defense proffer without permitting a showing that this examination is reliable. The test was administered by a Government examiner before knowledge of the urine result; the accused seeks support against an attack on the testimony supplying his defense. Egelhoff's meaningful-defense rule and the distinction between excluding unreliable evidence and forbidding the foundation inquiry support maintaining the CAAF's rejection of the per se obstacle. On this path, the evidence still must satisfy lawful foundation and other ordinary requirements; no automatic admission, new trial or acquittal is ordered.

**Strongest opposing arguments and their limits.** S must answer that individualized screening is the ordinary Daubert/Joiner solution and that an all-purpose ban can suppress reliable evidence. Egelhoff expressly preserves justified reliability limits and does not equate an examiner's credibility certification with the accused's underlying testimony. F must answer that the demanded hearing itself can impose collateral disputes and that investigative use does not establish trial reliability. That objection warrants rigorous screening, but F regards it as insufficient to exclude every potentially supportable defense proffer before inquiry. Neither path adopts scientific accuracy figures, an empirical consensus finding, a prediction of this proffer's ultimate admissibility or a constitutional entitlement to every relevant item.

## Feltner v. Columbia Pictures Television, Inc. — March 31, 1998

### Questions, paths and common remedial floor

All eight Associates reach the statutory allocation and the presented jury-right question. All would **reverse the judgment insofar as it sustains denial of the demanded jury determination of statutory damages, and remand for lawful proceedings**. No one reopens the unreviewed infringement-liability summary judgment solely because the damages procedure was erroneous. The previously vacated fee/cost award retains its distinct remand. No one adopts 440 as a new Supreme Court determination, decides every work-counting question, fixes willfulness or awards a replacement amount.

**Constitutional path (C).** Read §504(c)'s references to what the court considers just, court findings and court discretion as supplying no statutory jury right in this particular damages provision. Then apply the Seventh Amendment: a private infringement action seeking compensation and punishment through statutory monetary recovery is legal in character. The statutory range substitutes a damages measure for actual-loss proof; discretion and difficulty of quantification do not turn the award into equitable relief. The jury right therefore includes the amount and the facts properly underlying that damages determination. Tull's specific governmental civil-penalty allocation does not transfer to this private damages valuation. Markman's judicial construction of an instrument preserves, rather than absorbs, the distinct factual jury function; Gasperini and Hetzel concern review of a jury valuation, not original replacement of the jury.

**Statutory path (T).** In a legal damages action, court can identify the tribunal acting through its lawful judge/jury allocation. The language need not require the judge personally to decide facts and amount; the jury can supply the verdict within the statutory rules while the court gives the resulting judgment. This fair construction avoids an unnecessary constitutional disposition. It does not erase the judge's authority to explain statutory limits, resolve genuine legal issues or apply lawful review mechanisms.

**Competing judicial-amount path (J).** Tull sustains judicial assessment of its governmental penalty and Congress could fix a sum itself. The serious objection to extending that path is that the present action seeks private legal recovery with an unfixed amount serving compensation and punishment. The power to enact a fixed measure does not make a legislature-selected adjudicator interchangeable with the jury for an otherwise protected damages function. J supplies no sufficient ground for affirmance here. C must nevertheless address the plausible institutional meaning of court; the seven C adherents find the provision's repeated particular assignments insufficient to create a statutory jury entitlement and therefore reach the Constitution. T must address those repeated judicial references; Scalia regards the institutional meaning as available in the whole civil-action setting.

## United States v. United States Shoe Corp. — March 31, 1998

The exact date-eligible statutory provisions are supplied by [the validated jurisdictional supplement](../freeze/OT_1997CHUNK6_SHOE_JURISDICTION_VALIDATED.md), read with the existing neutral facts. They add no litigant event, timing fact or merits characterization.

### Jurisdictional component

**Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer each sustain the Court of International Trade's jurisdiction under §1581(i), specifically the administration/enforcement branch in (i)(4) operating with the customs-revenue treatment Congress prescribes.** Section 4462(f)(2) expressly directs courts to treat HMT as a customs duty for jurisdiction; §4462(f)(3) separately excludes the ordinary internal-revenue tax administration route. This action challenges administration of the congressional exaction itself, not a disputed Customs determination of cargo value, classification, applicable rate or a comparable protestable decision. Customs' collection and form response on this record do not supply such a decision merely because the company called its submission a protest.

The strongest competing channel is §1581(a), because §1514(a)(3) includes charges or exactions and the company sought a refund after a Customs response. But §1514 still requires a decision of the Customs Service, and §1581(a) requires denial of a qualifying protest under the designated statute. The disputed constitutional validity of this prescribed levy does not arise from a Customs exercise of judgment on the particular shipment. The residual route therefore does not evade an available protest remedy or cure an untimely protest. No deadline failure is established, and no exception to §1514's contemporaneous ninety-day rule is announced.

This shared statutory analysis is compatible with each Justice's named merits commitment below. Scalia, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas's Steel Co. positions reinforce deciding the actual jurisdictional route before merits; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's different positions on sequencing do not supply an objection to establishing jurisdiction here. The source text's §1581(i) antidumping/countervailing exclusion and §1581(j) bar for actions under Tariff Act §305 do not describe this HMT action. Section 4462(f)(1)'s distinct regulatory qualification for customs administration and its penalty measure are not rewritten as exceptions to the unconditional jurisdictional direction in (f)(2). Treating the levy as a customs duty for jurisdiction does not decide whether it is a tax or a genuine service fee under the Export Clause.

### Merits alternatives and proposed common conclusion

With the §1581(i) channel established, all eight Associates would **affirm the export-refund judgment** at its existing scope. IBM forecloses nondiscrimination as a defense to an actual federal export tax. Pace nevertheless allows compensation for genuine governmental services with reasonable administrative approximations. The question is characterization, not whether harbor maintenance is useful or whether taxes and fees must be labeled differently.

The HMT is imposed upon export loading and is calculated solely as 0.125 percent of cargo value. Its dedicated harbor fund shows the destination of receipts; it does not establish that cargo value fairly measures the service supplied to the charged shipment. ## 3. Common alternative-path comparison

**State entitlement.** One possible path treats Ohio's mandatory hearing and recommendation as protected procedural interests. Its strongest support is the substantive importance of clemency and the State's affirmative establishment of a regular review process. Its decisive obstacle is that the required steps do not constrain the Governor to grant relief or the Authority to recommend it upon particular substantive facts. Sandin, Doe and Johnson forbid converting mandatory administration alone into the requested entitlement. All eight reject a state-created right to a favorable result or a full adjudicatory hearing. Stevens's independent-life ground is not an exception fabricated from mandatory words.

**Evitts and retained life.** The Sixth Circuit's strongest argument is that a State must administer fairly the procedures it chooses to make part of its criminal system. The strongest objection is that first appellate adjudication and subsequent executive mercy perform different functions: clemency does not adjudicate an existing right to freedom from this lawful sentence. Seven Associates reject the lower court's general integral-stage rationale; Stevens also would not make protection depend upon an open-ended measurement of distance from trial, but preserves the remand through independently retained life and liberty. The competing retained-life path preserves a narrow constitutional floor against entirely arbitrary obstruction or exclusion without creating entitlement to mercy. Its strongest objection is that it might re-create the procedural entitlement Dumschat denied. The answer of its supporters is to confine the floor to an independently retained interest and to keep access distinct from a new merits tribunal. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas decline to adopt that additional procedural-life rationale for this discretionary process, while preserving independently applicable constitutional prohibitions.

**Actual safeguards and remedy.** The strongest reversal path is that Ohio offers a real opportunity to request personal consideration, gives advance notice and conducts a required advisory review. The requested counsel, hearing-participation and document rules seek more than protection against arbitrary denial of access. The strongest remand path is the pleadings posture: the combination of short preparation, exclusion of counsel from the interview and limits on personal or documentary participation may defeat meaningful access even if every restriction considered alone is permissible. Seven Associates conclude that those stated objections do not establish arbitrary exclusion from the mercy process and require no new federal adjudicatory procedure. Stevens would preserve a bounded remand to determine meaningful access in the actual process. Neither side assumes unmentioned written-submission channels or factual board practices. No one finds Woodard entitled to clemency, a new criminal trial or a judicial merits assessment of his clemency grounds.

**Fifth Amendment.** The strongest invalid-condition path is that the sole personal interview is offered without counsel, confidentiality or immunity to a person whose truthful answers might expose him to criminal consequences. Johnson forbids dismissing such a claim solely because the benefit is discretionary, and LaChance preserves actual compulsion analysis. The strongest answer is the absence of a legal demand to attend or speak or an identified sanction attached to a valid refusal. Voluntary presentation of favorable information may involve a difficult strategic choice without compelling testimony. The lack of immunity does not itself compel speech. All eight reverse the present Fifth Amendment remand on that limited basis. None adopts a categorical rule approving every adverse inference, a compelled waiver, or later criminal use of answers actually coerced. Baxter permits only its bounded inference practice; it does not decide the legal effect of an unspecified Ohio practice. Stevens's agreement is limited to the optional procedure and absence of a supported command or penalty, not a finding that every future interview answer would be voluntary.

## Governing propositions and scope

The property-use requirement is distinct from whether the owner's affairs have economic consequences. Hinds stipulated the other offense elements but disputed this one. Russell does not establish either categorical church coverage or a categorical commercial-owner prerequisite. Its holding does not resolve the present church's actual function.

## Supported alternatives and conclusion

### A. Affirm on the church's recurrent economic operations

The strongest affirmance argument is that this is an operating institution, not simply an idle building. It pays employees, buys an employee's out-of-state insurance, buys utilities and religious materials, and annually sends funds through a national denomination. The words any activity affecting commerce are broad; Russell specifically recounts removal of a proposed business-purpose limitation. The government need not prove that this one arson substantially affected the national economy. The religious use of premises does not immunize their genuine participation in interstate transactions.

The difficulty is that the panel treated the institution's economic relationships as sufficient without identifying the function the building itself performed in those relationships. Its statement of the statute omitted the connection between property use and an activity affecting commerce. Electricity consumption, procurement, an employee's insurance and an institution's payments can establish economic relationships without establishing that the building was employed in a qualifying activity. Robinson does not make every entity that spends money an operating commercial asset within every federal criminal statute. The available account identifies neither rental, sales, fee-based services nor the building's operation as a facility for interstate transfer or some other specific commerce-affecting function. No one of those examples is a newly required universal element, and their absence does not prove that a religious building can never qualify.

**Disposition selected by all eight Associates:** The panel's reasoning does not sustain affirmance under the enacted property-use requirement. Correct the legal standard and return the case for a decision on the actual trial record, with the remedy constrained as below. ### B. Reverse immediately for evidentiary insufficiency

The strongest argument for an acquittal is that the government's reported evidence consists only of ordinary institutional inputs and charitable transfers; the trial was expressly confined to the commerce element. If that is the complete evidence of the building's function, the government has failed to prove the necessary use and cannot obtain another trial to find a new commercial function.

The present commitment does not convert the absence of an additional function in the published appellate account into a finding that the complete trial record contains none. The legal defect in the panel's analysis is established without that inference. Its factual account describes the church's operations but does not determine the property's function under the correct construction. All eight therefore favor a bounded appellate reconsideration on the existing trial record, rather than an immediate Supreme Court finding of insufficiency or an unqualified new-trial mandate. This is not a direction to the prosecution to supplement its trial evidence.

### C. Decide the Commerce Clause instead

If the statute were construed to reach the property solely because of ordinary purchases, an employee's insurance or generalized arson costs, the enacted class and the actual interstate mechanism would still require separate constitutional analysis. Lopez does not permit those generic consequences to create the missing connection. Conversely, proof that a building actually operates in an identified interstate transaction or commerce-affecting activity could supply a materially different application; nonprofit status alone defeats none of the current recognized routes. None of the Associates would use Hale's machinegun anti-evasion rationale without an identified federal regime whose operation depends on including this conduct.

On the facts presented, all eight would reject the claimed inference from ordinary property protection to impermissible government regulation of religious teaching. The governmental act is prosecution for malicious destruction under a general property-protection statute. ### A-738 — own consular rights and the older civil judgment

**Denial ground 738-D.** Paraguay has its own asserted rights; Breard's habeas default is not the answer to its suit. Execution's irreversibility and serious international interests do not supply the missing significant prospect of relief through this civil case. No definitive treaty-remedy or general immunity holding is necessary to deny this application.

The strongest competing path is preservation for review of whether effective consular assistance entails a remedy against continued enforcement of a conviction obtained without the required opportunity.
