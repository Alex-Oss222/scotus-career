## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm, narrowly and on different reasoning.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No remand. Stone would affirm on a single ground: the denial of the church’s application to rent the M.S. 206B gymnasium each Sunday for its worship services is a reasonable, viewpoint-neutral exclusion of regular worship services as a category of use outside the forum the district actually opened, on the stipulated record that District 10 has consistently never rented school facilities for religious worship services. ## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Religious worship and teaching in an opened school-access program

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches both granted questions and keeps them separate: Question 2 is answered under the Lemon test as updated in Agostini, and Question 1 under independent limited-public-forum doctrine. The answer to Question 2 does not resolve Question 1. The judgment below sustained both the services and the instruction parts of SOP 5.9 as applied to the church, with Judge Cabranes dissenting as to instruction. The church’s application, however, sought the gymnasium for its Sunday worship services, which were to include Bible preaching and teaching, and the record disclosed no request to use the premises for discussing religious material or secular matters from a religious viewpoint. Stone therefore decides the case on the worship-service questions and states the religious-instruction point as a limit on the affirmance, not as a separate judgment.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Private worship in school facilities is constitutionally permissible. Under the Lemon test as updated in Agostini, letting a private church use public school space for its own worship would not, by itself, establish religion: the school administers access to its property, and the worship remains the church’s activity rather than a service the school organizes. The district therefore cannot defend its exclusion as compelled by the Establishment Clause or by a claim that the Constitution automatically forbids worship in a public building, and avoiding an Establishment Clause violation supplies no sufficient reason for excluding the church’s private worship. For the same reason, the Second Circuit’s rationale that the school may avoid identification with a particular church cannot support the exclusion absent facts establishing genuine governmental adoption. That removes the Establishment Clause objection to private worship; it does not create a right to use every public building. The free-speech claim turns on the forum the district actually opened. A limited public forum may be confined by reasonable, viewpoint-neutral limits on the categories of activity it admits, and the district is not necessarily required to open such a forum to regular worship services. On the stipulated record, excluding regular worship services as a category of use outside the forum actually opened is a reasonable, viewpoint-neutral subject-matter limit; the secular community uses the district admitted are not comparable worship services. The category must be defined by the activity—a worship service as such—not by religious content, identity or perspective. Once the district admits instruction on a particular subject, it cannot exclude an otherwise eligible speaker simply for teaching that subject from a religious perspective, so the affirmance does not approve the religious-instruction exclusion. If the record established that the forum was actually open to comparable activities, Stone would reverse the church’s exclusion.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Permitting a private church to use public school facilities for its own worship does not by itself establish religion under the Lemon test as updated in Agostini, so a school district cannot justify excluding the church’s worship as required by the Establishment Clause, or by an interest in avoiding the school’s identification with the church, absent facts showing genuine governmental adoption. A district that has created a limited public forum may nonetheless exclude regular worship services as a category of use through a reasonable, viewpoint-neutral limit it has consistently applied, where the category is defined by the activity of a worship service as such and the forum has not been opened to comparable activities; but once the district admits instruction on a subject, it may not exclude an otherwise eligible speaker for teaching that subject from a religious perspective.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** On the Establishment Clause question, Agostini directs the inquiry to the governmental act and the function it performs: government may not organize worship as its own activity, but private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context and not individually decisive. Here the governmental act would be renting school property to an outside congregation under the district’s facility-use rules, as the district had already done for the church’s sports programs and banquet; the services—hymn singing, communion, Bible reading, Bible preaching and teaching—would be the church’s own, sought by its pastor for its congregation each Sunday. Nothing in the record shows official selection, control, adoption, preference or obscured sponsorship. Tanford v. Brand marks the contrast: there the university selected a religious leader and incorporated prayer into its own commencement, and Tanford itself keeps neutral access to a genuine forum distinct from official worship. Lamb’s Chapel, Good News and Church on the Rock hold that equal private access on terms of real private sponsorship does not establish religion; Chabad holds that location alone does not make clearly private expression governmental; and Pinette supplies no automatic attribution from location. Because those holdings reserved standalone worship, the conclusion here rests on the attribution-and-function inquiry of the Lemon test as updated in Agostini, which they support. The Second Circuit did not rest on an Establishment Clause defense—it said there was “no need” for the Board to rely on the Clause—but it counted toward the exclusion’s reasonableness an interest in avoiding “the identification of a middle school with a particular church” and in the effect on middle-school children of designating their school as a church. Stone rejects that rationale: it makes location and religious identity decisive, which the updated Lemon test does not permit, and it treats private worship as the school’s without any fact of genuine governmental adoption. Good News held that an 11–15-year-old audience did not, on its record, convert a private after-school program into school speech; the ages of M.S. 206B’s students do not make an outside congregation’s Sunday rental the school’s worship. On the free-speech question, the exclusion survives instead through independent forum doctrine. Lamb’s Chapel does not require an unlimited forum or invalidate neutral restrictions on subjects and activities outside the actual forum; Good News holds that schools need not open every facility to every religious activity; Church on the Rock preserves standalone worship reservations; and Good News, applying Perry and Cornelius, permits genuine, reasonable and viewpoint-neutral management of a limited forum. Section 414 and SOPs 5.6.1 and 5.6.2 define this forum by instruction in any branch of education, learning or the arts and by social, civic and recreational meetings and entertainments and other community-welfare uses; § 414 does not list worship; and the parties stipulated that District 10 has consistently never rented school facilities for religious worship services. The uses actually admitted in 1994-95—tutoring, Scout and Beacon character training, Greek language and history classes, a Black History Month program, concerts, drug and AIDS workshops and GED classes—include no worship service or comparable activity. Grace Lutheran Church and the church itself used the facilities for admitted purposes, and SOP 5.9 expressly permits outside groups to discuss religious material and material containing a religious viewpoint. On that stipulated record, a congregation’s regular worship service is a category of use the district never admitted, not a religious viewpoint on an admitted subject, and confining the forum to its designated instructional, civic, social, recreational and community-welfare uses is a reasonable, viewpoint-neutral subject-matter limit without reliance on the identification rationale. That category is defined by the activity as a worship service as such, not by its religious content: Good News treats prayer and Bible instruction within an admitted moral-development program as a religious viewpoint, and Church on the Rock treats teaching a faith or inviting conversion or prayer within an admitted program the same way. Question 1’s reference to comparable secular community activities does not change the result on this record, because none of the admitted uses is a comparable worship service. The religious-instruction clause stands differently. The forum admits instruction in any branch of education, and the district admitted Girl Scout and Beacon character training and, as Judge Cabranes’s opinion records, Boy Scout ethical education. Under Lamb’s Chapel, Good News, Church on the Rock and Rosenberger, once a subject is admitted, an otherwise eligible speaker cannot be excluded for teaching it from a religious perspective; a clause permitting secular instruction on character and ethics while barring religious instruction on the same subjects excludes the religious perspective. Judge Cabranes dissented below on that ground; his separate opinion is recorded as the lower-court view, not as authority. Because the church’s application sought the gymnasium for worship services, Stone states that rule as a limit on the affirmance rather than as a separate judgment. The narrow result therefore does not depend on Lemon’s former independent prongs; it survives through independent public-forum doctrine, and its reasoning respects the Lemon test as updated in Agostini.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The affirmance is limited to the regular-worship-service category on the stipulated record of consistent exclusion, a record covering a single school year. If the record established that the forum was actually open to comparable activities, the rule of Lamb’s Chapel, Good News and Chabad against excluding an otherwise eligible religious viewpoint from access actually opened to comparable expression would control, and Stone would reverse the church’s exclusion. The position does not approve SOP 5.9’s religious-instruction exclusion as applied to subjects the forum admits, and it does not adopt the Second Circuit’s identification-with-a-church rationale. Genuine governmental adoption—official selection, control, adoption, preference or obscured sponsorship—could change the Establishment Clause answer, and government organization of worship as its own activity remains prohibited. The district keeps reasonable, viewpoint-neutral facility, scheduling, safety and supervision rules; the position creates no right to use every public building, no duty to keep a forum open and no public financing of devotion. The Free Exercise, Equal Protection and RFRA rulings below are outside the granted questions and are not addressed.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Does LMRA §301(a) confer jurisdiction over a union suit seeking to invalidate a collective-bargaining agreement without alleging its violation?
