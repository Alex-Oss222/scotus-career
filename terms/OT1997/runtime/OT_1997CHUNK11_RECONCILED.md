### Full temporal objection and answer

The Fourth Circuit supplied three distinct reasons: the statute governs filing rather than earlier provider conduct; the prohibition on bringing an inconsistent cause expressly directs application to later-filed complaints; and claim elimination does not impose a retrospective consequence because an unfiled claim is not vested and Zeran did not show reliance. Each has been considered, rather than bypassed by invoking Landgraf's name.

The best statutory argument for AOL is real: then-§230(d)(3) expressly governs bringing a cause and imposing liability, this AOL action began April 23, 1996, and Congress favored immediate protection against publication-related suits. A temporal instruction need not use prescribed words. Lindh confirms that statutory structure and contextual contrast can supply direction before the default presumption is reached. The absence of an express phrase naming pre-enactment conduct is therefore not itself decisive, and no super-clear-statement requirement is adopted.

The complete text nevertheless does not select the filing date as the exclusive temporal event. Subsection (d)(3) preserves consistent state law and bars inconsistent causes and liability; its two commands state the preemptive operation of the substantive publisher-treatment rule. They do not separately identify which earlier conduct is governed by that new rule. The prohibition on imposing liability is likewise not a freestanding instruction to extinguish every earlier claim whenever adjudication occurs after enactment. The statute supplies no contextual pending-case contrast comparable to the one that established direction in Lindh. Its findings, policies, good-faith provision, preservation clauses and definitions explain substantive scope and purposes but add no transition prescription for completed conduct.

The effect asserted here is elimination of an otherwise legally sufficient claim arising from earlier conduct, not merely allocation to another forum, exhaustion before filing or withdrawal of a litigation subsidy. Landgraf protects against impairment of existing rights as well as increased liability or new duties. Hughes specifically rejects a later-filing and jurisdictional-label shortcut where a change alters a complete defense and the substantive enforcement relation. Hughes withdrew a defense rather than adding one, but that difference does not make the claim holder's side of the legal relation disappear. Rivers rejects an actual-reliance prerequisite and the inference that a corrective legislative purpose alone settles temporal reach. The Fourth Circuit's ability-to-legislate/vesting point addresses a different question from the statutory presumption. Congress may direct retrospective operation subject to constitutional limits; the present holding is that it has not sufficiently done so here.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the common result by reading the enforcement clause with the actual rule it enforces: legal responsibility for publication-related conduct. The act of filing is not the sole relevant activity simply because Congress framed its substantive protection through causes and liability. They retain their own method, rather than treating the general framework's five-Justice history as unanimous. The remaining five adopt the sequential framework and concrete consequences analysis.
