## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Fourteenth Amendment personhood before live birth

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional question and ordinary review of the state remedial line.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Hold that the Fourteenth Amendment’s reference to persons does not include a fetus that was not born alive under controlling constitutional law. A State may use live birth as an administrable boundary for estate and derivative wrongful-death actions while preserving claims belonging to the pregnant person.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A stillborn fetus is not a Fourteenth Amendment person, and a State’s live-birth prerequisite for fetal estate and wrongful-death remedies is rationally related to administrable legal personhood and causation boundaries.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The constitutional text, precedent, and legal structure at this date do not establish prenatal constitutional personhood. New Jersey’s line fixes when an independent legal estate arises and avoids relitigating uncertain status through variable medical facts. The classification is imperfect but not a forbidden purpose or irrational sham.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not deny the State power to protect potential life, recognize broader state-law remedies, or adjudicate the mother’s own bodily-injury, emotional-distress, and malpractice claims.
