## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Meaning of willful and malicious injury

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only whether negligent or reckless malpractice satisfies the statutory willfulness requirement. He reserves substantial certainty and the independent meaning of “malicious.”
- **Stone's Legal Position:** “Willful” modifies “injury.” The creditor therefore must establish a deliberate or intentional injury, not merely a voluntary act that negligently or recklessly produces harm.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A debt arising from negligently or recklessly inflicted injury does not satisfy § 523(a)(6)’s willfulness requirement. This malpractice debt is dischargeable under that provision.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The judgment establishes malpractice rather than injury-directed intent. Reading willful to describe only the underlying treatment decision would collapse the statutory distinction between intentional injury and negligent or reckless harm.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not decide subjective knowledge of substantial certainty, define “malicious,” discharge a debt based on proved injury-directed intent, or foreclose another independently pleaded discharge exception.

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory § 3109 ground and the Fourth Amendment question. He does not decide the remedy for materially different destruction.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 3109 incorporates the traditional exigency exceptions. Under Richards, the suspected presence of a violent fugitive with access to weapons supplied case-specific reasonable suspicion. Property damage does not increase that entry threshold, but its nature and extent remain part of overall execution reasonableness.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Property damage does not increase the reasonable-suspicion showing required for a no-knock entry, although it remains subject to overall Fourth Amendment reasonableness. Section 3109 incorporates the traditional exigency exceptions, which were satisfied here.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reliable information placed a violent escaped prisoner with access to firearms in Ramirez’s home. Breaking one garage window to prevent access to the reported weapons was proportionate to that danger on this record. The single act of damage therefore did not make the otherwise justified entry unreasonable.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not authorize gratuitous, punitive, disproportionate, or objective-unrelated destruction, validate an entry lacking case-specific suspicion, or decide the remedy for materially different destruction.

### Issue 1: Criminal law — Assimilative Crimes Act

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** May the Assimilative Crimes Act import Louisiana’s first-degree-murder offense when federal murder law already punishes the killing?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** 18 U.S.C. §§13 and 1111; identify the conduct punished by federal law and whether assimilation would fill a gap or interfere with Congress’s offense scheme.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** REVERSE the assimilation ruling and REMAND for proceedings under the proper federal offense.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would read the Assimilative Crimes Act as a gap-filling statute. The existence of a more particular state label does not establish a federal gap if Congress already addressed this killing through its graded murder provisions. Assimilation that displaces that grading changes Congress’s chosen criminal scheme. At the same time, the mere existence of some federal criminal law cannot categorically foreclose assimilation of genuinely different conduct. The correct comparison is concrete: what acts and distinctions do the two laws regulate, and would adding the state offense complement or supplant federal law?
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Wells requires enforcing the enacted federal offense rather than supplying an additional element or route by preference. Here the inquiry is statutory occupation of the homicide field at issue, not whether state law better describes the victim’s age. Gaudin preserves jury determination of any elements that remain material after identifying the proper offense.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No acquittal of all homicide liability is implied. Verify the actual verdict before selecting the replacement judgment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Facially incriminating redacted codefendant confession

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the confrontation question. He does not decide harmless error.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Bruton applies when a conspicuous blank, deletion, symbol, or equivalent substitution directly identifies the defendant from the confession and its immediate context. Richardson remains limited to a confession neutral on its face that becomes incriminating only through later evidence.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A non-testifying codefendant’s confession remains within Bruton when a conspicuous blank, deletion, symbol, or equivalent substitution directly identifies the defendant from the statement and its immediate context. A limiting instruction does not cure that confrontation violation.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The blanks, grammar, limited number of participants, and prosecutor’s immediate linkage directly identified Gray. The jury did not need later independent evidence to infer whose name had been removed, so the limiting instruction could not cure the uncross-examined accusation.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not prohibit joint trials, every redaction, genuinely neutral substitute wording, independently admissible confessions, or testimony tested by cross-examination. It does not decide harmless error.
