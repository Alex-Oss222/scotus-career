# 154. Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County

*Stone pre-vote case brief. Alternate-history petition and merits treatment, not an actual Supreme Court merits decision.*

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. Status

- **Version:** OT1997 Stone-only forecast, version 2. **Approval:** **PROPOSED AND APPROVED** in the simulation, not an actual judicial decision. The revised position stated below was user-approved 2026-10-30.

## B. Judgment and remedy

- **Stone's vote:** **Vacate and remand the judgments invalidating the BBE and HBE programs under *Croson*; affirm the WBE judgment on the inadequate sex-discrimination showing.** To reach that result Stone applies the common substantial-justification standard that *Adarand* (1995) adopted for federal, state and local racial classifications. *Adarand* displaced *Croson*'s categorical strict-scrutiny formulation while retaining its demand for relevant evidence.
- **Remedy:** Maintain the injunction against race-conscious preferences pending constitutionally adequate, program-specific findings on remand, with authority to narrow or dissolve it only as a supported program is shown to fit. Leave the injunction against the WBE program in force. Permit lawful race-neutral outreach and procurement measures unaffected by the holding.

## C. Issue positions

### Issue 1: Racial repair and the standard of justification

- **Reach:** Merits. The challengers' unequal contracting opportunity is a legally cognizable injury, but it does not predetermine that every corrective classification is unconstitutional.
- **Received law and its application:** *Croson* imposed categorical strict scrutiny on Dade County as a local government; *Adarand* displaced that formulation, retaining *Croson*'s demand for relevant evidence, and adopted one standard for federal, state and local racial classifications. Stone applies that standard to supported, genuinely corrective local contracting measures. As applied here, it requires lawful local authority, objective evidence of identified exclusion or its continuing effects in the relevant procurement market, a substantial relationship between the chosen classification and actual remedy, reasonable limits on eligibility and duration, and meaningful opportunity for nonbeneficiaries. The government bears the burden of justification. This is an application of the governing *Adarand* standard, not a change to it.
- **Proposed holding:** The Constitution permits individual contract preferences when the County satisfies those conditions. The availability of race-neutral alternatives matters to fit but does not automatically invalidate a supported corrective program.
- **Application:** Dade offered multiple quantitative studies, some earlier Black-owned-firm receipt and owner-income disparities, and explanations for improved post-program results. The trial court evaluated the record through *Croson*'s strict-scrutiny frame and treated many disparities as inadequate under that frame. Stone will not presume the County wins or erase the trial court's factual findings under ordinary clear-error review. He would remand to reassess the legal sufficiency of the existing record, permitting findings strictly as the record and ordinary procedure allow, regarding firm-size explanations, the actual operation of goals or set-asides, the different evidence for Black and Hispanic contractors, and nonbeneficiaries' genuine opportunities under the governing *Adarand* standard.
- **Boundary:** This is not a judicial command to continue affirmative action, a constitutional right to proportional contracts, or immunity from discrimination challenges. BBE and HBE programs may still fail after the correct assessment. Record support for one does not automatically validate the other.

### Issue 2: Women-owned contracting preferences

- **Reach:** Distinct sex-classification claim.
- **Stone's legal position:** Apply received intermediate scrutiny. *Adarand*'s common racial standard does not silently replace the sex-specific rule.
- **Proposed holding and application:** Correcting identified gender exclusion may be an important governmental objective, but this judgment rests on the trial court's insufficient probative record for the WBE preference, not on a categorical prohibition of targeted repair. The appellate court found no clear error, including after analysis of the evidence actually offered. Stone therefore affirms WBE invalidation on the present record.
- **Boundary:** A later supported, differently designed women-owned-firm program is not decided.

## D. Certiorari position

- **Approved vote:** **Grant both petitions and consolidate** for review in the simulation.
- **Ground:** The case squarely presents whether the lower courts' rigid application of *Croson*'s displaced strict-scrutiny formulation mistakes an identified unequal opportunity for an automatic constitutional defeat of local repair.
- **Status:** Final Stone-only position.
