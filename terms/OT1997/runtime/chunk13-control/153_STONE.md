# 153. Libertarian Party of Illinois v. Rednour

*Stone pre-vote case brief. Alternate-history petition and merits treatment, not an actual Supreme Court merits decision.*

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. Status

- **Version:** OT1997 Stone-only forecast, version 2. **Approval:** **PROPOSED AND APPROVED** for the simulation, not adopted precedent.

## B. Judgment and remedy

- **Stone's vote:** **Reverse the Seventh Circuit and remand for relief against the challenged cumulative ballot-access burden.** The constitutional problem is not a five-percent petition in isolation. It is Illinois's use of gubernatorial-vote party recognition, district-by-district new-party designation, a substantially more demanding general-election nomination petition and additional slate requirements to give long-established parties a preferential electoral pathway.
- **Remedy:** Require Illinois to offer the plaintiffs a lawful, realistic congressional general-election nomination route without the unjustified accumulation of incumbent-party advantages. The State may demand a reasonable showing of district support and administer valid filing deadlines. On remand it may select a narrower petition requirement, sever any unjustified slate condition, or supply another constitutionally adequate route. No completed election is reopened and Stone does not place every would-be candidate on the ballot without qualification.

## C. Issue positions

### Issue 1: A nominal access route cannot disguise established-party entrenchment

- **Reach:** The recurring merits challenge to the whole congressional access pathway, not an abstract comparison of two percentages.
- **Stone's legal position:** Apply the *Anderson-Burdick* inquiry with *Norman*'s insistence that legally distinct electoral constituencies and properly qualified candidacies cannot be conflated. *Jenness* permits a workable five-percent route on its facts, not every regime that attaches that number to a filing form. *Timmons* recognized a limited nomination regulation precisely because other meaningful routes of party participation remained.
- **Proposed holding:** The State may request genuine district-level evidence of electoral support. It cannot multiply requirements against a recognized minor party through a gubernatorial-vote status rule, compulsory associated nominations and disproportionate petition demands when the combined effect burdens electoral association and the additional constraints are not shown necessary to a real administrative interest. A route need not be literally impossible before it imposes an unconstitutional comparative burden.
- **Application:** Illinois's established parties can organize congressional nomination through a much lower primary-petition gate while the Libertarian slate confronts a separate, substantially heavier general-ballot condition. The petitions use different denominators and functions, so a straight ten-to-one numerical comparison alone does not establish the violation. But the State still must justify the combination rather than point to its existence: governor-only statewide party recognition does not necessarily measure support for a particular congressional candidate; slate-linked rules can penalize independent qualification; and two minor-party successes in 1994 show possibility, not equality of political opportunity. The Seventh Circuit's summary-judgment treatment discounted the constitutional cost of the structure as a whole.
- **Boundary:** Illinois need not let candidates evade a fair demonstration of support, abolish party primaries, recognize a party for all local offices based solely on one unrelated statewide result, or suppress protection against electoral confusion. It must design the cumulative burden to serve election administration rather than the survival of existing competitors.

### Issue 2: Local support is relevant, but established-party status is not a constitutional title

- **Stone's proposed holding:** States may distinguish statewide from district support, but they cannot use a party-label classification as a self-justifying means of reserving the favorable nomination pathway to existing major parties. A legally sufficient district-specific demonstration of support must be considered on its own terms, consistently with *Norman*.
- **Application:** Illinois can verify support in the constituency electing its representative. It cannot answer the claim of burdensome access by pointing only to failure to reach the governor-based statewide threshold and disregarding the relative difficulty imposed on parties attempting lawful entry. The claim concerns election administration and political participation, not a demand to add substantive personal qualifications for Congress.

## D. Certiorari position

- **Approved vote:** **Grant** in the alternate-history simulation.
- **Reason:** Prevent demonstrated-support doctrine from serving as a shield for cumulative restrictions that preferentially preserve established-party control of the ballot.
- **Status:** Revised proposed and approved Stone-only forecast.
