## Statutory actors, independent grounds, and defense

**Primary fraud.** The lower opinion affirms intentional material nondisclosures and a coordinated deceptive scheme under §17(a)(1), §10(b), and Rule 10b-5, with the operative judgment expressed under §17(a), §10(b), and Rule 10b-5. The conduct satisfies the stated scienter requirement without resolving any lesser-culpability claim. Section 17(a) addresses conduct in the offer or sale of securities; §10(b)/Rule 10b-5 address deception in connection with a purchase or sale. A bare omission does not automatically establish primary liability: the lower opinion identifies the duty to speak, the misleading implications of the sales, materiality, transaction connection, and intentional concealment. See 101 F.3d at 1466–1468, 1471–1472 (lines 179–219, 255–263).

**Independent controller liability.** Section 20(a), 15 U.S.C. §78t(a), makes a person who directly or indirectly controls a person liable under the Exchange Act or its rules jointly and severally liable to the same extent as the controlled person, subject to the express defense. The beneficiary language reaches a person to whom the controlled person is liable; §3(a)(9), 15 U.S.C. §78c(a)(9), includes governmental agencies in the relevant definition of person. The lower opinion accordingly recognizes the SEC's enforcement authority, relying on *SEC v. Management Dynamics, Inc.*, 515 F.2d 801, 812 (2d Cir. 1975), and identifies the contrary circuit position in *SEC v. Coffey*, 493 F.2d 1304, 1318 (6th Cir. 1974). This is an actual textual actor question, not an assumption that every private securities rule automatically applies to government enforcement. See 101 F.3d at 1472–1473 (lines 268–277); F3.

**Both defense conditions are indispensable.** The statutory exception requires that the controller acted in good faith **and** did not directly or indirectly induce the violating acts. Neither condition substitutes for the other. The proved primary violation, actual control, and Brennan's deliberate participation establish the presented predicate without resolving broader disputes over pleading or the culpable-participation burden. The lower court's analysis of a reasonable, enforced supervisory system explains good faith in these facts; it is not the statutory defense's entire text. See id. at 1472–1474 (lines 268–285).

**Why the compliance defense fails here.** The compliance department used interdealer quotations without distinguishing markets the firm dominated; it checked acquisition prices only on the resale day, although the firm's repurchases occurred days earlier. Scripted sales, restricted information, and misleading questionnaires also supported the finding that compliance was cosmetic. These are actual findings combined with Brennan's orchestration, not an irrebuttable inference from imperfect supervision. Id. at 1473–1474 (lines 279–285).

**Do not extend §20(a) to §17(a).** The independent controller ground stated in this record is liability for §10(b) and Rule 10b-5 violations. Section 20(a)'s quoted text refers to its own chapter. Brennan's primary participation separately supports the securities-fraud judgment. Id. at 1472 (line 268); F2–F3.

## Preclusion and the settlement

The 1979 administrative proceeding concerned earlier transactions; the later transactions occurred in 1982–1985 and could not have been pleaded in 1979 or proved at hearings ending in 1980. Similar methods and some overlapping issuer names do not turn distinct transactions into the same claim. The fact that some later conduct preceded the 1984 settlement does not make supplementation compulsory. *Lawlor v. National Screen Service Corp.*, 349 U.S. 322, 328–329 (1955), supplies the separate-transaction principle; *Federated Department Stores, Inc. v. Moitie*, 452 U.S. 394, 398 (1981), supplies finality for the claim actually concluded. See 101 F.3d at 1462–1465 (lines 138–174); F1.

The 1984 consent promised dismissal of the 1979 proceeding and barred an administrative proceeding based on the specified Geosearch consent injunction; the instrument said the SEC made no other promises. That is not a general release of all 1982–1985 fraud or a promise never to enforce the securities laws against defendants again. Settlement may reach matters outside pleadings if the parties actually agree, but the reported language and correspondence show no such broader agreement here. Id. at 1462–1465 (lines 143–174).

The frozen copy has several apparent transcription or citation defects: it varies between 1975–1978 and 1975–1979 for the earlier transactions, gives Rule 15(c) for supplemental pleading, and contains distorted case-report numerals. The holdings need none of these discrepancies. Use “earlier transactions” and the accurately supplied case names/report citations; do not make a new factual correction or reproduce a questionable rule subdivision from this copy.

## Gain recovery, interest, and prevention remain separate

**Gain recovery.** The lower opinion permits a reasonable approximation of profits causally connected to proved violations, with calculation uncertainty attributable to wrongdoing borne by the wrongdoer. It distinguishes gain stripping from compensation fixed by a class settlement. Its application supports the actual credit and recovery of the remainder; it does not establish that every class payment must always receive the same treatment regardless of overlap. The freeze requires traditional restitutionary authority and connected gains, not deterrence alone. See 101 F.3d at 1474–1476 (lines 294–306); F4.

**Interest.** The award and rate remain matters of equitable discretion subject to fairness, relative equities, the statutory purpose, and the benefit actually retained. The lower opinion assumed for argument's sake that the SEC might have caused some delay; it did not find such fault, and it rejected the requested reduction because retained use continued. Thus neither the large ratio nor the long period alone proves punishment, but neither the IRS rate nor charging through every possible delay becomes mandatory. Do not invent compounding details, a precise rate schedule, a Treasury-bill alternative amount, or a new numerical constitutional test. See id. at 1476–1477 (lines 311–319); F5.

**Prospective injunction.** The legal trigger is a supported likelihood of continuing violations absent an injunction. Concrete support includes deliberate systematic fraud, prior regulatory sanctions, continued operation as a broker-dealer under Brennan's ownership, retained key personnel, and Brennan's ongoing securities activity despite sale of most retail branches. These findings support the prospective §17(a), §10(b), and Rule 10b-5 injunction. The later-law freeze requires independent prevention findings; contesting liability or refusing to confess alone is insufficient. No industry-wide exclusion or order directed to unrelated actors is supplied. See id. at 1461, 1477–1478 (lines 125, 324–329); F6.

## Special Agent: explain the existing judgment's boundary only

The district court proposed an investigator to find additional unpleaded 1982–1987 fraud and recommend further disgorgement after the pleaded claims and their gains had already been adjudicated. The Second Circuit reversed that component and otherwise affirmed. The present defendants already possess that favorable result; the neutral packet and F7 provide no SEC cross-petition to reopen it. The disposition should preserve the Second Circuit judgment, not reinstate the district decree in full and not manufacture a new merits reversal of the agent. See 101 F.3d at 1478–1479 (lines 334–351); F7.

If useful to explain the retained boundary, distinguish these actors and functions:

| Actor or device | Supported boundary |
|---|---|
| SEC under §21(a), 15 U.S.C. §78u(a)(1) | May investigate possible securities violations; this allocation does not appoint the court's agent. |
| SEC under §4(b), 15 U.S.C. §78d(b)(1) | May appoint officers, attorneys, examiners, and experts needed for its functions, within the statute's authority. |
| Court personnel under appropriate judicial authority | Rule 53 masters may assist adjudication; trustees may oversee compliance with the judgment. These functions differ from a new retrospective search for uncharged claims. |
| Consent-based officer | The lower opinion expressly reserves court-approved consensual investigative arrangements, illustrated by *Handler v. SEC*, 610 F.2d 656, 659–660 (9th Cir. 1979). No categorical condemnation follows here. |

All distinctions come from id. at 1478–1479 (lines 336–347). Disgorgement is a remedy for an established claim; it is not itself a new cause of action. *Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools*, 503 U.S. 60, 69 (1992), quoting *Davis v. Passman*, 442 U.S. 228, 239 (1979), supplies that analytic distinction in the lower opinion. The present record need not independently adjudicate the investigator's legality to leave its removal intact.

## Holding-drafting pitfalls to avoid

1. Do not import the historical *Central Bank* categorical accessory-liability result, its historical authorship, or a private plaintiff's reliance requirement into this SEC action. The freeze identifies the governing current-law domains and rests on Brennan's own proved deception and orchestration.
2. Do not turn either a five-percent guideline or a ten-percent historical proposition into a universal fraud rule. The supported case concerns dominated markets, negligible independent trading, deliberate concealment, and contemporaneous repurchase prices; illiquidity was within the firm's unilateral control and did not independently explain these prices. 101 F.3d at 1469–1471 (lines 229–245); F2.
3. State both the primary-participation and independent controller grounds, with their separate statutory scope and the complete two-part defense. Do not make ownership the scienter finding or the defense's defeat.
4. State $27,288,099 identified unlawful profit before the settlement credit, the $5,000,000 January 1987 payment, $22,288,099 remaining disgorgement, and $52,689,894 interest distinctly. The aggregate remaining award is $74,977,993; it is not another $27,288,099 assessment and is collectible jointly and severally only once.
5. Tie joint liability to this coordinated enterprise, Brennan's actual participation and corresponding enrichment. Do not announce recovery of everyone's gross receipts, unrelated profits, or wealth as such.
6. State that interest on the credited payment ended at payment. Preserve discretion, fairness, causal limits, and the assumed character of the SEC-delay contention.
7. Supply the concrete recurrence findings for the injunction. The amount of gains, a high number of past transactions, or defendants' refusal to confess alone does not replace that showing.
8. Preserve the Second Circuit's split disposition. F7 is an undisturbed favorable component, not a fresh holding that every investigative appointment is unlawful. Any discussion must retain the exceptions for legitimate adjudicative assistance, judgment compliance, SEC investigation, and consensual arrangements.
9. Keep the lower court's historical statements distinct from the rule the frozen commitments authorize. An appellate fact recitation supports this record's findings; it is not a substitute for statutory text or a new comprehensive securities-remedy code.
