# 158. Securities and Exchange Commission v. First Jersey Securities, Inc.

*Stone pre-vote case brief. Alternate-history petition and merits treatment, not an actual Supreme Court merits decision.*

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. Status

- **Version:** OT1997 Stone-only forecast, version 2. **Approval:** **PROPOSED AND APPROVED** for the simulation, not binding Court law.

## B. Judgment and remedy

- **Stone's vote:** **Affirm the Second Circuit judgment in full**: securities fraud liability, the proven disgorgement and interest, and the future-violation injunction stand; the unauthorized investigative Special Agent remains reversed.
- **Remedy:** Enforce one total recovery of approximately $22.29 million in unlawful profit and the judicially approved prejudgment interest, accounting for the class settlement credit and no double collection of joint-and-several sums. Keep the injunction tied to securities law and demonstrated recurrence risk; do not order a court-led search for other transactions.

## C. Issue positions

### Issue 1: Corporate status is not fraud immunity

- **Reach:** Statutory merits, after ordinary record and preclusion review.
- **Proposed holding:** The SEC can obtain relief under the enacted antifraud provisions upon proof of deceptive dealing. An officer's statutory controlling-person exposure and primary participation follow their own elements; corporate ownership alone is neither culpability nor a shield.
- **Application:** Brennan's intimate direction of the coordinated trading practices and ownership/control supply the case-specific basis for § 20(a) liability. Stone does not treat shareholders as liable for every company debt or imagine a personal political-membership right entitling management to trade fraudulently.
- **Boundary:** A passive investor or good-faith control person is different; the statute's actual defense remains available on evidence.

### Issue 2: Large restitution must remain restitution

- **Stone's position:** Equity may remove established unlawful gain and its time value to the extent the statutory enforcement scheme permits; the judgment must not become punishment for uncharged wrongdoing.
- **Proposed holding and application:** The evidence supports an approximation of the six-security profits; the prior $5 million settlement was credited. Interest reflects years of possession of the unlawful money rather than simply multiplying a fine, and the appellate record describes why the IRS borrowing measure was used. No identified calculation or legal error requires Stone to substitute the more favorable Treasury lending rate.
- **Boundary:** Disgorgement cannot reach unproved trades, collect twice for one recovery, or acquire an invented punitive component merely because the defendant is rich.

### Issue 3: Prosecution and adjudication remain separate

- **Stone's proposed holding:** A trial court's power to appoint a master to perform judicial work does not include appointing an investigator empowered to find new historical violations and recommend new charges. Congress assigned federal securities investigation to the SEC.
- **Application:** The District Court proposed investigating 1982–1987 transactions not charged and not proven at trial. Stone agrees that this role exceeds the adjudicative channel and would compromise institutional separation.
- **Boundary:** The SEC may conduct investigations Congress authorizes, subject to governing procedural protections; the court may use a proper master to administer its actual decree.

## D. Certiorari position

- **Approved vote:** **Grant** in the simulation.
- **Ground:** Clarify lawful reach and limits of extraordinary civil-financial remedies after proved securities misconduct.
- **Status:** Final Stone-only forecast.
