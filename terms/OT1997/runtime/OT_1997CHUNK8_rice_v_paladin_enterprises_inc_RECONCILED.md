## Justice-specific reconciliation

The common ground distinguishes purposeful operational participation in independently unlawful conduct from abstract advocacy. Brandenburg's imminence requirements remain intact for advocacy; they do not create an absolute defense to the different combination of purposeful assistance and actual assisting use assumed for this motion. Giboney and Claiborne Hardware supply the participation and protected-advocacy boundary; the generally applicable character of tort law alone is insufficient. No lesser mental-state rule is decided.

The eight commitments reject that extension while preserving its concern that advocacy must not be relabeled participation. The narrow stipulated purpose, concrete assistance, actual use and applicable tort causal requirements do the limiting work. An opinion must retain all those qualifications; intent alone or publication alone is insufficient.
