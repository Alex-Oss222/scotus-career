### Operative rules, alternatives, and preserved distinctions

**Parole and protest letters.** The strongest broader-remand argument invokes Sandin's rejection of mechanical mandatory-word analysis and the substantive public-safety/eligibility criteria in Texas law. The answer is substantive, not verbal: those conditions limit when release may be granted, but do not materially restrict the Board's authority to deny the initial release sought here. No release has already conferred the community liberty protected in Young. Conversely, the absence of entitlement does not decide whether a distinct First Amendment penalty is permissible. All eight will join the disposition only with that separation.

The protest-letter judgment can rest on ordinary rational review without deciding that no classification could ever exist absent an antecedent group. The strongest objection is the trial finding that some statements are inaccurate, vindictive, or unrelated to its preferred criteria. That finding does not establish that every receipt or consideration of a statement lacks a legitimate relation to parole judgment, or that the broad exclusion remedy follows. The decision does not approve knowing official falsification, racial discrimination, punishment of protected litigation, use of a judicially invalidated conviction in defiance of a binding order, or every conceivable use of an individual false statement. Those distinct claims are not established by this protest-letter class judgment.

Article 42.18 §8(f)(2)'s mandatory victim-related notice, permission for written and oral participation, absence of a numerical ceiling on written speakers, and mandatory consideration of the victim-impact statement are separate commands and permissions. Section 8(e)'s file-inclusion requirements do not command reliance on every included allegation. Section 18(a)'s confidentiality is not a finding that all claimed accuracy defects are immaterial under every possible constitutional theory.

**Retaliation and access.** A State's discretion to deny a benefit does not permit denial because of independently protected conduct. The claimant must identify that conduct, show the challenged adverse act and its substantial or motivating connection, and establish the ordinary injury and attribution predicates. The officials may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without the protected conduct. A potentially sufficient lawful parole reason, without an actual counterfactual showing, does not satisfy that defense. Ordinary competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available; no clear-and-convincing or categorical direct-evidence requirement is added.

Lewis governs a claim that inadequate assistance actually or imminently obstructed a qualifying nonfrivolous legal claim. It does not require a prisoner alleging retaliation for a completed protected filing to prove that the filing failed. Johnson v. Avery and Wolff preserve the significance of assistance when necessary to recipients' meaningful court access; the State may supply effective alternative arrangements and enforce valid penological restrictions. No free-standing entitlement to serve as another prisoner's chosen lawyer follows. The remand must distinguish that necessary-access possibility, Johnson's own nonfrivolous filings, frivolous activity, and any separately grounded communication or petition theory actually within the case. It may not declare that every assistance activity is protected or that all assistance is constitutionally irrelevant.

The strongest complete-affirmance path relies on the Fifth Circuit's correct rejection of blanket protection and on genuinely inadequate trial findings. It fails only where its directions import a categorical Lewis restriction or displace Mt. Healthy's burden allocation. Those legal corrections do not erase the legitimate need for further findings. The strongest reinstatement path relies on repeated mentions of litigation and the trial court's general chilling finding. It fails because the record does not connect identified protected activity and attributable Board retaliation to Johnson's parole decision, establish the necessary representative injury, or support the decree's statewide scope. Missing source originals prove no default; the published appellate account itself identifies the missing findings that make remand, rather than appellate merits factfinding, appropriate.

**Equality and remedy.** The rights-dependent writ-writer equality theory remains distinct from protest-letter process and independent ordinary-equality theories. Comparable treatment and purposeful adverse effect must be established under their proper legal predicates; no universal statistical-proof requirement is imposed. Likewise, a conceivably rational ordinary classification is not an actual Mt. Healthy defense to a proved protected-right penalty. Class certification does not establish standing or a statewide wrong. Systemic relief requires a supported common practice and affected group and must fit the proved violation, respecting valid security interests and applicable prospective-relief law. The holding adopts neither a universal Monell requirement for every state-officer injunction nor an entitlement to purge all litigation references. Here the challenged Board-wide practice itself requires proof of Board attribution.

The existing directive for further proceedings survives otherwise. No parole, release, fresh trial, compulsory discovery, liability, damages, fee award, or statewide injunction is ordered. Unappealed ex post facto, tentative-parole-month/progress, nonresident-furlough, and corporate-entity immunity dispositions stay closed. No personal qualified-immunity ruling is made.

## Enhanced zone-of-interests comparison

**Available path allowing review.** Section 109 itself sets the membership boundary whose alleged removal directly creates the challenged competition. These banks are participants in the same market, injured by agency expansion across that boundary. Clarke and the competitor cases it applies permit review without proof that Congress intended especially to benefit the plaintiff. This is a direct relationship to the provision's operative limitation, not a claim derived from some unrelated provision of the Act. Air Courier's postal employees had a collateral employment interest; its footnote 5 and discussion at 529 preserve the difference between employees and direct competitors.

**Strongest objection to that path.** A statute's restriction of entry or eligibility will often reduce competition incidentally. Treating that effect alone as sufficient can collapse the statutory zone into injury in fact, precisely what Air Courier forbids. The membership bond can protect the cooperative's members without protecting established commercial lenders against competition. Clarke found a statutory purpose beyond mere incidental benefit; Bennett found a particular protective data requirement.

**Available path denying review.** The occupational limitation concerns the character of a qualifying membership group. The banks' interest in keeping potential customers away from the credit union lacks the necessary protective connection to that particular membership condition. A shared preference for enforcing a restriction is not itself a sufficient statutory interest. This path does not require an express right of action in the FCUA, special-beneficiary status, or proof that Congress named banks; it requires an arguable relationship to the provision's purposes beyond economic consequences.

**Strongest objection to that path.** Clarke expressly rejects a special-intended-beneficiary requirement, and its discussion of Investment Company Institute accepts competitor challenges to restrictions primarily aimed at financial safety. A bank need not share every purpose of a statute to challenge agency removal of a statutory limit. The banks' direct market position provides a more immediate relationship than the postal employees' derivative interest.

## Enhanced common-bond and deference comparison

**Available path invalidating the policy.** Read as a limit on a federal credit union's membership, §109 requires the relevant occupational groups to share the common bond rather than letting each unrelated employer group supply a different one. The plural permits multiple groups sharing the bond; it does not render the bond requirement meaningless. The parallel geographic alternative supplies the same collective structure, consistent with NCUA's concession. On this reading, the agency's service-area requirements cannot replace the enacted occupational connection. MCI enforces a statutory boundary before deference, while NationsBank leaves permissible classifications inside it.

**Strongest objection to invalidation.** Ordinary plural grammar permits each group to have its own bond. Group-by-group qualification still excludes unaffiliated individuals and therefore need not make the statute surplusage. The occupational and geographic clauses use different grammatical forms, and NCUA retains group membership and administrative service-area requirements. Its policy therefore can be described as applying the condition to groups rather than abolishing it.

**Available path sustaining the policy.** The plural groups admits a distributive reading: every qualifying group must possess an occupational bond, but distinct groups need not share one bond with each other. Congress did not expressly say that one bond must unite the credit union's entire occupational membership. The agency may choose among the genuinely permissible readings within its express rulemaking authority over membership, and changed administrative policy alone is not a defect. That authority remains subject to the common-bond exception, whatever its proper unit of application. Clarke, NationsBank and Allentown Mack preserve agency policy and classification discretion within the statute.

**Strongest objection to sustaining it.** If every unrelated group can be added, the occupational clause ceases to connect the membership of the credit union as a whole. The geographic concession and the earlier decades of consistent common-bond administration reinforce the collective reading. An agency cannot use general access or viability objectives to remove a concrete statutory limitation; MCI controls that boundary even where the replacement has policy advantages.
