## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Public employment — false statements in investigations

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Does due process give a federal employee a right to make knowingly false statements when answering an agency’s misconduct inquiry?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Fifth Amendment due process; applicable federal employment protections; separate Fifth Amendment self-incrimination rules where actually invoked.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** REVERSE the categorical constitutional protection for knowingly false answers.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would preserve the employee’s chance to contest the charge while rejecting a right to manufacture evidence. A denial that is unproved, mistaken, ambiguous, or honestly held is not thereby a knowing lie. The agency must establish the actual falsehood under lawful procedures. But once deliberate falsity is established, disciplining it does not deny the opportunity to be heard. Any genuinely compelled-incrimination issue must be addressed separately rather than resolved through a supposed general right to lie.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Lanier’s fair-warning principle requires an identifiable prohibition before punishment, not a constitutional privilege to falsify facts. Gaudin concerns the jury’s determination of criminal elements and should not be transplanted into an administrative hearing. Agency proof of falsity and knowledge remains necessary under the governing employment rules.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No rule compelling waiver of a valid privilege, and no presumption that every unsuccessful denial is dishonest.
