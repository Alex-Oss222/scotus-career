## 1. Fixed scope, actual record, and source limits

The statutory question concerns Lindblom's own records and continuing retention; the Foundation has no Privacy Act claim within this review. The separate constitutional-standing question concerns both plaintiffs. The original collection, FOIA claims, and Fifth and Ninth Amendment theories remain outside the case. Historical party papers concentrating on statutory retention do not narrow the independently authorized hypothetical constitutional-standing question. The twelve earlier Chunk 3 events remain final.

The FBI's actual keeping of identified information concerning Lindblom's associations is supported by the reported agency response, released records, and lower-court examination. The district court found investigations of other persons and Lindblom's incidental, apparently innocent contact. Lawful original collection is the accepted litigation premise. Neither innocent contact nor closure of a file establishes that every retained item now lacks law-enforcement pertinence. The Government's quoted declaration concerning continuing interest in certain other individuals and organizations remains a supported alternative, not a finding that each disputed record presently satisfies the correct standard. Petitioner's narrowed position disclaims both an active-investigation-only test and continual universal review.

Lindblom's original request for damages is affirmatively attributed to him by the Government, as well as collectively reported by the appellate court. His statutory claim continued on appeal; other abandoned claims are expressly identified. No fact or rule established here limits all money to abandoned claims or abandons it on the statutory claim. Applying §(g)(1)(D)/(g)(4) to the actual statutory wrong and actual monetary request does not create a new count. Repeating the prayer or citing those subsection numbers at every appellate stage is not an invented preservation condition.

The complete complaint, amendment correspondence, and some original declarations remain unrecovered. Their absence is not a finding of nonpresentation, evidentiary default, lack of exhaustion, waiver, or immunity. Access-request exhaustion is not itself amendment exhaustion. No amendment request/refusal/review sequence or actual waiver of a valid exemption is found. The new §14 packet controls over the older neutral/source descriptions where they suggested that possession of another express preservation recital was indispensable.

The Foundation's affidavits concern possible applicant deterrence and future employment consequences. They do not support the missing mechanism by which applicants or employers would learn of the FBI references. Lindblom's separately presented constitutional argument was derivative; it supplied no independent argument or evidentiary showing of constitutional injury. His reported complaint allegation of personal chill is not converted into summary-judgment proof of emotional injury. The constitutional analysis below does not silently adjudicate his statutory adverse effect.

## 2. Shared antecedent law and independent statutory controversy

### P1. Article III is resolved before disputed retention merits

All eight adopt this proposition at the following bounded level.

The actual June 2, 1997 Steel Co. holding prohibits assuming contested Article III power in order to decide unsettled statutory merits. It also preserves Bell v. Hood's distinction between a substantial federal claim and eventual recovery. Its completed reporting default is different from an agency's continuing custody of the claimant's own identified associational information. The present conclusion does not use Norton/Avrech's already-controlled-merits exception; the Privacy Act construction is contested and is reached only after an affirmative jurisdictional analysis.

**Injury:** The identified personal interest is freedom from government maintenance of a dossier describing one's own protected associations beyond the limits Congress enacted. The concrete occurrence is actual continued official custody of that information, not a generalized objection to administration and not an unsupported prediction of an employer's reaction. Section (e)(7), the definition of record, and the individual civil-action provisions identify a personal privacy protection. Lujan preserves congressional recognition of concrete factual harms previously insufficient in law. Warth, 422 U.S. 499–502, distinguishes existence of an injury from success on the asserted prohibition. Havens, 455 U.S. 373–375, illustrates the difference between an actual invasion of the claimant's own statutory interest and an observer's grievance. Havens does not supply the Privacy Act waiver or replace the summary-judgment burden.

The factual incidence of the asserted intrusion is established here by the acknowledged records. Its legal wrongfulness remains contested. Standing does not require first proving that the records have lost all pertinence; that would make success on the statutory interpretation an antecedent jurisdictional condition. Nor does this ruling say that every procedural violation or the mere use of a statutory label creates injury.

**Traceability:** The FBI itself keeps the records. No independent third party must discover, dislike, or act on them for the asserted personal retention invasion to occur. That direct link distinguishes the constitutional reputational theory actually presented.

**Redressability:** An award under an applicable §(g)(1)(D)/(g)(4) monetary claim can address an injury caused by a covered statutory violation. Money was actually requested by the statutory claimant. The statute expressly makes an agency suable and the United States liable for qualifying violations; no rule in the permitted law makes all monetary redress of this category of personal injury legally impossible. The serious questions about statutory adverse effect, culpability, actual damages, and the minimum recovery govern entitlement. Their being unsettled or unproved in a judgment resting on a different statutory rule does not make the requested remedy categorically unavailable. Meaningful partial redress suffices; a guaranteed final award does not.

This is not reliance on hypothetical future amendment, litigation costs, an unpleaded Bivens action, an automatic $1,000 award, or presumed emotional/pecuniary loss. Nor does it hold that a substantial prayer by itself supplies Article III. Supported direct personal injury plus an actual claim within a legally available remedial category supplies the controversy. If a remedy is forbidden as a matter of law, Arizonans excludes it; that is not the situation established for every statutory remedy here. Steel Co.'s causal redress inquiry does not require deciding all contested substantive conditions in the claimant's favor before recognizing this dispute.

**Strongest opposite path:** Dismiss or remand for lack of statutory standing because only subjective chill is proved and expungement may be unavailable. That path correctly warns that Laird rejects subjective fear and that an unavailable injunction cannot confer jurisdiction. It fails as a comprehensive resolution because it disregards the independently supported personal retention invasion and actual monetary claim, converts unadjudicated liability questions into assumed jurisdictional failures, and supplies neither an actual abandonment nor a categorical legal exclusion of every monetary route. A route-specific barrier must still be enforced when established; no such barrier is invented.

### P2. Federal-question jurisdiction, statutory action, and sovereign consent are distinct

All eight join this proposition, including its reservations.

Sections 1331 and 1254 provide jurisdictional channels, not sovereign consent. Section 1361 does not authorize unspecified relief without its predicates. The Privacy Act supplies its own individual action against the agency in §(g)(1)(D), federal jurisdiction over matters under that subsection, and the conditional monetary liability in §(g)(4). Lindblom's substantial personal retention claim and requested money fall within the category to which those provisions speak. Section (e)(7) is a substantive duty, not by itself a freestanding unlimited remedial grant.

Under Bell and the actual Steel Co. distinction, §(g)(1)'s jurisdictional wording does not automatically turn the existence of every disputed statutory violation, adverse effect, culpability condition, or measure of damages into an Article III question. For this substantial statutory action, interpreting the protected right and determining whether the statutory civil-action and liability conditions are satisfied is adjudication within the identified federal and statutory jurisdiction. This is a construction of this scheme and claim, not a universal classification of every condition in every waiver.

Meyer keeps consent and cause separate. Here Congress, unlike the judicial agency-Bivens extension rejected there, expressly identified the agency defendant, individual action, and conditional public obligation. The Court must enforce those conditions; it need not create a remedy against a new defendant. The common threshold conclusion grants no damages entitlement and does not treat §702 as a monetary waiver.

Neither §(j) nor §(k) authorizes exemption from §(e)(7). A regulatory reference to §(g) cannot erase liability for that nonexemptible duty merely by withholding its enforcement, for the textual and structural reason explained in Doe v. FBI, 936 F.2d 1351–1353. This conclusion is not a finding that every other remedy or every record is exempt or nonexempt. A valid exemption from §(d) can defeat a derivative §(g)(1)(A) amendment claim. FOIA exemptions and a general CRS designation do not themselves establish all such predicates.

**Opposite path:** Treat all §(g) predicates as independently unestablished limits on sovereign consent and stop before retention. The express agency cause and money liability, the substantial claim within their subject, and Steel Co./Bell's separation defeat that wholesale inference. A later ruling that a specified remedy exceeds the waiver would bind that remedy; the current analysis neither broadens the waiver nor assumes silence waives it.

### P3. Adverse effect receives separate statutory treatment

**Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer** adopt this bounded construction: §(g)(1)(D) requires an adverse personal consequence of the alleged noncompliance; it does not require §(g)(1)(C)'s adverse agency determination, proved lost employment, public disclosure, or pecuniary loss. Actual unwanted maintenance of identified records describing this individual's associations, if outside §(e)(7)'s lawful alternatives, invades the very personal informational interest that provision protects and can itself supply the adverse effect. An abstract demand for compliant procedures, or records about someone else, would not establish that consequence.

That conclusion depends on the actual personal recordkeeping and the nature of the substantive right. It does not declare the records unlawful, presume distress, equate every violation of every Act provision with adverse effect, or establish actual damages under §(g)(4). The extra text in (D) continues to exclude technical noncompliance with no adverse personal consequence. With this kind of personal substantive protection, the invasion and its adverse effect can coincide; Congress did not demand an independent consequential employment or disclosure injury.

Albright, 732 F.2d 184–190, confirms that adverse effect, causal connection, willfulness, and compensable actual damages perform different work. It recognizes emotional trauma as a possible adverse effect but does not make it the exclusive kind, decide nonpecuniary actual damages, or decide the minimum-without-proved-pecuniary-loss question. Its trial findings are not this record's findings. Doe's unchallenged no-adverse-effect finding and abandoned money claim likewise belong to Doe.

**Scalia and Thomas** do not join P3's affirmative sufficiency construction. They accept the substantial statutory action and P1–P2, but reserve whether the custody injury alone satisfies (D)'s separate adverse-effect condition. Their sufficient statutory ground is that the challenged retention is not prohibited on the conceded original-collection premise. Having established Article III and the covered category of action, they need not decide an additional contested liability element to reject that claim. They do not find adverse effect absent or money abandoned.

**Strongest opposing reading of P3:** “In such a way as to have an adverse effect” must require a consequence additional to the maintenance violation, lest the phrase add nothing in an (e)(7) action. The six distinguish a demand for another type of injury from a requirement of real personal injury: actual invasion of an individual substantive privacy interest is an effect, unlike abstract statutory illegality. Scalia and Thomas regard that distinction as a serious unresolved construction but unnecessary to their nonviolation judgment. No one converts the source gap into a factual adverse-effect finding.

## 3. Statutory construction commitments

### P4. Six-Justice continuing-pertinence construction

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer adopt:

An agency maintaining records describing an individual's exercise of First Amendment rights must satisfy §(e)(7) during continued retention, unless retention is expressly authorized by statute or by the individual. Under the law-enforcement alternative, the retained record must remain pertinent to and within the scope of an authorized law-enforcement activity. Lawful pertinence at original collection is not an irrebuttable authorization for indefinite retention after every qualifying connection has ceased.

The defined verb “maintain” includes both keeping and collection. The prohibition applies to the ongoing act and its stated exception, not solely to historical acquisition. Congress elsewhere used “collect” or “compiled for” when addressing acquisition-based categories. A record's historical connection to an investigation is relevant evidence; it does not make the statutory condition permanently unreviewable. The competing noun-modification reading identifies what must be pertinent but does not answer when the condition attached to continuing maintenance must be satisfied.

“Authorized law enforcement activity” is broader than an active investigation of this plaintiff. Closed investigations may have continuing law-enforcement pertinence; old or initially innocent facts can contribute to counterintelligence, connected inquiries, later investigation, and legitimate preservation of a law-enforcement record. Neither innocence, age, a closed individual file, nor indexing by name requires expungement. No “currently necessary” or least-restrictive-recordkeeping test is adopted. An agency's general mission and the assertion that any information might someday be useful cannot alone conclusively establish the required pertinence of every record indefinitely.

The Act separately restricts disclosure under §(b). Department of Defense v. FLRA supplies its exact prohibition/exception structure, not a holding that the absence of public release eliminates Congress's separate retention protection. Law-enforcement disclosure is not confined universally to §(b)(7): the other statutory exceptions retain their own scope. The new construction does not decide any dissemination claim.

The definition and operative prohibition, rather than the preliminary Senate bill or a privacy-policy balancing test, carry this conclusion. The legislative material may corroborate a distinct concern with retaining information but does not supply a new element. The duty is statutory. No judicial periodic audit, automatic request-response explanation, deadline, particular form of justification, search of every office, or additional evidentiary presumption is imposed. A claim properly in court is resolved under ordinary procedure, the actual proof, and legitimate confidential/in camera handling.

**Strongest opposite path:** The record remains pertinent to the lawful activity that produced it even after that activity ends; including maintenance already forbids continuing to keep illegally collected records, so nothing is surplusage. Investigation history and preservation cannot readily be separated from future intelligence value, and Congress omitted “current.” The six accept the account's legitimate preservation concern but reject its conclusive legal consequence. Applying a present-tense restriction to continuing conduct needs no added word “current.” A broad statutory activity and an ordinary pertinence standard accommodate historical records without converting original legality into permanent immunity from the maintenance restriction. The actual Government alternative may succeed record by record under this construction.

### P5. Scalia–Thomas retention construction

Scalia and Thomas would affirm the statutory summary judgment:

Section (e)(7) requires the record to be pertinent to and within the scope of an authorized law-enforcement activity; it does not require that the activity remain currently active or that the agency demonstrate an additional present operational use. On the accepted premise that these records were collected within the scope of a legitimate law-enforcement activity, preserving the record of that activity does not cease to satisfy the described relationship because the investigation ends or the plaintiff was an innocent contact. The textual object is a record pertinent to an authorized activity, a category that includes completed activity.

The inclusion of maintenance has independent effect: prohibited acquisition does not become lawful when collection stops, and continuing custody of records never within the permitted category remains restricted. Other defined operations and §(b)'s limits continue to constrain use and disclosure. Their construction does not authorize collection unrelated to lawful activity, dissemination outside an exception, or immunity from another independently applicable statute. It resolves retention of the category conceded here; it does not find every speculative intelligence interest sufficient to justify collecting a new file.

**Strongest opposite path:** The continuing verb and Congress's deliberate use of different acquisition terms elsewhere require an independently continuing relation, and the Government's reading can preserve an inventory long after any legitimate need ends. Scalia and Thomas acknowledge the policy force and grammatical plausibility. They conclude that the proposed temporal requalification narrows “an authorized law enforcement activity” beyond the words enacted. A continuing prohibition on an impermissible class of records need not require an ever-renewed classification of a lawfully compiled historical record. The potential later significance of an investigation's own records reinforces that reading; administrative burden alone would not justify ignoring contrary text.

Their sufficient ground is statutory nonviolation. They do not affirm because statutory injury was unproved, because no money was preserved, because §702 necessarily bars all equity, or because Doe's unchallenged adverse-effect finding supposedly governs Lindblom. They reject P4's requirement that a further ongoing relation be established beyond the conceded lawful collection/recorded activity. Mere rewriting of P4 as “no active investigation required” does not remove that legal disagreement.

## 4. Constitutional claims, remedies, and mandate

### P6. Constitutional claim-specific disposition

All eight would affirm the rejection of the Foundation's constitutional standing on the supported absence of traceability. The affidavits do not establish how the FBI's actual custody produces the asserted knowledge and reactions of prospective grantees or employers. Meese's public governmental label and detailed supporting evidence supplied a link absent here. The law does not require a named lost grantee, proof of completed economic loss, or certainty about every third party's response. No universal rule says that informing others of government conduct makes all resulting injury self-inflicted.

All eight separately reject Lindblom's constitutional claim as actually presented. He offered no independent constitutional-standing argument/evidence beyond the failed derivative Foundation theory; his departure from the Foundation does not produce such a showing. The complaint's subjective-chill allegation is not enough at summary judgment under Laird, Lujan, and Lee. This is not a merits ruling that government recordkeeping can never violate the First Amendment. Nor does recognition of an actual personal statutory interest automatically supply the missing factual and legal presentation of a distinct constitutional claim.

**Scalia and Thomas additionally adopt P6a:** The reported possibilities of employee harm and applicant deterrence, without evidence supporting concrete exposure or sufficiently impending personal consequences, fail injury/imminence as well as traceability on this record. No particular affidavit form, named-lost-applicant rule, or heightened probability requirement is added. The other six do not join that independent additional holding; the causal failure disposes of the actual theory and avoids overstating what Meese allows.

**Opposite path:** The expert affidavits and predictable stigma could permit an inference of present organizational injury, as in Meese; Lindblom's records also directly concern him. None of the eight treats that proposition as categorically impossible. It fails here because uncontradicted statements predicting a response do not establish the missing exposure mechanism, and the constitutional claim actually presented supplies no independent Lindblom showing. The statutory theory has a different personal protected interest and a direct defendant-act connection; the two claims are not merged.

### P7. Relief and final component directions

All eight agree that the Court enters no final damages or expungement award. Intentional or willful noncompliance, actual compensable damages, causation of such damages, and the statutory minimum retain their independent requirements. No later Doe v. Chao or Cooper rule enters. No direct constitutional damages action against the FBI is created.

Section (g)(1)(A)/(g)(2) amendment relief requires its lawful request/review and exemption predicates. The source record does not establish their presence or absence. Nagel's express discussion of (e)(7) relief and exhaustion must be considered with Clarkson's actual exhaustion and limited injunction provisions and Doe's statutory channel analysis. None is an unconditional expungement warrant. Section 702's nonmoney waiver and Reich's independent equitable route remain analytically possible, subject to the Privacy Act's express or implied remedial preclusion and ordinary requirements. The current monetary route makes deciding that entire equitable dispute unnecessary to reaching statutory construction. The absence of a personal officer's name in the caption alone does not defeat every §702 action; any mandatory decree must identify the responsible officer or successor by name or title. No independent equitable count is fabricated.

For the six adopting P4, the statutory judgment is vacated and remanded for application of that construction and the ordinary remaining civil-action/liability/remedy inquiries. Current pertinence, an applicable independent statutory or individual authorization, record-specific exemptions relevant to a remedy, and any concrete Federal Records Act preservation obligation remain to be addressed when genuinely material. The Government's supported alternative about other ongoing investigative interests is available; the Court neither accepts it as a complete finding nor excludes it. Remand is justified by the erroneous conclusive original-collection rule, not by the researcher's lack of originals. No hearing, new evidence, discovery, renewed complaint, additional filing, or automatic purge is commanded. The earlier discovery refusal is not independently reversed; necessary procedure on remand follows the corrected issues and ordinary standards, with confidentiality protected.

Scalia and Thomas would leave the statutory summary judgment in place on P5. They join the no-award and statutory-remedy limits but not the command for statutory remand. A later change to the merits construction could change the logically dependent disposition; it would not transform their current reservation on adverse effect into a favorable finding.

For both constitutional claims, all eight require dismissal for want of Article III standing, without prejudice. The appellate rejection of standing is sustained; insofar as the district court's blanket with-prejudice judgment purports to dispose of those claims on the merits, that effect is vacated and the case remanded solely to conform the constitutional dismissals to their jurisdictional basis. This does not restore any constitutional injunction or declaration. Original collection and all abandoned/unreviewed claims remain undisturbed; their reasoning is not adopted.

## 5. Eight Justice-specific commitments and strongest alternatives

### John Paul Stevens

**Commitment:** Join P1–P4, P6, and P7 in full. Vacate statutory summary judgment under P4; remand without final relief. Sustain both constitutional-standing rejections on the narrower causal/claim-specific grounds, with the without-prejudice correction. Withhold P5 and P6a.

**Individual legal basis:** His own Meese opinion makes the actual governmental mechanism decisive and rejects treating reputational harm as inherently noncognizable. His unanimous Arizonans opinion requires a real remaining remedy and separates jurisdictional correction from merits. His Department of Defense v. FLRA opinion recognizes a real personal interest in control of information and keeps the Privacy Act's textually distinct requirements separate. His participation in unanimous Havens supports the distinction between a personal statutory invasion and a bystander's objection. His actual Steel Co. statutory ground does not dispense with that case's governing sequencing rule; here he affirmatively finds the direct injury and actual covered monetary action.

**Adversarial comparison:** The strongest full affirmance combines the absence of an active investigation requirement with independent dissemination controls and the uncertain expungement route. He accepts the first as a limit but rejects the resulting perpetual-retention rule; he rejects the second as substitution of one statutory protection for another and the third as disregard of money actually sought. A threshold-only remand would postpone a legal issue the supported personal controversy permits the Court to decide. P3 requires actual custody of this person's protected information, not presumed emotional distress.

**Join boundary and narrower alternative:** He will join the ordinary continuing-pertinence rule without a mandatory agency review program. He will not join a categorical lost-grantee requirement, a rule making publicity invariably self-inflicted injury, or a holding that the Foundation's uncertain injury in all possible settings is legally insufficient. ### Sandra Day O'Connor

### Antonin Scalia

**Adversarial comparison:** The strongest opposite reading is the defined distinction between collection and maintenance and the continuous character of the prohibition. **Join boundary and narrower alternative:** He would join a holding that age, innocence, a closed individual investigation, or name retrieval is not an automatic violation.
