## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Sex and parentage classifications in derivative citizenship

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches Miller’s own equality claim and the remedial question. He does not treat the father’s rights as the sole vehicle.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply intermediate scrutiny to the sex classification and equivalent elevated review to the parentage rule. Government may require reliable proof of biological parentage and a timely voluntary legal relationship, but may not use broad assumptions that mothers are naturally known and connected while fathers are absent. Equivalent objective procedures must be available to both.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Derivative-citizenship rules that impose materially different acknowledgment and relationship requirements on citizen fathers and mothers violate equal protection when the distinction rests on sex stereotypes rather than a substantial, nonstereotyped fit to proof and connection interests.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Biological maternity may often be documented at birth, but that fact does not justify a broader legal regime that presumes maternal commitment and paternal absence. Modern records, acknowledgment, adjudication, and support evidence can address the Government’s legitimate interests without categorical sex asymmetry. Miller’s father acknowledged her, so the challenged distinction did real work.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Congress may adopt neutral proof deadlines, safeguards against fraudulent claims, and rules requiring a genuine legal parent-child relationship. The Court does not define citizenship for every foreign birth.
