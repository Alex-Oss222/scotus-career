## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Gross disproportionality of punitive forfeiture

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional validity of forfeiting the entire transported sum. He does not review the existing $15,000 forfeiture.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A punitive forfeiture violates the Excessive Fines Clause when grossly disproportional to the gravity of the offense. The inquiry is deferential but real and considers the lawful character of the property, culpability, harm, related criminality, and the scale of other authorized sanctions without creating a fixed ratio.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A punitive forfeiture violates the Excessive Fines Clause when grossly disproportional to the gravity of the offense. Forfeiture of the entire $357,144 is excessive on this record.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The money was lawful property, no fraud, trafficking, laundering, or public loss was proved, the offense withheld required information, and the other authorized sanctions were dramatically smaller. Those facts make total forfeiture grossly disproportional.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This establishes no fixed ratio, protects no contraband or criminal proceeds, and does not adjudicate the existing $15,000 forfeiture or materially different forfeitures connected to fraud, trafficking, money laundering, or comparable harm.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Pennsylvania Supreme Court.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The unlawfully obtained evidence remains excluded from the revocation proceeding, and the state court may determine the lawful consequence for the revocation judgment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Exclusion of evidence obtained by parole officers

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches remedy because the State asks to use evidence obtained by the same parole officials in the proceeding they initiate.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply exclusion where parole officers themselves violate the Fourth Amendment to obtain evidence for revocation. The direct institutional connection creates meaningful deterrence, and permitting use would make supervision an incentive to bypass lawful search rules. Reserve evidence independently obtained by police, good-faith reliance, and attenuated sources.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Evidence obtained through an unconstitutional search by parole officers for use in parole supervision and revocation must be excluded from the resulting revocation proceeding.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The remedy targets the officials and proceeding most directly linked to the violation. Parole needs justify lawful supervision and tailored search conditions, not a free-standing exception. Without exclusion, the same agency could secure the practical equivalent of punishment through evidence it was forbidden to obtain.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The decision does not establish exclusion for every police search later relevant to parole, reject valid parole-search conditions, or foreclose good-faith, attenuation, independent-source, and inevitable-discovery rules.

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory removal jurisdiction before immunity. He does not decide whether Wisconsin waived immunity by removing or whether every remaining state-law claim should be retained.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Sections 1331 and 1441 authorize removal when the complaint presents federal claims. A potentially waivable Eleventh Amendment defense affecting one or more claims does not erase jurisdiction over the entire action; immunity and remand must be handled claim by claim.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A potential Eleventh Amendment bar affecting one or more claims does not destroy otherwise valid removal jurisdiction over the entire civil action. The federal court must address immunity and remaining remand questions claim by claim.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The complaint supplied federal-question jurisdiction independently of the immunity defense. Treating immunity as destruction of the entire removed action would merge a claim-specific defense with the statutory grant of adjudicatory authority.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not decide removal waiver, sustain any particular claim against Wisconsin, retain every supplemental claim, or alter immunity applicable to treasury relief.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The retaliation judgment remains in force. No ruling is made concerning liability for an accusation causing no material consequence or concerning any separate defendant.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Retaliatory false disciplinary charge after a grievance threat

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the retaliation claim and distinguishes it from the disciplinary adjudication and from procedural due process.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A correctional officer may not knowingly file a false disciplinary charge because an inmate filed or threatened to file a lawful grievance. Satisfaction of Hill’s evidentiary minimum for the hearing does not immunize the accusing officer’s retaliatory motive, and confinement or privilege loss supplies adverse action without separate proof of subjective chilling.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A correctional officer may not knowingly file a false disciplinary charge because an inmate filed or threatened to file a lawful grievance. Satisfaction of Hill does not immunize retaliatory conduct, and resulting confinement or privilege loss is sufficient adverse action without proof of subjective chilling.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An authorized prison grievance is protected petitioning, and a knowingly false charge motivated by that activity advances no legitimate penological objective. Hines received ten days’ confinement and lost privileges, so the case presents a concrete adverse consequence even though the discipline may fall below Sandin’s liberty threshold.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not protect false grievances, threats, or independent rule violations; foreclose a Mt. Healthy same-action defense; invalidate the disciplinary adjudication; impose liability on the hearing officer; or decide whether an accusation causing no material consequence is independently actionable.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the attorney-client privilege and does not decide work-product protection or an exception not established on this record.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The federal attorney-client privilege ordinarily survives the client’s death. Confidentiality must be predictable when advice is sought, and investigative importance alone does not create an ad hoc posthumous exception.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The federal attorney-client privilege ordinarily survives the client’s death. Investigative importance alone does not permit disclosure absent waiver, crime-fraud, or another recognized exception.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hunt, Upjohn, and Rule 501 preserve the privilege beyond death because clients may seek advice about matters affecting reputation, family, property, and legal interests after death. A case-specific need balance would make confidentiality unknowable at the time of consultation.
