## Shared legal boundaries

1. **The earlier notice claims are not the later ICJ claim.** A-738 concerns Paraguay's and its officials' own Vienna Convention and 1859 Treaty rights. Breard's failure to raise his personal treaty claim in state habeas does not itself default Paraguay's civil claim. A-771 alleges a new threatened violation of the April 9 international measure and Charter obligation. Describing the 1992 notice omission as completed does not adjudicate that new duty.
2. **The current officer cases require a genuine duty and real prospective relief.** Seminole Tribe permits the specified prospective officer enforcement of IGRA and leaves direct-State amenability without a controlling constitutional rationale. It does not overrule Union Gas or create either universal abrogation or universal immunity. Coeur d'Alene rejects a categorical sovereign-interest exception while preserving the requirement of an ongoing federal violation, a connected officer and a properly prospective decree. Its rule does not establish the present treaty right or remedy. Monaco remains relevant to a foreign sovereign's action against a State; labeling a defendant an officer is insufficient, but Monaco does not eliminate the actual Young inquiry.
3. **International jurisdiction, international obligation and domestic enforcement are different questions.** Optional Protocol Article I submits Convention disputes to compulsory ICJ jurisdiction; Articles II–III permit agreed arbitration or conciliation and do not create a mandatory two-month delay before ICJ access. Charter Article 94(1), Statute Article 41 and the April 9 order raise the question whether provisional measures carry a binding preservation duty. Even if they do, a domestic judicially enforceable right, proper defendant and lawful remedial channel must be identified independently. Article 94(2)'s Security Council route is relevant but cannot simply be asserted exclusive from its permissive wording. Conversely, the binding-force limitation in Statute Article 59 does not itself establish the domestic enforceability of every provisional measure.
4. **The ICJ order has not decided the treaty remedy or prejudice.** Its prima facie jurisdiction and irreparability findings preserve the international dispute. Its paragraphs 31–33 reserve the availability of Paraguay's requested remedy and whether prejudice must be proved. Paragraph 41 addresses the United States and the measures at its disposal; it does not itself vacate Breard's conviction, command a particular American judicial vehicle or resolve domestic procedural defenses. The United States' factual and remedial submissions are not independent ICJ findings.
5. **Article 36 has operative qualifications.** Notification to the consulate under paragraph (1)(b) follows the national's request; officials must inform the national of that right without delay and forward covered communications without delay. Paragraph (1)(a) protects communication and access. Paragraph (1)(c) protects consular visits, correspondence and arranging representation, including after judgment, but requires abstention if the national expressly opposes action. Paragraph (2) requires compliance with receiving-State laws and regulations while requiring those rules to enable full effect to the rights' purposes. Neither half settles the other's meaning by omission. The distinct 1859 most-favored-consul theory may require notification without the Article 36 request condition; its assumed force does not by itself establish vacatur or an ongoing violation after access has been allowed.
6. **Cause and prejudice remain separate from notice and from foreign-state rights.** Coleman (1991), Carrier and Reed supply the ordinary claim-default framework and external-novelty question. The known treaty and opportunity for reasonably diligent counsel are substantial answers to the novelty argument. The full-effect proviso presents a different argument about the permissible procedural rule. The lower appellate court rejected cause but expressly did not decide prejudice. Neither the reported notice stipulation nor counsel's plea affidavit establishes the contested counterfactual that consular advice would have changed the outcome. No innocence gateway is supplied merely by the loss of a proposed noncapital plea.
7. **Felker requires independent extraordinary-writ consideration.** Original habeas jurisdiction survives. Prior circuit authorization is not a filing prerequisite here; §§2244(b)(1)–(2) inform discretion without a holding that they bind original consideration directly. Rule 20.4 separately requires exceptional circumstances, no adequate relief in another form or court, an explanation for not applying to the district court and actual exhaustion or a statutory alternative. The present model neither finds nor rejects unverified compliance. A newly arising claim is not automatically a previously presented claim merely because both involve the same prisoner.
8. **Lonchar does not manufacture a hearing or additional return.** A court must preserve identified process that governing habeas law requires when execution would defeat that process and lawful expedition cannot complete it. Filing, novelty and irreversibility alone establish neither predicate. Rule 20.4(b) makes original habeas ex parte unless a response is ordered; absence of an unrequested response is not default or an incomplete mandatory filing. Felker's independently considered original denial and application denial show that substantial issues must be examined but do not automatically require another round of papers.
9. **Aktepe forbids a subject-matter shortcut.** Treaty interpretation can present an independently manageable legal duty. Foreign-relations implications and possible diplomatic embarrassment alone do not create a political question. Naming a treaty, invoking comity or seeking international accommodation also does not establish the asserted duty. A bounded denial can rest on the interim showing without inventing categorical nonjusticiability.
10. **Blessing requires provision-specific rights and remedy analysis.** All eight Associates joined the rule distinguishing a particular individual right, an enforcement channel, foreclosure, defendant, breach and fitted relief. A duty owed internationally to Paraguay is not automatically a personal §1983 right of its Consul General. An official title and beneficial consequences do not supply the missing entitlement. A distinct prospective officer action to protect an enforceable federal right must be assessed separately; failure of one §1983 theory does not alone decide that route.
11. **Woodard does not substitute mercy for judicial review.** The March 25 decision preserves a minimum against fundamentally arbitrary capital-clemency procedure, while denying an entitlement to mercy or triallike clemency procedures. O'Connor, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the retained-life minimum; O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer accept the pleaded procedure, while Stevens would preserve further examination. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas rely on the separate discretionary-entitlement ground. Neither holding makes clemency the exclusive constitutionally sufficient route for an otherwise judicial claim, and no actual Virginia clemency proceeding or result is established here. The present international claim therefore must be tested through its own judicial source and interim requirements; Woodard neither supplies nor eliminates it.

### A-738 — own consular rights and the older civil judgment

**Denial ground 738-D.** Paraguay has its own asserted rights; Breard's habeas default is not the answer to its suit. Assume for this interim inquiry that the invoked treaty notice duties were binding and violated, and assess the requested protection under the applicants' favorable review/reversal/equities formulation. The decisive obstacle remains the claimed ongoing violation and lawful prospective relief. Paraguay's officers have had free access since 1996. The continued consequence of earlier nonnotification does not itself establish that officials now deny their access, communications or representation rights, or that the treaties require undoing this conviction as the remedy for their past notice injury. The most-favored-consul theory may remove a request condition for notification, but does not itself answer that separate remedial question.

Seminole and Coeur d'Alene remove categorical shortcuts; they do not eliminate Young's present-duty requirement. The lower court's citation to a categorical historical Coeur d'Alene formulation cannot be adopted, but its separate completed-duty/prospective-remedy analysis survives examination under current law. Papasan and Milliken demonstrate that a decree may address a present violation produced by past conduct; they do not establish that every adverse consequence is a current violation of every claimant's rights. Execution's irreversibility and serious international interests do not supply the missing significant prospect of relief through this civil case. No definitive treaty-remedy or general immunity holding is necessary to deny this application.

The strongest competing path is preservation for review of whether effective consular assistance entails a remedy against continued enforcement of a conviction obtained without the required opportunity. Article 36(2)'s full-effect proviso, the direct consular rights and the 1859 theory make that a substantial legal contention, and relief directed at future official conduct cannot be labeled retrospective merely because it addresses an earlier wrong. The denying Justices nevertheless distinguish a genuine ongoing duty from continuing harm and find the interim prospect insufficient. They do not rely on political-question doctrine, Rooker-Feldman, a fictional foreign-state default or an automatic Monaco bar. Baker's bounded nonparty principles reinforce the need to examine Paraguay's actual rights rather than assume that Breard's litigation binds them; Baker does not decide jurisdiction or supply the requested remedy.

**Preservation ground 738-P.** The Court has a pending appellate matter and an expressly requested affirmative injunction. The unusual combination of a conceded notice failure, an unresolved treaty remedial question, current decisions rejecting categorical officer-immunity shortcuts, and a newly issued international preservation order warrants two days for orderly consideration before execution forecloses the execution-specific relief. That is a judicial assessment of the current emergency and legal questions, not an order to cure an operator's unavailable source. The proposed injunction rests on the Court's own §1651 authority in aid of pending review, not on a conclusion that the ICJ automatically commands this Court or that a treaty violation necessarily invalidates a conviction.

The strongest objection is that affirmative relief withheld below demands a concrete legal showing, and a new international event cannot change the older complaint's claim or dispense with Young. These Justices accept both limits. Their position is narrower than an injunction pending certiorari disposition: a brief administrative restraint permits considered resolution of that showing rather than assumes it is met. They regard the exigency and the serious existing federal claim as sufficient for this limited management of pending jurisdiction; they do not adopt an automatic capital pause or impose the full requested restraint on importance alone. Scalia's individual full-relief formulation does not, in their view, foreclose this limited full-Court action. No final choice on certiorari or the general injunction standard is made.

### A-767 — original habeas and the new international measure

**Denial ground 767-D.** Felker preserves this distinct original channel. It also requires a substantial basis for extraordinary relief, independent of the existence of jurisdiction. The April 9 measure preserves a dispute between two States. Optional Protocol jurisdiction, Charter Article 94 and Statute Article 41 do not by their mere existence establish a domestically enforceable personal custody claim requiring this Court to suspend execution pending the original petition. Article 94's undertaking to comply with a decision, Article 41's language about indicated measures and the available national enforcement channel present different questions. The instrument's preservation function and the United States' international interests are taken seriously; they do not establish the proposed domestic habeas consequence.

Even assuming provisional measures are binding internationally, the present legal showing does not supply a sufficient extraordinary domestic habeas basis. Nor does ordinary comity create a personal custody right or enlarge §2251. The original Convention claim remains subject to its own defenses; neither the new ICJ filing nor its provisional order resolves Article 36(2)'s full-effect/default dispute, cause, prejudice or a remedy. The notice stipulation and the disputed plea counterfactual do not themselves satisfy an innocence gateway, demonstrate prejudice or create a hearing entitlement. No conviction-level prejudice holding is newly made.

This denial assumes, without deciding against the applicant, the unresolved Rule 20 procedural matters; it rests on the separate interim legal showing. It applies neither the district authorization mechanism nor §2244(b)(1)–(2) as a mandatory original-writ bar. The new order is not declared a previously adjudicated claim. Section 2251 permits protection but does not compel it. No particular mandatory unfinished judicial step is identified, so Lonchar does not independently command a stay. A further response is not required merely because original habeas is pending. A-767 can therefore be denied without disposing of the writ or adjudicating every theory finally.

The strongest competing path treats the April 9 order as a new federal treaty obligation, capable of restraining execution independently of the earlier notice default, and argues that an original writ must receive a meaningful opportunity for consideration before its practical purpose is destroyed. That is not answered merely by relabeling it a repeated claim. The denying Justices find that its unestablished domestic custody consequence is too substantial an obstacle to the requested stay, and that a lawful present interim determination is possible without another compulsory step. This is not reliance on an unrecovered paper, a supposed State refusal or the prediction of a future ICJ remedy.

**Preservation ground 767-P.** The actual original petition and stay application present a new international judicial measure and a serious question about a present federal obligation.
