### Issue 1: Capital habeas — treaty claim and emergency relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the four requests for immediate protection against execution. Examine the legal channel and the required showing for each, without turning an interim decision into a complete treaty, habeas, or sovereign-immunity judgment.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** The simulated Coleman and Felker emergency standards control within their distinct channels. A serious federal treaty claim warrants judicial consideration through an available procedure but does not by itself supply an exception to default, a cause of action, or power to grant the particular relief. The severity of execution and the institutional interest in treaty compliance are material; neither eliminates those requirements.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Deny the four identified emergency applications on the presently demonstrated procedural and remedial showing. Reserve categorical treaty enforceability, ultimate prejudice, and final disposition of the separate petitions and original-action motion.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** For A-732, Stone recognizes execution as irreparable and treats Breard’s claimed loss of plea advice as a serious asserted injury. Under Coleman, however, he finds the present showing insufficient to establish the required prospect of relief through the surviving default barriers. The claim’s treaty source and asserted novelty do not alone establish cause, prejudice, or a lawful exception. This does not decide that consular assistance could never change a defense or that every notification violation is harmless.

For A-767, Felker requires actual consideration of the retained original habeas route rather than its automatic dismissal under the successive-authorization certiorari bar. Stone does not impose a court-of-appeals permission requirement on this original filing. He nonetheless finds no sufficient exceptional-original-relief showing, or separately identified unfinished legally required process, to justify the requested stay on the submissions described. The ICJ provisional request supplies a serious international context but does not by itself establish the required domestic order. His stay denial does not select a final disposition of the original writ.

For A-738 and A-771, Stone asks what domestic right, ongoing duty, jurisdiction, and remedy support Paraguay’s own requested protection. The revised Seminole Tribe decision preserves an officer route in its particular statutory setting and leaves direct-State amenability without a controlling rationale; Stone therefore cannot deny relief simply by importing the historical Supreme Court’s broader state-immunity result. He finds that the described emergency submission does not sufficiently establish Paraguay’s entitlement to the requested domestic injunction through either its review proceeding or the proposed original action. The imminent consequence of an alleged past notification failure does not, without the necessary legal connection, establish every predicate for relief against the named officials. He reserves a fully briefed determination of the separate cause-of-action and original-jurisdiction questions.

The common result is denial of interim protection, reached through the distinct requests. Neither the execution date nor the gravity of the treaty dispute replaces an independent assessment. The supplied position does not authorize a general rule excluding individual consular claims or an invented final denial of all associated petitions.

* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** The simulated Coleman stay ruling requires a reasonable probability that four Justices would grant review, a significant possibility of reversal, and likely irreparable harm. Execution supplies the irreparable stakes but does not establish the other elements. Breard must show a legally available way past the default or other applicable barriers; the alleged treaty violation is not itself that showing. Schlup requires reliable new innocence evidence and its probability showing, neither of which follows from a lost-consular-assistance allegation. Felker preserves an independent original habeas channel under §2241 while requiring exceptional circumstances and no adequate alternative relief under Rule 20; its §2244(b)(3)(E) certiorari bar concerns circuit successive-application authorization decisions and must not be applied automatically to Breard’s first federal habeas appeal. The original filing and emergency stay remain separate. The revised Seminole Tribe holding permits the specified prospective officer action under IGRA but has no controlling rationale resolving direct-State amenability; it neither forecloses every claim against a state officer nor supplies Paraguay a treaty cause of action. Paraguay must identify an enforceable continuing duty, an available remedy, and jurisdiction for its own claim. The comparator’s broader immunity reasoning is not adopted as simulation law.
