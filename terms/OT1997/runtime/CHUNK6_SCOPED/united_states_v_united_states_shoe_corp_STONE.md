## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Court of International Trade jurisdiction

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves statutory jurisdiction before the Export Clause question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1581(i), read with the HMT statute’s jurisdictional direction, supplies residual jurisdiction over administration of the levy and the refund claim.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Court of International Trade has residual jurisdiction over this Harbor Maintenance Tax refund action.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The claim challenges administration and collection of the HMT and falls within the residual jurisdiction Congress supplied when no more specific subsection governs.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding is confined to this statutory refund action.

### Issue 2: Export Clause classification of the Harbor Maintenance Tax

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional classification after establishing jurisdiction.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Liability attaches to export loading and follows cargo value rather than services rendered or facilities used. The levy is therefore an export tax, not the compensatory charge permitted by Pace.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The ad valorem Harbor Maintenance Tax is prohibited by the Export Clause as applied to exports because it does not fairly measure services or facilities used by the exporter.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Simulated IBM controls. The tax is triggered by exportation and calculated from cargo value, while the pooled revenue is not tied to the exporter’s use of a particular harbor service. General applicability cannot cure that constitutional defect.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not invalidate nonexport applications, prohibit reasonable inspection, storage, wharfage, or facility-use fees, or exempt exporters from general taxes imposed on legally independent events.
