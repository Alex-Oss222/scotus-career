## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Federal courts — ripeness and voting oversight

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Is Texas’s challenge to possible §5 preclearance of future school-district intervention measures ripe before a concrete intervention is selected?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Article III ripeness; fitness for decision and hardship; §5’s application to an actual change affecting voting.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AFFIRM dismissal as unripe.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would distinguish a presently coercive legal obligation from uncertainty about hypothetical future administration. Texas need not expose itself to sanctions whenever a legal dispute is already concrete, but this claim asks the Court to choose among unspecified future measures. The details determine whether §5 applies. A broad advisory exemption would exceed both the factual dispute and the proper judicial function. Dismissal leaves review available when a definite measure produces a real preclearance dispute.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Raines does not make every governmental plaintiff unable to sue; it requires a concrete dispute rather than abstract institutional disagreement. The revised election holdings turn on actual electoral arrangements and asserted injuries. Without a chosen intervention, the Court cannot know whether voting power changes at all.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No substantive exemption for all school-district interventions, and no blanket rule against pre-enforcement review.
