## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: FACE application to force and true threats

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The claim is preserved and controlled by *American Life League* unless the present application materially differs from the access-directed mechanism recognized there.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress may regulate intentional force or a true threat whose object is to prevent a person from providing or obtaining reproductive-health services in an interstate service market.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *American Life League* without expanding *Lopez* and without adding a new rule. The force or true threat is not federalized because violent conduct has economic consequences. The conduct is intentionally directed at preventing the provision or receipt of services in an interstate medical-services market, and that mechanism has already been accepted.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may apply FACE to intentional force and true threats directed at preventing provision or receipt of reproductive-health services because the conduct operates directly on access to an actual interstate service market. The constitutional ground is the access-directed market interference itself, not generalized consequences of violence, healthcare costs, fear, or lost productivity.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** FACE’s conduct and purpose elements do real work. The federal class is not all violence near a clinic. It is force, threat, or obstruction intentionally directed at stopping provision or receipt of the covered services. That mechanism was sustained in *American Life League*. A true threat or force within the same statutory mechanism remains within that rule. No new aggregate-effects theory is needed. On the supplied record, Bird threw a bottle through or against the vehicle of a physician who provided abortions, shattering glass, and threatened to kill him; the prosecution rests on threat, force, and access interference rather than protected anti-abortion advocacy.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not federalize ordinary assault, trespass, protest, or disturbance associated with medical facilities; does not decide materially different mixed-purpose conduct; and does not create a general federal violent-crime power.

### Issue 2: Protective supervised-release condition

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the supervision condition only if a live controversy remains.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 3583(d) permits strong protective restrictions, but a blanket 1,000-foot exclusion from every clinic for every purpose requires record findings showing that its breadth is reasonably necessary.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** On a live controversy, § 3583(d) does not permit the 1,000-foot condition without findings showing that its breadth is reasonably necessary; the conviction remains intact while the condition is vacated for tailoring.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The conduct supports no-contact, no-threat, no-trespass, and site-specific restrictions, but the present record contains no findings justifying exclusion from every clinic regardless of purpose.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Bird gains no right to threaten, intimidate, obstruct, trespass, or impermissibly contact clinics, providers, patients, or staff. A strong, temporary, fact-supported restriction remains available; if supervision has ended without continuing consequences, no merits decision on the condition is authorized.
