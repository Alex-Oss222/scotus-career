## 1. Cass County v. Leech Lake Band of Chippewa Indians — No. 97-174

### Review, question and result

Reverse the Eighth Circuit's exemption ruling as to the eight Nelson Act parcels sold to non-Indians and later bought by the Band in ordinary fee, and remand consistently with the parcel-specific rule below. The thirteen allotted parcels are outside this review. The reviewed tax is the county's ad valorem real-property tax; no transactional excise, personal tax, collection suit against the Tribe, or immunity from execution is adjudicated. No refund, interest or penalty amount is calculated.

### Competing paths and adopted ground

**C1 — Statutory disposal and continuing land-tax status.** The Nelson Act's actual disposition routes, rather than alienability as a freestanding test, establish the relevant congressional choice. Sections 4–5 separate and appraise pine land, then direct public cash sale, with unsold land available at the appraised price. Section 6 directs homestead disposal of agricultural land neither allotted nor reserved for future Indian use, subject to payment, occupancy, final-entry and patent conditions. These are congressional directions to convey the identified land out of the protected tribal holding into the ordinary non-Indian fee estate. Once a parcel actually passed through the specified route into that unrestricted estate, the county could tax the land ad valorem. The Band's later ordinary fee purchase does not undo the completed statutory disposition or independently recreate a federal land-tax protection. The rule preserves any independently applicable federal protection.

The tax-authority step is an interpretation of this particular disposal program, not an assertion that *Yakima* already decided all freely transferable Indian land taxable. *Yakima* supplies the distinction between a congressionally authorized tax following a qualifying parcel and personal tax authority over its Indian owner; its GAA/Burke Act source limitation remains. The Nelson Act's completed sales to non-Indians supply the additional source here. Neither reservation diminishment nor loss of tribal sovereign status is necessary.

**C2 — No clear tax authorization after reacquisition.** The strongest contrary path is that the Act mentions sale but never taxation; *Blackfeet* demands unmistakably clear authorization, and *Yakima* expressly refuses to equate alienability with taxability. The reservation remains Indian country, the present owner is the Tribe, and title can be alienable without losing every federal protection. On this reading the Eighth Circuit should be affirmed because ordinary conveyance terms cannot replace an explicit tax decision.

**Resolution:** C1 is adopted at the narrower completed-disposal level. C2 correctly defeats a universal alienability rule and any inference from current fee ownership alone. It does not show that the Act preserved a land-tax immunity capable of reappearing whenever the Tribe buys back a parcel already conveyed under Congress's ordinary public-sale or completed-homestead program. Giving these completed dispositions their ordinary land-tax consequence is compatible with the requirement of clear congressional choice; no prescribed tax formula is indispensable. This conclusion must not be restated as a presumption that every repeal of an alienation restraint authorizes every tax. The eight parcels' supplied sale provenance supports this decision; no inference is made concerning an uncompleted homestead entry or land reserved from sale.

## 2. Dooley v. Korean Air Lines Co. — No. 97-704

### Review, question and result

Affirm the D.C. Circuit's rejection of the requested general maritime survival recovery for predeath pain and suffering arising from the deaths covered by DOHSA. Do not disturb liability or other damages outside the reviewed category, invent a factual finding of conscious suffering, or determine an amount. No later aviation amendment governs.

### Competing paths and adopted ground

**D1 — Respect the high-seas remedial choice.** Where the then-effective DOHSA governs a death, general maritime law does not add the estate's proposed survival claim for the decedent's predeath pain and suffering. Sections 761–762 designate the personal representative, the specified beneficiaries and their pecuniary loss. Section 765 addresses the related problem of a pending personal-injury plaintiff dying from the same wrong: substitution is permitted and the suit may proceed under the Act for section 762 compensation. It is permission to continue in the specified statutory form, not a command to dismiss every pending claim or a broad preservation of all the decedent's personal damages. This treatment shows that Congress did not leave the effect of death on these related claims entirely untouched.

*Zicherman* supplies the domestic-law source of compensable categories and DOHSA's existing loss-of-society exclusion; it expressly reserved this survival issue. *Yamaha* preserves other remedies in the distinct nonseafarer territorial-water setting without enlarging Congress's occupied high-seas field. *Higginbotham* and *Miles*, as preserved by those holdings, require judicial maritime remedies to respect Congress's actual remedial choices. The new proposition answers the reserved survival question through the statute's treatment of beneficiaries, compensation and pending injury claims.

**D2 — Separate injury, separate common-law survival action.** The strongest alternative distinguishes injury before death, which belonged to the decedent, from the relatives' losses caused by death. It argues that excluding a beneficiary's loss-of-society damages under section 762 does not exclude a separate estate claim; section 765 concerns only an action actually pending when death occurs, and permissive language does not expressly abolish a general survival right. *Yamaha* warns against extending federal exclusivity beyond its field.

**Resolution:** D2 correctly identifies a question not already decided by *Zicherman*, but the proposed judicial survival remedy would enlarge the compensation and beneficiary arrangement Congress chose for deaths in this field, despite Congress's express treatment of the personal-injury/death transition. The Court should not create the parallel action in these circumstances. This is not a holding that all maritime survival actions everywhere are abolished or that every type of predeath economic loss, foreign-law remedy, state-law remedy outside this field, or unpresented claim has been adjudicated. Section 764's distinct foreign-law provision and section 767's savings/geographic text remain; their operation is not erased by describing this claim as displaced. No independent claim under either provision is supplied here.

## 3. Geissal v. Moore Medical Corp. — No. 97-689

### Reviewed components and common statutory ground

Set aside the Eighth Circuit's judgment insofar as it sustains the categorical COBRA denial based solely on other group coverage existing before election, and remand for the properly remaining questions. This commitment supports reversal of that legal construction; it authorizes neither an unconditional benefit award nor a disposition of every count. Count I is the COBRA count. The Rule 54(b) judgment also encompassed Count II, estoppel, but the reviewed statutory question does not require an independent estoppel holding. Counts III, waiver, and IV, documents, were outside that judgment. Their merits are not adjudicated. Any consequential treatment of Count II must follow the actual judgment and remaining relief on remand; no unsupported affirmance of its separate factual or legal grounds is supplied.

**G1 — Timing means a change after election.** Under the applicable section 1162(2)(D)(i), other-group-plan coverage supplies this termination event only when the qualified beneficiary first becomes covered after electing COBRA. Coverage already in place before election does not become a new post-election event merely because the beneficiary elects continuation or remains covered. The other plan may cover the beneficiary as employee or otherwise, but the applicable clause also requires that it contain no exclusion or limitation concerning any preexisting condition of that beneficiary. That is a condition-specific restriction, not an overall comparison of benefits, deductibles or adequacy. Clause (ii), subsequent entitlement under title XVIII, is a separate branch with no supplied application here. The later HIPAA language was not effective for this dispute.

**G2 — Coverage-gap purpose.** The employer's strongest alternative treats continuation as necessary only until the beneficiary has another source of group coverage and therefore regards preexisting coverage as sufficient to end the employer's obligation. That construction could avoid overlapping insurance, but it deprives the express post-election timing language of its function. Continued dual coverage is not an absurdity: plans can differ without the statute making judges compare their overall adequacy. No policy inference can replace the selected trigger.

**Remedy and limits:** The record reports no evidence of an Aetna exclusion for this condition, but that finding does not establish payment of every expense. Existing other coverage fails the timing condition regardless. Proper premiums, duration, other termination grounds, qualifying-event rules, actual benefit obligations and authorized relief remain separate. No new fiduciary-misrepresentation finding, equitable remedy, inter-plan reimbursement rule, damages amount or permanent welfare entitlement follows.

## 4. Hopkins v. Reeves — No. 96-1693

### Review and common result

Reverse the Eighth Circuit's Beck-based grant of habeas relief and remand for further proceedings consistent with rejection of the reviewed instructional ground. The separate due-process ground rejected below is not reinstated, and no unreviewed habeas claim, execution date or unconditional discharge is decided.

**H1 — Beck protects a real lesser included option.** The Constitution does not require the trial court to instruct on second-degree murder and manslaughter when, under Nebraska's governing offense definitions, they are not lesser included offenses of the charged felony murder. Beck prevents the capital-only withdrawal of an otherwise legally available, evidence-supported lesser included offense; it does not require a State to alter its substantive offense elements or permit conviction of a different, nonincluded offense merely to give the jury an additional punishment option. The actual Nebraska distinction matters: felony murder rests on the intent and commission of the predicate felony without an independently proved intent-to-kill element, and the requested crimes are not legally included under the State's settled definitions. The neutral packet's description of intent being conclusively presumed is not treated as a new jury finding of actual intent, or as adjudication of an unpresented presumption challenge.

**H2 — Reliability requires a noncapital outlet notwithstanding inclusion rules.** The strongest contrary path emphasizes that a jury convinced Reeves committed a grave wrong may convict of the capital offense rather than acquit, and that naming the alternatives nonincluded does not eliminate that pressure. Bowersox supplied genuine noncapital options; this case does not. The reliability concern is therefore real and cannot be dismissed by pretending Bowersox already decided the absence-of-alternatives case.

**Resolution:** H2 does not establish a right to these requested offenses. Beck's required option must be legally available, and *Spaziano*, Part II, rejects an illusory alternative for which the defendant cannot lawfully be convicted in the presented posture. That decision involved limitations rather than offense elements, but its rationality principle supports retaining the legal-availability condition. *Gaudin* protects jury determination of actual elements; it supplies no power to replace them with a lesser-punishment menu. This is a limited answer about the requested nonincluded offenses, not a general holding that any state label defeats independent constitutional review or that an arbitrary departure from established state law is immune from review.

Nebraska's judges rather than the guilt jury select death or life. This further distinguishes Alabama's structure in Beck but is not adopted as an independently sufficient rule that judge sentencing always cures an unreliable guilt choice. No finding is made about an unprovided jury instruction concerning sentencing responsibility. The decision does not decide the constitutional validity of Nebraska's judge-sentencing arrangement as a separate claim. *Buchanan* remains a sentencing-mitigation decision, not authority to deny a genuine lesser-included guilt instruction.

## 5. Muscarello v. United States / Cleveland et al. v. United States — Nos. 96-1654 and 96-8837

### Common posture and separable requirements

Both dockets require a merits disposition. Muscarello concerns the locked truck glove compartment; Cleveland concerns guns in the trunk of the car accompanying the drug transaction. The source supplement resolves the docket identity and March 23 argument date. No later possession-in-furtherance amendment applies.

Every Justice preserves *Bailey*'s distinction between use and carrying and the independent during-and-in-relation-to element. Mere possession or proximity does not establish carrying. A firearm's drug-related purpose cannot supply otherwise missing statutory conduct. Ratzlaf's knowledge-of-unlawfulness rule and Staples's weapon-characteristics rule are not new elements of this offense. Knowledge of the firearm's presence remains essential to the transportation construction; this commitment adds no general knowledge-of-illegality requirement.

### Two surviving constructions

**M1 — Accompanied, knowing vehicular carriage.** A person carries a firearm when he knowingly possesses it and conveys it with him in the vehicle he accompanies, including in the glove compartment or trunk. Carrying objects by vehicle is ordinary usage; the verb does not require the object to be supported by the person's body. The rule excludes mere stationary storage and does not decide shipment through another person or a common carrier when the defendant does not accompany the firearm. Immediate accessibility is not an additional statutory element. The Government must independently establish the trafficking relation rather than derive it from the chosen broad verb. Affirm both appellate judgments on the reviewed carrying issue, without imposing a new sentence or ruling on unrelated issues.

The strongest objection is that ordinary general-language carrying is broader than the familiar expression of carrying a firearm, which commonly refers to being armed. A long mandatory additional prison term and the separate use verb intensify the demand for a clear contextual reading. The M1 Justices answer that personal accompaniment and knowing conveyance are actual conduct, not the availability/possession theory rejected in Bailey; the contextual qualifier limits the offense's connection to trafficking, while the carry verb retains its ordinary vehicular sense. Lenity does not require choosing the narrowest available dictionary sense when ordinary contextual interpretation resolves the conduct here.

**M2 — Armed carriage, or residual lenity.** In the statutory use-or-carry setting, carrying a firearm means bearing it as an arm on the person or comparably immediately available for use. Transportation in a locked glove compartment or car trunk does not become that conduct solely because the defendant knows of it and travels in the car. At minimum, the competing established usages leave a material criminal ambiguity after ordinary interpretation, so Ratzlaf's residual-lenity holding prevents imposing the added five-year term on this transportation construction. Reverse both appellate judgments and remand under the narrower rule; do not order an acquittal or foreclose a properly available ground not adjudicated here.

The strongest objection is that this reading adds a readiness/accessibility condition not expressed in the statute and substantially overlaps carrying with active use. The M2 Justices answer that carrying a weapon without employing it remains distinct from use, while the ordinary phrase's armed-carriage sense is not supplied by the separate trafficking-relation element. Even if M1 is linguistically permissible, the penal ambiguity remains material. This path does not reopen Smith's barter-use holding or make all criminal lexical ambiguity automatically dispositive before interpretation.

## 6. New Mexico ex rel. Ortiz v. Reed — No. 97-1217

### Proposed summary action and sufficient ground

Grant review and summarily reverse the New Mexico Supreme Court's judgment, remanding for proceedings consistent with the federal extradition duty. Do not retry the alleged parole violation, decide the truth of threatened mistreatment, pronounce Ohio's prospective conduct lawful, or order a specific transfer time.

**R1 — The asserted duress does not remove fugitive status.** After a governor grants extradition, Doran limits asylum-state habeas review to facially adequate documents, a charge in the demanding State, identity, and fugitive status. The first three predicates were accepted below. Reed was convicted in Ohio, paroled, and left for New Mexico before the scheduled parole-officer meeting; his asserted fear of unlawful revocation or injury on return does not turn that departure into a failure of the fourth prerequisite. The fugitive inquiry does not authorize the asylum court to predict the demanding State's future treatment or adjudicate defenses to its proceedings. Article IV and section 3182 control over New Mexico's state constitutional safety provision insofar as it is used to refuse the federally required return.

**R2 — Credited fear and safety justify refusal or additional inquiry.** The strongest opposing path points to the credited, unrebutted testimony and the gravity of bodily harm, treats coercion as negating voluntary flight, and invokes New Mexico's own duty to protect persons within its borders. ## United States v. Bestfoods — June 8, 1998

**Reach and disposition.** All eight Associates reach the preserved CERCLA parent-liability questions and support vacating the Sixth Circuit judgment and remanding for application of separate derivative-liability and direct-facility-operation rules. They do not reinstate the District Court's liability judgment or find CPC ultimately liable. The Court has the ordinary appellate channel over the federal response-cost action; Peacock's lack of independent jurisdiction in a later ERISA judgment-collection suit supplies no jurisdictional obstacle here.

**Common operative propositions.** CERCLA's designation of persons who owned or operated the facility does not, without more, disregard the corporation's separate identity. A parent's ownership, selection of directors, overlapping officers, monitoring, and ordinary financial and policy oversight do not themselves impose the subsidiary's liability on the parent. Derivative liability remains possible when the applicable requirements for disregarding corporate separateness are proved. This case does not select state rather than federal veil-piercing law or decide whether those requirements are satisfied.

A parent may separately incur direct liability for its own facility operation, whether or not the subsidiary's veil can be pierced. The inquiry concerns the parent's management, direction or conduct of facility operations, particularly operations involving leakage or disposal of hazardous waste or decisions about environmental compliance. Direct liability is not restricted to the parent replacing the subsidiary entirely or operating through a formal joint venture. It is also not established merely by active participation in the subsidiary's business generally.

The ordinary presumption that a dual officer or director acts for the subsidiary when conducting its business preserves normal corporate arrangements. Evidence that the individual actually acted for the parent in managing the facility's relevant operations can overcome that account. The parent's own environmental official's role therefore requires a functional inquiry into whose business he was conducting and what authority he exercised. His title, environmental advice, or attendance alone establishes no answer. Courts must distinguish ordinary oversight from the parent's own operational intervention; they may not pool every employee's actions into the parent merely because personnel overlap.

**Competing paths and limits.** The strongest case for retaining the appellate judgment is that a broad active-control test would convert routine corporate governance into strict parent liability and defeat settled separateness. That objection defeats the District Court's general-business-control rule, not the distinct textual liability of a parent that itself operates the facility. The strongest case for reinstating the District Court's judgment is the parent's extensive personnel and environmental involvement. Those facts warrant examination under the direct-operation rule but do not eliminate the actor, function and capacity inquiries. Remand, rather than an appellate liability finding, answers both errors.

## National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt — June 8, 1998

**Reach.** All eight Associates accept the authorized review of the D.C. Circuit's final judgment and decide the Commerce objection to these challenged project applications. No new Supreme Court docket or argument date is supplied. The fly's intrastate location and absence of trade do not establish immunity by themselves. Sweet Home's statutory construction is binding, but neither its actual-injury rule nor its permission routes establishes Congress's power in every application.

**Shared entering-law premises.** Lopez requires identification of the enacted class and a constitutionally adequate interstate mechanism before aggregation measures magnitude. Congress has latitude over magnitude and regulatory design once that mechanism exists; courts independently identify its constitutional eligibility and the reasonable fit between it and the enacted class. Wickard, Perez and Hodel preserve genuine economic-class regulation. A court cannot insert a missing statutory commerce element. Hale, as applied in Rybar and Kirk, permits necessary inclusion of intrastate conduct only through a specific anti-evasion or material-impairment relationship to a valid interstate regime. American Life League, Robinson and Bird concern conduct whose statutory object and operation directly reach actual service markets or operating commercial assets; Hinds preserves independent work for the statute's actual predicate. No direct-interstate-externality branch has been adopted.

**Path A: sustain these development-related applications.** The relevant application of statutory harm is significant habitat alteration undertaken through hospital, road and commercial development. Building and converting land through the actual construction, land, financing, labor and material markets is economic conduct itself; it is not simply an eventual consumer-spending consequence of injury to a fly. Section 9, as construed through the habitat rule, reaches this physical development conduct because of the actual injury it causes. On this reading a court may sustain the economic development application of the existing harm category without pretending that every taking is economic or adding a new criminal element. Aggregation then measures the substantial effect of that development class. The limit is actual economic habitat conversion, not every property owner's purchases or every ecological benefit.

The strongest objection to Path A is that Congress enacted a taking prohibition without an economic element; the Court may not replace that class with a favorable subset derived from the defendants' business activities. Lopez refused to reclassify school-zone possession because the particular defendant expected payment, and Hinds refused to treat an owner's economic relationships as the statutory property-use connection. Path A's answer is that construction is the very habitat-altering activity regulated in these applications, whereas payment for a delivery and purchases by a church owner were separate surrounding relationships. That answer is the precise disagreement over class and fit; calling the projects commercial alone does not resolve it.

**Path B: reject the offered Commerce justification for these applications.** Section 9's enacted class is taking listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, regardless of whether the actor is building for profit, acting privately, or engaging in any exchange. The fit inquiry cannot be satisfied by selecting the economic attributes of these defendants' projects while the prohibition's defining object and operation remain injury to a species without a demonstrated market relationship. These projects' economic character is real, but Lopez requires more than a useful transaction in the factual setting. A generalized chain from species preservation through biodiversity, possible future uses, health or national prosperity does not identify the missing mechanism.

The strongest objection to Path B is that the Commerce Clause allows regulation of economic conduct for environmental ends, and that the objection confuses the absence of a trade in the protected animal with the presence of a real construction market. Path B accepts those propositions in the abstract: Congress can regulate actual economic land conversion within an appropriately fitted class. It rejects this application's class reconstruction because the taking prohibition does not define that economic relationship, and the record supplies no alternative specific interstate or regime mechanism. It does not revive a categorical State-subject exemption, require each project to cross a border, or impose universal legislative findings.

**Separate regime and externality paths tested by every Justice.** The anti-evasion/material-impairment route requires reasonable necessity to an identified valid interstate regime. The independent externality route requires a substantial interstate consequence that cannot reasonably be contained by the States through ordinary territorial authority, material support and reasonable statutory fit. Scalia and Thomas join the enacted-class ruling and regime route, reject the independent externality route and seek confined reversal. Stevens and Souter retain economic-application affirmance and withhold a join from the Court opinion. No route supplies an invented factual mechanism or a categorical environmental exemption.
