## Coalition, remedy and limits

The five share all operative Path A propositions: this candidate debate is a nonpublic forum; exclusions must be reasonable in light of its purpose and viewpoint neutral; the actual exclusion satisfies that rule. Reasonableness requires neither the best nor the only available debate design. Popular support and journalistic significance cannot serve as a proxy for disagreement with a candidate's views. The holding concerns the actual selective debate, not every public-broadcast program.

The three contrary Associates share a standards rationale distinct from the Eighth Circuit's unconditional ballot-qualification rule. If published, their dissent must explain why genuine, sufficiently objective selection standards are necessary for this governmental electoral platform and why the advance qualitative category remains inadequate despite the jury findings. It must preserve their willingness to allow reasonable selection and must not accuse officials of a motive the jury rejected.

Remand implements the selected First Amendment disposition within the remaining proper proceedings. No damages amount, immunity ruling, fixed polling threshold, compulsory future broadcast, election remedy, revived statutory-access claim or broader prior-practice finding is supplied. No separate writing is invented among the five merely to reproduce different general approaches to broadcasting. Stone and assignment remain for assembly.
