### Issue 2: Second Amendment coverage and permissible regulation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Love v. Pepersack (December 11, 1995) recognizes the personal right and protected acquisition within its scope. Hale Part III-A applies that rule without a militia-membership prerequisite and rejects automatic immunity or exclusion based on military usefulness. Hale Part III-B remands its thirteen possession counts for an actual weapon-and-regulation inquiry; it does not uphold or invalidate every §922(o) application. Miller’s surviving weapon-and-record inquiry remains. These propositions and their vote-supported limits are in the same [inherited-law extracts](law/united_states_v_rybar.md).
* **Other Governing Law:** Second Amendment; §921(a)(23) and §5845(b), whose completed-weapon, receiver, conversion-part, and assembly branches retain their distinct predicates. No comprehensive weapons classification or universal scrutiny tier has been established by the supplied holdings.
* **Record Application and Open Point:** Rybar may invoke the right personally even though these possessions accompanied sales. The question is whether the substantial restriction is justified for the two identified fully automatic submachine guns in the presented transactions. The government can argue that limiting additions to civilian automatic-weapon stock directly addresses sustained-fire risks that tracing alone does not prevent. Rybar can answer that a prohibition is more burdensome than licensing or registration, that military utility is not disqualifying, and that permitted older stock challenges an undifferentiated danger rationale. The constitutional assessment must address the characteristic, burden, regulatory purpose, and actual statutory exceptions. The prior-lawful-possession exception limits expansion and avoids requiring surrender of existing lawful stock; it does not establish that older examples function more safely. Ordinary firearms cannot be excluded merely because alternatives remain available, and the statutory word machinegun cannot replace analysis of materially different objects.

If a candidate for a given office receives a majority at the open primary, the candidate “is elected” and no further act is done on federal election day to fill that office. Since this system went into effect, over 80% of the State's contested congressional elections have ended as a matter of law with the open primary. Respondents, Louisiana voters, challenged this primary as a violation of federal law. Finding no conflict between the state and federal statutes, the District Court granted summary judgment to petitioners, the State's Governor and secretary of state. The Fifth Circuit reversed.
