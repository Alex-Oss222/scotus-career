## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Existing Davis damages action, administrative immunity, and discovery

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the continued availability of the existing damages action, absolute immunity, and the premature summary judgment. He does not find discrimination or decide qualified immunity on a developed record.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A nonstatutory internal judicial EEO procedure does not displace the individual-capacity action recognized in Davis v. Passman. Administrative hiring receives no absolute judicial immunity, and targeted discovery is required when the defendants control evidence bearing on motive and the plaintiff identifies specific discovery capable of testing their explanations.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Davis v. Passman permits this individual-capacity sex-discrimination action notwithstanding a nonstatutory internal EEO procedure, and administrative hiring receives no absolute judicial immunity. Duffy was entitled to appropriately limited discovery before resolution of intent and pretext.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Meyer preserves officer actions, Forrester classifies hiring by function as administrative, and Bush involved a congressionally authorized remedial substitute rather than an internal process created by the judiciary itself. Duffy’s requested discovery was specific and directed to evidence controlled by the defendants, making summary judgment premature.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not establish discrimination, require Duffy’s appointment, authorize discovery into adjudicative work, or decide damages or qualified immunity on a developed record. A congressionally enacted exclusive remedial scheme could present a different case.
