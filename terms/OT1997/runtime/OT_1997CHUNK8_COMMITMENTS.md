## Textron Lycoming Reciprocating Engine Division v. UAW — No. 97-463

### Proposed sufficient ground and scope

Reverse the Third Circuit and remand to reinstate dismissal of the presented §301 action for want of the asserted statutory jurisdiction. Section 301(a)'s category is suits for violation of covered labor contracts. A standalone formation-fraud action that alleges no violation by either party does not enter that category merely because it seeks to have the contract declared voidable. The statute permits a court to decide formation or validity when necessary to resolve a properly brought violation suit; this holding does not prevent such a defense or a genuine declaratory action concerning an actual or threatened violation. Neither predicate is established by the present complaint or reported record. The separate federal-common-law theory is outside the reviewed question and is not adjudicated.

Wooddell establishes that an individual beneficiary may enforce a qualifying interunion contract; it broadens neither the covered type of dispute nor the statute into all litigation concerning labor contracts. Kokkonen confirms that federal policy and a contract's relationship to federal law do not replace a statutory jurisdictional basis. Lexecon reinforces enforcement of the actual statutory allocation despite practical arguments for a different forum. These analogies supplement, rather than substitute for, §301's own text. No fraud, breach, contract validity, state-law remedy, or NLRA unfair-labor-practice question is decided.

## Montana v. Crow Tribe of Indians — No. 96-1829

### Components and lawful paths

The established invalidity of the particular Montana coal taxes is not reopened. The present question is the Tribe's entitlement to money paid by Westmoreland before registry deposits began. The Ninth Circuit's direction to disgorge the entire approximately $58.2 million does not follow automatically from tax invalidity. The payment periods, Interior's refusal to approve the earlier ceded-strip tribal tax, the 1982 agreement releasing Westmoreland from the earlier tribal obligation, the actual taxpayer's failure to preserve its refund route, and the distribution of the registry deposits are independent equitable circumstances. No finding establishes that the entire preregistry amount was money belonging to the Tribe or measures its financial loss.

**Path A — reverse the mandatory whole-collection award.** Equitable restitution requires unjust enrichment at this claimant's expense on the legally relevant circumstances. It is not a punitive transfer of every dollar obtained under an invalid levy. United States v. California distinguishes a claimant's economic burden from ownership of the taxpayer's refund claim; Fulton distinguishes unconstitutional taxation from the remedial entitlement and permits lawful correction fitted to actual procedures and claimants. The strongest contrary argument is that these taxes directly impaired federally protected tribal mineral development, so the State should not retain their proceeds merely because a lessee wrote the checks. ## Questions and competing lawful paths

The reviewed question concerns this state broadcaster's selection of participants for its managed candidate debate. The First Amendment does not generally require a public broadcaster to give every private speaker access to its programming. But candidates in this debate express their own political positions, and the State may not use retained control to discriminate against their viewpoints. The issue is what access rule governs this particular program and whether Forbes's exclusion satisfies it.

**Path A: reverse the Eighth Circuit's constitutional ruling.** The broadcaster planned a particular moderated program and selected invitees; it did not generally open its facilities or airtime to all ballot-qualified candidates. Selective admission does not itself create a designated public forum. The candidate debate is a nonpublic forum in which exclusions must be reasonable in light of the program's purpose and viewpoint neutral. Reasonableness does not require that the selected method be the best or only way to stage a debate. A supported assessment of public interest and candidate support can preserve a useful exchange within limited program time. On this record, the selection category preceded Forbes's request, and the jury rejected political pressure and disagreement with his views. Ballot qualification alone does not establish strong public support or displace the editorial inquiry. The supported exclusion is reasonable and viewpoint neutral.

The strongest objection is that state officials choosing which candidates count as serious can distort electoral discussion while describing their choice as journalism. That risk requires genuine viewpoint neutrality and review of the actual stated grounds; it does not make an invited debate an open forum or require a numerical admission formula in every case. This record establishes an advance selection category and a jury determination of the disputed improper motives. Neither finding licenses pretextual exclusion in another case. No categorical immunity for government editorial decisions follows.

**Path B: sustain the appellate conclusion that this exclusion is constitutionally insufficient, on a more definite-criteria ground.** A state-sponsored debate presenting competing candidates is a forum for private political speech even though the station retains control over its production. A State may manage its limited program and need not invite every person who wants airtime, but it cannot reserve effectively unbounded authority to decide which otherwise eligible candidate deserves public consideration. The announced category of major parties and strong popular support does not specify sufficiently definite, reviewable admission measures; organization, press treatment and officials' assessment of seriousness leave substantial discretion over access to the State's electoral platform. Objective, reasonably framed selection criteria can protect the journalistic function without that defect. The jury's findings reject improper motives in this instance but do not decide the separate constitutional adequacy of the governing standards.

The strongest objection is that reasonable journalism necessarily involves qualitative judgment, the category existed before this request, and requiring objective standards can discourage informative debates or collapse a selective program into universal access. This path therefore cannot rest on the false premise that AETC adopted no advance category. Its proposed rule is confined to government selection among lawful candidates for a debate presented as a platform for their competing views; it does not regulate every editorial choice, compel precise polling thresholds or guarantee participation to every ballot-qualified candidate. The remaining objection is substantive: whether First Amendment doctrine requires that additional constraint despite the supported neutrality of this exclusion. The eight commitments divide on that question.

## Competing sufficient grounds

### A. Reverse the mandatory full-disgorgement direction

The Tribe's independent federal interest is real, but invalidity of the State levy and lawful tribal tax coverage do not alone establish a right to every dollar paid by Westmoreland. Equitable restitution still requires a connection between this claimant's protected interest, the defendant's enrichment and the measure ordered. The actual commercial arrangements, the agreement, the taxpayer's refund position and the distribution already made remain relevant to the trial court's equitable assessment.

United States v. California distinguishes a claimant's economic connection from its own or derivative refund entitlement; Fulton separates invalidity from claimant-specific, procedurally lawful relief. The Indian-tax cases preserve exact legal incidence and authorization rather than an automatic monetary consequence. These authorities do not foreclose a distinct tribal restitution claim, but they prevent converting invalidity into a compulsory transfer without its separate equitable predicates.

The strongest contrary argument is that the State's benefit was obtained by burdening this Tribe's federally protected trust mineral estate, and that benefit-based restitution need not match proved loss. That argument is stronger once the erroneous approval refusal is acknowledged. The response is not that Interior conclusively disabled the Tribe. It is that lawful authority to tax and a direct interest in the minerals still do not conclusively make all money paid by the lessee the Tribe's equitable entitlement, especially under the actual agreement and commercial circumstances. A bounded reversal leaves any properly supported independent relief to its own predicates.

### B. Affirm this particular benefit-based restitution disposition

The Tribe and its trustee assert their own federal interest in exploitation of the trust minerals, not merely an assignment of the company's refund. The State extracted the challenged benefit by imposing taxes already adjudged incompatible with that protected interest. The 1988 coverage ruling removes the supposed absence of lawful tribal taxation as a substantial reason to let the State retain the benefit. Equity can require surrender of the benefit obtained through that particular interference without requiring proof that each dollar would otherwise have been collected by the Tribe.

The contemporaneous commercial obstacle and later release of Westmoreland concern what the company would pay or owe; they do not themselves settle what the State may retain from its own unlawful exaction. The trustee's June 1983 participation also defeats an account of belated federal involvement after the gross-proceeds deposit arrangement. The present restitution theory does not require a new damages claim or a finding of lost royalties equal to the State receipts.

The strongest contrary argument is that this transfers a different taxpayer's money to the Tribe despite no adjudicated earlier debt, limited registry ruling, and possible differences between gain and tribal loss. This path answers by treating the trust-resource and established preemption relationship as the independent equitable link. It does not treat all economically affected claimants as owners of invalid taxes. The full amount is the identified unlawful benefit in this specific relationship, not a presumed compensatory measure or a penalty for general governmental misconduct. Detailed accounting and any lawful interest determination remain distinct from entitlement.
