## Alternative paths and strongest objections

**Deny the current motion under Rule 39.8, assuming indigence.** Vey v. Clinton establishes this lawful path. The strongest application would be that the actual current paper supplies no arguable connection between official conduct and the Sixth Circuit's nonpayment dismissal, and develops no disqualification circumstance beyond adverse rulings. A defective laundry claim cannot itself justify review of an independent procedural dismissal. Prior losses may provide context, and inability to pay does not immunize an otherwise frivolous petition.

That path fails here because the frozen account does not establish those particular complete-paper defects. It says the precise causal connection and bias circumstances are not established in the supplied account; it does not establish that a reviewed petition affirmatively contains no arguable connection. The associates will not turn a missing research reproduction into an adverse finding or supply Vey's distinct arguments to Brown. Nor do they find the unusual allegations clearly baseless under Denton's relevant distinction. They therefore decline discretionary denial without declaring that the access label itself supplies a substantive claim. If denial were adopted on a later genuinely established current-paper basis, its lawful paid route would need its own stated terms; another case's date could not be copied.

**Grant on the financial premise and decline denial.** The strongest objection is that this could reward vague allegations, dilute Vey v. Clinton or turn incompleteness into automatic fee access. BR2 answers it at the proper scope: no universal access-label rule or complete-paper finding is announced. The Court has a supplied inability premise and no established basis for this discretionary refusal; an ordinary financial grant leaves separate certiorari screening and all actual filing requirements intact. It neither resolves the vagueness favorably as fact nor orders unnecessary fact production. The earlier Vey denial remains fully available on its distinct supported predicate.

**Impose the prospective restriction.** A sequence of failed filings and an earlier fee denial can indicate a need to protect scarce judicial resources; preserved paid and criminal routes can make an otherwise justified response narrower. The decisive objection is the controlling applicant/category/inadequacy rule, joined by every current associate in both Vey decisions and numerous predecessors. The facts here establish no relevant repetition or inadequacy of particular-paper control. Neither this financial grant nor a possible present denial can supply that missing showing. All eight therefore reject the restriction while preserving future supported controls.
