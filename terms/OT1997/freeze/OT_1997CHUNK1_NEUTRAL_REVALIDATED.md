## United States v. Rybar — Third Circuit No. 95-3185 — November 24, 1997

**Event and review object.** User-assigned merits review of the December 30, 1996 judgment reported at **103 F.3d 273**. The scheduled date is a simulation assignment; no separate Supreme Court docket or historical grant is established. Rybar seeks relief from two §922(o) possession convictions on Commerce Clause and Second Amendment grounds preserved by conditional pleas.

**Verified record.** A federally licensed dealer, Rybar possessed and sold a Chinese Type 54 7.62-millimeter submachine gun at the Monroeville, Pennsylvania gun show on April 4, 1992. On April 5 he possessed and sold a U.S. Military M-3 .45-caliber submachine gun to the same purchaser. The charges under review are possession Counts I and III; separate NFA transfer Counts II and IV were dismissed below. The case therefore concerns two completed fully automatic weapons and sales, not an unidentified receiver or conversion-kit count. No new finding places either weapon in a §922(o)(2) exception. An old model or manufacturing date does not establish lawful possession before May 19, 1986.

**Lower reasoning.** The Third Circuit sustained §922(o) as direct regulation of the machinegun market and treated possession control as a means of limiting demand and private-stock expansion. Its Second Amendment rationale required the missing militia relationship. The dissent's commerce objection emphasized the absence of a jurisdictional connection and adequate legislative findings. Those lower positions are source facts, not controlling simulated law. Hale now supplies the relevant unanimous direct-market holding; Love and Hale reject a personal militia-affiliation prerequisite.

**Commerce issue.** Hale Part I permits possession control integral to this transfer/stock regime without a transaction-specific crossing element. The completed sales make the commercial connection concrete, but do not add an element to the offense. Lopez's general crime-cost objection does not displace this separate enacted market relationship. Legislative findings are not invariably required by simulated Lopez. Commerce power establishes authority to regulate, not an answer to the individual-right objection.

**Arms issue and competing positions.** Rybar can invoke the personal guarantee despite the commercial setting. The government can argue that limiting additions of fully automatic weapons to civilian stock directly addresses sustained-fire risks that recordkeeping or licensing would not eliminate; government channels and existing lawful stock represent a bounded transition rather than a concession that all new possessions are protected. Rybar can respond that a prohibition burdens protected acquisition and keeping more severely than registration, that military usefulness is not disqualifying, and that continued lawful older stock complicates an undifferentiated danger theory. **Record and disagreement.** Alberta Jefferson died in a fire. No subsequent final judgment or completed factual trial is supplied.
