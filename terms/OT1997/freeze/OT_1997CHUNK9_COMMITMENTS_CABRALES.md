## Reach, rule, and remedy

All eight participating Associate Justices would affirm the Eighth Circuit's affirmance of pretrial dismissal of Counts II and III for improper Missouri venue. The authorized channel is §1254(1); venue is the threshold issue reached, and guilt is not reached. Count II alleges conducting a financial transaction under §1956(a)(1)(B)(ii); Count III alleges a monetary transaction in criminally derived property under §1957. For these charges, the transactions were begun, conducted and completed in Florida. Missouri supplied the criminal source of the property, not a charged laundering act by Cabrales. Neither the statutory necessity of proving illicit origin nor the financial-transaction definition's commerce connection makes her charged laundering conduct occur where someone previously generated the proceeds.

Article III, the Sixth Amendment, Rule 18 and §3237(a) require locating the charged offense by its nature and constituting acts. Anderson supplies that inquiry. Section 3237(a) permits actual multistate crimes; it does not combine every antecedent crime with the later transaction that uses its proceeds. United States v. Thomas (May 19, 1997), Ginsburg's unanimous Part III, illustrates a genuine continuing transportation completed at an authorized destination. This indictment alleges neither Cabrales's Missouri conduct nor her transportation of the funds from Missouri. Gaudin (June 19, 1995) preserves every statutory element and its proper factfinder; it does not identify each circumstance element as an act committed in the place that circumstance originated.

The lower court's June 12, 1997 amendment controls: Count I was not dismissed and is not before the Court. No Missouri overt act, valid conspiracy venue, conspiracy guilt or aiding-and-abetting theory is inferred. No acquittal, immunity from a properly venued prosecution, resolution of another indictment, or rule about a defendant who commits the predicate offense or transports proceeds follows.

## Enhanced comparison and Justice-specific commitments

Two lawful constructions were tested. The Government's integrated-offense construction relies on illicit origin being indispensable and on the connection between producing and laundering proceeds. Its strongest limiting possibility is to treat the predicate offense as the laundering offense's beginning. The competing conduct-location construction treats illicit origin as the property condition that makes the Florida transaction criminal. The Government's construction fails on this indictment because it substitutes uncharged predicate participation or transport for the acts these transaction statutes prohibit; functional connection cannot supply that missing conduct. The conduct-location construction preserves all elements and genuine continuing offenses, so its principal objection does not require reversal.
