## What the historical comparator establishes

The historical merits judgment belongs to the Eighth Circuit. Judge Magill wrote for a panel also comprising Judge Wollman and Judge Goldberg. It affirmed final summary judgment for Wolle, Vietor and Longstaff. The comparator contains no corresponding historical United States Supreme Court merits decision, no Supreme Court opinion topology and no individual Supreme Court merits votes. No merits inference is drawn from a certiorari disposition, and no private certiorari tally is supplied.

The lower decision itself rejected both absolute judicial immunity and the offered internal-EEO-plan preclusion argument. It recognized an individual Davis-type constitutional employment action and held that the right against arbitrary sex discrimination was clearly established before the conduct. Its defense judgment rested instead on the absence of a triable discriminatory-selection issue and its conclusion that refusing further discovery was within the district court's discretion. The remand therefore agrees with the comparator on the two categorical defenses and differs on the final disposition and procedural assessment. The three defendants defend their favorable final judgment; Duffy seeks reversal and further proceedings. This is ordinary review of a final judgment, not an interlocutory immunity appeal restricted by Johnson or Behrens.

The actual lower judgment has substantial force as evidence of a lawful competing path, but it is neither binding Supreme Court precedent in this timeline nor a vote by any Justice modeled here. Its strongest grounds must be answered rather than attributed to those Justices and then counted as defections.

## Component reconciliation and strongest contrary paths

### 1. Individual constitutional action and the internal EEO plan

**Retained commitment:** The plan's existence alone does not displace this individual Fifth Amendment employment-discrimination damages action of the received Davis kind. Meyer distinguishes a new direct agency action from received individual actions; it does not erase Davis. Bush requires attention to the actual congressional remedial design, not a categorical conclusion from the existence of any administrative procedure.

**Historical comparison:** This conclusion agrees with the Eighth Circuit's rejection of the offered bar. The lower opinion reports that the plan originated with the Judicial Conference and identifies no congressional direction making it exclusive. The frozen formulation is deliberately narrower than an assertion that only an express statutory sentence could ever establish a special factor or that every internally created procedure is legally irrelevant.

**Strongest contrary path and resolution:** Judicial employment and the exclusion from Title VII could support caution about supplying an additional remedy; an actual congressional scheme or another sufficient special factor would require consideration. But this is an individual claim within the received Davis employment action, and neither an unquoted plan term nor an unsupported statutory design can be supplied to close it. ALPA does not create a universal nonconsent exception to administrative exhaustion. Stevens and Breyer's support for a defined, adequate, prompt, nonbinding preliminary procedure there likewise does not establish permanent displacement here. The decision preserves proper case management and leaves a materially different, properly supported exhaustion issue for its own record. It creates no Title VII coverage, agency action or automatic equitable remedy.

### 2. Absolute immunity and the hiring function

**Retained commitment:** Selection of the chief probation officer is personnel administration, not adjudication of a dispute. Judicial office, statutory appointment authority, operational importance and the entry of an appointment order do not convert that function into an absolutely immune judicial act.

**Historical comparison:** This agrees with the lower decision's application of Forrester. Antoine's historical and functional inquiry reinforces that result; Bogan's protection of prospective departmental legislation expressly preserves the distinction from individualized administrative personnel decisions.

**Strongest contrary path and resolution:** A chief probation officer assists judicial work and exercises important responsibilities, so the appointment differs in practical importance from routine staffing. Those differences do not make the selection an adjudication. The protection follows the challenged act rather than the official's title or the form of the appointment instrument. No actual legislative abolition or adjudicative act is presented.

Qualified immunity remains distinct. Harlow and Anderson continue to govern each defendant's conduct-time notice and actual conduct. The relevant hiring events occurred in 1993–1994. Davis and Forrester predate them; DeCarlo and other later decisions guide present adjudication but supply no retrospective notice. Rejecting the offered absolute defense does not decide every individual defense, personal role, constitutional element or form of relief. A failure to prove discrimination is not relabeled as a holding that discrimination was legally permissible.

### 3. Summary judgment, discovery and the bounded remand

**Retained commitment:** The court must assess the concrete evidence and the supported Rule 56(f) request under ordinary procedural standards before treating the defendants' affidavits and plausible lawful qualifications as sufficient to foreclose material inquiry. Specific nonconclusory support remains necessary; a general hope of impeachment is insufficient. The court may clarify, limit or deny an inadequately supported request and may decide an actually dispositive legal issue first. The remand does not guarantee any particular discovery or a trial.

**Strongest historical affirmance path:** McPhillips had substantial qualifications, strong references and favorable interview assessments. Ailts could not identify the source of the reported interest and inferred which position was meant. The vacancy announcement was neutral, inclusive recruitment is lawful, the law-clerk comparison concerns different work and inadequately described pools, and the requested depositions could be viewed as a general effort to find support after the defense produced legitimate reasons. Rule 56(f) does not require discovery before every summary judgment. Crawford-El itself preserves these limitations, ordinary summary judgment and protection against a general credibility attack. Hicks leaves the ultimate discrimination burden with the plaintiff and does not make disbelief of an employer's account an automatic victory.

**Why the retained remand remains supported:** The affidavits do not describe precisely the same proposition. Ailts recounts an interest in appointing a female; Wolle describes advice to recruit a diverse qualified pool. Ailts's stated uncertainty limits the inference but does not permit that account to be conclusively rewritten as recruitment alone. No finding is made that the statement expressed the panel's policy or that any particular judge adopted it. The actual selection context and the specific request to test the stated reasons distinguish this record from a wholly unsupported wish to attack credibility. The district court's stated reason for refusing discovery relied on the defendants' and screeners' favorable affidavits; the appellate treatment also invoked the judicial defendants' status. Those facts require a genuine procedural assessment rather than treating defense production as conclusive proof of motive.

The Eighth Circuit did **not** announce a clear-and-convincing burden, and the present reconciliation does not invent one. The difference is its application of ordinary evidentiary and discovery rules to this record, tested against effective Crawford-El's separation of specific support, necessary inquiry, legal defenses and ordinary summary judgment. Hicks supplies the distinction between permissible inference and compulsory liability. Actual DeCarlo supplies further current support for separate claims, individual responsibility and adjudication at the correct procedural stage; it does not itself dictate this hiring case's outcome. None transfers Mt. Healthy's complete retaliation burden allocation to every constitutional discrimination claim.

The lower court's conclusion that an all-male judicial panel would be unlikely to discriminate against a male supplies no special legal presumption. The law-clerk counts still may be weak or immaterial because the positions, pools and relevant circumstances differ; no generalized personnel inquiry is ordered. McPhillips's qualifications remain genuine evidence of lawful reasons and are not reweighed to declare Duffy the better candidate. The proper court may require a more precise identification of facts sought, use written inquiry, sequence the issues, protect unrelated judicial work and enter a renewed summary judgment if warranted. It must evaluate each defendant's knowledge, participation and purpose separately.
