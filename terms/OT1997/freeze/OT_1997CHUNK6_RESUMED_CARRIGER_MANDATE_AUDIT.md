**Result:** The proposed formulation does not establish a common complete mandate. It preserves the Brady gateway for further decision, while O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas require rejection of that gateway on the existing submission. The frozen remedy split remains. A less explicit statement of the difference does not answer it.

## Proposed formulation and operative effect

> Set aside the present new-trial/writ instruction and remand for further proceedings under the controlling gateway and habeas rules, without a binding Supreme Court determination that the gateway has definitively failed.

Its first clause removes the current writ instruction, which each Associate supports. Its final clause does substantive work: the mandate does not foreclose renewed consideration of this gateway and the Brady route that depends on it. If the intervening remand language directs correct gateway review, it is the four remanding Associates' remedy. If that language instead leaves whether to revisit the gateway discretionary, it still does not carry the other four's required disposition that this submission fails the gate. If the gateway is silently treated as closed despite the final clause, the formulation contradicts its stated effect and the remanding Associates' commitments.

Calling the order a vacatur, using “further proceedings,” or omitting a conclusive-failure statement cannot resolve this distinction. The issue is what remains legally open after the mandate, not the verb attached to removal of the lower instruction.

## Legitimate shared scope and limits of further circulation

The following is a supported common **partial disposition**, with each Associate's distinct remaining position preserved:

> The present instruction to grant a new-trial writ on the Brady rationale is set aside. Independently available claims not resolved by that rationale remain for lawful disposition.

This identifies agreement about removal of the existing instruction and preservation of other claims. It supplies no agreed answer to whether this Brady gateway must be reconsidered or is defeated on the present submission. It therefore cannot be presented as all eight joining a complete remand mandate, used to count the four gateway-rejecting Associates as supporting renewed inquiry, or used to count the four remanding Associates as supporting definitive gateway rejection. Any final judgment and operative mandate must resolve the actual component support through the ordinary assembly process; this audit assumes no additional position and declares no Court result.

The four remanding Associates can accept a clearer expression of their already supported remedy: vacate the present writ instruction; require correct whole-record probability and state-finding analysis; allow Brady merits only through a lawful gate or independent route; preserve both actual-team attribution and the bounded acquisition theory when properly necessary; and order no automatic hearing or writ. This clarifies their scope but supplies no new join from the other four.

The four gateway-rejecting Associates can accept a remand expressly confined, as to this submission, to independently available other claims after rejection of the Brady gateway. That is their recorded disposition, not an accommodation to the four who require renewed gateway review. Neither conditional materiality nor reserving the acquisition duty offers an intermediate gateway result.

No existing recorded objection is answered by the proposed omission of a binding failure determination. Further circulation within these supported formulations can make the disagreement precise; the supplied material does not support convergence on a complete mandate or a changed Associate commitment. A substantive change would need an actual reason affecting the recorded legal or remedial objection and the Engine's required validation, not an ambiguous description of unchanged positions.
