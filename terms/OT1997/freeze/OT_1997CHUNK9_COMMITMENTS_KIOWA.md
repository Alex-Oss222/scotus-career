## Scope and supported facts

This is a direct money action against the federally recognized Tribe on its $285,000 note plus interest. The industrial development commission's stock purchase and the chairman's execution in the Tribe's name are the supplied transaction. The note's recited Carnegie execution location and the creditor's Oklahoma City execution/delivery and payment account remain distinct. The note preserves sovereign rights and contains no governing-law clause. No unequivocal waiver or congressional authorization is established. Promise, default and commercial dealings do not themselves establish consent to suit. The authority to contract, the debt's substantive validity, amenability to suit and execution against an asset remain separate.

No officer defendant, separate corporation, tribal forum remedy, secured asset, guarantor, personal undertaking or enforcement instrument is invented. The absence of such a claim here is not a declaration that every alternative remedy is unavailable.

## Enhanced alternatives

**A. Immunity follows the tribal defendant in this contract action.** Santa Clara Pueblo supplies the unequivocal-waiver and congressional-authorization rule. Potawatomi reaffirmed that doctrine while rejecting a proposed business-activity exception and separating the Tribe's substantive tax obligations from its amenability to suit. Puyallup III likewise distinguishes the Tribe from individual members subject to properly obtained jurisdiction. The particular Potawatomi holding involved trust land; it did not adjudicate an off-reservation private note. The present extension rests on the status-based rule and the lack of a supported federal exception, not a false assertion that its facts were identical.

The strongest objection is that protecting outside commercial conduct reaches beyond internal self-government and shifts loss to nonmembers where ordinary state law applies. Congress may alter the doctrine, and the Court's received immunity decisions recognize a federal legal protection against which Congress has legislated. Mescalero's substantive law rule does not itself abrogate that protection. Commercial character alone cannot distinguish Potawatomi; commercial character combined with location offers a possible new boundary, but does not supply one already enacted or adopted. Under this path both asserted execution locations yield immunity, making their resolution unnecessary. Reverse the judgment rejecting immunity and remand for termination of the presented direct monetary action consistently with immunity, without deciding the underlying debt or other unpresented claims.

**B. Limit immunity to exclude a genuinely off-territory commercial transaction.** Stevens's express Potawatomi reservation supplies a direct prior judicial basis for this path. The Court created and defined the common-law immunity, and its prior cases' governmental and territorial settings do not necessarily establish a rule governing a tribe's voluntary market transactions outside its territory. Mescalero's ordinary-state-law principle reinforces the proposed distinction, though regulation and adjudicatory amenability remain separate doctrinal steps. Congress's ability to legislate does not necessarily prohibit judicial refinement of a judicial doctrine at an undecided boundary.

The strongest objection is that this treats the factual limits of prior cases as a new exception to their broadly stated defendant-based rule, despite Potawatomi's explicit refusal to remove business immunity and its recognition of congressional choices. The narrower response preserves Potawatomi's trust-land business result and Puyallup's protection of the Tribe while limiting only the outside commercial setting expressly reserved by Stevens. It does not support stripping immunity from all commerce. The actual place and legally relevant off-territory character must be established rather than assumed merely from the creditor's assertion; if the existing lower judgment does not supply the necessary finding, a bounded remand is required. The clean record does not establish that predicate conclusively enough to affirm an unconditional money judgment on this path.

**C. Waiver or substitute defendant.** Not supported. No Associate treats a payment promise or default as an unequivocal waiver, especially with an express sovereign-rights reservation. A private law obligation is not identical to consent to adjudication. No officer or separate entity is substituted through reasoning alone.

## Coalition, remedy and source limits

Seven Associates provisionally support immunity in the presented direct contract action, with reversal and a bounded remand; Stevens rejects categorical immunity for genuinely off-territory commerce and supports a remand because the supplied location predicate remains disputed. These are independent commitments before historical reconciliation and before Stone enters, not a final tally. No Marks combination is needed or asserted. All eight reject inferred waiver from the transaction and preserve the distinction between debt and amenability.

The seven-vote proposition covers a recognized tribe sued directly on a contract without clear waiver or congressional authorization; commercial rather than governmental activity and off-reservation rather than on-reservation contracting do not themselves remove immunity. It does not decide a particular official's liability, a distinct entity's status, asset execution, every tort claim, the note's enforceability in another forum, or a universal remedial vacuum. Potawatomi's business protection is applied and its disputed geographic reach resolved; Mescalero and Chickasaw remain substantive-regulation authorities. Puyallup retains its defendant-specific limits. Venetie and Crow are distinguished rather than silently turned into immunity holdings.

The most direct personal authority for the geographic exception is Stevens's express Potawatomi reservation. The packet supplies no comparably direct prior statement by Thomas or Ginsburg about off-territory tribal contract immunity; their own substantive tax and land-status positions have been addressed at their actual scope rather than exaggerated into a disposition. This is a specific evidence limit for reconciliation, not an inference that they must follow any historical alignment and not a reason to invent another source.
