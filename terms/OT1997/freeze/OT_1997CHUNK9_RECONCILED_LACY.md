## Grounds and application

**Current probable cause despite the interval.** Sgro requires facts sufficiently related to issuance to support a present probability; Gates assesses the practical totality and whether the issuing magistrate had a substantial basis. Time is material, but neither an automatic deadline nor irrelevant. Ornelas's nonwarrant appellate rule expressly preserves this warrant distinction.

The affidavit reported completed downloads, rather than merely an order or attempt. BAMSE records identified the telephone number and files; telephone records tied matching calls to Lacy's apartment. At least two described files supplied the pertinent acquisition evidence. The experienced agent's account of possessors' valuation and lengthy secure retention, commonly at home, therefore had an individual foundation. Together the facts support a fair probability of continued possession over this ten-month interval.

The strongest contrary argument is that a general retention account could make a home permanently searchable. The accepted application does not: it depends on verified acquisition, the particular property and this interval. It neither assumes indefinite possession nor finds an ongoing distribution business. General habits without evidence of this person's actual acquisition would present a materially different affidavit. Subsequent discoveries and admissions provide no retrospective support.

**Particularity.** Andresen and Coolidge require limitations sufficient to prevent a general exploratory search. The required specificity depends on the supported crime, items and information reasonably available at issuance; inability to describe more precisely does not justify unrelated categories. The reported affidavit tied the system to actual downloads and explained that investigators could not determine whether the files were on the hard drive or disks or what equipment/software accurate retrieval would require. The reported authorization supplied objective offense-related document limits, including the BAMSE connection.

The strongest objection distinguishes authority to search for specified images from taking an entire system and unrelated papers. The accepted result depends on probable cause relating to the system, the explained storage/retrieval uncertainty and objective records limitations. The lower opinion's Kow comparison identifies precisely why an unrestricted all-records warrant is different. Its three considerations—category probable cause, objective selection limits and available specificity—are useful applications, not a new mandatory national checklist.

Pinson and Myers require lawful authority for protected domestic information; a later warrant cannot authorize an earlier warrantless intrusion. This case presents the sufficiency of the warrant itself, with no established antecedent thermal or comparable search. Their protective holdings remain intact. The ruling creates no digital exception, universal authority to seize computers, forensic protocol, cloud or smartphone doctrine, or unlimited authority to search unrelated contents after seizure. No unquoted clause, incorporation dispute or item-level execution question is resolved.
