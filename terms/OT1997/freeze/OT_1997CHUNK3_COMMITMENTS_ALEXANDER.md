## Common legal comparisons and exact limitations

### Personhood and protected liberty

**Principal route:** Roe, 410 U.S. 113, 156–159, expressly excludes the unborn from Fourteenth Amendment “person.” Its discussion at 161–162 preserves state prenatal-injury, property and stillbirth protections without converting them into constitutional personhood. The biological allegation must be accepted as an allegation; it is not a legal amendment of the Constitution. Simulated Casey retained protected reproductive liberty, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health protection while reserving fetal personhood. That reservation is neither a new personhood holding nor repeal of Roe's separate holding. States remain able to recognize broader tort protection within the governing constitutional framework.

**Strongest competing route:** A wanted near-term child allegedly harmed by a third party presents no immediate collision with a patient's abortion decision, and prenatal tort law recognizes substantial interests distinct from abortion regulation. Those points support the legitimacy of state protection and the mother's independent claims. They do not change Roe's express constitutional category without an actual reasoned limitation or overruling. No Justice's dissent from a reproductive-liberty rationale establishes that he would recognize federal fetal personhood here. No supplied own writing provides that doctrinal displacement, and medical development alone does not accomplish it. The commitments therefore apply the existing holding at its exact scope rather than invent a context-specific constitutional person.

**Maternal liberty:** Casey's complete six-step standard requires an actual duty, attributable concrete interference and its proper trigger; the State's substantiation burden does not arise merely because a law concerns pregnancy. Fargo and Miller preserve that allocation. The challenged statutes do not prohibit continuation of pregnancy, command abortion, displace clinical choice, impose a treatment delay, disclose confidential information or transfer decisionmaking authority on these allegations. A reduction in possible tort deterrence is not by itself the State's interference with the protected relationship. The Court need not decide the outer scope of prenatal familial liberty or make childbirth itself less protected. It decides only that the specified additional remedies do not follow from the protected decision absent the necessary burden. No obligation to create a universal tort remedy follows, and absence of such a general obligation is not a substitute for equality analysis of remedies actually created.

### Ordinary equality: two remedies, two applications

Beach permits reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable generalization and imperfect lines without formal findings; Heller requires a relation to the actual disadvantage; Romer forbids treating retained general protection as an automatic cure for a distinct disability. The live-birth rule is not valid merely because the class is nonsuspect or a tort action is not fundamental. Nor does the hypothetical possibility of prenatal proof make every chosen limitation rational. The actual relationship is as follows.

**Wrongful death:** This remedy replaces anticipated pecuniary contributions by the deceased to survivors, including qualifying support, services and advice, plus the separately authorized expenses. The State may identify a live-born independent decedent as the point at which that derivative recovery attaches. Birth supplies a clear event separating loss of an independently existing family member's future contributions from the parents' direct injuries arising from a pregnancy's loss. A newborn's future is also uncertain, and a stillborn near-term child's likely contributions may be predictable. Those concessions establish an imperfect boundary, not that the boundary has no rational connection to the defined derivative cause. The reason is not that fetal development is impossible to observe or that all stillbirth claims lack evidence.

**Survival:** The statute continues the deceased's own accrued cause through an executor or administrator, including conscious pain and suffering and the applicable state rules for other estate damages and expenses. On the appellate assumption, live birth establishes the independently existing legal holder whose own claim may survive death, including a prenatal injury carried into that person's life. This makes the law's treatment of prenatal injuries followed by live birth coherent without deciding that the injury or consciousness began only at birth. The State may recognize prenatal claims conditionally on live birth and decline to create an independently transmissible estate claim in its absence. That legal organization of an estate remedy is the rational ground; wrongful-death contribution estimates alone would not explain survival's exclusion.

**Strongest alternative:** Vacate the mother's equality dismissal because the panel relied on historical Heller, treated limits on liability as self-justifying, overstated prenatal proof difficulty, and overvalued a limited emotional-distress remedy. A near-term stillborn and a child born alive for minutes may have comparable causal evidence, conscious pain and expected support. Under current Heller, a label cannot rationalize its own disadvantage, and Romer makes residual protection insufficient by itself. This is a serious alternative, particularly for survival's already-incurred pain, and each Justice addresses it below.

**Reason for rejecting that alternative on this record:** The current rules permit an imperfect threshold connected to the event and legal interest the cause protects; they do not demand the best compensatory scheme or a clinical measurement of every near-boundary case. These statutes identify the subject and transmission of two decedent-based remedies, rather than impose a disadvantage unrelated to their substantive operation. The traditional line is not justified solely by tradition: the relationship between independent life, a decedent's own legal claim and survivors' derivative recovery performs the legal work. The reported absence of legislative history requires no empirical remand. Maternal tort protection is relevant context for the allocation of direct injuries but is not equivalent, guaranteed or independently dispositive. Carey requires physical manifestations or destruction of basic emotional security; ordinary worry and upset do not suffice. No successful recovery or full compensation is found.

Giardina definitively supplies the wrongful-death exclusion and the parents' own tort route. It did not decide survival. The federal courts' undisputed survival construction remains an express assumption for deciding this federal challenge, not a state-high-court ruling. Because the challenge fails even on that exclusion premise, no certification or authoritative state construction is necessary to grant relief. An actual different state construction would change the premise of a later case; it is not invented here.

### January 20 Lee and remedy reach

Lee requires claim-specific injury and relief, distinguishes a proposed representative from a sufficiently injured claimant, and rejects conversion of abstract legal change into concrete personal harm. All eight Associates joined those propositions. Its six-Associate participation remand corrected an assumed-duty analysis under an erroneous penalty rule; Scalia and Thomas instead found the reported trigger absent. Here the mother's actual denied application supplies the trigger absent in Lee's patient and facility theories. Her stillbirth does not end the separate controversy over letters needed for the identified cause. The lawyers' claims receive their own threshold treatment; a class or another claimant's loss cannot supply their missing personal predicate. Lee establishes neither a new prenatal tort right nor automatic jurisdiction over every named official.
