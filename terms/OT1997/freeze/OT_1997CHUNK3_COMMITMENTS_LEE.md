## Components and supported alternatives

The component letters identify adjudicative subjects, not independent new claims.

**A — patient risk of an unwanted assisted death.** All eight affirm dismissal for want of a sufficiently supported actual or imminent personal injury. Even accepting Elsner's statutory qualification for analysis and the general medical assertions as the lower court assumed, the reported evidence establishes a serious progressive illness and past depression, but no sufficiently concrete impending exposure to the challenged lethal-assistance process. The necessary requests, medical and witness decisions, persisting impairment, renewed decision and ingestion are not presumed to follow from her diagnosis. The conclusion does not require certain death, flawless safeguards, a specified document, numerical probability, or the circuit's “very significant possibility” formulation. It applies Lujan's specific-facts and favorable-reasonable-inference standard to the relationship between this person and the challenged risk, rather than mechanically counting contingencies.

The strongest contrary path treats progressive illness and depression as a personal existing vulnerability affected by an enacted regulatory regime, allowing a reasonable inference of threatened injury. That is materially stronger than an undifferentiated policy objection. It nevertheless lacks a supported impending application or other specific connection sufficient to turn this patient's vulnerability into the threatened injury alleged. Neither statutory qualification nor universal efficacy of the safeguards is decided. The injury ground suffices; traceability and redressability remain undecided rather than being inferred from the lower court's reservations. No alternative holding imports Lujan's noncontrolling absent-agency redressability application.

**B — lowered medical-care protection under §4.01(1).** All eight affirm this distinct injury dismissal, not by a prosecution test. Assuming for this purpose that good-faith immunity reduces a protection otherwise supplied by O.R.S. §677.095, the reported record does not show personal exposure to changed care or an actual or imminent injury from that reduction. The strongest alternative is that presently reduced legal protection for an existing patient itself impairs a concrete bodily-safety interest. Lujan permits a procedural protection tied to a threatened personal interest without proof that the procedure certainly changes the final result; it does not make the asserted legal reduction self-proving injury. No supported present application is identified, and §4.02's retained negligence and misconduct liability prevents treating the assumed construction as universal immunity. The immunity construction and eventual substantive protection remain open.

**C — Dutson, patient representation and proposed class.** All eight leave dismissal operative on these bases independently. Dutson died July 2, 1995, before the District Court's merits judgment; no surviving personal claim, substitute or certified class is established. His personal prospective controversy cannot support that judgment. The other claimants' interests require separate analysis. Under Powers and Lujan, provider representation does not create a missing injury to the patient, and an uncertified proposed class supplies no independently continuing certified controversy. No universal ban on provider representation, holding that the representative lacks every possible direct injury, class relation-back rule or damages-pleading rule is adopted. The strongest counterpath invokes patients' difficulty protecting their own interests and the doctors' relationship to them; those considerations cannot supply the missing patient injury or a class that was never certified. No deceased plaintiff's controversy is revived by speculation about unreported substitution or damages.

**D — compelled provider participation: record transfer, advice and facility witness.** Two lawful paths remain material. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the participation-claim dismissal and remand for the correct injury and ripeness analysis; Scalia and Thomas would affirm that dismissal on the reported absence of concrete application, while rejecting a categorical penalty prerequisite.

The shared legal boundary is that absence of an express criminal or statutory penalty alone does not establish absence of Article III injury. An actual, presently applicable legal duty or supported threatened civil enforcement may burden conduct before a penalty occurs. Lujan requires concrete personal application; Abbott Laboratories requires attention to both fitness and hardship, and Babbitt forbids demanding completed prosecution in its actual applicable-prohibition setting. Babbitt does not itself establish injury under these conditional civil provisions.

The remand path identifies the actual appellate error: after assuming plaintiffs' disputed duties, the court treated “no penalty” as conclusive, considered imminence probably satisfied if injury were cognizable, and separately acknowledged possible civil enforcement while finding no hardship. It did not resolve the statutory duties or plaintiffs' concrete practical exposure using the correct rule. Remand must examine §4.01(4)'s express refusal protection and request-and-transfer condition, §3.01(2)'s attending-physician context, and §2.02(4)'s long-term-care witness provision separately, followed by concrete burden, the proper defendants' relation to it, traceability, redressability, fitness and hardship. No duty, injury or ripe cause of action is adjudicated merely by rejecting the penalty rule.

The strongest objection to remand is Lujan's summary-judgment burden: no specific request, current coercive obligation or enforcement threat appears in the reported facts, and a legal error need not alter a correct dismissal. The remanding Associates conclude that the appellate court's express assumed-duty/nonpenalty analysis is not a sufficiently established independent application finding to substitute for the analysis it did not perform. The supplied record does not authorize treating unreproduced declarations as absent. Remand permits ordinary record-based resolution, not an opportunity invented to cure litigant default or an automatic evidence-production order.

The affirmance path takes the reported absence of any concrete statutory trigger, practical present burden or supported threatened enforcement as independently sufficient even assuming the asserted duties. The strongest objection is that current professional obligations may constrain conduct immediately and the lower court thought imminence probably present, making an appellate substitution doubtful. Scalia and Thomas distinguish the court's conditional imminence observation from a demonstrated personal burden: assuming a legal obligation in a possible future situation is not a finding that these plaintiffs presently confront it. Their affirmance disclaims the penalty-only rule and does not foreclose a later supported application.

**E — facility association/exclusion of participating physicians under §4.01(2).** All eight affirm dismissal. No allegation of an intending participating physician at these facilities or supported present operational restriction supplies actual or imminent injury. A present restriction on control of a facility could support a different application, but general objection to potential future participation cannot establish one. This is a different absence of threatened application from the penalty-based rationale for D. No statutory anti-penalty construction or First Amendment merits holding follows, and D's remand does not reopen E.

**F — judgment clearing and scope.** All eight preserve vacatur of the District Court's equal-protection declaration and permanent injunction. No established injured claimant supports that patient-protection merits judgment; Dutson's death and the absence of an independently continuing class remain pertinent. Arizonans supplies limited correction of jurisdictional error, with Bancorp's equitable framework and Bender/Corrick's corrective authority; §2106 is not substantive jurisdiction. Clearing a merits judgment entered without a supported live claimant prevents it from operating as a constitutional adjudication. This is not an automatic rule vacating every judgment whenever jurisdiction is questioned. Revival of an undecided provider claim supplies no retroactive or automatic foundation for the different patient-based injunction. The other constitutional, ADA, Rehabilitation Act and RFRA merits remain unadjudicated.
