## Legal kernel and record limits

**Identity and review.** Richenberg v. Cohen; December 8, 1997. The judgment reviewed is Richenberg v. Perry, 97 F.3d 256 (8th Cir. 1996), No. 95-4181, decided October 3, 1996. Cohen identifies the successor official. Section 1254(1) supplies review of the federal appellate judgment. The granted constitutional question concerns the actual availability of individualized rebuttal in this identity-statement discharge. Richenberg sought reversal and reinstatement or lawful renewed separation proceedings, with prospective relief. No separate Supreme Court docket, grant date, or argument date is supplied by these fragments.

**Material record.** A December 1993 hearing was followed by reconsideration under the new policy and a June 1994 hearing. The Secretary ordered honorable discharge on August 28, 1995. The District Court granted the Government summary judgment; the Eighth Circuit affirmed. Richenberg denied intending prohibited conduct and offered strong service, character, integrity, and discipline evidence. Cross-examination elicited attraction to men and equivocal answers about possible future sexual activity and lawful marriage. Those answers establish neither completed prohibited acts, an actual marriage, nor an express intent to disobey.

The Board of Inquiry made a brief propensity finding. The Board of Review relied on attraction and discounted character witnesses as not specifically asked about propensity. The appellate majority read the existing decision as a credibility assessment; Arnold disputed that reading and the adequacy of the actual explanations. Preserve this distinction. No express agency demeanor finding, excluded testimony, missing hearing, additional misconduct, or numerical proof burden is established.

**Operative rule and statute.** Thomasson applies ordinary rationality to a conduct-related identity-statement inference only with genuine rebuttal. Propensity requires likelihood beyond abstract desire. Completed misconduct and individualized operational injury are unnecessary; strong service and the absence of misconduct are relevant without automatically defeating the inference. Orientation or attraction cannot become conclusively disqualifying merely by being called propensity. Military judgments receive substantial weight while remaining subject to independent constitutional review.

Section 654(b)(2) requires separation on an approved statement finding unless a further approved finding establishes that the member demonstrated he does not engage in, attempt, have a propensity toward, or intend homosexual acts. The directive requires advice of the presumption and an opportunity to rebut. Section 654(e) permits omission of processing only upon both an avoidance-or-termination-intent finding and a military-best-interest finding; neither is established here, and permission is not a retention command. The separate §654(b)(1) retention route requires all five findings: departure from usual behavior, unlikely recurrence, absence of force/coercion/intimidation, continued presence consistent with discipline/order/morale, and no propensity or intent. That route and §654(b)(3)'s marriage or attempted-marriage route do not replace (b)(2)'s inquiry. Section 654(f)(3)'s sexual-purpose bodily contact or contact reasonably understood to demonstrate propensity or intent does not make ordinary attraction an act.

**Scope.** APA reviewability was assumed, expressly undecided below because the Government did not contest it. It does not bar review of the constitutional controversy and supplies no Supreme Court APA holding. No independent APA, due-process, speech, directive-validity, substantive-liberty, or other ungranted claim is adjudicated by the common application. No heightened scrutiny, governmental actual-purpose burden, general investigation checklist, or universal explanation duty is adopted.

### Posture

Richenberg challenges his honorable discharge under the military's identity-statement policy. After two administrative hearings, the District Court entered summary judgment for the Government and the Eighth Circuit affirmed. The question is whether this application afforded a genuinely available opportunity to rebut the conduct-related inference permitted by Thomasson.

### Proposed controlling holding and authority

**Rule and application.** Ordinary rationality permits the conduct-related statement inference sustained in Thomasson only when the member has a genuine opportunity to rebut it; propensity means likelihood beyond abstract desire. This record permits the inference after actual consideration of Richenberg's denial and supporting evidence, and the constitutional challenge therefore does not require displacement of the reviewed judgment or discharge.

**Controlling explanation.** Thomasson requires meaningful rebuttal, not acceptance of every denial. Neither completed prohibited conduct nor individual operational injury is a prerequisite to a permissible military prediction. Richenberg's service and character evidence mattered, but did not automatically overcome the inference. The hearings received that evidence and his denial; cross-examination also produced equivocal answers about future conduct. The existing finding and evaluation of that evidence support the conduct-related assessment of the record. The Board of Review's discounting of general character testimony can reflect its assessment of that testimony's probative reach rather than categorical refusal to consider rebuttal.

The strongest contrary reading is that the Boards' brief explanations merely repeat attraction and fail to explain disbelief of the undertaking to obey. The reasons and proceedings need not be read as making orientation conclusive. Their supported conduct-related reading does not require an invented demeanor finding or an additional agency reason. The availability of a competing inference does not itself compel remand. Sparse explanation alone does not establish a constitutional violation or a general constitutional demand for further findings. Attraction alone remains insufficient, and the Court does not approve replacing actual agency reasons with hypothetical appellate ones.

Walmer concerned admitted conduct at a preliminary stage and does not decide this statement record. Romer preserves ordinary rationality and the need for a relationship to the actual disadvantage; Weiss preserves constitutional review while giving military judgments substantial weight. Jeffries's insistence on a lawful actual disciplinary ground does not alter Thomasson's military rule or shift the statutory rebuttal burden. Lower courts must distinguish a genuinely rebuttable prediction of conduct, propensity, or intent from a conclusive orientation exclusion. Relevant service and character evidence must receive actual consideration, but does not automatically require acceptance of a denial or retention. This decision applies that framework to the existing record; it establishes neither an attraction-alone rule nor a general constitutional requirement of additional administrative findings. It resolves no independent APA standard or claim.
