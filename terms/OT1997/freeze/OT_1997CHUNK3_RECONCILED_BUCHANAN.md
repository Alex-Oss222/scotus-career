## Components and operative propositions

**C1 — constitutional mitigation instructions.** The reviewed claim concerns the refusal of the requested general mitigation direction and four factor-specific instructions. Review the complete five-paragraph charge and sentencing context under Boyde's reasonable-likelihood standard. Governing Graham and Johnson require a meaningful route for the independent culpability significance of supported mitigation; admission and advocacy cannot substitute for a judicial route that actually prevents that use. Tuilaepa permits relevant individualized selection without fixed labels or weights, while expressly preserving those constraints. Delo permits an evidentiary prerequisite for a particular-factor instruction; no finding that every requested factor was proved is made here.

**P1 — shared governing constraint.** The jury cannot be prevented from considering and giving effect to constitutionally relevant mitigation. O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt that governing obligation. Scalia joins application of the governing cases and the no-error conclusion while preserving the constitutional reservation described in his row; his reservation is neither a partial join in the application nor controlling rejection of P1.

**P2 — no automatic entitlement to the requested script.** None of the eight requires all four factor-specific instructions, guaranteed favorable weight, numerical weights, a universal definition, or those exact words in every capital case. The five affirming Associates find an adequate route in this charge. The three opposing Associates require an effective corrective explanation on this record because the charge's operation otherwise restricts relevant mitigation; they do not adopt a universal checklist of mitigating factors.

**P3 — the disputed application.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas find no reasonable likelihood of exclusion: paragraph three permits a life choice even after vileness is proved, and its all-evidence direction, the initial life/death choice, the evidence, both counsel's explanations and the mitigation-referencing verdict form confirm the available use. They do not hold that evidence or counsel can cure a judicial prohibition. Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer find a reasonable likelihood that the disjunctive wording and following paragraph make life appear to depend on failure of the aggravator, with no judicial clarification of mitigation's independent work. They reject the premise that lay jurors necessarily understand the specialized eligibility/selection parsing. The evidence and advocacy cannot cure that substantive instructional restriction. The verdict form's reference supplies no separate cure finding.

**C2 — statutory-factor due process theory.** The four statutory mitigation subjects and admissibility provision alone establish no federal entitlement to each requested formulation. All eight retain that limited conclusion; no independent state-law instruction entitlement or separately preserved arbitrary refusal is supplied. The three error positions do not affirm the reviewed instruction judgment on this ground: the separately sufficient Eighth/Fourteenth Amendment effective-mitigation defect remains. No unrelated due-process theory is resolved.

**C3 — collateral gates, harmlessness and remedy.** The five no-error positions affirm the reviewed habeas denial without deciding AEDPA temporal applicability, a new Teague bar, harmlessness or entitlement to a writ. The three error positions would vacate the Fourth Circuit's judgment only insofar as it rejects the reviewed mitigation-instruction claim, and remand for lawful further proceedings under the error determination, preserving actual applicable collateral restrictions and any necessary correction or harmlessness inquiry. They order no unconditional writ, new evidentiary proceeding merely to fill an operator research gap, immediate release, guilt vacatur or execution action. The missing federal application date and capital statutory predicates remain open; Lindh's specified noncapital holding does not resolve them. Brecht's Doyle and O'Neal/Roy's personal-intent rules do not supply a generic Chapman extension or a historical Brecht rule for this distinct claim. The guilt judgments and unrelated habeas claims remain outside the reviewed question.
