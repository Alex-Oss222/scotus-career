## C. SUPPORTED ALTERNATIVES AND JUSTICE-WRITING LEADS

These are analytical alternatives except the State's contextual submission identified below.

* **Bruton path:** An obvious blank or spoken deletion in a joint trial may itself point the jury directly toward a sitting codefendant, leaving the accusation functionally unchanged despite removal of the name. The argument focuses on the redaction's visible accusatory form rather than every inference produced by linking an otherwise neutral confession to later proof. The strongest objection is that the confession omits two names, other testimony describes six participants, and identifying Gray still requires context beyond the confession's words.
* **Richardson path:** The State can argue that the redacted statement does not name Gray and that the limiting instruction governs because any incrimination depends on linkage to other evidence. The strongest counterargument is that Richardson distinguishes a confession stripped of any reference to the defendant's existence from an obvious deletion that invites a juror to supply the omitted name immediately. Consider whether a line between obvious blanks and neutral replacements is legally supported; the packet supplies no generic approval of every substitute phrase.
