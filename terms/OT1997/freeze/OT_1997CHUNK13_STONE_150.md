## B. Judgment and remedy

- **Proposed controlled merits judgment:** **Affirm** the Sixth Circuit's rejection of the surviving facial challenges and its dismissal of the superseded claims as moot, on the narrower reasons below. Do **not** endorse the proposition that almost any limit is permissible merely because it is labeled a contribution limit.
- **Proposed remedy:** No federal injunction against the surviving statutes on this facial record. Moot components receive no merits declaration. Expressly reserve concrete future as-applied challenges to severe cumulative contribution restrictions or documented threats from disclosure.
- **Expected Stone-only outcome:** **Vote to deny certiorari; if review is nevertheless granted, vote to affirm on the narrow grounds stated.** A personal denial vote does not turn a conditional merits analysis into an actual adjudication.

## C. Issue positions

### Issue 1. The 1996 legislative amendments and Article III

- **Reach:** Threshold issue, taken first.
- **Stone's position:** Apply received mootness law. The revised definitions materially remove the challenged regulation of issue-only speech and nonprofit independent expenditures, and the appellate record supplies no concrete threatened reenactment.
- **Proposed holding:** The claims directed solely at superseded prohibitions are moot. A State's theoretical legislative ability to change its mind is not a continuing live controversy.
- **Record application:** Kentucky changed the text during appeal; the new law no longer governs the speech the old definition had threatened. No past assessment or redressable residual injury to these parties was established for those claims.
- **Boundary:** A credible official threat to reenact, continuing enforcement under old text, or outstanding retrospective relief would require a separate analysis; Stone does not decide their hypothetical merits.

### Issue 2. Nonprofit treasury gifts directly to candidates

- **Reach:** Surviving facial claim.
- **Stone's position:** Apply *National Right to Work Committee* and the direct-contribution distinction retained in *MCFL*. Stone's source-based approach strongly protects a voluntarily supported ideological association's **independent speech**, but does not turn a payment to a candidate into independent expression.
- **Proposed holding:** Kentucky may prevent candidate contributions from KRL's general corporate treasury while permitting its lawful independent issue and electoral advocacy under the amended regime.
- **Reasoning:** The transaction's destination and capacity for candidate obligation matter. KRLPAC remains a voluntary, separately organized path for candidate-directed support. The rule is not an anti-abortion viewpoint exclusion and is not validated merely because the State asserts dislike of large spending; it addresses donor-to-candidate corruption pathways.
- **Boundary:** The holding does not authorize the State to bar independent issue speech or to deny the special *MCFL* treatment of sufficiently voluntary ideological corporations in their own spending.

### Issue 3. Attribution in express independent candidate advertising

- **Reach:** Surviving § 121.190(1) challenge.
- **Stone's position:** Apply election-disclosure scrutiny with special attention to the distinction from *McIntyre*'s anonymous ballot-issue pamphleteering. On this facial record, a sponsor line identifying a paid candidate-election communication materially informs voters and supports policing of coordination without restricting the independent expenditure itself.
- **Proposed holding:** The narrow **candidate independent-expenditure** identification requirement survives this facial attack; the amended statute cannot be applied as a disclosure mandate on ordinary issue advocacy merely because it mentions a candidate.
- **Reasoning:** Kentucky removed the broader issue-ad rule in 1996. Sponsor attribution identifies the accountable purchaser of a candidate-election message and can help connect it to filed transactions. The State's interest is real, though public knowledge of sponsorship is not equivalent to a license to disclose vulnerable associational membership.
- **Boundary:** Stone expressly reserves as-applied relief where an organization proves a reasonable probability of threats or reprisals. *McIntyre* remains meaningful for genuinely anonymous independent issue speech. This is no approval of government permission before publication.

### Issue 4. The $1,000 direct and $1,500 cross-PAC contribution limits

- **Reach:** Preserved facial challenges.
- **Stone's position:** Apply received closely drawn contribution review, including cumulative effects rather than treating each dollar ceiling in isolation. The limitation must serve corruption or its appearance and genuine circumvention prevention, never an effort to equalize speakers' influence.
- **Proposed holding:** The $1,000 per-candidate limit and $1,500 yearly aggregate to all permanent committees survive **on this facial record**. Kentucky may block a donor from using committees as channels to exceed a candidate cap, and the petitioners have not shown the combined rules make effective association impossible.
- **Reasoning:** The per-candidate rule tracks an established federal analogue; state-election scale and local corruption risks are relevant rather than merely rhetorical. The $1,500 cross-committee ceiling burdens multi-organization association substantially more, so Stone does not accept the proposition that its amount is immune from scrutiny. Yet it concerns financing committees empowered to allocate money to candidates, and the record does not quantify a prohibitory effect or demonstrate that its anti-pass-through rationale fails at the facial level.
- **Boundary:** If genuine independent advocacy funds are improperly treated as candidate contributions, or later evidence shows an effectively prohibitory combined cap, this disposition is no shield. Stone rejects a categorical rule that every $1,500 aggregate ceiling is valid everywhere and indefinitely, including through inflation.

### Issue 5. The $150,000 gubernatorial PAC receipt cap

- **Reach:** Preserved facial claim.
- **Stone's position:** A source-specific limit on total PAC receipts may address dependency on an organized donor channel, but it requires justification tied to corruption, not disapproval of PAC political views.
- **Proposed holding:** Sustain this cap against the record's facial challenge, without deciding a later case in which the cap actually disables a candidate's ordinary fundraising or selectively forecloses associations.
- **Reasoning:** The $1,000-per-PAC candidate ceiling means the candidate-wide $150,000 ceiling would begin to restrict donations only after sufficiently numerous committees have given, and petitioners did not prove that it presently destroys effective advocacy. The circuit's one-hundred-and-fifty-committee illustration supports a limited facial judgment, not an irrebuttable empirical finding about all gubernatorial races.
- **Boundary:** No new constitutional numerical safe harbor. The cumulative effect of ceilings, party structures, inflation, and actual candidate reliance can matter in a developed as-applied claim.

**Stone canon note:** The position distinguishes source and transaction, independent expenditure and contribution, candidate advertising and issue speech, and facial judgment and future proof. It does not silently convert Stone's elections chapter into governing simulated law.

## D. Stone certiorari position

- **Approved character forecast:** **DENY.** Status: **PROPOSED AND APPROVED**, as directed by the user.
- **Institutional ground:** Many of the original claims became moot after the 1996 statutory amendments. The remaining rules present state-specific figures and a facial record lacking evidence of prohibitory operation. Existing contribution and disclosure precedents provide substantial guidance, and no clean conflict on the precise live issues is verified. Stone would prefer a concrete future as-applied vehicle to a broad Court holding on this changing record.
- **Limit:** This is an institutional choice, not a merits endorsement of every future application. His hypothetical merits vote is **AFFIRM**, and the Court's collective action is not forecast.
