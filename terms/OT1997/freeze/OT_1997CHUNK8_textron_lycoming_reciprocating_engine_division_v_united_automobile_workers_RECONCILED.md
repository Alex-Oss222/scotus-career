## Reconciliation at the frozen scope

The historical Court's decisive complaint-specific ground and all eight commitments agree: the complaint alleges neither party violated the collective-bargaining agreement, and it establishes no actual or threatened violation controversy that a declaration would resolve. Federal labor-contract policy does not transform a formation-fraud damages action into a suit for violation. Validity can nevertheless be decided when necessary to an actual violation suit. The source's footnote 1 confirms that the alternative general federal-common-law jurisdiction argument lies outside the granted §301 question, as the neutral supplement already states.

The historical opinion went beyond that common ground to express doubts about whether a nonfederal defense to an anticipated federal claim can support §1331 jurisdiction and whether §301 works the same way. It ultimately assumed those matters in the Union's favor and rejected jurisdiction because no actual controversy was shown. The frozen common opinion need not reproduce those doubts or definitively resolve every anticipatory declaration. Its preservation of a genuine declaratory violation controversy means this decision does not categorically bar that vehicle; it is not a new holding that every asserted threat satisfies Article III or §301.

That formulation answers Breyer's historical join barrier without changing his judgment or importing a new factual premise. He expressly agreed with the statutory distinction and absence of an actual threatened strike or employer lawsuit. His independent commitment already refuses a needless anti-declaratory rule. Omitting the historical majority's unnecessary doubts permits his full join in the actual common propositions. It does not warrant an unsupported declaration that the seven other Associates adopted every broader proposition in his historical concurrence.

Stevens's historical concurrence supplies an additional, compatible NLRB-primary-jurisdiction rationale. His full join in the historical Court opinion establishes that this additional ground was not a condition of agreement with the statutory holding. The frozen narrower opinion need not decide whether Textron actually violated a bargaining duty or the Board has exclusive authority over every possible claim. No concurrence is required merely to copy history.
