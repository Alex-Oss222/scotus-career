## Common rule boundaries and strongest paths

### Personal protection remains separate from what the window exposed

Fields's control is substantial: $125 weekly, 40–50 visits, a key, entry in Warner's absence and permission to bring guests. Jones as preserved in Rakas and Olson permits protection outside one's own residence without a technical estate. Illegality of the activity does not eliminate that protection. Neither the lack of stored clothes nor the absence of sleeping there is dispositive.

Crawley has no key, payment or established overnight plan that evening. Olson's holding concerns an actual overnight guest and does not itself settle his claim. The recognition route rests instead on this supported repeated relationship, several-hour permitted visit and Warner's respect for the jointly closed room, rather than legitimate presence alone. The reservation route leaves that extension undecided and assumes the personal interest only to reject the challenged intrusion; it does not hold that commercial visitors categorically lack protection. Crawley's earlier overnight stay is context, not a finding that this was another overnight visit. No Justice treats Fields's rights as automatically transferable.

### Window path A — lawful shared exposure

Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer conclude that the actual opening exposed the well-lit room to ordinary unaided viewing from an area other tenants could use without defendants' permission. The officers did not manipulate blinds, press against the building, stand on tiptoe or employ technology. The principal ground is this actual shared line of sight, rather than investigative purpose, hypothetical remote observation or an inference that the officers were invited. The defendants could retain privacy in other parts of the dwelling while exposing this observed activity.

The strongest objection is that access reserved to tenants is not an invitation to unannounced investigative strangers, and a fenced area beside a dwelling differs from public airspace. These Justices give that distinction weight but conclude that the defendants established neither control over this common area nor a basis for treating its ordinary, readily shared view as concealed. The observed gap was substantial and apparent from inside; the claimed privacy in the information did not depend merely on whether these particular observers had been invited by the host. A possible property trespass does not alone decide Katz's personal privacy question. Dunn demonstrates that viewing protected premises from an unprotected vantage need not be a search, while requiring attention to the actual vantage. They do not classify this developed yard as an open field or adopt a categorical rule for all common areas.

Pinson and Myers protect otherwise unobservable concealed domestic information. Their distinction of actual openings matters here: naked-eye viewing is not automatically lawful, but the particular revealed room activity differs from the heat information obtained from a closed home. The two thermal decisions do not make every intentional observation of a home a search.

### Window path B — correct the exposure shortcut

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg conclude that the appellate rationale inadequately distinguishes limited co-tenant access from the officers' chosen vantage. A closed interior door, host-respected privacy and lowered blinds establish more than a naked wish that obviously public activity stay secret. An imperfect blind does not negate subjective privacy as a matter of law independently of where the observer stands.

The strongest objection is that actual co-users could readily see the same activity and defendants had no exclusive yard control. This prevents a categorical holding that the observed information necessarily remained protected. It does not establish that deliberate police viewing from this route equates to ordinary public observation. Ciraolo and Riley retain their actual public-vantage context, and Dunn first determined that the officers stood outside curtilage. A shared yard can have materially different access and domestic-use characteristics from an open field; neither shared use nor fencing alone resolves it.

The resulting remedy corrects the legal shortcut without inventing the missing curtilage or access findings. Dunn's proximity, enclosure, actual use and protection against observation inform the inquiry without becoming a mechanical checklist or a universal first-fence rule. The available lawful record should establish the actual route, access and domestic relationship as far as material. ### Warrantless entry and announcement

A lawful observation supplies probable cause on the described record, but not by itself permission to enter. The four affirming Justices find actual urgency in the “5-0” shout, the speaker's entry and closing of the door, the known violence of this identified suspect and the disposal risk of the observed cocaine-bagging operation. These are contemporaneous circumstances supporting danger and destruction concerns rather than a felony-drug generalization. The eventual weapon under a mattress adds no earlier justification. Attempted flight after entry cannot retroactively justify the initial force.

The strongest objection is the prior opportunity to seek a warrant and the plan to induce flight or obtain entry. Police had observed the suspects but did not seek a warrant before arranging front and rear teams; the plan is part of the actual sequence. The affirmance does not find a secret evasion purpose or adopt a rule that police can always create their own exigency. The particular warning occurred when officers were positioning and supplied a present risk before the contemplated front-door knock. The earlier opportunity for a warrant does not alone eliminate the later actual danger/destruction need under the supplied law. The hour-plus warrant estimate supplies context, not a universal warrant-delay exception. Mere seriousness of crime or reputation would not suffice alone.

The four remanding Justices accept that this combination could support a lawful exception if based on a lawful observation and route; they do not hold exigency impossible or require completed destruction. They do not finally sustain it while the observation premise remains unsettled. On remand, untainted probable cause, the actual development of urgency and any properly presented causal objection to police conduct must be addressed without converting an unreviewed factual finding into ultimate law. The informant tip and corroborated vehicle details are relevant; this Court does not find that they independently established all entry or warrant predicates.

Announcement has its own question. Under Wilson and Richards, particular facts must connect announcement or delay to danger, futility or threatened evidence loss, with more than a hunch but less than probable cause. The four affirming Justices find the warning plus identified risk sufficient for danger/destruction; they do not require a separate holding that a porch shout conclusively conveyed formal identity and authority to every occupant. Shouting “police” after forced entry cannot cure force that was unlawful when begun. Entry through an open apartment door does not erase the earlier battering-ram force at the common-building door. Neither a valid warrant nor justified announcement omission supplies warrantless-entry authority by itself.

### Suppression, warrant and consent

If an earlier intrusion was unlawful, the existence of a later warrant does not cure it automatically. Murray requires actual independence of the decision to seek the warrant and of issuance from tainted information. The complete affidavit and findings of independence are not established here. Warner's consent after the premises and occupants were secured requires its own supported voluntariness, scope and any applicable attenuation analysis; a rights explanation and signature alone do not conclusively answer taint. No retrospective consent exception is found for the prior window viewing.

Pinson and Myers preserve the separation between constitutional error and suppression. Properly presented, supported warrant, reliance, independent-source or inevitable-discovery grounds remain available. Leon protects neither knowing/reckless deception, abandonment of neutrality, wholly unreasonable reliance on a bare affidavit nor reliance on a facially deficient warrant; its inquiry includes obtaining and supplying officers and assumes lawful execution scope. No ground is silently revived after actual waiver or forfeiture, and no waiver or forfeiture is inferred from missing research documents. Evans's court-clerical setting supplies no general police-search exception. No later categorical announcement/exclusion rule is imported.

Fields's jury conviction and Crawley's conditional guilty plea remain distinct. Correcting a legal premise does not itself award Crawley Rule 11(a)(2) withdrawal; he must prevail on the reserved issue under the eventual lawful disposition. The remand position vacates the appellate suppression-based affirmance to permit that disposition and orders no automatic trial, acquittal, release, new sentence, evidentiary hearing or affidavit excision.
