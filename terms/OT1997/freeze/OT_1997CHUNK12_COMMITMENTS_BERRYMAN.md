## Components and common operative commitments

**A — Interlocutory immunity appeal: affirm its jurisdictional dismissal as presented.** The actual arguments described by the validated lower-court account require choosing the disputed medical records, Mukherjee affidavit, defendants' denials or later nonparty treatment over Berryman's sworn account. Johnson, Doe, Behrens, Smith and Camilo prohibit that factual-sufficiency review. The preliminary Clerk's decision against dismissal at that time did not finally establish jurisdiction. An immunity label does not make those evidentiary disputes separable questions of law.

This is not a categorical rule that factual disputes preclude every immunity appeal. A genuine, otherwise authorized legal issue accepting the proper plaintiff-favorable assumptions remains reviewable. If necessary assumptions are unstated, the appellate court may identify them from material already before the trial court without selecting truth, or seek clarification when they cannot responsibly be identified. No particular additional legal issue is invented here. The unquoted three-issue letter is not deemed absent, waived or legally empty because the original is unrecovered; the commitment rests on the affirmative judicial account of the arguments actually pursued. It supplies no independent merits rejection of a hypothetical, unidentified legal argument.

The first mandate did not adjudicate qualified immunity: it expressly permitted proper presentation on remand. Neither that mandate nor a numerical one-appeal limit bars the later motion or an otherwise proper legal appeal. Behrens continues to prohibit repetition of unchanged legal rulings and relitigation of actually resolved mandate matters. Neither limitation converts the first mandate into an immunity ruling it never made.

**B — Section 1912: vacate the double-cost and attorney-fee award.** The court of appeals dismissed the appeal; it did not affirm a judgment. Section 1912's specified trigger therefore did not authorize its award. The panel's findings of unnecessary protraction do not replace that statutory condition. This Court's affirmance of the jurisdictional dismissal does not retroactively supply authority for the circuit's original award. This holding does not decide the full measure of recoverable damages in a case where §1912 actually applies.

**C — Alternative sanctions authority and process: remand the vacated award for lawful reconsideration, without directing sanctions.** Rule 38 can support just damages, including appropriate appellate attorney fees, and single or double costs upon a finding that the appeal is frivolous, after a separately filed motion or notice from the court and a reasonable opportunity to respond. Each condition matters. A request embedded in an appellate brief is not the separately filed motion the 1994 amendment requires. A jurisdictional show-cause order is not automatically notice that sanctions are contemplated. A prospective hearing limited to computing an already imposed award does not by itself supply an opportunity to contest entitlement before imposition.

The reported account does not establish whether some other sufficient motion or court notice and opportunity already occurred. There is no finding that notice was absent. Vacatur rests independently on the identified statutory defect; on remand the circuit may consider its existing record and provide any required process before deciding whether to impose a new award under valid authority. It must exercise that discretion on the actual appeal, distinguish allowable immunity presentation from factual relitigation, and address the procedural sequence, including its preliminary decision to let the appeal proceed. That preliminary decision is neither a perpetual jurisdictional ruling nor automatic immunity from sanctions. The mere fact of jurisdictional dismissal is not a finding of frivolousness.

Inherent power is not automatically substituted for §1912. Any such fee award must independently satisfy its bad-faith and fair-process requirements and the governing limits of Roadway Express and Chambers. The Court neither finds those predicates established nor adopts a new rule displacing or enlarging inherent power. Section 1927 addresses counsel and does not itself validate this award against defendants; no new sanction against counsel is adjudged. Ordinary Rule 39 costs upon dismissal remain a separate question, not authority for double costs or attorney fees. No ordinary-cost amount or new Supreme Court costs award is decided. The derivative instruction to compute the vacated enhanced award cannot proceed as an already established entitlement.

**D — Claims, defendants and further proceedings: no underlying merits determination.** The Eighth Amendment medical-care claim and First Amendment retaliation claim remain distinct. The latter is not defeated simply because a challenged act could have been lawful without retaliatory purpose; it still requires its actual protected-conduct, personal-action, causation and injury predicates. Crawford-El preserves competent direct and circumstantial evidence, ordinary preponderance, and the actual Mt. Healthy same-decision defense without a heightened motive burden or automatic discovery entitlement. It does not decide this claim.

Rieger's alleged refusal and direction to seek VA care, Kukla's alleged statement concerning her friend Fox, and Holzheuer's different alleged conduct and contested progress entries remain defendant-specific. The reported absence of a direct retaliatory statement by Holzheuer supplies neither a categorical direct-evidence requirement nor a finding that her liability is established. One defendant's statement is not attributed to another. Alleged forgery is not a finding of forgery; later nonparty care does not settle earlier conduct by these defendants. The Court decides no serious medical need, deliberate indifference, protected litigation, actual retaliation, ultimate immunity, damages or liability. No trial, discovery, hearing or other special merits procedure is commanded. Ordinary further proceedings and independent defenses remain available under their own rules. The exact treatment dates are unresolved; later Farmer, Crawford-El or Camilo law cannot supply earlier notice. No new constitutional merits holding is necessary to the present threshold disposition.

## Material alternative-path test

**Appeal — identify a legal issue and remand versus affirm the factual-only dismissal.** Behrens, Smith and Camilo make the first route lawful when an actual separable legal issue or an indispensable unstated premise is identified. Its strongest objection to affirmance is that the original three-issue letter is not reproduced. The validated opinion nevertheless affirmatively describes the grounds actually pursued and their reliance on disputed evidence; it does not merely report that some facts were disputed. No supplied legal contention survives while accepting the proper assumptions. The eight commitments therefore sustain the dismissal at that actual scope. They do not infer waiver or reject an unknown legal contention. If the original papers later establish a distinct preserved legal argument accepting those assumptions, the affected jurisdictional commitment requires fresh assessment, not a declaration that the argument was already rejected here. A remand solely to investigate whether an unidentified issue might exist is not necessary to decide the reported appeal.

**Sanctions — affirm on an alternative source versus vacate and remand.** The strongest case for affirmance is the reported repetition of factual disputes and unnecessary protraction, possibly sanctionable under Rule 38. The strongest objection is independent: the court used §1912 without an affirmance, and the supplied account does not establish all prerequisites or a distinct lawful exercise under another source. The eight commitments do not cure that error by assuming either compliance or noncompliance. Vacatur with bounded reconsideration resolves the known legal defect and allows the circuit to use its actual record and legally required process. Complete prohibition of any future award goes too far: jurisdictional dismissal does not immunize a genuinely frivolous appeal from properly imposed appellate sanctions. Conversely, remand is permission to reconsider, not an instruction to find frivolousness, sanction anyone or reimpose the same amount.

**Constitutional merits — decide immunity now versus stop at jurisdiction.** No identified separable medical-care or retaliation legal issue requires resolution. The second route controls without treating the three defendants alike, choosing factual truth, importing later notice, or turning a retained district-court denial into a final constitutional ruling. Earlier Estelle/Wilson and conduct-date access-to-courts research would become necessary only if a specific reachable merits issue were established; the unknown exact dates are not supplied by assumption to obtain a merits result.

## Exact statutory and rule anchors

The following operative sentences are mechanically copied from the frozen neutral packet's official-source extraction, not retyped or modernized. Its historical notes establish the 1948 §1912 text and Rule 38 amendment effective December 1, 1994. No December 1998 or later rule is imported.

**28 U.S.C. §1912:**

Where a judgment is affirmed by the Supreme Court or a court of appeals, the court in its discretion may adjudge to the prevailing party just damages for his delay, and single or double costs.

**Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 38:**

If a court of appeals determines that an appeal is frivolous, it may, after a separately filed motion or notice from the court and reasonable opportunity to respond, award just damages and single or double costs to the appellee.
