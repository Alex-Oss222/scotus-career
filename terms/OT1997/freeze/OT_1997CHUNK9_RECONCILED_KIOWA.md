## Historical comparison and changed-premise finding

Historically Kennedy's opinion carried six votes: Rehnquist, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Stevens dissented with Thomas and Ginsburg. The majority preserved direct contract immunity for both governmental/commercial activity and on/off-reservation contracting absent congressional authorization or waiver. The dissent would not extend immunity into purely off-reservation commercial conduct unrelated to tribal land or sovereign functions; it did not propose abolishing received tribal immunity wholesale.

The independent model correctly identified substantive regulation and amenability to suit as distinct. Its provisional Thomas and Ginsburg rows nevertheless treated the lack of a comparably direct earlier personal statement about tribal contract immunity as supporting the status-based immunity rule. Reconciliation now supplies their directly pertinent historical joins, which address the same doctrinal fork. Their concurrence in ordinary off-country taxation is not converted into a jurisdiction rule; instead, the historical dissent demonstrates their actual resolution of that separate rulemaking question.

The actual Chickasaw Nation holding preserves ordinary taxation of specified off-country resident wages and distinguishes territorial tax incidence; it creates no immunity exception and no new mandate to expand immunity. Venetie separates political recognition from Indian-country status and does not decide suit immunity. Crow rejects automatic restitution to a different claimant and preserves independently grounded remedies; it likewise supplies no direct contract-immunity rule. The revised Seminole/Coeur d'Alene officer holdings concern defendants and remedies materially different from this direct tribal money action. No altered holding, specific prior simulated commitment, new statutory authorization or factual premise explains why Thomas or Ginsburg would reject the off-reservation limitation they historically joined. Their provisional pro-immunity positions therefore cannot be retained merely from general restraint or remedy-source reasoning.

## Proposition, remedial and join constraints

Five Associates—O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer—directly support immunity for this recognized Tribe sued on a contract without unequivocal waiver or congressional authorization, regardless of commercial/governmental character or either asserted execution location. Stevens, Thomas and Ginsburg reject the extension to genuinely off-reservation commercial conduct. These are eight commitments, not a final nine-member tally. No aggregation is needed for the five direct immunity joins.

The five require reversal of the judgment rejecting tribal immunity and termination of the presented direct money action consistently with immunity. A bounded remand may implement that disposition; it must not imply that a location determination remains necessary under the controlling categorical contract rule. The debt's substantive merits, the authority of a signatory, a different defendant's liability, another forum, a secured asset, an execution measure and every tort claim remain undecided. The judgment does not extinguish contractual obligations or hold that ordinary state law is inapplicable.

The three contrary commitments support a limited off-territory commercial exception and would return the case for application of that rule to the actual supported transaction and existing lower-court findings. The note recites Carnegie; the creditor asserts Oklahoma City execution/delivery and payment. Neither version becomes a new finding. The historical dissent argues from the off-reservation commercial premise but does not separately state a detailed remand mandate; it must not be cited as though it expressly ordered new geographic factfinding. The bounded alternative here reflects the frozen packet's unresolved material premise. It is **not** a finding that the lower courts lacked evidence or that missing research requires litigants to create a record. Existing valid findings may resolve the predicate; only if the actual record leaves a legally material question open would further ordinary proceedings be needed. None of these three unconditionally affirms a money judgment on an invented location finding.

For the three, Potawatomi's trust-land business holding and Puyallup's protection of the tribal defendant in its actual setting remain intact. They reject the broader rule by treating outside commerce as an unsettled extension, not by claiming Mescalero silently overruled immunity, that all commerce waives immunity, or that Congress has abrogated it. Their substantive grounds also distinguish state-court comity from the Court's creation of a mandatory federal exemption; they do not make every existing tribal immunity question optional state policy.

All eight reject waiver inferred merely from signing a note, defaulting or undertaking payment, especially where the note preserves sovereign rights. No officer, separate corporation, guarantor, in-rem remedy or alternative adequate forum is invented. The location dispute is immaterial for the five-member rule and potentially material only to the contrary rule.
