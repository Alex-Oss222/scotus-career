## Bragdon: corrected review scope

The federal disability issue remains within the reported grant. Of the three reproduced direct-threat questions, only petition Question 3 was granted: whether courts should defer to a private provider's professional judgment about performing invasive procedures on an infectious patient in the office, so long as that judgment is reasonable in light of then-current medical knowledge. Question 4 separately proposed the proper standard of judicial review. Question 5 separately asked whether this petitioner raised a genuine factual issue for trial. Those separate questions were not granted. Their appearance in the petition does not make them independently granted issues.

This correction neither answers the provider-deference question nor decides dental-treatment risk. The statutory significant-risk requirement, the inability to eliminate that risk through the specified modifications or aids, and the regulation's individualized assessment and objective evidentiary basis remain distinct from the amount of judicial deference, the litigation-stage proof requirements, and the ultimate sufficiency of the whole record. A reasonable-professional-judgment formulation in the granted question is a proposed legal standard to be evaluated; it is not an established finding that this provider's judgment was reasonable.

The published account states that the briefing and argument did not concentrate on whole-record sufficiency in light of every summary-judgment submission. That is a judicial characterization of their focus. It establishes neither the absence of an exhibit from the Court's file nor waiver, default, lack of proof, or the ultimate absence or presence of a triable issue. The complete petition, original briefs, appendix, and expert materials were not recovered for this refresh.

## Clinton: direct review and the distinct certiorari channel

The complaints invoked both section 692 and ordinary federal-question and declaratory-relief grounds under 28 U.S.C. sections 1331 and 2201. The Government filed both a direct Supreme Court appeal and a protective notice of appeal in the D.C. Circuit. The reported request to eliminate any possibility that the district-court decision might escape review is an attributed litigation request. It does not establish a separate certiorari petition, a qualifying application in the existing papers, or a grant. No circuit judgment is supplied.

**Special direct review.** Section 692(a)(1) permits a Member of Congress or an individual adversely affected by the specified part to bring a constitutional action in the District Court for the District of Columbia. Paragraph (2) requires prompt delivery of the complaint to the Secretary of the Senate and the Clerk of the House and gives each House intervention rights; paragraph (3) preserves the House's intervention right without an authorizing resolution. Section 692(b) permits direct appeal from an order issued pursuant to an action brought under paragraph (a)(1), with a notice of appeal within ten calendar days and a jurisdictional statement within thirty calendar days after entry. A single Justice may not stay such an order. Section 692(c) requires the two courts to expedite a matter brought under subsection (a); expedition does not itself establish the statutory plaintiff qualification, compliance, appellate jurisdiction, or Article III standing. The complete section remains in the original neutral freeze. Its meaning and application are open issues, not findings supplied by this refresh.

**Certiorari before circuit judgment.** Section 1254(1) authorizes review of a case in a court of appeals by a writ of certiorari granted upon a party's petition before or after judgment. Section 2101(e) permits a before-judgment application at any time before the court of appeals renders judgment. Rule 11 permits a grant only upon a showing of such imperative public importance that deviation from normal appellate practice and immediate Supreme Court determination are justified. The protective circuit notice is therefore material to a potentially distinct channel; it does not itself satisfy that channel's prerequisites. Neither ordinary district-court jurisdiction nor a wish for expedition supplies the required Supreme Court action.

Section 1254(2)'s certified-question mechanism is distinct. No certification is established here. Section 2103 was repealed in 1988, subject to a transition preserving the specified then-pending cases and review of judgments entered before the effective date. The supplied 1998 litigation does not fall within that preserved class. Section 2103 cannot supply present conversion authority. Rule 18.12 permits summary appellate disposition, noting probable jurisdiction, or postponing consideration of jurisdiction until the merits hearing, and imposes the stated briefing, argument, and record-transmission conditions. It supplies no automatic conversion of an appeal into certiorari and does not conclusively determine appellate jurisdiction merely because a hearing occurs.

**Legally open paths.** The model must separately evaluate the special direct-review route and, if material, the possible before-judgment route arising from the protective circuit proceeding. It may select a supported direct-review construction; it may consider an express certiorari-before-judgment grant through the proper channel if the necessary application and Rule 11 conditions are established; or it may select an appropriate threshold disposition or further procedural step if the Court cannot yet reach the merits. A grant is an actual discretionary Court choice, not a consequence already accomplished by the source extraction or the existence of a circuit notice. If treating existing Supreme Court papers as the required application is proposed, the model must identify operative, date-eligible authority and a sufficient basis in the actual filings or supported published recitations; neither the repealed section 2103 nor Rule 18.12 supplies that missing analysis. This refresh does not establish that a new separate petition is indispensable, or that the existing papers are sufficient.

Each consolidated action and cancellation requires a supportable route and Article III standing; the availability of review for one does not automatically establish the other. If merits are reached, the constitutional alternatives and remedial limits already frozen remain open. If a threshold ground controls, it must carry its own procedural consequence without an unreached merits holding. A further dated Court action, if needed and selected, must be recorded through the ordinary stages rather than silently inserted as recovered history.

The entering-law snapshot remains unchanged. ## Additional neutral rule note: question scope

Source: Supreme Court Rules adopted July 26, 1995, effective October 2, 1995; official `https://www.supremecourt.gov/ctrules/rules/Rules_1995.pdf`, saved as `research/chunk12_control/Rules_1995.pdf` and `.txt`. These operative excerpts were checked in the saved full rule text. They add no current-case action.

Rule 14.1(a), operative scope sentences:

> The statement of any question presented is deemed to comprise every subsidiary question fairly included therein. Only the questions set out in the petition, or fairly included therein, will be considered by the Court.

Rule 24.1(a), operative merits-brief and optional plain-error sentences:

> The phrasing of the questions presented need not be identical with that in the petition for a writ of certiorari or the jurisdictional statement, but the brief may not raise additional questions or change the substance of the questions already presented in those documents. At its option, however, the Court may consider a plain error not among the questions presented but evident from the record and otherwise within its jurisdiction to decide.

The quotations join printed line-break hyphenation without altering words. Rule 48 makes the 1995 Rules effective October 2, 1995 and applicable to proceedings thereafter, except when the Court finds their application to a pending matter infeasible or unjust, in which event the former procedure applies. No such exception is established here.

### 28 U.S.C. 1254 (1997)

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1997-title28/html/USCODE-1997-title28-partIV-chap81.htm ; local `USCODE-1997-title28-chap81.html` and `.txt`.

```text
§1254. Courts of appeals; certiorari; certified questions






Cases in the courts of appeals may be reviewed by the Supreme Court by the following methods:


(1) By writ of certiorari granted upon the petition of any party to any civil or criminal case, before or after rendition of judgment or decree;


(2) By certification at any time by a court of appeals of any question of law in any civil or criminal case as to which instructions are desired, and upon such certification the Supreme Court may give binding instructions or require the entire record to be sent up for decision of the entire matter in controversy.
```

### Supreme Court Rule 11 (1995 rules)

Source: https://www.supremecourt.gov/ctrules/rules/Rules_1995.pdf ; local `Rules_1995.pdf` and `Rules_1995.txt`. The official PDF title page supplies adoption/effective dates.

```text
Rule 11. Certiorari to a United States Court of Appeals 
Before Judgment 
A petition for a writ of certiorari to review a case pending 
in a United States court of appeals, before judgment is en-
tered in that court, will be granted only upon a showing that 
the case is of such imperative public importance as to justify 
deviation from normal appellate practice and to require im-
mediate determination in this Court. See 28 U. S. C. 
§2101(e).
```
