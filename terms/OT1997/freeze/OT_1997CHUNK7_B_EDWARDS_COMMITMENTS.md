## Result and complete rule

All eight Associates would affirm the Seventh Circuit on the narrow claim presented. The alternative cocaine-or-cocaine-base instruction and general guilty verdict do not themselves require the sentencing judge to disregard related crack conduct when selecting ordinary Guidelines punishment within the independently authorized cocaine-conspiracy statutory limits. Guidelines sections 1B1.3 and 2D1.1 authorize independent, supported findings of drug kind and quantity within their relevant-conduct scope. The verdict does not establish that the jury rejected crack conduct, and there was no separate crack acquittal.

The permission is confined to reliable relevant conduct and lawful conviction-based punishment. It authorizes no statutory-maximum increase based on a fact omitted from an actual offense element, no removal of an element from the jury, no disguised separate punishment, no extraordinary sentence magnification rule and no disregard of due process, the applicable Guidelines, evidentiary requirements or Rule 32. The supplied record reports that every sentence fits the cocaine-only limits given the attributed cocaine quantities and that no contention placed the crack transactions outside the same course of conduct or common scheme. Those predicates permit disposition of this narrow alternative-verdict objection without declaring every drug quantity a sentencing factor.

Witte supplies the distinction between ordinary relevant-conduct selection and independent punishment. Watts confirms bounded consideration even after a general acquittal and therefore defeats the categorical claim presented by this less specific verdict, without making every below-maximum sentence constitutional. Gaudin protects actual elements. Almendarez-Torres's current statutory construction prevents treating a punishment ceiling as mere Guidelines selection and is distinguished because these sentences do not exceed the reported cocaine-only ceiling. Griffin supplies no jury finding that crack was proved and does not cure an element-diluting instruction. No such broad instructional issue is decided here.

## Alternative-path assessment

**Require cocaine-only Guidelines treatment:** The strongest argument is that an alternative verdict does not prove the jury chose crack, and a higher crack-based punishment could evade the jury's element role. That objection would matter if the sentence exceeded conviction-authorized punishment, an actual element had been withdrawn, a special factual rejection were shown or a preserved factual/Guidelines error defeated the calculation. The supplied cocaine-only-ceiling recitation, absence of a special rejection and interrelated-conduct predicate defeat this categorical argument. The Court does not pretend that the general verdict itself found crack.

**Remand for a new ceiling or conduct finding:** A supported ceiling excess or preserved factual challenge could require limited correction. The existing source specifically reports sentences within the cocaine-only statutory limits, and the asserted effect of a cocaine-only assumption on factual findings was not raised at sentencing. The source limitation is not affirmative proof of a defect. There is no reason to order a new sentencing inquiry merely because individual terms have not been copied into this packet.

**Forfeiture:** The alternative-instruction sentencing argument was first presented on appeal, which is failure to assert, not an established intentional waiver. Olano and Johnson preserve distinct error, plainness, substantial-rights and discretionary-correction conditions where an actual forfeited error exists. The selected ground identifies no error in the categorical treatment challenged, so no hypothetical plain-error finding or broad harmless-error rule is needed. Rogers supplies no omitted-element harmless-error rule.
