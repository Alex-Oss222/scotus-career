## Common operative rule and explanation

Article III, the Sixth Amendment and Rule 18 locate trial where the charged offense was committed. Anderson requires examination of the offense's nature and its constituting acts. Section 3237(a) permits prosecution where an offense actually begins, continues or is completed; its transportation/mail paragraph does not turn every commerce-linked financial transaction into transportation through the district where funds arose.

Count II charges a financial transaction under §1956(a)(1)(B)(ii), the reporting-avoidance branch. Count III charges a monetary transaction in criminally derived property exceeding $10,000 under §1957. The January 1991 Florida deposit and withdrawals are the transactions charged. Missouri supplied the antecedent drug proceeds, not a laundering act by Cabrales alleged in these counts. The Government does not allege that she transported the funds from Missouri to Florida or performed any constituting laundering act in Missouri.

The strongest contrary construction treats the predicate crime as the laundering offense's beginning because illicit origin is indispensable. It fails here because an indispensable attribute of the transaction's object need not be prohibited conduct by this defendant at the place where that attribute arose. All statutory elements retain their force; the ruling does not excise knowledge or unlawful source. Neither a functional description of a laundering scheme nor the practical value of Missouri evidence adds charged acts.

Actual Thomas illustrates a different application: an authorized interstate delivery completes a charged transportation at its destination. Here all charged transactions began, continued and ended in Florida. Genuine acquisition and transportation by a launderer, participation in the predicate offense, aiding-and-abetting liability or a differently charged conspiracy remain distinct possibilities requiring their own allegations and law. No universal prohibition against multistate laundering venue is adopted.

## Count I and exact remedy

The June 12, 1997 amendment at **115 F.3d 621** supersedes the original footnote's Missouri-overt-act explanation. The operative point is only that the district court did not dismiss Count I and that Count I is outside this appeal. Neither the original superseded footnote nor the historical Court's discussion of a possible conspiracy prosecution establishes a Missouri overt act, valid conspiracy venue, agreement, guilt or liability in the separate pending prosecution.

Accordingly, affirm dismissal of **Counts II and III only** for venue. The ruling is not an acquittal, immunity from a properly venued prosecution, a decision on another indictment, or an instruction to prosecute elsewhere. No conspiracy or aiding-and-abetting allegation is inserted into these substantive counts to cure their venue. No sentencing consequence is determined. The exact result requires no new hearing or factual resolution of guilt.
