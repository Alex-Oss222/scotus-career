# Waste Management — scoped public-ground reconciliation audit

**Event and cutoff:** May 27, 1998.
**Scope:** The public formulation in [public legal-ground handoff](OT_1997CHUNK9_WASTE_PUBLIC_GROUND.md); the listed frozen Waste neutral packet and Waste source extracts; the Camps supplement; refreshed commitments, remedy clarification, and final reconciliation; Engine §§7–9 and 14. No private position, approved brief, decision record, assignment material, or unrelated case substance was consulted. This is a compatibility judgment, not an account of draft exchanges.

**Result:** O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer may each join all four proposed holdings and their stated explanations. Their existing votes to vacate both reviewed injunction determinations and remand the Commerce Clause components do not change. The public-facility rule and the particular diversion-cost limitation are supported legal developments within the reserved categories, not rules already decided by Carbone, Tracy, Camps, Oregon Waste, or Fulton. The audit does not derive a join from the other three Justices' agreement.

## Operative propositions and objections

**Actual public service.** Read as a whole, Holding 1 requires the actual arrangement to be public: ownership and control, financing and risk, responsibility for the service, disposition of revenues, and absence of concealed private favoritism. These considerations have concrete work: they distinguish public provision from a preferred private enterprise given public title or a municipal purpose. They are not an unexplained point system, a requirement that every public service reproduce Tracy's utility obligations, or a presumption that Metro qualifies. NTTC's actual operation and the unestablished contractual economics remain material. The distinction can therefore supply the material service difference demanded by the C commitments; it does not erase that requirement through title alone.

The strongest objection remains that sovereign routing coerces independent purchases. Holding 3 squarely accepts that fact and denies the resulting claim of government-purchase immunity. Holding 1 instead answers the reserved discrimination question: after genuine public provision is established, equal treatment of private processors does not reserve commerce for a favored local private competitor. The actual public character, rather than equal exclusion of private competitors alone, supplies the additional premise. That distinction is a supported inference from the sources below, not a fictional finding of procurement or a claim that Carbone already decided the question. Pike and scrutiny of any established discrimination remain available under Holding 4.

**Separate diversion charge.** The proposed nexus concerns the selective charge triggered by disposal outside the public system. It does not establish a universal prohibition on neutral taxes or ordinary financing of public services. Its actual incidence and amount must connect to diversion-related costs; lost receipts alone do not establish that connection. The text supplies neither an incremental-cost-only formula nor a finding that public fixed costs can never be relevant. It also supplies no numerical approximation, presumed stranded cost, or successful defense for this enactment.

This is a new, bounded safeguard against using a cost label to penalize competing disposal. Oregon Waste's preservation of materially different proven cost justifications and its independent nondiscrimination requirement support examining the nexus; Fulton supplies only the separate cumulative compensatory-tax requirements. The existing compensation defense still fails for want of an identified matching intrastate burden. An origin-neutral cost relationship is necessary under the proposed diversion rule, not sufficient to establish ultimate validity: the applicable remaining scrutiny and the enacted incidence still govern. No failed compensation element is excused or silently retried through an assumed cost finding.

These are full proposition-level joins, not merely agreement with remand. No Justice is understood to adopt an invented operator arrangement, a presumed public-service predicate, blanket municipal immunity, or an automatic fee validation. The proposed text itself withholds each of those propositions.

## Remaining scope, dissent, and historical anchor

The residential written-authorization exception, conditional nonresidential allocations and reporting/notice machinery, fee incidence and exemptions, reported objectives and alternatives, and absence of an automatic evidentiary hearing remain operative. The four Justices retain separate reconsideration of routing and fee, ordinary component-specific relief, and exclusion of the passenger-car/pickup takings and ripeness questions. No new procedural burden, monetary remedy, or replacement program follows.

Stevens and Kennedy retain D: affirm both reviewed determinations because compulsory private transactions remain comparable and the supported public facts do not warrant the proposed distinction on this record. Their objection is acknowledged, not converted into a join. Scalia and Thomas retain R: reverse and end the reviewed negative-Commerce grounds; their comparative observations supply no vote for renewed Commerce Clause adjudication. Their source-supported Camps repudiation remains a dissent from the retained framework.

There is no same-matter historical Supreme Court merits disposition or coalition. The Sixth Circuit's November 5, 1997 judgment is the decision under review; it cannot serve as a historical vote anchor for any Associate. The relevant individual anchors are the supplied prior public positions assessed above. The full formulation resolves the category those decisions reserved, preserves the four C dispositions, and answers their recorded public-label, procurement, and compensation objections. No historical-departure line is warranted. No later waste decision, later judicial position, hidden circulation sequence, or authorship fact supports this conclusion.
