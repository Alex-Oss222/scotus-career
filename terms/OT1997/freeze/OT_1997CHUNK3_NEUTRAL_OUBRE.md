### Statutory requirements and burden

Section 626(f)(1) begins: “An individual may not waive any right or claim under this chapter unless the waiver is knowing and voluntary.” Except for paragraph (2)'s specified settlement setting, a waiver “may not be considered knowing and voluntary unless at a minimum” the listed conditions are met. The full text remains incorporated; its operative components are:

| Provision | Actual statutory requirement and relevance |
|---|---|
| (1)(A) | An individual/employer agreement written in a manner calculated to be understood by the individual or average eligible participant. Neither signature alone nor a general finding of sophistication replaces it. |
| (1)(B) | Specific reference to rights or claims arising under the ADEA. A conceded defect here. |
| (1)(C) | No waiver of rights or claims arising after execution. No additional defect or later agreement is inferred. |
| (1)(D) | Consideration additional to anything of value already owed. The source establishes the payment amount, not a finding resolving every consideration question. |
| (1)(E) | Written advice to consult an attorney before execution. Actual consultation is a separate reported fact. |
| (1)(F) | At least twenty-one days to consider an individual agreement; forty-five days for the stated group/program setting. Fourteen days were offered here and noncompliance is conceded. |
| (1)(G) | At least seven days after execution to revoke, with no effectiveness or enforceability before that period expires. The agreement lacked that opportunity. Mere passage of seven days is not a reported contractual revocation provision. |
| (1)(H) | For a covered group/program waiver, specified written class/unit, eligibility, time-limit, job-title and age disclosures at the start of the consideration period. No group/program facts are invented. |
| (2) | A settlement of an already filed EEOC charge or court action alleging covered discrimination must satisfy (1)(A)–(E) and provide a reasonable consideration period. The original precharge severance agreement is not shown to be such a settlement, nor is there a reported later settlement meeting it. |
| (3) | The party asserting waiver validity bears the burden in a competent court of proving a knowing and voluntary waiver pursuant to (1) or (2). This is an express statutory burden and is not replaced by a common-law presumption borrowed from another setting. |
| (4) | No agreement may affect EEOC enforcement responsibilities, and no waiver may justify interference with an employee's protected charge filing or agency participation. This institutional protection is distinct from private civil recovery. |

### Exact current Standards and prior holdings

**After-acquired misconduct and age-discrimination remedies — Present operation effective January 23, 1995, *McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co*.** O'Connor's unanimous Court opinion holds that misconduct unknown to the employer at discharge does not erase an ADEA violation or categorically eliminate all remedies. The employee retains the liability burden. To limit relief the employer must prove conduct so serious that it would in fact have discharged the employee for that reason alone. Once established, reinstatement and front pay are ordinarily inappropriate and backpay's starting measure ends at actual discovery, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting legitimate interests. This is a specific liability/remedy distinction, not a severance-payment accounting holding. The proof requirement and its scope cannot be transferred mechanically to this different defense.

**Advance plea-statement waivers for impeachment — Present operation effective January 18, 1995, *United States v. Mezzanatto*.** Thomas's Court opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer, enforces the specified knowing and voluntary advance waiver for impeachment of the defendant's own inconsistent testimony absent an affirmative indication of unknowing or involuntary acceptance. Its exact scope is limited to then-Rules 410 and 11(e)(6), the actual agreement and the specified use. The opinion preserves right-specific constraints, individual voluntariness review and the possibility of nonwaivable statutory protections. It distinguishes *Brooklyn Savings Bank v. O'Neil*'s public statutory protection rather than overruling it. It decides no universal rule permitting waiver of every protection and supplies no replacement for §626(f)(3)'s express burden.

## Strongest supported alternatives

These are analytical positions developed under Engine §14(4), not claims that the unrecovered briefs contain particular quotations, concessions or preservation facts.

**Statutory bar to enforcing this defective waiver.** The words “may not waive,” “unless at a minimum,” and the express validity burden support treating the stated requirements as necessary for a waiver that extinguishes the ADEA claim. Retention cannot cure the agreement's missing ADEA reference or retroactively create the required preexecution consideration period and postexecution revocation opportunity. Calling the result ratification does not necessarily place it outside a statute controlling when a right may be waived. Treating retention alone as a new waiver can also evade the same requirements and lacks a reported new agreement. A tender-back condition preventing the action until money is returned may give operative force to the same defective waiver. On this reading the Court can reverse the release-based summary judgment and remand without finding age discrimination or resolving every contract consequence.

The strongest contrary argument is that the text regulates the original waiver's formation while leaving ordinary later ratification and rescission principles in place. The statute does not expressly use the words “ratification,” “void,” “voidable,” or “tender back.” The retained benefits and actual attorney consultation distinguish a claim of uninformed execution from Entergy's asserted later informed election. The statutory reading must explain why a purported later election is itself within the prohibition, rather than assume all federal statutory waivers disable general contract law. *Mezzanatto* supports attention to lawful consent but expressly remains right-specific and does not overcome explicit minimum conditions by itself.

**Later ratification or tender-back path.** Entergy's strongest version accepts that the original instrument failed the OWBPA and argues that later informed retention is a distinct affirmance of a voidable transaction. General contract doctrines ordinarily resist keeping an exchange's benefit while repudiating its burden; a timely tender could restore the parties' positions without adjudicating age discrimination. On that theory the Court could affirm only if the governing statute permits the asserted ratification or rescission condition and the reported record supplies its actual predicates.

Its central objections are the breadth of “may not waive,” the detailed minimum safeguards and statutory burden, and the absence of a compliant later waiver. Treating nonreturn as sufficient after several months may extinguish the very claim the defective instrument could not waive while providing none of the missing statutory protections. The Court cannot deem the entire statutory design satisfied merely because counsel was consulted or money remained unreturned. Nor can it resolve statutory meaning by finding inability to repay: that fact is unknown. A valid accounting remedy, if one exists, does not establish the separate legal premise needed to bar suit. The pathway must therefore confront the text directly, not relabel a prohibited waiver as ratification.

**Equitable estoppel.** Entergy also invokes estoppel from retaining benefits while rejecting burdens. Retention is established; separate misleading representation, reasonable detrimental reliance, or other elements of a particular estoppel rule are not independently established by the sanitized record. If the proposed estoppel merely enforces the same invalid waiver because consideration was retained, the statutory objection remains. If a materially distinct equitable claim depends on additional facts, this packet does not fabricate them or adjudicate that claim. No categorical rule on every form of employee misconduct is warranted by this record.

**Preserve lawful accounting without adjudicating it.** A ruling permitting the ADEA action need not declare a right to keep every dollar in every circumstance. The employer may seek any restitution, recoupment, setoff or other recovery that applicable law and the actual agreement permit, subject to defenses and the federal statutory requirements. Conversely, preserving that possibility does not establish an award, automatic deduction of $6,258, a new debt, a payment deadline, forfeiture of other claims, or a universal accounting remedy. The contract may cover other rights; its full allocation and survival provisions are not reproduced. Any lawful remedy must be distinguished from conditioning access to the ADEA action on prior tender. *McKennon* supports keeping liability and relief analytically separate while supplying no answer to these contract questions.

## Judgment and remand limits

The pertinent judgment components are the ADEA release/ratification bar and the ordinary further proceedings that follow its retention or removal. Reversal or vacatur on the release issue reopens the ADEA action to the extent controlled by that ground; it does not establish constructive discharge, age causation, willfulness, damages, fees or reinstatement. Entergy retains lawfully available defenses unrelated to the rejected ground. Oubre retains the ordinary burden to establish liability. The EEOC rejection does not become a civil judgment against her.

The sources do not establish every state-law claim's disposition or the complete agreement's non-ADEA effect. A mandate must preserve those separately rather than invalidate the entire agreement or restore every claim by inference. No restitution judgment, mandatory tender, universal repayment immunity, automatic trial, evidentiary hearing, additional contract or new filing is supplied. An affirmance on ratification would likewise resolve the defense, not the truth of discrimination allegations.
