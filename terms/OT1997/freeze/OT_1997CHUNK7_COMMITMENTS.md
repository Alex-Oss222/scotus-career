## Component commitments

### P1. Governing facial/access framework

Casey's complete six-step framework, reaffirmed in Fargo, governs previability regulation. Neither an independent numerical large-fraction threshold nor Salerno's no-valid-applications bar governs this challenge. The court must identify each actual duty, authoritative construction, exception and sanction; examine function and objectively supported purpose; identify concrete attributable interference and a stable affected population; apply the independent baseline/purpose/obstacle prohibitions, then any remaining severe-interference scrutiny and meaningful lesser-burden justification/fit review; protect supported clinical judgment and truthful communication; and match relief to the established violation under state severability law. A failed obstacle showing does not end review of remaining meaningful interference. No new study, professional veto, numerical cutoff, universally least restrictive policy or automatic invalidity follows merely because a law regulates abortion. The detailed burdens and qualifications in the entering-law six-step rule remain unchanged.

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join this proposition directly, based on their Fargo access joins and, for Stevens, O'Connor and Souter, their complete-framework Casey joins. Kennedy recognizes it as the governing rule for lower courts but retains his published disagreement with general residual lesser-burden substantiation; he does not join this rationale. Scalia and Thomas retain their Fargo/Casey constitutional objection and do not join it. Those separate objections do not amend current law.

### P2. Actual wording, notice and fault

Do not sustain a categorical facial-vagueness judgment simply because the named D&X definition can reach conduct occurring during some procedures called D&E. The enacted category is purposeful insertion of a suction device into the fetal skull to remove the brain, excluding suction curettage and suction aspiration; the prohibition concerns knowingly performing or attempting that defined act. A disagreement between the statute's act-based category and a medical label does not itself make the act unintelligible. The testimony that physicians sometimes purposefully seek skull suction during D&E is material to the ban's substantive reach and burden; it is not transformed into accidental contact or into proof that every D&E falls within the prohibition.

Likewise the postviability necessity/emergency terms use specified outcomes, good faith and reasonable medical judgment. Fargo establishes that professional forecast criteria can supply notice without certainty or an express fault term directed to every subsidiary judgment. Colautti requires separate attention to the actual trigger and fault concerning error; X-Citement Video supplies no universal knowledge-of-illegality rule. Reasonable later disagreement alone does not establish every element of an offense, but no general immunity, bad-faith-only safe harbor or universal scienter cure follows. Criminal and civil liability must be examined separately under their actual words. Vacate the generalized notice/scienter ground rather than affirm all concrete future enforcement.

All eight Associates support this bounded rejection of the categorical rationale. None commits to a final ruling on a concrete prosecution, civil damages claim, disputed element-specific Ohio culpability rule or materially different authoritative state construction. No Justice treats absence of express culpability alone as sufficient to invalidate the entire Act.

### P3. Necessary physical and mental health after viability

Casey's retained life-and-health protection requires a usable exception for abortion necessary to avert severe, nontemporary, potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury established through competent individualized medical judgment. A State may prohibit postviability abortion, but cannot categorically exclude that necessary care or require the patient to await catastrophe. This holding concerns the presented serious clinical injury; it does not declare every preference, distress or transient emotional reaction sufficient, dispense with competent medical support, or constitutionalize every physician's asserted preference for a method.

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join this necessary-care proposition. Casey's protected baseline and Doe's individualized physical-and-mental-health principle supply it; Kennedy's narrower Fargo writing expressly retained meaningful necessary-care safeguards. The intelligibility of an emergency term is different from its substantive adequacy. Pennsylvania's emergency-delay construction does not automatically validate Ohio's broader postviability prohibition, and Pennsylvania's separate harmful-disclosure defense is not an Ohio emergency exception.

Affirm protection insofar as the judgment prevents enforcing the Ohio postviability regime to exclude the described necessary mental-health care. A protective construction remains permissible only if fairly supported by the actual statutory words and governing state law. Do not declare that a federal court may insert an unlimited mental-health clause or that no limiting construction is possible. The lower court must identify whether §§2919.17(A), 2919.16(F)/(J) and any dependent duty can operate under a lawful construction; otherwise the relevant restriction remains unenforceable to the extent of the established violation. Scalia and Thomas reject P3 as a constitutional requirement and would reverse the injunction on their continuing reasonable-regulation approach, without adopting a false construction or inventing medical facts.

### P4. Method restriction requires its own substantive application

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the conclusive whole-Act merits disposition insofar as it substitutes categorical vagueness or the wrong facial formula for application of Casey to the actual method prohibition, and remand that component. The supplied findings that purposeful skull suction occurs during some D&E support a real scope and clinical-interference issue. They do not alone establish the magnitude, affected applications, practical alternative care or final scope of relief under all six steps. The needed inquiry is identified by those legal predicates, not by the operator's lack of original transcript files.

The method prohibition operates before and after viability, but those applications are not legally interchangeable. Its criminal defense requires a prima facie showing that all available alternatives pose greater health risk, followed by the prosecutor's burden to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that at least one available alternative does not. The civil defense is separate and does not carry that criminal burden shift. The lower court must examine whether these actual protections permit necessary care in practice and whether the demonstrated added interference is justified at the applicable level. Existing safe alternatives matter only insofar as usable for the patient and clinical circumstances; lack of proof that D&X is universally superior does not answer whether prohibited conduct is necessary in a supported subgroup. Conversely, a clinician's unsupported preference or an assumed prohibition of all D&E cannot establish invalidity.

Kennedy would reverse the previability method injunction on his reasonable-regulation approach, while preserving P3 and a supported application protecting necessary care. He regards the act-defined method restriction, actual alternative procedures and enacted health defense as sufficient to defeat the present categorical method challenge absent an established denial of protected choice or necessary care. He does not adopt an all-applications bar or find every clinical alternative equally safe. Scalia and Thomas likewise would reverse the method injunction under their ordinary reasonable-regulation position; they join neither the six-step remand nor the constitutional necessary-care rationale.

### P5. Tailored relief and independently operable duties

The five P1/P4 supporters would affirm in part, vacate in part and remand: retain protection against the P3 constitutional health exclusion; remove the categorical P2 rationale; obtain the P4 substantive application and a provision-specific remedial judgment. Casey Step 6, Fargo and Leavitt require attention to Ohio §1.50 and Geiger/Maurer, not automatic elimination of the whole Act. Separate provisions or applications may survive if capable of independent operation, consistent with legislative intention and without judicial insertion of terms; severance cannot fundamentally disrupt the statutory scheme. No conclusion that Utah's express independent-enactment provision automatically determines Ohio's distinct statute is adopted.

The remand must separately address the twenty-second-week testing obligation and its medical-emergency exception, the rebuttable twenty-four-week viability presumption, and the five postviability duties: the attending physician's written necessity certification; second-physician written certification after record/test review; a facility with neonatal access; the survival-favoring method subject to the statute's distinct maternal-risk qualification; and a second physician present to care for the fetus. The five duties have their own emergency exemption. No fixed week is adopted as constitutional viability; a rebuttable presumption and a testing trigger are not identical. Preserve lawful severable administration while identifying certifications, civil remedies, criminal sanctions or other requirements genuinely dependent on an invalid command. The method-selection maternal-risk qualification cannot be silently replaced by a different section's exception.

No automatic blanket enforcement follows from vacating the categorical grounds, and no permanent blanket injunction follows merely because the case returns. Existing protection should be conformed promptly to the affirmed necessary-care holding; the lower court must promptly determine interim protection for unresolved applications under the ordinary equitable standard and present record. The mandate should not finally dissolve protection for those unresolved applications before that determination, or order a new evidentiary trial without a demonstrated need. No complete injunction against every testing/reporting duty is affirmed. This is a bounded remand preserving adjudicated protection, not an indefinite merits reservation.

Kennedy joins state-law severability, the P3-linked remedial inquiry and preservation of independently lawful commands, but would lift the previability method injunction on his P4 disposition; he does not join five-Justice continuation of unresolved method protection for a six-step remand. Scalia and Thomas support severability principles as law but would reverse the constitutional injunction rather than supply votes for P3 protection or P4 reconsideration. Do not merge these distinct mandates merely because each contemplates further ministerial proceedings.

## Common committed propositions

1. Affirm the Third Circuit. Section 1023(e)(3)(B) does not unambiguously confine reserve strengthening to increases caused by changed computation methods or assumptions. Treasury has authority to implement the transition, and its specified net-addition construction is a permissible interpretation of the undefined statutory term in this particular fresh-start exclusion. The commitment is to that statutory-authority and Chevron ground; it does not require an independently sufficient judicial holding that this is the uniquely correct reading.
2. For an unpaid loss reserve attributable to an accident year before 1986, the operative comparison is the reserve at the end of the taxable year beginning in 1986 against the immediately preceding year-end reserve reduced by claims and loss-adjustment payments during the 1986-beginning year attributable to that reserve. Reinsurance ceded in that year is treated as a loss payment. Net strengthening is excluded from the fresh start and treated as occurring in the first taxable year beginning after December 31, 1986. Reasonableness of the reserve and discretion over setting it are not additional statutory prerequisites to this computation.
3. Preserve both regulatory exceptions: exclude a nondiscretionary loss amount reported from a mandatory state or federal assigned-risk pool; exclude payments for reinsurance assumed during a 1986-beginning year and additions for a line of business's reinsurance assumed then, only up to the hypothetical reserve computed with the assumptions, other than assumed interest rates, used for that line's reinsurance assumed in a 1985-beginning year. Reinsurance assumed or ceded in a 1985-beginning year is treated as occurring in the succeeding year when its appropriate unpaid-loss reserve is not adjusted until that year. Section 845 separately applies to a reinsurance transaction having avoidance of the strengthening limitation as one purpose. Neither exception is asserted to apply here; no new avoidance finding is made.
4. Congress supplied a fresh-start exclusion while expressly removing reserve strengthening. Restricting that removal to formal methodology changes is not compelled by the words or the transition's operation: increases based on revised loss information can enlarge the excluded difference as well. Treasury reasonably treats the specified net addition as strengthening without conducting an artificiality or taxpayer-intent trial. The anti-inflation purpose corroborates this reading but does not create authority apart from the statute.

**Event:** April 21, 1998; No. 97-5310; argued March 2, 1998.

## Common committed rule, explanation and remedy

Affirm. When the right to federal rescission expires under §1635(f), a borrower cannot revive it by asserting rescission defensively in a later foreclosure action. The ordinary expiration is three years after consummation or sale of the property, whichever occurs first, even if material disclosures have not been made; the statutory agency-proceeding extension below remains part of that rule. Section 1635(f) terminates the right itself, not merely the time for filing an affirmative action. A defense label does not preserve a right Congress has ended.

The extension applies when an agency empowered to enforce the subchapter institutes a proceeding to enforce §1635 within three years of consummation, finds a violation, and the obligor's rescission claim rests in whole or part on a matter involved in that proceeding. In that event the right expires at the later of the ordinary endpoint—three years after consummation or earlier property sale—and one year following conclusion of the proceeding, any judicial review, or the period for judicial review. No qualifying agency proceeding is identified here. The holding neither deletes this exception nor extends it to every private suit or regulatory inquiry.

Reiter and Bull preserve same-transaction recoupment against an affirmative-action limitation, only up to the plaintiff's liability. That rule does not convert the expiring §1635 rescission right into an unlimited defense. Congress separately made §1640 damages assertable by recoupment or setoff in a collection action after its one-year affirmative-action period. This express damages preservation and §1635(f)'s right-expiration language supply distinct grounds for treating the remedies differently. Consumer-protection purposes do not erase Congress's selected endpoint for unwinding a secured transaction.

The 1986 refinancing preceded the 1991 cessation of payments and 1992 foreclosure by more than three years; on the stated record the federal rescission right had expired in 1989. Affirmance leaves the $396 actual-damages and $1,000 statutory-damages offsets untouched. It does not adopt the trial court's alternative residential-mortgage classification, adjudicate timely rescission notice or how a timely exercise is enforced, settle tender obligations, invalidate or validate the entire loan on another ground, or eliminate independent state-law remedies. No new money award, hearing or injunction is ordered.

**Event:** April 21, 1998; No. 96-1584; argued January 20, 1998.

## Propositions and scope

**P1 — Excluded jurors' equal-protection rights.** Campbell's race does not prevent him from challenging purposeful racial exclusion in the judicial selection of a Louisiana grand-jury member. Powers supplies the required concrete injury to the defendant from discriminatory selection of the tribunal, his relationship with the citizens selected to act in his prosecution, and excluded citizens' substantial practical obstacles to litigating individual exclusion. McCollum confirms that the ordinary third-party-standing conditions operate in jury selection without requiring shared race. The foreperson is selected from the venire before the other members are drawn and has full voting powers: this is the selection of a member, not merely appointment to clerical office within a previously constituted body. This proposition is supported by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. It neither presumes discrimination nor provides general standing to vindicate every citizen's rights.

**P2 — Campbell's own due process.** Irrespective of his race, Campbell may challenge purposeful racial discrimination in the selection of a member of the grand jury that indicted him as an injury to the fairness and integrity of his own criminal process. Hobby does not defeat that route. Hobby addressed selection of a federal foreperson from an already selected grand jury and the office's ministerial duties; Louisiana's initial choice supplies a full member of the accusatory body. The distinction is the composition of that body, not a finding that every foreperson duty is substantive. All eight Associates support this personal-right route and the remand; no third-party-standing premise is necessary to it.

**P3 — Proof and remedy.** Reverse the Louisiana Supreme Court's judgment rejecting Campbell's challenge for lack of standing, and remand for consideration of the discrimination claim under the applicable proof rules and further proceedings. The alleged absence of any Black foreperson from January 1976 through August 1993, despite more than twenty percent Black registered voters, is uncontested as recited; it is not a Supreme Court finding that purposeful discrimination is established. The intermediate appellate court considered the evidence inadequate and contemplated an evidentiary hearing. The remand permits necessary fact development and legal assessment without itself adjudicating discrimination, ordering an unconditional new trial or quashing the indictment now. Any ultimate relief must follow the adjudicated violation and applicable remedial law. All eight Associates support this disposition.

**P4 — Unresolved Sixth Amendment issue.** Do not decide the fair-cross-section theory. Initial presentation below is not enough to establish the missing appellate presentation; the packet's specific procedural recitations establish that limitation. This reservation is not an inference from unrecovered research files. All eight Associates join the reservation.

## Common committed propositions

**I. In-rem jurisdiction without State possession.** The Eleventh Amendment does not bar this federal admiralty proceeding against a wreck that California neither possesses nor holds through an officer, solely because California asserts ownership. The Davis supplies the actual-possession distinction for a sovereign's asserted property interest; Ex parte New York (No. 2) involved a State-owned vessel in State possession and governmental service, which is absent here. A title assertion or statutory constructive possession is not the actual custody whose displacement would require process against the sovereign. This is a conclusion about this traditional in-rem action, not a general immunity exception whenever a plaintiff writes the name of property rather than the State in the caption.

**II. Jurisdiction and title are distinct.** The district court may exercise in-rem jurisdiction and decide the competing claims under the governing law without first accepting California's ownership assertion or requiring it to prevail on the ASA as an immunity prerequisite. This permission is no judgment that California lacks title. It directs no personal damages, officer coercion or execution removing property from State possession, and decides no case involving a State-possessed wreck. Coeur d'Alene's officer/title limits and Seminole's unresolved direct-State constitutional rationale do not displace the distinct historical admiralty rule. No Article I abrogation, waiver or new general state-amenability holding is needed.

**III. ASA predicates and maritime abandonment.** Section 2105 asserts United States title and transfers it to the State for an abandoned shipwreck (a) embedded in State submerged lands, (b) embedded in coralline formations protected by the State on its submerged lands, or (c) on State submerged lands and included in or determined eligible for the National Register. Abandonment remains a distinct predicate, informed by its maritime meaning: renunciation may be express or inferred from appropriate circumstances; mere passage of time, failure to recover previously inaccessible property, or an assertion of State ownership does not automatically establish it. The Court should not determine on this incomplete title record that all insured and uninsured cargo and the ship have one settled ownership or abandonment status. Nor should it approve a universal express-renunciation-only rule or declare every old wreck abandoned.

The public must receive adequate notice of the location of a wreck to which title is asserted. The Secretary of the Interior, after consulting the appropriate State Historic Preservation Officer, makes the written eligibility determination for the third category. Public-land wrecks are United States property and Indian-land wrecks belong to the owning tribe under §2105(d). Rights reserved under 43 U.S.C. §§1311, 1313, 1314 and 33 U.S.C. §§414, 415 remain reserved. Section 2106 excludes salvage and finds only for wrecks to which §2105 applies; it leaves federal shipwreck law otherwise unchanged and does not affect a proceeding brought before April 28, 1988. This action began in 1991. None of these qualifications is a present factual finding that the Brother Jonathan satisfies the predicate.

**IV. Disposition and next stage.** Vacate the appellate judgment and remand for further proceedings consistent with the absence of a possession-based immunity bar, with ASA applicability and competing title claims addressed independently under the correct statutory and maritime principles. Do not affirm a categorical title conclusion reached as a preliminary jurisdictional predicate. California's statutory claim, insurer-derived interests, uninsured cargo and the United States' asserted property interest remain for lawful determination. No ownership, distribution, salvage award, entitlement to a completed salvage operation or final National Register fact is adjudicated. Necessary lawful custodial preservation remains for the district court; the Court neither orders surrender to California nor converts DSR's custodial/exclusive-salvor status into title. Do not decide California §6313 preemption broadly where jurisdiction no longer depends on defeating that asserted State title. ## Remedial analysis now adopted by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer

Their original view correctly distinguished constitutional correction from equitable naturalization, but the original unexplored remaining-eligibility reservation is no longer supportable. Heckler v. Mathews requires recognition that equality can be repaired by withdrawing or extending a benefit; it does not command withdrawal. Califano v. Here the narrower correction removes the invalid deadline from the claimant's available paternal route, retaining legitimation and the independent transmission prerequisites. ## Result and operative ground

All eight would affirm the Pennsylvania Supreme Court's priority judgment, on a bounded construction of the actual collision between 26 U.S.C. section 6323(a) and 31 U.S.C. section 3713(a)(1)(B). Where the Government's federal tax lien is not valid against an existing-property judgment lien protected by section 6323(a), the Government cannot use section 3713 to obtain payment of that same tax claim from the encumbered property ahead of that protected judgment creditor merely because the taxpayer's estate is insolvent. Section 6323's specific tax-lien allocation governs this conflict; section 3713 otherwise retains its operation.

The established lien predates the federal notices and attaches to the identified real estate, not an after-acquired asset. The Government's acknowledgment of section 6323 protection establishes the protected-creditor predicate. It does not concede that every separately claimed federal-insolvency exception based on greater specificity, possession, execution or title transfer is met. The Court need not resolve that distinct exception because the specific statutory protection itself resolves this priority conflict. Thus no remand to decide that unused ground is required.

## Explanation and precedent treatment

The Government's strongest reading distinguishes invalidity of its tax lien against the creditor from the independent insolvency payment command. That distinction is legally intelligible, but it cannot preserve the operative protection Congress gave this judgment lien if the same Government takes this property's entire value first solely by relabeling the collection claim. In this insolvent estate containing no other asset, the asserted payment priority would defeat the specific allocation on the very property the allocation protects. Reading the provisions together gives effect to that allocation without excluding every tax debt from section 3713 or recognizing a universal lien exception.

Morton v. Mancari supplies the duty to harmonize provisions and give the specific scheme its actual work; it does not justify implied repeal from general policy. Randall illustrates specific statutory distribution displacing general priority at a defined conflict, without deciding this tax category itself. Kimbell Foods identifies Congress's rejection of unlimited tax-lien preference and the notice scheme's role; its lending/solvent-debtor setting means it is corroborative statutory context, not a controlling holding on insolvent estates. Emory and Key retain the demanding inconsistency requirement; the concrete property-specific conflict satisfies that requirement here.

McDermott is distinguished, not limited: a judgment lien had not attached to the later-acquired land before federal notice there. Here the protected lien already attaches to the existing property, and section 6323 protection is acknowledged. Holywell and Hill preserve attention to the exact statutory category and prevent commercial expectations or equitable hardship from supplying an exemption independently of text. No decision is overruled.

The district court found these plaintiffs' goods inseparably connected to their protected messages; nonprofit status does not make otherwise identical activity less obstructive or less commercially competitive.

The ruling concerns this expressive activity and actual classification. It does not establish a general right to sell ordinary merchandise, invalidate every neutral vending or solicitation rule, require the least restrictive means or require a city to regulate every source of congestion at once. The City may pursue lawful physical, location, time or manner controls, but their imagined availability does not validate the classification enacted here.

## Controlling explanation available for common adoption

Ward and Clark permit adequately tailored content-neutral regulation serving significant interests while leaving adequate channels. A valid general aim does not dispense with the need to defend the actual line drawn. Discovery Network supplies that connection between the selected class and the harm: the problem is an unexplained distinction among comparable physical activities, not that the restriction achieves only a small reduction. Its commercial-speech setting does not diminish the protection of the plaintiffs' fully expressive activity.

The City's administrative proxy argument has force where an actual category distinguishes commercial activity from expression or corresponds to different harms. Here the trial findings establish the expressive character of both plaintiffs' activity. Forming or joining a nonprofit would change their paperwork or affiliation without changing the asserted congestion or competition. It therefore does not establish the missing fit and is not accepted as a constitutionally adequate substitute for individual expression. Neither the government nor the Court may replace the actual ordinance with an unadopted comprehensive vending ban.

Content classification and alternative channels need not be independently resolved after this tailoring failure. The message-relevance requirement creates an additional content question; assuming neutrality grants the City the more favorable standard and still resolves this dispute. Ladue permits that mode of analysis and preserves attention to actual channels, but its residential holding is not a universal boardwalk-sales right. Madsen's more demanding injunction standard is inapplicable to this legislative ordinance. Rosenberger protects private viewpoint equality within its actual program but does not convert every organizational distinction into viewpoint discrimination. McIntyre confirms the protection of ordinary individual advocacy without transferring its exacting-scrutiny disclosure rule to this ordinance.

## Adversarial alternatives and remedy

**Sustain the nonprofit proxy:** Excluding commercial vending while accommodating expressive nonprofit activity is a lawful municipal objective, and the City need not choose the least restrictive technique. The flaw is the unsupported premise that unaffiliated speakers here are commercially distinct from nonprofits engaged in the same expressive activity. The district findings and the argument that Newman could simply obtain nonprofit status expose that mismatch. A materially supported neutral classification would present another case.

**Apply strict scrutiny as a content rule:** The message-relevance limitation and selective permissions create a serious content question; benign objectives alone do not settle it. This path would broaden the rationale without being necessary to the judgment, because even the assumed-neutral rule fails ordinary tailoring. No Associate's outcome requires a new categorical strict-scrutiny holding here.

Affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal and leave ordinary further proceedings to implement the constitutional limitation. The Court does not order deletion of an exemption, extend every exemption to every seller, design a permit system, decide severability without its governing basis, award damages or fees, or declare an imagined alternative channel adequate. The separate printed-matter exemption and the nonprofit/message conditions must remain distinct in describing the ordinance. No separate writing is necessary if the common opinion retains this narrow standard, remedy and limits.

# Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights — independent non-Stone commitments

**Stage and cutoff:** Independent modeling; May 4, 1998; No. 97-501; argued April 21, 1998. All eight Associates participate. No author or assignment selected. Same-day Perry supplies no entering law.

## Threshold and common propositions

All eight reach the preserved reasonableness challenge to automatic station-house custody. The separate search settlement and abandoned no-probable-cause claim remain closed. The independently raised warrant-clause theory was waived; no judgment relies on a warrant requirement or contradicts the recorded concession about arrest under a warrant. The ordinance's civil, regulatory, fine-only character is retained. State/local arrest authorization and probable cause are accepted, but neither removes Fourth Amendment review.

Whren establishes objective authority for an ordinary initial traffic stop and preserves independent manner, scope and duration limits. It does not already decide this civil business-license custody. Wilson permits relevant common-law history to inform reasonableness without constitutionalizing every historical or local rule. Robinson and Gustafson describe consequences of a lawful custodial arrest and cannot prove the disputed antecedent lawfulness by circular reasoning. Soldal prevents the civil label from eliminating coverage, while leaving violation and liability distinct. Robinette rejects a universal script while separating initial intervention from continued restraint. No hidden investigative purpose is a ground of decision.

## Two supported result paths

**Four would vacate and remand:** Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer would reject the categorical sufficiency of probable cause plus local authority for this automatically imposed civil licensing custody. Taking an identified operator to a station and confining him adds a substantial restraint to the authority to investigate and initiate enforcement. In this fine-only regulatory setting, that added custody must reasonably serve an actual enforcement need; a municipality cannot establish the constitutional necessity or proportionality of custody merely by making station-house paperwork its uniform policy. They would require consideration of the actual connection between confinement and enforcing this ordinance, without a least-restrictive-means rule, a universal citation entitlement or a prohibition on all fine-only arrests.

They select vacatur, rather than an immediate constitutional damages judgment, because the reviewed judgment treats the accepted initial predicate and lawful local policy as sufficient. The material omitted legal inquiry is whether the additional station-house restraint, in this actual regulatory setting, was reasonably justified by the enforcement function it performed. The existing facts about continued operation, potential accumulated fines, one hour of processing and recognizance release must be assessed under that inquiry; they are not erased or transformed into unknown facts. Remand permits further adjudication on the existing record and whatever ordinary procedure actually warrants, not an automatic evidentiary hearing.

**Four would affirm on a bounded application:** Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas would hold the actual custody reasonable: accepted probable cause of a continuing licensing violation, accepted local authority to take the offender into custody, and a short period used to prepare the enforcement documents and release him provide an adequate objective relation to enforcement. The Fourth Amendment does not require proof that on-site citation would have been impossible before this ordinary processing may occur. The fine-only civil character matters to proportionality, but does not alone prohibit custody. Their affirmance does not approve every fine-only arrest, every duration or method, later unrelated detention, or treatment bearing no reasonable relation to processing the violation.

## Adversarial assessment and precise remedy consequences

The vacatur position's strongest difficulty is that the existing law does not yet impose a distinct individualized custody-necessity burden for every petty or regulatory violation, and the actual custody is connected to a continuing violation. Its rule is a new bounded application of reasonableness here, not a proposition already dictated by Whren. Its practical limits allow custody when reasonably connected to securing identification or appearance, ending an actual continuing violation, protecting persons or evidence, or performing necessary processing in the circumstances; these examples are possibilities, not findings that any arose here, and they do not form an exhaustive prerequisite list. The inquiry does not make a citation mandatory whenever conceivable.

The affirmance position's strongest difficulty is that probable cause establishes grounds to act, while the Village's policy might make every regulatory infraction a reason to confine someone solely for administrative convenience. Its bounded answer relies on the actual continuing offense and short enforcement processing, not on a general elimination of scope review. The four would not use Robinson or Gustafson to assume the disputed custody lawful, and would not make an officer's hidden motive decisive.

For the four voting to vacate: set aside adverse summary judgment on the preserved custody-reasonableness claim and return for proceedings applying the stated inquiry. No constitutional damages award, individual-immunity disposition, particular hearing, replacement municipal policy or reopening of the search/probable-cause claims follows. The immunity defense has neither been decided nor established waived. Lanier and Ayeni require conduct-time legal clarity and independent responsibility analysis if those questions actually arise.

For the four voting to affirm: sustain the judgment on this narrow constitutional application, without reaching immunity or making an independent damages holding. Their reasonableness conclusion preserves all stated execution limits. The opposing opinions would need substantive summaries explaining the enforcement-connection disagreement; no additional separate concurrence is needed if each ground is preserved accurately.
