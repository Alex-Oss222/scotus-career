# 152. Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith

*Stone pre-vote case brief. Alternate-history petition and merits treatment, not an actual Supreme Court merits decision.*

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. Status

- **Version:** OT1997 Stone-only forecast, version 2. **Approval:** **PROPOSED AND APPROVED** under the user's simulation stipulation, not enacted Supreme Court law.

## B. Judgment and remedy

- **Stone's vote:** **Reverse the Florida Supreme Court and remand.** Florida cannot use a party's failure to hold five percent of statewide registered voters as a categorical reason to withhold the institutional filing-fee rebate from a legally recognized minor party whose candidate has already qualified to compete. The reason offered below, preventing factionalism and fostering established parties, improperly treats the existence of dominant political organizations as an end in itself.
- **Relief:** Enjoin further distribution under the challenged discriminatory classification after orderly implementation on remand. Florida may end the rebate, extend it on evenhanded terms to recognized parties with qualified candidates, or adopt a genuinely support-sensitive financing measure justified by an independent constitutional interest. Stone does not order repayment of completed distributions or create an unrestricted entitlement to public campaign money.

## C. Issue positions

### Issue 1: A state may not finance established-party entrenchment with unequal treatment of qualified candidates

- **Reach:** First and Fourteenth Amendment challenge to a mandatory candidate-fee distribution scheme, not a freestanding demand for identical public subsidies.
- **Received rule:** *Anderson* and *Burdick* require an examination of the injury to political association and the State's actual interest; *Buckley* allows certain objective public-financing thresholds based on demonstrated support. These holdings do not make any percentage threshold valid in any financing system, and *Norman* reinforces that a candidate's valid qualification cannot be nullified by unrelated restrictions on party organization.
- **Proposed holding:** A State may require demonstrated political support for rationally designed election assistance. But once it recognizes a party and admits its candidate to the ballot, it cannot redirect the benefits of a compulsory filing-fee rebate scheme exclusively to parties already holding a dominant share of registered voters solely to preserve their organizational stability. Favoritism toward the present major parties is not itself a legitimate government objective. A threshold must be defended by its connection to the specific financing mechanism and the actual burden imposed on excluded electoral association.
- **Application:** The Libertarian candidate qualified and paid a concededly lawful filing fee. The State nevertheless treated the major-party candidate's payment as generating support for that candidate's established party, while denying the same form of organized electoral assistance to the qualified Libertarian's party. Florida's five-percent statewide-registration criterion measures accumulated institutional dominance, not the seriousness of this candidate's qualified race or a demonstrated administrative cost of rebating the fee. The State's own rationale emphasizes preserving established parties and avoiding factional alternatives, which does not answer why recognized smaller parties must be categorically excluded from this particular benefit. The constitutional defect is the operation and purpose of this allocation, not the mere existence of unequal subsidies.
- **Boundary:** The judgment neither guarantees the survival of every minor party nor requires a State to finance every prospective candidate. A genuine public program keyed to evidence of support, compliance and fiscal administration remains possible; *Buckley* is not overturned.

### Issue 2: Government's own fee mechanism cannot predetermine political competition

- **Reach:** Clarifies the protected interest and the narrow remedy.
- **Stone's position:** The party asserts the political participation of its members and voters, not a claim that every legal entity must enjoy personal rights. Florida's collection and reallocation of candidate fees is governmental action. When that action assists entrenched party structures and excludes ballot-qualified smaller parties, constitutional review is warranted even without proof of total ballot exclusion.
- **Application:** Stone invalidates the discriminatory allocation and leaves Florida free to choose an evenhanded fiscal design. This holding concerns state-distributed candidate filing fees, not expenditure of a corporation's general treasury in elections.

## D. Certiorari position
