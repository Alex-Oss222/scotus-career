### Issue 1: Repeated stops after judicial warnings

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the actual threshold and reviewable legal question before deciding factual matters outside the record.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The supplied case-specific position and proposed contextual defense control this reconstruction of Stone. Current Court law still retains the Harlow/Anderson defense: Crawford-El did not adopt Stone’s replacement. His proposed official-burden framework and rejection of a duplicative excuse for deliberate violations must therefore be identified openly as proposed doctrinal changes.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An official cannot automatically claim reasonable mistake after receiving applicable judicial rulings rejecting the same basis for materially similar stops.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would reverse summary judgment on the identified unconstitutional stops and remand the specified related issues, preserving the latest source’s distinctions. An official cannot repeatedly use a premise after pertinent authoritative rulings have removed it and then invoke general uncertainty about traffic enforcement. At the same time, a new violation or genuinely different factual premise may justify a later stop. The defense therefore turns on the law, the correction communicated, and the information available to the particular official. The remand must preserve those differences rather than announce liability for every encounter.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Failure of immunity does not itself prove the constitutional claim, causation, damages, supervisory liability, or a right to trial.
