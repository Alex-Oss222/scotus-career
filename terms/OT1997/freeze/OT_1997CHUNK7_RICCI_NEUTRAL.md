* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed summary judgment for the defendants, 116 F.3d 288 (June 20, 1997), No. 96-2229. Supreme Court No. 97-501 was argued April 21, 1998. The underlying ordinance’s civil/regulatory character must be retained rather than recasting it as a breach-of-peace misdemeanor.
* **Relief Requested:** Ricci argues that a fine-only licensing violation by an identified business operator provides no proportionate basis for automatic station-house custody, invoking the limited common-law arrest power and Fourth Amendment reasonableness.
* **Material Record:** Randall Ricci operated Rudeway Enterprises, a telemarketing business. Officers investigating complaints arrested an employee on an outstanding warrant, then asked Ricci about his own business license. He admitted he lacked one. Under Village policy they took him to the station, confined him in an interview room for about an hour, prepared paperwork, and released him on recognizance. His wife obtained the license and the charge was dismissed. The separate business-premises search claim was settled; Ricci did not appeal dismissal of his no-probable-cause claim. The live challenge concerns custodial processing under the licensing ordinance, not the existence of probable cause.
