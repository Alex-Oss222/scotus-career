## Validated neutral record and review scope

The Bakers' Missouri wrongful-death action against GM was removed to federal court on diversity grounds. Their mother died after a February 1990 collision involving a 1985 Chevrolet S–10 Blazer. Defect, fire causation and cause of death were contested. The Bakers subpoenaed former GM employee Ronald Elwell, who had investigated vehicle fires and assisted product-liability defense counsel. He supported their fuel-system theory and identified a 1973 memorandum. The jury awarded $11.3 million.

The separate Michigan litigation was between GM and Elwell. GM's fiduciary-duty, confidentiality and misappropriation allegations remain allegations. A November 1991 preliminary restriction protected trade secrets and attorney-client/work-product material. The August 26, 1992 stipulated permanent injunction substantially repeated that protection and added a broad testimony/consultation restriction in GM litigation without GM's written consent. Its exception protected the jurisdiction of the specified then-pending Georgia court. The separate settlement agreement, rather than the injunction, protected court-ordered testimony in other proceedings against an action for breach of the agreement or injunction. These are distinct instruments and exceptions.

The Bakers were not Michigan parties. The federal trial court allowed testimony after reviewing the instruments, relying on Missouri policy favoring relevant nonprivileged evidence and on the decree's modifiability. The Eighth Circuit reversed on grounds including the testimony's admission; it found neither its assumed public-policy exception nor sufficient evidence of Michigan modification supported that admission. The word 'including' matters: rejecting this testimony ground does not automatically restore the complete damages judgment. The present question concerns the effect of the Michigan injunction on the separate Missouri proceeding, rather than general revision of Michigan's decree between its parties.

The report attributes to the Bakers an undisputed representation that GM made no attorney-client, work-product or trade-secret objection to a particular Missouri question or answer. This is not independent transcript verification, a finding that every conceivable item was nonprivileged, or a universal waiver. Ordinary admissibility, actual privilege, separate agreement enforcement and grounds outside the reviewed issue retain their own rules.

## Exact entering-law work

The slice reproduces the complete pertinent holdings and operative standards, including all authority and remedy limits:

- **Matsushita (February 27, 1996):** O'Connor's unanimous Part I applies §1738 to state consent/class-settlement judgments and begins with rendering-state effect; Part II has seven joins, Part III eight, and Part IV-A unanimous constitutional representation protection. The eight Associates expressly reserve the complete collateral-review boundary in IV-B. Consent, state-law error or exclusive federal jurisdiction supplies no categorical escape from recognition; recognition neither creates representative authority nor approves coercive interference with another federal proceeding.
- **Richards (June 10, 1996), unanimous:** Common interests, vigorous advocacy, public importance or fiscal reliance alone cannot bind absent persons' own rights. Sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate representation, or another due-process-consistent relationship remains possible; issue preclusion as well as claim preclusion needs that basis. Notice alone creates no general intervention duty. This is no universal exception to final judgments.
- **Celotex (April 19, 1995):** Thomas's actual-relatedness, interim §157 authority and qualified orderly-review rationale command six joins (O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer); coverage and remedy command seven. Its actual jurisdictional relation, applicable order and concrete relief/review mechanism are material. It does not authorize every injunction, establish a universal contempt rule or extinguish another person's rights without authority. The preserved substantial-jurisdiction, transparent-invalidity and frustrated-review qualifications cannot be erased or treated as a free license to disregard an order.

## Material lawful alternatives and limiting principles

One path gives the Michigan judgment its lawful effect between persons it binds while declining to export the broad testimony restriction as an evidentiary command against the Bakers or the separate trial court. It must explain the legal boundary without turning a policy disagreement into a free-standing exception to valid judgments, inventing lack of Michigan jurisdiction over GM/Elwell, or adjudicating all privileges.

The strongest competing path begins with Michigan law and argues that a valid in-personam command to Elwell remains effective outside Michigan, so requiring testimony circumvents a judgment that must be recognized and challenged through orderly procedures. It must answer the Bakers' nonparty status, the forum's own authority to obtain evidence, the difference between judgment effect and local enforcement machinery, and the distinct court-ordered-testimony protection. Celotex's bankruptcy allocation does not answer those predicates by itself.

A narrower construction may turn on the decree's actual scope and the separate agreement's protection; it cannot invent unquoted settlement terms, an agreement merging into the judgment, an adjudicated Michigan modification, or privity. Remand can preserve unresolved lawful evidentiary/other appellate issues without restoring the entire verdict automatically.
