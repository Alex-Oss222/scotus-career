## 1. Comparator and the concrete change in the available inputs

The [clean comparator](../freeze/OT_1997CHUNK3_COMPARATOR_CLEAN_MACARTHUR.md) supplies a lower-court decision, not historical Supreme Court merits votes. Circuit Judge Douglas H. Ginsburg, joined by Randolph, affirmed the statutory judgment; Tatel dissented on retention and concurred in the constitutional-standing rejection on narrower traceability grounds. Those identities supply no Supreme Court alignment, authorship or assignment. Justice Ruth Bader Ginsburg is not the author of that lower opinion. No certiorari action is used as merits evidence.

The statutory majority held that information pertinent when collected does not lose its permissible relation to the authorized activity merely because its present operational interest ends. Its strongest grounds are the statute's broad word “activity,” the record's continuing relation to historical law enforcement, independent work for the ban on maintaining unlawfully collected information, and the value of preserving an intelligence history. Tatel's strongest response is the independently defined continuing act of maintenance and Congress's different acquisition-based wording elsewhere. Neither the majority's conclusion nor the dissent's characterization establishes that all of Lindblom's records presently lack a lawful connection.

The new [§14 neutral packet](../freeze/OT_1997CHUNK3_NEUTRAL_MACARTHUR_SECTION14_RESEARCH.md) is the authorized correction to the source and legal framing. The actual supported changes are these:

1. **Affirmative monetary-request evidence is now claimant-specific.** Opposition 3, citing C.A. App. 10, expressly attributes the damages request to Lindblom. The reported original statutory claim, dismissal of the entire complaint and continued Privacy Act appeal are affirmative evidence of the existing controversy. They permit applying the governing statutory remedy without inventing another count. The complete neutral validation found no express money abandonment. Missing renewed subsection citations or a repeated prayer do not themselves trigger a preservation rule. This is not a finding that every condition for recovery has been proved.
2. **The original district order is recovered.** Petition App. 20a, September 6, 1995, grants summary judgment and dismisses the complaint with prejudice. It states lawful compilation, FOIA exemptions and incidental, apparently innocent contact, but no express statutory adverse-effect, damages or amendment-remedy determination. Its incorporation of the defendant's reasons does not authorize inventing the contents of unrecovered motion papers. The with-prejudice language concretely supports correcting the constitutional dismissal's preclusive form.
3. **The actual requested retention construction is more limited than a purge-on-closure or universal-review demand.** Reply 2 and 4–5 disclaim an active-investigation-only condition and continual review, while pressing a present articulable use. That is advocacy, not adopted law. It removes an overbroad characterization as a reason to reject the actual alternative. Conversely, Opposition 9 n.6 preserves the Government's contention that other persons and organizations remain of investigative interest. That is a quoted record account and argument, not a conclusive current-pertinence finding about each disputed record.
4. **Statutory standing is distinct from ultimate liability.** The actual June 2, 1997 *Steel Co.* holding, *Bell*, *Lujan*, *Arizonans*, *Lee*, and *Alexander* require an affirmative personal controversy but do not require proof of eventual statutory success as its price. The source gap cannot become a finding of no injury, no claim or no consent. Equally, a wholly unavailable remedy cannot furnish redressability. These rules require the separate inquiries in §2 rather than either assumption.

These are changed available evidence and corrected analytical premises relative to the earlier neutral descriptions, not newly invented events in the parties' case. The new packet controls where the original neutral packet, monetary addendum or source-extraction cautions suggested a further express preservation recital was indispensable. Those older documents remain intact as sources with their originally stated coverage limits. No comparison to an earlier modeled position has been made.

## 2. Shared governing analysis and the distinct open questions

The exact 1997 text of §552a, §702 and the FBI regulation, and the actual entering-law slice plus all six increments through February 12, govern. The following P labels retain the fresh independent handoff's labels and scope. They are reconciled proposed commitments; this freeze itself establishes no Court holding.

### P1 — Article III before disputed retention merits: all eight

**Injury and traceability.** The supported occurrence is the FBI's actual continued custody of identified records describing Lindblom's own associations. The claimed protected interest is his personal informational privacy under the statute's restriction on keeping such information. It is not a citizen's abstract wish for lawful government or a prediction that an employer will react to unknown records. Whether the conduct ultimately violates the temporal restriction is the disputed merits question. The actor retaining the information is the defendant, so this particular injury theory needs no unsupported third-party exposure mechanism.

*Lujan* requires specific facts at summary judgment and permits congressional recognition of concrete personal harms previously insufficient in law. *Warth*, 422 U.S. 499–502, distinguishes the existence and personal incidence of injury from ultimate success on the claimed prohibition. *Havens*, 455 U.S. 373–375, illustrates a direct statutory invasion without a required downstream transaction or loss. Its private-defendant cause and pleading-stage posture neither waive federal immunity nor excuse missing evidence here. The acknowledged personal records supply the factual incidence; the reported subjective-chill allegation is not promoted into proof of distress, economic loss or disclosure. *Laird* remains controlling for subjective constitutional chill and does not decide Congress's subsequent §(e)(7) protection.

**Meaningful redress.** Lindblom actually requested money. Sections (g)(1)(D) and (g)(4) expressly authorize an individual agency action and conditional United States monetary liability for qualifying statutory noncompliance. An award within that category can redress a personal statutory injury if its requirements are established. No permitted authority or supported case-specific fact makes every monetary remedy for this claim legally impossible. The remaining issues of adverse effect, culpability, actual damages and the minimum are real liability/remedy questions, not findings already resolved for the claimant. Partial redress is sufficient; an assured final award is not required.

*Steel Co.* requires deciding power first, distinguishes substantial federal claims from statutory success, and rejects relief that could not repair its completed reporting deprivation. This case concerns continuing actual custody and an individual money request, not Treasury penalties or litigation expense alone. *Arizonans* prevents a prayer from creating damages against a defendant the governing statute does not make liable; §(g) expressly addresses the agency and United States here. *Alexander* permits meaningful partial relief despite uncertainty about ultimate recovery, with its pleading-stage and defendant-specific limits preserved. Its controlled-merits exception is not used to decide this unsettled retention question.

**Strongest contrary route and answer.** The strongest threshold objection is that only subjective chill is supported and expungement may be unavailable, so no lawful relief repairs an established personal injury. It cannot dispose of this action without addressing both the independently supported personal custody invasion and the actual statutory money category. A genuinely applicable bar would defeat the affected remedy, but none may be inferred from unrecovered papers, an unresolved amount or the absence of an already favorable willfulness finding. This is an affirmative controversy determination, not hypothetical jurisdiction, an unpleaded agency-Bivens action, or reliance on a bare monetary prayer alone.

### P2 — Jurisdiction, statutory action and sovereign consent: all eight

Sections 1331 and 1254 provide jurisdictional channels, not consent to suit. Section 1361 supplies no unspecified remedy without its conditions. Section (e)(7) imposes a substantive duty and is not an unlimited relief grant. The covered individual action, statutory jurisdiction and conditional money obligation are identified in §(g)(1)(D)/(g)(4). The claim falls within the substantial category those provisions address; adjudicating its statutory conditions is not creation of another action or defendant.

*Bell*, 327 U.S. 681–685, and actual *Steel Co.* permit determining a substantial federal claim without treating every disputed liability element as an Article III defect. Neither case is a sovereign-immunity waiver. *Meyer* requires consent and cause to be separately identified; Congress has expressly supplied the relevant agency action and public obligation here. P2 does not announce that all conditions of all federal waivers are nonjurisdictional, or prejudge the classification or satisfaction of a genuinely distinct remedy limitation.

Neither §(j) nor §(k) permits exemption from §(e)(7). *Doe v. FBI*, 936 F.2d 1351–1353, explains why an agency cannot evade a nonexemptible duty by exempting civil liability, while a valid exemption from §(d) can defeat a derivative §(g)(1)(A) amendment claim. The common conclusion adopts that statutory distinction. A CRS designation, a FOIA withholding exemption or lawful collection is not alone an adjudication of every Privacy Act remedial exemption. No exemption waiver is presumed.

**Strongest contrary route and answer.** Treating every unresolved §(g) requirement as an unestablished limit on sovereign consent would conflate the identified statutory cause/consent with entitlement on the claim. The express money category and substantial action permit the statutory questions to be adjudicated. This does not guarantee recovery or allow §702 to supply money; a demonstrated route-specific statutory exclusion remains enforceable.

### P3 — Adverse effect: six direct joins; two reservations

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain this separate statutory construction: actual unwanted maintenance of identified information about this individual's protected associations, if outside §(e)(7)'s lawful alternatives, can itself be an adverse personal effect under §(g)(1)(D). The claimant must establish the real personal invasion; abstract or technical noncompliance with no adverse personal consequence is insufficient. Section (D) does not require (C)'s adverse determination, public disclosure, proved lost employment or pecuniary loss as such.

This is a supported new construction of this statutory phrase, not an existing holding of *Havens*, *Albright* or *Doe*. *Havens* bears on the direct personal character of an injury, not the additional Privacy Act text. *Albright*, 732 F.2d 184–190, distinguishes adverse effect, causation, culpability and monetary recovery; it permits emotional trauma as an adverse effect but does not make that consequence exclusive or decide its actual-damages/minimum questions. *Doe*, 936 F.2d 1360–1361 and nn.1, 4, enforced a no-adverse-effect finding not challenged there, where damages were not appealed. It supplies no such finding or abandonment here. Neither source is treated as already having adopted P3.

**Strongest contrary construction and answer.** The words “in such a way as to have an adverse effect” could require a consequence additional to the violation, making a recordkeeping invasion insufficient by itself. The six give those words independent work by requiring an actual adverse impact on the individual's protected information interest, rather than merely a breach of governmental procedure. A substantive intrusion and its personal adverse consequence can coincide without making every violation of every Act provision actionable. Their rule does not equate adverse effect with actual damages, presume distress, or settle the minimum recovery.

Scalia and Thomas reserve this legal sufficiency issue. They join P1–P2 and decide that the challenged retention does not violate §(e)(7); they need not decide an additional contested liability element to reject the statutory claim. Their reservation is neither a finding of no adverse effect nor a refusal to recognize the actual monetary request. Reconciliation does not turn their reservations into joins or opposition to P3. No identical earlier judicial formulation is required for the six's legally supported rule, and no prior general privacy join by itself compels it.

### P4 and P5 — Retention constructions and strongest alternatives

**P4: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** During continued retention an agency must satisfy one of §(e)(7)'s three alternatives: express statutory authorization, the individual's authorization, or pertinence to and scope of an authorized law-enforcement activity. Original lawful collection is not an irrebuttable authorization to retain the information after every qualifying relation has ceased. The defined ongoing act “maintain” and the contemporaneous condition supply the rule; it is not a privacy-policy balance or a borrowed necessity standard from another subsection.

Authorized activity is broader than an open investigation or current investigation of this plaintiff. Historic records can remain pertinent to counterintelligence, connected or later inquiries, and legitimate preservation of a law-enforcement record. Age, innocence, closure or name retrieval alone proves no violation. General agency responsibilities and the possibility that any datum might someday be useful do not conclusively establish every record's continuing pertinence indefinitely. Neither the Court nor the source record finds present nonpertinence here. The Government's supported current-interest contention remains available on remand.

Section (b)'s disclosure controls do not eliminate §(e)(7)'s distinct retention restriction. Actual *Department of Defense v. FLRA* supplies the prohibition and enumerated-exception structure, not the comparator's suggestion that all interagency law-enforcement disclosure must proceed only through (b)(7). Other statutory exceptions remain distinct. No dissemination claim is adjudicated.

No periodic agency audit, automatic response duty upon a request, explanation format, deadline, search of every office, public disclosure of classified reasons, burden shift, least-restrictive means inquiry, current-necessity standard or purge upon closure is imposed. The reply's suggested convenient occasions for review are party advocacy; P4 does not adopt them as new legal duties. Ordinary procedure governs a claim properly before a court.

The strongest contrary reading is that the record's pertinence to a completed authorized activity persists, while “maintain” independently forbids keeping records unlawfully gathered; preserving intelligence history itself is legitimate. The six accept the legitimate historical function but reject its conclusive consequence. A present restriction on an ongoing act requires no added word “current”; the broader activity and ordinary pertinence inquiry accommodate genuine historical value without making original acquisition permanently dispositive. Their result does not depend on the preliminary Senate report's different bill.

**P5: Scalia and Thomas.** The record must be pertinent to and within the scope of an authorized activity, and that category includes a completed activity. On the accepted original-collection premise, preserving the record of that lawful activity remains within the described relationship without an additional present-use showing. Maintenance still independently forbids keeping records that were never within an authorized category; limits on collection, use, disclosure and other applicable law remain. They affirm on statutory nonviolation, not injury failure, abandonment or unavailability of every remedy.

The strongest contrary reading gives independent temporal work to “maintain,” reinforced by Congress's use of “collect” and “compiled for” elsewhere. Scalia and Thomas retain their textual disagreement: a continuing prohibition against an impermissible category does not require repeatedly requalifying a lawful historical record by a new operational connection. The six's bounded approach reduces practical burdens but does not remove that substantive distinction. Administrative convenience is supporting context, not authority to disregard contrary text. Both would join the narrower shared proposition that closure, age, innocence or name retrieval alone requires no purge; neither thereby joins P4.

### P6 and P6a — Separate constitutional claims

**P6, all eight.** The Foundation's affidavits do not establish the causal mechanism by which actual FBI custody causes potential applicants or employers to learn of the records and react. *Meese*, 481 U.S. 473–476, involved an actual governmental public label and detailed evidence of its effect. It does not demand a completed loss or identification of every deterred person; it also does not supply this record's missing exposure link. No categorical rule makes all injury from a claimant's own publicity self-inflicted. Tatel's possible FOIA-request mechanism remains hypothetical evidence, not evidence actually supplied.

Lindblom's constitutional claim, separately, was presented through the failed derivative theory without independent constitutional-standing argument or evidence. His personal-chill pleading does not become summary-judgment proof. The constitutional dismissal reaches no First Amendment merits and does not decide statutory adverse effect. The direct statutory information interest and the constitutional claim as actually presented are legally and evidentially distinct.

**P6a, Scalia and Thomas only.** On the particular constitutional record, the predicted employment and applicant consequences also lack sufficiently concrete, actual or imminent personal injury. They retain that independent ground, consistent with their *Lujan* and actual *Lee* positions. No named-lost-applicant rule, required affidavit form or numerical probability standard is adopted. The other six withhold this additional holding and rely on the narrower causal/claim-specific defects.

**Strongest opposite route and answer.** Uncontradicted expert descriptions of stigma may support anticipated organizational harm, and personal records may affect their subject. Those possibilities do not establish how the harm asserted in these constitutional claims arises on this record. The rejection is claim-specific and leaves supported later controversies open; it does not turn reputation or personal privacy into inherently noncognizable interests.

### P7 — Exact remedy and mandate commitments

All eight support no final money or expungement award. Intentional or willful noncompliance, resulting actual compensable damages and the statutory minimum remain separate. No later *Doe v. Chao* or *Cooper* rule enters. No direct constitutional damages cause against the FBI is created.

The §(g)(1)(A)/(g)(2) route remains subject to the legally applicable amendment request/refusal/review and exemption requirements. Access exhaustion does not prove amendment exhaustion. *Nagel*, 725 F.2d 1440–1442 and nn.1–5, expressly recognizes the distinct (e)(7) claim and money route; its favorable expungement discussion must be read alongside *Clarkson*'s actual exhaustion and limited injunction categories and *Doe*'s rejection of a remedy bypass. No unconditional expungement entitlement results. Under actual *Reich* and §702, prospective equity remains an analytical possibility subject to the existing action, statutory preclusion and ordinary legal/equitable limits. The money route makes resolving that entire controversy unnecessary now. Section 702's decree must identify the responsible officer or successor by name or title; lack of an officer's personal name in the caption is not alone a categorical bar.

The six adopting P4 vacate the statutory summary judgment and remand for application of the corrected construction and ordinary remaining civil-action, liability and remedy inquiries. Actual continuing pertinence, any independent statutory or individual authorization, an exemption material to a particular remedy, and a concrete Federal Records Act preservation requirement retain their own predicates. Remand corrects the conclusive original-collection rule. It does not compensate for unrecovered research originals, require new evidence or pleadings, independently reverse the discovery refusal, or order a hearing, automatic discovery or destruction. Confidential and in camera handling remains available under ordinary standards.

Scalia and Thomas leave the statutory judgment in place under P5 and withhold that statutory remand command. They join all common relief limits. A possible future change to their statutory construction would require a fresh supported determination, not an automatic favorable finding on adverse effect.

For both constitutional claims, all eight sustain the standing rejection and require dismissal without prejudice. To the extent the district court's blanket with-prejudice judgment purports to adjudicate those claims on the merits, they vacate that effect and remand only to conform the dismissals to the jurisdictional ground. Neither constitutional declaration nor injunction is restored. Initial collection, abandoned FOIA/Fifth/Ninth Amendment components and other unreviewed matters remain undisturbed without adoption of their reasoning.
