## Associate-specific findings

**O'Connor.** Full join and affirmance are supported. Her frozen commitment accepts Path B's enacted-class objection and affirms on the regime path. The class holding accepts that objection; Hale, Rybar and Kirk supply necessary inclusion in a valid interstate regime, and Hinds preserves independent work for the enacted predicate. Part II-A identifies the Act's valid regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife and the function the taking prohibition performs within it, so the relationship rests on the enacted scheme rather than on construction expenditure or conservation's national importance, and the outside-class, invalid-regime and pretext showings keep the boundary judicially enforceable. Her join in Kennedy's historical Lopez concurrence supports practical federal authority with such a boundary. The externality route's concrete interstate effect, territorial limitation and reasonable fit answer her demand for a mechanism; recognizing that expressly reserved route is a bounded inference, not a claim that Hale already adopted it, and she joins it as an independent alternative that this case does not require.

**Kennedy.** Full join and affirmance are likewise supported. His frozen commitment accepts the enacted-class objection and affirms on the regime path. His American Life League, Rybar, Robinson and Bird opinions require a prohibition's actual operation and reject labels or remote cost chains. Part II-A supplies that operation: the taking prohibition protects the populations whose interstate exploitation and movement the Act regulates, a relationship appearing from the statute's own structure without a further record about this fly, not merely a hospital label or construction expenditure. His historical Lopez concurrence supports practical analysis rather than rigid categories, while preserving an enforceable federal boundary; the Court's independent identification of the valid regime, the covered class and a genuine, non-pretextual relationship does that limiting work. He joins the externality route, with its record, territorial and fit conditions, as an independent alternative. The packet attributes no ecological finding to him and supplies no commercial-transaction prerequisite that would withdraw his join.

**Ginsburg.** Her economic-application objection remains intact, and she dissents from Part I. Sweet Home, Hinds, Robinson and Bird support her view that the habitat-injuring construction is itself economic conduct, rather than a property owner's collateral purchases; her frozen commitment affirms on that ground and on the regime path. That disagreement is not falsely presented as answered by a repeated enacted-class assertion. Part I nevertheless has five independent votes, and Part II-A sustains the same judgment on that classification. Her join in Breyer's historical Lopez dissent supports class-wide practical analysis and legislative respect for a national regulatory system; the Act's integrated regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife and its protection of the regulated populations form such a system, and the relationship is specific, not a claim that every environmental restriction is valid because owners buy goods. She joins Part II-A and its application, Part II-B's independent externality route as an available alternative, Part III and the affirmance. Her economic-application position remains an additional, noncontrolling ground for the same judgment.

**Breyer.** His economic-application objection remains intact, and he dissents from Part I. His historical Lopez dissent and effective functional-market joins would not support simply declaring that economic development has ceased to be economic. The packet makes no such claim about his position; his frozen commitment affirms on Path A and on the regime path. An independent five-Justice Part I settles the statutory-class question for the Court, and Part II-A sustains the applications on that premise. He accepts Part II-A because it rests on a specific supporting relationship, the taking prohibition's function within the Act's regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife, not on possible future benefits of species preservation or a claim that every noneconomic application is essential to some larger regime. He accepts Part III because it requires identification of a genuine relationship without de novo judicial environmental science, formal findings in every case, a numerical benchmark or proof that every exemption would defeat the regime. He joins Part II-B as an available alternative. The concurrence must preserve his disagreement with Part I and his complete assent to the remaining parts and judgment; his original class objection has not disappeared.

**Stevens.** Affirmance and joins in Parts II-A, III and IV are supported. His Hinds opinion's actual-function approach and Sweet Home, Robinson and Bird positions support the economic habitat-conversion ground, which he continues to regard as sufficient; he does not join Part I. His frozen commitment also affirms on the regime path: the same actual-function approach identifies the taking prohibition's role within the Act's valid regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife, and requiring proof that this fly is traded would mistake the unit of analysis. Part II-A rests on no speculative biodiversity effect and no claim that Sweet Home settled the Commerce issue. He does not reach the independent externality route, which Part II-A makes unnecessary here.

**Souter.** Affirmance and joins in Parts II-A, III and IV are supported, together with his join in Stevens's concurrence. His Sweet Home opinion locates liability in actual habitat-causing activity with ordinary causal limits; his Hinds and Robinson joins support examination of actual function. He continues to regard the economic application as sufficient and does not join Part I. Part II-A rests not on comprehensiveness alone but on the identified function of the taking prohibition within the Act's interstate regulation, and it leaves Sweet Home's injury and causation limits intact. He does not reach the independent externality route, which the case does not require, and adopts no bare biodiversity-value theory or finding about unproved interstate purchases.

**Scalia.** Join in Part I, refusal of Parts II-A through IV, and confined reversal/remand are supported. His Lopez no-insertion and Hale-line commitments accept an enacted interstate-regime mechanism when the relationship is demonstrated and reject constructing an economic predicate from the actors' circumstances. He finds no such relationship here: the record shows no trade in this fly and no evasion or material impairment of the Act's trade provisions from its local taking, and a functional relationship stated at the level of the whole statute would let a comprehensive conservation program supply its own justification. The independent externality route remains objectionable to him because territorial regulatory need does not itself establish commerce or necessity to a valid commerce regime. His Part I join does not imply assent to the necessary-inclusion application, the externality route or the affirmance. Sweet Home remains binding and no categorical intrastate-species immunity is attributed to him.

**Thomas.** His Part I join and Scalia-separate-opinion join are supported by the frozen enacted-mechanism commitments. His historical Lopez concurrence adds a Justice-specific objection: a problem affecting several States is not for that reason an enumerated subject. He accepts the governing Hale architecture but finds no demonstrated regime relationship here, and he withholds assent from the independent externality route. The packet does not attribute an overruling of current law or revival of his Sweet Home statutory disagreement.

## Qualifications and integrity

The Government's identification responsibility requires a genuine relationship, not a fixed quantum of proof or judicial substitution for environmental science. Courts independently determine the legal boundaries of the regime, the covered class and the relationship while respecting reasonable congressional judgments about regulatory necessity and practical enforcement; no least-restrictive-means test applies. National desirability, biodiversity, possible future value or comprehensiveness alone remains insufficient. No ecological spillover is found on this record merely because the opinion recognizes an available route.

The affirmance rests on the Act's text and structure and the existing record. It invents no market, interstate purchase, ecological linkage, actual taking, permit entitlement, default or filing event. It supplies no blanket development permit and does not reopen Sweet Home's statutory construction. Source unavailability must not be treated as a litigant's failure of proof.

The D.C. Circuit is the court below, not a Supreme Court merits comparator. Its 2-1 vote and alternative rationales supply no Associate vote or controlling Supreme Court law. No historical Supreme Court merits position exists to be labeled a departure. The individual-position sources below inform inference only and do not supersede entering law.

This audit confirms the Associate compatibility described in the public packet. It does not inspect or validate Stone's private authority, independently perform assignment, or authorize a full-opinion attribution to Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg or Breyer.
