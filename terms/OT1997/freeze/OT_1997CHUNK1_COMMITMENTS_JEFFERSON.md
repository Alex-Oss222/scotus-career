## Decision supported by the record

**All eight Associates would dismiss the writ for want of jurisdiction under §1257(a).** They would directly join the same narrow finality rationale. The Alabama Supreme Court's interlocutory remedy ruling does not satisfy statutory finality or any supported Cox route on the supplied record. The dismissal does not affirm that court's federal merits and creates no holding on compensatory survival under §§1983 and 1988.

The distinction is between a conclusively resolved legal issue and the finality of the controversy for federal review. Petitioners allege racially discriminatory failures to rescue and revive Alberta Jefferson; the City disputes delay and causation and contends that she was already dead. The trial court allowed compensatory federal recovery and certified a legal damages question. The Alabama Supreme Court rejected that treatment and returned the action for further proceedings. The same petitioners' wrongful-death, outrage, and related liability/causation issues remain unresolved. No order finally terminating their entire relevant controversy or making the remaining outcome preordained is supplied.

Those facts are sufficient for the threshold determination. The Court need not reconstruct the eventual trial result, require possession of the entire appendix, or assume that an unavailable research document was never filed. A favorable or adverse later outcome cannot be selected merely to establish jurisdiction now.

## Adversarial alternative-path assessment

**Path A — dismiss for lack of finality.** The remaining proceedings are substantial, concern the same petitioners, and may remove the factual or remedial need to resolve the federal damages question. No categorical barrier to later review after an appropriate adverse final judgment is established. A decision allowing compensatory recovery would permit litigation to continue; it would not remove the relevant cause from court. Existing doctrine therefore supplies no finality route.

The strongest objection is that the state ruling has already eliminated meaningful municipal recovery for the federal death claim: Alabama supplies punitive-only wrongful-death relief, while Newport bars municipal punitive damages. Treating that federal claim as extinguished could make its later implementation seem formal and the remaining state litigation collateral. The response is that Richards made practical completion depend on the actual disposition of the reviewed parties' remaining claims, not simply final resolution of an important legal issue. The same petitioners' factually connected claims and causation dispute remain here. No order makes the relevant remaining outcome preordained, and the Court cannot presume one.

**Path B — reach the merits through practical finality.** The strongest supported form invokes category one by treating the federal death claim's remedial foreclosure as conclusive. A second form invokes category two because the ruling binds the trial court throughout the remaining case. The potential federal-policy importance is real, and a later successful recovery might remove the practical occasion to obtain Supreme Court review.

Those arguments do not satisfy the controlling predicates on this record. The strongest category-one claim lacks the actual completed-party controversy that supported Richards. Category two requires necessary survival through every lawful further outcome, as in Sullivan; a binding interlocutory rule may become unnecessary if the relevant factual predicates or need for additional recovery disappear. Category three requires the absence of later effective review whichever outcome occurs, not merely the possibility that success would end the need for review. Category four requires that reversal preclude further litigation of the relevant cause plus a threat to federal policy; Casarotto's forum-protection premise is absent when reversal would enable damages litigation.

The threshold's application to all four routes is therefore material, rather than a formal observation that the state court used an interlocutory label. Conversely, neither a broad finality rule forbidding all review of interlocutory state orders nor a federal-policy exception allowing every important civil-rights question is adopted.

**Possible accommodation and its limit.** The eight can join an opinion acknowledging that complete elimination of a reviewed party's remaining claims, a necessarily surviving federal issue, or a proven absence of later review might satisfy a different Cox route on a different established posture. That acknowledgment is a reservation, not conditional jurisdiction in this case. A newly supplied final order terminating these petitioners' remaining claims would require a fresh neutral chronology/posture assessment; no vote to reach the §1988 merits is presently committed. A discretionary dismissal as improvidently granted is not substituted for the supported jurisdictional disposition.
