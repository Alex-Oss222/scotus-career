## 1. Common threshold, scope, and assumptions

All eight Associates participate: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Each was seated at argument and at the proposed event date; the authorized record establishes no disqualification or nonparticipation. The ordinary participating-majority and six-member-quorum rules govern. The table below concerns only these eight commitments, not a completed Court tally.

All eight accept review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Eleventh Circuit's final conviction-sustaining judgment on Counts II and III. Rogers is the defendant and appellate party. No supported jurisdictional, standing, finality, or mootness defect defeats this review. The separate machinegun conviction, reversed below for insufficient evidence, remains outside the writ and outside every proposed mandate.

The supported actual granted question is the reported paraphrase whether failure to instruct on an offense element may be harmless when the defendant admitted that element at trial. The inquiry whether this charge actually omitted the element is an analytical antecedent, not a verified second question granted. The exact petition and grant wording are not invented. The Government may defend its favorable judgment on an available record-supported ground without obtaining enlarged rights, subject to the question-presentation limits stated in the slice.

All eight would reach the granted harmlessness issue rather than dismiss the writ on the supplied record. The lower court expressly found an omission and relied on harmlessness, so the question has a concrete adjudicated predicate. Its legal framework may be assessed without deciding a distinct statutory question or conclusively construing unreproduced instructions. Izumi does not require dismissal merely because an antecedent issue exists, and the operator's unavailable originals are not an in-world vehicle defect. This does not declare every antecedent question fairly included.

For the present merits assessment, all eight assume in Rogers's favor that both reviewed offenses required knowledge of the characteristics making this item a silencer and that the charge failed adequately to submit that factual knowledge. This expressly includes the §5861(i) count, whose element the lower court only assumed. The assumption is not a new holding extending Staples to every silencer, every NFA device, or §5861(i). It also does not assume knowledge of nonregistration, the serial-number law, a registration duty, or illegality was required.

All eight treat the recorded requested instruction and adverse ruling as preserving the characteristic-knowledge instruction claim under review. Counsel's local acceptance of inserting “knowingly” does not establish withdrawal of the separate later objection. The admissions are evidence, not an intentional relinquishment of jury determination. No blanket forfeiture, waiver, concession of guilt, or litigation default is found. If a distinct claim is later shown to be forfeited, its own Rule 52(b) predicates would govern; that possibility supplies no present judgment ground.

## 4. Material alternative paths and strongest objections

**Affirm for no instructional error.** The definition cross-reference and “knowingly possessed a ‘firearm’” language support an argument that the jury had to find factual knowledge, while the later disclaimer addressed only registration-law knowledge. Estelle requires contextual reading; Staples distinguishes factual knowledge from law. The strongest objection is the subsequent separate enumeration of knowing possession and objective firearm status, the rejected requested instruction, and the limited count-three reproduction. No Associate commits to definitive no-error construction on these excerpts. The six affirming Associates instead assume the disputed omission; the two remanding Associates preserve lawful whole-charge consideration below. A genuinely supported no-error ground would not require an omitted-element holding.

**Affirm under qualified Chapman review.** The six affirming Associates rely on the relationship between the admissions, the actual charge, the possession dispute, and the verdict, not on an admission standing alone. The strongest objection is that later identification and familiarity do not logically entail offense-time feature knowledge, and a jury may reject one part of testimony without accepting another. Roy does not require logical entailment in every case. For the six, the direct trial connection supplies the beyond-reasonable-doubt no-contribution inference notwithstanding the distinct propositions. For Scalia and Ginsburg, that connection is not adequately established here; the proposed inference would move too much of the elemental determination to an appellate court. Neither side treats rejection of the presence defense as a concession by Rogers or as a universal feature-knowledge finding.

**Vacate for confined reassessment.** Scalia and Ginsburg identify a substantive defect in the lower admission-based harmlessness rationale and would allow proper assessment of the charge and any reliable actual-verdict connection. Their remedy does not rest on unavailable research files. The strongest objection is that the supported recitations already show a direct connection to the submitted possession issue, making a remand unnecessary. The six affirming Associates accept that objection and resolve harmlessness themselves. The dissenting two find a live legal task: whether the actual jury determination, in context, supplies the required connection; they do not demand record production, a new hearing, or repetition of an already adequate inquiry.

**DIG or decline the granted issue.** The unresolved statutory reach and competing readings of the charge make vehicle review legitimate, and Izumi preserves the Court's prudential authority. The strongest obstacle to dismissal here is that the lower court actually adjudicated the omitted-element/admission issue, and favorable assumptions allow a bounded decision within the reported grant. No independent filing defect, unpresented claim essential to relief, or verified disappearance of the question is established. All eight decline to convert source uncertainty into a dismissal ground. This commitment would require reconsideration if a later authorized source materially changed the actual grant or showed a genuine vehicle defect.

**Automatic reversal, or Rule 52(b) affirmance.** Automatic structural treatment conflicts with Roy's rejection of omission alone as sufficient and with Sullivan's distinct global reasonable-doubt defect. A plain-error shortcut lacks the relevant forfeiture predicate: the requested instruction and adverse ruling remain supported. Johnson neither resolves this preserved claim nor makes every powerful evidentiary record immune from correction. All eight reject both routes on the existing premises.

## 5. Precise remedies and reserved matters

**Six-Associate affirmative commitment:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer would affirm the Eleventh Circuit judgment insofar as it sustains Counts II and III. The ground is the assumed preserved omission's harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt in these circumstances. Affirmance requires no merits remand, new trial, acquittal, release, resentencing, or independent sentence calculation. It does not reinstate the machinegun count, declare the complete charge valid, decide a silencer constitutional claim, or endorse a universal harmlessness rule based on admission.

**Two-Associate alternative:** Scalia and Ginsburg would vacate the affirmance of Counts II and III and remand to the Eleventh Circuit. That court may consider the complete charge under the applicable contextual standard and any properly available, record-supported statutory ground without treating this opinion's favorable assumptions as new statutory holdings. If an actual preserved constitutional omission remains, the Government must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt through a reliable connection to the actual verdict, consistent with Roy. A proper no-error or harmlessness determination may sustain the convictions. If a preserved violation exists and the Government fails that showing, the affected conviction must be set aside, with lawful retrial available; the Supreme Court does not presently order retrial or acquittal. No habeas-style conditional-writ deadline, automatic hearing, release order, or new sentencing calculation is imposed. The reversed machinegun count stays reversed and beyond remand.

No present disposition adjudicates a new sufficiency claim, invalidates the statutes, decides the exact outer scope of §5845's definitions, resolves every possible objection, or establishes that all admissions have the same procedural or evidentiary effect.
