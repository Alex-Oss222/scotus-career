## Common current law and prior writings

**Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects.** The exact registered standard is effective through *Mussari/Schroeder*, June 30, 1997; its architecture is *Lopez*, April 26, 1995, and its anti-evasion branch is *Hale*, February 12, 1996. Identify the enacted class and constitutionally eligible interstate mechanism before aggregating its magnitude. A reasonable, judicially identifiable fit must connect class and mechanism. Congressional predictive judgments receive latitude within an eligible mechanism. Generalized consequences through crime, health, insurance, productivity, or public expenditure cannot create the missing connection. Jurisdictional words alone are no safe harbor, and a court cannot insert an omitted element. *Harris* and *Bishop/Stokes* concern direct protection of a covered interstate instrumentality against forcible taking with an enacted vehicle-specific nexus; *Pic-A-State* concerns an interstate commercial transmission; neither erases another statute's actual elements. *Rybar* (November 24, 1997) and *Kirk* (February 14, 1998) apply the machinegun regime's material-impairment rationale, not generic dangerousness or marketability. *Parker* (December 1) retains proof of the actual cross-border support relationship without inventing travel or payment elements. *Robinson* (March 23) supplies the actual-business depletion setting of the Hobbs Act. Their exact public passages are copied into the slice.

*American Life League v. Reno*, November 13, 1995, is especially important. Kennedy's unanimous Part I sustains targeted force, threats, obstruction, and facility destruction intentionally directed at and directly interfering with the interstate reproductive-services market. It requires no separate interstate trip by each actor and does not rest on national crime costs. It leaves the alternative § 5 and persons-in-commerce theories unresolved. These are current holdings, including for Justices who might have preferred different historical Commerce doctrine.

**Lemon test.** *Agostini*, June 23, 1997, retains Lemon as one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function. A specific prohibition or distinct doctrine resolves a covered case; an established permissible relationship resolves a covered relationship; otherwise attribution and function are assessed through ordinary procedure. Purpose, effects, and entanglement are evidence within this inquiry, not three independent or fallback prongs. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas joined the controlling modification. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer dissented. *Tanford*, December 22, applies the specific-prohibition route to officially arranged university commencement prayer; Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas disagreed with that application, not by restoring an obsolete test. Neutral protection of religious property must be analyzed within the current framework.

**Habeas and evidence.** Exact registered standards included in the slice are **Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims** (*Schlup*, January 23, 1995), **Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility** (*Kyles*, April 19, 1995), **Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar** (*Felker*, June 28, 1996), **Federal-prisoner limitation and successive-motion certification**, **Second or successive state-custody habeas applications**, **Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments** (*Lindh*, June 23, 1997), **No delay-based substitute for statutory habeas dismissal**, **Interim protection necessary for required habeas process**, and **Equitable delay independently of a habeas procedural bar** (*Lonchar*, April 1, 1996). The slice contains each complete trigger, qualification, and authority rather than a name-only list.

*Schlup* requires reliable new evidence assessed with the whole probative record and a showing that, more likely than not, no reasonable properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. It requires more than doubt or ordinary prejudice, but is a gateway, not an affirmative adjudication of innocence. Evidence is not categorically excluded because it is impeachment, comes from a prisoner, or would be inadmissible at trial; reliability and the full record remain essential. Stevens wrote the controlling gateway holding, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer among the Associates. Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas preferred a stricter quantum; that objection is a prior position, not current law.

*Herrera*, January 25, 1993, establishes that its particular showing was insufficient; it establishes neither a general freestanding innocence threshold nor a controlling categorical denial of a constitutional right. O'Connor and Kennedy explained why execution of an established innocent person would be intolerable. Stevens and Souter joined Blackmun's legal position supporting a probable-innocence inquiry and remand. Scalia and Thomas's categorical objection did not command the Court.

*Kyles* assesses the cumulative lawful uses of favorable suppressed evidence for a reasonable probability of a different result. It does not require proof of innocence or item-by-item materiality. The prosecution must learn of favorable evidence known to police acting on its case team; it has no categorical duty to search every government office. The preserved distinction for evidence independently known to the defense cannot be erased. Once material Brady suppression is established, a second harmless-error screen cannot defeat relief. Souter's controlling opinion was joined by O'Connor, Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer among the Associates. Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas accepted cumulative materiality but disputed its application. The registered **Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review** has its own scope; historical broad descriptions of *Brecht* cannot displace the actual simulated rule or *Kyles*'s claim-specific consequence.

*Felker* preserves original habeas despite the authorization-review bar but does not permit relabeling barred appellate review. Rule 20 requires exceptional circumstances, no adequate relief elsewhere, relevant exhaustion, and an explanation for not applying to the district court. Section 2244(b)'s restrictions inform original-writ discretion; their direct binding force in that channel remains reserved. Original jurisdiction alone supplies neither a writ nor a stay. *Triestman*, March 12, 1998, unanimously holds that § 2255's certification cross-reference incorporates § 2244(b)(3)(E)'s ordinary-certiorari bar; a petition presenting only refusal of certification cannot be converted into a different proceeding. It leaves the savings clause, substantive retroactivity, carry liability, and factual inquiry unresolved.

*Lindh*'s registered holding concerns pending noncapital chapter 153 applications and does not decide every Title I transition or automatically settle chapter 154 in a capital case. *Trest*, December 9, 1997, holds that courts need not raise an omitted nonjurisdictional default sua sponte; it does not hold that an asserted default is waived, or decide the full range of permissible sua-sponte practice.

## 1. In re Dorsainvil — April 2, 1998

**Vehicle and lower disposition.** The supplied matter concerns the Third Circuit's refusal to authorize a second § 2255 motion, 119 F.3d 245, No. 96-8074, July 23, 1997, amended August 15. The court denied certification without prejudice to a § 2241 application in the district of confinement. The existing record does not supply a judgment in such a § 2241 proceeding or an independently filed original habeas application. The event anchor does not itself establish an appellate vehicle that Congress barred.

**Material facts and dispute.** The first § 2255 proceedings, decided in 1993–94, raised ineffective-assistance and double-jeopardy claims before *Bailey/Robinson*. The § 924(c) evidence included a gun in an open paper bag beside the driver in a truck registered to Dorsainvil, with his wallet and papers nearby. An officer described reaching movements; no handling of the gun was shown. A later statutory decision restricting “use” creates the claim, but does not automatically eliminate “carry.” The majority found no newly discovered evidence and no new constitutional rule satisfying successive-motion certification. The Government conceded *Bailey* retroactivity below, argued that inadequacy of § 2255 meant practical inability to use that forum, and did not assert abuse of the writ there. Those positions do not establish a general waiver in an unfiled proceeding.

The majority recognized a narrow possible savings-clause route for a prisoner with no earlier opportunity to present an intervening statutory noncriminality claim. It left the charge, full record, and carry question for the proper district court. Stapleton concurred in denial but thought driving the truck with a loaded gun accessible to the driver established carrying under the indictment and use-or-carry charge. He emphasized the absence of a trial objection to the carry instruction and distinguished an erroneous use instruction from affirmative innocence.

**Decisive entering rule.** *Triestman* is effective before this date and supplies the same authorization-review problem. Ordinary certiorari review of this certification refusal is barred by § 2244(b)(3)(E) as incorporated in § 2255. A merits disposition cannot rest on treating the threshold as a discretionary preference or on the All Writs Act, which creates no jurisdiction. A genuinely separate original habeas application would be assessed under *Felker* and Rule 20; a § 2241 case would require its actual proper forum and savings-clause predicate. Neither is silently supplied.

**Supported paths and readiness.** The existing vehicle supports threshold disposition under current *Triestman*, leaving the lower court's separate-forum possibility as such. A merits ruling on the savings clause, retroactivity, or firearm charge requires an identified independently lawful vehicle and its actual record. That omission is material and is not a reconstructible filing formality. No writ, transfer, retrial, hearing, or acquittal follows merely from the claim's seriousness. The court below supplies competing arguments for any later properly presented merits issue; it does not make those issues present here.

## 2. United States v. Hinds — April 6, 1998

**Vehicle and actual record.** Review concerns the Fourth Circuit's unpublished October 15, 1997 affirmance, No. 97-4030, of a conviction under 18 U.S.C. § 844(i). Hinds waived a jury and stipulated the elements except the commerce connection. He and an accomplice burned Pleasant Ridge United Church of Christ in Greensboro. The church sent annual funds through its national denomination for missionaries, seminaries, and universities; employed one full-time and two part-time workers; bought out-of-state insurance for the full-time employee; and bought utilities, religious materials, and other goods. The opinion does not identify the origins of every purchase, rental of the premises, commercial sales, or another commercial use of the building. No exact offense date is supplied for choosing among penalty amendments.

The panel held that a rational factfinder could find the enacted commerce element, distinguishing legislative substantial-effects authority from the evidentiary burden in a particular prosecution. It rejected the Establishment objection because the arson law was religiously neutral and applied neutrally. The supplied citation to 74 F. Supp. 2d 637 is not the actual judgment under review; the official circuit opinion supplies it.

**Statutory distinctions.** Section 844(i) requires property “used in” interstate or foreign commerce or in an activity affecting it; its malicious-damage language and qualifications are reproduced in the statutory annex. The question is the use of the damaged property under the enacted words, not whether churches as an abstract category can affect the economy. *Russell*, 471 U.S. 858 (1985), concerns rental property as business property; *Bass*, 404 U.S. 336 (1971), supplies the need for a clear statutory basis before expanding federal criminal reach into traditional state jurisdiction. Later decisions do not enter.

The distinct § 247 is not the charged offense. Its 1994 text requires the religious-character damage or specified force/threat obstruction in subsection (a), plus the then-existing subsection (b) circumstances: interstate travel or use of a facility or instrumentality in interstate or foreign commerce, and, for an (a)(1) offense, damage exceeding $10,000. It also requires the Attorney General or designee's written notification. The July 3, 1996 amendments changed the commerce language and added a race-based branch, among other changes; both official editions are copied in the annex. Neither the older nor newer § 247 elements may be substituted for § 844(i), presumed satisfied, or treated as an implicit repeal without textual analysis.

**Current law and lawful paths.** Apply **Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects** and the actual statutory-use element separately. The Government can argue that the church's employment, interstate insurance, denominational payments, and purchases show the property used in a commerce-affecting activity; Hinds can argue that these are incidental inputs to a noncommercial religious function, not the active use Congress enacted. The presence of a jurisdictional element does not decide its meaning or constitutionality. If the statute does not reach the proven use, there is no need to resolve its outer constitutional limit. If it does, the actual interstate mechanism must satisfy the current Commerce standard; generalized arson costs cannot do that work.

For Establishment, apply **Lemon test** as modified in *Agostini*, together with the established permissibility of ordinary neutral police/fire protection reflected in *Everson*, 330 U.S. 1 (1947). Applying a general property-protection law is not automatically an establishment because the beneficiary worships; a disputed use limitation is not automatically a religious exemption. *Tanford*'s official governmental prayer is a materially different function. No old three-prong shortcut supplies the answer.

Affirmance requires the actual statute and constitutional ground; a statutory reversal must distinguish an erroneous legal standard from failure of the trial evidence to prove an element. *Burks*, 437 U.S. 1 (1978), and the current *Jacobson* remedy prevent retrial after a determination of evidentiary insufficiency. A change in statutory interpretation does not justify an unexplained generic remand. No historical Supreme Court disposition or future controlling construction is used.

**Readiness:** sufficient neutral record for statutory, Commerce, Establishment, and remedy analysis after independent validation and refresh of preceding current-term law. The omitted offense date matters only if a penalty/amendment question is introduced; it does not authorize importing § 247. No separate Supreme Court argument date, docket, or recusal is invented.

## 3. United States v. Bird — April 13, 1998

**Record and lower claims.** The Fifth Circuit affirmed on September 24, 1997, 124 F.3d 667, No. 95-20792. On December 13, 1994 Bird threw a bottle at Dr. Theodore Herring's car as Herring entered America's Women Clinic in Houston, broke the windshield, and made a death threat; no physical injury occurred. Herring was the only physician witness and a Texas resident. No evidence showed that this particular clinic had out-of-state patients, staff, or supplies. Bird was convicted by jury under § 248(a)(1). On September 14, 1995 the district court imposed one year's imprisonment, one year's supervised release, restitution of $820.67, and a $50 assessment. The condition requires remaining at least 1,000 feet from abortion clinics, specifically America's Women Clinic. It is not a personal stay-away order limited to Herring or his home. Prior trespass at a clinic and a physician's residence, together with resistance to earlier restrictions, supplied the lower court's protective rationale.

The appeal presented Commerce and alternative § 5 authority; an equal-protection objection to the own-minor parental exception; overbreadth; vagueness; the supervised-release condition; and the judgment's description “Blocking Entrance” despite a threats/force prosecution. The panel rejected standing for the parental-exception claim because Bird's conduct did not implicate the relevant relationship; rejected the speech/vagueness challenges; and held the judgment description did not obscure his actual conviction. It upheld supervision under § 3583(d). DeMoss dissented from the Commerce holding and rejected § 5 as an alternative; the majority did not reach § 5. Bird otherwise did not contest the facts establishing the charged conduct.

**Current precedent and component distinctions.** *American Life League* is not optional background. Its unanimous Commerce holding concerns this same enacted service-directed class; the absence of a separate crossing by Bird or a particular-clinic interstate shipment is not, by itself, a failure of that class-based theory. DeMoss's concern about transforming all crime into federal commerce jurisdiction is tested against the present direct-service-interference mechanism, not generic national cost aggregation. *Lopez*, *Hale*, *Mussari/Schroeder*, *Rybar*, *Kirk*, and *Robinson* retain their different predicates.

The exact standard **FACE conduct predicates and responsibility for civil relief** includes statutory terms, intent, service purpose, movement/bodily-harm/ingress/passage qualifications, and attribution limits. *American Life League*'s Kennedy Part II unanimously treats the defined conduct law as content-neutral and applies intermediate scrutiny; Part III-A unanimously rejects the facial overbreadth challenge; Part III-B's facial-vagueness rationale had all eight Associate Justices. These decisions do not establish the validity of every criminal sentencing condition. The own-minor parent/legal-guardian exception removes FACE penalties and civil remedies for its covered conduct; it neither authorizes other unlawful conduct nor creates a general parental veto. Preserve the standing problem before deciding Bird's exception argument on its merits.

*American Life League* also separates attribution, damages, religious targeting, *Smith*, and RFRA. Its unanimous attribution holding concerns responsibility for a defendant's own covered conduct and legally attributable acts, not automatic liability for a movement's activities. Its *Smith* rationale had Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer, with O'Connor and Souter reserving that ground. The RFRA remand had O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. None supplies an unasserted RFRA claim here. The exact entries are in the slice so no partial summary erases a pertinent qualification.

For the actual supervision condition, § 3583(d) requires reasonable relation to § 3553(a)(1) and (a)(2)(B)–(D), no greater liberty deprivation than reasonably necessary for deterrence, public protection, and treatment, and consistency with pertinent Sentencing Commission policy statements. The district court's discretion is bounded by those requirements. **Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction** (*Madsen*, June 30, 1994) supplies the more-demanding speech-fit rule for injunctions and an analogy concerning spatial burdens; it does not mechanically decide a criminal supervised-release condition or authorize a uniform 1,000-foot zone. The protective history and the breadth across all clinics must both be addressed.

**Live-status blocker and scope.** The recovered certiorari petition, No. 97-1270, appendix C, establishes that the Fifth Circuit issued its mandate November 13, 1997. It supplies the imposed sentence but no custody commencement, bail pending appeal, release date, tolling, or actual supervision termination. It contains no evidence resolving whether the condition remained in force on April 13, 1998. Counting two years from sentencing is not a factual finding. Modeling the supervision component is not ready until an authorized source or bounded factual clarification establishes a live burden or the relevant mootness posture. This limitation does not itself erase the conviction controversy or establish an exception to mootness.

The historical petition principally asks Commerce, § 5, and abortion-right questions and describes the supervision condition as not pertinent there. The supplied new-run matter includes supervision. Its authorized scope must therefore remain explicit; do not invent a historical grant covering that condition. Other actual lower claims must not disappear merely because the supplied focus emphasizes Commerce and supervision. A demand to reconsider *Roe* does not itself displace the existing *Casey* law or create a need to decide a constitutional question unnecessary to a valid Commerce ground.

**Supported paths:** affirm or reverse the conviction through the actual statutory and constitutional questions; decide standing before the own-minor exception's merits; if live supervision is established, review the imposed condition under § 3583(d), its actual breadth, and the applicable speech analysis, with any modification/remand tied to the legal defect. The live-status omission prevents an unconditional merits disposition of supervision at this preflight stage.

## 5. Carriger v. Stewart — April 16, 1998

**Actual writ and posture.** The Ninth Circuit's en banc December 17, 1997 judgment, 132 F.3d 463, No. 95-99025, vacated its panel opinion, reversed the district court, and remanded with instructions to grant the writ; its explanation calls for a new trial. It did not order an acquittal or merely direct an evidentiary hearing, and it did not specify a fixed retrial deadline. Carriger's operative federal petition was filed in August 1995, before AEDPA; it was a capital proceeding. The lower court used former § 2254(d). Current *Lindh*'s noncapital holding must not be expanded into a universal transition rule to eliminate the need to identify the applicable provision.

Carriger was convicted of the 1978 murder/robbery and sentenced to death, with later resentencing. His present petition followed earlier federal/state proceedings and a 1993 state petition containing the later confession and Brady allegations. The district court rejected the claims as abuse of the writ. The en banc court separated three questions: an assumed freestanding innocence claim failed because the showing undermined the State's case without affirmatively proving innocence at the very high assumed level; the *Schlup* gateway was satisfied; and the suppressed evidence established a material *Brady* violation requiring relief. Those are not interchangeable standards.

**Evidence and competing readings.** Robert Dunbar was the central prosecution witness. He confessed to the crime in 1987, implicating his wife Joyce, recanted about three weeks later, and died in 1991; a later cellmate reported a further confession. The state court's 1988 findings credited his trial testimony and rejected the confession. Former § 2254(d)(8) requires the actual inquiry whether that factual finding is fairly supported by the record as a whole, not automatic displacement whenever later evidence exists. The majority found that support lacking; the dissent defended the state finding and emphasized countervailing evidence.

The withheld corrections file contained a history of lying, shifting blame, violence, and armed burglaries contradicting the prosecution's portrayal. It was more than the six known burglary felonies and a disclosed false gun-form statement. The majority considered the file's cross-examination, investigation, and preparation uses cumulatively with the confession evidence and weaknesses in corroboration. The dissent stressed the defense's knowledge of the file's existence, including an April 18, 1978 letter asking counsel to obtain it, and denied that Brady required the prosecutor to retrieve independently accessible evidence. Knowledge that a file exists is not necessarily knowledge of its material contents, but current *Kyles* also does not impose an unlimited duty to search every government repository.

A fingerprint on adhesive tape is material disputed corroboration. The State's later expert offer placed the print 21.5 inches from the tape's end. The majority discounted the offer because it was not an admitted trial demonstration, the roll was not established as the same roll, and defense inspection had not occurred. The dissent emphasized its probative force and the circumstances of the defense's withdrawal of its challenge. Neither version may be omitted when considering the whole record. Joyce and the children's statements, the timing and detail of confession/recantation, immunity or lack of immunity, and the possibility of more than one perpetrator also matter. Discrediting Dunbar alone does not establish that Carriger could not have participated in a felony murder.

**Controlling law and supported paths.** Apply the exact **Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims** to all reliable new and old evidence, including impeachment and inculpatory evidence; respect its no-reasonable-juror formulation without converting it into affirmative innocence. Apply the applicable former § 2254 factual-deference provision to the specific state findings, with the record-as-a-whole qualification. Apply **Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility** separately: favorability, suppression attributable to the prosecution under the actual case-team rule, and cumulative materiality. Identify who held the file and that office's relation to the case before treating *Kyles* as a rule requiring retrieval. Assess whether the defense independently knew the relevant evidence, not merely whether counsel could have investigated harder. Do not impose an innocence requirement on Brady or a second harmlessness screen after material suppression is established.

The competing legally available outcomes include sustaining a writ/new-trial remedy if both the procedural gateway and independent Brady entitlement are established; reversing if the gate or merits showing fails under the correct rules; or remanding for application of a materially misapplied legal standard or genuinely necessary unresolved factual determination. A remand must identify that work, not merely announce that innocence warrants a hearing. The lower disposition is relief on Brady after a gateway, not a freestanding innocence writ. Its alternative mitigation, trial/sentencing-competence, and resentencing-counsel issues were not reached; they cannot be silently adjudicated by a disposition confined to the controlling grounds.
