## 1. Statutory authority and limits

The exact operative texts below were copied by script from the candidate and compared with the complete supplied official GPO texts and amendment histories. The 2014 editions of §§1251 and 1651 show their last amendments as 1978 and 1949 respectively. The 2005 edition of §2251 retains the quoted 1948 text without an intervening amendment. The publication editions are retrieval sources for unchanged eligible language. Historical revision notes describing superseded statutory arrangements are not the operative text.

- Section 1251(b)(1) supplies original **but not exclusive** jurisdiction for the defined diplomatic and consular party class. It does not itself settle whether the pleaded action fits Article III, immunity, a domestic cause of action, discretionary leave or the requested interim remedy.
- Section 1651(a) authorizes writs necessary or appropriate **in aid of** independently held jurisdiction and agreeable to the usages and principles of law. It does not supply a freestanding treaty-enforcement jurisdiction or cure a missing substantive right. Its subsection (b) concerns a justice or judge of a court that has jurisdiction.
- Section 2251 is discretionary in its text. Its temporal terms are before final judgment, after final judgment **of discharge**, or pending appeal; its covered state proceeding must concern the detained person and a matter involved in the pending habeas proceeding. A granted stay invalidates covered state proceedings; without one, filing leaves them valid. Current Lonchar's distinct conditional protection for process actually required remains applicable on its own predicates, not because filing automatically stays execution.

## partIV-chap81-sec1251

§1251. Original jurisdiction






(a) The Supreme Court shall have original and exclusive jurisdiction of all controversies between two or more States.


(b) The Supreme Court shall have original but not exclusive jurisdiction of:


(1) All actions or proceedings to which ambassadors, other public ministers, consuls, or vice consuls of foreign states are parties;


(2) All controversies between the United States and a State;


(3) All actions or proceedings by a State against the citizens of another State or against aliens.






(June 25, 1948, ch. 646, 62 Stat. 927; Pub. L. 95–393, §8(b), Sept. 30, 1978, 92 Stat. 810.)










## partV-chap111-sec1651

§1651. Writs






(a) The Supreme Court and all courts established by Act of Congress may issue all writs necessary or appropriate in aid of their respective jurisdictions and agreeable to the usages and principles of law.


(b) An alternative writ or rule nisi may be issued by a justice or judge of a court which has jurisdiction.






(June 25, 1948, ch. 646, 62 Stat. 944; May 24, 1949, ch. 139, §90, 63 Stat. 102.)










## partVI-chap153-sec2251

§2251. Stay of State court proceedings






A justice or judge of the United States before whom a habeas corpus proceeding is pending, may, before final judgment or after final judgment of discharge, or pending appeal, stay any proceeding against the person detained in any State court or by or under the authority of any State for any matter involved in the habeas corpus proceeding.


After the granting of such a stay, any such proceeding in any State court or by or under the authority of any State shall be void. If no stay is granted, any such proceeding shall be as valid as if no habeas corpus proceedings or appeal were pending.






(June 25, 1948, ch. 646, 62 Stat. 966.)

## 3. A-738: affirmative restraint and competing standards

**Applicant's arguments, not findings:** Paraguay addresses affirmative injunctive protection as an alternative to a stay. It argues that the distinction between suspending a judicial alteration and granting intervention withheld below principally limits a single Circuit Justice, and should not determine the full Court's standard. It alternatively claims satisfaction even of the more demanding formulation: an injunction necessary or appropriate to preserve this Court's jurisdiction and legal rights sufficiently clear to justify extraordinary relief. It invokes the threatened execution's effect on its pending case, the conceded notice violation and its merits arguments. Its assertions that the whole action would become moot and that the relevant enforceable rights are clear are contentions, not established jurisdictional or merits facts. A concession about notice does not concede the requested remedy, a continuing violation or a domestic right to prevent execution.

Paraguay further proposes one review-prospect, reversal-prospect and irreparable-harm standard for orders preserving the status quo, with the lower judgments receiving their appropriate weight in that inquiry. It contends that relief meeting the applicable review-and-reversal showing would be appropriate in aid of jurisdiction under §1651. The proposed uniformity is an argument; referral does not silently settle it or supply jurisdiction.

**Eligible primary support checked independently:**

- *Ohio Citizens for Responsible Energy, Inc. v. NRC*, 479 U.S. 1312, 1313–1314 (December 31, 1986), is Justice Scalia's individual in-chambers opinion. It distinguishes a §2101(f) stay of enforcement of a final judgment from affirmative intervention withheld below. For the Circuit Justice's original injunction, it requires critical and exigent circumstances, clear legal entitlement and necessity or appropriateness in aid of the Court's jurisdiction; it declined to treat a request as seeking that relief where counsel had neither requested nor addressed it. That last procedural fact is not present by inference here: A-738 expressly addresses injunction relief. Its former Rule 44.1 reference is not a substitute for the supplied 1995 Rules. Full official opinion: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-479/pdf/USREPORTS-479-1312.pdf . Cross-checked against https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/479/1312 .
- *Lucas v. Townsend*, 486 U.S. 1301, 1304–1305 (May 30, 1988), is Justice Kennedy's individual in-chambers opinion granting an election injunction pending timely docketing of an appeal. It examined prospects for four votes to accept review, five to find error below, irreparable harm and, as appropriate, competing equities. Its application involved an identified statutory preclearance obligation and a likely reversible lower-court error. This supplies an eligible example of affirmative equitable relief using that inquiry; it is not a full-Court holding establishing a universal treaty-injunction standard. Full official opinion: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-486/pdf/USREPORTS-486-1301.pdf . Cross-checked against https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/486/1301 .
