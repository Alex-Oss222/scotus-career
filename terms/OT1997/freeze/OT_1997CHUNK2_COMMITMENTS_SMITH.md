Section 1254(1) permits review of the case in the federal court of appeals. The circuit's underlying §1291 authority remains limited to the separable legal immunity question. Mattox presents that question: whether the accepted plaintiff-favorable conduct was clearly established as unlawful on June 11, 1993. Disputed evidence does not eliminate that legal jurisdiction. All nine seats are occupied; no case-specific nonparticipation is supplied. The eight Associates modeled are Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The full participating Court has a quorum and requires five votes for judgment. No Supreme Court docket, grant order, oral-argument date, reargument, or recusal is invented. This handoff supplies no Chief Justice commitment or final Court count.

The operative account includes the whole encounter: Smith threatened with the bat, initially refused to drop it, fled after dropping it, and feigned renewed flight before submitting to the direction to get down. Mattox could reasonably retain concern about renewed danger or flight and use some restraining force. Smith nevertheless was entirely docile and unresisting during the challenged cuffing maneuver. With a knee on Smith's lower back, Mattox pulled the left arm behind him; after Smith complained of discomfort, the described grunt and blow accompanied force that fractured the arm in several places and required surgery. The account reports no sign of anger. It does not establish completed handcuffing, a long interval of peaceful custody, that every possibility of danger had disappeared, an intent to break the arm, or an exact amount or duration of applied force.

Mattox's testimony that Smith continued resisting and reached for his gun remains disputed. The Court does not decide credibility, evidentiary sufficiency, actual mechanics, or what ultimately occurred. The legal application takes the proper nonmovant-favorable assumptions and permissible inferences as supplied; it does not independently adjudicate them.

The amended complaint did not allege or hint at a warrantless-entry or no-probable-cause arrest claim. A general Fourth Amendment reference does not permit revival of those theories or make every use of force unlawful. The judgments for the city and chief are outside this appeal. Graham supplies the arrest-force standard; no separate substantive-due-process, prisoner-force, prosecutor-immunity, municipal, or supervisory holding is needed.

**P1 — Review and assumptions.** The separable clearly-established-law question is reviewable on the properly assumed nonmovant account. **P2 — Source, timing, and degree of clarity.** The asserted arrest force is governed by Graham's objective Fourth Amendment rule. Harlow and Anderson require that the particular unlawfulness have been apparent under law existing on June 11, 1993. An obviously covered application may satisfy that requirement without a previously reported factual twin, while an abstract prohibition or later doctrinal enlargement cannot. Neither subjective malice nor its absence controls; neither a severe injury alone nor a rule forbidding every use of force after submission is adopted.

**P3 — This assumed application defeats the legal immunity defense.** On the complete assumed account, Graham made the considerable force embodied in the described blow and arm manipulation against the wholly unresisting arrestee apparently unlawful by June 11, 1993. The earlier bat threat, flight, feigned flight, and unfinished handcuffing justified control and caution; they did not leave reasonable uncertainty about the permissibility of this substantial additional force when Smith was offering no resistance. The inference concerns the degree and circumstances of force described, supported together by the maneuver, blow, and injury, rather than a rule that a fracture proves wrongdoing. The application is objective and does not rest on an unproved purpose to punish or injure. It rejects the asserted legal defense on those assumptions, without finding that they are true or imposing liability.

**P4 — Preserved scope.** The Court does not entertain an unpleaded search or unlawful-arrest claim, treat excessive-force damages as damages for such a claim, or reopen the city/chief judgments. The Fourth Amendment force ground suffices; no distinct Fourteenth Amendment or other constitutional ground is decided.

**P5 — Disposition and remaining adjudication.** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit's judgment sustaining denial of Mattox's qualified-immunity summary judgment on the presented legal issue. Ordinary further proceedings remain available. Mattox may contest the operative facts and seek any lawful later immunity determination on facts properly established. The judgment establishes no damages, punitive entitlement, final liability, compulsory trial, special interrogatory, new evidentiary hearing, particular discovery plan, or automatic return to this Court.
