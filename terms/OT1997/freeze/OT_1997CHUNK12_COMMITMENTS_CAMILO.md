## Conduct-date notice and opposed paths

The notice inquiry concerns each challenged decision or omission before the May 13, 1994 injury. Gutierrez-Rodriguez v. Cartagena, 882 F.2d 553, 560–66 (1st Cir. 1989), supplies especially concrete preconduct notice: personal reckless or deliberate indifference to known violent police conduct, effective assignment or disciplinary authority and an affirmative causal connection could establish individual §1983 liability. It affirmed liability after trial, not an independent qualified-immunity determination. Its causation discussion allows responsibility for initiating a foreseeably injurious chain without personally inflicting the injury; its actual supervisory defendants possessed assignment or disciplinary powers. That distinction must remain explicit when applying the general causation principle to professional advice.

Febus-Rodriguez v. Betancourt-Lebron, 14 F.3d 87, 92–95 (1st Cir. January 18, 1994), supplies the limiting comparison. Unrelated complaints, ordinary training criticism and a tenuous theory that nonpunishment encouraged a later assault did not establish those supervisors' own constitutional wrongdoing. It did not independently immunize otherwise established knowing misconduct because the law was unclear. Its actual-or-constructive formulation remains relevant earlier authority; the narrower present account does not require adjudicating its full reach. Febus predates the January 20 threat, January 26 transfer, February 28 recertification and later acts. It cannot supply notice for an earlier January act of uncertain date or any completed 1993 act. Gutierrez supplies the earlier baseline. A month-only reference does not establish post-January-18 receipt or response, and no continuing duty is invented.

Anderson, 483 U.S. at 639–41, requires particularized apparent unlawfulness without an identical prior act. Graham, 490 U.S. at 394–97, supplies the underlying arrest-force rule. Martinez distinguishes a State's defense to its own tort law from federal immunity; its remoteness analysis concerned a parolee who was not a state agent, unlike the alleged continuing exercise of police authority here. Malley's existing rejection of automatic insulation from an intervening official decision informs, but does not prove, the claimed causal chain. These rules do not make every professionally mistaken certification a federal tort.

Farmer, Doe, Johnson, Behrens, Richardson, Lanier, Smith, DeCarlo, Ringuette, Chipman, Diamondstone and Bauchman govern current analysis only within their actual scopes. Their later dates create no earlier notice. Elder's February 23, 1994 all-relevant-law method governs review; no defendant is charged with a later decision before the actual challenged act. Farmer's prison standard and Doe's school rule are not mechanically transplanted. Title VII employer attribution in Ellerth and ADA risk assessment in Bragdon supply neither §1983 personal responsibility nor professional immunity. Monge supplies no civil proof-repair rule.

**Strongest opposed lawful path:** Grant individualized immunity because a professional assessment, advisory participation, remote command relationship or reasonable expert reliance was not clearly prohibited in these precise circumstances. Anderson, Febus, and the present DeCarlo/Ringuette applications require serious consideration of that path. Its strongest support is the absence of final rearming power for the doctors, Diaz-Pagan's limited immediate command and absence, and the possibility of reasonable medical evaluation or reliance. Its barrier on the adopted assumptions is that it replaces knowing, causally effective disregard of a repeatedly demonstrated danger with a reasonable or merely negligent response. A factual twin or proof that an appellant ordered this particular beating is not required. The three officials' powers closely match Gutierrez's concrete assignment/disciplinary setting; the doctors' distinct affirmative certification must satisfy the causation principle independently.

**Strongest claimant path and its limit:** Concrete notice of repeated violent abuse combined with each person's own authority or effective certification makes the alleged knowing facilitation of further police coercion apparently unlawful. Its strongest counterargument is that foreseeability and professional imperfection alone are insufficient. The narrow holding proceeds on actual awareness, culpable response and an affirmative causal connection, leaving broader constructive-notice sufficiency open and preserving reasonable professional judgment and reliance. It establishes no compulsory checklist, constitutional duty to obtain a particular report, general duty to rescue, or strict prohibition against returning any person with mental illness to police work.

**Narrower alternatives:** Assume contractor eligibility rather than decide the Richardson reservation; resolve the state/federal immunity distinction without construing unresolved Puerto Rico coverage; use the supported arrest-force claim without a new substantive-due-process holding; preserve actual factual disputes rather than decide their sufficiency. A clarification remand would be necessary if the court could not identify material assumed facts, but the validated packet identifies them. An automatic trial, blanket liability finding or universal supervisor/contractor immunity rule is not a lawful substitute.
