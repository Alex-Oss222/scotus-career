## Judgment and review scope

All eight Associate Justices would **vacate the Eighth Circuit's affirmance of final summary judgment and remand for ordinary, claim-specific reconsideration of the discovery request and the resulting summary-judgment record**. They would reject the offered categorical bars based on the internal judicial EEO plan and absolute judicial immunity. This is not a finding of discrimination, a directive to appoint Duffy, a guarantee of any deposition or trial, or a grant of damages.

Review under §1254(1) reaches an actual final judgment, including the Rule 56(f) ruling. Johnson/Behrens's limits on factual issues in an interlocutory immunity appeal do not restrict this channel. Wolle, Vietor and Longstaff defend their favorable final judgment; Duffy seeks further proceedings. The three defendants' participation and motives must be evaluated individually; the hiring panel's joint action does not establish equal knowledge or discriminatory purpose.

## Component 1: The internal plan does not itself displace this individual action

**Operative rule:** The existence of the Judicial Conference's internal EEO procedure, without a supported congressional remedial design or another sufficient special factor displacing this received action, does not alone bar an individual Fifth Amendment employment-discrimination damages claim of the Davis v. Passman kind. Davis supplies the established individual action and constitutional employment protection; Bush requires serious attention to congressional remedial design, not a presumption that every administrative procedure either bars or preserves every constitutional action.

The actual claim concerns the judges' personal administrative selection decision, not a direct action against the federal court as an agency. The actual Meyer holding rejects a direct federal-agency Bivens extension while expressly preserving received individual Davis/Bivens/Carlson actions at their own scope. The internal plan's administrative origin and its mere availability do not establish that Congress replaced this individual remedy. The lower opinion supplies no congressional direction or sufficiently defined alternative structure that compels the proposed bar. No modern Bivens restriction, universal agency analogy or rule that only an express statutory sentence can ever count as a special factor is adopted.

ALPA does not make nonconsent an exception to every administrative requirement. It distinguishes a private union procedure from statutory/institutional allocation and preserves proper administrative exhaustion and case management. The asserted Duffy defense is preclusion of the individual action because an internal plan exists; no well-supported separate mandatory-exhaustion rule or adequate-plan predicate is established by that contention alone. Stevens and Breyer's ALPA initial-recourse position concerns a specific adequate, prompt and nonbinding union procedure; it does not establish permanent displacement of a Davis claim by an unspecified internal plan. This decision need not prohibit lawful, properly justified case management or decide every possible exhaustion claim concerning a different record.

The Court does not turn Title VII's exclusion into statutory coverage, imply a new action against the judiciary as an institution, adjudicate the plan's complete terms, or establish every requested equitable remedy. A prayer for appointment or declaratory relief is not an entitlement to it.

## Component 2: Personnel selection is administrative, not absolutely judicial

**Operative rule:** Selecting the chief probation officer is an administrative personnel function rather than an adjudication between parties. Absolute judicial immunity therefore does not protect this selection merely because district judges possess the statutory appointment authority, the office is important to judicial operations, or the appointment was entered in an order.

Forrester supplies the functional distinction for a judge's personnel decision involving a probation officer. Antoine requires the immunity claimant to establish the historically and functionally justified protected act; importance to court operation does not suffice. Bogan distinguishes prospective departmental legislation from an individualized administrative employment act. None makes the judge's status or the document's form dispositive. The distinctive responsibilities of a chief probation officer matter to qualifications but do not turn hiring into resolving a judicial controversy.

Rejecting absolute immunity does not reject every personal defense or establish a constitutional violation. Harlow/Anderson remains governing law; the alleged recruitment and selection occurred in 1993–1994. Later DeCarlo and the other current decisions guide present review but supply no retrospective notice. Davis and Forrester predate the conduct and cannot be replaced by the later decisions' dates. No categorical ground that judges were free to use sex as an arbitrary selection criterion is sustained; the actual proof of an unlawful criterion and each defendant's responsibility remain open.

## Component 3: Reconsider the premature final disposition under ordinary proof and discovery rules

**Operative rule:** When a plaintiff supplies specific nonconclusory evidence relevant to discriminatory selection and a timely Rule 56(f) explanation that essential information concerning the decisionmakers' stated reasons is in their control, the court must assess the requested opportunity to obtain that information under ordinary procedural standards. It may limit or deny an inadequately supported request, but may not treat the defendants' favorable affidavits, judicial status or the existence of plausible lawful qualifications as sufficient by themselves to foreclose material inquiry. Neither automatic discovery nor proof of discriminatory intent before necessary discovery is the rule.

Crawford-El preserves ordinary competent direct or circumstantial evidence, specific nonconclusory support, dispositive legal defenses, tailoring and ordinary summary judgment. It rejects a categorical demand for the unavailable motive proof as a precondition to discovery needed to obtain it. Its express limit against a general credibility attack remains. Hicks requires proof of actual discrimination and rejects automatic liability when explanations are disbelieved; it also rejects a mandatory additional category of direct or independent proof. Burdine's ultimate persuasion allocation is unchanged. DeCarlo requires the actual record and procedural stage, rather than converting a different ruling or an official's position into established liability or defense. None imposes an identical burden framework on every Title VII, constitutional and retaliation claim.

The qualifications offered for McPhillips are substantial: longer probation experience, larger-district assignments, national-office work, a law degree, strong references and interview assessments. Duffy's different strengths and his outgoing supervisor's recommendation do not entitle him to the position or establish that the chosen candidate was less qualified. The Court does not reweigh those attributes as a personnel department. They establish legitimate possible reasons and the production of a nondiscriminatory explanation; they do not by themselves establish that sex played no role.

The important contrary evidence is not simply Duffy's dissatisfaction. Ailts's affidavit recounts Wolle reporting an interest in the **appointment of a female**, while Wolle describes advice to advertise nationally for an open diverse pool. Ailts's uncertainty about the source and his inference about the position limit the statement's force. Those limits do not permit the plaintiff-favorable account to be recast conclusively as recruitment alone. Inclusive recruitment is different from discriminatory selection. The statement, the actual selection context and the request to test the stated reasons provide a sufficiently concrete basis to require a genuine Rule 56(f) assessment rather than reliance solely on defendants' denials. The Court finds neither that the statement expressed the panel's actual policy nor that any particular judge adopted it.

Duffy also identified the law-clerk hiring pattern of two judges. Different positions, applicant pools, periods and qualifications limit its possible inference; the supplied counts establish neither a discriminatory pattern nor a basis for discovery into all judicial employment. It may be assessed for actual relevance and adequate foundation. The fact that the defendants themselves are men does not establish that discrimination against a man is too extraordinary to examine or supply a special evidentiary presumption in their favor. No particular additional law-clerk discovery is ordered.

Counsel submitted a Rule 56(f) affidavit expressly requesting an opportunity to depose the defendants to test their explanations. The district court reasoned that discovery would not help because affidavits from defendants and screening members supported defendants. The circuit reinforced that treatment with the judges' status and its characterization of the supporting allegations. The recorded request and concrete statement require reconsideration under the proper standards. The court may require clarification of the material facts sought, use written discovery, narrow subjects, protect unrelated judicial work, sequence proceedings or decide an actually dispositive legal issue first. It must distinguish a general wish to impeach credibility from the identified factual issue in this record. The remand orders no particular deposition, discovery plan or automatic evidentiary hearing.

The Court does not decide that the current record necessarily requires a trial once the discovery question is properly treated. A renewed summary-judgment ruling may be proper on the actual record after lawful consideration of the request. Any ruling must address each defendant's participation, knowledge and motive without collective attribution or a special quantum of proof.

## Enhanced alternative-path conclusions

**Affirmance path:** The defendants produced substantial objective qualifications and strong references; the reported outside interest is ambiguous, inclusive recruitment is lawful, the law-clerk comparison is weak, and counsel's request could be read as a general search for impeachment. Crawford-El itself permits summary judgment and rejects a bare credibility attack. Those points may ultimately defeat the claim and justify significant discovery limits.

**Selected vacatur path:** The record contains a specific statement about appointment rather than only general recruitment, and the court denied access to material reasons principally controlled by defendants because their affidavits supported themselves. A sufficient production of lawful reasons does not settle motive. The selected disposition corrects that legal/procedural assessment without adjudicating either candidate's superiority, the statement's truth, discriminatory selection or the precise discovery entitlement. On that limited ground, the affirmance path would give conclusive weight to the defense explanation before the supported request was lawfully assessed.

**Alternative threshold path:** Institutional importance and the EEO plan could counsel caution. Forrester/Antoine/Bogan nevertheless foreclose status-based absolute immunity for this administrative act, and Meyer preserves the received individual Davis action. The record supplies no actual congressional design or plan term sufficient to displace that action. Rejecting those defenses is limited to the presented grounds and does not announce immunity from all future legislative or administrative remedial choices.
