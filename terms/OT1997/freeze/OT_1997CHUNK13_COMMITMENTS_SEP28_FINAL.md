## 156. Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue

### Entering law and judgment components

The neutral record is the full Washington opinion, 132 Wash.2d 103, with the corrections in the preparation receipt. The state court's narrowing constructions are part of the ordinance reviewed. Barnes, 501 U.S. 560, is pre-divergence authority with distinct plural and concurring rationales. O'Brien, Ward, Renton, Erznoznik, FW/PBS, Michigan v. Long, Maher, Hensley and Christiansburg retain their own scopes. The opening Madsen and Schenck holdings require provision-specific attention to a speech burden; they do not convert a generally applicable legislative restriction into a contempt injunction. Their protection against suppression of images merely because viewers dislike their message is particularly relevant. Farrar's controlling simulated eligibility/amount distinction and National Private Truck Council's qualified state-success fee rule govern §1988; historical shorthand inconsistent with those holdings is not adopted.

The operative Standards entry is **Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction**, present operation effective Schenck, February 19, 1997, retaining Madsen's June 30, 1994 standard and its distinction from ordinary legislative regulation. It supplies the distinction and the preserved principle, not the operative scrutiny for every ordinance. **Nominal civil-rights relief and reasonable fees**, effective Farrar, December 14, 1992, governs eligibility separately from reasonable amount. No nominal damages are awarded here.

Component A — Reviewability: reach adverse federal questions under the scheduled merits review; leave the independently sufficient Washington manager-license invalidation untouched. No opinion that federal law requires fourteen days of restraint, no restoration of the invalid state-law provision, and no correction of state-law privacy or state procedural rulings merely because the Court would have interpreted them differently. The unresolved fee accounting is collateral to the determined federal merits. Distinguish this from Jefferson's unresolved underlying merits.

Component B — Operational restrictions: affirm the federal rejection of the four-foot, eight-foot stage, qualified lighting and 2 a.m.–10 a.m. hours challenges. Treat the expressive performance as protected and test the restrictions against O'Brien/Ward rather than adopting the lower court's rational-basis-only account of stage dancing. Actual contact incidents, visibility difficulties and the late-night patron-flow problem supply substantial conduct interests. Four feet is between the dancer and that dancer's customer, not outstretched fingertips; stage elevation is eighteen inches; thirty lux is measured horizontally thirty inches above the floor on ten-foot centers. The remaining performance and sixteen-hour daily operation are meaningful channels on this record. The First Amendment guarantees no particular profit margin, but a proved elimination of practical expressive channels would matter; no such finding is made here.

Component C — Overbreadth and ancillary claims: sustain the definition and dance-distance provisions against the presented substantial-overbreadth challenge at the state court's limiting constructions. Preserve the nonobscene dramatic-work exception and the state's confined obscenity construction of A(5); do not transfer that exception automatically to B(2). Leave state-law information-disclosure, postjudgment evidence and amendment dispositions outside the federal holding. No new informational-privacy test, takings ruling or substantive-due-process holding is announced.

Component D — Outdoor visibility: reverse B(2)'s approval insofar as it prohibits protected nonobscene adult-cabaret performances or their pictures from being visible outside solely because the specified anatomy is shown. Limiting the subject to cabaret entertainment does not turn offense at its message into a secondary effect or narrow the ordinance to obscenity, material obscene as to minors, intrusion into a captive home, or actual sexual conduct. Erznoznik's public-display principle and the surviving Madsen images holding require this separation. The ruling creates no right to violate independently valid public-nudity, obscenity, trespass or neutral sign rules. The state courts determine severability and conforming relief; this Court invents no replacement display code.

Component E — Fees: vacate the categorical denial of plaintiffs' federal fees for consideration in light of the actual federal success and §1988's eligibility/amount distinction. Do not order an amount or deem all unsuccessful claims compensable. Independently, a state equitable rule cannot shift a municipality's expense of defending a substantial, nonfrivolous §1983 request merely because interim restraint was dissolved; Christiansburg/Hughes's federal limitation cannot be evaded by calling those defense expenses equity damages. Vacate the City's award to that extent and remand to distinguish expenses attributable to an independently permissible state claim or actual procedural misconduct. The mere decision to seek preliminary relief is not such misconduct. Do not adjudicate an unpresented bond-enforcement claim or erase every possible state-law fee basis. The state court's disagreement about an unposted bond does not itself settle the federal issue.

## 157. Newton County — replacement operative commitments

### N1: Completed-plan request and its stale WSRA rulings

All eight would vacate the May 1997 appellate judgment insofar as it decides the merits of the request to stop the timber sales until comprehensive plans exist, and remand with directions to dismiss that preliminary request as moot. The government's January 1998 brief reports actual completion of the river plans and incorporation into the adjacent forest plans in October 1996. On that supported, attributed recitation, an order to await those plans can no longer supply the relief requested. The exact missed September 30, 1995 deadline remains a historical fact; the Court does not excuse it or adjudge the completed plans adequate.

This is an actual equitable disposition, not a direction to control to choose one. Bancorp, joined by all eight, permits a just disposition under §2106 and rejects automatic vacatur after voluntary settlement. Here petitioners did not settle or abandon review; the prevailing agency's reported performance ended the specified prospective request. The mootness predated the appellate merits adjudication, and no surviving damages or independent remedy is supplied for this particular request. Those differences justify clearing that stale appellate merits decision, as the limited corrective jurisdiction in Arizonans permits. The January 14, 1997 district-court planning interpretation remains vacated: it cannot be revived as an operative merits determination of the exhausted request. The Court preserves that result on mootness grounds without adopting a general rule that any interlocutory appeal divests a district court of all related merits authority. Any distinct adjacency-protection, coordination or site-effects obligation is neither extinguished nor newly brought within review.

### N2: Superseded preliminary MBTA relief

All eight would vacate the 1997 appellate affirmance insofar as it decides whether interim MBTA restraint should have issued pending the district court's merits adjudication, and direct dismissal of that obsolete preliminary appeal. The subsequent district-court final judgment and the April 1, 1998 affirmance establish that the earlier pending-trial posture has ended. Camenisch separates a temporary preservation order from an underlying cause's final disposition. The later judgment is recognized as dated posture evidence, not adopted as Supreme Court law and not brought into review on every other statutory ground.

The reason for clearing the interim ruling is its displaced function and the absence of a live request for that former interval, not a finding that all logging is complete. This confined disposition neither erases the final judgment on NFMA, NEPA, CWA, Wilderness Act, ESA, record-review or distinct WSRA site effects nor orders those issues retried. The MBTA dismissal requires its own treatment below. Ordinary future relief on any returned MBTA claim requires a present remedial basis, and no existing preliminary motion is automatically revived.

### N3: The MBTA dismissal is within review and was lawfully connected

All eight commit to deciding the reviewed MBTA dismissal's legal validity. The supplied Question 2 and the actual August 5, 1996 partial summary judgment, expressly affirmed in 113 F.3d 110, 116, establish the reviewed component. Section 1254(1) does not impose the universal final-judgment requirement applicable to state-court review. No expanded grant, replacement petition or new claim is required. The prior freeze's condition that control establish inclusion of the MBTA component is withdrawn on the verified original question and judgment, not supplied by assumption.

All eight also conclude that the court of appeals had authority to examine this particular dismissal with the July 29 injunction denial. This is a narrow legal conclusion about the entered orders. The injunction was denied on the asserted unavailability of the same MBTA claim; one week later the district court entered a dispositive dismissal on that same legal obstacle, and plaintiffs appealed both. Reviewing that categorical obstacle was necessary to meaningful review of the asserted legal basis for refusing the same equitable relief. It was not a second party's independent liability issue, a separate damages defense, a factual issue introduced for efficiency, or merely a claim sharing some evidence. Rejection of the obstacle requires correction of the actual dismissal that otherwise forecloses consideration of the requested relief; acceptance of it determines the same legal defect in both orders.

Deckert's injunction-appeal rule supplies a preexisting basis for examining a clear legal obstacle to the very equitable claim. Swint did not overrule such established examples and reserved genuinely necessary or inextricable review; its rejected separate municipal-liability question could be decided independently of the officers' immunity. Clinton resolves that reservation for the same integrated temporal-protection order and supplies a bounded analogy, not a universal doctrine covering all connected liability questions. This application requires identity of the dispositive legal obstacle and the equitable claim, an actually entered dismissal, and no additional unresolved factual adjudication to decide that obstacle. It does not announce that every merits ruling relevant to likelihood of success is immediately appealable.

The strongest opposing path would vacate the appellate MBTA disposition and instruct dismissal of the partial-summary-judgment appeal because review of an injunction's likelihood-of-success premise need not confer power to determine final merits. That path would be required if the only merits ruling were preliminary reasoning, or if a separate nonappealable ruling required additional legal or factual issues unnecessary to the proper appeal. Camenisch guards that boundary. Here an actual categorical dismissal was entered and separately appealed, and the same legal foreclosure supplied the injunction denial. The Justices therefore reject that threshold-only disposition on this record. They do not rely on eventual merger or the later final judgment to cure an earlier excess. If connected authority were absent, neither source would manufacture the independently proper later review route; none is invented here.

### N4: Continuing review, legal disposition and remedial facts

All eight reject wholesale mootness of the MBTA dismissal on the planning and final-judgment events. A final rejection of a litigated claim does not itself extinguish the controversy about that rejection. The last supported progress account states that approximately three-fourths of road work and harvesting was complete at December 10, 1997 argument; it proves neither completion by September 1998 nor a precise present quantity. The record identifies the concrete sale authorizations, alleged bird injury and actual killing concession that made this a controversy. No supplied subsequent event establishes complete satisfaction or loss of all meaningful relief on the MBTA claim. The legal dismissal can therefore be reviewed without an invented finding that a particular operation remains underway on September 28.

This is not an exemption from Article III's continuing-case requirement. A supported later showing that no effective relief remains would require its own mootness assessment. The present decision must not order current cutting stopped, a permit issued or obtained, a completed sale undone, or a court to adjudicate a moot claim. On the four Justices' vacatur path, the lower court must apply the correct law to any still-remediable authorization and address present relief and preservation on the actual record. The four affirmance Justices would end the reviewed agency-permit claim on its legal coverage ground without declaring all forestry activity lawful. Exact remaining harvest status is material to an affirmative prospective decree, which none now orders; its absence is not a reason to manufacture either completion or an injunction.

## 158. First Jersey Securities, Inc. v. SEC

### Entering law and common component commitments

The securities provisions and the supported trial findings control. The opening Central Bank holding preserves a confined private affirmative-assistance action and its own culpability requirements; it is not the historical categorical rejection of accessory liability and is not a comprehensive SEC-enforcement rule. O'Hagan's primary-deception holding, effective June 25, 1997, preserves duty, deception, scienter and transaction connection. **Culpable affirmative assistance to a securities fraud**, effective April 19, 1994, and **Primary securities fraud through source deception**, effective June 25, 1997, retain those separate domains. First Jersey's intentional deceptive sales and Brennan's orchestration meet primary-liability requirements without needing to expand either doctrine.

F1 — Reject claim preclusion. The 1979 proceeding's earlier transactions and the 1984 settlement do not encompass the later 1982–1985 transactions. Lawlor supplies the separate-transaction principle; Moitie supplies real finality, not immunity for later conduct. Permission to supplement an old complaint is not a duty to add every new wrong. The actual settlement language supplies no broader release. Baker preserves lawful adjudicative effect at its actual scope and does not enlarge a settlement by resemblance alone.

F2 — Affirm First Jersey's and Brennan's primary securities-fraud liability. Intentional concealment of the dominated market, repurchases, splitting and resale plan materially distorted what customers were told; actual hands-on coordination and knowing participation establish Brennan's own responsibility. The Court adopts no general rule that an owner, supervisor or assistant is necessarily a primary violator. The contemporaneous repurchase prices are the supported benchmark in this dominated, thinly traded market; illiquidity was under the firm's unilateral control and is not a proved independent explanation for the markups. No universal five-percent or ten-percent per se fraud rule is necessary.

F3 — Affirm the independent §20(a) controller ground at this scope. The enactment expressly includes governmental agencies within its relevant person definition; the SEC can enforce this supported controller liability. The established primary violation, actual control and deliberate culpable participation satisfy the presented requirements. Brennan cannot establish both good faith and lack of direct or indirect inducement on the supported findings. No comprehensive resolution of disputed culpable-participation pleading burdens is necessary because it was proved. Cosmetic compliance that deliberately fails to examine the relevant transactions does not establish this defense.

F4 — Affirm $22,288,099 disgorgement, the credited settlement, and joint and several responsibility for this coordinated enterprise's gains. The amount is the supported unlawful profit approximation, not a second compensatory award for the same sums; the $5 million credit prevents duplicate recovery. Brennan's actual collaboration, complete ownership, unrestricted access to the funds and corresponding enrichment support responsibility for the enterprise gains. The rule is confined to the proved collaboration and benefit; unrelated profits, wealth as such and every person's gross receipts are not recoverable merely because fraud occurred. Porter and Mitchell permit ancillary restitution within statutory equity unless displaced; Mertens's different, specifically limited ERISA remedial categories do not eliminate this traditional gain-recovery function.

F5 — Affirm $52,689,894 prejudgment interest. The court could approximate the value of retaining the unlawful gains using the IRS underpayment rate rather than the rate paid to a lender to the Government; it stopped interest on the credited $5 million when paid. The ratio to principal and duration do not alone establish punishment where retained use continues. Equity retains actual fairness and causal limits; neither the IRS rate nor interest through every possible litigation delay is made universally mandatory.

F6 — Affirm the supported prospective injunction. Systematic deliberate misconduct, relevant prior sanctions, ongoing market activity, retained personnel and continuing control establish a supported recurrence risk. ## 159. United States ex rel. McKenzie v. BellSouth Telecommunications, Inc.

### Entering law and component commitments

The official 1994 Code carries the 1986-amended §3730(e)(4)(A)–(B) and (h). No later FCA amendment or later Supreme Court original-source decision enters. The case's jurisdictional text must be respected without treating every unresolved fraud element as a jurisdictional fact. Steel Co.'s current Article III/statutory-element distinction remains relevant but does not repeal this provision's express jurisdictional form. The original-source issue is not decided by general sympathy for whistleblowers.

Robinson's **Former employees within Title VII retaliation protection**, effective February 18, 1997, supports contextual treatment of a retaliation provision without importing Title VII's substantive elements into the FCA. Current Lexecon, Brogan and Oubre decisions distinguish enacted requirements from added exceptions or procedural obstacles at their own scopes. Their divergent separate positions do not establish one generic statutory-construction vote.

M1 — Vacate the qui tam dismissal because the Sixth Circuit imposed an additional original-source timing requirement. A relator must possess direct and independent knowledge of the information on which the allegations rest and voluntarily provide that information to the Government before filing the action. Congress did not require disclosure before any public account appeared, first disclosure to the Government, or responsibility for the publisher's source. The purpose of discouraging derivative claims cannot add those requirements. Conversely, personal employment and assertion of an insider status do not automatically establish the two knowledge requirements or the disclosure condition.

M2 — Remand for the actual predicates. For this judgment it is unnecessary to settle every possible meaning of based upon. Even assuming the identified public disclosures trigger the bar, the original-source exception must be assessed under its enacted terms. The reported copying and awareness, the earlier workplace knowledge, the particular allegations and the timing/content of voluntary disclosure require their proper record treatment. The Court does not declare jurisdiction established or order a fraud trial. If genuinely independent information and qualifying prefiling disclosure are absent under the correct standard, dismissal remains available. Do not invent a government nonintervention inference, a public-disclosure date, or a new first-to-file finding.

M3 — Preserve the reversal of the retaliation dismissal and the remand. Section 3730(h) protects lawful acts in furtherance of an FCA action, including qualifying investigation or assistance before an action is filed; it does not condition that protection on obtaining a qui tam recovery or surviving the public-disclosure bar. McKenzie's allegations of reporting the fraud beyond ordinary dispatcher tasks, showing the employer a newspaper report of similar federal qui tam litigation, and threats and harassment permit inference of protected conduct, employer notice and causation sufficient to avoid the reviewed dismissal. Ordinary performance complaints without a connection to contemplated FCA enforcement are not automatically protected. The employer need not be told the precise statutory label if the described facts give the required notice.

M4 — Leave liability and remedy unproved. No finding of actual false claims, fraudulent refund obligations, scienter, retaliation, disability causation, back pay, reinstatement or damages follows. The lawful refund exceptions remain part of the alleged underlying scheme: diminished rather than lost service, inaccessible premises, an employee rather than customer report, or a customer-extended repair period. Those exceptions are facts in the alleged contractual arrangement, not judicial findings of fraud. Distinguish §3730(b)(5)'s related-pending-case rule and (e)(3)'s government-party bar from the public-disclosure/original-source exception.

### Adversarial alternatives

The strongest affirmance argument is that the statute rewards a true initial whistleblower and excludes someone who repeats public allegations after another person sounded the alarm. It also relies on nearly identical complaint passages. That argument may establish derivation or failure of actual independent knowledge on an adequate record; it cannot amend the definition's timing. Direct and independent knowledge and genuine voluntary prefiling disclosure perform Congress's gatekeeping work without an additional before-publicity condition.

The broadest reversal alternative would construe based upon exclusively as actually derived from public reports and declare this employment-based suit admissible. The record and remedy do not justify that final conclusion: the source of particular allegations and the prefiling disclosure predicate remain unresolved. The narrower common ground is not an avoidance of the live legal question; it answers the additional timing rule that actually produced dismissal while leaving genuinely different definitions for a case requiring them.

The retaliation-affirmance alternative treats internal complaints as ordinary job activity and requires an explicit threat of an FCA suit. The alleged article about a closely parallel qui tam action, presentation to supervisors, and conduct outside ordinary dispatch duties supply more than generic dissatisfaction. The statutory examples include investigation and future actions, so the claim cannot be dismissed solely for lack of a completed successful suit or precise legal terminology. Discovery may disprove notice, protected conduct or causal connection; no present favorable finding follows.
