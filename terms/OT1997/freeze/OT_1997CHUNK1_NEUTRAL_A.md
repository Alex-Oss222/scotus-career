## Bates v. United States — No. 96-7185 — November 4, 1997

### Vehicle, question and record

The scheduled event is plenary certiorari review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), on the supplied question whether knowing and willful misapplication under the conduct-date version of 20 U.S.C. §1097(a) requires the additional specific intent to injure or defraud the United States. The question is an analytical formulation, not a quotation of an unrecovered petition or grant. The supplied argument marker is October 7, 1997; no new grant date is invented.

**Correction:** the judgment under review is the **Seventh Circuit**, *United States v. Bates*, 96 F.3d 964, No. 95-2203, decided September 20, 1996. It vacated the Northern District of Indiana's **pretrial dismissal of a twelve-count indictment** and reinstated the prosecution. There is no conviction, jury instruction or trial-evidence sufficiency ruling before the Court in this posture. The earlier short field identifying another circuit and suggesting a trial-instruction inquiry was inconsistent with both the supplied detailed narrative and the retrieved judgment below.

Garrit Bates was Education America's vice president and Acme Institute of Technology's chief financial officer/treasurer. The indictment alleges that Acme received federally guaranteed student-loan funds and was obliged to refund the appropriate amounts when students withdrew; that Bates knew the refund obligations; and that he directed employees not to make required refunds while giving priority to management fees and payments to the owners. Twelve specified alleged misapplications occurred between January 15 and June 15, 1990. The indictment was returned September 8, 1994; the dismissal motion followed February 7, 1995. For facial sufficiency, these are **allegations assumed for the legal inquiry**, not findings of guilt, actual knowledge, diversion, financial loss or concealment.

Section 1097(a)'s conduct-date text expressly used **“knowingly and willfully”** and included misapplication among its prohibited dealings with Title IV funds, assets or property. The statutory question must use the version applicable to the alleged 1990 conduct. A later refund-specific amendment cannot be applied retroactively by calling it the offense charged. Section 2 aiding-and-abetting liability was also charged; the supplied review question creates no independent §2 ruling.

The lower court reports two arguments by Bates: an additional intent-to-injure-or-defraud element, and inadequacy of the allegations of conversion. It rejected both. It reports that the Government conceded intentional conversion for the benefit of the accused or another was required, while disputing additional injurious or fraudulent purpose. That is a **reported position below**, not an invented current concession or a claim that the complete merits briefs were retrieved. The lower court's working construction also required knowingly unlawful, voluntary unauthorized control. A judgment that rejects an extra fraudulent-purpose element must not erase the knowing/willful misapplication elements or silently settle every meaning of willfulness.

### Entering law and alternatives

Read [A map — Bates](../entering-law/OT_1997CHUNK1_A.md#bates-v-united-states--november-4-1997) and the exact linked headings. The principal simulated authorities are *Wells*, **Section 1014 does not add an objective materiality element** (1997-02-26); *Ratzlaf*, **Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful** (1994-01-11), including its independently sufficient residual-lenity ground; and *Staples*, **Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun** (1994-05-23). Their corresponding Standards have the same present-operation dates. *Wells*'s separate preservation holding is bounded to the statutory question actually presented, decided and granted. *Morissette* and *Liparota* retain their predivergence culpability and statutory-context force.

**Path supporting the appellate statutory construction.** Treat the express knowing/willful misapplication terms as performing the requisite culpability work; do not add a distinct fraudulent-purpose element without textual, historical or established term-of-art support. *Wells* supports fidelity to enacted elements; *Morissette* does not make fraud an invariant component of every property offense. The strongest contrary point is that borrowed criminal terminology may include settled culpability beyond dictionary meaning, especially when civil accounting defaults could otherwise become serious crimes. The response must explain why the retained knowing/willful, unauthorized-conversion requirements adequately distinguish innocent failure or mistake, rather than relying on prosecutorial discretion or the mere existence of wrongdoing allegations.

**Path supporting Bates's construction.** Determine that the statutory context and legal meaning of willful misapplication incorporate the additional specific purpose, or that genuine ambiguity remains after ordinary interpretation and triggers lenity. *Ratzlaf* requires giving a separate culpability word operative effect, and *Staples/Morissette* guard against dispensing with fault by silence alone. The strongest objection is that §1097 already contains express knowing/willful terms and the bank-funds cases' additional intent element may derive from the distinct history of 18 U.S.C. §656. The analysis must establish the proposed element's basis in this statute; a general preference for more culpability is insufficient.

The lower court's comparison with §§656, 665 and 641 is persuasive reasoning under review, not simulated Supreme Court authority. Its §656 history matters to the analogy: it understood the bank-intent language as having been omitted in an earlier technical revision, unlike §1097. No current packet supplies a universal mental-state formula for these different enactments.

### Remedy and limits

Affirming the rejection of the extra element would permit the prosecution to proceed subject to every actual offense element, properly available indictment objection and ordinary procedure. It would not convict Bates, adjudicate evidentiary sufficiency, order a particular sentence, or establish harmless instructional error. Accepting the extra element would require a disposition addressing the indictment and dismissal under the chosen construction, not an acquittal on untried facts. A remand may preserve properly available independent issues without deciding that they are preserved merely because the source is incomplete. The supplied question does not require a freestanding ruling on every conversion theory.

## State Oil Co. v. Khan — No. 96-871 — November 4, 1997

### Vehicle, question and record

The scheduled event is plenary certiorari review under §1254(1) of the maximum vertical resale-price issue. The supplied argument marker is October 7, 1997. The question is whether an agreement imposing a maximum resale price is unlawful per se under Sherman Act §1, including whether the received *Albrecht* rule should remain in force. It is not a general grant to revise all vertical-restraint law or decide final market liability.

Khan leased a gasoline station from State Oil and contracted to buy its gasoline. State Oil's suggested retail price minus 3.25 cents per gallon set Khan's purchase price. Khan could charge less and accept a smaller margin. If he charged more without a corresponding adjustment by State Oil, he owed State Oil the excess. Thus the agreement's rebate term removed the extra return from exceeding the suggested ceiling. The lower-price provision did not itself obligate State Oil to reduce its wholesale price or establish a contractual minimum resale price. The action followed State Oil's termination of the arrangement for unpaid rent.

The district court entered summary judgment on the federal antitrust and state contract claims. It applied the rule of reason, found missing evidence on its essential competition elements, and excluded an expert's injury analysis. The Seventh Circuit, 93 F.3d 1358 (1996), reversed the antitrust disposition because *Albrecht* remained binding and it found the injury evidence sufficient to avoid summary judgment; it **affirmed the separate contract dismissal**. That partial lower judgment must be preserved in identifying the component actually under review.

The expert compared the receiver's later five-month operation of the station with Khan's operation. The lower court treated the inference of injury as admissible and sufficient on the then-presented summary-judgment record, while recognizing that changed competitive conditions could weaken it. These are lower-court procedural/evidentiary rulings, not established actual damages or competitive harm. The record does not justify findings about multiple wholesalers, a dealer cartel, particular exclusive territories or overall market power. The lower concurrence identifies possible service and distribution effects but makes no current-case finding that they occurred.

### Entering law and alternatives

Read [A map — State Oil](../entering-law/OT_1997CHUNK1_A.md#state-oil-co-v-khan--november-4-1997). The received *Albrecht*, 390 U.S. 145 (1968), maximum-price rule has not been displaced by identified simulated authority. *Sylvania*, 433 U.S. 36 (1977), governs nonprice vertical restraints; *Business Electronics*, 485 U.S. 717 (1988), distinguishes the significance of a price agreement; *Atlantic Richfield*, 495 U.S. 328 (1990), and *Brunswick*, 429 U.S. 477 (1977), preserve antitrust-injury requirements. *Brown v. Pro Football*'s 1996 labor exemption and its Standards module expressly do not classify nonlabor product-market restraints. Neither *Kodak* nor *Brooke Group* supplies a maximum-price holding. There is no controlling generic extended stare decisis test to synthesize from the divided *Hubbard* rationales.

**Retain and apply Albrecht.** Its binding rule covers an actual agreement limiting the dealer's effective freedom to raise prices, even when the sanction takes the form of rebating excess revenue. Possible restrictions on dealer services, survival or nonprice competition and the value of predictable rules support adherence. The strongest objection is that a price ceiling often promotes consumer interests and that later nonprice-restraint and injury doctrine undermines *Albrecht*'s economic premises. Retention requires answering those arguments and explaining why per se classification remains justified; lower-court obedience alone does not answer this Court's reconsideration question.

**Expressly replace the maximum-price per se rule with rule-of-reason treatment.** The argument is that experience no longer supports treating such agreements as invariably or predictably harmful without examination; actual anticompetitive cases remain actionable under the ordinary competitive-effects inquiry. It must address the precedent's rationale, experience, administrability and reliance in concrete terms. The strongest contrary argument is that ceiling arrangements can also suppress services or disguise minimum prices, and that statutory stare decisis deserves weight. A rule-of-reason path must preserve those possible harms as matters for proof and must not turn the proposed classification change into automatic legality.

**A supported distinction or narrower ground.** An actual unilateral suggestion without agreement, a materially different restraint, or a properly available independent injury issue could in principle narrow adjudication. Here the reported agreement and rebate obligation are established parts of the summary-judgment record; they cannot be replaced by a unilateral-policy premise. The lower court rejected the formal distinction between a rebate sanction and an express ceiling. A narrow path must genuinely preserve an operative domain for *Albrecht*, rather than silently nullify it. Do not invent an unpreserved alternative ground or factual finding to avoid the presented precedent question.

### Remedy and limits

Retaining the rule would sustain the appellate rejection of summary judgment on that classification ground, subject to the actual reviewed component. Changing the rule would support vacatur or reversal of the affected antitrust judgment with remand for the appropriate analysis; it would establish neither that Khan wins nor that State Oil wins under that analysis. Whether existing evidence satisfies the resulting standard must be addressed in the proper forum and scope. The independent state-contract affirmance, expert ruling and injury issues must not be automatically reopened or finally resolved by an imprecise whole-case disposition. Horizontal maximum prices, minimum resale prices, final damages and other Sherman Act requirements remain distinct.

## City of Monroe v. United States — No. 97-122 — November 17, 1997

### Vehicle, question and record

This is a direct appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1253 from the Middle District of Georgia's three-judge injunction judgment, 962 F. Supp. 1501. It is not certiorari or an application. The supplied scheduled event is November 17, 1997; no oral-argument event is established. The question concerns the **scope and independent effect of the Attorney General's preclearance of Georgia's 1968 municipal-election code**, not judicial reassessment of that approval's substantive wisdom.

The supplied record extraction reports that Monroe used plurality voting before 1966 although its charter did not specify plurality or majority. A 1966 charter amendment required majority voting but was not submitted for §5 preclearance. Georgia's 1968 municipal code deferred to municipal charter provisions prescribing plurality and imposed a majority default where the charter lacked such a provision. Section 34A-1407(a) was submitted and precleared. Monroe's 1990 charter submission did not request preclearance of its majority-vote provision. The Attorney General objected and sought an injunction requiring return to plurality voting. The district court rejected the City's argument that the statewide approval already supplied authority for majority voting and entered summary judgment for the United States.

The distinction between **a charter silent about plurality** and a charter that expressly required plurality is material to the relationship between the code's exception/default and *City of Rome*. An unprecleared 1966 amendment cannot become approved merely by continued use. Equally, an independently and sufficiently identified precleared statewide rule must be evaluated on its own actual terms; the existence of a separate unsubmitted amendment cannot alone establish that the statewide rule lacked effect. The actual submission and approval determine whether the latter characterization is supported.

### Entering law and alternatives

Read [A map — Monroe](../entering-law/OT_1997CHUNK1_A.md#city-of-monroe-v-united-states--november-17-1997). The required simulated authorities are *Lopez v. Monterey County* (1996-11-06), *Young v. Fordice* (1997-03-31), and especially **Foreman v. Dallas County — Whether approval of the 1985 state recodification precleared undisclosed county formulas** (1997-06-27). The corresponding Standards, **Administration and enforcement of voting changes requiring preclearance**, incorporates all three with component-specific dates and no blanket exemption for state-compelled local administration. *Foreman* also forbids treating baseline uncertainty itself as proof of a covered change. *Bossier*'s purpose/effect rules and §2's independent operation do not settle submission scope. *City of Rome*, *McCain* and *Clark* remain received authorities; the enforcement/preclearance channel distinction survives.

**Independent precleared-default path.** Monroe may show that the 1968 submission fairly identified and obtained approval of the actual majority default that directly governs charters lacking a plurality provision. On that account, applying the precleared rule requires no implied retroactive clearance of the 1966 amendment. The strongest objection is that statewide approval cannot silently approve local changes never identified for federal review, and *City of Rome* addressed the same code. The City must establish the precise submitted rule, its applicability and the material charter distinction; the word “statewide” alone is insufficient.

**Uncleared-local-change path.** The Government may establish that the challenged local change was distinct from the actual submission or that the statewide approval did not fairly identify majority voting for the relevant class of municipalities. *McCain*, *Clark*, *Lopez*, *Young* and *Foreman* require genuine identification rather than approval by implication. The strongest objection is that this theory could demand duplicate approval of an already identified self-executing default and disregard a material plurality-charter distinction. It must explain why the rule actually approved does not supply the City's asserted independent authority.

**Limited remand path.** If the dispositive scope or applicability cannot be established from the supported submission/charter record, the Court can reject an erroneous blanket rule and remand for the necessary determinations without inventing an approval or denial. This path must identify a genuinely unresolved material fact; unavailable operator copies alone do not prove the district record lacks the relevant instruments. It is not an automatic evidentiary-hearing requirement.

### Remedy and limits

A conclusion that the statewide default already clears this actual rule would support relief from the conflicting injunction on the §5 ground, while preserving independent discrimination challenges and any genuinely distinct unapproved change. A conclusion that the actual change remains unprecleared supports prospective nonuse under §5, with relief fitted to the established baseline, change and submission facts. Neither route lets the Georgia court grant substantive preclearance, invalidate the Attorney General's substantive approval through collateral review, decide a §2 claim not presented, cancel completed elections automatically, or choose a replacement system without lawful remedial authority.
