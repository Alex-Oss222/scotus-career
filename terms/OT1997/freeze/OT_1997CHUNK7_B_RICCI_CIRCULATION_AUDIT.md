## Audit conclusion

Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg, and Breyer can join the proposed expressly limited reversal and remand. Their original remedial commitment was to set aside the adverse summary judgment so the preserved custody claim could be decided under the proper constitutional inquiry, without determining present unreasonableness or liability. The public draft preserves that operative consequence. Its use of “reversed” identifies the rejected legal ground and the judgment resting on that ground; it does not direct judgment for Ricci on the ultimate constitutional claim.

This is a supported limited revision of the formal disposition word, from vacatur to targeted reversal, not a new finding about custody or a change from open application to established violation. The result must be stated as reversal and remand on the preserved custody-reasonableness claim, with actual reasonableness expressly unresolved. The draft may not be summarized merely as reversal “because Ricci's arrest was unconstitutional.” None of these four supplies that conclusion.

The date-eligible analogy is concrete. Wilson's unanimous opinion reversed and remanded for evaluation of the actual entry and supported justification while finding neither a completed constitutional violation nor automatic suppression. All four audited Justices joined that disposition. It demonstrates that reversal for a deficient constitutional framework can leave application and remedy open. Soldal likewise reversed adverse summary judgment while separating Fourth Amendment coverage from ultimate reasonableness and liability; Stevens and O'Connor joined it, and its controlling rule governs the later Court. These authorities support this permissible use of reversal-and-remand rather than an exclusive verbal rule requiring vacatur whenever an application remains unresolved. No new interpretation of section 2106 or independent jurisdictional ground is adopted.

## Exact proposed accommodation and its limits

The operative accommodation is the combination of these draft sentences:

> The judgment of the Court of Appeals is reversed insofar as it sustains summary judgment on the categorical premise that probable cause and state or local arrest authority alone establish the reasonableness of station-house custody for this fine-only licensing violation.

> The Court does not decide whether this custody satisfies that requirement on the supported record, award damages, resolve individual immunity, or reopen the settled search and abandoned probable-cause claims.

The second sentence is essential to the four joins, as are the remand's actual-fact directions and rejection of a mandatory citation or least-restrictive-means rule. Read together, the draft removes the judgment on the preserved custody claim and returns its unresolved application for ordinary adjudication. It reverses no independent search or probable-cause judgment and grants Ricci no present damages entitlement.

For implementation clarity, the eventual mandate should expressly state that **the adverse summary judgment on the preserved custody-reasonableness claim is set aside**. That makes the existing proposal's intended consequence unambiguous without changing its substance. Reversal of an abstract reason while leaving the same adverse custody judgment operative would not satisfy the four commitments. Conversely, directing judgment that this custody was unreasonable would exceed them.

The lower opinion did discuss continuing operation, potential accumulated fines, the processing purpose, and one-hour duration. The draft appropriately preserves those facts and does not describe the lower court as wholly ignoring context. The four find the legal framework insufficient despite that discussion; on remand those circumstances must receive assessment under the added-restraint rule. The record is not declared factually empty, and no evidentiary hearing is mandated merely to recreate already supported facts.

## Other commitments and comparator

Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas remain committed to affirmance on the bounded conclusion that this actual continuing violation and short enforcement processing satisfy constitutional reasonableness. The revised draft does not answer their different application position and changes none of their votes. Their rejection of unlimited authority from probable cause does not make them supporters of reversing the adverse judgment.

The historical comparator remains an unexplained dismissal as improvidently granted. It provides no individual vote, rationale, or merits position. The already recorded institutional difference between a reasoned disposition and that dismissal remains; changing the four proposed dispositions from vacatur/remand to this expressly limited reversal/remand creates no newly identifiable historical personal-vote departure. No historical poll or reason for dismissal is inferred. The four revised commitments rest on their own date-eligible precedents and the preserved legal/remedial boundary, not the need for a particular tally.
