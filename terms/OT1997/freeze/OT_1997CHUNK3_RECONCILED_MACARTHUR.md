## The actual lower comparator and source corrections

The December 24, 1996 D.C. Circuit majority, written by **Douglas H. Ginsburg**, with Randolph, affirmed summary judgment. It held collection-time law-enforcement pertinence sufficient for retention and rejected the two constitutional claims for lack of standing. Tatel dissented on retention construction, while agreeing that the Foundation's constitutional claim failed on the narrower missing causal-link ground. Douglas H. Ginsburg is not Justice Ruth Bader Ginsburg. No lower judge's position supplies a Supreme Court Justice's vote.

The complete CAP opinion, majority footnote and Tatel writing were read and the material text was checked against the OpenJurist primary copy. The source supports the following distinctions, with OpenJurist paragraph numbers providing additional pinpoints:

- **Actual records and initial-lawfulness assumption:** 102 F.3d at 601–602, ¶¶3–8, reports the 23-page Lindblom file, five pages referring to the Foundation elsewhere, redacted associational information and the district court's in camera account of investigations concerning other persons with incidental, apparently innocent contacts. Lindblom did not challenge initial collection. This is not a finding that present retention is unlawful, that every record is currently irrelevant, or that a particular Privacy Act exemption governs the chosen remedy.
- **Original money demand and appellate abandonment:** At 602, ¶4, the parties sought monetary damages as well as injunctive and expungement relief. Paragraph 6 reports abandonment of FOIA and Fifth/Ninth Amendment claims; it does not state abandonment of all money relief on surviving claims. The report does not assign the original money prayer to a surviving statutory count, decide its preservation, identify an amount or establish intentional/willful action or actual damages. The clean monetary addendum correctly preserves both sides of that limit.
- **The lower statutory holding:** At 602–605, ¶¶9–22, the majority distinguishes an authorized law-enforcement activity from a currently open investigation and reads pertinence as a quality of the lawfully gathered record. Its definitive holding at ¶22 reaches beyond merely rejecting destruction whenever one investigation closes: it says no expungement is required when the records are no longer pertinent to a current law-enforcement activity. A reconciled decision cannot call that only a narrow investigation-closure holding, or transform it into Supreme Court law by affirming the constitutional component.
- **The separate retention argument:** At 607–609, ¶¶38–42, Tatel relies on the defined word maintain, the independent work of retention, Congress's use of collection/compilation language elsewhere, and protection against unnecessary inventories distinct from disclosure. He does not establish that all these records have lost every continuing lawful connection. His construction is not an automatic command to destroy a file when an investigation closes.
- **The constitutional evidence:** At 606–607, ¶¶27–34 and n.1, the affidavits describe possible stigma, applicant behavior and employment effects without supporting the operative third-party knowledge/response link. Lindblom's separate constitutional standing presentation offers no independent injury and relies on the Foundation's harm. Tatel's proposed regular FOIA-request practice, ¶44, and the majority footnote's possible later avoidance evidence are examples of evidence, not findings that it exists.
- **Discovery:** At 605, ¶23, the majority sustains denial of discovery on its actual merits posture, classification concerns and the in camera examination. It does not establish that the documents were examined for every statutory remedial or exemption issue now material, nor does its ruling promise unrestricted disclosure if another legal question arises.

**Precision concerning Meese.** Tatel's account at ¶45 compresses the FARA designation and disclosure scheme. Stevens's own Meese opinion, 481 U.S. 465, 470–471, states that the words **political propaganda did not appear on the prescribed film disclosure form**; footnote 14 says the Department made no public announcement using that description. His standing analysis at 473–477 and nn.7–9 nevertheless found supported reputational and electoral risk from the statutory characterization, actual planned exhibitions, detailed affidavits, and the expected press/opponent/audience response. Thus the neutral/model shorthand about a public governmental label means that concrete statutory-designation and publicity mechanism, not a mandatory on-screen pejorative label. The relevant distinction here is supported exposure and causal connection. Neither literal government publication nor identification of each lost supporter is a universal standing requirement. This clarification changes no selected component or factual premise in MacArthur.

## Common constitutional ground and tested alternative

**Ground joined by all eight:** Lujan and actual Lee require a concrete personal injury, fair traceability and likely redress at the proper litigation stage. At summary judgment, specific facts and favorable reasonable inferences are required. Meese recognizes adequately supported reputational risk, and Laird rejects subjective chill without specific objective harm or a supported threat. None requires completed harm, a numerical probability, a prescribed affidavit form, a named lost applicant or an on-screen government label in every case.

Accepting the affidavits' general account of stigma does not supply the link between continued FBI retention and knowledge or responsive conduct by the potential grantees or employers whose reactions support the asserted injury. The source says the Foundation publicized the FBI references. That fact cannot establish an automatic self-inflicted-injury bar, but neither does it prove that the relevant outsiders learned of the references and will act as asserted through a supported causal mechanism. The narrow deficiency remains after giving the evidence reasonable favorable inferences. The Court therefore need not adopt the panel's broader assessment that reputational injury is inherently too speculative, its language about all publicity-related harm, or any categorical named-grantee requirement.

Lindblom's presented constitutional case independently supplies no supported own injury. His departure from the Foundation is a fact, not an automatic rule mooting an ongoing personal-record claim. The distinction is what he actually supported for this constitutional theory, not a universal prohibition on another properly presented constitutional privacy theory. Monetary relief cannot cure the missing injury and does not create a direct FBI Bivens cause under Meyer.

**Strongest alternative:** Vacate constitutional dismissal because Meese permits the claimed reputation injury and because the panel's self-publicity and specificity language could impose excessive demands. All eight reject that alternative on the independent causal deficiency. Correcting those broad propositions does not establish the missing connection; a remand to search for an imagined FOIA or audience practice would not be a consequence of Lee or Meese. A genuinely supported different mechanism could warrant a different standing result, but neither the majority's footnote nor Tatel's illustration provides it. The jurisdictional disposition is without prejudice, not a constitutional validation of FBI retention.

## Statutory threshold, civil action and relief

**Why this remand has a judicial basis.** The mere absence of a jurisdiction section in a published opinion does not prove that no court considered jurisdiction, and missing research originals do not establish litigant default. The remand does not rest on either proposition. Here the court's actual statutory analysis supplies a disputed retention holding without identifying a statutory personal-injury/redress route separate from the rejected constitutional theory. The enacted civil-action provisions make that route consequential: the requested expungement is not automatically an available remedy for every (e)(7) violation, while the confirmed original money prayer prevents a conclusive equity-only redressability dismissal. The actual June 2, 1997 Steel Co. holding, effective after this lower judgment and before this review, precludes assuming disputed Article III power to decide the contested retention merits. That present obligation, the specific statutory remedy structure, and the unresolved scope of the actual surviving claim warrant a bounded first-instance determination; they do not require assembling nonexistent filings or proving that the lower court made an unreported jurisdictional concession.

**Personal injury remains distinct from the merits.** Congress may recognize a concrete personal interest in information maintained about an individual. The FBI's acknowledged continuing possession of Lindblom's own associational records is an actual event affecting an identified person, unlike the Foundation's predicted third-party response. It cannot be dismissed merely as another version of that failed causal theory. Equally, a bare allegation of statutory noncompliance supplies neither every Article III element nor every statutory prerequisite. The court must identify the supported personal interest and alleged present invasion without deciding the contested retention construction under the label of injury. Disagreement about whether (e)(7) ultimately forbids the records is a merits dispute, not by itself absence of a substantial federal claim.

Recognizing a present injury in fact now and remanding only the remaining issues is a lawful alternative. All eight decline that additional holding because the complete statutory controversy, particularly lawful redress, is unresolved. Leaving injury open is not a ruling that lost employment, disclosure or an additional downstream injury is always necessary. No hypothetical future leak or mere existence of a file becomes the Court's universal standing test.

**Nagel cannot be minimized.** Scalia joined Weigel's full panel opinion, 725 F.2d 1438, 1440–1442 and nn.1–5. It expressly distinguishes ordinary amendment exhaustion from a suit for illegal recordation under (e)(7), says the latter requires no exhaustion, and describes damages and amendment/expungement as available forms of relief. Its concern about the personal effects of recording protected speech is real prior judicial evidence, not merely an assumption that Scalia would favor the agency. The reconciliation therefore imposes no universal exhaustion requirement on every (e)(7) claim. But Nagel does not establish this claimant's adverse effect, every civil-action predicate, a remedy notwithstanding every lawful exemption, or an adjudicated continuing-pertinence rule. Its actual law-enforcement rationale concerned an employer's evaluative records; it is no ruling that all innocent third-party contacts may be retained forever.

**Doe and Clarkson supply the actual limits.** Doe, 936 F.2d at 1351–1353, gives effect to a lawful (d) exemption against a derivative amendment cause while distinguishing nonexemptible obligations and the Tijerina rule against evading them by a blanket civil-remedy exemption. At 1360–1361 it rejects direct (e)(7) relief bypassing (g)(1)(D), because that claimant's no-adverse-effect finding was unchallenged. Footnotes 1 and 4 also identify Doe's unappealed damages denial and the statutory expungement scope. No equivalent adverse-effect finding or money abandonment is established for Lindblom. Doe's alternative lawful-collection determination did not decide this contested continuing-retention issue.

Clarkson, 678 F.2d at 1375–1377, identifies the amendment provisions as a possible expungement route even for qualifying (e)(7) records outside a name-retrieved system. Footnote 11 expressly confines statutory injunctions to the access and amendment categories, recognizes the damages route for other violations and reserves actual jurisdictional facts. That combination defeats both an assertion that every (e)(7) claim automatically permits expungement and an assertion that no such relief can ever be sought. These date-eligible circuit opinions require reconciliation with the exact statute and actual claim; none is silently converted into Supreme Court resolution of the remaining route questions.

**Exemptions are record- and route-dependent.** The event-effective July 1997 §16.96 lists Central Records System exemptions, including (d) and (g), only to the extent the statutory (j)/(k) authority allows. Neither statutory provision permits exemption of the (e)(7) substantive duty. A lawful amendment exemption can matter to an amendment cause; the nonexemptible duty does not create every remedy. The government must establish applicable coverage, rather than treat the list as proof that every record satisfies every category. The previously invoked FOIA Exemptions 1, 2, 7(C) and 7(D) are not interchangeable with that Privacy Act inquiry. No actual exemption waiver is assumed.

**Monetary history is kept exact.** The original demand cannot be erased because the appellate opinion concentrates on expungement. Nor does that collective prayer prove a surviving, properly presented (g)(1)(D)/(g)(4) demand by Lindblom. The lower courts must identify the actual surviving claim and relief when that issue matters. If all lawful relief truly fails, dismissal may follow; if a genuine monetary or equitable route survives, it must be assessed under its own requirements. No new damages claim may be invented to establish redressability, and no waiver may be inferred from an unavailable original paper.

**Ordering is bounded.** Steel Co. prevents assuming disputed Article III authority to decide this contested retention question. It does not turn all adverse-effect, exemption, statutory-entitlement or remedial issues into constitutional jurisdiction or impose an exhaustive sequence among nonmerits grounds. A genuinely dispositive lawful threshold may end the case without deciding every other issue. Arizonans supplies limited corrective authority and equitable judgment clearing, not substantive jurisdiction under §2106. The Court vacates this unresolved statutory merits disposition without declaring jurisdiction absent, finding a violation or compelling extra litigation after a valid dispositive ground exists.

## Alternative paths and the unadjudicated merits fork

**Immediate whole-action threshold dismissal:** This is the strongest alternative for Justices emphasizing the exact remedy scheme. Section (g)(1)(D) does not itself provide a freestanding unlimited injunction, a valid (d) exemption can matter, and §702 preserves other statutory restrictions. But resolving those propositions alone does not dispose of every genuinely available route in this action. The actual amendment/exemption context, independent equitable route if presented, and surviving scope of the reported monetary request remain unestablished. Treating all as absent would choose unsupported premises; treating any as satisfied would be equally improper. A limited remand is therefore supported without requiring that relief ultimately exist.

**Recognize concrete statutory injury now:** Actual possession of personal associational records and Congress's protection of that personal interest make this a serious alternative, especially under the privacy recognition in FLRA, Lujan's retained congressional-recognition principle and Scalia's Nagel join. Yet an injury-in-fact holding would not resolve adverse effect, a lawful remedy, sovereign consent or complete redressability. Each Associate elects to leave that additional ruling open while requiring separate consideration. This choice does not adopt the stronger adverse proposition that retention cannot itself injure.

**Reach retention merits now:** A sufficiently established statutory controversy could permit either merits construction. Nagel's relief language and the actual file make the route substantial; they do not alone establish the presently unresolved prerequisites. Neither an assumption that expungement would physically end retention nor a broad desire to settle a pure legal question supplies power to grant that remedy. The merits are contested, and no controlling Supreme Court ruling resolves them as in the limited Norton/Avrech settings preserved by Steel Co. All eight reject a present merits decision on assumed power.

**Merits arguments remain accurately preserved:** The majority's strongest reading emphasizes the record's historical pertinence, the breadth of an authorized activity beyond a single active investigation, and legitimate future usefulness. Its principal textual difficulty is the independently operative defined term maintain, including retention, where Congress could have chosen collect or compiled for. Tatel's strongest reading requires a continuing authorized connection whenever records are maintained; its principal objection is the breadth and continuity of lawful enforcement activities and the difficulty of converting that connection into immediate investigative necessity. That reading need not require automatic destruction at closure, a universal periodic audit or public exposure of classified reasons. Conversely, the separate disclosure restrictions do not themselves nullify a retention protection. Neither construction receives an operative vote or a contingent majority here. The statutory and individual authorization alternatives remain separate and no unreported preservation duty is presumed.

**Discovery consequence:** The merits-dependent materiality assessment returns only as necessary to the limited statutory disposition. Existing confidential and in camera procedures may be used lawfully. The mandate does not require new evidence, renewed FOIA access, a special hearing or a search for a missing affidavit. A supported question's materiality, rather than the operator's incomplete archive, governs any further inquiry.
