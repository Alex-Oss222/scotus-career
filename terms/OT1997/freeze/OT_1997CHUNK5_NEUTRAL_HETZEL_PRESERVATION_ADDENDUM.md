## Source-supported objection and sequence

Respondents contend that Hetzel did not raise the Seventh Amendment new-trial-election claim in her earlier petition concerning the prior damages-recalculation decision. They argue that the present claim therefore should not be considered. This is an actual reported party objection, not an analytical objection newly attributed to them. The original earlier petition and mandamus papers have not independently been recovered.

The earlier appellate mandate reversed and directed recalculation of damages. It did not expressly state that Hetzel could not reject the revised amount and seek a new trial. After she rejected the recalculated award and sought a new trial, the District Court's memorandum addressed the Seventh Amendment election issue and allowed a damages trial. That memorandum and claim were before the court of appeals when it later issued mandamus requiring entry of final judgment and staying retrial. The present challenge concerns that later command. The appeal from the subsequent $15,000 district judgment remained separately pending.

The earlier mandate's omission of an express no-election instruction is a supported description of its content. Whether that mandate nevertheless already necessarily imposed such a restriction is a disputed interpretive issue, not an established neutral premise. Likewise, respondents' assertion about the earlier petition is preserved with its source attribution; this packet does not claim an independent examination of that filing.

## Neutral threshold alternatives

**Earlier-opportunity objection:** If the prior mandate already conclusively required an unconditional reduced judgment, respondents can argue that the election challenge was available then and should have been presented in the earlier petition. Their objection tests whether the present submission attempts to reopen that earlier direction rather than challenge a genuinely later restriction. Its strongest difficulty is the earlier mandate's lack of an express prohibition on rejecting the reduced award and the district court's subsequent treatment of an election as available.

**Later-restriction response:** Hetzel can argue that an instruction merely to recalculate did not yet deprive her of an election, and that the later mandamus command first made the contested restriction explicit after the district court had addressed the constitutional issue. On that account, her current claim challenges the later command and was presented to the court that issued it. The strongest objection is that a later explanation or enforcement of an already conclusive instruction need not create a fresh opportunity to challenge the original ruling. The model must assess the actual distinction; it may not presume that every later enforcement order either reopens all prior issues or forecloses all newly concrete objections.

These paths do not establish waiver, forfeiture, an adequate independent procedural ground or entitlement to review by themselves. Failure to include a contention in an earlier petition, presentation of the contention to the lower court before the challenged later action, and the point at which the alleged injury became explicit are separate matters. Do not invent a rule making every omission from an earlier petition jurisdictional, or a rule making later presentation an automatic cure. Any dispositive preservation rule must have its own date-eligible legal basis.
