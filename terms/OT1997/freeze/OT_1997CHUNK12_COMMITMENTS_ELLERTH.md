## Entering rules and issue divisions

**A. Actionable environment and actual-authority attribution.** The current Standards and Tests entry *Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment* operates from Andrade, February 26, 1997, and Harrison, September 15, 1997, for the express burden and apparent-authority distinction. Its current-wording authority is Faragher, September 8, 1997. Ellerth must prove by a preponderance that actual delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment. Rank, employment and proximity alone do not suffice. Higher-management notice, alter-ego status, hiring/firing power, an express threat, a completed employment decision and tangible economic loss are not invariably required. Personal purpose and breach of an employer's prohibition do not by themselves defeat actual aid. Ordinary scope, negligence and apparent authority remain independent inquiries. Apparent authority separately requires an employer-attributable manifestation, reasonable belief and reasonable reliance; those are not added elements of the actual-power route.

The entry *Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite* operates from Harris, November 9, 1993, and Oncale, March 19, 1997, for same-sex coverage and causation. The plaintiff must separately establish conduct because of sex, objective severity or pervasiveness sufficient to alter employment conditions, and actual subjective perception of abuse. Psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, lost productivity and economic loss are unnecessary; sexual content or gravity alone does not conclusively prove discriminatory causation. The district court's treatment of severity/pervasiveness supplies the reviewed posture, not a new Supreme Court factual finding.

**B. Prevention, policy and nonuse.** Faragher and Harrison reserve a general complete defense based on genuinely effective protection and unreasonable nonuse, including elements, burden, relation to tangible employment action and distinct remedial limits. They establish neither universal liability for supervisors nor an affirmative-defense formula. A written prohibition, noncomplaint and eventual correction are not independently conclusive. There is no universal reporting or dissemination duty, policy immunity, negligence per se or shifted attribution burden. Gebser does not resolve the Title VII reservation. Its three-Justice avoidable-recovery proposal is an earlier published position, not controlling law.

**C. Judgment and relief.** The challenged categorical barriers can be decided without deciding constructive discharge, damages, a complete defense or every surviving summary-judgment ground. The 1993–94 conduct is outside Landgraf's pre-enactment bar on new remedies, but attribution does not establish compensatory or punitive relief, fees, jury entitlement or an amount. If section 102 relief later becomes material, its separate conditions remain: compensation excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other section 706(g) relief; punitive damages require malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights; either party may demand a jury when available section 102 compensatory or punitive relief is sought, and the jury does not hear the caps. No such entitlement is adjudicated here.

## Frozen proposition-level commitments

**P1 — No submission or completed-retaliation prerequisite.** An employee who rejects sexual advances and suffers no completed tangible retaliation may still pursue a Title VII hostile-environment claim. The ordinary sex-causation, objective/subjective environment and employer-attribution requirements remain. The Court decides no independently preserved quid-pro-quo claim by relabeling this one.

**P2 — Existing attribution rule applied.** The plaintiff retains the preponderance burden on actual delegated power and material aid. Slowik's supported role in hiring/promotion, though subject to superior approval, and the alleged use of work-related authority cannot be excluded merely because the threats were unfulfilled, his purpose was personal, he was not the alter ego or higher management lacked notice. These features permit the supported actual-authority inquiry on ordinary summary-judgment principles; they establish neither liability nor every element as a matter of law. A supervisor's claim of power alone would require the distinct apparent-authority analysis if relied on instead.

**P3 — Limited policy disposition and continuing reservation.** Ellerth's knowledge that a policy existed and failure to report during employment do not alone establish a complete defense or eliminate earlier material aid. The Court does not find this policy effective or ineffective, the reporting routes safe or unsafe, or Ellerth's conduct reasonable or unreasonable. The general complete-defense question remains open on the precise terms stated in B. The Court neither adopts a special tangible-action exception nor a new attribution burden shift.

**P4 — Formal judgment and ordinary remand.** Affirm the Seventh Circuit's judgment insofar as it reversed summary judgment on the appealed Title VII employer-attribution claim, and remand for further proceedings consistent with P1–P3. The judgment does not endorse every rationale in the en banc opinions. The lower courts may resolve supported, genuinely preserved independent grounds through ordinary procedures. No mandatory trial, second agency factfinding proceeding, liability finding, award or discovery order follows. The separately dismissed constructive-discharge claim remains outside this disposition absent established preservation; no new resignation-based claim is created.

**Support:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer each support P1–P4 at this stated generality and the same formal judgment. This is an eight-Associate commitment coalition, not a final nine-Justice tally. Their separate recoverability positions below are not added to P3.

## Three-Associate proposed remedial position, kept separate from shared holdings

Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support a separately expressed Title VII avoidable-consequences proposal. It is an application of their own June 22 Gebser published remedial position to the employment setting, not a claim that Gebser decided Title VII or that this proposal already controls.

Under their proposed rule, after the plaintiff establishes the underlying claim and otherwise recoverable harm, the employer bears the burden of establishing by a preponderance that internal remedies were adequately publicized and likely to provide redress without undue risk, effort or expense; that the employee unreasonably failed to use those measures; and that the particular loss sought consequently could have been avoided. Recovery would be reduced or barred only to that proved extent. The explicit preponderance term is part of this modeled civil remedial proposal, not an assertion that Gebser announced a new standard of proof. A paper policy, nonreporting alone, an unsafe or ineffective route, eventual correction or the possibility of a complaint does not establish the defense. Harm incurred before a reasonable opportunity to obtain protection, or harm that those measures could not have prevented, is not eliminated by later nonuse.

This proposal creates no automatic complete employer immunity and does not shift Ellerth's existing attribution burden. It creates no special immunity conditioned on the absence of a tangible employment action and no categorical exception triggered by the presence of one: those questions belong to the distinct unresolved complete-defense issue. If all otherwise compensable loss were proved avoidable, the causal proposal could eliminate that loss recovery; it would not erase the discrimination, establish that every form of relief was unavailable or decide fees. No facts here establish its application. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join adoption of these proposed terms. The three proponents may join P1–P4 while explaining this additional, noncontrolling position; no current author or assignment is designated.
