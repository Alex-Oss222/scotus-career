## C. Issue positions

### Issue 1. Source-bound definition of electoral advocacy

- **Reach:** If the Court grants and reaches the statutory question, decide delegated authority first.
- **Question resolved:** Whether an agency may use a context-and-timing definition that predictably classifies genuine issue discussion as FECA-prohibited electoral advocacy.
- **Stone's legal position:** Apply the established *Buckley* and *MCFL* statutory limit; apply the personal Administrative Law commitment that agency power precedes any deference. The public-law baseline leaves room to argue about unmistakable indirect exhortations, but does not grant the FEC power to regulate an issue communication merely because an imminent election makes it electorally useful.
- **Proposed holding:** Section 100.22(b), as promulgated and justified, exceeds the express-advocacy boundary that governs the FEC's asserted authority. Its reliance on nearby electoral events for deciding the character of the same otherwise issue-oriented words gives regulated speakers insufficient fixed guidance at that boundary.
- **Reasoning and application:** MRLC publishes candidate surveys and policy advocacy on a recurring schedule and accepts corporate treasury contributions. The financing source allows regulation where the statute and constitutional doctrine authorize it, but source cannot turn every reference to a candidate's public record into a contribution or express exhortation. The agency's 1995 explanation would allow proximity to an election to change the meaning assigned to a communication. The legal vice is the extension of *this* enacted category by regulation, not the claim that indirect electoral advocacy can never exist. Although the FEC's reading may be administratively convenient and linguistically plausible, it cannot prevail when the governing judicial construction has set a narrower line.
- **Boundary:** An explicit command to elect or defeat, including a functional phrase unambiguously instructing a vote for named candidates as in *MCFL*, is different from policy persuasion susceptible to electoral inference. Stone does not constitutionalize an exclusive vocabulary list in this case or decide later enactments.
- **What remains unchanged:** Valid regulation of direct contributions, genuinely coordinated electoral expenses, and corporate treasury expenditures within lawful statutory categories. The FEC may adopt rules within those categories using proper process; it may not expand the category through deference.

### Issue 2. Judicial access without an FEC permission slip

- **Reach:** A necessary threshold issue, resolved before merits.
- **Stone's legal position and proposed holding:** MRLC presents a live facial, pre-enforcement legal challenge on the trial findings of chilled recurring speech and a directly applicable final regulation. FECA's optional advisory-opinion channel creates no exhaustion barrier. Corbett's claimed receipt interest supports the context, but the association's actionable chill is enough; no need to fashion novel recipient-standing doctrine.
- **Reasoning:** The association cannot be required to submit every possible editorial sentence for government preclearance. The rule's legal reach is justiciable without a concrete enforcement penalty, while an entirely hypothetical speaker or future ad would be another case.
- **Boundary:** This is not a general license to challenge any dormant or unthreatened FEC provision.

### Issue 3. Appropriate scope of judgment

- **Reach:** After statutory invalidity.
- **Stone's legal position:** Complete relief requires a declaration that the operative rule is unauthorized, not an injunction against a federal officer for whom no supported enforcement threat was shown. The district court's refusal to reach vagueness was correct under constitutional avoidance.
- **Proposed holding:** Affirm the tailored declaration; leave severable § 100.22(a) standing; preserve enforcement of genuine FECA violations through lawful channels.
- **Boundary:** No invented prospective system of advisory opinions, new offense, or court-authored regulatory replacement.

**Stone canon note:** This draft applies the stable commitments on electoral speech, source-based regulation, legislative authorization, fair notice, narrow remedies, and honest precedent. It does **not** convert the Master Jurisprudence into a controlling simulated holding.
