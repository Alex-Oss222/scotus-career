## Event

Bay Area Laundry and Dry Cleaning Pension Trust Fund v. Ferbar Corp. of California, No. 96-370. Argued November 10, 1997; decided December 15, 1997. Certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 73 F.3d 971, concerning limitations on withdrawal-liability collection.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision. No nonparticipation is established.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: none. The Fund may pursue timely later installments under the rules below, but may not recover the first $345.50 installment. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment excluding that first claim. The remand awards no money, directs no full-debt payment, and grants no automatic prospective injunction.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Breyer delivers the opinion of the Court. All eight other Justices join Parts I–II on accrual, installments, acceleration and the bounded remand. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg join Part III on the first-installment exclusion; Stone-Zsela agrees in that component's judgment only. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

**1. Collection accrual requires a payment violation.** Withdrawal creates liability under 29 U.S.C. §1381, but a collection cause does not accrue until the plan has assessed liability, set a schedule and demanded payment, and the employer fails to pay an installment that is due. Section 1451(f) supplies the longer of six years after accrual or three years after the plaintiff acquired or should have acquired actual knowledge of the cause; in fraud or concealment, an action may be brought within six years after discovery of the cause. No fraud or concealment finding is made here. Parts I–II are the unanimous opinion of the Court.

The statutory sequence distinguishes liability from an enforceable delinquency. A limitation cannot begin on this collection claim before the Fund can sue for the missed payment. Knowledge of withdrawal does not itself supply knowledge of an accrued cause, and the discovery provision need not be decisive in every collection case. Milwaukee distinguishes computational dates from actual demand and payment; its calculation holding does not already decide this accrual question. North Star concerns borrowing when Congress has supplied no period, whereas §1451 supplies its own. The Ninth Circuit's March 1985 withdrawal trigger is therefore erroneous.

The plan still must assess and demand payment as soon as practicable under §1399(b)(1). Proper objections to delay remain available on their actual grounds; concern about creditor delay does not authorize substitution of withdrawal-date accrual or a finding that every delayed demand is valid. Scheduled payments commence no later than sixty days after demand notwithstanding review or appeal, as the statute provides. No exhaustion requirement postpones those payments.

**2. Installments accrue separately unless valid acceleration changes maturity.** Each missed installment gives rise to a separate collection claim and its own limitations period. An expired first-payment claim does not extinguish later timely installments; after a legally effective election to accelerate, the period on the accelerated debt runs from that exercise. A later aggregate demand cannot revive installments already barred.

Congress authorized installment obligations and distinguished ordinary delinquency from default and acceleration. Nonpayment default requires failure to cure within sixty days after written notice; an alternative plan-defined default must indicate a substantial likelihood that the employer will be unable to pay. Even a default does not itself establish an election to accelerate. The December 1986 demand, April 1987 cure warning and continued nonpayment must retain their distinct functions. Neither the warning nor delinquency proves an earlier acceleration. Whether the February 1993 complaint's alternative full-debt request itself accomplished acceleration is left open. The lower courts must identify actual demand, due dates and any effective acceleration, without inventing a payment schedule or awarding every requested installment.

**3. The Fund cannot revive its first-payment claim on this petition posture.** Having obtained review on its representation that the conflicting installment rules determined the case and that the single-period rule barred its suit, the Fund may not now recover the first $345.50 installment while obtaining reversal for the later installments. Part III controls through Breyer, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

Other submissions argued that the first payment was timely under the sixty-day provision and the demand's receipt-based terms. Those arguments do not undo the premise on which the Fund presented the controlling conflict for review. Exclusion of the first claim does not decide the unverified demand-receipt date, impose a universal February 1 maturity rule, or establish that every alternative argument forfeits a claim.

## Precedent Treatment

Milwaukee is applied for the distinction between computational assumptions and actual payment, default and acceleration; its installment-calculation rule remains unchanged. Concrete Pipe's neutral adjudication and lawful factual and actuarial rebuttal burdens remain intact; the assessment is not conclusively validated here. North Star's borrowing rule is distinguished because Congress enacted the relevant limitation. Inter-Modal's distinction between substantive plan rights and enforcement predicates is consistent with this decision but supplies no preexisting withdrawal-accrual rule. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Withdrawal-liability collection accrues upon the actual missed due payment after assessment, scheduling and demand. Independently due installments retain separate periods, subject to any valid acceleration and the particular first-claim exclusion here. The as-soon-as-practicable demand duty, interim payment obligation, neutral adjudication, ordinary defenses and statutory fraud/concealment qualification retain their own predicates. No equitable tolling, automatic assessment validity, arbitration outcome or right to every requested remedy is established.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for installment-specific timeliness and the properly remaining assessment, acceleration and remedial questions. The first $345.50 claim is excluded. The Court does not resolve the uncompleted arbitration, calculate an award or order future payments by injunction. No jurisdiction is retained and no return date is set.

## Source Notes

The Ninth Circuit's reported judgment, 73 F.3d 971, and the published recitation of the December 12, 1986 demand, April 14, 1987 cure warning, February 9, 1993 complaint and petition representations support the chronology. The demand assessed $45,570.80 and offered 240 monthly installments of $345.50; the complaint alternatively sought the full debt or $25,375 in then-due installments plus prospective relief. Original instruments and pleadings are not reproduced. The decision relies on the recorded representations without finding a new concession, exact receipt date, pre-suit acceleration, or arbitration disposition.
