* **Question Granted:** Whether the Hobbs Act may constitutionally be applied to a local robbery based on a de minimis effect on an individual business engaged in interstate commerce, on the theory that the aggregate class of robberies substantially affects interstate commerce.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit affirmed Robinson’s convictions for Hobbs Act conspiracy, aiding and abetting three Hobbs Act robberies and aiding and abetting one firearm violation, 119 F.3d at 1209, 1219. It held the Hobbs Act a permissible exercise of the commerce power, requiring only a slight effect in each case where the defendant’s conduct is of a general type which, viewed in the aggregate, substantially affects interstate commerce, id. at 1208, and rejected the facial and as-applied challenges, id. at 1214–15. It relied on evidence that the targeted stores were robbed of thousands of dollars and that the robberies impaired their ability to cash out-of-state checks and to restock goods shipped from other states, a disruption the jury necessarily found to be an effect on interstate commerce, id. at 1215. It vacated the sentence, which had been enhanced on the ground that the victims, Asian-American merchants, were unusually vulnerable, and remanded for resentencing, id. at 1208, 1219.
* **Relief Requested:** Robinson seeks reversal of the Hobbs Act convictions on Commerce Clause grounds.
* **Material Record:** Robinson committed a series of robberies of operating commercial establishments. The robberies directly depleted business assets, and the Government relied on the resulting effect on businesses engaged in interstate commerce to satisfy the Hobbs Act nexus. Sentencing issues remain part of the historical lower-court judgment but are outside the simulated grant.
