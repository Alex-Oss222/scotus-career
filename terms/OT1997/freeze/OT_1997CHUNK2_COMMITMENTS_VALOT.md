## Preserved claims and common limits

The August 16 Board refusal was admittedly caused by the three drivers' applications for and receipt of unemployment compensation. The applications concerned their personal summer benefits, not an established public-concern communication. They had successive nine-month jobs but no promise of renewal or summer employment. The superintendent recommended rehiring them. Those facts neither create a renewal entitlement nor erase an independently protected reason for governmental action.

The actual complaint has five counts. Count 1 is petition/redress; count 2 is administrative access, advanced here as retaliation for successful invocation rather than an allegation that filing or payment was prevented; count 3 includes both a rights-dependent equal-protection theory and a rational-basis alternative; count 4 is expressly limited to substantive due process; count 5 invokes Ohio public policy. The express stipulation and oral concession exclude procedural due process. No renewed-job property claim or new procedural hearing claim is created.

The later reasonable-assurance practice is a possible lawful prospective method of limiting unemployment costs. It proves neither assurance nor fraud in summer 1993. The district court's supposition of ineligibility is not adopted. The Board's fiscal exposure was dollar for dollar; the cost is real, but its constitutional significance depends on the right under examination. The repayment offer followed the refusal and does not retroactively eliminate the original expense.

All eight accept these legal boundaries:

- Perry and Mt. Healthy distinguish lack of entitlement from an unconstitutional condition, while requiring an independently protected activity. Admitted motive alone does not establish that activity's protection.
- Crawford-El (June 16, 1997), joined by every Associate, preserves ordinary proof, actual constitutional elements, individual responsibility and independent defenses. An actual same-decision defense requires the defendant's preponderance showing, not a hypothetical permissible reason. Beach's conceivable-basis rule performs a different job.
- Collins (February 26, 1992) separates the federal wrong from municipal attribution and supplies no universal public-employment substantive entitlement. Albright (January 24, 1994) has no controlling general rationale; its plurality cannot be used as a new universal specific-source or state-remedy bar.
- Personal-damages immunity concerns conduct-time law, not the clarity of today's ruling. The Board does not receive an individual's qualified immunity. Official-capacity and personal-capacity claims, actual personal participation and municipal policy attribution remain distinct.
- Chicago (December 15, 1997) distinguishes supplemental power from §1367(c) discretion. The Ohio tort claim is not the state administrative appellate proceeding that concerned the Chicago dissent. No Justice treats a change in the federal claims as a judgment on Ohio public policy.

## Competing paths and resolution of the live forks

**P — Protection for this formal benefits petition.** California Motor Transport (1972) identifies administrative adjudication within petition protection; Smith v. Arkansas State Highway Employees (1979) distinguishes the absence of a duty to grant requested relief from impermissible retaliation. Perry (1972) and Mt. Healthy (1977) prevent the lack of a renewal entitlement from deciding the retaliatory-condition question. San Filippo (3d Cir., 1994), as presented in the lower opinions, offers a persuasive distinction between ordinary workplace expression and good-faith invocation of an established governmental redress mechanism. It is neither controlling Supreme Court law nor 1993 notice.

The resulting commitment is confined to a lawful, arguable application through the State's established unemployment-benefit mechanism, punished by refusal to renew the existing recurring employment relationship solely because of that invocation and its lawful award. It does not constitutionalize the continued existence or level of benefits; the State may change entitlement rules, enforce truthful applications and valid eligibility conditions, or make independently lawful staffing decisions. Success is evidence of this record's genuine invocation, not a new universal requirement that a petition prevail. No general protection for every informal request or internal grievance is decided.

The strongest objection is that formalizing a personal employment grievance should not evade Connick. That objection has substantial circuit support and creates a serious notice issue. The five protection commitments below answer it narrowly: these employees used the separately established statutory adjudicative mechanism to obtain a lawful benefit; the Board penalized the use of that mechanism, rather than disciplining workplace speech or a supported interference with bus service. O'Hare's distinct affiliation and speech inquiries illustrate why identifying the particular First Amendment activity precedes importing a universal public-concern gate. Neither O'Hare nor Umbehr itself decides petitions.

Those five do not adopt Engel's alternative balance. Fiscal stewardship can justify ordinary economies. On this record, however, the expense offered as justification is the lawful award produced by the protected invocation itself, and deterrence of asserted abuse cannot rest on an unproved premise of abuse. No separate impairment of driving, discipline or service has been established. Successful receipt, non-tenure and the possibility of future benefit expense do not make that retaliation lawful. The five reject the stated grounds without ordering a universal employee-petition balancing code or resolving every form of petition-related disruption.

**C — Connick public-concern treatment.** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas treat the protection claimed against an employment nonrenewal as subject to the established public-employment public-concern limit. The content, form and context concern the applicants' own income replacement. A governmental program's public importance does not make each private claim public-concern expression. Filing with an agency does not, for these three, change the nature of this employment retaliation dispute enough to remove the limit. They affirm counts 1 and 2 on that sufficient ground.

The strongest objection is that the Petition Clause protects personal requests for governmental redress and that the State may not punish access to its adjudicative mechanism. These three recognize the distinction between entitlement and retaliation, and do not say successful filing itself disproves retaliation. They nevertheless read Perry as requiring antecedent protection and regard California Motor Transport and Smith as insufficient to displace the specific employee-law threshold here. Umbehr and Jeffries preserve that threshold for their actual public-concern settings. They need neither a finding of fraud nor a categorical holding that benefits applications can never be petitions. They do not adopt Ryan's claim that petition protection would make benefit legislation constitutionally unrepealable.

**Other proposed affirmance grounds.** Ryan's benefits-entitlement premise and an across-the-board rule that fiscal savings defeat a protected petition are not adopted by any commitment. A hypothetical same-decision explanation cannot affirm on causation. A renewed-employment property theory and a procedural hearing remand are unavailable. Personal immunity, even if ultimately dispositive for damages against individuals, cannot by itself dispose of the entity claim or every potentially available remedy.

**Equality fork.** Ordinary economic-classification review alone permits the Board to take account of the potential recurring benefit costs associated with rehiring known applicants; that relation to future fiscal exposure is not negated by a later repayment offer, nor does a less restrictive alternative alone establish irrationality. No suspect or quasi-suspect class is presented and no independent bare-harm finding is warranted. Beach's economic rule, Heller's requirement of a relation to the actual disadvantage, and Romer's retained limits are all respected. All eight reject the independently asserted rational-basis alternative on that bounded understanding, without finding the petitions unlawful or validating a burden on protected petitioning.

The preserved argument that this classification penalizes exercise of a constitutional right is different. The five who adopt P vacate count 3's judgment to that extent because the lower court's express premise that no protected right is burdened no longer holds. They do not announce automatic strict scrutiny for any claim styled as an access right, award separate relief, or predetermine whether any additional equality holding is necessary once the petition injury is addressed. The three adopting C affirm the entire count: their antecedent-right conclusion removes this dependent argument, and the ordinary classification survives. Affirming all of count 3 while adopting P would disregard the actual alternative presented below.

**Substantive-due-process fork.** All eight affirm count 4. These state-created economic interests and the assertedly wrongful hiring decision establish neither a fundamental substantive entitlement to continued work or benefits nor a distinct conscience-shocking constitutional deprivation on this record. The actual petition injury, if recognized, receives its own protection. This conclusion does not convert all intentional employment wrongdoing into negligence, declare that intentional government injury can never offend due process, or promote Albright's plurality into a Court rule. Treating the same alleged arbitrariness as a new property or procedural claim contradicts the preserved scope.

## Individual commitments and join barriers

### Stevens

**Commitment:** Adopt P; reverse the categorical constitutional rejection of counts 1 and 2, vacate the corresponding defense judgment for further proceedings, vacate the rights-dependent portion of count 3 and the count 5 retention disposition, and affirm count 4 and rejection of the independent ordinary-classification alternative. Deny the requested immediate plaintiffs' liability judgment. Join the bounded remand and all qualifications stated below.

**Individual authority:** His O'Hare opinion (June 28, 1996) separately identifies the relevant protected activity and rejects property-entitlement and generalized-benefit shortcuts. His Jeffries joins in Parts I–III (November 17, 1997) reject a categorical employment-balance disposition and preserve individual causation and remedies. His Collins opinion (February 26, 1992) supplies both §1983's right/attribution separation and the substantive-due-process limit. His Heller proof-burden join (June 24, 1993) requires attention to the actual disadvantage; his Romer join (May 20, 1996) preserves ordinary review rather than automatic heightened scrutiny.

**Alternative and barrier:** Connick treatment has a substantial employee-management rationale, but it would make formal invocation of this external statutory remedy unprotected solely because the award is personal. Stevens distinguishes that activity instead of declaring these applications public-concern speech. He will not join a universal immunity for every grievance, a perpetual benefit entitlement, a final liability finding against the superintendent merely because of office, or Merritt's compulsory-trial remedy. His own Collins ground supports affirming count 4 even while the petition claim survives.

### O'Connor

**Commitment:** Adopt C; affirm counts 1–4 and the discretionary nonretention of count 5. Her ground on counts 1–2 is the lack of public concern, not absence of a renewal entitlement. No personal immunity disposition is necessary to her judgment.

**Individual authority:** Her joins in Umbehr and O'Hare (June 28, 1996) preserve both existing employee protections and the distinct predicate applicable to each kind of First Amendment activity. Her Jeffries dissent (November 17, 1997), joined by Scalia and Thomas, gives genuine administrative interests their proper role within employment doctrine. Her Collins joins (February 26, 1992) reject a general employment substantive-due-process guarantee. Her Beach joins (June 1, 1993) and her separate Heller component joins (June 24, 1993) accept plausible economic distinctions while rejecting irrationally assigned disadvantages; her Romer join (May 20, 1996) prevents treating rational basis as no review.

**Alternative and barrier:** P has force because this is a formal and successful benefits claim with no established driving disruption. She nevertheless regards the claim against an employment decision as a private employee grievance within the public-concern boundary. Her Jeffries position does not itself establish the petition rule; it supports maintaining the employer-specific inquiry rather than treating the agency form as dispositive. She would not join a categorical assertion that mere savings excuse retaliation against established protected activity, a fraud finding, or automatic personal immunity from lack of tenure. The direct fiscal relation suffices for the independent equality claim without overriding a protected right she finds absent here.

### Scalia

He will not join Ryan's inference that a protected right to ask would entrench the benefit program, or use his contractor dissents as controlling law. ## Immunity, mandate and remedy boundaries

For the P disposition, return the case to the Sixth Circuit for further proceedings consistent with the identified federal corrections, with ordinary district-court proceedings as appropriate. The final defense judgment cannot stand as an undifferentiated rejection of all four federal counts. Count 4 and the independent ordinary-equality alternative remain rejected; only the identified petition/access and rights-dependent equality portions are reopened. The mandate does not create additional privileges-and-immunities claims or a freestanding general right of administrative access under every clause cited below. Whether another constitutional characterization would add anything to the same surviving injury need not be decided to grant the bounded relief.

The Board's admission settles its stated reason for the refusal; it does not concede that each individual defendant caused it or that every required entity-liability predicate has been established. The superintendent's favorable recommendation must be retained and cannot be converted into a hostile vote. The remand permits adjudication of actual participation, capacities, final policymaking authority and any legally sufficient same-decision defense. It neither invents a factual dispute against the admission nor orders defendants to prove motives already admitted.

Harlow (1982) and Anderson v. Creighton (1987) supply the particularized objective protection. Elder's current appellate rule and Lanier's current fair-warning principles permit all relevant pre-existing authority, including lower-court decisions and genuinely obvious applications; neither requires a factual twin or a Supreme-Court-only precedent. They cannot be used to supply notice after the fact. The pre-August 1993 public-concern circuit decisions recited below, including White Plains Towing, Schalk, Hoffmann, Rathjen, Belk, Gearhart and Renfroe, are substantial obstacles to an assertion that today's protected-petition application was then clearly established. Their actual contexts and the pre-existing petition/access authorities must be assessed rather than counted mechanically. The lower concurrence's reference to Bradley (1990) as benefits-related dicta is not converted into binding or automatically sufficient notice.

The strongest competing remedial path is to grant personal immunity now because that authority demonstrates a substantial unsettled boundary. The five nevertheless return the defense, which the panel expressly left undecided, for its distinct conduct-specific legal application and remaining personal predicates. This is a definite limited-remand judgment, not an assumption that more discovery or a trial is required. Supported legal immunity may dispose of damages promptly; the remand makes no finding that the right was clearly established. The first-impression petition merits question remains worth deciding because the entity and other proper relief are not automatically extinguished by personal immunity. This sequencing is permitted, not declared mandatory for other cases.

San Filippo (1994), Umbehr/O'Hare (1996), Crawford-El and Jeffries (1997), and this January 1998 petition holding cannot establish August 1993 notice. An admitted retaliatory purpose or a state-law wrong does not independently defeat objective immunity. A genuinely established earlier challenged act must be examined at its own date; no additional act is assumed from the June conversation. Neither punitive relief nor liability against a particular Board member follows from the collective admission.

No reinstatement, backpay, punitive damages, refund, compulsory hearing, compulsory trial, unrestricted discovery or ongoing entitlement to employment is ordered. Injury, eligible defendants, individual defenses, mitigation and the prerequisites of each requested remedy remain independent. The passage of school years and the later assurance policy may matter to a requested prospective remedy, but supply no automatic mootness or continuing-eligibility finding.

Section 1367(c)(3) is a permission triggered by dismissal of all original-jurisdiction claims, not an absence of power and not mandatory dismissal. Under P that stated basis requires reconsideration because federal claims survive; other lawful §1367(c) considerations remain available on their actual predicates. Under C the original premise remains and nonretention is permissible. Under neither disposition is the Ohio tort established, rejected on its merits or ordered retained. No source gap establishes that plaintiffs forfeited it.
