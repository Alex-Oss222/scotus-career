The grant's scope is an authenticated analytical formulation; unrecovered original petition or charge pages are not claimed to have been read. **Text and conduct.** The full 1988-edition §1001 reproduced in the entering-law slice governs the 1993 answer. Its false-statements clause reaches one who, within the specified department-or-agency matter, knowingly and willfully “makes any false, fictitious or fraudulent statements or representations.” Its separate concealment clause's trick/scheme/device wording and material-fact reference are not silently redistributed among the clauses. Its then-existing fine/prison language is not replaced by the 1994 or 1996 revisions. Labor and IRS are executive agencies; this case does not present Hubbard's excluded-court-entity nexus.

Brogan agreed to answer during an unexpected home visit. Asked about JRD money or gifts, he said no. Agents already had contrary records; they disclosed those records and warned that lying was criminal **after** the answer. Their later warning does not establish knowledge before the answer, and his failure to revise it is not a new charged affirmative statement. The reported concession of falsity and knowing/willful conduct is preserved without treating it as a universal definition of willfulness or a reconstruction of the charge. No custody, coercion, entrapment, illicit purpose, or actual manipulation finding is invented from the unannounced visit.

## Adversarial alternatives and remedial consequences

**Path A — reject the exception and affirm the reviewed conviction.** A negative answer conveys a factual proposition; “any” statement is not naturally limited to a volunteered narrative, regulatory submission, benefit application, oath, or successful deception. Gilliland's description of protecting governmental functions does not add a requirement that investigators actually be deceived or a separate perversion element. An authorized criminal investigation remains a governmental function. Fifth Amendment protection permits lawful silence or resistance, rather than a general privilege deliberately to answer falsely. General background rules of interpretation may qualify broad text, but this proposed exculpatory-no rule needs its own sufficient statutory basis.

The strongest objection is contextual: a statute originating in fraud against the Government and later regulatory reporting can be understood to leave an ordinary defensive oral denial outside its intended felony coverage. Surprise questioning with the facts already known can generate a new federal offense even where the underlying conduct is unprovable, time-barred, or innocent. Long lower-court practice, executive policies, and prior narrower readings of general criminal statutes support a serious legal argument rather than mere dislike of punishment. The Path A Justices nevertheless conclude that these considerations do not authorize this categorical exclusion from the enacted false-statements language. Ginsburg and Souter treat the abuse concern as substantial and demand legislative attention; the other four also accept the distinct response that it does not justify revising Congress's separate offense and that a simple-denial exception would not reliably prevent escalation into an elaborated story. These positions must not be flattened into one unqualified account of prosecutorial propriety.

**Path B — recognize the bounded statutory exception and reverse as to §1001.** A bare reactive denial of the questioned person's own suspected wrongdoing during the unsolicited criminal inquiry falls within a settled, limited construction, while volunteered deception, affirmative misdirection, regulatory reports, benefit submissions, and embellished exculpatory stories remain outside that exemption. The Second Circuit treated this answer as a true exculpatory no under the doctrine used elsewhere; the exact record therefore permits application without constructing a hypothetical border case. The construction concerns congressional intended coverage informed by the statute's purpose and established practice. It is not a constitutional entitlement to lie, an enforceable right under the prosecution manual, legislative ratification by silence, or an adopted provision of an unenacted bill.

Its strongest objection is the complete statutory language: a knowing negative assertion remains a statement, and the proposed reactive/volunteered distinction is not express. Gilliland rejected a narrow financial-interest limitation, the lower courts disagreed over the doctrine's edges, and the Fifth and Second Circuits rejected it. The majority's general-background-principle versus discretionary-exception objection is substantial. Stevens and Breyer nevertheless find a defensible bounded statutory construction in the distinct ordinary-denial context and decades of concrete practice; for them the historical purpose and established interpretation do legal work rather than merely supply an equitable wish. No supplied simulated decision displaces that distinction. Their reversal concerns the §1001 count; it does not vacate the separate payment conviction, order an acquittal on that offense, mandate release, or decide unprovided sentence consequences.

**Path C — reject the exception but order a different remand.** Actual materiality, culpability, notice, compulsion, or jury error could support a different remedy on a properly presented record. The lower court's reservations about knowledge of illegality and surprise are not adjudicated errors or proof that such a claim is before this Court. Gaudin does not establish that an element was withheld from this jury; Lanier does not establish an unforeseeable-enlargement violation here; the warning sequence does not itself prove a dispositive error. None of the eight selects this speculative remand. The affirmers decide the reviewed construction and leave genuinely unpresented issues alone; the two reversers have a sufficient construction ground without invented factfinding.

**Framework breadth.** The six affirmers can support the narrow operative proposition that the offense-date statute has no categorical exception merely because a knowingly/willfully false statement is a simple exculpatory denial. This proposition is narrower than full assent to every supporting framework. It does not establish that every denial is willful, that agents' knowledge is always immaterial, that everyone must confess, that every silence inference is lawful, that an oath or warning can never matter to an actual issue, or that every false-denial prosecution is proper. The two reversers support a bounded criminal statutory exception, not an across-the-board privilege against employment consequences or a rule exempting every unsworn statement.
