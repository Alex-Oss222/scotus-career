## Result and operative rule

All eight Associates affirm the Ninth Circuit's rejection of the nonprofit distinction and reversal of the adverse Rule 52(c) judgment. Even assuming content neutrality, the City may not permit a qualifying nonprofit's protected expressive sales and donation solicitation in a traditional public forum while prohibiting materially comparable activity by unaffiliated individuals without a sufficient relation between that distinction and the asserted congestion or commercial harm. The actual record establishes no such relation. Nonprofit status does not make the same activity less obstructive or less competitive with local merchants.

The trial court found these plaintiffs' goods inseparably connected with their protected messages. Perry's music and Newman's message-bearing materials are therefore not treated as ordinary merchandise merely because a transaction includes money. The City's accepted significant interests do not by themselves justify the organizational line. Its suggestion that Newman could obtain nonprofit status underscores that status changes paperwork or affiliation without changing the relevant physical activity.

The ordinance's two exceptions must remain distinct: newspapers, magazines, periodicals, and printed matter commonly sold or disposed of by news vendors; and nonprofit donation solicitation plus nonprofit merchandise bearing a religious, philosophical, or ideological message relevant to the organization's purpose. The ruling concerns the nonprofit distinction applied to the protected activity, not an unexplained invalidation of the separate printed-matter exception.

## Ground, alternatives, precedent, and remedy

Ward and Clark permit content-neutral time, place, and manner rules that serve significant interests with proper tailoring and adequate channels. Discovery Network requires defending the selected line's relation to the actual harm; reducing some activity is not sufficient merely because any numerical reduction helps. It does not require regulating every harm simultaneously or selecting the least restrictive means. Heffron recognizes legitimate management of public movement without erasing protection because expression includes solicitation or sales.

The strongest affirmance-of-the-City path treats nonprofit status as an administrable proxy for expression rather than ordinary commerce. The trial findings defeat that premise for the very conduct burdened here. A supported classification correlating with actual physical or commercial effects would present a different case. The City may defend such laws when enacted, but an imagined blanket vending ban cannot validate this ordinance.

The message-relevance requirement presents a serious content-classification question. The common rationale assumes neutrality in the City's favor and resolves tailoring; it neither declares the actual restriction content neutral nor infers neutrality solely from benign purpose. Ladue supports that method of resolving a case on a sufficient assumed-neutrality ground, without transferring its residential-sign holding into a universal boardwalk-sales entitlement. Rosenberger retains its bounded viewpoint rule and does not make every organizational distinction viewpoint discrimination. McIntyre retains its own disclosure scrutiny. Madsen's injunction standard is distinguished, not extended.

No independent alternative-channel holding is needed after the tailoring failure. Free distribution, other locations, or compelled nonprofit affiliation are not adjudged adequate substitutes on speculation. The ordinary remedy is affirmance of the Ninth Circuit's reversal, leaving further proceedings to implement the constitutional limit. No exemption is mechanically deleted, every seller admitted, permit program prescribed, damages or fees awarded, or severability question decided. Ordinary merchandise and otherwise lawful physical, temporal, and location controls remain outside this holding.
