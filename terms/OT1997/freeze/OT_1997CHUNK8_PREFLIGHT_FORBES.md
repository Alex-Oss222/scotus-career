## Record ready for modeling

The reviewed Eighth Circuit judgment is reported at 93 F.3d 497. AETC is a state-owned public broadcaster. In spring 1992 its staff and an Associated Press bureau chief planned five candidate debates, with approximately 53 minutes of questions and answers in each hour. The selection category was major-party candidates plus other candidates with strong popular support. Invitations to the two major candidates preceded Forbes's ballot qualification, which followed submission of 2,000 signatures. Forbes sought access after qualification and was refused. The stated grounds concerned organization, voter support and press recognition as a serious candidate. No broader past selection practice is established.

The jury rejected political pressure and disagreement with Forbes's views as causes of exclusion. Those findings must be respected; the remaining legal inquiry includes whether the actual selective program is a forum and whether the supported criteria and exclusion are constitutionally sufficient. A standards-based objection cannot be presented as a jury finding of viewpoint bias, and the stated spring selection category cannot be described as an absence of any advance criterion. Its definiteness and constitutional sufficiency are disputed legal questions.

The prior statutory-access claim was dismissed for nonexhaustion and is no longer pressed. Ballot qualification establishes that Forbes was a lawful candidate, not by itself a constitutional right to every broadcast. The lower court's judgment applying a public-forum theory is the judgment under review, not entering Supreme Court law. No exact petition quotation, recovered original affidavit, full trial transcript or new factual finding is claimed. The supported published recitations suffice for the bounded constitutional question; no invented record premise is needed.

## Entering law after the May 11 decision

Bronx's final public projection was read in full. Its controlling instruction rule forbids excluding a religious perspective on an actually admitted instructional subject. Its five-Justice worship holding applies that principle to otherwise comparable use in the particular broad community forum, preserving genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral subject, activity and operational boundaries. Consistent exclusion alone does not prove neutrality. Its unanimous establishment holding concerns attribution of private worship and creates no speech-access right by itself.

For Forbes, the material consequence is that a program's actual operation and admitted uses matter. The government cannot establish a neutral boundary simply by consistently excluding a disfavored perspective. But Bronx creates neither a universal public-forum designation nor an entitlement to every government-supported expressive opportunity. A managed, selective candidate debate can be distinguished from broad rental access if its genuine structure and editorial function support that distinction. The jury's actual neutrality findings remain independent of the question whether sufficient access standards exist.

Rosenberger's program-specific viewpoint rule, Good News/Good Sports' actual-comparator inquiry and Denver Area's component-specific protection of expression remain operative at their stated scopes. Denver Area establishes no universal cable or broadcasting forum rule and no general private-editor immunity for a government broadcaster. Its divided permission rationales cannot be combined into a new controlling test. Perry Education and Cornelius preserve genuine forum definition and reasonable viewpoint-neutral management. No prior rule makes either the public ownership of the station or its journalistic label conclusive.

The Benjamin public projection was read in full. Its PLRA termination, changed prospective duties and evidence-presentation holdings supply no new broadcast-forum rule or compulsory evidentiary proceeding here. Its case-specific need for current proof is not a reason to disregard Forbes's completed jury findings.

The earlier B preflight's case description was incorrect. The dismissal supplies no new merits rule and changes none of the B commitments or this Forbes analysis.
