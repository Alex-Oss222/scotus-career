## Reconciled result and exact reach

All eight Associates affirm the Seventh Circuit. Historical unanimity confirms the frozen result because the decisive factual premise remains intact: each sentence is within the applicable cocaine-only statutory maximum, given the attributed cocaine quantities, and the crack transactions form the same course of conduct or common scheme as the convicted conspiracy. Current Witte and Watts preserve bounded ordinary relevant-conduct sentencing. Current Almendarez-Torres requires attention to offense-based punishment authority but does not invalidate ordinary findings within that authority or announce a universal drug-quantity classification.

The Court can assume, without deciding, that the drug statutes or Constitution require treating the general verdict as a cocaine-only conviction. The assumption does not change the Guidelines range here: section 1B1.3 includes conduct of the conviction and conduct within the same course or common scheme; section 2D1.1 uses the kind and quantity properly attributable under those rules. The judge therefore could make supported cocaine and crack findings for this ordinary sentencing function. The Court need not decide the merits of the proposed cocaine-only interpretation, the general constitutionality of alternative instructions, or every statutory drug-element question. It must not say the jury actually found crack or rejected it.

## Grounds, alternatives, and remedy

Witte supplies the distinction between use of relevant conduct and independent punishment; Watts supplies ordinary conduct-finding permission within conviction-based authority, with its actual proof, reliability, due-process, Guidelines, and Rule 32 limits. The common rationale need not demand new adherence to every past writing by Justices who did not join it. Gaudin continues to require jury determination of actual offense elements; Almendarez-Torres preserves the distinction between a new maximum and an ordinary within-range calculation. Griffin does not supply a factual finding by this jury or cure an element-diluting instruction.

The strongest contrary argument is that crack sentencing evades the jury's role because the general verdict might be cocaine-only. It fails as a categorical objection here even on that assumption: the same-course conduct rule yields the same Guidelines range, while the cocaine-only statutory limits bound the sentence. A sentence beyond those limits or conduct outside the relevant-conduct rule would present a different case. No such premise is supported here, and source incompleteness alone does not require a new sentencing inquiry.

The related claim that the cocaine-only assumption would have changed the judge's factual findings or leniency was not presented at sentencing. Failure to raise it is not an invented intentional waiver. The published account describes independent findings unaffected by the proposed assumption; the Court need not create an Olano/Johnson plain-error holding when its selected ground identifies no error on the categorical claim. Rogers supplies no general omitted-element harmless-error rule.

Affirmance orders no replacement sentence, new trial, acquittal, range, or factfinding hearing. It approves neither disguised independent punishment nor extraordinary magnification, removal of an element, punishment above conviction-based authority, or disregard of independent safeguards. No separate writing is necessary if the opinion retains these boundaries. No material historical departure is established; no author is assigned.
