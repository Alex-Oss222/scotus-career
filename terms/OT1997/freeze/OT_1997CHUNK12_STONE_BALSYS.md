## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Self-incrimination based on foreign prosecution

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the effect of a real risk of prosecution by an independent foreign sovereign. He reserves whether sufficiently coordinated foreign prosecution could become attributable to the United States.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The Self-Incrimination Clause protects against compelled testimony used in a criminal case within the constitutional system it binds. Murdock and Murphy prevent domestic sovereigns from evading a shared constitutional restraint; they do not extend the privilege to an independent foreign prosecution.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A real fear of prosecution by an independent foreign sovereign, without more, does not permit refusal to testify under the Fifth Amendment. The subpoena may be enforced subject to any distinct domestic-use, immunity, treaty, or preserved objection.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Balsys proved a genuine foreign risk but no threatened domestic prosecution and no coordinated foreign proceeding attributable to the United States. The undeveloped possibility of a domestic proxy cannot supply an exception on this record.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not authorize domestic derivative use contrary to immunity, unlawful transfer of testimony, breach of a treaty, or circumvention through a coordinated foreign proxy. It does not decide when foreign cooperation becomes attributable to the United States.
