# United States v. Scheffer

**Docket:** 96-1133. **Proposed event:** 1998-03-31. **Argument:** November 3, 1997.

**Question (analytical):** Whether Military Rule of Evidence 707’s categorical exclusion of polygraph evidence unconstitutionally abridges an accused’s right to present a defense. [Source formulation; not certified as a verbatim petition quotation.]


**Reported record, copied from the supplied neutral packet:** A polygraph examination of respondent airman indicated, in the opinion of the Air Force examiner administering the test, that there was “no deception” in respondent's denial that he had used drugs since enlisting. In denying his motion to introduce the polygraph evidence to support his testimony that he did not knowingly use drugs, the military judge relied on Military Rule of Evidence 707, which makes polygraph evidence inadmissible in court-martial proceedings. Respondent was convicted on all counts, and the Air Force Court of Criminal Appeals affirmed. The Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces reversed, holding that a per se exclusion of polygraph evidence offered by an accused to support his credibility violates his Sixth Amendment right to present a defense. [Factual and procedural account from the reported opinion’s introductory material; disputed submissions remain disputed.]

### Law entering the event

The question concerns Military Rule of Evidence 707's categorical treatment of polygraph results, examiner opinion and references to taking, offering or failing to take a polygraph. The full rule, including subsection (b)'s preservation of otherwise-admissible statements made during an examination, must be carried from its term-effective source. The constitutional claim is not a general right to introduce any exculpatory material, and excluding the examiner's credibility assessment is not necessarily excluding the accused's underlying factual testimony.

The exact standard **Retained mental-state elements and intoxication rebuttal** (Egelhoff, **1996-06-13**) forbids unjustified restrictions on otherwise-admissible rebuttal of retained elements, while expressly preserving relevance, reliability, privilege and fair-management rules. It does not validate or invalidate Rule 707 automatically. **Scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702** (Daubert, **1993-06-28**) displaces mandatory general acceptance under those Rules, requires the proponent's preponderance foundation of reliability and fit and supplies flexible nonexclusive considerations. Joiner, **1997-12-15**, adds uniform abuse-of-discretion review and permits examination of inferential connection. Neither interpretation of the general Federal Rules itself invalidates a separately enacted military rule or requires categorical constitutional acceptance of polygraphs.

### Lawful paths and strongest counterarguments

1. **Sustain Rule 707 in the presented application:** Reliability disagreement, limitations of the proffer, protection of the factfinder's credibility role and collateral litigation burdens may supply sufficient grounds for a uniform military evidentiary rule where the accused still presents his account and supporting ordinary evidence. The strongest counterargument is overbreadth: a complete ban may exclude a particular reliably established defense proffer without examining its foundation, contrary to the justified-restriction principle in Egelhoff and Rock.
2. **Reject categorical exclusion as applied and remand for lawful foundation review:** Distinguish an unreliable proffer from a rule forbidding all inquiry even where meaningful reliability and defense significance could be shown. The strongest counterargument is that an individualized hearing itself may impose the very costs and credibility confusion the rule reasonably addresses; Daubert's statutory flexibility is not a constitutional command for every rulemaker. A constitutional defect in the categorical premise does not establish admissibility, the truth of the test result or acquittal.
3. A narrower rationale may sustain this proffer or recognize regulation without a universal conclusion about every polygraph use. The actual question and lower remand must support such a narrowing. No new scientific accuracy percentage, individualized reliability finding or automatic reinstatement of every charge is supplied.

### Date-eligible Justice-specific anchors

O'Connor wrote Egelhoff with Stevens, Souter and Breyer; its ordinary reliability reservation is material to each. Souter's concurrence expressly leaves room for a convincing evidentiary justification. Breyer/Stevens emphasize testing the actual mental element rather than substituting a proxy. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas's dissent would permit the specific historical intoxication restriction, while Ginsburg dissents solely on genuine culpability definition and does not join the additional evidentiary rationale. Joiner's inferential-fit rule and review rule are unanimous; Stevens resists that record's first-instance application, and Breyer emphasizes practical judicial assistance without making it mandatory. These are relevant individual commitments without resolving the different categorical military rule.

### Record, remedy and source limitations

The precise CAAF remedy, the opportunity for foundation evidence and Rule 707(b) must be verified from the sanitized source sheet. **Docket:** 96-1768. **Proposed event:** 1998-03-31. **Argument:** January 21, 1998.

[Factual and procedural account from the reported opinion’s introductory material; disputed submissions remain disputed.]

### Law entering the event

The questions are statutory allocation under 17 U.S.C. §504(c) and, if necessary, the Seventh Amendment allocation of statutory-damages facts and amount. Statutory interpretation comes first, but whether the text permits a jury is an issue rather than an established threshold conclusion. The historical phrase referring to what the court considers just must be read in context; it cannot alone settle constitutional power. The full offense-period provision, its per-work rule, range, willfulness increase, innocent-infringer reduction and qualifying mandatory remission must be provided by the sanitized source text rather than filled from current-dollar provisions.

The exact standard **Judicial claim construction and the retained infringement jury function** has present operation **Vitronics, 1997-05-27** for permissible technical consultation, while **Markman, 1996-04-23**, retains the actual judge/jury allocation. A legal damages action can contain a judicial legal-meaning function; that holding does not place every discretionary amount in judicial hands. **State compensation limits within federal new-trial review** (Gasperini, **1996-06-24**) separates initial factfinding, Rule 59 review and bounded appellate review. Hetzel, **1998-03-23**, unanimously preserves the election between lawful excessiveness remittitur and appropriate new jury trial, with separability required for a damages-only retrial. Neither review doctrine supplies an original copyright amount-setting rule.

Tull, 481 U.S. 412 (1987), uses the historical analogue and remedy to identify jury protection but allows judicial amount-setting for the particular governmental civil penalty; Scalia and Stevens object to removing an unfixed legal award from the jury. Whether copyright statutory damages have the relevant historical legal analogue and amount function must be analyzed rather than assumed from the civil-penalty label.

### Lawful paths and strongest counterarguments

1. **Statutory jury construction:** If §504(c), read in its full setting, fairly permits the jury to determine disputed damages facts and amount, resolve the statutory issue without unnecessarily deciding the constitutional claim. The strongest objection is that the enactment may designate the judge, with text/history and the different subsection functions supporting that reading. Avoidance is not authority to rewrite an unambiguous assignment.
2. **No statutory jury right, constitutional jury allocation:** If the statute does not itself provide a jury, the legal nature and history of copyright monetary relief may require jury determination of the amount and its factual predicates. The strongest objection is Tull's issue-specific allocation and the discretion involved in the statutory range; answer whether copyright's historical function differs materially, not merely whether the action seeks money.
3. **Judicial amount-setting permitted:** If the historical allocation and particular remedy properly fall within Tull's distinction, sustain the statutory procedure subject to ordinary legal constraints. The strongest objection is the traditional jury role in compensatory and punitive damages and Markman's warning that complexity or convenience alone cannot remove protected facts. Each constitutional path must identify the scope of any preserved jury demand and legal/factual division.

# United States v. United States Shoe Corp.

**Docket:** 97-372. **Proposed event:** 1998-03-31. **Argument:** March 4, 1998.

**Question (analytical):** Whether the Harbor Maintenance Tax is an unconstitutional tax on exports or a permissible user fee. [Source formulation; not certified as a verbatim petition quotation.]


**Reported record, copied from the supplied neutral packet:** The Harbor Maintenance Tax (HMT) obligates exporters, importers, and domestic shippers, 26 U. S. C. § 4461(c)(1), to pay 0.125 percent of the value of the commercial cargo they ship through the Nation's ports, § 4461(a). The HMT is imposed at the time of loading for exports and unloading for other shipments. § 4461(c)(2). It is collected by the Customs Service and deposited in the Harbor Maintenance Trust Fund (Fund), from which Congress may appropriate amounts to pay for harbor maintenance and development projects and related expenses. § 9505. Respondent United States Shoe Corporation (U. S. Shoe) paid the HMT for articles the company exported during the period April to June 1994 and then filed a protest with the Customs Service alleging that, to the extent the toll applies to exports, it violates the Export Clause, U. S. Const., Art. I, § 9, cl. 5, which states: “No Tax or Duty shall be laid on Articles exported from any State.” The Customs Service responded to U. S. Shoe with a form letter stating that the HMT is a statutorily mandated user fee, not an unconstitutional tax on exports. U. S. Shoe then sued for a refund, asserting that the HMT violates the Export Clause as applied to exports. In granting U. S. Shoe summary judgment, the Court of International Trade (CIT) held that it had jurisdiction under 28 U. S. C. § 1581(i) and that the HMT qualifies as a tax. Rejecting the Government's characterization of the HMT as a user fee, the CIT reasoned that the tax is assessed ad valorem directly upon the value of the cargo itself, not upon any services rendered for the cargo. The Federal Circuit affirmed. [Factual and procedural account from the reported opinion’s introductory material; disputed submissions remain disputed.]

### Law entering the event

**Issue 1: jurisdiction.** The Court of International Trade's authority must be established under the correct branch of 28 U.S.C. §1581. A Customs protest response and an independently applicable congressional exaction are not necessarily the same kind of agency decision. The strongest §1581(i) route treats this as a revenue-law administration/enforcement controversy without a protestable Customs determination; the competing route treats a refund challenge and Customs response as within the protest system and §1581(a). The appropriate exhaustion and jurisdiction analysis turns on actual statutory authority and agency action, not the word protest alone. The stale packet's assertion that jurisdiction already exists is removed.

**Issue 2: Export Clause.** IBM, **1996-06-10**, controls: nondiscrimination is no answer to a federal tax upon exports or the export-transit insurance category already protected by Thames & Mersey. IBM leaves other genuine services and tangential transactions open. No separate Export Clause Standards module is present; the full IBM Holdings entry controls. The standard **Reasonable aircraft-operator airport charges** (Northwest Airlines, **1994-01-24**) construes a different statute using fair approximation, relation to benefits and nondiscrimination; it is a possible fee analogy, not an automatic replacement for the Export Clause. Pace supplies the distinction between an export tax and compensation for actual governmental services and safeguards, while allowing reasonable administrative approximations.

### Lawful paths and strongest counterarguments

1. **Export tax:** A charge attached to export loading and calculated directly on cargo value may tax the protected goods rather than compensate for particular services. Dedicated expenditure on harbors and the user-fee label alone do not establish the necessary connection. The strongest counterargument is that harbor maintenance yields actual services to all shipping and a legislatively chosen proxy need not exactly measure marginal benefit for every shipment.
2. **Genuine service fee:** If value reasonably measures the relevant services or benefits within the constitutionally permissible approximation, sustain the charge at that scope. The strongest objection is an unsupported relationship between ad valorem value and actual harbor usage or services; the Export Clause's absolute tax prohibition cannot be reduced to general nondiscrimination or aggregate funding rationality. A tax may not become a fee merely because revenues fund a related service.
3. A jurisdictional defect would prevent the chosen merits remedy through that channel. A merits holding must address the export application actually sued upon; it does not automatically decide imports, domestic movements, a differently measured replacement levy or all harbor expenditures. Refund relief remains confined to properly preserved payments and claims, without invented interest, new claim periods or computation.

### Date-eligible Justice-specific anchors and source limits

Thomas's IBM majority has O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer's joins; each accepted the categorical rejection of nondiscrimination as a defense to the tax at issue while reserving broader service classification. Kennedy's IBM dissent, joined by Ginsburg, distinguishes a purchased indemnity service from goods, would reconsider Thames & Mersey to that extent, and does not adopt a nondiscrimination safe harbor for actual goods taxation. Stevens did not participate in IBM; no missing position or current recusal is inferred. Ginsburg's Northwest Airlines majority includes Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; Thomas's disagreement with that statutory construction must not be converted into an Export Clause view without reasons.
