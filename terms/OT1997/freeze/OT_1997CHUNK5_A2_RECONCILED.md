## Component commitments

**U1 — announcement standard.** Property damage during warrant execution does not create a categorical higher degree of exigency than the particularized reasonable suspicion that Richards requires for dispensing with announcement. Officers must have facts known when the challenged force begins supporting reasonable suspicion that ordinary announcement would create danger, be futile or impede the investigation in a recognized way. A no-knock warrant label and the offense category do not independently meet that requirement. Destructive means remain subject to separate Fourth Amendment reasonableness scrutiny.

**U2 — constitutional application and force.** The credited pre-entry information supplies particularized reasonable suspicion of danger: a reliable informant reported seeing the dangerous escaped prisoner at the house and possible weapons in its garage, and an agent corroborated the presence of a similar-looking man. The single-window break and pointing a firearm through that opening were directed at preventing access to the reported weapons during entry. On these supplied facts that limited destruction was reasonable. Neither the number of officers outside nor the possibility that the believed fugitive was absent eliminates the particular indoor danger known beforehand. The subsequent gunfire does not supply the justification; the later failure to find Shelby does not retroactively remove it. This application authorizes neither gratuitous damage nor any amount of destruction merely because announcement can be excused.

**U3 — independent §3109 construction and application.** The statute's ordinary command requires notice of authority and purpose and refusal of admittance before breaking, subject to its express liberation alternative. Simultaneous announcement and breaking do not establish ordinary compliance, and no trapped officer or assistant establishes the liberation alternative. Nevertheless, the common-law announcement rule codified by §3109 includes a narrowly supported danger exception; particularized reasonable suspicion that following the ordinary process would expose officers or occupants to danger is sufficient for this exception. The statute includes opening a closed unlocked door within breaking, as Sabbath holds, and does not create a separate announcement-exigency tier when entry also damages property. The same pre-entry violent-fugitive and garage-weapons information satisfies this narrowly adopted statutory danger exception here. No statutory rule concerning every futility, escape or evidence-destruction situation is necessary to the decision.

**U4 — judgment and remedy.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remand. The suppression judgment cannot stand on the categorical heightened standard or on the challenged entry and single-window destruction as assessed under U1–U3. No universal exclusionary rule is decided, and the later statements, subsequent warrant and recovered guns are not declared automatically attenuated, independently sourced or inevitably discoverable. Because the identified entry and property acts are justified, no causal exception is needed to resolve those asserted suppression grounds. Any genuinely separate, properly preserved claim remains governed by its own facts and law; no unsupported alternative claim, acquittal, conviction or sentence is created.

## Enhanced statutory and force comparison

The strongest competing constitutional path would treat destructive entry as categorically requiring more exigency because property damage aggravates the intrusion and announcement protects occupants against mistaken resistance. The answer is to scrutinize the destructive means themselves, rather than change Richards's announcement threshold whenever any glass or wood is broken. The reliable dangerous-fugitive report and location-specific weapons information justify the stated security objective, and the limited act is proportionate on this supplied record. A broad rule allowing whatever damage an officer chooses fails that same independent scrutiny and is not joined.

The strongest competing statutory path reads Congress's express notice/refusal conditions and separate liberation language as excluding any additional bypass. Miller and Sabbath did not hold a general exception, so their reservations must be respected. The contrary conclusion adopted in U3 rests on statutory construction now: §3109 codifies the common-law entry rule, and its traditional limited danger qualification is part of that rule rather than an unrestricted judicial addition. Sabbath's footnote 8 supplies direct interpretive support while its actual application rejects unsupported danger; Wilson's common-law account and Richards's particularized standard reinforce that limited understanding. The express liberation alternative deals with breaking to free an officer or helper already impeded; recognizing the antecedent common-law danger qualification does not erase or expand that distinct alternative. The conclusion is not that every constitutional exception automatically rewrites a statute.

The counterargument that the officers' objective is not proof of necessity remains valid as a limit: purpose alone cannot establish justification. Here the corroborated escaped-prisoner report and specific garage-weapons information supply the additional basis, assessed before force. No unreported testimony, refusal of admittance, occupant awareness or subsequent-warrant fact is supplied by inference. The accepted record permits this bounded legal application without inventing an additional factual finding.
