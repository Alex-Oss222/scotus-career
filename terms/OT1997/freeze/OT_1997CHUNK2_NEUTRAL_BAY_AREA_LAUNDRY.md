## 3. Bay Area Laundry and Dry Cleaning Pension Trust Fund v. Ferbar — December 15, 1997

### Event, record, and question

The Fund is the petitioner seeking reversal of a limitations dismissal; the runtime's description of the employer's position as the position supporting requested change is incorrect. Ferbar defends dismissal. Argument was November 10, 1997. Ferbar Corporation and Stephen Barnes owned three laundries; contributions for one stopped in 1983 and for the others in March 1985. The December 12, 1986 demand assessed $45,570.80, offering full payment within sixty days after receipt or 240 monthly installments of $345.50 beginning February 1, 1987. Ferbar sought trustee reconsideration without receiving an expressly responsive answer, initiated arbitration July 8, 1987, and made no payments. Arbitration had not occurred at the reported stage. An April 14, 1987 letter warned that failure to cure within sixty days would constitute default.

The Fund sued February 9, 1993, demanding the entire $45,570.80 or alternatively $25,375 in then-due installments plus an injunction for future payments as due. The District Court applied the three-year discovery period and alternatively found the whole obligation untimely six years after the first missed installment. The Ninth Circuit, 73 F.3d 971 (1996), recognized the later-of formulation but held that the six years ran from complete withdrawal in March 1985. It followed Thibodo, reasoning that missed-payment accrual could make the discovery provision superfluous and give the creditor control of accrual; Judge Trott noted the conflict. Joyce, 871 F.2d 1119 (D.C. Cir. 1989), used a missed-payment trigger; Kahle, 43 F.3d 852 (3d Cir. 1994), separate installment periods; Navco, 3 F.3d 167 (7th Cir. 1993), one period from first missed payment. Those are competing lower authorities, not adopted simulated Supreme Court law.

The Fund's appellate and merits submissions invoked §1399(c)(2)'s sixty-day provision and the demand's receipt-based prepayment period to argue even the first payment timely. Its petition, however, represented that the Kahle/Navco conflict determined the case and Navco's rule barred the suit. Preserve those attributed representations when assessing question scope; do not silently turn them into either waiver or permission to ignore the petition. Ferbar argued that the complaint's full-debt demand amounted to acceleration. No earlier acceleration is established merely by delinquency, the April warning, or the complaint's alternative request.

The questions concern accrual under the enacted limitation and whether an untimely first installment bars subsequent installments within six years. The Court is not choosing a borrowed period, recalculating liability, deciding an uncompleted arbitration, or assuming every assessment defense resolved.

### Law entering the event

Under 29 U.S.C. §1381(a), covered withdrawal produces withdrawal liability. Section 1399(b)(1) requires notice, assessment, schedule, and demand as soon as practicable. Section 1399(c) separately governs actual payment commencement, installments, overdue interest, default, and optional acceleration. Section 1451(a)(1) permits suit by an adversely affected fiduciary; §1451(f) provides the longer of six years after the cause arose or three years after actual or constructive knowledge of the cause, with its fraud/concealment qualification if actually implicated. These distinct events cannot be collapsed merely because the underlying debt originated at withdrawal. Nor does the existence of a schedule itself establish timely notice, acceleration, or a particular maturity date for the entire debt.

**Milwaukee Brewery Workers' Pension Plan v. Jos. Schlitz Brewing Co. (February 21, 1995)** is directly material and omitted from the runtime selection. Breyer's unanimous opinion distinguishes an assumed computational starting date from actual demand, payment, delinquency, and acceleration. Actual payments begin no later than sixty days after demand, notwithstanding review or appeal; intervals may differ if the plan specifies them. Default based on nonpayment requires that failure remain uncured sixty days after written notice; an alternative plan-defined default must indicate substantial likelihood of inability to pay. Default and election to accelerate are separate from the first overdue payment. Its installment-calculation holding does not already decide §1451(f) accrual.

**Concrete Pipe (June 14, 1993)** separates sponsor assessment/enforcement from neutral adjudication and preserves ordinary statutory preponderance rebuttal. It supplies no conclusive finding that this assessment is correct. **North Star Steel (May 30, 1995)** governs borrowing for WARN, not an express federal limitation such as §1451(f); it decides no accrual question here. **Inter-Modal (May 12, 1997)** separates protected substantive plan rights from action predicates and available relief, but its §510 holding is not a withdrawal-liability accrual rule.

Relevant exact modules: **Neutral adjudication and withdrawal-liability rebuttal** (Concrete Pipe, June 14, 1993), **Borrowing limitations periods for WARN claims** (May 30, 1995), and **ERISA interference with prospective welfare-plan rights** (Inter-Modal, May 12, 1997). The latter two are limiting comparisons, not substitutes for §§1399 and 1451. Milwaukee's exact rule is in its canonical Holdings entry; no invented additional Standards module is supplied.

### Supported competing paths

The Fund can distinguish statutory liability at withdrawal from an enforceable failure to pay, argue that separate due installments produce separate claims, and resist acceleration by implication. The employer can invoke statutory repose and the concern that unilateral delay in assessment cannot postpone limitations indefinitely; it can also defend a single cause upon initial default, if the enacted payment structure permits that construction. The strongest answer to delayed-demand concerns is to examine the “as soon as practicable” duty and any properly preserved delay defense on their actual predicates, without rewriting accrual. The strongest answer to automatic separate accrual is the effect of a validly invoked acceleration on later maturity; no such invocation is presumed.

Lawful resolutions include a withdrawal-based rule if text and action availability support it; a first-default rule for the entire matured claim if legally justified; or separate accrual for independently due installments, with any actual acceleration analyzed separately. A remand can permit collection of timely claims while leaving earlier installments and remaining defenses for ordinary adjudication. The distinction between a payment becoming due, a statutory grace/cure mechanism, and acceleration must be resolved from verified facts before declaring even the first installment time-barred. No final amount, all-installments recovery, tolling finding, forfeiture, or payment order follows solely from rejecting the Ninth Circuit's rationale.
