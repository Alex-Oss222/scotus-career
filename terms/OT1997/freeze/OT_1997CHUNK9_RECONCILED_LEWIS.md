## Historical structure and changed-premise assessment

The historical judgment was unanimous, but only six Justices joined the Court opinion: Souter, Rehnquist, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens concurred in the judgment on qualified immunity alone. Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurred in the judgment on a separate constitutional ground, rejecting the conscience-shocking methodology. The separate full-join concurrences were Rehnquist alone, Kennedy with O'Connor, and Breyer alone. Thomas wrote no separate opinion of his own.

The frozen commitments preserve the central historical factual premises: an unintended collision during a rapid pursuit, dangerous speed and following distance, alleged policy breaches, and no supported purpose to injure unrelated to enforcement. The fuller plaintiff-favorable lower-court detail does not establish a purposeful collision, a terrorizing scheme, an adjudicated good motive, or a materially deliberative encounter. The actual simulated Collins, Farmer, Doe, Pinder, Uhlrig and Lanier rules retain important affirmative-injury and noncustodial possibilities but do not resolve this pursuit's culpability. Thus neither those cases nor the record differences justify changing the historical judgment positions.

**Glucksberg is materially different from its historical counterpart.** The actual entry, June 26, 1997, gives Kennedy's five-Justice opinion the narrow assistance-in-suicide holding and expressly states that no exclusive most-specific-history methodology governs all liberties. Scalia and Thomas joined that limited opinion. Accordingly, their historical assertion that the Lewis majority repudiates a universally binding methodology adopted the previous Term cannot be imported as a statement of current law. However, the removal of that premise does not resolve their independent objection to judges defining constitutional torts through an open-ended conscience test. Their earlier Collins joins existed in historical Lewis as well. Neither the actual Glucksberg qualification nor the actual Lanier prohibition on categorically excluding direct assaults requires adoption of the majority's emergency-culpability framework.

The provisional seven-Associate count for the purpose-to-harm rule therefore overstates the support established by the sources. Scalia and Thomas accept that this reckless pursuit and unintended collision supply no constitutional violation, but their no-established-right rationale is not a direct join in a conscience-based rule identifying purpose to harm as the necessary culpability. Agreement in the negative result cannot be added to the five direct framework joins. No Marks aggregation or fabricated circulation concession cures that difference.

## Proposition-level support and join conditions

All eight support reversal and termination of the reviewed federal individual claim. Five—O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—directly support the following contextual merits rule: in this rapidly evolving high-speed pursuit, requiring instant choices amid competing enforcement and safety obligations, reckless or deliberately indifferent risk-taking alone is insufficient; a purpose to cause harm unrelated to a legitimate enforcement objective is required to satisfy the conscience-shocking constitutional threshold. They support its application to the assumed record without a finding that the officer acted wisely, lawfully under departmental rules, or with an adjudicated benevolent motive.

Scalia and Thomas support no constitutional liability on the independently sufficient, carefully described no-right-supported ground. Stevens supports only qualified immunity. These three cannot be counted as joins in the contextual culpability rule or its conscience-based explanation. The five direct votes already suffice for that proposition among the nine-member Court if preserved in assembly; no aggregation with distinct rationales is needed. This is a proposition count, not a final judgment tally or assignment.

The framework must retain its emergency and actual-deliberation distinction. Deliberate indifference may suffice in recognized custodial medical or safety settings; the holding supplies neither a universal intent requirement nor an automatic deliberate-indifference rule whenever an official had some opportunity to desist. Purposeful collision, deliberate terrorizing conduct, a contrived encounter or materially different opportunities for considered action require their own actual facts and legal analysis. The decision cannot create a seconds-based rule or allow an official to manufacture immunity by merely naming an emergency.

The specific-provision explanation remains bounded: Brower requires intentionally applied stopping means; no seizure is established here. Graham and actual Lanier require using a specific provision when it covers the claim, not eliminating every noncustodial due-process claim. Albright's actual fractured judgment supplies no additional general displacement rationale. No broader merits resolution follows for a procedural due-process challenge to available state remedies, which is unpresented.

## Remedy and notice

Reverse the Ninth Circuit's reviewed ruling against Smith and return with instructions consistent with termination of this federal individual claim, leaving the district court's favorable individual judgment in effect. This is not a revival of the municipal claims, which were affirmed below, or of unappealed state claims. No damages, new evidentiary hearing, mandatory trial, municipal retrial or state-law judgment is directed.

For Stevens's sufficient ground and the independently acceptable immunity alternative, all relevant law at May 22, 1990 must be considered. Garner/Brower concern intentional force/stopping means; Checki concerns intentional misuse to terrorize; Wood II involves affirmative interference with protective circumstances; the other reported pursuit cases did not establish this constitutional boundary merely by recognizing a possible egregious case. Daniels expressly reserved intermediate culpability. Later authorities explain the method but cannot supply retrospective notice. Neither absence of an identical case, a numerical consensus, disagreement among judges, policy violation nor injury severity alone decides immunity.
