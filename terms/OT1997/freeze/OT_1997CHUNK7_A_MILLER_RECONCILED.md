## Statutory and record premises retained

The administrative denial expressly rests on §1409(a)(4). The agency accepted Charlie Miller's citizenship and sufficient physical presence to transmit citizenship and reported sufficient evidence of access at conception. Biological paternity is undisputed, although no formal clear-and-convincing finding is reported. The Texas decree establishes the reported biological and legal parent-child relationship, but July 1992 follows both the amended eighteenth-year deadline and Miller's June 1991 twenty-first birthday. No earlier qualifying recognition event is invented.

Under the amended §1409(a), the distinct conditions are clear-and-convincing blood relationship; paternal United States nationality at birth; a written agreement by the father, unless deceased, to support the person until eighteen; and, while the person is under eighteen, legitimation under residence or domicile law, the father's written sworn acknowledgment, or a competent-court paternity adjudication. These last alternatives are disjunctive, not a marriage requirement. Miller may elect the former subsection, which required legitimation before twenty-one and did not contain the later support condition. The absence of a separately recovered election form establishes no waiver or new formality. Do not combine the former deadline with the amended support/proof regime.

The separate §1401 transmission predicates remain applicable; the former ten-total-years/five-after-fourteen presence requirement is distinct from the amended five/two rule, and neither can be used to invent a deficiency contrary to the agency's acceptance. Section 1409(c) separately transmits the citizen mother's nationality at birth to a person born abroad and out of wedlock after December 23, 1952 if she previously had continuous presence for one year in the United States or an outlying possession. The remedy proposed by the three does not import this maternal period into the paternal route. The constitutionality of the different presence periods and every independent support application remain undecided here.

## Stevens's sufficient merits position

Stevens would affirm because Congress may condition this citizenship-at-birth route on affirmative paternal recognition or legal establishment of the relationship during minority. Even under Virginia's genuine-important-objective and substantial-relationship test, formal recognition supports reliable parentage identification and an opportunity for a parent-child relationship and connection with the United States during childhood. The birth event ordinarily establishes maternity and initial opportunity in a way conception alone does not establish paternity or knowledge of the child's existence. Lehr permits attention to a father's timely formal step rather than treating biology as the sole relevant fact. The amended alternatives include acknowledgment and adjudication; a voluntary marriage or assent by the father is not the sole route. The available former route's age-twenty-one limit likewise was not met.

Accepted biology and later genetic proof do not, on this account, supply the childhood legal recognition Congress required. Virginia, Kelley and Klinger prohibit assumed paternal irresponsibility as sufficient justification, but permit relevant differences and actual substantial fit; they impose no least-restrictive-means test. He does not infer that every mother develops durable American ties, uphold every father-only requirement, decide Fiallo's maximum deference, or deny all constitutional remedies. The deadline concerns when the qualifying act occurs, not a separate bar on later proving a timely act. No late act becomes timely merely because evidence of it is conclusive.

## O'Connor and Kennedy's distinct affirmance and separate-writing function

Both recognize the daughter's concrete denial but treat the heightened sex-equality claim as the father's. Powers, Singleton and Campbell require a sufficiently close relationship and genuine impediment in addition to injury. He brought his own claim and did not appeal dismissal; ordinary cost, inconvenience and transfer do not establish the necessary obstacle. They distinguish Craig and the excluded jurors' practical enforcement barriers rather than treating the relationship prong as sufficient by itself.

Assuming Miller may present her own constitutional objection, they regard the relevant distinction for that claim as parentage rather than her own sex and apply rational review. Recognition during minority reasonably connects this statutory route with the citizen parent. A legitimacy-versus-illegitimacy challenge is outside the granted comparison of children of citizen mothers and fathers. Their own-claim conclusion does not adopt Stevens's heightened-scrutiny analysis. On that higher standard they would not accept the stereotype-based justification; the standing decision prevents that assessment from becoming their operative father's-claim merits ruling. Their position creates no general nationality immunity from review and orders no new evidentiary proceedings.

## Scalia and Thomas's distinct affirmance and separate-writing function

These Justices accept third-party permission under the existing Craig line but would affirm because the requested declaratory citizenship cannot be provided by deleting an unmet congressional condition. Wong Kim Ark and Bellei distinguish Fourteenth Amendment birthright from citizenship of persons born abroad under Congress's enactments. Pangilinan prohibits courts from conferring citizenship contrary to statutory conditions. Sections 1401 and 1409 form an integrated grant; the latter is a precondition, not a later confiscation of citizenship unconditionally conferred by reading §1401 alone.

Their answer to severance is substantive: the naturalization power and §1421(d)'s instruction that citizenship be obtained only in the prescribed manner and conditions prevent a judicial enlarged grant. In their view that specific limitation defeats reliance on the INA's general severability clause, §406. Mathews recognizes that equality can be restored by withholding or extending a benefit; it does not make extension inevitable. Interlocking paternal and maternal conditions prevent assuming Congress would retain exactly the enlargement requested. Westcott's ordinary benefit-extension remedy does not answer this citizenship-specific objection. They need not adjudicate the sex classification, strip anyone else's citizenship or declare all constitutional challenges nonjusticiable. Their ground remains legal after accepting every corrected predicate and the former route's absence of a support promise; no missing eligibility fact supports it.

## Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's common reversal, directed remedy and writing functions

**Threshold and merits.** Miller herself is denied a statutory opportunity according to the sex of her citizen parent; using parental sex does not cease to be a sex classification because the child is female or male. Virginia, Kelley and Klinger require examination of that actual classification and substantial fit. The father's claim is alternatively available under their Craig/Powers practical-hindrance assessment. Calling her an alien cannot assume the answer to the disputed statutory citizenship claim. Fiallo's admission preferences for conceded aliens do not automatically settle citizenship transmitted at birth.

Reliable biological proof is an important objective and real differences between maternity and paternity can support appropriate evidence requirements. They do not justify the additional categorical age restriction here. Biological proof and formal acknowledgment during childhood do different work; later reliable proof can establish parentage, while a timely formal act does not itself ensure durable ties. The maternal route does not condition citizenship on continuing custody, support or transmission of American culture. A rule based on actual knowledge, care or connection would address those functions without using sex as an overbroad proxy. The argument identifies mismatch, not a universal least-restrictive-means requirement, denial of all early-connection conditions, or constitutional right to citizenship based on biology alone.

**Historical-stereotype function.** All three also preserve the substantive critique developed in the historical Ginsburg writing: citizenship legislation historically subordinated mothers' independent citizenship and later privileged maternity for children born out of wedlock. That changing treatment undermines the claim that the present distinction necessarily reflects timeless respect for the mother-child bond. A preference that appears favorable to women can reinforce assumptions of obligatory maternal care and absent paternal responsibility. Genuine custody, support and country-connection objectives should be examined functionally. This statutory-history account reinforces their actual-fit analysis; it does not replace scrutiny, create a conclusive historical-purpose presumption or invalidate an unpresented condition. The detailed threshold/fit/remedy function and this history/stereotype function must both survive in substantive separate-writing summaries; assembly decides whether their supported content calls for separate or combined writings and chooses authors independently.

**Directed remedy.** They would reverse and remand for declaratory recognition under the available former-law route after removing the unconstitutional age limitation for Miller's claim. Legitimation and independent §1401 requirements remain; the decree and accepted predicates make this an application of the remaining statutory citizenship-at-birth entitlement. It is not equitable naturalization, a new one-year presence route, or a hearing to revisit undisputed facts. Mathews permits consideration of both equality remedies, while Westcott and the benefit-conferring structure support removing the unequal barrier rather than withdrawing a separate maternal protection. The INA's general severability provision supports constitutional correction; §1421(d), in their view, does not bar recognizing citizenship the remaining statute itself confers. They reject Scalia and Thomas's characterization of every such correction as an unauthorized new grant.

Ordinary implementation of the available election may occur if needed, without inventing a waived option, additional formality, new support promise or proof deficiency. They direct appropriate declaratory relief rather than an unpresented passport-processing order. The historical dissent's broader proposal to excise amended (a)(3) and (a)(4) is not mechanically substituted for this independently selected former-route implementation. The full historical main opinion itself identifies uncertainty about the added support condition's applicability. These three's selected remedy provides the same requested citizenship recognition by removing the condition that actually bars this available route; it supplies no affirmative holding that the separate support distinction is valid. This is an expressly identified remedial-scope choice, not a changed factual premise or unexplained contrary judgment.
