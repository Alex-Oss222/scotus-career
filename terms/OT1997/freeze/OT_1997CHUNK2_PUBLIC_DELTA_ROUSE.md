## Event

Inmates of Suffolk County Jail v. Rouse, review of the consolidated First Circuit judgment in Nos. 97-1261, 97-1262, 97-1263 and 97-1334, 129 F.3d 649. Decided January 15, 1998, on statutory and constitutional objections to termination of continuing consent-decree obligations. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is stated.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated on submission and at decision. No Justice was absent.

## Public Action

The First Circuit's judgment is affirmed, including its direction to end the decree's future operation without retrospectively vacating its historical existence.

## Holdings

**1. The independent statutory termination route and amended preservation condition govern.** Section 3626(b)(2) permits termination of covered prospective prison-conditions relief approved without the substantive need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness findings. The State must establish that predicate. Existing actual findings can suffice without ritual statutory wording, and Rufo's separate equitable significant-change threshold is not an additional prerequisite. Part I is unanimous.

Under amended (b)(3), relief is preserved only on written, record-based findings that it remains necessary to correct a current **and** ongoing violation of a federal right, extends no further than necessary to correct that violation, is narrowly drawn and is the least intrusive necessary means. The November 26, 1997 amendment applies to this pending case. Lewis and Plyler addressed the earlier current **or** ongoing language; their holdings must not be read as though the conjunction had always been the same.

Plyler supplies the independent route and preservation inquiry. Consent decrees fall within prospective relief; pretrial detention falls within covered prison conditions. Negotiated protections do not automatically become independent federal rights, although a necessary corrective remedy may exceed the minimum substantive conduct rule. The statute does not authorize terminating a useful term solely because it exceeds that minimum.

**2. The supported findings assessment permits termination without a speculative new inquiry.** The reported judicial evaluation establishes no qualifying findings or supported federal violation requiring preservation of this relief. No violation meeting the broader original condition but excluded by the amended conjunction is identified. The narrower text therefore creates no outcome-determinative difference on this record. Part I is unanimous.

The District Court evaluated periodic reports received since June 14, 1994 concerning assaults, rape, disease and related conditions, and assessed its existing findings; the circuit reviewed that work. This is a substantive evaluation, not rejection for missing statutory words. Decree compliance and absence of a federal violation remain conceptually different, and this Court does not independently find all jail conditions lawful or permanently safe. The asserted future deterioration was speculative, not a supported current violation or preserved refusal to consider qualifying evidence. Independent judicial review and a proper opportunity to present relevant evidence remain necessary; no new hearing follows merely because additional information could be useful or originals are not reproduced here.

**3. This prospective law change preserves Article III adjudication.** Congress may change the conditions for continuing prospective federal relief while leaving courts to determine actual violations, necessary findings and remedial scope. Applied here, the statute does not dictate a result under unchanged law, command false facts or annul accrued damages and completed historical effects. Part II is unanimous.

Plyler directly addresses conditional prospective termination. Robertson and Wheeling Bridge distinguish changed governing law from legislative adjudication; Klein does not prohibit a genuine legal change preserving the judicial task. Morgan Stanley and Plaut permit their defined reinstatement of securities limitations dismissals, but supply neither an absolute finality bar nor an unrestricted reopening power here. Rufo retains its different equitable role. The Court does not decide a different application in which the amended conjunction excludes an actually supported violation that the original safeguard would have preserved.

**4. Vested-right and procedural due process do not preserve unchanged future enforcement.** Beneficiaries possess no perpetual entitlement to federal enforcement of the mutable decree's unchanged prospective terms against valid later remedial law. The reported motion, opposition and review support rejection of the presented procedural objection. Part III is unanimous.

Plyler preserves the distinction between future enforcement and accrued rights. No unreported hearing is presumed, no denial of a demonstrated necessary opportunity is approved, and no independent accrued claim is adjudicated. The statute ends continuing judicial obligations, not the decree's historical legal existence. Relabeling those same judicial obligations as a contract cannot preserve federal coercive supervision contrary to the governing conditions.

**5. The assumed independent contract-impairment objection fails at its presented scope.** Assuming a contract and substantial impairment solely to decide the objection, the federal-nonparty rationality framework permits this restriction on continuing federal supervision. The United States does not become a contracting party merely because a federal court approved the decree or Congress enacted legislation benefiting States. Part IV has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejecting the objection.

National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Atchison supplies the relevant distinction between federal nonparty regulation and governmental impairment of its own bargain. Allocating institutional responsibility and resources supplies a rational ground for the prospective supervision rule while protection of federal rights remains available. The Court does not determine that a separate contract exists, survives, has been breached or is enforceable in any particular forum, and does not cancel accrued contractual liability.

**6. The presented equality challenge fails without diminishing pretrial rights or meaningful access.** Ordinary rational review permits the statute's restriction on continuing prison-decree supervision because it reasonably relates the actual supervisory disadvantage to legitimate institutional responsibility and resource concerns while retaining enforceable federal-right protection. Part III is unanimous.

Beach permits legitimate conceivable grounds; Heller and Romer require a relationship to the actual disadvantage and preserve the limits on irrational classifications. Absence of suspect status alone does not establish validity, and no mandatory subjective-motive finding or universal economic-classification shortcut is adopted. Bell v. Wolfish continues to protect pretrial detainees against punishment before conviction, and Lewis preserves meaningful access rather than merely nominal courthouse availability. The Court creates no blanket prohibition on appropriate protection against a sufficiently supported threatened injury. An actual later violation remains actionable under the governing requirements.

**7. Termination ends prospective judicial operation, not past adjudication.** The statutory direction to terminate, together with the inclusion of consent decrees in prospective relief, requires ending the decree's continuing judicial obligations themselves. It does not declare the original judgment erroneous, erase completed determinations or payments, or retrospectively vacate lawful past effects. The eight-Justice Part V opinion affirms the circuit's existing direction; all nine agree on the prospective/historical distinction.

The Commissioner's separate Rule 60(b) position was conditionally set aside upon obtaining termination. The condition is met, so the Court neither rejects that theory on its merits nor treats it as unconditionally abandoned. No new prisoner-release order, automatic-stay ruling or statutory clock follows from this disposition.

## Precedent Treatment

Plyler supplies the independent statutory route, judicial preservation inquiry and bounded constitutional operation under its original text; the present amendment is applied without rewriting that holding. Lewis retains the statute's scope, transition, findings and meaningful-access limits. Rufo retains its independent significant-change and tailored-modification rule, not an extra statutory prerequisite. Morgan Stanley and Plaut retain their limited completed-dismissal reinstatement holdings; Robertson, Klein and Wheeling Bridge preserve the changed-law/adjudication distinction. Bell preserves pretrial protection. Beach, Heller and Romer supply ordinary review within their actual limits, and Atchison supplies the assumed federal-nonparty contract analysis. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

The amended current-and-ongoing condition governs this pending termination matter. On this record, it supports ending unsupported continuing judicial obligations while preserving independent adjudication, past lawful effects and unadjudicated independent claims. The decision resolves the presented contract-impairment and equality objections at the stated scope, not every PLRA application or every possible effect of the amended conjunction. It finds no permanent constitutional compliance and preserves lawful remedies for an actual later violation.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with the statutory and constitutional grounds in Parts I–III and with ending unsupported prospective obligations while refusing retrospective vacatur. He would express the mandate as affirmance as modified and remand to implement the end of the decree's future operation. That return would not reopen every condition, require a new hearing or invite invention of a current violation. The reported monitoring record does not support continuation, and fear of later double bunking or abuse does not itself establish a present violation. Completed determinations, payments and other lawful historical effects remain protected; an actual later violation remains actionable. His different mandate does not restore federal supervision by calling the same obligations contractual.

## Procedure After Action

The First Circuit's judgment stands, including its direction that the District Court implement termination of the decree's future judicial operation. The decree's historical existence is not annulled. Independent accrued or contractual claims retain whatever law otherwise supplies, without a present recognition or enforcement ruling. No new merits remand, compulsory hearing, release order, stay, deadline or Supreme Court return is ordered.
