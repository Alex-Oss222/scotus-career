## Exact proposal tendered

The following is the complete test from the unchanged approved Section II, reproduced without substantive or verbal revision:

### Continuing Pertinence Test

Proposed by Justice Stone for OT1997

1. **Does the challenged record fall within the statutory protection?**

   The plaintiff identifies records describing their First Amendment activities and establishes that the government continues to maintain them. The court first checks whether retention is independently authorized by statute or by the individual.

2. **Can the government identify an authorized law-enforcement activity?**

   The agency must establish the legal authority for that activity. It need not prove the plaintiff committed a crime. Intelligence investigations, criminal investigations, and authorized civil enforcement may qualify.

3. **Does the information remain pertinent to that activity?**

   The court examines the actual relationship, not a hypothetical future possibility. Relevant considerations include:

   - Why the information was collected.
   - What investigative purpose it still serves.
   - Whether it preserves necessary context or evidence.
   - Whether subsequent events have changed its relevance.
   - Whether continued use or indexing exceeds the justified investigative scope.

   No single consideration automatically decides the case.

4. **What disposition and remedy does the law authorize?**

   If a qualifying connection remains, the agency may retain the information under the statutory exception. If no qualifying connection remains, the court determines the violation and available relief under the Privacy Act.

   The court separately accounts for lawful evidence-preservation and federal records-management duties.

## Whole-test compatibility and strongest alternatives

The proposal was tested in its entirety rather than reduced to the proposition that lawful initial collection is insufficient. Its strongest merits case is the defined word “maintain,” which includes keeping as well as collection, together with the independent protection against unnecessary inventories of protected activity. Step 3 accommodates genuine continuing context and evidence; it does not demand destruction simply because an investigation closes, and none of its five considerations automatically controls. Step 1 preserves the express statutory and individual-authorization alternatives. Step 2 does not equate incidental innocent contact with criminality. Step 4 preserves retention where a qualifying connection exists and distinguishes violation, available relief and actual preservation duties.

The strongest competing merits construction treats pertinence as a quality of a historically relevant record within a law-enforcement activity broader than one currently open investigation. Its difficulty is giving independent effect to continued maintenance. The strongest objection to the proposal is that its actual-relationship language and five considerations could be applied as a stricter present-necessity rule than the statute's broader “pertinent to and within the scope” standard. The proposal's context/evidence consideration and no-single-consideration qualification answer a categorical investigation-closure objection; they do not by themselves settle the full statutory construction or establish every Associate's assent to its burden, form and operative reach. No new periodic audit, public disclosure of classified reasons or automatic purge requirement is read into the test.

The strongest route to immediate adjudication would identify the admitted personal associational records as a concrete injury and use Nagel or Reich to establish lawful redress. Each Associate has already examined the narrower injury-only alternative and the stronger immediate-merits route. The exact test does not resolve the reason for withholding either: the actual surviving civil action, adverse-effect issue, remedy and exemption application remain undetermined. A physically useful remedy is not necessarily authorized relief. Step 4 acknowledges that distinction; it does not prove a legally available route. Because Section II itself conditions merits reach on jurisdiction, the test is not interpreted as permission to postpone Article III redressability until after a disputed merits holding.

The opposite whole-action dismissal is equally unsupported. The original suit expressly requested monetary damages, although the published report does not establish a surviving claim-specific Privacy Act damages demand or its abandonment. A failure of one expungement route therefore cannot be converted into absence of all statutory redress. Neither an amendment request nor its omission, a particular exemption, an independent equitable cause, a new officer defendant or preserved monetary relief may be invented. The bounded threshold remand leaves those questions to ordinary determination when genuinely necessary; it neither promises relief nor orders discovery to repair the research archive.
