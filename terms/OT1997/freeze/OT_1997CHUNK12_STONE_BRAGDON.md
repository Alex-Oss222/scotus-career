## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: HIV disability and direct threat

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches Abbott’s record-specific disability and the governing direct-threat method. He does not decide whether HIV is invariably disabling or whether Bragdon ultimately proves a direct threat.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Asymptomatic HIV is a physical impairment that substantially limits Abbott’s major life activity of reproduction on this record. A provider may refuse treatment only on an individualized assessment of a significant risk grounded in objective medical evidence reasonably available at the time, considering reasonable precautions.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** On this record, asymptomatic HIV is a physical impairment that substantially limits Abbott’s major life activity of reproduction. A direct-threat defense requires an individualized judgment grounded in objective medical evidence reasonably available when treatment was refused, not special deference to sincere professional belief.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The medical consequences of HIV materially constrained Abbott’s reproductive choices before visible symptoms. At the same time, the ADA does not compel treatment despite a proved significant risk. The lower court must evaluate the contemporaneous evidence and precautions without substituting professional sincerity for objective support.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This does not declare every person with HIV disabled, decide the ultimate risk, require services outside professional competence, or foreclose refusal where objective evidence establishes a significant risk that reasonable precautions cannot reduce.
