## Zeran v. America Online, Inc. — June 15, 1998

### Reviewed action and controlling premise

Review is of the Fourth Circuit's affirmance of judgment on the pleadings for AOL. The alleged publication-related omissions arose in 1995; the action against AOL was filed April 23, 1996, after enactment on February 8, 1996. The January 4 action against KRXO is not this suit. Removal timing is disputed. Allegations are accepted under Rule 12(c), not found true after trial. No damages, common-law liability, substantive state-law choice, or independent promise is established.

Landgraf supplies the initial temporal-direction inquiry and, absent sufficient direction, the examination of new consequences for completed events. Rivers rejects purpose or correction alone as temporal direction. Hughes Aircraft applies that inquiry to an amendment affecting a defense and a later-filed action: jurisdictional form and filing date cannot substitute for the consequences analysis. That unanimous substantive holding is especially material to Kennedy, its author, and to every other Associate who joined it. The direction of the change differs here—extinguishing an asserted accrued claim rather than withdrawing a defense—but Landgraf protects existing rights as well as against increased liability.

The original section 230(d)(3) preserves consistent state law and forbids bringing causes or imposing liability under inconsistent state or local law. It does not expressly identify preenactment conduct, accrued claims, pending litigation, or a filing-date transition. Section 230(c)(1) addresses publisher or speaker treatment; section 230(c)(2)'s good-faith restriction protection is a separate provision. Then-subsections (d)(1), (d)(2), and (d)(4) retain their federal-criminal, intellectual-property, and specified communications-privacy limits; definitions were then in subsection (e). No modern amendment or renumbering is used.

### Material alternatives and disposition

**Path A — later filing permits application.** The strongest argument for AOL is that the prohibition on a cause being brought speaks directly to commencement, that this action began after enactment, and that immediate comprehensive protection implements the express federal preemption policy. This is more than reliance on the word immunity. If accepted, the Court would have to decide whether notice-based distribution is publisher treatment. The lower opinion's strongest scope argument is that continuing publication, withdrawal, screening, and retraction are publication functions despite the notice element. Zeran's strongest response is that Congress selected the legally used term publisher while the common law distinguished a distributor's knowing participation, and that the separate good-faith provision must retain independent work. These competing scope arguments need not be resolved if the statute does not govern this conduct.

**Path B — accrued substantive liability remains governed by prior law.** The stronger temporal reading is that subsection (d)(3) expresses the substantive supremacy of the new rule, not a transition applicable to earlier events. Virtually any new statutory bar could otherwise be made retrospective merely by describing the future act of filing or adjudication. Extinguishing an otherwise legally sufficient claim for completed 1995 conduct changes the parties' legal relation. Congress may prescribe that effect, but the ordinary preemption formulation does not sufficiently prescribe it. Unlike a new exhaustion step or a restriction on subsidized filing, the asserted application removes the claim itself. No actual-reliance showing, final judgment, or constitutional vesting finding is needed to invoke the statutory presumption.

The contrary lower rationale that an unfiled tort claim cannot count because it is not vested confuses legislative power with the default rule of construction. Its reliance discussion cannot survive Rivers' distinction between retrospective consequences and proof of personal reliance. Hughes forecloses treating new access-to-court wording as conclusive when substantive rights or defenses change. The policy favoring effective online communication explains the prospective rule but does not settle its reach into completed conduct.

**Frozen result:** All eight Associates would reverse the Fourth Circuit and remand for further proceedings on claims based on pre-February 8, 1996 conduct under the otherwise applicable law. No liability is ordered. The disputed removal date is not resolved by this Court, and the remand does not grant an exemption for independently alleged and established post-enactment conduct. Neither a new post-enactment claim nor a continuing omission after enactment is invented from that dispute. The prospective meaning of section 230(c)(1), including distributor notice liability, is not reached because it is unnecessary to overturn the judgment on the presented preenactment ground. An unaddressed issue is not a holding for either party.

## Caron v. United States, No. 97-6270 — June 22, 1998

### Reviewed issue and law

The question is whether the residual Massachusetts firearm restriction activates section 921(a)(20)'s unless clause, leaving the Massachusetts convictions available for federal firearm consequences. Massachusetts restored ordinary civil rights by operation of law; that restoration is agreed apart from this federal question. Caron could possess rifles and shotguns under the identification-permit regime, and the remaining restriction concerned handgun possession outside the home or business. It was not a complete handgun ban. The reviewed judgment counts the Massachusetts convictions; the initial sentence used one California and three Massachusetts convictions. The original qualification of those convictions as otherwise eligible is not independently contested here.

The statute identifies the conviction as the unit excluded or retained. Section 922(g)(1) then prohibits possession, shipment, transport, or receipt of any firearm or ammunition under its distinct commerce predicates; section 924(e)(1)'s qualifying convictions and fifteen-year minimum are a separate enhancement provision. Beecham selects the convicting jurisdiction and preserves the express-firearm-restriction qualification without deciding this partial-restriction issue. Automatic restoration is accepted in this posture; the Court need not create a general restoration mechanism.

Love's personal and incorporated arms holdings are binding but expressly preserve the distinction between coverage and the validity of a particular restriction. Love leaves Lewis's extant-felony disability unchanged. Neither Love nor Staples determines section 921(a)(20)'s partial-restoration text. Bryan, now earlier effective law, construes willfulness for unlawful dealing under different provisions and supplies no new restoration or possession exception. No preserved constitutional challenge or distinct mental-state issue is created.

### Material alternatives

**Path A — conviction-wide federal consequence.** The unless clause qualifies exclusion of a conviction, not exclusion of particular weapon transactions. A state restoration expressly leaving a substantial possession restriction on a firearm category is not an unrestricted restoration for this purpose. Congress uses that retained state restriction as the trigger for its own broader federal disability. On that reading, Massachusetts' permission for long guns does not make the same conviction disappear for those guns while remaining a federal predicate for others. The strongest objection is that federal law consults state law and yet criminalizes possession the same State permits. The answer is that Congress borrowed the state restoration status as a classification while prescribing its own federal consequences; state permission is not itself a federal exemption.

**Path B — restriction-sensitive reading and residual lenity.** The opposing reading gives operative force to the restoration's actual permission: the unless clause preserves federal disability only as to the firearms the State expressly withholds. The statute does not say that any limitation on any firearm withdraws every consequence of restored rights. A location-specific handgun restriction sits uneasily with a declaration that the person may not possess firearms when he is expressly allowed to possess these long guns. The strongest objection is the conviction-centered grammar and the resulting weapon-dependent federal status. Scalia and Thomas regard the express state-law exception as sufficient reason to accept that limited status; at minimum, after considering the textual context, the unresolved penal ambiguity favors Caron. They do not treat competing readings alone as enough to invoke lenity.

## Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, No. 96-1866 — June 22, 1998

### Current rule, supported record, and paths

Kinman has already resolved the recipient-attribution question that Franklin left open. Its controlling rule requires actual notice to an official empowered to correct harassment or its substantial risk, then deliberate and unreasonable disregard of the known danger causing injury or continuation. Ordinary constructive notice, teacher status or authority-assisted access, a policy breach, and the harasser's knowledge alone do not suffice. The rule concerns recipient compensation, not a personal qualified-immunity defense. A reasonable response is not made unlawful solely because harm continues. Kinman's additional circumstantial-awareness formulations were not joined by a majority and cannot be inserted as controlling presumptions.

The principal received an October 1992 complaint about classroom comments, met with Waldrop, received his apology and assurance against repetition, cautioned him, and informed the guidance counselor. He did not report it to the superintendent/Title IX coordinator. The supplied evidence does not identify a report to the principal of the sexual relationship. Gebser made no such report to officials before police discovery; termination followed that discovery. The content and recipient of actual notice, and the response to it, remain separate questions. No rule demands the victim's own report, a written complaint, a confession, or official knowledge of completed intercourse before a known substantial risk can matter.

The evidence about policies is conflicting, and handbook distribution uncertain. Gebser's statement that she probably would have reported if she had known what to do is testimony, not a causation finding. Sections 106.8(b) and 106.9(a) impose distinct regulatory obligations; their possible breach is not itself a finding of intentional recipient discrimination or statutory compensatory liability. Administrative enforcement and injunctions retain their own sources. Only the district's Title IX judgment was appealed.

**Path A — apply Kinman and affirm.** The actual-notice standard cannot be replaced by the teacher's own knowledge or imputed knowledge from ordinary delegated authority. Assuming the principal had adequate corrective authority, the reported classroom complaint does not establish actual knowledge of the sexual relationship or its substantial risk; moreover, the supported meeting, caution, counseling notification, and later termination do not establish deliberate and unreasonable refusal to act. This is a record-specific sufficiency conclusion, not a rule that verbal caution always suffices. A possible failure to publish grievance procedures, even considered with the testimony about reporting, does not by itself prove the actual culpable nonresponse Kinman requires.

**Strongest objection to Path A:** A school should not benefit from ineffective reporting channels it was obliged to provide, and its teacher's educational power may have enabled the injury. The answer under existing law is that these facts may be evidence but cannot substitute for Kinman's institutional notice and culpability elements. There is no supported finding of deliberate suppression or diversion of complaints here. The notice requirement cannot be satisfied merely by combining the teacher's knowledge, an official's knowledge of classroom comments, and an uncertain policy record.

**Path B — renew the published objection to Kinman's exclusive recipient threshold and vacate.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer retain their expressly published Kinman agency and negligent-correction position. They would reconsider that rule insofar as it excludes responsibility when actual delegated educational power materially aided intentional discrimination, or when responsible officials knew or reasonably should have known and negligently failed to correct it with causal injury. Franklin permits compensation for intentional discrimination; their view locates the teacher's intentional wrong within a properly proved agency relationship rather than demanding a second intentional wrong by higher management. Their separate approach does not automatically transfer every Title VII rule, make every teacher act attributable, or treat every regulatory omission as damages liability.

Under that alternative, a grant of summary judgment because no supervisor knew of the relationship is too narrow. Further proceedings should examine actual aid from delegated authority, what responsible officials knew or should have known, the adequacy of corrective measures, the disputed complaint mechanism, and causal injury. The four do not find those elements proved merely from the existence of a teacher-student relationship. Gebser's reporting testimony and the policy dispute are material evidence under this alternative, not conclusive proof. They support vacatur and adjudication under their proposed broader grounds, not immediate damages or a compulsory trial.

**Strongest objection to Path B:** Kinman's recent, express five-Justice holding rejected those very substitutes, and Titles VII and IX have different text and enforcement structures. The four must therefore acknowledge their proposal is a renewed disagreement with controlling law, not an application of an unresolved attribution question. They would reconsider the exclusive threshold for the reasons in their prior published position: the intentional discrimination to which Title IX speaks may occur through delegated educational authority, and section 1682's administrative process does not supply a private-suit higher-management prerequisite. The regulatory dispute alone supplies no reason to assume a vote change by any Kinman threshold joiner.
