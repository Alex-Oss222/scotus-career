## Reachability, supported record, and component boundaries

The authorized event is merits review of the federal court of appeals judgment, within the ordinary §1254(1) review channel. The assigned merits posture is not represented as a recovered historical grant or assigned Supreme Court docket. No case-specific nonparticipation is established. Each of the eight Associates participates. The preserved constitutional challenges survive the conditional pleas; no separate preservation, finality, or jurisdictional obstacle is identified in the validated packet.

Counts I and III concern the possession of two completed fully automatic weapons: a Chinese Type 54 7.62-millimeter submachine gun sold on April 4, 1992, and a U.S. Military M-3 .45-caliber submachine gun sold the next day, each at the Pennsylvania gun show to the same purchaser. These are identifiable whole weapons, not receiver or conversion-part counts. Counts II and IV, the separate NFA transfer counts, were dismissed below and are outside the proposed merits remedy. No newly established §922(o)(2) exception applies. Neither the model designation nor an old manufacture date proves lawful pre-May 19, 1986 possession.

The two questions are distinct. Congress's authority to include possession in the direct transfer/private-stock scheme is settled by Hale Part I. The constitutional permissibility of applying its possession prohibition to these weapons remains an independent arms question. The lower court's reliance on the absent militia relationship is incompatible with Love and Hale. A valid alternative ground could nevertheless sustain the judgment; identifying that legal error alone does not make remand mandatory in every subsequent case.

## Propositions submitted for possible later joins

These labels identify exact proposed grounds, not presently adopted Court holdings.

**C — Commerce application.** Reject the Commerce Clause challenge under Hale's direct transfer/private-stock ground. These completed sales reinforce the direct commercial setting but do not become a new offense element. No transaction-specific interstate crossing, universally mandatory legislative findings, or generalized crime-cost rationale is necessary. No Associate proposes reconsidering Hale.

**A — Personal invocation and separate application.** Rybar can invoke the personal arms guarantee without militia enrollment or service. Military usefulness neither excludes the weapons nor establishes immunity. Commercial possession does not categorically erase a personal constitutional objection; necessary acquisition is protected only when connected to protected keeping. The actual weapons, conduct, and prohibition must be assessed separately from commerce power and from lawful registration requirements.

**R — Corrected application and bounded remand.** Vacate the appellate affirmance of the two §922(o) possession convictions and return the preserved Second Amendment application because the material affiliation premise is invalid and the lower court supplied no adequate independent weapon-and-restriction application. The completed-weapon and transaction facts are settled inputs for that inquiry, not an instruction to relitigate them. Further consideration may occur on the existing record or through ordinary proceedings genuinely needed. The remand orders no automatic evidentiary hearing, new trial, acquittal, release, return of weapons, or resentencing; any consequential relief follows from lawful disposition of the affected counts. It does not reopen the dismissed transfer counts.

**F — Independent merits affirmance, considered but not joined on this handoff.** A possible affirmance would identify and defend a constitutional rule permitting control of new private-stock additions of these completed automatic weapons because of sustained-fire characteristics and the relationship of the restriction to their risks. It would have to explain why licensing/registration and the retained lawful older stock do not defeat that justification. Neither the word “machinegun,” the sales, military use, public danger alone, nor Congress's commerce authority supplies that complete constitutional analysis. The proposition is a lawful path to examine, not an entering-law holding or a fact already adjudicated.

**I — Substantive as-applied relief, considered but not joined on this handoff.** A possible merits invalidation would require a supported conclusion that these actual weapons and conduct receive protection against this prohibition, with constitutional treatment of its exceptions and regulatory purpose. Miller's military relationship, Love's acquisition protection, and the greater severity of prohibition compared with recordkeeping give this argument real force. They do not alone establish its necessary application or require acquittal. This handoff does not declare the two weapons constitutionally unprotected or protected against this restriction.

## Individual commitments and strongest alternative paths

### John Paul Stevens

**Commitment:** Join C, A, and R. Reject the commerce objection; vacate the two-count appellate affirmance for the preserved arms inquiry. His Hale joins are direct authority for each proposed proposition, including the distinction between the registration duty and a possession ban. His Love objection preserved the old rule against state application; it supplies no ground for denying this federal claim or restoring an affiliation requirement.

**Best alternative and its limit:** Stevens's own Staples dissent treats knowing possession of a dangerous weapon as sufficient fault for a regulatory registration offense under Balint and Freed. Together with the clearly automatic weapons and Congress's bounded stock restriction, that is his strongest source-based route toward F. The objection is that Staples construed culpability and Hale subsequently reserved the prohibition's independent constitutional application while Stevens joined. Dangerous-device treatment under a tax-registration statute cannot be converted into constitutional exclusion from all possession protection. Rybar's cleaner identification removes Hale's mixed-object difficulty, but does not answer that remaining distinction. He therefore joins a remand based on the invalid legal premise, not a demand for missing empirical studies.

**Join barriers and permissible accommodation:** He could consider an independently supported regulation analysis of these completed weapons; he does not precommit to invalidation or deny legislative room to address sustained-fire risks. He withholds F as presently stated, a blanket military-weapons exclusion, and I based solely on military suitability. His R join permits decision on the existing record and no compulsory evidentiary program.

### Sandra Day O'Connor

**Commitment:** Join C, A, and R. Her own Love and Hale opinions separate holder, coverage, restriction, and remedy. The direct market statute survives the power objection, but the affiliation premise cannot support these convictions. Clear identification of two completed automatic weapons satisfies an identification concern; it does not itself establish the permissible constitutional restriction.

**Best alternative and its limit:** F gains force because Rybar presents two sales of complete weapons, without Hale's unresolved count-to-object mapping. Her Ginsburg-concurrence join in Staples likewise directs attention to the precise characteristic placing an object in the statutory class. The strongest objection to R is consequently that the Court could complete the legal application now. O'Connor nevertheless rejects treating factual classification as the constitutional answer: her Hale Part III-B expressly refused to derive the complete rule merely from group-harm capacity and the statutory exceptions, and Part IV sustained registration on a different ground. The present facts do not supply an additional constitutional rule. R is a supported exercise of appellate judgment, not a universal remand duty.

**Join barriers and permissible accommodation:** She can join an opinion that recognizes the government's sustained-fire and transition arguments as potentially sufficient on proper application. She will not join an automatic full-auto exclusion, a general scrutiny tier asserted as existing law, a categorical right to every purchase, or a remand reopening established weapon facts. I requires more than the recognition of a personal guarantee.

### Antonin Scalia

**Commitment:** Join C, A, and R. His Hale Part I join forecloses relief merely because Congress omitted a separate interstate element. His joins in Love III–IV and Hale III preserve the textual personal holder and actual weapon inquiry while rejecting military-utility immunity. His Staples majority join supports careful attention to the defined full-weapon characteristic without turning a statutory definition into the constitutional scope rule.

**Best alternative and its limit:** His strongest alternative favoring Rybar is I: an outright prohibition burdens keeping more directly than registration, the actual arms' military utility fits Miller's historical inquiry, and Love protects necessary acquisition. The adverse point is equally concrete: his own Hale join denied that military utility establishes an exemption, and Love expressly reserved which actual weapons and restrictions the guarantee protects. Neither an asserted military role nor the commercial acquisition relationship disposes of those limits. The opposing F path would need a historically and legally supported coverage or regulation rule; statutory placement and dangerousness cannot supply it automatically. He chooses R without importing a future weapons rule.

**Join barriers and permissible accommodation:** No formal-enrollment prerequisite, no categorical military-use exclusion, and no judicial addition of an interstate offense element. He can join a bounded corrected application that leaves legitimate regulation open, but not a free-standing public-safety balancing formula with no identified legal source. He joins no present ultimate-invalidity holding and no order reopening the plea's settled statutory facts.

### Anthony M. Kennedy

**Commitment:** Join C, A, and R. His controlling Lopez opinion's limited commerce boundary coexists with his direct Hale market join. His Love III–IV and Hale III joins preserve the independently enforceable personal guarantee and reserve actual application. He rejects treating success on enumerated power as sufficient authorization to burden the right.

**Best alternative and its limit:** F offers a bounded distinction from ordinary arms: these are completed automatic weapons transferred in actual commerce, and the statute regulates expansion of an existing private stock while leaving specified channels. His Staples majority join acknowledges the practical distinction between ordinary semiautomatic rifles and the characteristics making a weapon a machinegun. But Staples's constitutional reservation and his later Hale III-B join preclude carrying that statutory distinction over without legal explanation. The commercial conduct makes the power analysis easier; it does not eliminate the rights question. I likewise cannot rest on military utility or purchase alone. He chooses the correction of the specific lower legal error with application preserved.

**Join barriers and permissible accommodation:** C must retain the direct enacted-market connection rather than authorize every marketable possession. A must remain independent. R must preserve both genuine regulation arguments and the possibility of substantive relief, without predicting either. He does not require a hearing or a general theory of all weapons before the two-count claim can be decided.

### David H. Souter

**Commitment:** Join C, A, and R. His joins in both Love's affirmative personal/acquisition holdings and its reserved arms application, followed by Hale III-A–B, directly support correction and return of this preserved claim. Unlike the three Love state-nonapplication dissenters, he joined Love's controlling arms rationale and remedy. His Staples majority join supports an actual characteristic inquiry while preserving the difference between proof of statutory facts and a separate constitutional conclusion.

**Best alternative and its limit:** F's best point is that the precise guns and sales are known and the justifications for limiting additions to a dangerous stock are intelligible without a comparative casualty study. That defeats a claim that an appellate court lacks power to decide the merits now or that empirical files are always prerequisites. It does not supply the missing independent constitutional application below. His Hale join expressly treated harm capacity and exceptions as arguments rather than a complete answer. R therefore corrects a demonstrated legal error; it is not a sanction for an unrecovered appendix. I's military-relationship argument receives consideration without being equated to an absolute exemption.

**Join barriers and permissible accommodation:** He can join a decision allowing the existing record to support a lawful result under Love and Miller. He refuses a burden invented from unavailable research, an assumption that the government conceded insufficient justification, or a remand that prejudges the prohibition unconstitutional. A reasoned later affirmance is not foreclosed by this commitment.

### Clarence Thomas

**Commitment:** Join C, A, and R. His Hale market join controls commerce despite any more general concern about the reach of federal power. His Love III–IV and Hale III joins supply a personal-right foundation independent of organized service. His own Staples opinion requires attention to the actual statutory characteristics; it explicitly did not decide a constitutional right to possess them.

**Best alternative and its limit:** I has the strongest personal-right argument for him: Miller's historical militia relationship makes military usefulness relevant to coverage, and Love refuses to exclude acquisition necessary to protected keeping. A ban is more intrusive than the registration duty he sustained in Hale. But those propositions do not establish that this precise restriction is forbidden; his Hale III-A join expressly rejected automatic military-utility immunity, and his III-B join preserved coverage and regulation as separate work. Conversely, F cannot treat the ordinary-rifle/machinegun distinction in his statutory Staples analysis as a constitutional exclusion already adopted. With neither direct application established by those authorities, he joins the legally grounded R disposition.

**Join barriers and permissible accommodation:** No collective-only right, no exclusion just because an arm is militarily useful, no rule that dangerousness alone ends the analysis, and no general deference standard imported from commerce review. He permits lawful registration and actual regulation analysis. R does not require fresh proof of the admitted full-auto characteristics or adopt a new criminal element, and does not resolve future receiver/parts prosecutions.

### Ruth Bader Ginsburg

**Commitment:** Join C, A, and R. Her Hale joins provide her own direct federal-claim authority. Her Love separate join concerned state nonapplication and does not negate that later federal personal-holder and reserved-application position. Her own Staples concurrence draws the statutory distinction precisely, while declining a broader rule about public-welfare crimes or resolving statutory validity.

**Best alternative and its limit:** F draws support from the absence here of a knowledge or weapon-identity uncertainty and from the relationship between completed automatic fire, supply limits, and the exceptions. Her narrower Staples method permits decisions tailored to the precise regulated feature; it does not itself supply constitutional approval. Her Hale III-B join then confirms that identification plus asserted danger is not the entire constitutional inquiry. The lower militia premise remains a material error, and no sufficient independent substitute has been established. I is also too quick if based only on military character or on the difference between prohibition and registration. She joins R at that bounded level.

**Join barriers and permissible accommodation:** No renewed incorporation question is necessary. She can join a disposition using the settled two-weapon facts and preserving a decision on the existing record. She does not join a special evidence-production command, a universal protected-weapons catalogue, a declaration that registration is always an adequate substitute, or an order disturbing the dismissed transfer counts.

### Stephen G. Breyer

**Commitment:** Join C, A, and R. His own Hale joins—not a generalized account of his judicial outlook—supply the decisive commerce, federal personal-holder, regulation, and reserved-application authority. His Love state-nonapplication objection does not govern federal Rybar. He is not attributed a Staples position from before he joined the Court.

**Best alternative and its limit:** F has substantial practical force for his application: limiting new automatic weapons directly addresses expansion of sustained-fire capacity, whereas licensing and recordkeeping regulate accountability without preventing additions; preserving already lawful stock can be a transition rather than a negation of the policy. That is an analytical government argument, not an empirical finding or recovered concession. His own Hale III-B join nonetheless withheld a complete constitutional answer based merely on harm capacity and exceptions. Rybar's count clarity strengthens the argument without completing the coverage/restriction reasoning. He therefore joins R while leaving that argument genuinely available; I is not compelled merely because acquisition is protected or the weapons are militarily useful.

**Join barriers and permissible accommodation:** He does not demand empirical certainty or a new universal scrutiny tier. He can join a remand that allows supported legislative judgments to matter within the independent arms inquiry. He cannot join a commerce-only affirmance, a military-affiliation barrier, a categorical prohibition on meaningful regulation, or an assertion that the existing exceptions conclusively prove either constitutionality or invalidity.
