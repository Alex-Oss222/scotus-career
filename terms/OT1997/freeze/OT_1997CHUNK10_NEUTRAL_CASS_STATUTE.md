# Cass County: Nelson Act statutory text only

Primary source: Act of January 14, 1889, chapter 24, 25 Stat. 642-646, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-25/pdf/STATUTE-25-Pg642.pdf. Full act downloaded and read. Local statute source: `../research/chunk10_comparator/Nelson-Act-25-Stat-642.pdf` and `.txt`. This source contains statutes, not an opinion in the pending case. The companion `OT_1997CHUNK10_NEUTRAL_CASS_STATUTE_EXTRACT.txt` mechanically copies complete sections 5 and 6, pages 644-645, retaining OCR line wraps and marginal headings. It contains no judicial outcome, opinion, vote, or reasoning.

Section 1 authorizes negotiation of written cessions of Chippewa reservation title, with specified White Earth/Red Lake exceptions, assent thresholds, presidential approval, and protection of previously allotted land unless the individual allottee separately consents. Presidential acceptance/approval establishes assent and extinguishes Indian title for the purposes and on the terms of the Act. This is a cession provision, not an explicit tax clause.

Section 3 provides allotments in severalty in conformity with the General Allotment Act of February 8, 1887; confirms prior White Earth allotments with the same tenure/conditions; deducts previous allotments from entitlement; and permits Indians on existing reservations to elect an allotment where they live rather than removal to White Earth.

Section 4 directs surveys and examination in forty-acre lots, identifies land with standing/growing pine as pine lands, requires appraisals at no less than three dollars per thousand board feet, allows approval/modification/rejection and substitute appraisal, and supplies public/local records. Other acquired land is termed agricultural land. These survey/appraisal directions supply the predicates for the sections 5/6 sales.

Section 5 requires public cash auction to the highest bidder after prescribed publication, usually in forty-acre parcels, at no less than appraised value; remaining parcels become available for private cash sale at appraised value. Section 6 disposes of surveyed agricultural land that is neither allotted nor reserved for future Indian use to actual settlers under homestead law after thirty-day notice. It requires $1.25 per acre in five annual payments, full payment and proof of five-year occupancy before patent, and voids pre-final-entry conveyances/contracts. It preserves existing valid entries and permits a specified second homestead entry after an earlier failure to perfect title. The complete provisos appear in the companion statutory extract.

Section 7 places net disposal proceeds (after enumerated expenses) in a Treasury fund credited to the Chippewa, prescribing interest and distribution; it concerns proceeds, not an express reservation of trust title in the sold land. None of sections 4-6 expressly uses a tax-authorization clause. Whether their text, incorporated laws, or the applicable controlling precedents satisfy a tax-authorization standard is reserved for independent adjudication.
