## Bates v. United States — No. 96-7185

### Judgment, reach and common propositions

All eight associates support **affirming the Seventh Circuit's reinstatement of the indictment on the additional-intent question**, with ordinary further proceedings in the prosecution. They reach the conduct-date construction of 20 U.S.C. §1097(a) on certiorari under §1254(1). They do not review a conviction, an instruction or a sufficiency determination. The twelve allegations concern 1990 conduct; neither the later refund amendment nor a hypothetical trial supplies the rule.

The following propositions receive each of the eight associates' express support at the same level of generality:

- **B1 — Additional purpose:** Knowing and willful misapplication under the applicable §1097(a) does not additionally require a purpose to injure or defraud the United States. Express culpability language and the prohibition of misapplication must be enforced, but the bank-statute analogy does not establish an extra purpose element in this enactment.
- **B2 — Culpability and limited application:** This conclusion does not make an ordinary unpaid refund, accounting error, insolvency or breach of a funding condition sufficient by itself. The case concerns allegations of deliberate unauthorized diversion with knowledge of the refund duty, considered on the lower court's culpable-misapplication construction. Allegations remain allegations. The Court need not exhaustively define willfulness or decide every possible conversion theory to reject a distinct injurious or fraudulent purpose.
- **B3 — Construction and lenity:** Morissette's treatment of borrowed legal terms and Ratzlaf's contextual reading remain obligatory parts of interpretation; statutory silence alone is not universally conclusive. Here the proposed separate purpose is not established as the received meaning of this particular enactment. After text, context and the distinct history of the bank offense are considered, the uncertainty necessary for an independently sufficient lenity holding on that extra element is absent. Ratzlaf's residual-lenity holding remains intact.
- **B4 — Remedy and reservation:** Affirmance permits prosecution, without finding guilt, actual knowledge, loss, concealment or evidentiary sufficiency. It imposes no trial, sentence or harmless-error result. Properly available independent indictment objections retain their own requirements; their preservation is not assumed. Section 2 and the separate conversion objection receive no freestanding expanded holding.

Each Justice supports this judgment and directly joins B1–B4. Their individual grounds below explain compatible routes to these propositions; they do not authorize additional general holdings or a broader mental-state formula.

### Alternative-path test

**Retain the additional-purpose requirement.** The strongest path for Bates is that willful misapplication is inherited criminal terminology carrying an intent-to-injure-or-defraud meaning, reinforced by Ratzlaf, Morissette and residual lenity. It would preserve the dismissal if the indictment failed to allege the required element, or require an appropriately bounded remand rather than an acquittal. Stevens's Wells dissent and the Ratzlaf majority's attention to culpability make this a substantial interpretive path, not a frivolous demand for more favorable law.

Its obstacle is statute-specific: the received bank construction rests on a history involving omission of earlier language in a technical revision, while the supplied §1097 history does not establish that same incorporation. The word willful can do meaningful work through culpable intentional misapplication without requiring the distinct ulterior purpose. A shared vocabulary alone cannot establish the requested element. The associates therefore reject this path on the supplied statutory question, while preserving it as a different outcome if genuinely different statutory history were neutrally established.

**Reject the additional purpose while retaining culpable misapplication.** The strongest objection is that a literal omission approach could turn financial distress or a misunderstood refund obligation into a felony. The adopted response is B2, not an assurance about prosecutorial restraint: no ordinary default alone establishes the offense, and the Government must prove the actual knowing/willful misapplication requirements. This distinguishes the innocent-conduct concerns in Morissette, Liparota and Staples without narrowing those decisions. The proper pretrial affirmance does not require choosing a complete trial instruction now.

**Join accommodation:** A common opinion containing B1–B4 is available to all eight. Omitting the retained culpability or treating the decision as an actual finding of intentional conversion would change several joins. No separate writing is required by these commitments; a broader rejection of context or lenity is not supported merely because the judgment is unanimous among associates.

## State Oil Co. v. Khan — No. 96-871

### Judgment, reach and common propositions

All eight associates support **vacating the Seventh Circuit's antitrust disposition and remanding for proceedings under the rule of reason**. The separate affirmance of the state-contract dismissal is outside the disturbed component. The Court does not directly restore the district court's antitrust summary judgment or decide ultimate injury, admissibility, competitive effect, damages or liability.

Each associate reaches the supplied maximum vertical resale-price question through §1254(1) and expressly joins these propositions:

- **K1 — Classification and precedent:** A vertical agreement setting a maximum resale price is evaluated under the rule of reason, rather than condemned per se merely because it imposes that ceiling. Albrecht's contrary maximum-vertical-price rule is expressly overruled. Its rule governs the lower court until this Court changes it; the Seventh Circuit's obedience to it was institutionally proper.
- **K2 — Sufficient reason for change:** The categorical equation between a ceiling and predictable competitive harm cannot be maintained when ceilings can restrain a distributor's exercise of market power and promote interbrand competition, while the identified contrary effects depend on the arrangement and competitive circumstances. Sylvania's competition-based treatment of vertical distribution and Atlantic Richfield's separation of low-price benefits from cognizable injury expose the weakness of treating this class as conclusively harmful. That concrete defect, rather than dislike of an older result alone, supports revision of this Sherman Act rule.
- **K3 — Continuing exposure and boundaries:** A ceiling is not automatically lawful. Possible service suppression, exclusion, exploitation of dealer dependence, disguised minimum-price agreements, or a proved horizontal arrangement remain legally significant under the rules appropriate to the actual conduct. This decision finds none of those facts here and changes no rule for minimum resale prices or horizontal price fixing. Brunswick and Atlantic Richfield retain the independent antitrust-injury requirement; no rule makes every dealer's lost margin a recoverable antitrust injury.
- **K4 — Component remedy:** Vacate the antitrust appellate disposition and remand to apply the new classification and address properly available remaining matters. The agreement and rebate mechanism cannot be relabeled a unilateral suggestion. The state-contract affirmance is undisturbed. The classification change does not by itself decide the expert-evidence or injury questions or require their wholesale reopening; their lawful significance under the resulting standard belongs in the further proceedings.

These are direct joins at the stated scope. They do not create a generic controlling stare decisis checklist or import one from the divided Hubbard rationales. The common rationale is the particular unjustified per se classification; each Justice's additional emphasis remains individual unless expressly adopted.

### Alternative-path test

**Retain Albrecht and sustain the appellate classification.** The arrangement actually removes the return from charging above the supplier's ceiling; changing the sanction's form does not avoid the precedent. Its strongest rationale combines dealer-service and survival concerns, the difficulty of proving adverse effects, administrable protection of price independence, and statutory stare decisis. Stevens's received dissents and O'Connor's and Souter's published statutory-precedent positions give these considerations particular weight. This path would preserve the antitrust remand without deciding final damages or altering the contract judgment.

The decisive weakness is that those possible harms do not establish that a purely vertical maximum-price term is predictably anticompetitive across its lawful uses. The received injury cases already acknowledge low-price competitive benefits while preserving actionable injuries. Classification must address those benefits candidly; treating every loss of freedom to increase resale price as conclusive competitive injury cannot do that. A legal rule that examines the adverse effects remains available. The associates therefore choose the express, limited revision in K1–K3. They do not find that litigation will be costless or that the old rule generated no reliance.

**Replace the per se rule and remand.** The strongest objection is that a manufacturer may use a ceiling to suppress valuable services, disguise a minimum, or coordinate dealers, and that rule-of-reason litigation may make those harms harder to redress. The adopted rule leaves those mechanisms legally available and does not import findings that would defeat them. It changes the automatic inference from the category, not the possibility of proving unreasonable restraint. The contract itself supports a ceiling agreement, but not an inference that it is an actual minimum or horizontal conspiracy. Those distinctions also explain Stevens's accommodation: the decision changes the substantive premise directly and preserves the injuries his prior dissents said should not be immunized by labels.

**Narrow avoidance paths fail on this record.** The rebate obligation is an agreement, not a unilateral suggested price. No supported alternative injury or preservation ground independently resolves the reviewed disposition. An artificial exception confined to this contractual wording would leave Albrecht's label while removing its substance. No associate adopts such a distinction.

**Join accommodation:** All eight can join the bounded competitive explanation and remedy. Stevens's join is unavailable for a categorical procompetitive-ceiling rule, rejection of actual adverse market evidence, or erosion of horizontal price-fixing doctrine. O'Connor and Souter require the express precedent explanation. The others likewise join neither an all-vertical-restraint revision nor final summary judgment for State Oil. A separate writing may emphasize these limits but is not necessary to the present commitments; no author is selected.

## City of Monroe v. United States — No. 97-122

### Judgment, reach and common propositions

All eight associates support **reversing the three-judge court's §5 judgment requiring plurality voting on the stated ground and remanding for proceedings consistent with the independent operation of the approved 1968 default**. This is direct appellate review under §1253 of the injunction, not certiorari and not an interim application. On the supplied submission-stage record, each supports a reasoned summary merits disposition; no oral-argument event is invented.

The operative premise is exactly the frozen packet's account: §34A-1407(a)'s majority default was submitted and precleared, and the legally relevant Monroe charter had no plurality requirement. Treating that explicit rule as applicable is a legal application of reported record propositions, not a reconstruction of an approval letter. The 1966 local amendment remains unprecleared; it is not the source of the independent authority recognized here.

Each associate expressly joins:

- **M1 — Proper review channel:** An enforcement court may determine the actual scope and applicability of an Attorney General preclearance. It may not reconsider the substantive wisdom of the approval or itself grant substantive preclearance. The appeal presents the former inquiry.
- **M2 — Independent submitted rule:** Approval of an identified, operative statewide majority default supplies §5 approval for that rule's application to a municipality whose charter lacks the plurality provision to which the default yields. A separate unsubmitted local amendment does not negate the independent effect of the rule actually submitted and approved. On the supplied facts, Monroe falls within that default, so the absence of preclearance for its 1966 amendment alone does not support the challenged injunction.
- **M3 — Specificity remains essential:** Neither an approved delegation of local discretion nor approval of a statewide compilation silently approves distinct local methods. City of Rome's rejection of implied approval of its local electoral amendments remains intact. Its charter's plurality provision materially distinguishes it from the operation of the identified default here. Lopez, Young and Foreman remain controlling: actual submission scope matters, and unpresented local changes do not become approved by association, long use or state compulsion.
- **M4 — Relief and limits:** Remove the conflicting §5 plurality injunction and remand within the legal scope decided. No relief rests on a newly approved 1966 amendment. No separate unapproved change, discrimination claim, §2 action, completed election, damages issue or replacement-system design is decided. No new approval request or evidentiary hearing is automatically required. A different practice genuinely outside the submitted default remains subject to ordinary coverage and preclearance law.

All eight support both the judgment and M1–M4. The common proposition is confined to an identified self-executing default and the supplied charter facts; agreement is not a broad rule that state preclearance obviates local inquiry.

### Alternative-path test

**Affirm because the local majority amendment remains unprecleared.** The strongest Government path invokes City of Rome, McCain, Clark and the recent unanimous submission-specific cases. It prevents a jurisdiction from concealing a local change inside statewide approval, and Monroe's long use or 1990 submission cannot cure the 1966 omission. That rule would require prospective nonuse of a proved distinct covered change lacking preclearance, with relief fitted to the actual baseline.

The difficulty is that this path must answer the independent authority identified in the frozen facts. Unlike Foreman's undisclosed partisan formula, the default here is itself the submitted rule; unlike Rome's express plurality charter, Monroe's charter supplies no plurality provision taking it out of that default. Treating every reliance on a state rule as an attempt to clear the local amendment would collapse the two claims. The associates preserve the Government's specific-submission principle but reject its application as a categorical bar to the rule actually approved.

**Give the approved default its independent effect.** The strongest objection is that the statewide submission may not fairly identify the local electoral change and that the Attorney General cannot be expected to infer all local charter practices. M2 does not require such inference: the supported rule expressly governs the identified class of charters and directly supplies the majority default. The Court recognizes that operation without declaring every local implementation or earlier amendment approved. No conclusion is drawn about undisclosed qualifications in an unrecovered letter. If a neutrally validated source established an actual limitation withholding approval of this default or a material charter provision excluding Monroe, that would change the premise and require fresh affected modeling; it is not presently established.

**Limited legal-error remand.** If the supplied record established only approval of a comprehensive code, without establishing approval of the operative default or the charter's pertinent terms, a remand to decide actual scope and applicability would be lawful under Lopez and Foreman. It could not presume nonproduction, require an evidentiary hearing automatically, or equate uncertainty with an unprecleared change. That is not the adopted disposition because the present frozen packet supplies the actual default's submission/approval and the material charter distinction. Demanding the original letters solely to duplicate those supported propositions would confuse a research limitation with a record defect.

**Join accommodation:** The common opinion must retain M3's specific-submission limits and identify why this default, rather than the unsubmitted amendment, governs. An assertion of blanket statewide clearance would lose the associates' stated rationale; a remand premised on assumed missing litigant proof is unsupported. No separate writing is necessary to the present commitments, and none is assigned.
