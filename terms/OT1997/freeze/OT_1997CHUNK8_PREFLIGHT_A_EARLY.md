**Entering law:** Rouse (January 15, 1998) binds all eight Associate Justices to §3626(b)(2)'s independent statutory route, the amended current **and** ongoing preservation condition, termination of the prospective judicial obligations themselves without retrospective annulment, rejection of the presented separation-of-powers, equality and vested-enforcement objections, and no automatic conversion of judicial duties into a state-enforceable contract. Its contract-impairment rationale has all eight Associates: federal approval does not make the United States a contracting party; Atchison's rationality inquiry applies on the assumed-contract premise, without deciding an actual independent contract. Rouse preserves independent judicial findings and a proper opportunity to present material evidence, but requires no new hearing from speculation or absent research files alone. Here the reported legal restriction on the evidentiary record supplies the distinct remand predicate.

The November 26, 1997 amendment, Pub. L. 105-119 §123(a)(2), (b), applies to pending cases. Section 3626(b)(3) requires written record-based findings that relief remains necessary to correct a current **and** ongoing federal violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn and is the least intrusive necessary means. Original current-or-ongoing wording in the lower opinion is not current law. Section (b)(2) applies to relief granted without the necessary substantive findings; absence of ritual wording alone is insufficient. The definitions include consent decrees in relief and distinguish private settlements; §3626(d) preserves state-court relief based solely on state-law claims, not an automatic entitlement to enforcement of a terminated federal decree.

Rufo retains its separate changed-circumstances and tailoring rule but supplies no additional prerequisite to statutory termination. Morgan Stanley and Plaut permit their bounded securities-dismissal reinstatement procedure while preserving judicial determination; the contrary historical Plaut account in the lower opinion is not governing law. Kokkonen preserves the independent-jurisdiction requirement and the distinction between an incorporated decree and a private contract. Bell's protection of detainees from punishment survives. Neither a decree's breach nor relief exceeding the minimum conduct rule alone determines a federal violation or remedial necessity.
