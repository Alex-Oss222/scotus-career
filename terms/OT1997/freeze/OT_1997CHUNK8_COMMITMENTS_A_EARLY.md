## Benjamin v. Jacobson — May 7, 1998

**Common disposition and scope:** Affirm the Second Circuit insofar as it rejects the presented constitutional challenges and rejects retrospective destruction of completed judicial effects; vacate its treatment of the same future decree obligations as necessarily surviving for state enforcement, and remand for application of the amended statute. Preserve its direction that the plaintiffs receive a meaningful opportunity to establish §3626(b)(3)'s preservation findings: on the reported refusal to consider relevant current evidence, the district court must permit the necessary current evidentiary presentation and decide its sufficiency. That case-specific direction is not a hearing entitlement in every termination case and does not find a violation. No ruling determines an independent state contract's existence, breach, accrued liability or proper forum. End unsupported prospective judicial obligations upon the statutory determination, not completed effects. The existing lower-court stay is addressed on remand under lawful authority; no new Supreme Court stay, automatic-stay ruling, expiration clock or deadline is supplied.

**Shared sufficient grounds:** Rouse directly controls the statutory and constitutional questions. Congress changes the conditions for continuing relief, leaving courts to decide actual violations and remedial scope. Section (b)(2) does not require a separate Rufo showing; section (b)(3) requires the amended current-and-ongoing findings and every need/narrowness/least-intrusiveness qualification. The Second Circuit's surviving-decree-as-contract premise cannot circumvent termination of judicial duties. Conversely, a silent record made artificially closed to current evidence does not adjudicate the statutory preservation condition. The constitutional challenges fail within Rouse's boundaries, including the assumed-contract rationality rationale; a supported application excluded solely by the amended conjunction remains reserved.
