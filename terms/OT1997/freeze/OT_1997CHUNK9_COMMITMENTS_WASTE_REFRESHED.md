# Frozen independent commitments — Waste Management v. Metropolitan Government of Nashville

**Stage and source scope:** Independent non-Stone commitments grounded in the clean Waste packet, Camps supplement and Tracy/Fulton context. The eight Associate positions and their component-specific remedies are stated below.
**Event:** May 27, 1998; Sixth Circuit Nos. 95-5863 and 95-5900, 130 F.3d 731. No Supreme Court docket or argument date supplied.
**Authorized law:** The initial clean Waste packet and exact source companion; the new Camps supplement and its full public entry, Tracy and Fulton holdings; full entering-law slice; frozen May 21 public projection; actual May 26 ALPA/Ashmus/Lewis public refresh. Cutoff May 27, 1998. Those three new holdings do not alter a waste-service comparison, tax-compensation requirement or these injunctions' reach. No proposed case outcome enters as law.

## Reach and common factual/remedial limits

All eight Associates reach both granted Commerce Clause questions under §1254(1). The Sixth Circuit's §1292(a)(1) injunction appeal is a lawful vehicle; the passenger-car/pickup takings and ripeness remand remains outside review. Metro's actual ownership must be recognized. NTTC's operation supplies no finding about operator ownership, profit distribution, nationality or contractually transferred risk; no complete contract or purchase by Metro of every covered private transaction is invented.

Residential routing has the Director's written-authorization exception. Nonresidential routing occurs only through the reported allocation needed to meet the 6,300-ton weekly operating requirement, using monthly reports, at-least-quarterly pro rata calculations based on best available data, and advance notices. The outside-system charge is independently $2 per cubic yard or $8 per ton on the specified Metro-generated/collected waste disposed at non-Metro facilities inside or outside Metro; Metro-owned sites and NTTC are exempt. The narrative's $36 tipping fee and footnote's enacted $44 rate effective January 1, 1995 are distinct; neither is used to invent a quantified burden.

Reliable fuel, disposal service and the State's 25-percent reduction requirement are supported objectives. Competitive prices, increased direct or contracted municipal collection requiring preparation and possibly legislation, and a generator/container tax are the recorded alternatives. They are not automatically costless, equally profitable or judicially prescribed. The absence of an identified equivalent intrastate tax burden is a real defect in the compensation defense, not a finding that government incurs no costs.

## Three supported paths

**D — Extend discrimination scrutiny to the compulsory private transactions.** Public title is materially different from Carbone's private facility, but these rules still reserve independent generators' and haulers' purchases to one local processor or impose a charge for choosing its competitors. SSC protects actual disposal bought by government, not this use of regulatory authority over others' purchases. Camps refuses to let public-benefit labels cleanse restrictions on private transactions; Tracy requires a supported difference in the actual offering and does not presume one from institutional identity. The shown environmental and fuel purposes do not establish that competing disposal services are incomparable for these compelled purchases. This expressly resolves the reserved public category for these measures, rather than pretending Carbone already did so.

Under D, both measures discriminate. The reported alternatives defeat the asserted necessity of this discriminatory mechanism on the supplied record; the rule asks whether reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives adequately serve legitimate purposes, not whether every imaginable substitute is disproved. The fee separately fails Oregon Waste/Fulton's cumulative compensation requirements: identified actual intrastate burden, approximate nonexcessive match and substantially equivalent taxable events. Metro has not identified the first, and general financing need supplies none of them. Affirm both reviewed injunction rulings, without a substitute program or monetary award.

**C — Public-facility and independent diversion-cost rules.** Routing locally generated waste to a genuinely public facility does not facially discriminate against interstate commerce when local private processors and out-of-state processors are treated alike. Whether the facility is genuinely public turns on its actual ownership, control, financing, public-service responsibility and revenues, and the absence of concealed private favoritism; public title alone does not settle the question. A diversion fee survives only insofar as its actual incidence and amount are genuinely tied to diversion-related costs rather than interstate origin or protection of local commerce. A permissible public routing distinction does not establish that connection, and a compensatory-tax defense separately requires an identified actual intrastate burden, rough approximation without excess, and substantially equivalent taxable events reasonably treated as mutually exclusive proxies.

For C, if the actual public service is materially different so the measure is evenhanded rather than discriminatory, Pike requires the supported interstate burden to be clearly excessive relative to the local benefits. If the actual offering does not support that difference and discrimination is established, Oregon Waste/Camps's legitimate-purpose and adequate-nondiscriminatory-alternatives rule applies. No new burden of perfect proof is adopted. The fee's incidence needs its own examination; a routing distinction does not establish a matched burden or compensatory tax. Its existing compensation claim fails absent the required identified burden, even if another legally supported classification ultimately sustains it. The remand does not direct a new hearing, presume the public-service predicate, or guarantee validity.

**R — Reject the judicial negative Commerce Clause ground expressly.** Scalia and Thomas's actual published Camps position supplies an independent constitutional objection to invalidation under the doctrine itself. They would reverse both reviewed injunction rulings because the affirmative commerce grant does not, in their view, authorize this judicial restriction on local action absent governing congressional legislation. This is an express proposal to displace contrary negative-Commerce authority, not law already in force. Their proposal would reject the doctrinal basis for the adverse applications of Carbone, Oregon Waste, Fulton and Camps; their earlier joins in those cases remain historical facts and controlling law unless a Court majority actually changes them. SSC's separate actual-purchase protection need not be reversed to remove the underlying regulatory prohibition.

The tentative Camps Import-Export alternative supplies no additional disposition. These are routing and per-ton disposal measures, not the value-based real-property tax considered there. No import/export status, party presentation or definitive constitutional tax rule is supplied here. R therefore grants no adjudication under that Clause and leaves independent unreviewed grounds intact.

## Enhanced adversarial conclusions and separate-writing functions

D's hardest objection is the actual public ownership and the later Camps/Tracy distinction. Stevens and Kennedy conclude the supported facts still leave the compulsory private service comparable and the government's ends adequately attainable through the recorded nondiscriminatory alternatives. Their rule is confined to these mechanisms, not all municipal provision or public purchasing. No government financing interest is denied.

C's hardest objection is that regulatory compulsion still reserves local demand and that Metro has already failed to prove tax compensation. Its proponents accept both concerns; the defect in compensation survives. They nevertheless regard a categorical refusal to examine the publicly owned service as a legal error requiring correction before the proper scrutiny can decide both measures. They do not assume every public duty changes the relevant offering or require utility regulation as the only way to show a difference.

R's hardest objection is the settled body of controlling precedent and the absence of any entering-law overruling. Scalia and Thomas answer through their actual published constitutional objection, expressly proposing that change rather than claiming it already occurred. No historical same-matter outcome motivates the change. Their separate-writing function is essential if the Court retains existing doctrine: it states their different sufficient constitutional ground, greater reversal remedy and withholding of assent from judicial policy balancing. Ginsburg's narrower comparative ground must not be attributed to this repudiation.

## Remedy and final freeze receipt

All dispositions remain confined to the two reviewed Commerce Clause injunction components. None decides the passenger-car/pickup taking or its ripeness, damages, refund, contract breach, a substitute tax or fee, new procurement, title transfer, guaranteed business or compelled acceptance of waste. All routes preserve any properly remaining independent proceedings at their actual stage. A C remand authorizes lawful component-specific evaluation, not automatic discovery, trial or an indefinite research remand.

Eight Associate positions and their grounds are fixed here. The complete public-facility and independent diversion-cost formulation is joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; their mandate remains a bounded vacatur and remand on both components.
