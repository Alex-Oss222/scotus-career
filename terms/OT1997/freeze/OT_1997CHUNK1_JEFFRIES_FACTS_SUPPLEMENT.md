# Jeffries — chair duties and reported disruption mechanisms

Objective source extraction for fresh neutral validation only. This supplement adds no current-case framing, legal conclusion, vote, author, procedural premise or requested result. The previously frozen artifacts are unchanged.

**Sources:** The judicial texts of [21 F.3d 1238 (2d Cir. 1994)](https://openjurist.org/21/f3d/1238/jeffries-v-v-harleston), particularly paragraphs 9, 11–18, 22, 25, 27–30, 49, 52 and 55–57; and [52 F.3d 9 (2d Cir. 1995)](https://openjurist.org/52/f3d/9/jeffries-v-v-harleston), particularly paragraphs 8, 15, 28 and 34. The latter is also available in the [Caselaw Access Project reporter transcription](https://static.case.law/f3d/52/html/0009-01.html). Paragraph numbers refer to OpenJurist's judicial-text numbering, not its modern summaries. No original trial transcript or complete bylaw document was independently recovered here.

## Actual chair duties and evidentiary status

- **Bylaw evidence described in the reported trial record:** The chair carried out policies made by the department, faculty and Board of Trustees. Listed duties were department recordkeeping, assigning faculty courses, representing the department at internal CUNY meetings, presiding over department meetings, helping prepare departmental budget proposals, and evaluating professors for tenure. These are the duties the panel says the introduced bylaw specified, not a newly reconstructed job description. *21 F.3d 1238*, paragraphs 22 and 55, pages 1243 and 1246–1247.
- **Reported administrative inquiry:** The Provost told Harleston that interviewees inside and outside the department considered Jeffries's performance adequate. The reported activities included course scheduling, department meetings, faculty recruitment, records and representation at meetings. The Social Sciences Dean separately reported adequate performance of ordinary administrative responsibilities but warned that his review was perfunctory and promised follow-up. These are reported administrative assessments, not an independent audit or a jury finding of flawless performance. Paragraphs 12–14.
- **Panel characterization and evidentiary limit:** The 1994 panel described the role as essentially ministerial, not policymaking. It relied on the bylaw's implementation duties and the Provost's task-focused evaluation. It reported no trial evidence of special loyalty/confidentiality obligations or public contact exceeding an ordinary professor's. Absence of such evidence in that reported record is not proof that no chair ever performs public functions. Paragraphs 55–56. The 1995 panel repeated the ministerial/nonpolicymaking characterization and the no-greater-public-contact comparison. *52 F.3d 9*, paragraphs 15 and 34.
- **Speech setting:** The festival was unaffiliated with CUNY, although Jeffries was introduced as department chair and as a former curriculum-review committee member. Public identification by office is established; CUNY sponsorship of the event is not. *21 F.3d 1238*, paragraph 9.

## Forecasted harm, actual-performance evidence and findings

- **Stated forecast:** In the letter reporting the one-year-term vote, Harleston said the speech threatened recruitment, fundraising and CUNY's relationship with the community. Those are the concrete institutional interests named in the published account. The account does not quantify expected losses or identify a particular lost recruit, withheld donation, donor commitment or disrupted community program. Paragraph 17.
- **Related asserted concerns and investigation:** The opinion identifies possible impaired interaction with Jewish faculty, stigma to the department/university and discouragement of benefactors. It states that Harleston and Reynolds did not investigate those matters before recommending the one-year term. That is the panel's account of the record, not an independent factual finding by this extraction. Paragraph 15.
- **Public reaction and institutional statements:** The speech received extensive media attention. Harleston publicly condemned it as undermining CUNY's racial, ethnic and religious harmony policy; other officials announced an examination and possible remedies. Publicity and these official statements are reported events. They do not themselves establish a particular service failure or prove the forecast's reasonableness. Paragraph 11.
- **Later reported incidents:** After the term-limitation decision, the Dean and Provost reported angry exchanges with faculty/administrators about that decision and an outburst in an interview with a Harvard student reporter. The defendants offered ineffectiveness evidence and maintained that the term would have been limited without the speech. Paragraphs 18 and 25. The 1994 panel said the record did not show that the Board considered those incidents as a reason for denying reappointment; the source also describes conflicting testimony over discussion of performance at the later replacement meeting. Paragraphs 20 and 52. These distinctions preserve chronology and attribution without resolving an independent defense.
- **Actual jury findings:** The jury answered that the speech had not hampered effective and efficient operation of the department, college or university, while also finding that defendants were motivated by a reasonable expectation of disruption. *21 F.3d 1238*, paragraphs 29–30; *52 F.3d 9*, paragraph 8. The interrogatories report the general reasonable-expectation finding, not separate quantified findings on recruitment, fundraising, faculty relations or community programs.
- **Appellate assessment, kept separate:** The 1994 panel characterized actual-disruption evidence as meager and discussed the absence of evidence before the Board of threatened harm. The 1995 panel treated the reasonable-expectation finding as operative and independently evaluated potential disruption against speech value. Those are differing appellate assessments under their respective legal analyses, not additional jury findings. This supplement adopts neither as the current legal resolution. *21 F.3d 1238*, paragraphs 49 and 57; *52 F.3d 9*, paragraph 28.

## Limit of the extraction

**Requested individual-vote mapping:** At the late-October term vote, **Edith Everett, Herman Badillo and Harold Jacobs voted to reject reappointment outright; Sylvia Bloom abstained** because she had previously criticized Jeffries's views. Blanche Bernstein was the fourth original outright-rejection voter; her later death and discontinuance of claims account for the three such voters among the six defendants discussed in the earlier extraction. This mapping is explicit in *21 F.3d 1238*, paragraph 16, and is consistent with the grouped description in *52 F.3d 9*, paragraphs 6–9. It describes that vote only, without resolving personal causation or liability.

The published sources provide real duties and specific forecasted interests beyond the verdict's general label. They do not supply a detailed evidentiary model of how many recruits, donors or working relationships would be lost, a quantified disruption estimate, or separately attributed predictions for every trustee.
