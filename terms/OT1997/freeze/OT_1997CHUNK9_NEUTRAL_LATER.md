## United States v. Lacy — May 28, 1998

### Identity, vehicle, and scope

Ninth Circuit No. 95-30370, **119 F.3d 742 (July 10, 1997)**; no Supreme Court docket or argument date supplied. The Ninth Circuit affirmed a conviction after a jury trial in the Western District of Washington. This is ordinary review of that completed appellate judgment, not an interlocutory proceeding. Lacy seeks reversal of the adverse suppression ruling and resulting conviction judgment; the United States defends the warrant and judgment.

The two authorized questions are staleness of the ten-month-old download information and particularity of the authorization to seize the computer system, storage media and related records. Instructional scienter, the statutory meaning of “matter,” the interstate-commerce element and plain error were separately decided below but are **outside this grant**. Receipt was acquitted and the importation charge dismissed before trial; do not reinstate either or transfer them into the possession review.

### Supported record and actual lower reasoning

BAMSE, a Danish electronic bulletin board, recorded sixteen calls by a user identifying himself as “Jim Bakker” and six downloaded picture files. Customs traced the number to Lacy's apartment; telephone records matched the recorded dates. The warrant affidavit specifically described at least two downloaded GIF files depicting minors engaged in sexual activity. This is evidence of receipt, not merely an order or attempt to purchase. The agent described, from training and experience, possessors' high valuation and lengthy retention of such material in secure locations, often their homes. Lacy contested the foundation for applying those retention observations to him. The lower court considered the confirmed downloads sufficient foundation; it expressly declined an indefinite-retention assumption.

The warrant authorized an apartment search and seizure of computer equipment, records and BAMSE-related documents. The reported authorization described hardware generically. The agent stated that the investigators could not determine whether the images were on the drive or disks, or specify in advance the hardware and software needed for accurate retrieval. The Ninth Circuit found probable cause regarding the system and objective document limits, including the BAMSE connection. It distinguished *Kow*, whose authorization and affidavit lacked comparable limitations. Its particularity analysis considered the basis for each described category, objective selection standards, and whether greater specificity was possible with information available at issuance. Those are the circuit's formulation, not a newly adopted Supreme Court checklist.

Agents seized the computer, more than 100 disks and documents. **Some disks came from a separate storage room searched with Lacy's consent**; do not describe every disk as seized from the apartment under the challenged warrant. The district court suppressed several documents both sides called inconsequential. Images subsequently discovered and trial admissions cannot retrospectively establish probable cause or particularity. The record's March 6, 1993 possession date is reproduced in the reported jury instruction; no separate exact warrant date is supplied by this preparation.

### Law entering, lawful alternatives, and remedies

*Sgro*, 287 U.S. 206 (1932), requires an adequately current basis; *Gates*, 462 U.S. 213 (1983), requires the practical totality and substantial-basis review of a magistrate's determination. *Andresen*, 427 U.S. 463 (1976), and *Coolidge*, 403 U.S. 443 (1971), supply protection against general exploratory searches. The lower opinion's *Weber*, *Dozier*, *Gann*, *Cardwell*, *Kow* and *Spilotro* are date-eligible circuit authorities, with their actual facts and limits, not national law merely because cited below.

Simulated *Pinson* (May 8, 1995) and *Myers* (June 11, 1996) protect concealed domestic information but do not invalidate every technologically related warrant or prescribe digital-search protocols. Their Scalia opinions and exact joins are copied in the reading aid. Their remedial rules keep the violation, preservation and exclusion questions separate. *Ornelas*'s nonwarrant appellate-review rule, reproduced in **Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause** (effective May 28, 1996), expressly preserves Gates's distinct warrant review. The earlier source's O'Melveny reference has no demonstrated role in these search questions and supplies no controlling digital-search rule.

The warrant-supporting path connects actual receipt, durable electronic storage and the supported retention account to continued possession after ten months; unknowable storage location and retrieval requirements can support inclusive equipment categories while offense-linked document limits constrain execution. Its strongest objection is the risk of turning generalized habits into perpetual probable cause and investigative convenience into permission to seize everything. A lawful version must address this person's proven acquisition, this interval and the particular categories.

The challenge path treats the interval and generalized retention inference as insufficient on this affidavit, or distinguishes the justification for looking for two files from seizure of an entire system and unrelated records. Its strongest objection is that the affidavit does not merely guess at acquisition, the circuit reports specific limits, and investigators could not know where the files were stored. A narrower treatment may decide one issue without issuing a categorical rule for all electronic evidence. No modern cloud, smartphone, remote-server, forensic-protocol or later digital-search doctrine enters.

If a defect is found, suppression and conviction relief still require preserved remedial analysis. *Leon* is available only on a properly presented, supported ground, excluding knowing/reckless magistrate deception, abandonment of neutrality, an affidavit so devoid of probable-cause indicia that reliance is wholly unreasonable, and facial deficiency preventing reasonable reliance. It includes relevant information-supplying officers and presupposes execution within scope. *Evans*'s court-clerical-error rule creates no broader police technology immunity. The reported opinion did not decide a Leon fallback; do not assume it was waived, established, or automatically dispositive. No automatic acquittal, release, retrial, new hearing, or suppression of consent-obtained evidence follows merely from correcting the warrant ruling.

## DeCarlo v. Fry — May 28, 1998

### Corrected judgment, channel, and components

Second Circuit Nos. 97-7702, 97-7704, 97-7754, **141 F.3d 56 (April 6, 1998)**. The supplied description of what a source “proposes” is replaced by the actual appellate disposition. The Second Circuit **affirmed qualified-immunity summary judgments for Astle and Blank; vacated the official-capacity judgment against DuRose and remanded for a correct municipal-liability determination; required clarification of the disposition of the distinct post-expungement personal-capacity claims against him; allowed amendment of pre-expungement claims; and affirmed Turner's personal-capacity jury verdict.** No personal damages judgment against DuRose was entered by that disposition.

The authorized question concerns Astle/Blank's immunity and the distinct proceedings remaining concerning DuRose. The DeCarlos seek relief from the adverse immunity judgments; DuRose's successful challenge to the official-capacity judgment and the unresolved personal claims require separate review rather than describing both parties as seeking the same disposition. A party wishing to disturb an additional lower component requires authorized scope; the unappealed Perales declaration and Turner's affirmed personal verdict are not automatically revived. This is §1254(1) review of the appellate decision following trial, cross-appeals and summary judgments, **not an interlocutory appeal from a denial of qualified immunity**.

### Record and lower grounds

The 1985 “indicated” abuse finding led to revocation of Juanita DeCarlo's day-care certification. The plaintiffs alleged a deficient bad-faith investigation; that allegation is not a new finding. A state administrative judge affirmed revocation in April 1986. The State expunged the indication against Juanita in May 1987 but retained the household link while the indication against Vito remained. The Appellate Division affirmed revocation in October 1987. After a later hearing, the administrative judge expunged Vito's indication in September 1988 for lack of credible evidence. Expungement and the earlier appellate judgment are distinct events; the packet does not convert either into an automatic reinstatement adjudication.

Juanita requested reinstatement in October 1988; her application was complete by February 1989. The lower opinion identifies the operative state regulations as requiring investigation within 75 days of a valid application and notice within 30 days after completion, with a fair hearing available after denial. The county failed to decide. These requirements do not themselves establish the federal right, entitlement to certification, constitutional damages or an individual defendant's culpability.

Astle testified that she did not recall instructions on the deadlines, was uncertain how to reconcile the prior revocation decision with expungement, and asked Blank. Blank consulted Turner; Turner and Blank consulted state regional director Klump rather than DuRose. Klump told **Turner** that the county had to decide. No decision followed. Do not infer that Astle or Blank received every later instruction, or that DuRose knew what the consultation chain conveyed. Conversely, consultation is not a new universal immunity defense.

The original pro se complaint concerned pre-expungement acts. The later amended complaint added post-expungement claims, including personal and official claims against DuRose and claims against Turner, Astle and Blank. At trial the judge directed official-capacity liability against DuRose under Rule 50(a), treating an earlier denial of his summary-judgment motion as though it established liability as law of the case. The jury awarded $300,000; fees and costs were $70,324.47. The Second Circuit found the premise mistaken: denial of summary judgment was no judgment for the plaintiffs. It left the familiar trial court to decide whether the trial evidence warrants judgment for plaintiffs, judgment for the county, or a jury trial under actual Monell standards. It did not decide that liability exists or fails, and did not reach damages/fee amounts.

The post-expungement personal claim's disposition was unclear: an earlier order could not logically dispose of claims added later, and an informal letter did not substitute for a ruling. The circuit required clarification and the proper stage-specific decision. DuRose's Article 78 adequate-process argument remained expressly undecided. The lower court affirmed Astle/Blank because reasonable officials in their actual circumstances sought supervisory advice; it supplied no wider categorical rule excusing all known deadlines.

### Entering law, competing paths, and limits

Harlow/Anderson governs objective, particularized **conduct-time** notice. *Elder* (February 23, 1994, unanimous Ginsburg opinion) requires all relevant legal authority on a preserved immunity question and permits no retroactive notice. *Lanier* (March 31, 1997, unanimous) rejects a Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin requirement while preserving uncertain applications; a state-law violation or bad intent alone supplies no federal right. Apply the law available during the actual 1988–1989 delay, not later decisions as new notice. The state scheme must establish the asserted entitlement and required process; missed timing alone does not decide the federal claim.

*Monell*, *Pembaur*, and *Kentucky v. Graham* distinguish county policy and causation from employee liability. The operative Standards module **Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation**, effective as supplemented by *McMillian* June 2, 1997, requires the correct entity and function-specific policymaker inquiry; operational discretion or a job title alone does not establish final policy. Its Alabama sheriff holding does not identify Oneida's policymaker. The damages judgment's official capacity forecloses using **personal** qualified immunity to defeat that county claim. The federal deprivation and attribution remain distinct.

*Crawford-El* (June 16, 1997) and **Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity** preserve competent evidence and ordinary burdens, necessary bounded discovery and actual causation without a special heightened quantum. They establish neither motive as an element of every process claim nor a new immunity defense. Current *Clanton*, *Valot*, and *Smith* preserve personal responsibility, conduct-date notice, proper assumed facts, and the separation of notice from present merits. Johnson/Behrens's interlocutory limits are real law but do not turn this postjudgment appeal into a barred factual-sufficiency appeal.

On Astle/Blank, one supported path affirms reasonable reliance on the actual administrative uncertainty and supervisory consultation without declaring every deadline breach constitutional. The competing path finds that an unreasoned consultation label does not resolve the specific prolonged deprivation or the actual extent of authority, knowledge and notice, and remands for a properly particularized inquiry. The counterarguments are reciprocal: genuine uncertainty cannot be erased by hindsight, while simply consulting someone cannot immunize otherwise plainly unlawful conduct. The model must not resolve absent knowledge facts or treat a discretionary license decision as exemption from all procedural duties.

On DuRose, a supported path preserves the circuit's correction of the mistaken Rule 50 premise and its separate clarification remand; another may seek a narrower disposition if the correctly reviewed trial record compels a legally available outcome. The latter cannot be selected merely because liability seems implausible: the full trial evidence is not reproduced and the circuit expressly reserved the factual application. No new county policy finding, personal liability, damages award, trial entitlement, recertification order, or universal Article 78 answer is supplied. ## City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence — June 1, 1998

### Actual channel, parties, and review scope

Sixth Circuit Nos. 96-5993 and 96-5996, **126 F.3d 856 (October 8, 1997), rehearing denied November 13, 1997**. The consolidated appeals were brought by **the plaintiffs** after individual-officer dismissals under Rule 12(b)(6) and municipal summary judgments. This is **not** an officers' interlocutory appeal from denial of immunity. The source warning about Johnson factual-sufficiency jurisdiction must not misdescribe the channel. Ordinary pleading and summary-judgment standards still prohibit resolving disputed facts against the nonmovant.

The City and official-capacity defendants **won below**: their summary judgments were affirmed. Black's individual-officer substantive-due-process claim and Stemler's individual-officer orientation-based equal-protection claim were reinstated. All other challenged dismissals remained affirmed. “City of Florence” is the inventory caption, not proof that the city lost or may claim personal qualified immunity. The review opposing reinstatement belongs to the adversely affected individual-officer components; no new petition number or unnoticed party substitution is fabricated. The authorized questions concern the compelled dangerous placement, discriminatory selection despite probable cause, and conduct-time immunity. They do not independently reopen municipal liability, false arrest, malicious prosecution, abandoned excessive force or an unpleaded fabrication claim.

### Supported facts and distinction between claims

**Conni Black died; Susan Stemler did not.** The opinion recites evidence in the plaintiffs' favor unless otherwise indicated. Black and Steve Kritis had been drinking; witnesses described his assaults, threats and dangerous pursuit after Black asked Stemler to drive her away. These are reported allegations and evidence, not this Court's findings. Barker warned Lieutenant Dusing that Kritis threatened the women's safety. Stemler described intoxication, assaults and threats. Other Florence and Boone officers arrived. Kritis called Stemler a lesbian and accused her of taking Black; the label spread among the officers. Stemler denies being a lesbian, which does not eliminate a claim based on their perception.

The account supports knowledge of Kritis's intoxication and violence, but the officers' roles and knowledge differ. Reuthe told Dusing that Kritis smelled of alcohol; Dusing's later claim conflicts with other evidence. Dusing directed Dolan to tell Black she would be arrested for public intoxication if she did not leave with Kritis. Dolan and Alsip lifted her from Stemler's car; Alsip placed her in Kritis's truck. The estate alleges this was against her will; Dolan's contrary account remains disputed. Alsip said he never heard a request to leave and would have acted differently if he had known the preceding events. No collective inference supplies every officer's personal knowledge, culpability or causal role.

The crash occurred about five minutes after departure. Kritis reported a renewed struggle; reconstruction could not determine what caused the steering movement. He was later convicted of state offenses. Causation is not automatically established by proximity, nor erased merely because an officer did not drive the truck. Avoid gratuitous injury detail; the legally material facts are fatal injury, timing and causal dispute.

Stemler was arrested for DUI, prosecuted twice and acquitted at the second trial. A separate state civil judgment found probable cause; Kentucky treated it as final for issue preclusion despite a pending appeal. The circuit used that predicate to affirm false-arrest and ordinary malicious-prosecution dismissal, while preserving reconsideration if the state judgment later reversed. No such later reversal is established here. A fabrication theory raised after the officers had been dismissed was not pleaded against them and did not support reversal. A separate excessive-force claim was voluntarily dismissed. Equal protection remains distinct: the opinion described a supported comparison between Stemler's arrest and the nonarrest of visibly intoxicated Kritis, plus alleged purposeful group-based animus.

### Lower reasoning and material concurrence

The circuit rejected a general inference that uncertainty at the edges of substantive due process defeats every claim. It characterized Black as actually restrained by threats and physical placement, distinguished a general failure to protect, and found the alleged knowing dangerous transfer sufficient under the custody/deliberate-indifference authorities. It declined to decide noncustodial state-created danger. Its formulation sometimes says “knew or reasonably should have known”; the actual entering law and any new Lewis holding must determine the proper culpability, not that shorthand.

On Stemler, the court held probable cause did not foreclose selective enforcement, used effect and purpose plus an actually comparable unarrested person, and rejected a blanket claim that orientation-based animus could never violate equal protection. It found no need for heightened scrutiny. Its references to historical Armstrong, Whren, Romer and Bryan County must be replaced by their actual simulated scope; in particular Armstrong's federal-prosecutor discovery rule does not automatically supply a merits or police-stop discovery rule here. The supplied entering module identifies Bryan County's hiring/fault issue as reserved, not a historical holding to import.

Judge Wellford agreed with the disposition but would avoid declaring facts, broadening culpability or equating classifications. He found sufficient allegations of intentional arbitrary conduct or conscious disregard toward a person in custody and would leave actual constitutional tort proof below. At rehearing he stressed actual awareness and actual drawing of the serious-risk inference under Farmer. He also preserved questions concerning later Kentucky appellate rulings and claim splitting rather than adjudicating them. This separate lower opinion is a legal alternative, not a Supreme Court vote predictor.

Simulated *Collins* (February 26, 1992) denies a general safe-workplace duty while expressly reserving compulsion, direct bodily injury and different affirmative-danger claims. Its separate attribution and substantive-right inquiries remain intact. Simulated *Pinder* (May 21, 1996) rejects custody from knowledge, promises or detention of an assailant, but leaves distinct affirmative deprivation analytically open. Its 5–4 immunity holding concerns a noncoercive 1989 undertaking, not this alleged forced 1994 transfer. O'Connor's conduct-time application has Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas with Stone; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissented. The precise public positions matter without compelling a vote here.

*Harlow*, *Anderson*, *Elder* and simulated *Lanier* require particularized apparent unlawfulness at the conduct date without a factual twin. **The event occurred overnight February 18–19, 1994.** Elder's February 23 opinion, Farmer later in 1994, and all later decisions may clarify present analysis but do not themselves give earlier notice. The conduct-date inquiry must rely on DeShaney, Youngberg, Daniels, relevant pre-event circuit authority and other law then effective. ## Duffy v. Wolle — June 3, 1998

### Corrected relief and actual channel

Eighth Circuit No. 96-3210, **123 F.3d 1026 (August 19, 1997)**; no Supreme Court docket or argument date supplied. The circuit **affirmed final summary judgment for Wolle, Vietor and Longstaff** after the district court converted their supported motion to dismiss. Thus **Duffy seeks reversal and further proceedings; the judges defend the favorable judgment**, including alternative immunity and remedial arguments. The supplied request had those positions reversed. This is not an interlocutory immunity appeal; ordinary review of the entire final summary-judgment record and the Rule 56(f) ruling is available within the granted questions.

The three questions concern the availability of an individual constitutional damages action despite internal judicial EEO remedies, absolute immunity for administrative hiring, and summary judgment/discovery on alleged sex discrimination. Duffy sought a declaration, appointment injunction, damages, fees and costs below. The existence of those prayers does not establish entitlement, an automatic appointment remedy, an action against the court as an agency, or identical immunity consequences for all relief.

### Record and lower reasoning

The three district judges selected a chief probation officer under 18 U.S.C. §3602. A screening committee considered sixteen applications and forwarded McPhillips, Duffy and Stites; local applicants were forwarded as a courtesy. Duffy and McPhillips were experienced supervising probation officers. McPhillips had two more years of probation experience, two larger-district assignments, Administrative Office/Sentencing Commission work, a bachelor's and master's degree and a law degree. Duffy had a bachelor's and master's degree, incomplete doctoral study, extensive local work and strengths in management, pretrial and supervision. The outgoing chief recommended Duffy; Minnesota judges and McPhillips's supervisor recommended her. Relative strengths, stated reasons and the inferences they support must not become an unreported finding of actual motive.

Wolle described an Administrative Office recommendation for national advertising and a diverse qualified pool. Ailts recalled a passing comment about interest in appointing a female, but could not identify the source and inferred the position involved. Duffy reported Ailts's statement about recruiting minorities and women. The vacancy announcement itself did not specify gender. The circuit assumed the reported Administrative Office interest could influence recruitment; it did not find an actual sex-specific recruitment measure. The panel unanimously selected McPhillips and each judge swore that sex played no role, citing experience, education, interview and references. Duffy disputed the explanations and invoked the female-majority law-clerk hiring history of two judges. No motive finding follows simply from either sworn denial or accusation.

The district court rejected absolute immunity and the EEO-remedy bar but granted summary judgment. The circuit likewise treated an individual Davis-type employment action as cognizable; it rejected absolute immunity under Forrester because selecting an employee is administrative despite importance. It found no congressional direction making the Judicial Conference's internal EEO scheme exclusive. It also said the equal-protection right was clearly established, then analyzed whether the evidence supported a violation rather than granting immunity for an unclear right.

Applying the McDonnell Douglas framework, it found a prima facie case, legitimate nondiscriminatory explanations, and insufficient evidence of pretext/intentional discrimination. It distinguished inclusive recruitment from discriminatory selection, evaluated actual qualifications, and upheld disregarding the law-clerk comparison. It held Rule 56(f) did not compel discovery merely in hopes of finding support. Duffy had filed a counsel affidavit specifically asking to depose defendants to test their explanations; the district court reasoned that affidavits from defendants and screening members made additional discovery unhelpful. Whether that was permissible is genuinely presented, not an omitted request. Rule 56(f)'s text, reproduced in the lower opinion, permits postponement, affidavits, depositions, discovery or another just order on a supported inability to present essential opposing facts; it does not mandate every requested deposition.

### Entering law, supported paths, and limits

*Davis v. Passman*, 442 U.S. 228 (1979), supplies the received individual federal employment-discrimination action and Fifth Amendment protection. *Bush*, 462 U.S. 367 (1983), addresses congressional remedial design; the actual source, coverage and completeness of an alternative scheme matter. Simulated *Meyer* (February 23, 1994, unanimous Thomas opinion) rejects a **direct agency** Bivens extension, expressly preserving received individual Davis/Bivens/Carlson actions at their own scope; it does not decide this claim by agency analogy. A model proposing that statutory employee coverage itself forecloses relief must establish that exact date-effective predicate rather than import later remedial doctrine.

*Forrester*, 484 U.S. 219 (1988), treats personnel administration as a different function from adjudication. Simulated *Antoine* (June 7, 1993, unanimous Stevens opinion) places historical/functional justification on the immunity claimant; importance alone is insufficient. Current *Bogan* (March 3, 1998, unanimous Thomas opinion) distinguishes prospective departmental legislation from individualized administrative personnel decisions. It creates no general employment immunity and does not make an appointment judicial merely because formalized in an order. Absolute immunity, qualified immunity, constitutional merits, personal participation and remedy remain separate.

*McDonnell Douglas*, *Burdine*, *Aikens* and simulated *Hicks* (June 25, 1993) supply the actual discrimination-proof background. Hicks's controlling Scalia proposition, joined by Stone, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, rejects both automatic liability from rejected explanations and a mandatory extra category of direct or independent evidence. Burdine's ultimate persuasion allocation remains. No universal equivalence of constitutional and Title VII claims follows; Title VII's exclusion is not silently undone. Simulated *Virginia* (June 26, 1996) requires a genuine important objective and substantial fit for a proven government sex classification, but a disputed discriminatory hiring decision first requires proving the challenged classification or motive. Inclusive recruitment is not automatically a discriminatory preference or a compelled constitutional program.

Most directly, *Crawford-El* (June 16, 1997, unanimous) rejects a heightened motive quantum and a categorical demand for the unavailable proof before necessary discovery. Its rule preserves specific nonconclusory support, early dispositive legal defenses, protective orders, tailoring and ordinary summary judgment. It orders neither unrestricted discovery nor a particular deposition; a general credibility attack is insufficient. The operative modules **Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity**, **Rejected employment explanations and circumstantial discrimination proof**, **No direct federal-agency Bivens damages action**, and **Ministerial court reporting and absolute judicial immunity** have the authorities and dates stated in the copied Standards. Current Clanton/Valot/Smith preserve actual proof and conduct-date notice; current Bogan supplies the directly relevant functional distinction. Any new DeCarlo holding must be refreshed before June 3 rather than importing its proposed treatment.

One supported path affirms final judgment because the supported nondiscriminatory qualifications/references and the actual limited contrary showing leave no genuine issue, and the Rule 56(f) request fails to identify how the requested inquiry could materially rebut it. This path must address the prima facie showing and distinct discovery request rather than accept defendants' denials as conclusive merely because judges made them. It cannot invent an additional direct-evidence burden or special judicial defendant exemption.

The competing path preserves the individual action and rejection of absolute immunity but vacates premature judgment for bounded discovery or ordinary reconsideration, because the reported statement about appointment, hiring pattern and comparative reasons require fair opportunity to test facts principally controlled by defendants. Its strongest objection is that no automatic discovery follows from pleading discrimination, the qualifications are substantially documented, recruiting a diverse pool is lawful, and the affidavit must satisfy the real Rule 56(f) requirements. A narrower remand may correct a legal evidence barrier without deciding that discrimination occurred or guaranteeing any requested deposition. An independent cause-of-action or immunity path must be supported under date-eligible law, not contemporary Bivens restrictions or a personal substitute framework.

No path supplies an appointment injunction, liability, backpay, damages, trial, open-ended inquiry into judges' work, or equal treatment of every defendant without actual grounds. The appointment took effect May 2, 1994; the alleged recruitment and selection conduct spans 1993–1994. Later decisions cannot retroactively furnish conduct-time notice, though Davis and Forrester predate it. A distinct merits failure and a finding that existing law was unclear must not be mislabeled as the same holding.
