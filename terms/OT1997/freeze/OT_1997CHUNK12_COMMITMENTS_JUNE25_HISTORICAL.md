## Bragdon v. Abbott

### Components, posture, and supported rules

The review concerns summary judgment for Abbott on federal disability coverage and direct threat. State-law claims are outside these questions. All eight Associates accept the certiorari channel and reach the federal questions. No threshold defect, recusal, forfeiture, or independent alternative statutory disability finding is established.

**B1 — Impairment.** HIV infection can constitute a physical impairment before its most serious symptoms appear. The statute is not limited to symptoms visible in everyday activity. That conclusion does not establish that every HIV-positive person has an actual disability under section 12102(2)(A), which additionally requires substantial limitation of a major life activity of that individual.

**B2 — Reproduction.** The proposed affirmative rule treats reproduction as a major life activity because the ability to conceive and bear a child is a central human activity; the statutory words do not confine major activities to tasks performed daily. This is separate from whether Abbott has a substantial limitation. The narrower alternative assumes reproduction qualifies and resolves only the adequacy of the individual showing.

**B3 — Individual limitation.** The affirmative position rests on Abbott's infection, the record's serious transmission risks, and her unchallenged statement that knowledge of HIV controlled her decision against having a child. It does not require proof that she intended to conceive before infection, and does not make childbearing desire an eligibility requirement. Her negative answer about impaired life functions must be understood alongside the specific childbearing testimony rather than erased. The opposing summary-judgment position treats that tension and the limited individualized account as insufficient to establish the statutory element conclusively; it does not hold that infection is harmless or that childbearing is physically impossible only when wholly prevented.

**B4 — Direct-threat law.** Section 12182(b)(3) concerns a significant risk to others that cannot be eliminated through modifications of policies, practices, or procedures, or auxiliary aids or services. Regulation 36.208 requires an individualized assessment using reasonable judgment founded on current medical knowledge or best available objective evidence: nature, duration and severity; probability of injury; and mitigation through reasonable modifications. The information date is the refusal date. A practitioner's sincere fear does not by itself satisfy the exception. Public-health expertise deserves assessment for its actual scientific foundation and fit; neither an institutional name nor the absence of a confirmed dental transmission decides significance. The offered hospital treatment does not itself prove privileges, equipment, availability, an adequate modification, or a duty to accept it. Section 12201(d) expressly preserves the individual's ability to decline an offered accommodation.

**B5 — Risk disposition.** Vacate the federal direct-threat summary-judgment determination and return for reconsideration under the objective, contemporaneous standard, including the actual foundation and relation of the sources used. The 1993 CDC recommendation to reduce transmission and the separate 1991 dental association statement of safe office treatment do different evidentiary work. The possible dental-worker cases are not confirmed transmissions; the 42 transmissions among other health-care workers are not findings of dental risk; the power-instrument research and expert acknowledgment of no positive spray-transmission evidence must be evaluated at their real scope. Later explanations do not establish what was reasonably knowable in September 1994. This remand finds neither a significant risk nor its absence, and does not direct a trial, new discovery, exclusion of a particular exhibit, or judgment for Bragdon. Ordinary evidentiary and summary-judgment rules govern what remains.

## Clinton v. City of New York

### Separate threshold and merits questions

The consolidated order concerns two different cancellations and different plaintiff groups. The direct-review route must exist independently of general district-court federal-question jurisdiction. Section 692(a)(1) specifies a Member of Congress or an adversely affected individual; subsection (b) authorizes direct appeal of an order in an action brought under that paragraph, with its own filing requirements. A three-judge court is not assumed. The Government's protective circuit appeal supplies no automatic Supreme Court certiorari grant. No timely-filing or service defect is established; no additional procedural compliance is invented.

**C1 — Statutory route.** The broad construction reads individual in this particular remedial scheme as an identifiable adversely affected claimant, including the entities whose legal and economic interests cancellation actually changes. Its basis is the statutory setting, which exposes both individuals and entities to the designated cancellations and supplies direct review of their constitutional challenges. The contrary construction reads individual as a natural person; statutory purpose cannot substitute the broader word person. That is an independent threshold objection, not a standing holding.

**C2 — New York Article III injury.** The loss of enacted protection against the identified recoupment/provider-assessment exposure is a present adverse change in the plaintiffs' legal and financial position. The HHS waiver applications remain undecided and the $2.6 billion estimate is not an assessment; nevertheless cancellation removes a legal protection from an existing asserted liability and restores its concrete risk. At least the hospital interests facing the described provider-assessment mechanism have a particular stake. Restoring section 4722(c) would restore the statutory protection on its actual conditions. It is unnecessary to find independent union injury or assume every plaintiff has standing.

**C3 — Snake River Article III injury.** The affirmative position treats elimination of the seller's incentive as removal of a concrete commercial bargaining opportunity for this cooperative, supported by actual identified acquisition discussions before cancellation, not merely a generalized desire to buy someday. A signed contract, final price, or proved inevitable acquisition is not required for injury to that opportunity. The contrary position treats the asserted loss as too dependent on unproved seller choices, price, qualification and transaction completion. The latter also rejects treating Cranney's co-op office or membership alone as proof of an independent personal injury. No conclusion supplies a contract, seller refusal or agreed price absent from the record.

**C4 — Presentment.** The invalidity position holds that, after signing the two measures into law, the President cannot use an ordinary statute to remove the legal force or effect of their provisions through a unilateral cancellation. Article I's enactment process governs the legal change. Earlier enactment of a general cancellation power and Congress's ability later to enact disapproval do not substitute for the required process concerning the provisions actually deprived of force. The valid-delegation position reads the later measures together with the previously enacted cancellation law as Congress's contingent legal choice, implemented through the President's specified fiscal determinations rather than a second act of legislation. The difference concerns legal operation, not the label veto.

**C5 — Nondelegation.** This is separate from C4. The Justices supporting C4 invalidity do not need an independent holding that every discretion of the stated breadth violates nondelegation. Those sustaining the cancellations must also sustain the actual assignment: the enumerated fiscal categories, whole-item requirement, five-day window excluding Sundays, required deficit/function/national-interest determinations, required consideration of statutory purposes and information, special-message disclosures, and enacted-disapproval control constrain the task. Loving's genuine military execution ground does not automatically validate this different fiscal assignment. Conversely, its limits do not declare every nonmilitary post-enactment discretion unlawful.

## Eastern Enterprises v. Apfel

### Questions and operative alternatives

The review concerns the constitutional retroactive tier-three liability assessed under section 9706(a)(3), not a renewed statutory assignment claim, other assignment tiers, unassigned beneficiaries, or the separate 1992 plan. Eastern employed these retirees before 1966 and participated in the early benefit undertaking. It also retained subsidiary ownership until 1987 and received substantial dividends; direct withdrawal in 1965 is not equated with severance of every economic relationship. Conversely, those later relationships are not proof of a lifetime promise or an established indemnity construction.

**E1 — Channel and remedy.** The due-process challenge to an enforced federal payment obligation is justiciable in the district court, with actual assessment supplying injury. Its existence does not depend on the Government's abandonment of a jurisdictional objection. The Tucker Act compensation question must be addressed independently for a takings ground. The three Justices choosing takings invalidity would permit declaratory/injunctive relief against this application because the challenged statutory scheme imposes ongoing payments to the privately operated fund and supplies no compensatory feature that would preserve its intended allocation through a Treasury award; their conclusion is about this statutory enforcement setting, not a general permission to enjoin a compensable appropriation. The remaining five do not use compensation jurisdiction to avoid the due-process merits or impose a claim for damages never sought. They reject the asserted takings ground at the antecedent property/regulatory distinction and do not decide an entitlement to Treasury compensation.

**E2 — Takings predicate and application.** The invalidity path treats the substantially retrospective extraction from Eastern's assets for this bounded class as an extraordinary regulatory burden examined under the economic-impact, reasonable-expectations and character factors applied in Concrete Pipe and Connolly. Large projected payments alone are not enough. The long retrospective reach, relationship between the pre-1966 undertaking and much later benefit guarantees, and concentrated assignment of their expense matter together. The competing path holds that an obligation to pay money from unspecified general assets does not identify an appropriated fund, parcel, earned interest, or other particular property interest; the objection belongs principally in due process. Phillips's ownership rule for actual interest does not make every payment obligation an appropriation. Concrete Pipe's rejection of a taking through a contextual analysis does not compel recognition of a taking for every general liability; at minimum its analysis supplies an independently sufficient rejection where applicable, not a categorical victory for either party here.

**E3 — Retroactive due process.** Carlton requires a legitimate purpose rationally served by the retrospective operation itself; reliance does not automatically defeat legislation, nor does revenue collection automatically validate it. Concrete Pipe sustains proportionate pooled-benefit allocation in its actual participation setting. The validity path finds a rational allocation to the employers whose workers earned benefits within the shared undertaking, with actual former employment and participation rather than assignment of strangers. A private contractual duration limit cannot alone constitutionalize immunity from later adjustment. The invalidity path finds the particular remote and severe imposition insufficiently connected to what this pre-1966 employer undertook or could reasonably have anticipated; employment and participation cannot by themselves justify assigning later lifetime obligations without limit. Neither path depends on a proved binding lifetime promise, an exact damages total, a fixed maximum retrospective period, or an invented fact about retained corporate control.

## National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley

### Reachability, text, and alternative paths

All eight Associates accept review of the surviving facial First/Fifth Amendment challenge. NAAO's representative controversy concerning member organizations remains live on the supplied record; subsequent grants to two artists and restrictions on some individual grants do not establish that every affected member organization is ineligible or that the entire dispute disappeared. This conclusion neither adjudicates all 1998 appropriations exceptions nor gives settled individual claims new life. No prior grant denial is attributed to a statutory provision enacted afterward.

Section 954(d)(1) requires the Chairperson's regulations and procedures to use artistic excellence and merit as criteria, taking account of general decency and respect for diverse public beliefs and values. Taking account is mandatory; the statute specifies neither an automatic disqualification nor a fixed weight. It cannot be read as requiring no consideration at all. Panel diversity and an agency resolution are not a binding construction eliminating the statutory duty. Section 954(d)(2)'s obscenity prohibition is separate and is not substituted for the contested clause.

**F1 — Narrow facial-validity ground.** In this competitively selective arts program, the statutory considerations can operate as contextual aspects of artistic evaluation without establishing a categorical viewpoint exclusion, a penalty for expression outside the program, or an automatic denial whenever an official finds offense. Scarcity and artistic selection permit judgments about content and suitability within genuine program purposes, but are not a universal immunity from viewpoint constraints. On that construction the facial challenge does not establish invalidity; an actual discriminatory application remains separately reviewable. The construction preserves meaningful consideration while refusing to add a mandatory exclusion absent from the text.

**F2 — Vagueness.** Indeterminate artistic judgment is inherent in this selective grant scheme. The considerations, read with artistic excellence/merit and the program's actual selection function, do not impose a criminal prohibition, licensing requirement, or objective entitlement to a grant that demands the specificity of a coercive speech rule. This limited setting defeats facial vagueness invalidation. It does not make vague grant standards universally valid or excuse a discriminatory use, and does not declare all artists' speech unaffected in practice.

**F3 — Broader subsidy ground.** Scalia and Thomas would sustain these discretionary appropriations as the Government's selection of what art to subsidize, including its own judgment about decency and respect, without treating every refusal as unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination. An applicant remains free to produce the work without this subsidy. Their position distinguishes Rosenberger's generally available private-publication benefit and Forbes's forum for competing candidates, and does not authorize conditions controlling independently financed speech, punishing dissent, or transferring all forum law into government subsidy selection. Their broader ground need not join F1's full reservation of viewpoint-neutral selection as an across-the-program rule.

**F4 — Facial-invalidity ground.** Souter would affirm the injunction against the challenged decency/respect criterion because it directs public decisionmakers to judge private expression against public beliefs and values and therefore makes offensive viewpoints an adverse consideration. A requirement to take that assessment into account cannot be saved by calling it only advisory, and panel diversity supplies no limiting rule. A public program supporting varied private arts does not become the Government's own message. Its selective nature permits genuine artistic judgment, not a mandatory direction to assess conformity to public sentiment. His disposition rests on the First Amendment ground; no separate Fifth Amendment vagueness holding is necessary to affirm the actual relief.

## United States v. Balsys

### Independent legal foundation and scope

All eight Associates reach the foreign-prosecution privilege question in the subpoena-enforcement proceeding. Balsys is a resident alien questioned in the United States. His status does not exclude him from the Fifth Amendment's protection. Deportation is civil, but a civil or administrative inquiry may seek answers incriminating in a criminal case; its label therefore does not independently decide the privilege. No domestic criminal exposure is asserted. The real and substantial risk of prosecution in Lithuania and Israel is established below and uncontested; Germany is not silently added. The Government's limitations representation is not a new judicial calculation of every domestic limitations period.

**Balsys A — Sovereigns bound by the privilege.** The privilege permits resistance to compelled testimony threatening incrimination in a criminal prosecution governed by the constitutional protection. The ordinary threat of prosecution by an independent foreign sovereign supplies no such privilege where no domestic criminal exposure is asserted. Murphy's state/federal holding prevents whipsawing among sovereigns bound to respect the same constitutional rule; Kastigar demonstrates how use and derivative-use protection keeps the witness and prosecuting authorities in the position silence would have preserved. Those decisions do not establish that a foreign sovereign must honor American immunity or that domestic courts can impose it. Zicarelli reserved rather than decided the real-risk foreign question. Verdugo-Urquidez's trial-right distinction assists the analysis without denying a domestic resident's constitutional status or treating a different Fourth Amendment holding as controlling this case by itself.

**Balsys B — Domestic compulsion against foreign penal exposure.** The contrary construction locates the protected act of resistance in domestic compulsion of testimonial self-incrimination. The constitutional text does not expressly confine a criminal case to a domestic one. Murphy's history, rejection of the old separate-sovereign approach, and account of the privilege's personal protections support applying it when a domestic witness faces a proved real and substantial foreign criminal danger. Kastigar's coextensiveness principle then prevents an ineffective domestic immunity promise from replacing the right to remain silent. The conclusion regulates the American command, not the foreign court. Zicarelli's reservation leaves this issue open; Verdugo-Urquidez concerns a foreign search of a nonresident's premises, unlike domestic compulsion of a resident.

**Balsys C — Cooperation limit.** OSI's liaison mission, the Lithuania memorandum and earlier sharing with Israel do not establish foreign direction of this subpoena, a joint prosecution, or use of domestic process as a proved foreign proxy. The A coalition leaves a materially different case of such concerted use open rather than creating and applying an undefined cooperation exception here. The B coalition does not need to prove such a relationship because ordinary real and substantial foreign exposure suffices on its view. No existing Israeli disclosure treaty, foreign immunity, secrecy assurance, or factual finding of coordinated prosecution is invented.
