## A. Case, record, and posture

- **Case:** *SEC v. First Jersey Securities, Inc.*, 101 F.3d 1450 (2d Cir. 1996).
- **Supreme Court petition:** No. 96-1862, captioned *First Jersey Securities, Inc. v. SEC*. **Term:** October Term 1997. **Simulation:** petition proposed for grant, merits date unassigned.
- **Question presented, reconstructed:** Whether the record supports civil federal securities fraud, controlling-person liability, substantial disgorgement plus prejudgment interest and a permanent injunction, and whether a district court may appoint its own agent to investigate uncharged violations after adjudication.
- **Judgment below:** Second Circuit **affirmed fraud liability, disgorgement, interest, and injunction**, but **reversed the district court's appointment of an investigatory Special Agent**.
- **Record:** First Jersey Securities, a broker-dealer, and sole owner/chief executive Robert Brennan challenged findings of a coordinated practice involving manipulative pricing and excessive markups in six over-the-counter securities. The trial court ordered approximately **$22.29 million disgorgement** after credit for a $5 million class settlement, **$52.69 million prejudgment interest**, prospective securities-law injunctions, and a court-appointed agent to seek additional past misconduct outside the pleadings and trial. The Second Circuit found the last component beyond a judicially ancillary appointment.
- **Relief sought:** Reversal or reduction of liability and equitable relief; defendants sought removal of the investigative appointment.
- **Threshold:** Civil SEC enforcement jurisdiction and equitable remedial authority; res judicata concerning prior proceedings; fact findings after a bench trial; separability of a court's adjudication of pleaded charges from executive investigation of uncharged wrongdoing.
- **Dates:** Second Circuit decided December 10, 1996.

## B. Law entering the case

The Securities Act, Exchange Act, SEC rules, and federal equitable powers supply the governing law. Any monetary or preventive order must follow the statute and proof of the actual violations. The Court has not conferred a general investigative commission on a district judge merely by permitting equitable administration of an SEC judgment.

### Issue 1: Securities fraud and controlling persons

- **Governing law:** Securities Act of 1933 § 17(a), 15 U.S.C. § 77q(a); Securities Exchange Act of 1934 § 10(b), 15 U.S.C. § 78j(b); SEC Rule 10b-5; 1934 Act § 20(a), 15 U.S.C. § 78t(a), on controlling persons and the good-faith defense. Fraud proof and private-res-judicata theories retain their statutory and procedural elements.
- **Open point:** Which acts and proceeds are attributable to Brennan individually versus to First Jersey under the enacted controlling-person rule and proven conduct.

### Issue 2: Equitable recovery and injunction

- **Governing law:** SEC civil enforcement authority, equity's restitutionary disgorgement principles recognized in prevailing decisions, judgment interest, and a demonstrated risk of repeat wrongdoing for preventive injunctions.
- **Open point:** How to avoid a punitive windfall or multiple recovery when high interest accrues over lengthy litigation.

### Issue 3: Judicial investigative appointments

- **Governing law:** 15 U.S.C. §§ 78u(a), 78d(b), allocate securities investigations to SEC; courts may appoint masters or other officers for adjudicative or compliance functions under appropriate law, including Fed. R. Civ. P. 53.
- **Open point:** Whether a new search for uncharged historical fraud can be called incidental relief in a lawsuit whose pleaded claims have already been decided.

## C. Decision-material litigation positions

- **Below:** Coordinated wrongdoing and Brennan's direct involvement supported primary and controlling-person liability; disgorgement corresponded to demonstrated illegal gains after class settlement credit. The court accepted the IRS underpayment interest measure and an injunction supported by systematic conduct. It rejected a free-standing district-court prosecutor/investigator.
- **Petitioners:** Prior proceedings arguably precluded some litigation, markups or remedies were contested, very large interest resembled punishment, and sole ownership should not automatically mean personal liability for all firm gains.
- **SEC:** The same fraud and participation were established at trial; the judgment must remove benefits from unlawful transactions, and joint control supports enacted liability. SEC authority to investigate more wrongdoing is distinct from authority for a court-appointed investigator.
- **Limit:** Wrongdoing permits only relief sourced to the particular violations and does not make the defendant's wealth a legal wrong in itself.

## D. Lawful paths and uncertainty

1. Affirm the Second Circuit's split disposition, preserving specific restitution and rejecting investigatory expansion.
2. Vacate interest or joint liability only if legal basis, calculation, actual benefit, or the statute's explicit limits fail; such errors are not established merely by a high dollar ratio.
3. Remand a contested numerical component only on a proven calculation or legal error, not for speculative new violations.
