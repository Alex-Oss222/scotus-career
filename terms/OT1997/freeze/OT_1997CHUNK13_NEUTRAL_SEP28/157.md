## A. Case, record, and posture

- **Case:** *Newton County Wildlife Association v. Rogers*, 113 F.3d 110 (8th Cir. 1997); district-court and appellate case also captioned against United States Forest Service.
- **Supreme Court petition:** No. 97-774. **Term:** October Term 1997. **Simulation:** petition proposed for grant; merits date unspecified.
- **Question presented:** (1) Whether the Wild and Scenic Rivers Act bars timber sales until comprehensive management plans for nearby protected segments are prepared and coordinated; (2) whether the Migratory Bird Treaty Act requires the Forest Service to obtain a permit for logging allegedly taking or killing protected birds. These track the official petition-stage questions summarized by the Solicitor General.
- **Judgment below:** Eighth Circuit affirmed **denial of preliminary injunctions** against four Ozark National Forest timber sales. The merits proceedings on other statutory claims were not exhausted in this appellate disposition.
- **Record:** Congress designated six Ozark river segments in 1992. Forest Service had a three-fiscal-year obligation to create management plans, due September 30, 1995, and did not meet it. Timber sales were approved between 1994 and 1995 on lands outside the designated river corridors. The agency had amended forest planning and prepared environmental assessments for individual sales. Wildlife groups alleged plan delay and likely logging-related bird deaths. Forest industry parties intervened.
- **Relief sought:** Preliminary orders halting timber sales pending plan completion and bird permits.
- **Threshold:** Interlocutory review of preliminary relief, APA final agency action, available statutory enforcement and sovereign channels, judicial-review limits concerning FWS nonenforcement, factual predictions of sale impacts.
- **Dates:** Eighth Circuit decided May 6, 1997; rehearing denied August 6, 1997.

## B. Law entering the case

The Wild and Scenic Rivers Act, Migratory Bird Treaty Act, National Forest Management Act, National Environmental Policy Act, and Administrative Procedure Act supply the enacted framework for the 1994–1997 approvals. In *Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society* (1992), this Court applied an express congressional substitution of timber-sale compliance rules for particular covered Northwest sales; that holding did not generally relieve Forest Service operations of the bird, environmental or forest-planning statutes applicable elsewhere.

### Issue 1: River-plan obligation and timber-sale authority

- **Governing law:** Wild and Scenic Rivers Act, 16 U.S.C. §§ 1271–1274(d). Designated river boundaries, comprehensive plan deadline, and coordination with adjacent federal resource planning are distinct statutory obligations. National Forest Management Act and National Environmental Policy Act supply other forest-planning and environmental review duties without automatically converting the WSRA plan deadline into an approval bar. *Robertson* enforces a specific textual congressional amendment where Congress actually enacted one; it supplies no implied blanket exemption for these Arkansas timber sales.
- **Open point:** Whether the omitted plans were a legal precondition to these sales, especially on land outside designated boundaries, or were a separately enforceable plan obligation.

### Issue 2: Bird-take prohibition and federal agencies

- **Governing law:** Migratory Bird Treaty Act, 16 U.S.C. §§ 703, 704, 707; statutory penalty language naming persons, associations, partnerships and corporations; presumption that general “person” wording does not include the sovereign absent contrary statutory indication. The APA's review provisions and recognized enforcement discretion shape any remedy.
- **Open point:** Whether the MBTA's listed activities extend to indirect incidental effects of otherwise lawful timber harvesting and, separately, whether Forest Service itself is a statutory violator obligated to secure an FWS permit.

## C. Decision-material litigation positions

- **Below:** Section 1274(d) did not make completion of corridor plans a precondition to approving outside-corridor timber sales. The Eighth Circuit construed MBTA take or kill as directed toward hunter- and poacher-like conduct rather than indirect logging effects, and reasoned that federal agencies were not among the listed criminally liable persons. It declined to review FWS's discretionary permit-enforcement stance.
- **Petitioners:** Congress's mandatory river-planning date must have practical force, and logging adjacent to protected rivers can defeat preservation aims. The MBTA's expansive prohibition on killing migratory birds protects wildlife irrespective of landowner, including harms from federal logging.
- **Respondents and federal government:** Mandatory planning is not statutory sequencing language invalidating already approved projects; these four sales are outside designated components; general MBTA penalty words do not subject the sovereign to criminal liability; FWS nonenforcement generally is not a court-supervised permitting case.
- **Limits:** A statutory deadline remains binding despite the absence of requested injunctive remedy. Laws independently protecting habitats or wildlife remain enforceable by their own terms.

## D. Lawful paths and uncertainty

1. Affirm denial of preliminary injunction where neither statute supplies the asserted immediate sale prohibition.
2. Remand if statutory text compels particular coordinated planning for a site and the record reveals the sales intrude into a designated component or evade a separately applicable duty.
3. Decline to resolve unresolved final merits claims through an interlocutory injunction appeal. Future agency obligations may be enforceable through a different reviewable action and remedy.

## F. Certiorari addendum

- **Petition:** Appeal from denial of preliminary relief, not a final merits judgment settling all claims. Official government brief identifies two specific questions above.
- **Split:** Ninth Circuit decisions on incidental timber-harvesting bird impacts provided persuasive parallel authority; the record does not prove a square live split warranting a broader rule.
- **Vehicle/finality:** Important disadvantage: concurrent challenges to final agency actions were still proceeding, as the federal respondents stressed. Statutory injunction and procedural review questions can be separated from the ultimate logging merits.
- **Objective importance:** Administration of protected rivers and migratory birds on federal land, and whether congressionally mandated planning dates operate as bars to other agency activity.
- **Solicitor General:** Seth Waxman, for the federal respondents, opposed certiorari on interlocutory and substantive grounds.
- **Term treatment:** Confine any hypothetical grant to the preliminary injunction orders and the two reviewable statutory questions; do not assume later case developments.
