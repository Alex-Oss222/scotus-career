## 159. McKenzie — corrected factual and statutory limits

McKenzie alleges personal participation in dispatcher reporting practices beginning long before suit. Public reports and the Falsetti/Dorris allegations overlapped, and the appellate court observed nearly identical complaint passages; those observations do not decide whether each material factual allegation was independently known. The published opinion reports compliance with the sealed submission procedure and governmental declination. It does not independently establish every original-source fact or convert sealed filing itself into the separate required voluntary provision before filing.

The statute then in force requires direct and independent knowledge plus voluntary disclosure to the Government before filing. It does not say before public disclosure or require that the relator supply the newspaper's source. The §3730(b)(5) related-pending-action bar and §3730(e)(3) government-party bar remain distinct and are not adjudged satisfied from similarity alone.

Retaliation is independently pleaded. Her alleged internal complaints, presentation of a newspaper account of a similar federal qui tam case, harassment and threats permit further proceedings under the then-existing pleading standard. Internal dissatisfaction alone does not necessarily qualify, and no successful qui tam recovery is an element of the retaliation protection. Protected activity, employer knowledge, causation and actual relief remain distinct. No fraud, retaliation, disability-causation or damages finding is made.

## A. Case, record, and posture

- **Case:** *United States ex rel. McKenzie v. BellSouth Telecommunications, Inc.*, 123 F.3d 935 (6th Cir. 1997).
- **Supreme Court petition:** No. 97-850. **Term:** October Term 1997. **Simulation:** petition proposed for grant; merits date unassigned.
- **Question presented, reconstructed:** Whether an employee with personal knowledge of alleged fraud loses False Claims Act qui tam jurisdiction solely because allegations appeared in prior public reports, and what allegations suffice to state the FCA's separate anti-retaliation claim.
- **Judgment below:** Sixth Circuit **affirmed dismissal of the qui tam claim** under the statutory public-disclosure/original-source rule but **reversed dismissal of McKenzie's own retaliation claim**, remanding for proceedings on that claim.
- **Record:** Mary McKenzie worked for South Central Bell as a dispatcher. She alleged the company falsified telephone outage and repair reports to avoid contractual refunds to customers including federal facilities. She said she objected internally for years and suffered harassment and threatening responses. Other employees' allegations and litigation concerning comparable repair-report falsification had appeared publicly before her suit. She pursued a sealed qui tam filing and her own employee-retaliation claim. The government did not intervene.
- **Relief:** Reinstatement of the government's qui tam enforcement action through the relator and pursuit of personal statutory retaliation remedies, subject to proof.
- **Threshold:** 1986-version 31 U.S.C. § 3730(e)(4) public-disclosure bar and original-source conditions; separate coverage and allegations under § 3730(h); motion-to-dismiss posture requiring care not to substitute untried facts.
- **Date:** Sixth Circuit decided August 26, 1997.

## B. Law entering the case

The claim is governed by the False Claims Act as amended in 1986, including the public-disclosure, original-source and anti-retaliation provisions actually in force for this litigation. Later amendments to § 3730(e)(4) have no role in the statutory interpretation here.

### Issue 1: Public disclosure and original source

- **Governing law:** 31 U.S.C. §§ 3729, 3730(e)(4)(A)–(B), as then enacted. A publicly disclosed allegation may trigger a qui tam bar unless the relator has direct and independent knowledge and voluntarily provided the information to the government before filing. Circuits disagreed about whether “based upon” means substantially similar to or actually derived from public information and whether original-source status includes extra chronology requirements.
- **Open point:** Whether McKenzie's actual personal knowledge and timing of voluntary communication satisfy the statutory words, apart from resemblance to other suits.

### Issue 2: Anti-retaliation

- **Governing law:** Then-current 31 U.S.C. § 3730(h), protecting covered lawful acts in furtherance of FCA proceedings; pleading and notice of protected activity and causation are distinct from government qui tam jurisdiction.
- **Open point:** Whether internal objections and communication of a related newspaper article reasonably alerted the employer to protected FCA activity, to be determined after discovery.

## C. Decision-material litigation positions

- **Below:** The Sixth Circuit construed “based upon” broadly, imposed a prior-to-public-disclosure government-notice prerequisite on original sources, rejected jurisdiction over the qui tam claim, but recognized sufficient allegations to permit the retaliation claim to proceed. A panel dissent favored affirming both dismissals.
- **Petitioner:** Information from her employment may be direct and independent regardless of a third party's later publicity; Congress expressly wrote voluntary disclosure **before filing**, not before news publication. The statute also protects good-faith activity tending toward an FCA action.
- **Respondent:** Substantially duplicated accusations would make bounty litigation parasitic; no first public disclosure by McKenzie; ordinary job-related complaints may not communicate pending federal whistleblower litigation.
- **Limit:** Even a relator whose allegations survive § 3730(e)(4) must prove the actual false claim, obligation to the United States, scienter, and loss under § 3729. Retaliation does not establish substantive fraud.

## D. Lawful paths and uncertainty

1. Affirm qui tam dismissal if the statute reaches substantially identical allegations and required voluntary advance government submission is missing.
2. Vacate qui tam dismissal under a more text-specific “based upon” and original-source reading, remand for first-hand knowledge and filing-sequence facts not resolved below.
3. Preserve reversal of retaliation dismissal at the pleading stage without adjudicating causation or damages.
