# Newton County — neutral review-scope and posture addendum

**Scope:** Neutral preflight only, matter 157, September 28, 1998. No commitment, private position, comparator, assignment or Court disposition was read or created. This addendum supplements `157.md`; it does not expand the authorized questions or revise any completed adjudication.

## Bounded conclusion

The authorized review already includes the separately adjudicated **Forest Service MBTA permit-obligation question**. The first judgment is not solely an appeal from preliminary relief: 113 F.3d 110, 114 and 116 expressly identifies the August 5, 1996 partial-summary-judgment dismissal, says plaintiffs appealed that order as well as the injunction denial, and affirms both. The January 1998 government opposition confirms this in footnote 5 and Argument 1. Its Question Presented 2 asks whether the Forest Service violated the MBTA by undertaking the sales without a Fish and Wildlife Service permit. Treating that existing merits component as within the scheduled review therefore requires **no new claim, expanded grant or user choice**.

The October 1996 completion of river plans ends the particular request to stop the sales until those plans exist. The subsequent final district-court judgment and April 1, 1998 affirmance also displace a request merely to preserve the position pending trial. They do **not** establish that the independently entered MBTA dismissal was reversed, withdrawn, satisfied or rendered unreviewable, and do not by themselves moot all statutory claims. The final appeal decided other specified statutory and record-review issues; it did not freshly decide MBTA.

## Lawful channel and the remaining legal distinction

Section 1254(1) authorizes certiorari in a court-of-appeals case before or after rendition of judgment. The January 1998 government source reports a November 4, 1997 petition invoking that section to review the May 6 judgment after August 6 rehearing denial. No historical Supreme Court action is imported. The supplied scheduled review and Question 2 concern the MBTA component actually adjudicated in that appellate judgment. Federal certiorari jurisdiction is not subject to a universal final-judgment prerequisite equivalent to §1257's state-court limitation.

Ordinary final-judgment merger explains why an earlier partial dismissal is no longer merely an unresolved interlocutory district-court ruling. It does **not** automatically validate an earlier excess of court-of-appeals jurisdiction, create an appeal or petition that was never taken, reopen a completed Supreme Court proceeding, or add every issue decided in the later final appeal. The proper description remains review of the MBTA dismissal already affirmed in the 1997 judgment, with the later final judgment recognized as intervening posture. No replacement petition directed to all of 141 F.3d 803 is invented or required for that description.

The actual authority for the Eighth Circuit's connected review remains a legal question within this authorized case, rather than an unresolved scope authorization:

* **Potential merits route:** The Court may determine that review of the same MBTA claim's dismissal was lawfully connected to the injunction appeal because the same dispositive legal ground was inextricable or necessary to meaningful review. Here the identity of claim and requested statutory obligation gives a materially stronger connection than the separate party's independent liability question in Swint. The later final posture removes an ongoing need to defer merely because other claims await trial. This route must state its actual legal basis; the circuit's “inextricably intertwined” label is not conclusive.
* **Potential threshold route:** If the Court concludes that the asserted connected jurisdiction exceeded the permitted immediate appeal, it may correct that jurisdictional defect and determine the appropriate bounded further proceedings. The later final judgment cannot by itself cure the old excess. An independently proper final-review route, if needed to reach merits despite that conclusion, must be established from the actual appellate record rather than presumed from merger. The current sources do not establish a separate later petition encompassing MBTA, a new appeal expressly reasserting it, or a particular final mandate incorporating a renewed MBTA determination. This is the precise additional predicate required **only if** that alternative route becomes necessary; it is not a reason to characterize the already authorized review as PI-only.

Swint preserves the necessary/inextricable-review question beyond its actual separate-party setting. Clinton v. Jones resolves it for the same integrated temporal-immunity order and excludes independent liability issues; it does not announce a general connected-jurisdiction rule for every injunction case. Those limits must travel with any analogy. University of Texas v. Camenisch, 451 U.S. 390 (1981), distinguishes an expired preliminary issue from a continuing underlying controversy and forbids converting a likelihood-of-success assessment into a final merits judgment. Newton differs because there is an **actual separately entered summary-judgment dismissal**, not merely preliminary reasoning mislabeled as merits.

## What remains within, and outside, the reviewed question

Within Question 2 are the Forest Service's own asserted permit obligation, the actual dismissal of that theory, and the jurisdictional, statutory-channel and remedial predicates necessary to resolve it. The absence of an MBTA private action is distinct from APA review of a completed agency authorization allegedly contrary to another applicable law. Bennett preserves those separate conditions. Ohio Forestry's programmatic-plan ripeness ruling does not automatically bar review of these actual site-specific sale authorizations; neither does it dispense with injury, finality, applicable law or redressability.

The later appellate judgment's NFMA, NEPA, Clean Water Act, Wilderness Act, ESA, separate WSRA site-effects and administrative-record rulings are not added to the grant. Completion of the plans does not establish that every coordination or protection duty was satisfied; conversely, no unpleaded continuing coordination violation is manufactured to save the original missed-deadline request. Contractor-permit and contractor-approval theories retain their distinct preservation questions; the government's assertion that a theory was not raised below is a contention, not an agreed waiver. No new FWS nonenforcement suit, private criminal enforcement or independent treaty claim is supplied.

The last supported progress account is counsel's statement at December 10, 1997 argument that approximately three-fourths of road work and harvesting was complete. It proves neither September 1998 completion nor the exact remaining work on that date. A Court disposition requiring a precise amount of ongoing work or concluding that no effective prospective relief remains needs that particular fact. Missing completion research does not prove completion, mootness, default or entitlement to an injunction. The case-local legal review can retain its ordinary remand/remedy limits without pretending to find a harvest status not established by the sources.
