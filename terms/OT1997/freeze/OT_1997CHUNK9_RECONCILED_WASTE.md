## Supported grounds and join limits

**D — Stevens and Kennedy:** Although Metro owns the plant, its sovereign rules reserve independent generators' and haulers' purchases to a local processing service or burden choosing competitors. SSC's protection of actual purchased performance does not cover all transactions reached by this regulation. Camps and Tracy require a supported difference in the offering; on this record, ownership and the stated environmental/fuel functions do not establish one adequate to remove the private transactions from discrimination analysis. This resolves the public-system question expressly reserved by Carbone; it does not pretend Carbone already decided publicly owned systems.

Once discrimination is found, Metro must establish a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve. Competitive prices, increased municipal collection or contracts, and the reported generator/container tax supply alternatives on this record. They are not assumed free, immediate, equally profitable or judicially prescribed. The fee independently lacks an identified equivalent intrastate burden and therefore fails Oregon Waste/Fulton's cumulative compensation conditions. D affirms both reviewed injunction rulings while preserving independent valid licensing, lawful purchases and unreviewed claims.

**C — O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer:** Routing locally generated waste to a genuinely public facility does not facially discriminate against interstate commerce when local private processors and out-of-state processors are treated alike. Whether the facility is genuinely public turns on its actual ownership, control, financing, public-service responsibility and revenues, and the absence of concealed private favoritism; public title alone does not settle the question. A diversion fee survives only insofar as its actual incidence and amount are genuinely tied to diversion-related costs rather than interstate origin or protection of local commerce. A permissible public routing distinction does not establish that connection, and a compensatory-tax defense separately requires an identified actual intrastate burden, rough approximation without excess, and substantially equivalent taxable events reasonably treated as mutually exclusive proxies.

Vacate the two reviewed determinations and remand for that comparison, separately for routing and fee incidence. If a material public-service difference makes the particular measure genuinely evenhanded, Pike asks whether the supported interstate burden is clearly excessive relative to local benefits. If the distinction fails and discrimination is established, the legitimate-purpose/adequate-nondiscriminatory-alternatives rule governs. The remand supplies neither a presumed public-service predicate nor an automatic new evidentiary hearing.

The fee does not become valid merely because routing may be distinguishable. Its existing compensatory defense fails without the identified actual burden, rough nonexcessive match and substantially equivalent taxable events. The remand does not pronounce that defense successful, reopen its defect by invented evidence, or require validating the charge. A different supported classification must fit the enacted incidence and record independently.

**R — Scalia and Thomas:** Their sufficient proposed ground is that the affirmative commerce grant does not authorize this judicial negative-Commerce restriction in the absence of governing congressional legislation. They expressly propose displacing the contrary doctrine rather than treating it as already overruled. Their actual Camps writing supplies issue-specific textual/structural objections to exclusivity, congressional silence and judicial policy balancing. Their earlier Carbone, Oregon Waste, Fulton and SSC joins remain actual precedent history; the later published objection warrants the proposed present vote, not a fictional automatic change to governing law.

Their result terminates these reviewed Commerce grounds and removes injunction relief dependent on them. Ordinary mandate proceedings concerning the already unreviewed takings/ripeness claim or independently preserved matters may continue. They do **not** join a mandate requiring renewed actual-service classification, discrimination justification or Pike adjudication. Their comparative observations under existing precedent do not create that assent. Ginsburg's narrower Camps comparison position must not be attributed to their repudiation.

Thomas's tentative Camps Import-Export alternative supplies no independent disposition. A value-based real-property tax there differs from this routing command and per-ton disposal charge. No import/export status, preserved party presentation or definitive Clause rule is supplied here.

## Operative record and remedial qualifications

The Sixth Circuit reviewed injunction dispositions under §1292(a)(1); Supreme Court review is under §1254(1). The passenger-car/pickup takings and ripeness remand is outside the grant. This is neither a single §1291 final-judgment description nor a state-finality problem.

Metro owns the facility and NTTC operates it. No operator ownership, nationality, profit distribution, contractual risk transfer or purchase by Metro of all private transactions is found. Residential routing retains the Director's specific written-authorization exception. Nonresidential routing operates only through allocations needed for the reported 6,300-ton weekly requirement: monthly reports, at-least-quarterly pro rata calculations on the best available data and advance facility/collector notices. It is not an unconditional all-waste command.

The separate charge is $2 per cubic yard or $8 per ton on the specified Metro-generated/collected waste disposed at non-Metro facilities within or outside Metro; NTTC and other Metro-owned sites are exempt. The narrative's $36 tipping fee differs from the footnote's enacted $44 rate effective January 1, 1995. Neither rate supports an invented numerical burden finding. Exclusion of local competitors does not itself cure or establish discrimination. The 25-percent State reduction requirement, dependable disposal and fuel needs are real objectives; missing compensation proof does not establish that Metro incurs no costs.
