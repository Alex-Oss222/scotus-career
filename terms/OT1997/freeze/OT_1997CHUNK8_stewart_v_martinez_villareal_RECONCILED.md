# Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal — historical reconciliation

**Event and cutoff:** May 18, 1998; No. 97-300; argued February 25, 1998.

**Result:** Retain the frozen substantive division. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the narrow classification of this previously presented, premature Ford claim as outside the second-or-successive mechanism. Scalia and Thomas reverse that exemption under the application-based, repeated-claim rule. All eight retain ordinary review of the circuit's distinct threshold classification. No historical departure or unresolved source blocker.

## Channel and substantive comparison

The circuit determined authorization was unnecessary and transferred the proceeding back. It did not apply the substantive prima-facie permission criteria to grant or deny authorization. Felker, Triestman and Dorsainvil govern the actual authorization channel; Calderon's current channel holding distinguishes ordinary review of a different first-application action. That distinction supports all eight frozen channel commitments without making the threshold classification correct merely because the circuit used a label. A true authorization refusal remains unreviewable by the ordinary route. The Court can review and decide whether this action belongs in that mechanism.

For the six affirming Associates, the March 1993 claim was actually presented and dismissed as premature at the State's request; the 1996 mandate preserved its later consideration; it first became adjudicable after the execution warrant and state process. The later filing seeks its first federal merits determination. Treating that return as a continuation is a construction of second or successive as a procedural term, not an equitable power to disregard a conceded statutory bar. The exhaustion-dismissal analogy supports the construction, while the actual prior presentation and prematurity delimit it. Neither Lonchar nor Lindh independently resolves every successive threshold, and Calderon's restriction on reopening completed merits does not turn this unadjudicated Ford claim into a completed merits determination.

The complete historical Court opinion expressly reserved a Ford claim first presented only after the initial federal application was rejected. Its text therefore confirms, rather than enlarges, the frozen narrow scope. The broader circuit formulation exempting every competency-only petition is not adopted. Its reference to a merits hearing means entitlement to federal consideration of the claim; this record supplies no adjudication of the adequacy of Arizona's procedures or a source-specific necessity for a new live evidentiary hearing. Otherwise applicable habeas procedures govern the form of consideration.

Scalia and Thomas's full dissents corroborate their frozen application-based objection. The first merits application ended, its other claims were adjudicated, and the Ford claim was dismissed rather than retained in abeyance. The later request presents again a claim from that prior application. They distinguish a wholly unexhausted application dismissed without prejudice from an application on which claims received merits adjudication. A judicial reservation cannot itself create an exception to Congress's enacted repeated-claim rule. Their argument does not require an assumption that the State waived the statute by earlier seeking prematurity dismissal.

**Dissent-remedy precision:** For Scalia and Thomas, applying §2244(b)(1) to this repeated claim requires dismissal; it is not a discretionary opportunity to authorize it under an invented exception. Their frozen reference to requiring the statutory mechanism must be read with their express repeated-claim ground. Reversal returns the matter for that statutory treatment, without ordering execution, deciding competency, adjudicating a different original application or directing the result of any genuinely separate lawful proceeding. This clarifies the consequence of the frozen rule rather than changing their vote.

## Assembly and public-writing limits

All six affirming Associates share the same narrow sufficient rationale and district-return remedy. The two dissenters share the statutory repeated-application objection, but their published grounds should preserve both the text/application argument and the objection to replacing Congress's alteration with prior judicial practice. Two historical dissents do not by themselves require two new opinions; whatever writings assembly uses must convey the substantive grounds actually adopted.

No holding finds respondent competent or incompetent, approves or invalidates the state competency proceedings, orders a new evidentiary hearing, grants a writ or stay, or authorizes execution. Historical post-divergence Supreme Court orders mentioned in a separate opinion are not imported as current Court actions. The first three nonexhaustion dismissals supply no hidden Ford merits decision. A never-presented later Ford claim, all other later-arising claims and every possible nonmerits dismissal remain outside this decision. Stone and assignment remain for assembly.
