## Reconciliation result

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer retain full agreement on the statutory property-use construction, its bounded application to the appellate reasoning, and vacatur of the appellate affirmance with existing-record review in the Fourth Circuit. They do not presently vote for immediate acquittal, a new trial, constitutional invalidation, constitutional approval of the application, or a substitute conviction under §247. No non-Stone commitment changes at this stage.

The exact common ground is that §844(i) requires a connection between the function performed by the damaged property and interstate commerce or an activity affecting that commerce. The owner's economic relationships do not establish that connection merely by existing. Religious or nonprofit ownership supplies no categorical exclusion; profit-making, rental, sales, interstate travel by Hinds, and a substantial effect from this particular fire are not new universal elements.

**Required lower-court characterization:** The Fourth Circuit expressly recited the correct property-use element before applying it. The objection is to the application: it treated the church's national-denominational payments, employment, employee insurance, and ordinary purchases as sufficient without establishing their relation to the function performed by the damaged premises. Do not say the panel entirely omitted, never recognized, or refused to apply a statutory commerce element. Its distinction between Congress's substantial-effects power and proof of a particular offense also remains accurately stated; the present statutory ruling does not require substantial individual magnitude.

## Remedy reconciliation

The Fourth Circuit's affirmance, rather than an independently reconstructed trial finding, is the immediate object of vacatur. On remand the appellate court must apply the construction to the complete existing trial record and determine the lawful disposition of the conviction. The Supreme Court handoff itself does not decide that every possible use basis was absent, uphold any newly imagined basis, or authorize the prosecution to establish additional facts.

If the existing trial evidence establishes only institutional relationships without the required connection to the property's use, the resulting insufficiency determination requires reversal for entry of acquittal. Burks forbids another trial to supply missing proof; the current Jacobson remedy applies that distinction. If existing proof supports a legally adequate property-use finding, the lower court must identify that basis and address any necessary, properly presented constitutional question under current law. The remand is not a direction to affirm on any assumed alternative and does not settle an unpresented procedural route.

Bailey/Robinson supplies a bounded analogy for correcting a statutory premise while leaving a genuinely available unresolved basis for lower-court examination. Its distinct carrying branch cannot create an alternative element in §844(i). Nor does it support treating the absence of the trial transcript in this research packet as proof that the Court's file lacks it or that a litigant defaulted. No production order, additional hearing, factual stipulation, or new evidence is reconstructed.

## Constitutional holdings remain separate from constitutional background

The controlling Commerce standard continues to require a constitutionally eligible mechanism before aggregation, rejects generic downstream costs as a substitute, and gives no automatic safe harbor to jurisdictional phrasing. That law constrains any necessary constitutional inquiry after remand; it does not become a new Hinds constitutional holding merely because it supports attention to the statute's actual limit. The handoff adopts neither a categorical immunity for noneconomic conduct nor a general power to punish every arson affecting purchases.

The current Agostini formulation of Lemon likewise remains law. Neutral protection against property destruction performs a different governmental function from organizing worship, controlling doctrine, or conferring religiously allocated civil authority. Everson supports that distinction, and Tanford's official-prayer setting does not contradict it. These considerations explain why all eight can reserve the Establishment question without treating a church as categorically immune. They are not approval of hypothetical religious governance or a reinstatement of Lemon's former independent prongs. The four Agostini dissenters' acceptance of current law is not a fictitious retrospective join in its original majority.
