**Association:** Roberts and Rotary separate intimate personal relationships from association to pursue protected expression or religious activity and require attention to the actual relationship. They neither establish a right to civil same-sex marriage nor make absence of civil recognition dispositive of every associational claim. Whether and how the public-employment balance applies to the protected association asserted here is a live legal question, not resolved by describing a ceremony as either private or public. The claim concerns employment consequences, not an order recognizing a civil marriage.

**Free exercise:** Smith retains its neutral/general-law rule. Lukumi's controlling objective-targeting, comparable-secular-conduct, and justification/tailoring holdings apply at their actual scopes. Brown v. Polk County (March 17, 1997), unanimously, holds that a public employer's legitimate power over work, resources, coercion, and official worship does not justify religion-specific commands reaching protected private practice without the required justification and tailoring. Brown does not itself resolve this withdrawal or add statutory claims. The current register module is “Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny,” present operation effective June 11, 1993, Lukumi; its exact components are in the supplement. Test the actual alleged restriction and cause rather than assuming religious targeting from religious impact or treating all employee rights as a single speech claim.

**Equality:** Romer applies ordinary rationality to the actual disadvantage, permitting reasonable predictions and generalizations but not bare harm or irrational prejudice. It establishes no orientation-based heightened tier or general employment outcome. Walmer's preliminary admitted-conduct military holding, Thomasson's genuinely rebuttable conduct-related military inference, and Richenberg's December 8, 1997 application do not decide civilian exclusion, irrebuttable orientation assumptions, religious association, or this employer's record. Their absence of an individualized-operational-harm requirement also cannot be converted into a requirement here. Bowers v. Hardwick remains at its specified-conduct scope; no violation of law is established by orientation or ceremony alone.

**Employment analogies and causal proof:** Waters supplies its express majority propositions, not a universal investigation checklist: a genuinely relied-upon reasonable account protects only discipline of conduct the governing substantive rule permits. Umbehr separates benefit entitlement from constitutional retaliation and requires actual causation, with a preponderance same-decision defense after the claimant establishes protected conduct as a substantial or motivating factor. NTEU's broad prospective-restraint burden is not automatically the burden for one hiring withdrawal. O'Hare preserves distinctions between affiliation and speech claims.

**Current-term public law:** Jeffries (November 17, 1997) requires attention to actual expression, job functions, and institutional needs; reasonable prediction matters but does not alone settle protection, and prevention need not await actual harm. It preserves personal causation and distinct remedies. Valot (January 5, 1998) distinguishes an ordinary conceivable rational basis from justification for penalizing protected conduct or proof of a same-decision defense; it does not make every private request protected. Danahy (February 12, 1998) preserves conduct-date immunity, actual-function inquiry, and proper factual assumptions; general confidentiality, an at-will label, or employment in a prosecutor's office supplies no categorical merits exception. Its November 1995 immunity result does not decide this July 1991 action.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasons below:** The en banc majority assumed associational rights without finally deciding their existence, applied Pickering, and gave weight to confidentiality, public representation, possible reassignment, and forecasts of disruption. It distinguished conduct from orientation for equality and assumed free-exercise protection before using the same balance. It found no actual disruption. The dissents emphasized sincere religious and enduring intimate commitment, absence of a civil-marriage demand or established lawbreaking, an invitation-only out-of-state ceremony without publicity, Bowers's lack of knowledge of sexual conduct, and refusal to discuss his concerns with Shahar. They challenged forecasts resting on prejudice and unsupported assumptions. Tjoflat agreed with judgment but rejected assumed-right balancing, denied protected intimate-association status, and found no evidence that the ceremony's religious character caused withdrawal. These are attributed lower-court legal grounds and readings of the record, not new findings adopted here.

**Strongest supported alternatives, developed analytically:** Shahar can argue that a private intimate/religious commitment cannot become an employment disqualification through public hostility or unsupported assumptions about lawbreaking, office endorsement, or inability to do assigned legal work. She can challenge the appropriateness and application of a single employment balance for distinct claims, and the use of unknown later facts to justify the original decision. Bowers can argue that the legal office's actual responsibilities require trust and credible representation, that supported preventive judgments need not await harm, and that a decision responding to a publicly represented marital relationship rather than religious character differs from a rule suppressing religion. He can contest the asserted protected association and causation while preserving proper defenses. Neither argument is a recovered new filing or concession; neither establishes contested motive or disruption as fact.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain judgment on a properly supported claim-specific ground, reject an asserted protected right within the actual question, or correct the governing standard and remand unresolved material questions. If it assumes a right to decide an otherwise sufficient employment justification, it must say what protection it assumes and why the justification remains sufficient; if it recognizes protection, it must separately decide the permissible employment burden and remedy. A claim-specific reversal need not establish ultimate liability on every theory or entitlement to employment.

No categorical rule follows that an actual disruption must occur, that every reasonable prediction prevails, that private hostility is a permissible reason, or that every confidential government lawyer may be dismissed for any personal association. Religious targeting, the constitutionally protected relationship, ordinary equality, actual causation, legal sufficiency of known grounds, and immunity/relief remain separate inquiries.

The precise ceremony date is a bounded source conflict, not necessary to the pending legal review; a decision actually depending on pre-withdrawal occurrence would require a verified date. Later-acquired facts cannot establish contemporaneous knowledge; their remedial relevance is separately assessed. Summary judgment cannot resolve a genuine material factual conflict. Published recitations permit a bounded decision or ordinary remand without inventing a new interview, new misconduct, or a hypothetical filing.
