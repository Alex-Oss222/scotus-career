### Two supported paths and their limits

**Employee path — reverse Furuno's legal immunity award; set aside the common ground for the other individuals.** Accepting the public-concern assumption, the reported communication concerns accuracy of attendance accounting that affects school funding. Pickering and Givhan already protected that combination of substantive public concern and internal communication. Furuno heard a report of errors, not an imputed accusation of fraud against himself. The supported account identifies minor coworker friction and a general trust relationship, without a concrete operational circumstance making this report sufficiently harmful to justify the nonrenewal. Those concerns cannot become a finding of insubordination, refusal of duties or knowingly false reporting. The employee-supporting Justices conclude that the particular assumed retaliation is sufficiently covered by the preconduct cases to defeat this legal damages defense. McKinley corroborates, at circuit level, the distinction between genuine operational concerns and a close-relationship pretext; its public audience and different employment facts are not imported.

**Strongest contrary ground:** Pickering expressly distinguished criticism of a remote Board from criticism within a daily working relationship. Givhan reserved the latter context, and Connick permits prospective protection before actual breakdown. Brewster conceded a teacher–principal relationship requiring trust; the secretary expressed actual distrust, and the employer need not wait until classroom work deteriorates. Thus a reasonable official could regard the assumed setting as sufficiently different from Pickering to remain personally immune even if a court now finds the speech protected. General financial subject matter does not dispose of Anderson's particularity requirement.

**Answer adopted by the employee-path Justices:** The relationship is relevant and prevention is permitted, but neither identifies the missing operational harm on this assumed account. Treating a report of errors as proof of inability to work with the principal would permit ordinary protected criticism itself to supply the necessary impairment. The private audience reduces dissemination-related harm and cannot independently diminish the First Amendment protection Givhan established. Error after investigation is not knowing or reckless falsity. These conclusions are an application of conduct-date law, not an actual-disruption requirement or a transfer of NTEU's broader burden. They leave materially different supported facts and independent defenses open.

**Official path — affirm the personal speech-immunity judgment without deciding ultimate constitutional protection.** The concrete close working relationship and reported coworker distrust distinguish the case sufficiently from Pickering and the predominantly clerical role in Rankin. Connick and Givhan leave genuine room to consider prospective loss of trust and the circumstances of an internal confrontation. The preconduct cases do not make the immunity question clear enough to impose personal damages here. This path does not treat the presence of balancing as a defense, rely on probationary status, attribute fraud to Furuno, find malicious falsity, or invent a same-decision showing. It need not endorse every consideration in the Ninth Circuit's opinion.

**Strongest contrary ground:** Public-funding accuracy is a substantial concern, the disruption is described as minor, and internal reporting is affirmatively protected. A forecast built merely from a coworker's hurt feelings or the fact of criticism is not a legally adequate governmental interest; Givhan is a teacher–principal case and therefore prevents reliance on the relationship's label alone.

**Answer adopted by the official-path Justices:** Those points may prevail on ultimate protection, and no generic trust exception follows. But the personal-damages question is distinct. Existing law recognizes the relevant employer interests and leaves sufficiently unsettled their application to this acknowledged daily relationship and reported distrust. The officials' objectively reasonable legal uncertainty can support immunity without a merits holding that the nonrenewal was constitutional. This is not a ruling that every reasonable forecast wins the balance.
