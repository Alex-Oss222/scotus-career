# 155. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Johnson & Higgins, Inc.

*Stone pre-vote case brief. Alternate-history petition and merits treatment, not an actual Supreme Court merits decision.*

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. Status

- **Version:** OT1997 Stone-only forecast, version 2. **Approval:** **PROPOSED AND APPROVED** for the simulation, not actual Supreme Court action.

## B. Judgment and remedy

- **Stone's vote:** **Affirm** the Second Circuit's ADEA liability and the bounded prospective injunction.
- **Remedy:** Prohibit forced termination of covered employment at age 60 or 62 under the linked policy. The company may separately structure retirement from ownership or board seats as statutory law permits, so long as it does not indirectly end covered employment because of age.

## C. Issue positions

### Issue 1: The EEOC possesses its own enforcement authority

- **Reach:** Statutory authority before individual merits.
- **Proposed holding:** The enforcement powers actually enacted for the Commission do not depend on a director affirmatively requesting suit. Congress separately specified conditions for valid private age-claim waivers and preserved agency responsibilities.
- **Application:** The company was informed of the violation theory and declined to conciliate on that basis. The agency was not required to repeat an unproductive offer indefinitely. Opposing affidavits by individual directors do not extinguish the Commission's statutory public suit, and the submitted waivers lacked important enacted safeguards.
- **Boundary:** Agency discretion does not permit unbounded remedies or action beyond the covered employment practice.

### Issue 2: An equity owner may also be a statutory employee

- **Reach:** ADEA coverage on the admitted record.
- **Stone's legal position:** Statutory terms and real employment arrangements control. Corporate personhood creates neither human rights for the firm nor a general exemption for individuals who help govern it.
- **Proposed holding:** Persons continuing regular managerial employment, reporting through an organizational hierarchy, and receiving review-based compensation may qualify as ADEA employees while separately serving as directors and holding stock.
- **Application:** Johnson & Higgins chose a corporate charter and continued describing directors as officers and employees after their elections. Stone will not rewrite this arrangement as a partnership for purposes of defeating a statute that reaches the employment relationship. Nor does he hold that an inactive director is an employee merely by bearing the title.
- **Boundary:** A genuine nonemployee director retains lawful governance autonomy. Ownership rights are distinct from statutory employment protection.

### Issue 3: Succession may be reasonable but age is not “other than age”

- **Proposed holding:** A facial rule terminating a covered employee at age 60 or 62 cannot satisfy the reasonable-factors-*other-than*-age provision by identifying a sensible motive for using age. That statute asks what criterion the employer actually selected.
- **Application:** Succession planning could be implemented using another legally permissible criterion or by separating directorship from employment. Here the triggering fact was age; the enacted exception does not cover it.
- **Boundary:** No free-floating heightened equal-protection age tier is created. The outcome is driven by the ADEA as Congress wrote it.

## D. Certiorari position

- **Approved vote:** **Grant** in the alternate-history docket.
- **Ground:** Clarify that choosing a corporation and employing its owners have different, concurrently enforceable legal consequences.
- **Status:** Final Stone-only forecast.
