## United States v. Parker — December 1, 1997

**Identity and posture.** Third Circuit No. 95-2018, reported 108 F.3d 28; lower decision March 7, 1997. No separate Supreme Court docket number supplied. Section 1254(1) permits Supreme Court review of a case in a court of appeals before or after judgment; no state-court-finality condition is imported.

**Questions and relief.** Whether original 18 U.S.C. §228 constitutionally reaches willful nonpayment of an existing support obligation to children residing in another State under the Commerce Clause and Tenth Amendment. Parker seeks reversal and dismissal; the United States seeks preservation of the appellate reinstatement and ordinary further prosecution.

**Neutral record.** Parker resides in Florida, and his two children reside in Pennsylvania. The charge concerns willful failure to pay an existing state-fixed support obligation. The district court dismissed on commerce and Tenth Amendment grounds. The federal prosecution accepts the support order as a predicate rather than determining parentage, custody or the support amount. The charge is an allegation; the supplied record establishes no trial finding of willfulness, means to pay or satisfaction of every statutory element.

**Issue snapshot — commerce.** *Mussari/Schroeder*, June 30, 1997, directly governs legally fixed cross-border support payments. Different-state residence is essential. The legally owed cross-border payment may be protected without a completed transfer. *Lopez* forbids remote social-cost reasoning and judicially added elements; it neither overrules Mussari nor requires flight, travel, a wire transfer or evasion motive. A challenge to the predicate order's existence or continuing force remains cognizable if actually presented.

## Salinas v. United States — December 2, 1997

**Identity and posture.** Supreme Court No. 96-738; plenary merits; argument October 8, 1997. Review concerns a Fifth Circuit judgment sustaining two federal-bribery convictions and a RICO-conspiracy conviction. The substantive §1962(c) charge ended in acquittal; that acquittal is not reopened.

**Questions and relief.** Whether §666 requires the bribe itself to affect federal funds; whether §1962(d) requires each conspirator personally to agree to commit two predicate acts. Salinas seeks relief from the challenged convictions under those constructions. The government defends the convictions under the enacted institutional/transaction predicates and ordinary agreement liability.

**Neutral record.** A Texas county jail housed a federal prisoner pursuant to a federal agreement. The sheriff received money and deputy Salinas received two watches and a truck in connection with unauthorized contact visits for the prisoner. The jury convicted Salinas of two §666(a)(1)(B) counts and conspiracy under §1962(d), but not the substantive RICO count. This is the supplied factual account; it does not independently establish a disputed dollar valuation, exact instruction, annual-assistance computation or appellate harmlessness finding.

**Issue snapshot — §666.** The enacted conditions must remain distinct: the defendant's covered agency relationship; corrupt solicitation/demand/acceptance or agreement to accept a thing of value with the specified intent; connection to the covered government's or organization's business, transaction or series involving at least $5,000; and the §666(b) federal-benefits threshold exceeding $10,000 in the specified one-year period. The statute's benefits and ordinary-compensation exceptions retain their actual terms. The issue is whether a further bribe-specific federal-funds connection must be shown, not whether the express nexus predicates can be ignored.

Wells is an analogy against inventing elements, not a construction of §666. Ratzlaf supplies residual lenity only after genuine ambiguity remains following ordinary interpretation. A federalism/constitutional-avoidance argument must identify a plausible reading and a material constitutional issue; it cannot erase explicit statutory conditions or manufacture a new commercial transaction. No free-standing constitutional holding about all federal bribery prosecutions is needed if the case can be resolved within the statutory issue and established federally connected jail setting.

**Issue snapshot — RICO agreement.** Section 1962(d)'s agreement offense and §1962(c)'s substantive offense must be construed separately. Reves supplies the actual substantive direction requirement but expressly reserves conspiracy liability. Shabani supports attending to Congress's agreement language and preserves knowing participation beyond reasonable doubt; its §846 no-overt-act holding is not automatically a holding under §1962(d). A conspirator's knowing agreement to advance an enterprise violation is distinct from personally carrying out every substantive act. Mere awareness, friendship, employment or association does not establish agreement. The substantive acquittal neither reinstates substantive guilt nor automatically resolves a distinct conspiracy charge.

**Supported paths and counterarguments.** One statutory path declines both additional requirements, while retaining every enacted §666 predicate and proof of knowing agreement to a substantive RICO violation. Salinas's strongest contrary path argues that statutory context, the federal nexus and criminal-law clarity require a bribe-specific connection or personal predicate agreement. Its difficulty is identifying those additional requirements in the actual text without converting RICO conspiracy into personal commission. A narrower §666 disposition may reserve the outer constitutional boundary where the bribe concerns operation of a jail housing a federal prisoner. ## Foster v. Love — December 2, 1997
