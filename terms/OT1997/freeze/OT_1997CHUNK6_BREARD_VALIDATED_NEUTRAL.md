## 1. Validation qualifications and independent framing

1. **Findings, submissions and concessions are distinct.** The Fourth Circuit actually affirmed the treaty-default disposition in Breard on exhaustion/default and absence of cause; it expressly declined to discuss prejudice. Its conclusion that no innocence gateway was shown is a lower-court conclusion to be examined under actual current law. The district court's treatment of prejudice does not become an appellate holding. The State stipulation concerning notice is reported by the original habeas pleading and is not an admitted stipulation about causal prejudice or remedy. The United States' recognition of deficient notice before the ICJ is likewise confined to notice. The ICJ's recitations of guilt, language competence, adequate counsel, non-deliberateness, State practice, lack of prejudice and diplomatic conduct in paragraphs 18–22 and 28–30 are the United States' submissions, not independent ICJ findings.

2. **The two Paraguay actions allege distinct duties.** A-738 seeks protection of the earlier civil case asserting Paraguay's and its officials' own consular rights under the Vienna Convention and the 1859 Friendship Treaty; it is not next-friend habeas. The Fourth Circuit decided the Eleventh Amendment ground, including no ongoing violation and retrospective relief, leaving Rooker/Feldman and political-question defenses undecided. A-771 alleges a new threatened failure to honor the April 9 provisional measure, UN Charter obligations, comity and the Consul General's official-capacity §1983 claim. The earlier immunity characterization cannot itself decide that new theory. Neither a new pleading label nor the seriousness of international consequences establishes its enforceability, cause of action, proper defendant or remedy.

3. **The full ICJ order is preservation, with express merits reservations.** Paragraphs 23–34 establish only prima facie international jurisdiction at the provisional-measures stage. Paragraph 25 reports both States as parties to the Convention and Protocol without reservation. Paragraph 31 identifies a Convention-remedy dispute; paragraph 33 reserves both the existence of the remedy and whether prejudice is required for the merits. Paragraphs 35–40 find urgency and threatened irreparable loss of the requested relief, disclaim criminal-appellate review, preserve both States' rights and state that the provisional measures do not prejudge the merits. Paragraph 41 directs its request to the United States: all measures at its disposal to prevent execution pending the final ICJ decision, and information to the ICJ about measures taken. It does not itself specify a United States judicial vehicle, find a domestic cause of action, vacate a conviction, adjudicate prejudice or state that an American court must disregard a procedural rule. Whether the cited international provisions generate a binding provisional obligation and a judicially enforceable domestic duty remains a legal question, not a source fact.

4. **Current political-question law is material.** Aktepe, effective March 10, 1998, requires a claim-specific examination of an independently manageable legal duty. Military or foreign-relations subject matter and possible diplomatic embarrassment alone are insufficient. Its refusal to adjudicate the particular policy-dependent military duties supplies no categorical treaty-enforcement bar. Conversely, naming a treaty or comity does not establish the independent duty. The exact Aktepe public law and separate position are copied below; no raw Record was opened.

5. **Interim authority and ultimate relief remain separate.** Each application requires its own lawful channel, requested duration, governing interim standard and supported grounds. A grant of temporary protection would not itself grant certiorari, an original writ, leave to file, or ultimate relief. A denial would not by implication dispose of those accompanying proceedings. A limited temporary action requires its own stated legal purpose, ending event and later disposition; no specific pause, response deadline or mandatory hearing has been created in preparation. If any lawful protection is entered in one application, the effect on the urgent timing of the others must be addressed from that actual order rather than assumed now.

6. **Readiness is bounded, not a finding of entitlement.** The three ready applications present supported legal and emergency questions even though domestic enforceability and the likelihood of relief are contested. A model choosing a path that needs a more specific unresolved fact must identify that fact and obtain targeted sanitized support under Engine §14, rather than invent it or turn an unrecovered paper into litigant default. ## 2. Neutral record, supported competing positions and entering-law framework

The following verified candidate portions retain the supported arguments. Newly formulated comparisons are analytical possibilities, not representations that a party filed or preserved them. Published lower-court citations to historical post-divergence Supreme Court holdings describe the lower courts' reasoning only. In particular, their categorical Seminole Tribe and Coeur d'Alene formulations must be tested against the actual law stated here.


**Habeas and evidence.** Exact registered standards included in the slice are **Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims** (*Schlup*, January 23, 1995), **Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility** (*Kyles*, April 19, 1995), **Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar** (*Felker*, June 28, 1996), **Federal-prisoner limitation and successive-motion certification**, **Second or successive state-custody habeas applications**, **Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments** (*Lindh*, June 23, 1997), **No delay-based substitute for statutory habeas dismissal**, **Interim protection necessary for required habeas process**, and **Equitable delay independently of a habeas procedural bar** (*Lonchar*, April 1, 1996). The slice contains each complete trigger, qualification, and authority rather than a name-only list.

*Schlup* requires reliable new evidence assessed with the whole probative record and a showing that, more likely than not, no reasonable properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. It requires more than doubt or ordinary prejudice, but is a gateway, not an affirmative adjudication of innocence. Evidence is not categorically excluded because it is impeachment, comes from a prisoner, or would be inadmissible at trial; reliability and the full record remain essential. Stevens wrote the controlling gateway holding, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer among the Associates. Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas preferred a stricter quantum; that objection is a prior position, not current law.

*Herrera*, January 25, 1993, establishes that its particular showing was insufficient; it establishes neither a general freestanding innocence threshold nor a controlling categorical denial of a constitutional right. O'Connor and Kennedy explained why execution of an established innocent person would be intolerable. Stevens and Souter joined Blackmun's legal position supporting a probable-innocence inquiry and remand. Scalia and Thomas's categorical objection did not command the Court.

*Kyles* assesses the cumulative lawful uses of favorable suppressed evidence for a reasonable probability of a different result. It does not require proof of innocence or item-by-item materiality. The prosecution must learn of favorable evidence known to police acting on its case team; it has no categorical duty to search every government office. The preserved distinction for evidence independently known to the defense cannot be erased. Once material Brady suppression is established, a second harmless-error screen cannot defeat relief. Souter's controlling opinion was joined by O'Connor, Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer among the Associates. Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas accepted cumulative materiality but disputed its application. The registered **Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review** has its own scope; historical broad descriptions of *Brecht* cannot displace the actual simulated rule or *Kyles*'s claim-specific consequence.

*Felker* preserves original habeas despite the authorization-review bar but does not permit relabeling barred appellate review. Rule 20 requires exceptional circumstances, no adequate relief elsewhere, relevant exhaustion, and an explanation for not applying to the district court. Section 2244(b)'s restrictions inform original-writ discretion; their direct binding force in that channel remains reserved. Original jurisdiction alone supplies neither a writ nor a stay. *Triestman*, March 12, 1998, unanimously holds that § 2255's certification cross-reference incorporates § 2244(b)(3)(E)'s ordinary-certiorari bar; a petition presenting only refusal of certification cannot be converted into a different proceeding. It leaves the savings clause, substantive retroactivity, carry liability, and factual inquiry unresolved.

*Lindh*'s registered holding concerns pending noncapital chapter 153 applications and does not decide every Title I transition or automatically settle chapter 154 in a capital case. *Trest*, December 9, 1997, holds that courts need not raise an omitted nonjurisdictional default sua sponte; it does not hold that an asserted default is waived, or decide the full range of permissible sua-sponte practice.

**Supported record.** Breard first raised the consular-notification issue in federal habeas filed in August 1996; the official report and pleading differ on the day, which is immaterial here. The pleadings allege that officials failed to advise Breard of consular rights and report a State stipulation concerning that failure. Counsel's affidavit describes a noncapital offer, advice to accept, and Breard's refusal and insistence on testifying. Whether consular advice would have changed those choices is disputed. Notice failure is not itself a finding of causal prejudice or a judicial remedy. Article 36(1)(b) requires notice to the consulate on the detained national's request, forwarding communications without delay, and advice to the individual of those rights without delay. Paragraph (1)(c) protects visits, communication, and arrangements for representation but requires abstention if the national expressly opposes consular action. Paragraph (2) subjects exercise of those rights to the receiving State's laws and regulations **subject to the requirement that those laws and regulations enable full effect to the rights' purposes**. ### Vienna Convention Article 5

Source: official UN Convention text, https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/9_2_1963.pdf ; full 29-page text read

```text
Article 5
Consular functions
Consular functions consist in:
(a) protecting in the receiving State the interests of the sending State and of its nationals, both
individuals and bodies corporate, within the limits permitted by international law;
(b) furthering the development of commercial, economic, cultural and scientific relations between the
sending State and the receiving State and otherwise promoting friendly relations between them in
accordance with the provisions of the present Convention;
(c) ascertaining by all lawful means conditions and developments in the commercial, economic,
cultural and scientific life of the receiving State, reporting thereon to the Government of the sending
State and giving information to persons interested;
(d) issuing passports and travel documents to nationals of the sending State, and visas or appropriate
documents to persons wishing to travel to the sending State;
(e) helping and assisting nationals, both individuals and bodies corporate, of the sending State;
5
(f) acting as notary and civil registrar and in capacities of a similar kind, and performing certain
functions of an administrative nature, provided that there is nothing contrary thereto in the laws and
regulations of the receiving State;
(g) safeguarding the interests of nationals, both individuals and bodies corporate, of the sending
States in cases of succession mortis causa in the territory of the receiving State, in accordance with the
laws and regulations of the receiving State;
(h) safeguarding, within the limits imposed by the laws and regulations of the receiving State, the
interests of minors and other persons lacking full capacity who are nationals of the sending State,
particularly where any guardianship or trusteeship is required with respect to such persons;
(i) subject to the practices and procedures obtaining in the receiving State, representing or arranging
appropriate representation for nationals of the sending State before the tribunals and other authorities of
the receiving State, for the purpose of obtaining, in accordance with the laws and regulations of the
receiving State, provisional measures for the preservation of the rights and interests of these nationals,
where, because of absence or any other reason, such nationals are unable at the proper time to assume
the defence of their rights and interests;
(j) transmitting judicial and extrajudicial documents or executing letters rogatory or commissions to
take evidence for the courts of the sending State in accordance with international agreements in force or,
in the absence of such international agreements, in any other manner compatible with the laws and
regulations of the receiving State;
(k) exercising rights of supervision and inspection provided for in the laws and regulations of the
sending State in respect of vessels having the nationality of the sending State, and of aircraft registered
in that State, and in respect of their crews;
(l) extending assistance to vessels and aircraft mentioned in subparagraph (k) of this article, and to
their crews, taking statements regarding the voyage of a vessel, examining and stamping the ship’s
papers, and, without prejudice to the powers of the authorities of the receiving State, conducting
investigations into any incidents which occurred during the voyage, and settling disputes of any kind
between the master, the officers and the seamen insofar as this may be authorized by the laws and
regulations of the sending State;
(m)performing any other functions entrusted to a consular post by the sending State which are not
prohibited by the laws and regulations of the receiving State or to which no objection is taken by the
receiving State or which are referred to in the international agreements in force between the sending
State and the receiving State.
```

Article 36
Communication and contact with nationals
of the sending State
1.With a view to facilitating the exercise of consular functions relating to nationals of the sending
State:
(a) consular officers shall be free to communicate with nationals of the sending State and to have
access to them. Nationals of the sending State shall have the same freedom with respect to

action.

### Aktepe v. United States; Eleventh Circuit No. 96-2167. — 1998-03-10

#### Event

Aktepe v. United States; Eleventh Circuit No. 96-2167, judgment below 105 F.3d 1400. Plenary decision on justiciability, March 10, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 5. The Court reviews under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) the February 20, 1997 affirmance of summary judgment for the United States. The question is whether the pleaded maritime negligence controversy is nonjusticiable, including whether a separable operational claim can be adjudicated without choosing military policy. No separate Supreme Court docket or oral-argument date is established. The sailors seek adjudication of two wrongful-death and 299 personal-injury claims arising when the Saratoga fired live missiles at the Muavenet during an intended mock NATO attack.

#### Participation

All nine Justices participate at submission and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

#### Public Action

The Court affirms the Eleventh Circuit's political-question judgment.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 5–4. Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. **Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. The political-question summary judgment remains effective. No negligence, damages, statutory-coverage, sovereign-immunity or Feres ruling, discovery order or new trial follows.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer | Affirms | Complete claim-specific nonjusticiability ground and limits |
| Dissent | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg | Would vacate and remand | Issue-specific separation of execution allegations from policy choices; no guaranteed surviving claim |

#### Holdings

### The pleaded duties cannot be adjudicated without choosing military training policy

**Holding and operative rule:** A military setting alone does not make a damages action nonjusticiable. These claims nevertheless cannot proceed because determining the alleged duties concerning drill notification, command terminology, live arming and questioning orders would require choosing among military training, obedience and safety policies without an independently manageable legal standard on the supplied record.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer; five of nine participating Justices directly adopt the rule, application and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker requires examination of the particular claim, not rejection because its subject is military. Nixon preserves that issue-specific inquiry. Here ordinary negligence terminology does not identify a duty independent of the disputed training choices. Deciding which personnel should know that an attack is simulated, what live-weapons commands should mean, and when operators should question an apparent firing instruction would select the balance between realistic preparation, command obedience and safety that the pleaded allegations challenge.

The contrary argument has force: this was an unintended live discharge during an intended simulation, and compensation after an accident differs from continuing supervision. Gilligan expressly left specific-injury controversies distinct from the ongoing supervision it rejected. But that reservation neither makes every damages action justiciable nor supplies a manageable standard for these proposed duties. Retrospective relief can still depend on judicial selection of training policy. No independently fixed operational command is established in the supplied record that separates the alleged breach from that selection; this is not a finding that no such command exists or that plaintiffs withheld evidence.

Weiss and Loving preserve enforcement of actual constitutional and enacted limits. Dalton concerns a particular statutory review scheme and supplies no general executive immunity. Richenberg likewise combines substantial military weight with an enforceable legal standard. None supplies the independent duty missing from this claim-specific inquiry. NATO participation, possible embarrassment and military status alone would be insufficient. The Court affirms the political-question judgment without finding that the firing was lawful, deciding negligence or damages, or resolving statutory coverage, sovereign immunity or Feres. A materially different claim enforcing an independent legal duty remains outside this holding.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Baker v. Carr supplies the claim-specific political-question inquiry; military subject matter alone remains insufficient.
- Nixon v. United States preserves review of independently fixed legal limits while recognizing the confined nonjusticiability of an actually committed procedural choice; its impeachment holding is not military immunity.
- Gilligan v. Morgan, 413 U.S. 1, is applied to the policy-selection concern, while its reservation of specific-injury controversies remains intact and supplies no categorical damages exception.
- Weiss v. United States and Loving v. United States preserve constitutional and statutory review in military matters; their appointment and delegation holdings do not establish the maritime duty proposed here.
- Dalton v. Specter retains scheme-specific statutory preclusion and is not extended to these maritime statutes.
- Richenberg v. Cohen preserves substantial military weight within actual legal constraints, without creating a military-tort immunity. No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

The issue-specific political-question rule applies to these pleaded training and communication duties. Military status does not alone bar judicial review, and the decision establishes no categorical military-tort immunity. Independent statutory defenses and any materially different claim resting on an independently manageable duty retain their own requirements.

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, dissenting.** The dissent would vacate and remand for separate examination of the claims. The intended simulation and accidental live firing supply a concrete reason to distinguish execution from design; listing training and command purposes does not establish that every allegation requires choosing policy. Gilligan's reservation of specific-injury cases and Nixon's claim-specific limitation oppose the blanket effect of the judgment. Weiss and Loving preserve enforceable legal constraints despite military subject matter, and Dalton does not supply general executive insulation.

The dissent accepts that a court cannot invent a safer training rule, call it operational, and thereby evade the political-question doctrine. The remand would require the Eleventh Circuit to identify which allegations actually require selection of constitutionally committed military policy or lack manageable standards, without guaranteeing a surviving negligence claim, discovery, liability or compensation. No particular written protocol is found or presumed violated. Statutory coverage, sovereign immunity, Feres, causation, damages and the justiciability of any specifically identified high-level policy challenge remain unresolved. The dissent's proposed examination is not the Court's mandate.

#### Procedure After Action

This Supreme Court review is complete. Summary judgment on the political-question ground remains in force; the Court directs no further merits proceedings.
