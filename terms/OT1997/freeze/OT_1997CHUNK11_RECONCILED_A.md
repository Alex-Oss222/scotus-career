### Preserved substantive positions and boundaries

Path P treats actually generated interest as an incident of the conceded private principal under the background ownership rule. Webb's forbids government conversion through a new declaration alone; Roth supplies the independent-law source of title; Lucas preserves the antecedent-limit distinction without deciding this unpresented taking question. A lack of positive net income does not itself eliminate possession, control and disposition rights. Banking permission and tax treatment do not independently determine ownership. The property question can be answered within the granted scope while the other issues remain for the lower courts. These four Justices will not join the contrary integrated-remand proposal insofar as it displaces the ownership question presented. They do not endorse automatic compensation or program invalidation.

The integrated-remand position treats the regulatory setting and inability to obtain net income as material to whether recognizing title alone has any consequence under the Takings Clause. Penn Central's economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations and character of governmental action may defeat a taking claim; compensation ordinarily measures the owner's loss, not the recipient's gain. These remain questions for the lower courts, not findings of no taking or zero compensation by this Court. The distinction between property identification and valuation remains real, but the four regard deciding them in isolation as potentially distorting the constitutional inquiry. The principal is unquestionably the client's property; a claim about the compulsory use of that principal is not extinguished by their conditional rejection of the claimed interest entitlement.

Their alternative N answer concerns only the proper no-net-return program setting. It distinguishes Webb's independently productive fund and rejects treating a return made available only through the operative legal arrangement as an already available private benefit. It cannot rest on the false premise that every individual client is legally prohibited from holding a NOW account. Corporate banking ineligibility, individual eligibility, administrative and reporting costs, and gross versus net yield remain distinct. Their objection does not authorize confiscation of principal or diversion of interest that the client could obtain independently. No new finding determines whether the particular funds could yield net interest outside IOLTA.

The reported alternative-pooling concession retains exactly its source status. It permits assessment through feasible attorney pooling; it is not a finding that these funds produced a positive net alternative return. This distinction was available historically and supplies no new ground to change a Justice's judgment. The Federal Reserve interpretation's validity, standing, First Amendment claims, taking, compensation amount and ultimate injunction remain unresolved. The ownership rationale does not prohibit reasonable charges for actual services; it does not convert this program into such a fee arrangement.
