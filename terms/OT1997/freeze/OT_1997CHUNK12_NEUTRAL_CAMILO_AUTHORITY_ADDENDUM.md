## Culpability, awareness and affirmative connection

**Gutierrez, 882 F.2d at 560-69 & notes 4, 6, 8, 11-12.** A supervisor answers for personal acts or omissions involving deliberate, reckless or callous indifference, with an affirmative link to the constitutional injury. Gross negligence suffices only when it amounts to that indifference; the opinion expressly rejects a lesser gross-negligence instruction. Its verdict survived because the punitive-damages findings necessarily supplied sufficient culpability. That trial-specific cure supplies no factual finding here. The opinion distinguishes individual liability from municipal policy/custom liability and does not separately decide qualified immunity.

Its causal-initiation rule reaches a legally required omission or a sequence initiated with knowledge or reasonable foreseeability of constitutional injury. Direct participation in the final assault and final decisionmaking authority are not indispensable. But foreseeability in the causation inquiry is not a replacement for the required constitutional culpability, a source of an unspecified affirmative duty, or proof that any advisory act caused this injury. Its actual supervisory applications involved concrete assignment, evaluation or disciplinary power and meaningful warning information. Its alternative failure-to-intervene theory was expressly left undecided.

**Febus, 14 F.3d at 91-95 & notes 4, 6, 9-11.** The opinion repeats the personal-conduct and affirmative-link rules, expressly recognizing actual or constructive notice. Its reasonable-official formulation asks whether the likelihood of a constitutional violation would be manifest, not whether ordinary professional negligence occurred. It rejects liability where unrelated complaints gave no warning of the relevant danger, training or recruitment criticisms did not establish conscious disregard, and the proposed causal inference was too weak. Even an assumed culpable training omission did not dispense with causal proof. A deficient investigation after the injury, without more, supplied neither sufficient culpability nor linkage.

Accordingly, distinguish three inquiries: what information a defendant actually had or was legally chargeable with under the relevant rule; whether that person's response reached constitutional culpability; and whether the response affirmatively caused the constitutional injury. Neither actual knowledge alone nor constructive notice alone answers all three. These earlier circuit formulations cannot accurately be reduced to an exclusive actual-subjective-awareness test. Conversely, they do not establish that every failure to discover information is deliberate indifference.

Gutierrez's complaint-file discussion, at 572-75, treats the volume and similarity of reports as possible notice requiring investigation, although underlying accusations were unproved or dismissed. It permits bounded use for supervisory notice and response, not proof of the accusations' truth or character-propensity use. Febus supplies the complementary limitation: unrelated complaints and speculative claims that nonpunishment emboldened an officer are insufficient. Neither prescribes how the assumed Camilo evidence must be evaluated.

## Immunity, medical roles and review limits

These are relevant preconduct circuit authorities, not Supreme Court decisions adopting a nationwide supervisory test or adjudicating these appellants' clearly established obligations. Their specificity, factual analogy, practical powers and limits matter under the unchanged Harlow/Anderson framework. They supply support for both recognizing a concrete warning-and-response obligation and distinguishing a materially different role or an insufficient causal connection. No factual twin is automatically required; neither is a general supervisory principle automatically decisive.

Neither case decides private medical contractors' qualified-immunity eligibility, professional certification liability, an absolute medical immunity, or Puerto Rico statutory employee immunity. Gutierrez's causal rule does not automatically exonerate an adviser who lacks final rearming power, but its actual official-supervisor applications do not independently resolve how that rule reaches the psychiatrists' medical decisions. Their own supported information, alleged disregard, advisory function, practical influence and causal connection remain necessary. Reasonable expert reliance and medical error remain distinct from reckless disregard. No contractual term, market condition or professional standard is supplied by this supplement. Richardson's reserved eligibility questions and the separate state-law defense remain as framed in the original packet.

Febus discusses Harlow/Anderson but resolves its appeal for insufficient proof of a constitutional violation by the particular supervisors, not through a separate ruling that established misconduct was protected by unclear law. Its 1994 interlocutory treatment predates Johnson and Behrens; it does not authorize factual-sufficiency review beyond the current jurisdictional boundary. Gutierrez reviewed an actual verdict. Neither procedural posture may be imported as a Camilo verdict, a permissible new factual finding, or an automatic instruction to grant summary judgment or hold a trial.
