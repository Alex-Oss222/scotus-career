## Thresholds, claims and actual disposition

All eight Associates participate. Section 1254(1) authorizes review of this court-of-appeals case, but does not enlarge the Tenth Circuit's authority over the underlying interlocutory appeal. Johnson and Behrens permit separable legal immunity questions on the proper assumed facts and bar factual-sufficiency appeals. No successive earlier appeal is established; Stevens and Breyer's Behrens objection to repeated appeals therefore supplies no separate bar here. Jefferson's state-finality rule does not govern this federal review channel.

Clanton asserts her own arrest and detention injury. Whether the constitutional protection she invokes reaches use of another person's coerced statement is a merits/source question, not an absence of Article III injury. It cannot be answered by borrowing Michael's personal privilege against self-incrimination. The action seeks individual damages from Cooper. The dismissed other defendants and state-law claims are outside the established review scope; no cross-petition or enlarged grant revives them.

**Common formal judgment commitment:** Each Associate would affirm in part, vacate in part, and remand. Preserve rejection of legal immunity for the properly assumed knowing/material warrant-deception and NCIC detention-deception claims; dismiss, rather than adjudicate, the factual-sufficiency portions of Cooper's appeal. Leave the three rejected subsidiary warrant theories undisturbed. Vacate the appellate disposition of the distinct coerced-third-party-confession theory and require proper consideration of the preserved legal issues on the correct assumed facts, including constitutional source, ultimate legal sufficiency and conduct-date notice. This is an actual bounded remand commitment, not a provisional choice among unentered possible dispositions.

The confession remand corrects an identified judicial error: after analyzing constitutional source and notice, the circuit treated the ultimate coercion conclusion as beyond interlocutory review merely because coercion involves circumstances and facts. That characterization prevented the required separation of historical disputes from the legal immunity issue. The remand does not rest on the operator's lack of original filings. It neither orders production of papers nor implies that the Court lacks them.

The Court does not award immunity on the remanded theory, establish liability, choose historical facts, order a trial, reinstate dismissed claims or decide damages. The District Court's denial remains operative except insofar as lawful further appellate proceedings dispose of a separable claim. A surviving claim is no reason to deny an otherwise authorized claim-specific legal immunity appeal.

## Components and strongest competing paths

### 1. Appellate legal review and factual limits

**Adopted:** Johnson's and Behrens's unanimous assumed-fact rules govern all three surviving theories. Cooper's contention that Clanton supplied no evidence he knew the confession was false is a factual-sufficiency objection beyond immediate review. His contentions that assumed conduct causes no legally cognizable injury or violates no clearly established right are potentially reviewable legal questions. Calling a mixed ultimate constitutional issue factual does not foreclose that legal review; calling disputed conduct legal does not authorize reweighing it.

**Strongest contrary path:** Affirm the confession disposition without correction because the district judge found a coercive atmosphere and Johnson bars revisiting the record. That path properly protects the underlying history but wrongly treats the ultimate constitutional characterization and notice inquiry as historical facts. Miller v. Fenton supplies the specific voluntariness distinction. Ornelas reinforces task-sensitive allocation, without making every mixed question independently reviewable or importing habeas burdens. The common ground does not require Scalia to abandon his Ornelas dissent over nonwarrant probable-cause review.

**Alternative ultimate disposition considered:** Decide the complete confession immunity question immediately on the summarized assumed conduct. That remains a lawful possibility in a properly framed case, but the circuit expressly declined the ultimate task and used an unqualified third-party-use proposition. Correcting that allocation and requiring claim-specific legal analysis is sufficient here. This does not prescribe a mandatory merits-first sequence; a genuinely dispositive notice defense may be addressed first under the existing framework.

### 2. Warrant affidavit and the three subsidiary theories

**Adopted legal proposition:** An officer who knowingly or recklessly supplies false factual assertions material to probable cause cannot obtain qualified immunity merely because a judge issued the warrant. Franks (1978) and Malley (1986) supplied particularized notice before the August 31, 1993 affidavit. The protected nonmovant account includes Cooper's knowing presentation of false incriminating facts material to obtaining Clanton's warrant. The circuit may reject his categorical legal immunity while dismissing his request to reassess evidentiary support.

**Strongest contrary path:** A judicial warrant and an accomplice's confession could make the arrest objectively reasonable, and Bobby's earlier derivative report could provide an independent basis. Those considerations could matter on a supported corrected-affidavit account. They cannot be converted into adjudicated independent probable cause from this opinion's juxtaposition of Bobby's report and its description of Michael's statement as the sole implicating evidence. Franks concerns the affiant's knowing or reckless falsity, not a witness's later recantation alone. A corrected truthful account sufficient for probable cause, a good-faith report of a statement later shown false, or absence of personal responsibility remains a lawful defense when supported.

The three subsidiary theories remain rejected at their actual scope: sufficiently reliable accomplice hearsay may support probable cause under Gates, but does not invariably do so; the Fourth Amendment does not invariably require filed charges before a warrant; and the federal grand-jury indictment guarantee does not govern state prosecutions. State-law procedural defects alone do not establish a §1983 deprivation. No expanded review is needed to preserve these unchallenged favorable dispositions.

### 3. Later NCIC message and additional detention

**Adopted:** This is a distinct claim concerning the post-arrest message, not merely a second description of initial arrest under the arson warrant. On the assumed facts Cooper knowingly supplied false homicide and no-bond information to procure otherwise unjustified additional physical detention, and the message caused that additional detention. A reasonable officer could not escape the established prohibition on materially deceptive procurement of an unreasonable seizure by communicating the deception electronically to custodial officers instead of presenting it to the warrant judge. Franks and Malley's pre-1993 rules supply the relevant concrete notice by obvious application; Lanier explains the lawful notice method in 1997 but is not itself 1993 notice.

This proposition requires the actual constitutional injury: a material lie used to procure or prolong an unreasonable physical seizure, personal responsibility and causal additional detention. It does not constitutionalize every erroneous entry, every state bond rule, every period of detention, or every inaccurate description of an otherwise lawful hold. The source-specific Fourth Amendment ground is sufficient for the assumed unreasonable pretrial custody; no universal rule extending the Fourth Amendment to every later restraint or every stage of a prosecution is adopted.

**Strongest immunity path:** The arson warrant already authorized custody, Texas controlled release and bond, and the Oklahoma bond amount did not itself confer a federal right to immediate release. Those are genuine limits. They would defeat this claim if the same detention remained lawful and would have occurred without the lie, or if Cooper had no causally effective role. They do not establish categorical noncausation on the accepted account that false homicide/no-bond information induced the extra hold. Independent authority to set bond does not prove an actual independent decision or eliminate material reliance as a matter of law.

**Strongest notice objection:** Franks concerns a warrant affidavit, while this claim concerns post-arrest communications and release. An abstract right to liberty or the message's bad purpose would be insufficient. The adopted ground is narrower and concrete: deliberate material factual deception procuring an otherwise unjustified physical detention. The communication channel and custodian's location do not create an apparent license for that conduct. No identical NCIC case, fixed circuit count or later notice formulation is required.

The District Court treated the NCIC allegations as within the general federal claims despite their lack of a separately specified count. On this supplied posture the Associates do not dismiss the claim sua sponte for failure to use a separate label, create a new claim, or resolve every pleading question. Actual authorship, authorization, participation, materiality and duration remain disputed. The report that detention lasted one to three days permits no fixed damages or duration finding.

### 4. Coercion, third-party use, constitutional source and 1993 notice

**Adopted review rule:** Historical facts concerning threats, promises, evidence lies and the proposed confession remain protected by Johnson. The legal effect of the properly assumed facts and the specificity of 1993 notice do not become unreviewable merely because the ultimate standard considers the totality. The state court's later voluntariness ruling and Michael's 1994 plea do not automatically bind Clanton; parties, issues, procedure and the governing preclusion rule have not been established. A Miranda waiver does not by itself decide voluntariness.

**Two substantive paths requiring separation:** First, coercively manufacturing a statement known to be false and using it to procure arrest may satisfy the already identified material-deception claim. Its viability does not require creation of a freestanding third-party privilege or elimination of Franks's culpability/materiality requirements. Second, using an involuntary third person's statement may be asserted to violate Clanton's own due process even without proof that Cooper knew its substantive assertions were false. That distinct theory requires analysis of her own deprivation, the applicable constitutional source and the particular use at issue. The second path cannot simply borrow the first path's clear notice or treat all involuntary statements as established knowing fabrications.

**Strongest plaintiff path:** Jackson, Blackburn, Spano and the pre-1993 circuit cases recognize that governmental use of coercively obtained evidence threatens the person against whom it is used, not only the person interrogated. The combination alleged here—fabricated physical evidence, a threatened twenty-five-year term unless Michael confessed, a promise of leniency and a suggested narrative incriminating Clanton—could distinguish ordinary questioning and make the deprivation an obvious application of settled protection. No factual-twin requirement defeats an actually obvious case.

**Strongest defense path:** Frazier rejects coercion from an evidence lie alone; Hutto and the actual totality rule reject an automatic prohibition on every promised benefit. Griffin, Garot and Fountain require a will-overbearing inducement in its circumstances, not merely use of the word promise. The pre-1993 third-party authorities supplied here concern trial/admissibility settings. Their protections cannot automatically be transferred to damages for another person's pretrial detention, particularly where a specific seizure protection may govern. The later Seventh Circuit Buckley (1994), Albright (1994), and 1996–97 notice formulations cannot themselves establish what was clearly unlawful in August 1993.

**Bounded conclusion:** Reject both a categorical third-party-rights dismissal and the circuit's categorical treatment of ultimate coercion as factual. Remand the distinct theory for the preserved legal issues under the proper allocation. The mandate requires assessment of the actual pre-conduct law and its application, including relevant lower-court authority and obvious applications, without deciding that the described trial authorities either necessarily establish or necessarily fail to establish notice. It does not adopt an independent due-process damages right, find coercion, declare an August 1993 violation obvious, or award immunity. That substantive restraint follows from the actual legal issues returned for adjudication; it is not a missing-source default.

## Proposed common proposition joins

All eight Associates support the following propositions at this exact level of generality:

1. **Immediate review:** A qualified-immunity appeal may determine legal sufficiency and clearly established law on the properly assumed facts, but may not reweigh whether the alleged conduct occurred. Ultimate constitutional voluntariness is not made wholly factual by its totality-of-circumstances standard. Correcting that allocation does not itself decide voluntariness or immunity.
2. **Warrant deception:** The assumed knowing or reckless materially false warrant affidavit falls within Franks and Malley's pre-August-1993 rule. Judicial issuance does not automatically immunize the affiant; a materially sufficient truthful account and other supported personal defenses remain available. Cooper's mere evidence-insufficiency attack is outside this interlocutory appeal.
3. **NCIC deception:** An officer has no categorical immunity for knowingly communicating material falsehoods to procure otherwise unjustified additional unreasonable physical detention. On that assumed account the change from warrant affidavit to custodial electronic message does not require an identical earlier case. Personal participation, materiality, constitutional injury and actual additional detention remain necessary; an existing warrant and another State's bond authority do not categorically decide those elements.
4. **Confession remand:** The distinct third-party-use claim must be separated from the already established knowing-deception theory. Preserved legal issues include the plaintiff's own constitutional protection, actual use of the statement, the legal effect of the assumed totality and particularized August 1993 notice. Neither the interrogated person's privilege alone, a district judge's coercion label, nor later authorities resolves those issues.
5. **Remedy and proof:** The mixed mandate preserves ordinary direct/circumstantial proof, source-specific elements, personal responsibility and conduct-date immunity. No automatic liability, immunity, trial, new hearing, revived claim or damages award follows. Johnson and Behrens constrain appellate jurisdiction; Crawford-El and Jeffries do not transfer retaliation burdens to this case.

These are direct common joins, not an aggregation of Albright's incompatible rationales. No general due-process fabrication right, universal Fourth Amendment displacement rule, categorical state-remedy bar, fixed circuit-consensus threshold or mandatory merits-first sequence is supported by this handoff. An opinion adding one would exceed at least some recorded commitments and require new modeling rather than being treated as a stylistic clarification.
