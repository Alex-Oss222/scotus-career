## 1. Validated posture and factual framing

The scheduled review concerns the Ninth Circuit's recall of its mandate in the first federal habeas case. Ordinary review of that recall judgment must remain distinct from review of a statutory permission or refusal to file a successive district-court application. Felker's bar on reviewing the latter is not itself a bar on the former. The supplied question is an analytical formulation, not a verified petition quotation. No unsupported grant date or broader grant wording is supplied.

The supported sequence is:

- Thompson was convicted in 1983 of first-degree murder and forcible rape. The jury separately found an intentional personal killing and the rape special circumstance supporting death eligibility. The California Supreme Court affirmed the convictions and special circumstance in 1988; its penalty dissent did not reject those guilt and eligibility determinations.
- The first federal application was filed in January 1990. After state exhaustion proceedings and a November 1993 federal evidentiary hearing, the District Court's March 28, 1995 order granted relief on rape and the rape special circumstance for ineffective assistance, invalidating the death sentence, while denying relief on murder. Its assessment that the two prosecutions differed principally in emphasis is an attributed federal-court conclusion, not a new factual determination here.
- The panel reversed the rape-related relief on June 19, 1996, denied rehearing and rejected the en banc suggestion on March 6, 1997, and issued the mandate denying relief on June 11. California then set execution for August 5. The March order reported circulation and no active judge's request for a vote; it does not establish that every judge affirmatively considered and rejected en banc review.
- The fourth state petition, filed July 3 and denied July 16, relied on an alleged Leitch parole account suggesting consensual intercourse. Thompson invoked that account in his July 22 recall motion and July 23 Rule 60(b) motion. The District Court denied the latter July 25, treating it as successive; the circuit denied the prisoner's recall motion July 28. Those classifications and credibility assessments are attributed lower-court decisions, not independent holdings of this Court.
- The full circuit voted July 30 to consider recall and heard argument August 1. Its subsequent August 3 action recalled the June 11 mandate, 53 days after issuance. The court described its action as its own initiative based on the claims and evidence in the first federal petition, expressly excluding the new claims or evidence in the prisoner's recall motion. It identified interrupted internal en banc consideration and the risk of injustice as its reasons. A clerk transition and a judge's failure to notice the panel opinion are supplied descriptions of the procedural failures, with the latter detail attributed to the lower dissent.

The circuit's stated waiting for state review presents a comity justification to assess, not proof that every day of delay was necessary. The source identifies no precise date on which each judge first knew the relevant facts, requested action, or could have obtained a timely vote. Do not invent that more detailed chronology. The capital sentence and approaching execution make consequences serious but prove neither that recall was lawful nor that Thompson satisfied any innocence standard.

The exact en banc decree separately affirmed the District Court's grant of the writ concerning the rape conviction and rape special circumstance and its vacation of the death sentence; remanded for further review of the first-degree murder conviction; and vacated the State's execution order. Thus the en banc action did not merely restore the District Court's entire original disposition: the murder component received further review, not an acquittal or final grant of relief. No retrial deadline or finer conditional-writ term appears in the supplied decree. A later disposition must account for each component it affects.

The Governor's refusal of clemency and strong evaluations are executive actions and opinions. They do not adjudicate the federal recall power, the gateway, or the constitutional claim. ## 2. Evidence that can be assessed, with its limits

The safe evidence extract permits comparative legal assessment; it does not authorize new historical findings. The State-side evidence includes semen consistent with Thompson's blood type, alleged restraint injuries, altered clothing, the gag, and informant accounts of confessions. Semen supports intercourse but does not by itself distinguish consent from force. The significance and timing of injuries must be considered against the expert dispute. Thompson's sleep account must be assessed with the other evidence; it is testimony, not an admitted reconstruction of the killing.

The first-petition material includes additional impeachment of Fink and Del Frate and Root's contrary forensic opinions. Existing trial impeachment was substantial: convictions, informant activity and benefits, dishonesty, drug use, pending charges, other reported confessions, and changes of account. The additional material nevertheless includes more specific reliability information, alleged benefits, Del Frate's earlier association with Leitch, and newspaper correspondence. It is not categorically irrelevant because it impeaches, and its incremental force is not automatically decisive because an informant was important.

The recitation inconsistently identifies the crushing injury as right-wrist and left-wrist; this supplement makes no correction. The quoted transcript's August 5, 1997 date is not used to invent another evidentiary hearing or reclassify the source's expressly described first-petition evidence.

## 3. Entering law and genuine alternative paths

No controlling in-world decision in the supplied law establishes a comprehensive standard for capital-habeas mandate recall. The existence of an exceptional judicial power and its permissible exercise are separate questions. The following are alternatives for independent modeling, not findings that any is adopted or satisfied.

**A. A genuine repair of the court's own process.** The court may be able to restore consideration lost through its internal procedural malfunction, within a restrained power over its own judgment. Hazel-Atlas Glass Co. v. Hartford-Empire Co., 322 U.S. 238, 244–250 (1944), confirms appellate power to recall a mandate to address a demonstrated corruption of the judicial process. Its actual predicate was established fraud, not ordinary error; the majority expressly preserved proper procedures for disputed facts. United States v. Ohio Power Co., 353 U.S. 98, 99 (1957), illustrates exceptional self-initiated reopening to avoid injustice in application of the Court's own procedures. Neither decision creates an automatic right to renewed merits review whenever a court regrets its judgment.

The strongest argument for this path is that a court-caused failure to complete its own review is categorically different from a prisoner asking to litigate a second application. The strongest objection is that the listed lapses and eventual delay may amount only to missed discretionary review, and labeling substantive reconsideration as institutional repair would erase finality. The model must evaluate both the actual malfunction and its causal relation to the missed review, the court's explanation for timing, the scope of correction, and the State's reliance. These are relevant considerations grounded in the supported dispute, not an already enacted multifactored Supreme Court test. Fraud is not alleged or found merely by invoking Hazel-Atlas.

**B. Exceptional recall to avert a demonstrated substantive injustice.** A court could require a stronger justification when recall reopens the merits of completed habeas review. Schlup and Sawyer provide established, distinct innocence gateways in their own successive, abusive, or defaulted-claim settings. Extending an appropriate gateway to this recall would require an articulated legal reason. Neither gateway already governs every exercise of recall power. Conversely, ordinary Strickland prejudice or disagreement with a panel cannot simply be renamed innocence. A model choosing this path must identify the new recall proposition it would adopt, its scope, and why the procedural-repair alternative does or does not remain available.

**C. Refusal of recall on the supported procedural circumstances without a universal gateway.** A Justice may accept exceptional power but conclude that these internal lapses, the timing, and the stated justification are insufficient. Such a judgment need not hold that all recalls require actual innocence. Its strongest objection is that it may substitute appellate judgment for legitimate correction by the court best placed to understand its internal failure, particularly where the action concerns an original application and serious alleged constitutional error. A refusal must explain why the established circumstances fail the selected standard; a research gap is not proof against Thompson.

**D. Statutory successive-application analysis.** If the action is legally a second or successive application, the amended §2244(b) conditions have independent force: repeated claims and new claims face different requirements; the new-fact route requires both due diligence and the stated clear-and-convincing showing; authorization is distinct from ultimate eligibility. But a court's reopening of its own first-application judgment is not automatically a new district-court application. The rejected Rule 60(b) motion does not settle that classification for the separate sua sponte recall. Carlisle's prohibition on using inherent authority to circumvent an actual rule restriction supports examining substance; it does not supply an otherwise unestablished restriction. Felker preserves provision-specific timing and channels.

**E. Bounded further consideration.** If an articulated legal mistake in the recall framework or an indispensable undecided predicate prevents affirmance or reversal on an adequate ground, vacatur and a limited remand are possible. Carriger supplies an example of correcting an inconsistent gateway and state-finding analysis without deciding the evidence's ultimate effect. It does not compel remand in every close evidentiary case, nor license a hearing merely because research originals are unavailable. A disposition on recall authority may leave the underlying constitutional merits unadjudicated.

## 4. Gateways, merits, and former §2254(d) must remain separate

For an otherwise barred challenge to a conviction, Schlup requires reliable new evidence assessed with the whole probative record showing that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. New evidence for this inquiry can be new relative to the trial even if already included in the first federal petition; it need not necessarily have arisen after the mandate. Its availability does not establish the separate successive-application due-diligence requirement.

For innocence of capital eligibility, Sawyer requires clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find eligibility under state law. The rape conviction and the rape special circumstance are distinct legal components even though their evidence overlaps. An offense-innocence attack and an eligibility-only attack therefore require explicit identification. Mitigation concerning discretionary sentence selection does not satisfy Sawyer. Defeating the rape special circumstance would not by itself acquit Thompson of murder. Gateway passage proves neither ineffective assistance nor entitlement to a writ.

Strickland's deficient-performance and reasonable-probability-of-prejudice inquiry governs the ineffective-assistance merits if a lawful route reaches them. The District Court's prejudice conclusion, the panel's contrary assessment, and a later court's view that the verdict is unreliable do not substitute for the different gateway burden. The record allows serious arguments on both the additional impeachment's cumulative force and the remaining physical evidence. A result must explain their combined effect under the chosen rule rather than announce that all impeachment is cumulative or all physical evidence conclusive.

Carriger's April 16 controlling temporal construction extends Lindh to an already pending capital application outside established chapter 154 qualification: the identified ordinary amendments to §2254(d) and replacement factual presumption do not govern. Thompson's January 1990 first application is pre-enactment. No California chapter 154 qualification is established by this packet. This does not exempt every later filing from every AEDPA provision or decide a hearing provision's separate temporal reach.

Former §2254(d) protects qualifying state factual determinations made after the requisite merits hearing and evidenced by adequate written indicia. Under paragraph (8), the pertinent produced state record must be considered as a whole in determining fair support. Marshall v. Lonberger's historical/credibility protection remains, subject to the statute's exceptions. A different plausible inference or later evidence does not alone erase the protection, while a prior finding cannot conclusively decide evidence it never considered. The whole-record gateway and state-finding inquiries remain distinct.

The packet does not identify a particular state postconviction credibility finding on each new impeachment item or Root opinion. Do not invent one, treat a federal panel's conclusion as a state finding, or make the jury verdict conclusive on the augmented record. A decisive reliance on a specific §2254(d) finding requires identifying its actual text, hearing, support, and scope. This is a path-specific source requirement, not a determination that qualifying findings do not exist.
