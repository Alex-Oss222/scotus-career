## Comparison and preserved scope

The panel upheld the presented constitutional validity of §3626(b), rejected the district court's vacatur, treated the decrees as surviving contracts or judgments enforceable in state court, and required an evidentiary opportunity if plaintiffs continued seeking federal relief. Its constitutional analysis repeatedly depended on limiting federal jurisdiction while preserving the underlying decree obligations. That dependency is the material difference from current controlling law; it is not a historical position attributable to any Supreme Court Justice.

Rouse, decided January 15, 1998, supplies the direct intervening rule. Section 3626(b)(2) independently permits termination of covered prospective relief granted without the substantive need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness findings; ritual statutory wording is unnecessary when the actual findings suffice. The amended §3626(b)(3) preserves relief only on written, record-based findings that it remains necessary to correct a current **and** ongoing federal violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn, and is the least intrusive necessary means. Rufo's separate equitable significant-change route is not an additional statutory prerequisite. Termination reaches future judicial duties themselves, without erasing completed adjudications, payments or other lawful historical effects. Transferring enforcement of those same duties to state court cannot evade that statutory termination. A genuinely independent state-law contract or accrued claim is neither presumed nor adjudicated away.

The source supports the distinct evidentiary disposition. Paragraphs 71–73 report that the district court considered itself confined to the earlier record, treated that record's silence as decisive, and did not receive current evidence. The United States supported supplemental evidence when necessary to decide termination. Rouse preserved a proper opportunity to present material proof but required no hearing based merely on speculation or missing research originals. Correcting Benjamin's reported legal restriction on the record therefore applies Rouse rather than enlarging it. The remand permits the necessary current presentation and a real §3626(b)(3) determination; it establishes neither a current violation nor entitlement to retain any particular decree term, and creates no hearing requirement for every termination motion.

Footnote 5 supports the applicability of the immediate-termination route to these decrees without requiring the separate two-year route. Paragraph 74 and footnote 3 establish the lower appellate stay and its reported terms. The commitments properly leave its treatment to lawful proceedings under the mandate; they create no Supreme Court stay, elapsed statutory clock, automatic-stay adjudication, deadline or new filing event. The source's original current-**or**-ongoing statutory recitation is historical text, superseded for this pending case by the November 26, 1997 amendment already reflected in Rouse and the preflight.
