## Component reconciliation

**Marital privacy.** Retain affirmance on the precise absence of the claim's voluntary-intimacy premise: the wife initiated the act to distract an ongoing armed assault while Thompson reloaded after the loaded handgun failed to discharge. Physical initiation and marriage do not establish the asserted voluntary intimate choice. This agrees with the lower outcome but deliberately does not adopt its general suggestion that every protected marital act must be judicially assessed as furthering marriage. Existing autonomy law supplies the narrower ground already frozen. Whether Article 125 may punish genuinely consensual private marital conduct remains open. The ruling creates no military exemption from constitutional review.

**Closed-session contact.** Retain the acknowledged Article 39(b) violation and the Government's burden to rebut Allbee's presumption of prejudice under Article 59(a). The affirmative reported record matters: findings had already been reached; the questions concerned entering lesser included offenses on the worksheet; the answers gave no substantive deliberation instruction and would not have been objectionable in open session; a verbatim record was furnished to the parties at trial. The lack of an identified defense objection or clarification reinforces that account but does not replace it or shift the burden. The lower court's nonconstitutional classification is not expanded into a rule covering all judge-member contacts or a decision of an independently unpresented Sixth Amendment issue.

**Remedy.** Retain affirmance of the reviewed judgment on both issues, leaving the convictions and sentence undisturbed without a remand, new evidentiary hearing or broader Article 125 ruling. The reported sentence is a bad-conduct discharge, thirty months' confinement, total forfeitures and reduction to the lowest enlisted grade. No distinct sentencing error is presented or decided.
