## Material alternative paths and strongest objections

The prisoners' strongest Article III route treats a final consent decree as binding adjudication and the targeted termination mechanism as legislative decision of its result. Rouse, Benjamin and Gavin foreclose that route for prospective obligations under the actual preservation conditions; Congress changes law while the court determines facts and relief. The reservations for completed effects and independent accrued claims answer the scope objection without approving general reopening of completed merits judgments. Each of the eight Justices directly joined the current controlling rule and has no supported changed premise here.

The State's strongest immediate-termination route relies on the absence of original findings and the statutory entitlement. That is not an independently sufficient ground to skip the preservation condition. The constitutional-only denial leaves that condition unadjudicated. Each Justice therefore rejects immediate wholesale termination on this record. Conversely, retaining the entire decree indefinitely because current facts have not yet been found would evade the termination inquiry. A bounded remand applies both provisions without choosing the missing facts.

Possible narrower disposition: affirm the Article III rejection and simply leave the existing appellate remand intact, rather than create a new merits hearing or fact inquiry. The frozen formulation does precisely that, adding only the already-governing amended conditions and legally necessary evidentiary opportunity. All eight accept it. None requires a separate writing or a change to current law to support the judgment. No conditional forecast is counted as a vote.
