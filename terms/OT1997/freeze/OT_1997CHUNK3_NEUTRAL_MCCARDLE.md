## Verified record and lower judgment

Kearse wrote for Kearse, Calabresi, and visiting District Judge Munson; no separate writing appears in the complete reporter opinion. The panel affirmed the judgment and supplemental judgment, each side to bear its own appellate costs. The conduct occurred March 24, 1992; suit began in 1994. The published opinion reproduces material testimony, the answer, Rule 50 motion statements, and fee-request particulars. The full underlying trial transcript and motion papers are not independently recovered.

The jury rejected the unlawful-stop claim but found an unlawful search and awarded no compensatory damages. The court entered $1 nominal damages, refused a punitive-damages instruction, and awarded $0.33 in fees and costs on a $5,330 request. An adverse general verdict on the stop does not establish every historical fact in Haddad's favor for the separate search claim. The judgment-as-a-matter-of-law inquiry takes the search evidence, credibility assessments, and reasonable inferences in McCardle's favor; law is reviewed de novo.

McCardle testified that, after accusing her of buying drugs, Haddad placed her in the rear of his patrol car and searched her vehicle for about ten minutes, thrusting his head and most of his body inside and looking beneath seats and into compartments. His question about a badge kept in the glove compartment supported an inference that he also opened it. Haddad denied any search and testified that he looked from outside, saw nothing suspicious, and arranged a private tow because her license had expired. He said she was not arrested, that he took no custody of her car, and that he did not search to find a gun or protect his safety. Counsel withdrew the consent justification. The panel found no probable-cause, arrest, inventory, consent, or properly supported protective-search basis for judgment as a matter of law.

**Preservation is claim-specific.** The answer expressly pleaded qualified immunity, so this is not a failure to plead the defense. The opinion reports no immunity summary-judgment motion, relevant other pretrial motion, discovery or conference invocation, pretrial-memorandum reference, or proposed immunity instruction. At the close of the defense evidence, counsel requested judgment on the **stop** on the merits or immunity. Counsel also argued the **search** was lawful but did not raise immunity on that claim. The post-trial motion added immunity for the search. The circuit held that new ground unpreserved under Rule 50 and found no demonstrated manifest injustice warranting exceptional review. It did not adjudicate ultimate qualified immunity on the merits.

The separate punitive ruling applied Smith v. Wade's evil motive or intent/reckless or callous indifference requirement and found no sufficient supporting evidence. Failure of qualified immunity was not equated with punitive culpability. The fee ruling questioned mechanical comparison with an unspecified damages demand, but upheld the nominal award using the historical Farrar no-fee reasoning. That latter rationale must be checked against the different governing **simulated Farrar** rule below if fees are lawfully reached.

Rule 50(b) renews a motion made at the close of all evidence and the legal questions it raised; it supplies no general new-ground post-verdict entitlement. Its ten-day period must not be replaced by a modern period. Ordinary preservation is distinct from whether qualified immunity substantively exists. Gomez v. Toledo, 446 U.S. 635, 640 (1980), treats immunity as an affirmative defense. The published decision's record recitations adequately establish the described motion history; missing originals establish no additional default.
* **Immunity:** Harlow, Anderson, and Malley supply objective, particularized clearly-established-law protection at the March 24, 1992 conduct date. Elder requires all relevant law on a **preserved** issue and does not revive omitted grounds or facts. Crawford-El governs motive proof and retains ordinary procedure; it does not excuse Rule 50 default or impose motive as a Fourth Amendment element. Fletcher preserves distinct liability and personal defenses; its prosecutorial-function holding supplies no immunity for this officer. Johnson/Behrens/Clanton/Smith preserve legal-factual separation but concern interlocutory appeals, whereas this is review after final judgment. No collateral-order restriction should be mechanically substituted for final-judgment review.
* **Search law if necessary:** Michigan v. Long, 463 U.S. 1032, 1049–53 & nn.14,16 (1983), requires specific articulable grounds reasonably supporting danger and possible access to weapons, with a protective scope. It does not license a search during every valid stop. The officer's denial of a search does not itself establish its illegality; the verdict and complete plaintiff-favorable evidentiary record control. The separate stop victory does not prove an unarticulated safety belief or erase the search verdict. Later cases explain current review but cannot supply retrospective 1992 notice.
* **Fees if within scope:** Governing Farrar recognizes nominal prevailing-party eligibility and requires an explained Hensley assessment of actual success and unrelated unsuccessful work. Its remand does not categorically forbid a sufficient alternative-ground affirmance in another case.
