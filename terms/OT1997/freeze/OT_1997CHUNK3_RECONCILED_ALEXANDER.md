## Governing grounds, strongest alternatives and limits

### Claim-specific power and the controlled personhood question

The mother alleges an identified malpractice action and actual refusal of letters by the named Monmouth County surrogate. Her injury is the present legal barrier to that asserted remedial route, not merely a past pregnancy or speculative future pregnancy. Removing the barrier could provide meaningful partial relief despite unresolved malpractice and damages. At the pleading stage those supported allegations suffice against the responsible surrogate; a summary-judgment affidavit requirement would be misplaced. Nothing in the record establishes later settlement, expiration, mootness or full relief. No official title or consent order automatically establishes every additional defendant's enforcement connection, and no relief is ordered against one.

Roe, 410 U.S. 113, 156–159, expressly excludes the unborn from “person” in the Fourteenth Amendment. Its pages 161–162 discuss prenatal tort and property protections, including interests contingent on live birth, without making all state protections constitutional personhood. The full directly relevant passage and notes 51–66 were checked. This survives the simulated Casey reservation: leaving fetal personhood undecided in Casey neither repeats nor overrules Roe's separate holding. The mother has an independent live challenge to the remedial classification; the exact, already-controlled personhood issue can be resolved within Steel Co.'s preserved Norton/Avrech setting without inventing every proposed estate or class capacity. This is not a general weak-claim exception allowing assumed jurisdiction over unsettled merits.

The strongest contrary personhood route emphasizes the wanted, near-term pregnancy and absence of conflict with the mother's abortion decision. It may support broader state tort protection and makes the maternal claims independently important. It does not itself change Roe's express constitutional category. A judicial recognition of constitutional personhood before birth would require supported limitation or overruling of that proposition; biological development allegations, state common-law vocabulary, a different view of Roe, or a lower judge's concurrence do not accomplish it. The ruling neither determines when life begins nor forbids lawful prenatal protections.

### Maternal liberty requires the actual constitutional trigger

The choice to continue a pregnancy is not diminished by treating this as a remedial case. Casey's retained foundation, full applicable six-step rule, Fargo and Miller require an actual duty and attributable interference before their heightened access justification burdens arise. On these allegations the statutes impose no reproductive prohibition, compelled termination or treatment, delay of necessary care, disclosure or third-party veto. Generalized reduction of deterrence from denying one additional damages route does not itself establish state interference with the protected choice or relationship. The existing parental tort action is relevant context but neither an award nor proof of complete protection.

The strongest contrary argument is that excluding protection against prenatal injury burdens continuation of a wanted pregnancy. It must still identify the challenged law's causal operation on the protected interest; the subject of pregnancy alone supplies no heightened trigger. No original medical paper is needed to resolve that legal failure on the pleaded operation. The Court leaves the outer scope of prenatal family liberty open and does not declare that every tort limitation can never burden a protected right. Valot's distinction between ordinary equality and a distinct penalty on a protected exercise remains intact.

### Actual equality review separates wrongful death from survival

Beach permits reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds and imperfect classifications without formal findings. Actual Heller requires those grounds to relate to the disadvantage imposed; its invalid proof disparity does not erase its separate sustained family-party classification or create an empirical-certainty requirement. Romer prevents treating the existence of general tort protection as an automatic answer to a separate disability. Lack of a fundamental wrongful-death right and nonsuspect classification identify the review standard; they do not themselves establish validity.

**Wrongful death:** The New Jersey cause compensates survivors for the decedent's expected pecuniary contributions, including qualifying support, services and advice, with separately authorized medical, hospital and funeral expenses. Live birth supplies an administrable event identifying the independently existing decedent from whom those derivative interests are measured. The State may distinguish that derivative recovery from parents' own injuries associated with loss of pregnancy. A newborn's likely contributions are uncertain and may resemble those of a near-term stillborn; the source itself recognizes that uncertainty. The ground does not require a contrary medical or economic finding. Ordinary review permits the boundary although a more inclusive state remedy would also be lawful.

**Survival:** The cause continues the decedent's own accrued injury through an estate, including conscious pain and suffering and relevant state-law loss-of-enjoyment and expense rules. The live-birth condition identifies the independent legal holder whose cause continues after death, including prenatal injury carried into that person's life. That relationship to ownership and transmission of the cause differs from the survivors' future-contribution rationale. The State need not recognize an independently transmissible fetal estate merely because it recognizes a prenatal injury carried into a live-born person's cause. This is a legal allocation of the decedent-based remedy, not a finding that fetal pain is impossible or injury begins only after birth.

The **strongest maternal alternative** is vacatur for separate review under actual Heller/Romer: a near-term stillbirth and an infant surviving minutes can present equivalent causal proof, pain and future support; a bare statement that the State may draw limits merely repeats the exclusion; and the Carey threshold leaves parents without compensation in some negligent-loss cases. Survival is the sharper comparison because its principal pain damages may already have accrued before birth. Each objection is accepted in testing the model. A claim-specific explanation is necessary; “limiting liability” alone, unknowable fetal proof, no fundamental right, or the availability of Giardina alone is insufficient.

The retained affirmance rests instead on the relationship of live birth to the independent decedent and the distinct derivative or transmitted interests each cause defines. Birth is not chosen merely because the existing classification uses it: it is an ascertainable event of independent postnatal existence, and the causes attach legal consequences to a decedent's life and claims. Ordinary review does not require replacing that event with a gestational threshold or case-specific medical proof whenever adjacent cases resemble each other. The State could adopt a broader remedy; the Constitution does not select it on this record. No finding that the legislature actually used this explanation is made, because Beach permits a sufficiently relevant conceivable ground. No universal proof-development remand follows.

Giardina authoritatively excludes stillbirth from wrongful death and recognizes parents' direct tort injuries. It did not decide survival. The federal courts' parallel survival construction was undisputed on the lower appeal and is used only as that express premise. The federal challenge fails on the assumed exclusion, so no new state-law construction, certification or state remedial rewrite is necessary. Maternal distress is neither the same beneficiary interest nor the same measure as the two excluded statutory recoveries; its availability is supplementary context, not a sufficient substitute by itself.
