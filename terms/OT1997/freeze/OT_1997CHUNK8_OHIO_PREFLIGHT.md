## Validated posture and lower judgment

The case reaches the Court by review of the Sixth Circuit's reversal of the District Court's summary judgment for the Forest Service. The Sixth Circuit remanded for further proceedings consistent with its conclusion that the plan was arbitrary and capricious and contrary to NFMA. The present question concerns ripeness of this challenge, not a free-ranging appeal deciding all substantive forest-management issues. The recorded request is to vacate and secure dismissal as unripe.

The lower opinion establishes these points:

- The 1988 ten-year Wayne National Forest plan identified 126,107 acres from which timber could be removed, an annual allowable volume of 7.5 million board feet, and eighty-percent even-aged management, generally involving clearcutting. These describe what the plan permits or contemplates; they are not findings that all designated timber will inevitably be harvested.
- Administrative review concluded in 1990 when the Chief of the Forest Service affirmed adoption. Final adoption is a substantial fact supporting review, but does not by itself decide either Article III or ripeness.
- The majority accepted aesthetic and recreational injury, reasoned that the plan can predetermine future management, and worried that delaying review would defeat meaningful citizen participation. It held that plaintiffs need not await a particular project when their grievance concerns the overall plan. These are the lower court's legal reasons, not adjudicated Supreme Court rules.
- On the merits the majority found artificial narrowing of alternatives and improper preference for clearcutting. It relied on the record's treatment of recreation, surrounding land and diversity, as well as broader institutional-bias observations. The latter observations are not independently established underlying facts.
- Batchelder concurred in the result and accepted the record-specific NFMA violation. She preferred *Marita*'s standing/ripeness approach: an adopted plan directs management unless amended. She expressly rejected the majority's largely undocumented institutional broadside as unnecessary speculation. This concurrence must not be omitted or represented as a dissent on justiciability.

The quoted statutory condition in the lower majority is section 1604(g)(3)(F)(v)'s requirement that the relevant practices be consistent with protection of soil, watershed, fish, wildlife, recreation and aesthetic resources, and regeneration of the timber resource. The next statement that even-aged management is contemplated only in exceptional circumstances is the Sixth Circuit's interpretation. It is not text quoted from that provision. Likewise, the optimum-method discussion in the lower opinion must not be collapsed into subsection (v)'s separate protection condition. This threshold proceeding need not approve or reject either substantive interpretation.

## Material neutral additions and qualifications

The clean packet establishes that the plan makes harvesting more likely but does not itself authorize cutting. Before authorizing logging the Service must identify a site and harvesting method, ensure consistency with the plan, provide notice and an opportunity to be heard, perform environmental analysis, and reach a project decision subject to administrative and judicial challenge. The Service must revise the plan as appropriate. The lower concurrence's direction-unless-amended observation and these later decisions can both be true. Neither may be deleted to make the threshold result automatic.

The supported present challenge attacks the substantive logging/clearcutting choices embodied in the plan, including the alleged arbitrary assumptions that produced them. An allegation that the planning process was biased or its alternatives artificially narrowed does not, without more, establish an independently pleaded denial of a specific procedural entitlement. The lower opinion and packet establish administrative participation; they establish no particular omitted NEPA statement, denied notice, or excluded comment. No such claim, concession or waiver is invented.

A properly presented procedural-right claim can have a different point of injury. Under controlling *Lujan*, a procedure protecting a threatened concrete personal interest need not be shown certain to change the ultimate decision. That rule neither establishes a procedural claim here nor dispenses with personal injury. It must survive any formulation of the ripeness holding.

No member affidavit is manufactured from the lower court's standing discussion. Nor does the operator's lack of the complete filing record prove that the litigants failed to submit one. A bounded ripeness disposition can leave the separate constitutional standing issue and APA final-action issue undecided; it cannot declare the members strangers to this forest merely because affidavits are not reproduced in the supplied sources.

## Law-entering snapshot and competing paths

**Path A — present substantive challenge is premature.** Apply *Abbott*'s fitness and hardship inquiry, as currently applied unanimously in *Texas*. Specific project proposals would show which places, methods, resource conflicts and plan provisions actually matter. On the supplied record no cutting occurs before intervening reviewable decisions. Delaying this substantive challenge need not expose the plaintiffs to completed harvesting before they can contest the project and the plan provisions on which it depends. A plan's adoption and planning influence alone need not establish sufficient present hardship. *Toilet Goods* prevents equating final agency action with ripeness. *Bennett* remains distinguishable by its completed legal constraint and supported connection to plaintiffs' present water loss.

The strongest objection is that final plan adoption channels later projects and the present attack is directed precisely at plan-level choices. *Bennett* forbids dismissing an ongoing constraint merely because another agency or a later decision retains discretion. Path A therefore cannot rest on a universal rule that any additional decision defeats review. It requires this record's combination of later project choices, continuing review, no present logging authorization, and the usefulness of application-specific facts. It must preserve review of an independently consequential planning restriction or properly established procedural injury.

**Path B — hear the plan-level challenge now.** The final adopted plan's direction of later management and completed administrative review supply a substantial argument that review concerns a concluded agency choice. Under *Bennett*, retained discretion does not necessarily undo finality or causation. Batchelder's narrower concurrence supports this route without the majority's speculation about institutional motives. A pure legal defect in the plan itself might require no later site facts.

The strongest objection is that finality is not ripeness, the presented challenge concerns the amount and methods of future harvesting, and its alleged practical harm remains subject to the concrete project decisions described in the packet. *Texas* and *Toilet Goods* require attention to what withholding review actually does. The record does not identify the present compliance burden that justified review in *New York* or *Pic-A-State*. If Path B is selected, a merits ruling cannot be supplied from the question's analytical formulation: the necessary review scope and substantive record would first require validation. A conclusion that the controversy is ripe alone would leave those separate merits matters for the proper proceedings.

**Procedural distinction, not an invented third claim.** A specific failure to provide a procedure protecting concrete interests could be reviewable at plan adoption, subject to its own prerequisites. The present materials do not establish that distinct claim. It is a limitation on the scope of either path, not an alternative merits disposition of a nonexistent pleaded count.
