## 1. Fixed record, scope and governing law

The jury convicted Bird under §248(a)(1) after he threw a bottle at Dr. Herring's approaching car, shattered its windshield and threatened to kill him as he entered America's Women Clinic. Bird otherwise does not contest guilt under the statute. There was no physical injury. Herring resided in Texas, and the prosecution supplied no clinic-specific proof of interstate patients, staff or supplies. The conviction cannot be supported by inventing that proof or an additional element.

The September 14, 1995 sentence imposed one year in prison followed by one year of supervised release, restitution of $820.67 and a $50 assessment. The special condition excludes Bird from within 1,000 feet of any abortion clinic, specifically America's Women Clinic. It is not merely a no-contact condition protecting Herring or his residence. Prior clinic and provider-home trespass and resistance to restrictions are supported lower-court reasons. They are not a finding that every conceivable restraint is necessary. The November 13, 1997 appellate mandate does not establish when imprisonment or supervision began or ended, whether either was stayed or tolled, or a surviving consequence on the event date.

The verified petition principally presents Commerce, §5 and abortion-right questions and identifies the supervision condition as not pertinent to that petition. The resumed authorization separately includes supervision. The remaining lower-appeal issues—familial classification, facial overbreadth, vagueness and the written judgment's descriptive label—are preserved as record context, not silently added as independent reviewed questions. The present commitments do not enlarge that scope. There is no independent RFRA claim.

The operative sources are:

- **Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects**, present operation June 30, 1997, with Lopez's April 26, 1995 architecture and American Life League's November 13, 1995 direct service-market application. Identify the enacted class and an eligible interstate mechanism before aggregation; require reasonable identifiable fit and an enforceable federal boundary. General crime, health or productivity costs do not supply a missing connection. No mandatory interstate crossing by each actor, universal jurisdictional-element requirement or categorical immunity for local noneconomic conduct follows.
- **FACE conduct predicates and responsibility for civil relief**, November 13, 1995. Section 248(a)(1) requires force, threat of force or physical obstruction; intentional injury, intimidation, interference or attempt; and the specified service-related purpose. Interference restricts movement, intimidation requires reasonable apprehension of bodily harm, and obstruction makes ingress or egress impassable or passage unreasonably difficult or hazardous. Protected expression remains protected. The parent/guardian exception removes every FACE penalty and civil remedy only for activity directed exclusively at the actor's own minor; it creates neither an outsider exemption nor immunity under other law.
- **Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review**, current wording through Lebron, February 21, 1995. Rule 14.1(a)'s strong presumption excludes distinct unpresented questions absent supported exceptional circumstances. A supporting argument for an included claim is different from an additional independent claim. The duty to notice jurisdiction is separate.
- **Spencer**, March 3, 1998. An actual continuing redressable interest must exist after the challenged restraint ends; consequences of a conviction cannot simply be presumed for a different challenged adjudication. Spencer's decided setting was an expired parole-revocation sentence, not every direct sentencing appeal. Its underlying Article III inquiry nevertheless requires identification of the particular challenged component and surviving injury. Speculative future discretionary use is insufficient. The capable-of-repetition exception requires both insufficient duration for full review and a reasonable expectation that this claimant will again encounter the action. Delay alone supplies neither jurisdiction nor an exception.
- **18 U.S.C. §3583(d)**. An additional condition must be reasonably related to the offense and offender characteristics and the specified deterrence, protection and treatment considerations; deprive liberty no more than reasonably necessary for deterrence, public protection and treatment; and accord with pertinent Sentencing Commission policy statements. The requirements are distinct. A permissible protective objective does not dispense with the liberty limit, and the limit is not a universal least-restrictive-means command.
- **Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction**, Madsen, June 30, 1994, with Schenck's confined counseling qualification, February 19, 1997. An injunction must burden no more speech than necessary for its established remedial interest. Its greater-than-ordinary precision requirement and rejection of unsupported territorial restraints inform fit, but a criminal supervised-release condition is not automatically governed by the identical injunction test. Neither precedent establishes a universal permissible distance.
- **28 U.S.C. §2106**, effective well before this event, permits appropriate further proceedings in a case lawfully before the Court. It does not confer merits jurisdiction over a moot component. **Hinds**, April 6, 1998, preserves the independent work of §844(i)'s property-use language; it neither inserts that language into FACE nor alters American Life League's commerce holding.

## 3. Enhanced comparison: authority, scope and threshold

### Commerce: direct interference versus the proposed local-act distinction

**Path supporting C:** Apply American Life League to targeted violence against the physician's provision of clinic services. The strongest objection is that this prosecution lacks the clinic-specific patients, staff and supplies evidence described in American Life League and concerns a local noneconomic attack. The objection identifies a real record difference but does not overcome the precedent's class-level holding: it requires no separate crossing by each actor or each transaction. The service-directed predicate supplies the operative connection; its absence in a hypothetical different offense could matter. It is not enough to invoke any victim's eventual effect on commerce.

**Proposed contrary path:** Treat this application as outside the class and reverse for insufficient interstate proof, or demand a new trial with such proof. Its strongest support is Lopez's connection-first rule, Hinds's insistence on enacted predicates, and the lower dissent's warning against federalization of ordinary crime. Its decisive difficulty is that it substitutes an individual-crossing or clinic-use requirement for the very service-market mechanism each Associate adopted in American Life League. Hinds construes different language and provides no omitted FACE element. The lower dissent's proposed fungibility, comprehensive-regulatory-program and local-subject exclusions are not conditions of current law. On this supplied record this is not a second surviving disposition under current law; adopting it would require an expressly reasoned distinction or reconsideration that these commitments do not support.

Justice-specific consequence: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas can and do retain their Lopez joins while applying their later service-market joins. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer need not adopt a broader aggregate-cost rationale to reach the same disposition; their own American Life League joins suffice. ### Scope: necessary reasoning versus new independent adjudications

**Selected path N:** Resolve the accepted Commerce question and leave unnecessary §5 and protected-choice contentions without a merits ruling; leave the independent lower-only claims outside review. Strongest counterargument: a general constitutional challenge and the same criminal judgment arguably bring all supporting objections within reach, while a respondent may defend on an alternative ground. The answer is component-specific: an alternative source of authority may be available in principle but need not be adjudicated after the sufficient ground; unrelated facial and label challenges do not become fairly included merely because they target the same judgment. Nothing in this record establishes the exceptional-review circumstances needed to expand them.

**Broader path tested:** Reach §5, reconsider protected choice or decide each lower-appeal argument to dispose comprehensively of the statute's validity. Its strongest support is practical completeness and prior adversarial treatment. Its barriers are the absence of necessity or supported exceptional circumstances and the difference between a constitutional argument within an accepted claim and a distinct request for relief. Stevens's Madsen and Caspari scope objections especially favor N; the seven other Associates' scope authorities identified in the table also do not authorize the proposed extension. The separate supervision authorization supplies scope for that question alone, not for every independent constitutional claim.

If §5 ever becomes necessary in a materially changed posture, City of Boerne's June 25, 1997 **Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement** rule permits genuinely preventive or remedial legislation but forbids replacement of the underlying right. The guarantee, constitutional injury and remedial relationship would need examination. The lower dissent's private-conduct discussion is not a substitute for that current rule. No §5 merits commitment, state-failure finding or new abortion-right rule is frozen here.

### Supervision: inquiry versus presumed liveness, presumed mootness or immediate merits

**Selected path J:** Separate the conviction from the special condition and obtain the jurisdictional determination needed for the latter. Its strongest counterargument is that an otherwise live direct criminal appeal and a possibly ongoing sentence make further inquiry unnecessary, or that the supplied sentencing dates make expiration apparent. Neither resolves actual commencement, tolling, restraint or redressability. A live conviction does not authorize advisory adjudication of every expired component, while simple date addition cannot establish expiration. The limited appellate-component vacatur leaves the sentencing restriction unaltered until a lawful determination and avoids issuing a merits mandate without authority.

**Alternative lawful procedural path:** Retain this component and first conduct a bounded jurisdictional inquiry at the reviewing court, reserving its disposition until actual continuing interest is resolved. Its strongest advantage is avoiding vacatur of an appellate merits ruling that may have been valid when entered. Its disadvantage is retaining a separate fact-dependent sentencing issue when a focused remand can determine status and, if live, apply the existing condition law in the ordinary sentencing channel. Each Associate prefers J but can join this narrower inquiry-first formulation if assembly confines it to actual Article III facts and leaves merits reserved. **Non-surviving alternatives:** Immediate affirmance or reversal of the condition on an assumed live controversy; dismissal based on presumed expiration; presumed collateral consequences because the conviction survives; or an automatic capable-of-repetition exception because the supervision term is short. ## 4. Conditional merits comparison and individual join barriers

It must identify the unsupported deprivation or actual constitutional defect. No replacement distance, permission to threaten or obstruct, alteration of the valid conviction, general sentence reopening or new evidence to prove criminal guilt follows.

## Enhanced alternatives by component

### Applicable law

**Former-law path.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would decide that the chapter 153 merits and factual-treatment amendments at issue do not govern this already pending application. Each joined Ginsburg's Lindh opinion. Its decisive statutory contrast is between chapter 154's express pending-case clause and the omission of that instruction from the ordinary amendments enacted alongside it. That comparison does not depend on whether the already filed application concerns a capital sentence. The proper extension is to the ordinary chapter 153 treatment of this application, not to an exemption from all of AEDPA.

**Strongest contrary path.** Lindh expressly resolved noncapital proceedings, and Congress separately regulated capital habeas in chapter 154. A court must therefore test a capital case against the actual statutory provisions rather than enlarge Lindh by a label. The five answer that chapter 154's special reach requires a qualifying counsel system; capital status alone cannot supply it. No qualification is established here. The original federal application also preceded enactment, unlike Felker's authorization proceeding. Their joins stop at these identified ordinary amendments and do not decide every later procedural event or the operation of every successive provision.

**Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** Their published join in Thomas's Felker temporal concurrence identifies the activity regulated by the particular provision and rejects a universal timing answer. Scalia's Lindh dissent, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, supplies more specific personal support for applying amended §2254(d) to a still-pending adjudication: it regulates present relief authority, and chapter 154's distinct State-qualified scheme does not, in that view, reliably imply chapter 153's contrary reach. The controlling majority rejected that statutory inference in Lindh's noncapital setting; their dissent cannot erase that holding. Here they would not resolve the separate capital question merely to deny a claim that fails under former law. The strongest objection is that a habeas opinion should identify governing law before discussing the gateway. Their sufficient disposition expressly assumes the former regime, applies its controlling probability standard, and relies on no restrictive amendment; the outcome is independent of the disputed transition. They would not join a broad declaration that a pre-enactment filing alone disables every AEDPA restriction. If a later disposition requires affirmative relief or new fact development, that necessary provision's temporal operation must first be decided. Their published present-relief-authority rationale supplies a competing §2254(d) path, not a vote here that amended §2254(e), every successive rule, or every Title I provision governs. This reservation is not a present holding that the new statute applies.

### State findings and gateway

**Sustain the lower gateway.** The strongest supported path combines Dunbar's detailed confession without immunity, its framing explanation, the later confession report, the file's pattern of shifting blame, and the dependence of much of the physical evidence on Dunbar. The supposedly corroborating proof would look different to a reasonable juror aware that the principal witness had repeatedly manufactured accounts. His inaccurate explanation of how he acquired crime-scene knowledge and fear of capital prosecution when recanting strengthen the defense. The lower majority also recited the correct probability formula and expressly applied it in its final gateway paragraphs. Those features make the case stronger than a bare recantation.

**Counterargument.** The record permits guilt by joint participation rather than exclusive responsibility. Joyce's account, Larry White's understanding, Patience's report and Stephanie's account cannot be treated as exclusively exculpatory. The tape reconstruction is a probative offer excluded after withdrawal of the challenge and a relevance ruling, not a judicial finding that the science was unreliable. Its weaknesses and defense-testing limits remain relevant, but it cannot be erased for inadmissibility. The state judge observed the confession and its retraction. A finding that an alternative interpretation is equally plausible does not show that the state finding lacked fair support. Finally, the majority's footnote 4 expressly substituted confidence in the actual verdict for the hypothetical-juror question; its treatment of participation evidence may reflect that substitution. Correct introductory and concluding words cannot cure a materially different operative inquiry.

**Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer select corrected assessment, not present gateway success.** Stevens authored, and the other three fully joined, both Schlup's probability rule and its bounded legal-error remand. Each also joined Felker's later, record-specific refusal to find that gateway met despite substantial new scientific criticism. They therefore reject both automatic passage whenever evidence undermines a trial witness and automatic failure whenever contrary evidence survives. Here the lower court used incompatible descriptions of its task and did not adequately connect the inculpatory family evidence, tape offer and state findings to a complete properly instructed-juror assessment. Those are concrete defects warranting renewed consideration. The strongest objection to remand is that the complete published record already requires rejection. Their answer is confined: meaningful exculpatory evidence remains, its role is interdependent with contested credibility and physical-proof inferences, and the required inquiry has not reliably been performed under a single correct standard. A remand permits an adverse conclusion; it is not a commitment that the gate will open.

**O'Connor selects gateway failure.** Her full Schlup join prevents substituting a stricter innocence burden, and her Kyles join prevents confusing a substantial Brady claim with failure of the gateway. Her Herrera plurality and concurrence, however, separately examine delayed and inconsistent innocence evidence against surviving physical proof rather than treat serious doubt as decisive. Her later Felker join likewise accepts failure under the actual probability standard. On this supplied record, even allowing Dunbar's confession probative weight, the evidence of joint action and the tape evidence permit a reasonable properly instructed juror to find participation beyond a reasonable doubt. The repeated false accounts and motives give fair support to the state credibility finding. The strongest remand argument is the lower majority's legal error and untested tape reconstruction. She finds that correction does not require an additional factfinding opportunity on this gateway: the reliable new material does not reduce the supported adverse assessment to the probability required. This is not a rule that one possible guilty juror always defeats Schlup, nor a finding that every defense account is false.

**Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas select gateway failure under current law.** Kennedy authored the earlier stricter-standard dissent, with the joins specified in the slice; Scalia and Thomas supplied their separate qualified-refusal ground. Those dissents are not the operative standard. Their shared later Felker join demonstrates acceptance of a record-specific insufficiency conclusion under controlling Schlup. Here each accepts the confession and impeachment for assessment rather than excludes them categorically, but finds the adverse family evidence, tape, association and postcrime handling of property sufficient to sustain reasonable guilty assessment to a degree inconsistent with Carriger's demanding probability burden. The best objection is that much of the physical case came through a discredited witness. Their answer rests on the independent participation evidence and the difference between showing that Dunbar was guilty and showing that no reasonable juror would find Carriger guilty. They would not join the lower dissent's categorical statement that a repudiated recantation can never qualify, its demand for an affirmative innocence verdict by twelve imagined jurors, or an assumption that every item in the tape offer is conclusively established.

**Separate findings remain separate.** The four remanding Justices do not themselves hold the state credibility finding unsupported. The four rejecting the gate regard it as fairly supported and independently assess the expanded record, including the later cellmate account. None treats the finding as conclusive on evidence and events it did not assess. No Justice's position changes the statutory exception into de novo credibility review merely because the appellate court prefers another view.

### Materiality, acquisition, actual attribution and nonretroactivity

**Conditional full-file materiality path.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer apply the Kyles opinion they joined; Souter authored it. The file supplies more than additional convictions: a pattern of blaming others, indications of violence and armed conduct contradicting the presentation of a truthful nonviolent burglar, and leads for preparation and examination of the investigation. Those uses directly concern the indispensable witness and the prosecutor's emphatic reliance on his honesty. Collective evaluation can undermine confidence without proving that Carriger was elsewhere or excluding joint participation. This is an independent, lesser prejudice inquiry than Schlup. The confession and later material may illuminate the gateway and record; the trial-disclosure counterfactual must still concern lawful uses of evidence that should have been disclosed then, not a fiction that testimony first produced years later was available in 1978.

**Strongest nonmateriality path.** The jury knew six burglaries, immunity and a gun-form falsehood; counsel was alerted to the file and informant history; the tape, keys and conduct supply corroboration. Additional bad-character material may be partly cumulative or inadmissible. The five answer that the supported lawful uses concern the exact framing defense and the quality and independence of the investigation, not merely a larger tally of wrongdoing. Kyles rejects both item-by-item dismissal and legal sufficiency as the complete materiality answer. They nevertheless withhold an operative violation because the suppressed set and the legally attributable duty remain unresolved. Their conditional conclusion cannot be applied to a materially smaller set without renewed collective assessment.

**Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not reach a materiality disposition.** Their Kyles dissent accepted cumulative analysis while finding the surviving corroboration persuasive on that different record. It supports close attention here to the tape and defense knowledge, not a categorical conclusion that impeachment of an already impeached witness is immaterial. The strongest pro-relief argument is the specific habitual framing evidence and its effect on investigation. Each accepts that this can be materially different from additional burglary convictions. Their gateway disposition makes the merits choice unnecessary; none supplies a vote for treating the full file as nonmaterial, a requirement of probable acquittal, or a second harmlessness screen. Any later lawful materiality decision must distinguish evidence actually known to the defense and assess the collectively useful suppressed remainder.

**Existing case-team path.** All eight accept the current Kyles rule as governing. If prosecutors or investigating police acting for this case held or knew the pertinent favorable information, the prosecutor's personal ignorance does not excuse disclosure of material evidence. There is no need to invent a new all-agency rule to enforce that responsibility. The strongest objection is factual: the source does not find the relevant possession or knowledge, and an investigator's familiarity with Dunbar as an informant is not knowledge of the complete prison file. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer therefore support a bounded attribution determination only after a lawful route to merits is established. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas leave that factual question unresolved because this gateway fails; they do not adjudicate the absence of team knowledge.

**Known-central-witness acquisition path.** The strongest argument for the lower rule is limited and concrete: a prosecutor choosing to make a known career criminal the centerpiece of a murder case can obtain an identified corrections file and should not assure the jury of the witness's credibility while remaining avoidably ignorant of that file. Brady and Giglio protect trial fairness, not merely prosecutorial good faith. This rationale does not require searching every agency in every prosecution.

**The eight decline to sustain the present writ on that acquisition rule.** Even the narrowed proposal changes the operative trigger from information held by those acting for the prosecution to information that an outside repository could supply. Kyles's present affirmative duty stops at the former; it expressly disclaims unrestricted investigation and universal imputation. A witness's criminal history and the importance of his testimony do not establish that the repository acted for this prosecution. Stevens and Souter preserve the possibility that a properly presented, factually distinct practice of deliberately avoiding case-team information would fall within existing Brady; no such practice is found here. Ginsburg and Breyer insist that the constitutional duty be tied to the actual organizational relationship, not the convenience of access alone. O'Connor requires that same link before importing the file as suppressed. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject enlarging the obligation merely because the State could have acquired more impeachment. These are limitations on the current sufficient ground, not a factual finding that Arizona lacked the evidence.

**All-government imputation fails for every Justice.** Government custody somewhere is not enough. The proposed witness-specific duty must not be rejected or approved by substituting that much broader theory for it. Conversely, rejecting all-government imputation does not by itself decide whether the narrower acquisition theory is constitutionally sound. No present writ is supported on the narrower theory here because it is a disputed extension with unaddressed collateral predicates, whereas the lower court treated the duty as already supplied by Kyles.

**Nonretroactivity and narrower alternatives.** No Justice would cure that problem by inventing a preserved Teague defense or silently holding it waived. The four remanding commitments permit the actual case-team theory to be resolved under existing law if the gateway is satisfied; they do not direct adoption of a new duty. If acquisition alone remains indispensable, the court must determine the actual presentation and finality predicates and then the new-rule question before relief. A rule requiring new evidence acquisition regulates trial procedure; neither a substantive exception nor a watershed exception follows merely from the importance of impeachment. The contemporaneous exceptions remain available only on their actual terms. O'Connor's Caspari authorship and Stevens's narrower presentation position in that case are preserved: the majority's subsidiary-defense rule governs when its preservation and same-rule conditions exist, but those conditions are not supplied here. Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer's Lambrix joins preserve flexible sequencing of actual nonjurisdictional defenses without inventing one.

## The proposed rule and its genuine competing ground

The lower court's proposal is that, before making this known career criminal its central prosecution witness, the State must obtain and review his identified corrections record and disclose the material favorable credibility information under Brady and Giglio. That is an affirmative acquisition duty. It differs from both Kyles's duty to learn of information already held by those acting for the prosecution in this case and imputation of every government's office's files.

**The supported affirmative case for an extension:** The prosecution chooses the immunized witness and relies centrally on his credibility. A defined corrections file directly concerns that credibility; its pattern of blaming others and its contradictions of the State's nonviolent-truthful-witness presentation are more than a generalized hope of finding impeachment. Brady and Giglio protect the fairness of the trial, and Kyles makes clear that personal prosecutorial ignorance does not itself settle disclosure responsibility. A duty confined to this known witness and an identified file can be evaluated on its own scope without equating it to unlimited discovery or every agency's constructive knowledge. The fact that counsel knew the file existed does not establish that counsel knew its materially favorable contents.

**The supported substantive objection:** Brady's existing responsibility for information within a case's prosecution team does not automatically require the prosecutor to create or conduct every useful credibility investigation. The DOC repository's relationship to this prosecution remains unestablished. The proposed rule adds a duty to obtain evidence before it is held by those acting for the prosecution. Its justification must therefore explain why this defined witness-file relationship makes acquisition part of the State's constitutional trial obligation, and what bounds that obligation. The known history and the defense's own letter also matter to the distinction between State suppression and a defense investigation that did not occur. Those facts do not conclusively answer the distinction or supply an ineffective-assistance holding. The source establishes no categorical inability of the defense to obtain the file, no deliberately arranged avoidance by prosecutors, and no finding that all the relevant contents were already known by either side.

Neither argument establishes litigant misconduct from a research gap. No new accessibility, cost, control or file-sharing fact is assumed to make either path easier. The constitutionality of a bounded duty can be assessed without deciding that it applies to every central witness or to every government repository. The word bounded is not itself a justification: the identified prosecution choice and the file's relationship to fair trial preparation must do the legal work.

## The narrower path and the mandate

For Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, a corrected gateway inquiry legitimately precedes any present writ. That is the sufficient remand ground. If the gateway fails and no independently available route exists, there is no need to decide acquisition or Brady merits. If a lawful route to merits exists and actual case-team possession or knowledge is established for the material suppressed evidence, existing Kyles can resolve the claim without deciding the extension.

**Actual attribution is only a potentially obviating route.** Its presently inconclusive state is not proof that this route succeeds. A court must not require the prisoner to prove actual team possession as the exclusive way of proceeding while purporting to leave the acquisition theory open. If actual attribution is absent or insufficient and the bounded acquisition theory remains necessary and properly presented, it must be considered on its constitutional merits and on any actually applicable collateral conditions. The remand may permit supported attribution findings, but does not mandate extra factual proceedings solely to avoid an otherwise properly decidable legal question. It orders no automatic hearing or acquisition of all government files.

For O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, current-standard gateway failure independently defeats this otherwise-abusive Brady route. They can leave the acquisition duty undecided without implying that it could never govern a properly available claim. Their dispositions retain independent treatment of the other unaddressed claims. Should later proceedings lawfully reopen this particular merits issue on a different established basis, their reservations supply no predetermined constitutional rejection.

The primary remedial language is therefore clarified to preserve **both** supported merits theories after any required gate: existing case-team attribution and the proposed bounded acquisition obligation. Neither theory is established by the present record simply because the other is uncertain.
