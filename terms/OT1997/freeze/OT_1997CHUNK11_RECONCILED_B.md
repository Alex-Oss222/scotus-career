### Full temporal objection and answer

The Fourth Circuit supplied three distinct reasons: the statute governs filing rather than earlier provider conduct; the prohibition on bringing an inconsistent cause expressly directs application to later-filed complaints; and claim elimination does not impose a retrospective consequence because an unfiled claim is not vested and Zeran did not show reliance. Each has been considered, rather than bypassed by invoking Landgraf's name.

The best statutory argument for AOL is real: then-§230(d)(3) expressly governs bringing a cause and imposing liability, this AOL action began April 23, 1996, and Congress favored immediate protection against publication-related suits. A temporal instruction need not use prescribed words. Lindh confirms that statutory structure and contextual contrast can supply direction before the default presumption is reached. The absence of an express phrase naming pre-enactment conduct is therefore not itself decisive, and no super-clear-statement requirement is adopted.

The complete text nevertheless does not select the filing date as the exclusive temporal event. Subsection (d)(3) preserves consistent state law and bars inconsistent causes and liability; its two commands state the preemptive operation of the substantive publisher-treatment rule. They do not separately identify which earlier conduct is governed by that new rule. The prohibition on imposing liability is likewise not a freestanding instruction to extinguish every earlier claim whenever adjudication occurs after enactment. The statute supplies no contextual pending-case contrast comparable to the one that established direction in Lindh. Its findings, policies, good-faith provision, preservation clauses and definitions explain substantive scope and purposes but add no transition prescription for completed conduct.

The effect asserted here is elimination of an otherwise legally sufficient claim arising from earlier conduct, not merely allocation to another forum, exhaustion before filing or withdrawal of a litigation subsidy. Landgraf protects against impairment of existing rights as well as increased liability or new duties. Hughes specifically rejects a later-filing and jurisdictional-label shortcut where a change alters a complete defense and the substantive enforcement relation. Hughes withdrew a defense rather than adding one, but that difference does not make the claim holder's side of the legal relation disappear. Rivers rejects an actual-reliance prerequisite and the inference that a corrective legislative purpose alone settles temporal reach. The Fourth Circuit's ability-to-legislate/vesting point addresses a different question from the statutory presumption. Congress may direct retrospective operation subject to constitutional limits; the present holding is that it has not sufficiently done so here.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the common result by reading the enforcement clause with the actual rule it enforces: legal responsibility for publication-related conduct. The act of filing is not the sole relevant activity simply because Congress framed its substantive protection through causes and liability. They retain their own method, rather than treating the general framework's five-Justice history as unanimous. The remaining five adopt the sequential framework and concrete consequences analysis. The distinction preserves both the common holding and every material join limit.

### Souter's discrepancy and the dissent's actual scope

Souter historically joined Thomas's entire dissent, including its textual and lenity grounds. His provisional affirmance relied on his Beecham join, Love join and the conviction-centered grammar. Beecham remains a sovereign-selector decision; its agreement on the connected restoration text did not decide the residual-restriction question historically or here. Love is a material change in constitutional law, but preserves Lewis and reserves the validity of particular restrictions. Souter's own personal-right join does not require him to adopt a broader federal disability; nor does it make state permission a constitutional exemption. Staples and Bryan address distinct fault provisions and supply no answer to this restoration question. The same state permission, same location restriction and same federal text remain. There is no concrete changed premise justifying replacement of Souter's historical textual objection with the provisional federal-policy reading. His reconciled vote is therefore reversal on the grounds of the dissent he joined, following legal comparison rather than automatic historical copying.

The primary dissent also clarifies Scalia and Thomas's frozen formulation. It is not a rule that the same conviction exists federally for a state-forbidden handgun but disappears for a permitted rifle. The issue is whether this restoration expressly forbids possession of firearms so as to retain the conviction at all. Massachusetts permitted rifles, shotguns and handgun possession at home or business while restricting the latter outside those locations. For Scalia, Souter and Thomas, that partial restriction does not meet the express statutory condition. Their reading treats the civil-rights restoration and actual firearm permission as legally significant, and regards Congress's choice to rely on state restoration as consistent with excluding convictions unless the State prohibits firearm possession altogether. The conviction-centered objection therefore does not answer their construction: they choose the defendant's side of the all-or-nothing inquiry, rather than a weapon-dependent federal predicate status. This correction narrows the original model's transaction-specific language without changing Scalia or Thomas's preferred remedy.

The three also regard their textually natural, purpose-compatible construction as sufficient at least to leave unresolved penal ambiguity after the ordinary interpretive tools. Beecham's Chapman/Granderson rule permits lenity at that stage; the existence of two proposed readings alone is not their entire argument. A general federal protective purpose cannot replace the express statutory condition or make every partial restriction the equivalent of a total prohibition. They share both the primary textual ground and the independent residual-lenity ground, not merely the outcome.

### Affirming rationale and limits

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer retain the common conviction-wide ground. State law identifies the conviction and restoration, but federal law supplies the consequence of the surviving firearm restriction. The unless clause qualifies exclusion of the conviction and does not classify the currently possessed weapons one by one. The substantial residual handgun restriction is enough under their construction, even though the seized long guns were permitted under state law. They reject the defendant's all-or-nothing alternative because it would make a continuing state firearms disability immaterial to the federal status so long as some firearm possession remained lawful. Text and statutory structure, informed by the federal protective purpose, resolve the ambiguity for them; lenity therefore does not require the competing result.

Their historical support is confirmed without importing a collective-only arms theory or an adjudicated individualized dangerousness finding. ## Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District — No. 96-1866

### Current-law correction to the historical setting

Kinman, May 27, 1997, already resolves the teacher-harassment recipient threshold by five direct proposition votes. Its rule requires actual notice to an official empowered to correct harassment or its substantial risk, deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that known danger, and causal injury or continuation. It is not a private qualified-immunity rule. Constructive notice, teacher status, ordinary authority-assisted access, policy breach and the harasser's knowledge alone do not suffice. The sole-Justice additional circumstantial-awareness formulations are not a majority rule. The historical Gebser premise that Franklin's recipient-attribution question remained unresolved is displaced and cannot be imported by any Justice.

**Historical departure:** The doctrinal starting point differs because Kinman already establishes the recipient threshold: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, who directly joined that rule and sought summary judgment there, apply it here; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, who published their contrary agency and negligent-correction grounds in Kinman, expressly renew that disagreement rather than treat attribution as open. This is a difference in the status and work of precedent, not an unsupported change in any Associate's side of the judgment.

### Independent grounds and remedy envelope of the four seeking remand

For Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer individually, actual delegated educational authority materially aiding the intentional wrong is an independently supported attribution route; negligent failure to correct after actual or constructive notice, causing injury or continuation, is a second independently supported route. The teacher's intentional discrimination and the attribution of that wrong are separate inquiries. Franklin supplies compensation for intentional discrimination, while Cannon's established private action and the absence of a statutory prohibition on these attribution principles support their challenge to Kinman's exclusive higher-management requirement. Section 1682's administrative fund-termination structure does not, in their view, require an additional intentional wrong by recipient management in every damages case. Their proposed negligence route concerns recipient responsibility for the teacher's intentional discrimination, not compensation for every unintended discriminatory effect.

The four would set aside summary judgment and return actual delegation, material aid, the responsible officials' information, correction, reporting arrangements and causal injury for ordinary consideration under those grounds. They do not treat teacher employment alone as conclusive attribution; do not find negligence, causation, all harassment elements or damages established; and do not order a compulsory trial. ### Three-Justice proposed avoidable-consequences defense

The full historical Ginsburg dissent, joined by Souter and Breyer, adds a materially distinct proposed limit to their broader attribution theory. No current holding displaces that position: Faragher and Harrison expressly left a general effective-protection/nonuse defense open, and Kinman's four-Justice alternative did not reject one. Its omission from provisional B is therefore supplemented rather than treated as abandonment without a changed premise.

Ginsburg, Souter and Breyer would place on the district the burden to show that internal remedies were adequately publicized and likely to provide redress without undue risk, effort or expense. Only to the extent the plaintiff unreasonably failed to use those measures and consequently suffered avoidable harm would recovery be barred. A paper policy, nonreporting by itself, an unsafe or ineffective route, or eventual correction does not meet the defense. This is a causal limit on avoidable harm, not an automatic complete immunity. Stevens reserves whether to adopt that defense and does not join its operative terms. All four can agree that the uncertain policy and dissemination record does not establish it conclusively here.

This proposed defense belongs to the three Justices' alternative liability framework. It is not imported as an additional defense under Kinman's controlling actual-notice rule or as a new Court holding merely because those three support remand. A separate explanation is needed if their full qualification is stated, but historical authorship does not decide assignment.

### Affirming application and limits common to the decision's scope

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas preserve the frozen record-specific application. The complaint concerned classroom comments, not a reported sexual relationship. Even assuming the principal had corrective authority, the supported information and response do not establish actual knowledge of the relationship or its substantial risk followed by deliberate and unreasonable refusal to protect. The principal met with Waldrop, received the apology and assurance, cautioned him and informed the guidance counselor. Termination followed police discovery. These events are not declared perfect responses, and a warning is not sufficient in every case; they fail to establish the culpability required on this supported record. The four do not pool Waldrop's own knowledge with the principal's knowledge or treat a possible policy omission as proof of institutional actual notice.

The source-qualified dispute over grievance procedures does not itself prove Title IX intentional recipient discrimination. Section 106.8(b)'s procedure-adoption/publication duty and §106.9(a)'s nondiscrimination notice duty remain distinct; administrative enforcement and injunctions retain their own sources. The possible reporting consequence of a deficient system is evidence, not an automatic actual-notice substitute or conclusive causation finding. No rule requires a written complaint, victim report, confession or knowledge of completed intercourse as the exclusive way to show a known substantial danger. No sole-Justice awareness presumption is promoted into Kinman's rule.

Historical O'Connor/Scalia/Kennedy/Thomas support confirms this application with the changed prior-precedent setting. Historical Stevens/Souter/Ginsburg/Breyer opposition confirms their continuing recipient-attribution objection, but they must confront Kinman expressly. Ordinary statutory precedent is a substantial barrier to their proposed reconsideration; their own prior dissent does not erase it.
