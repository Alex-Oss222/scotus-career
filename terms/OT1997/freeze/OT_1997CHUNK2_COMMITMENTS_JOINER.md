## Review channel and distinct decision components

Section 1254(1) permits review of the Eleventh Circuit's reversal. The reported grant identifies the review standard. Its application to the disputed expert ruling is related but distinct: the Court must justify reaching that application rather than assume a separately verified grant. The record supports both a review-standard decision returning application to the circuit and a decision correcting that standard and resolving the bounded PCB-only ruling. Petitioners' request for reinstatement does not authorize disposition of the unchallenged furan/dioxin exposure ruling or adjudication of medical causation.

**A. Appellate allocation.** Admission and exclusion of scientific expert evidence under the original Rules receive abuse-of-discretion review. That standard includes independent correction of legal mistakes and respect for the proper protection of factual findings. A discretionary ruling must rest on supportable grounds under Rules 104(a) and 702. Exclusion receives no different standard because it contributes to summary judgment; admission and exclusion are reviewed under the same allocation. Careful examination of a dispositive ruling is permissible. An appellate policy favoring admission does not justify substitution of its own admissibility judgment for a permissible district-court determination.

**B. Scientific inference and legal boundary.** Daubert's inquiry into principles, methodology, and valid application permits examination of whether the scientific material supports the inference offered. Describing a technique as accepted or an approach as “weight of evidence” does not immunize the application. Conversely, a conclusion is not inadmissible merely because no individual study proves it, a study concerns animals, a reported association lacks statistical significance, or the relevant exposure has other possible causes. The preliminary foundation must fit the actual promotion/acceleration theory; it need not prove that PCBs were the sole cause. The judge may exclude a causal opinion when its inferential connection is insufficient under this flexible foundation inquiry, but may not substitute an ultimate merits finding or impose an invariant matching-study requirement.

**C. PCB-only application.** Seven Associates conclude that, on the published proffers and lower-court explanations supplied, the District Court permissibly excluded the PCB-only causation opinions. This is a conclusion about the allowable range of its evidentiary judgment, not that the opinions were necessarily inadmissible in every permissible assessment or that PCBs cannot promote lung cancer. Stevens would leave the actual admissibility application for reconsideration by the Eleventh Circuit under A–B, without sustaining exclusion here.

**D. Alternative exposure and remedy.** The Eleventh Circuit found evidence supporting furan/dioxin exposure; petitioners did not challenge that ruling in the petition. The District Court did not explicitly assess causation admissibility assuming those exposures. None of the eight supports reinstating the complete defense summary judgment on the strength of a PCB-only ruling. The unchallenged exposure ruling remains operative, and the alternative causation theory requires lawful further treatment. No expert is ordered admitted and no causation or damages finding is made.

## Application support and adverse-path test

**Ordinary discretionary review versus heightened or independent review.** The strongest argument for closer review is the interaction of Rule 702's liberal admissibility approach, scientific judgments susceptible to inconsistent treatment, and exclusion's potentially dispositive consequence. It does not establish that the district judge loses the usual evidentiary allocation. Koon demonstrates that discretion remains meaningful despite consequential applications, while independently correcting legal boundaries. Ornelas addresses a distinct constitutional task and expressly does not settle all mixed questions. Both parties' use of the words “abuse of discretion” is not dispositive; all eight reject a legal rule making exclusion more stringently reviewable while permitting careful review under the ordinary standard.

**Permissible PCB-only exclusion.** The affirmative published support consists of the actual study descriptions and the lower court's identified fit objections, not the absence of research originals. The animal material involves infant mice, concentrated injections, and alveologenic adenomas, unlike the adult occupational exposure and small-cell carcinoma at issue. Of the four described human materials, two report nonsignificant excesses whose authors did not draw the proposed exposure link; the Norwegian material concerns a specified mineral oil rather than an identified PCB exposure; the Yusho material involves multiple potential carcinogens and does not isolate the PCB contribution in the supplied account. These features provide a supported basis for the District Court to judge the particular PCB-only inference insufficiently connected. Its characterization that the animal-study response defended such studies generally is retained as a judicial explanation, not converted into an expert concession.

The strongest objection is that this presentation disaggregates a legitimate combined inference, overlooks a cancer-promotion theory compatible with other carcinogens, and omits research the experts also invoked. That objection prevents a rule requiring each study to prove the ultimate point and prevents a claim that every possible scientific bridge was refuted. The seven nevertheless conclude that the trial judge could permissibly find the specific described materials insufficiently fitted even crediting the reported aggregate method, medical-record review, interviews, and promotion theory. The permissive appellate conclusion does not require calculating a missing effect size or deciding whether PCBs scientifically cause this cancer. It is narrower than independently reweighing the entire scientific record. If fuller source recovery establishes a materially different actual bridge or that the lower court imposed a forbidden categorical requirement, that is a substantive source correction requiring refresh, not an assumed fact supporting today's commitment.

**Return PCB application to the circuit.** Stevens's strongest basis is the concrete mismatch between judging isolated studies and assessing the experts' reported cumulative reasoning. The Eleventh Circuit reports materials from at least thirteen researchers and WHO sources, while the available descriptions do not purport to exhaust all testimony. His Daubert position supports restraint in prescribing scientific sufficiency without examining the actual application, and the grant squarely establishes the review question. The strongest objection is that the published lower-court reasons and described study limits already suffice for a permissible-discretion determination, so remand needlessly postpones the application. He nevertheless favors appellate reconsideration because accepting a cumulative method in the abstract does not establish that the District Court evaluated it as offered. This is a legal and review-scope judgment, not a finding that the Court lacks papers or a demand for production to solve a research problem.

**Find an abuse and command admission.** The claimed method and the possibility of combined inferences challenge exclusion, but do not conclusively establish preliminary foundation and fit or negate every permissible district-court concern. No Associate adopts mandatory admission. Stevens's position also leaves open a properly supported exclusion on reconsideration. Neither cross-examination nor the jury's role eliminates Rules 104(a) and 702 screening.

**Reinstate the entire summary judgment.** Even sustaining the PCB-only ruling does not answer causation assuming the furan/dioxin exposure that the circuit found supported and petitioners left unchallenged. The District Court's earlier no-exposure premise cannot be silently restored. Every Associate rejects complete reinstatement on the present grounds. Further proceedings may sustain or reject the alternative theory under its actual foundation; no compulsory hearing, additional scientific study, new claim, or ultimate result is ordered.
