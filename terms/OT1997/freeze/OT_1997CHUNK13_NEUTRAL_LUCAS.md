Claims 3–7 were first raised in the third state application and dismissed as abuse under state law. The Fifth Circuit reports no cause-and-prejudice showing, states the Schlup gateway and then **assumes gateway sufficiency to decide those constitutional claims adversely on independent grounds**. That assumption is neither a finding of innocence nor a binding rejection of a correctly assessed gateway. A valid independent constitutional merits ground may permit denial without deciding the gateway; if that ground fails, the applicable default, adequacy/independence and gateway issues cannot simply be skipped.

## Evidence disputes and factual-development limits

The employer's post-trial audit is offered as confirmation of Florida work records. The court says the audit did not unequivocally establish accuracy; trial testimony described forged attendance and kickbacks, including Lucas's own account. The State disputes the neighbors' chronology, asserting he moved into that neighborhood after the murder. Expert false-confession evidence is offered to explain incentives and reliability. The report's new Florida car-fire account could support his presence there, while federal-hearing testimony identified two acquaintances from whom he could have learned about it. Favorable treatment while supplying confessions bears on reliability. Polygraph evidence is contested and would have been inadmissible at trial. These are competing evidentiary accounts, not new factual findings by this Court.

A gateway must consider reliable evidence with the whole probative record; it may not categorically discard corroboration, impeachment or trial-inadmissible evidence. Nor must it accept every affidavit, expert assertion or polygraph result as true. The court must assess reliability, timing, consistency and force. A trial verdict does not conclusively answer the augmented-record question. Conversely, doubt about some confessions does not itself prove that no reasonable properly instructed juror could convict. No automatic hearing follows from either proposition.

### Material legal applications and open questions

**Former law.** Lindh's chapter 153 construction and Carriger's pending-capital extension govern the identified merits and factual-presumption amendments on the reported posture outside established chapter 154 qualification. Carriger does not decide every AEDPA provision or §2254(e)(2)'s separate temporal/trigger questions. Former §2254(d) requires qualifying state findings and preserves its enumerated exceptions; paragraph (8) asks whether the pertinent state record as a whole fairly supports the finding. Historical/credibility findings are distinct from mixed constitutional questions. Wright retains Jackson without a general reasonable-state-decision deference rule. No §2255 savings-clause analysis applies to this state prisoner's first federal petition.

**Innocence and suppression.** Herrera's actual controlling proposition is record-specific insufficiency; it adopts neither a freestanding entitlement nor a uniform extraordinary threshold. Schlup and Carriger require the whole-record probability gateway and preserve independent constitutional elements and state-finding treatment. Bousley confirms that factual innocence is not mere legal insufficiency; its plea-specific bargain conditions are not imported into this trial conviction. Kyles requires an actual suppressed favorable set, prosecution-team attribution and collective materiality; no separate duplicative harmlessness test follows a completed Brady violation. Carriger's proposed corrections-file acquisition duty remains unadopted and is not a rule governing all government files.

**Default and new rules.** Coleman supplies an adequate independent state-default barrier with separate cause/prejudice and miscarriage-of-justice routes. Trest rejects a mandatory appellate duty to invent an omitted default defense. Lambrix rejects universal sequencing between actual default and nonretroactivity questions. Teague requires the actual finality-era dictated-rule inquiry and preserves both then-existing exceptions: substantive restrictions on punishment and watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. An extension is not automatically new; neither an unasserted defense nor waiver is presumed. The lower opinion places conviction finality in 1990; no more precise finality date is required unless a proposed authority makes it material.

**Counsel, confession and prejudice.** Strickland's ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice survives Fretwell's displaced-substantive-rule holding. Miranda, Edwards, Roberson, Smith, McNeil and the actual Davis clarification holding remain distinct from Sixth Amendment attachment and waiver. Withrow preserves Miranda review and any independently pleaded coercion issue; Connelly requires actual police coercion for due-process involuntariness and Miller separates legal voluntariness from historical facts. Brecht and O'Neal do not make Chapman universal; the prosecutor-comment claim's antecedent no-violation ground and any necessary extension of the prejudice rule must be independently assessed. A lower court's use of historical Brecht is not controlling here.

**Hearing and remedy.** Keeney preserves Townsend's deliberate-bypass qualification for ordinary counsel negligence on properly presented claims with material facts inadequately developed; other hearing grounds, statutory conditions, claim defaults and adequacy of an existing record remain distinct. The fact that hearings occurred does not conclusively establish adequacy, and an adverse result alone requires no additional hearing. A proved available conviction-level violation may support an appropriate conditional writ; no finding of innocence, immediate release, new hearing or fixed retrial clock follows automatically from identified analytical error.

**Commutation and mootness.** Continuing conviction-based custody supplies a controversy for conviction claims. Carafas and Lane distinguish conviction consequences from challenges only to ended punishment; Spencer confirms that custody at filing and continuing Article III injury are separate and reserves the distinct conviction setting. A death-only claim needs an actual surviving consequence, not a hypothetical one. Bancorp makes disposition of a judgment affected by mootness equitable under §2106; it does not require automatic vacatur or create an independent collateral reopening action.
