## 1. Affirmative authority and its proper reach

**A court need not allow the subject of a substantial jurisdictional dispute to disappear while it determines its authority.** In *United States v. Shipp*, 203 U.S. 563, 571–575 (1906), the Court considered contempt charges arising from destruction of the subject of a pending habeas appeal. It held that the Court itself had authority to determine whether the case was properly before it and, from the necessity of that inquiry, to preserve existing conditions and the subject of the petition for the consideration required before announcing a judgment declining jurisdiction. At 573–574 it also expressly rejected the characterization of the habeas grounds as frivolous or a pretense. This is affirmative preservation authority, not a holding that an allegation of federal jurisdiction permits adjudication of the merits regardless of jurisdiction.

*United States v. United Mine Workers*, 330 U.S. 258, 290–293 (1947), applies *Shipp* beyond its habeas setting. A federal civil action sought a declaration of contractual rights and ancillary restraint. Whether the Norris-LaGuardia Act withheld injunctive power presented a substantial, previously unresolved question. The Court expressly held that the district court could preserve existing conditions pending determination of its authority to grant the injunction. At 293 it stated that a different result would follow if the jurisdictional question were frivolous and not substantial. The operative distinction is a real legal question requiring decision, not uncertainty manufactured by the mere assertion of jurisdiction.

There are two important posture limits. *Shipp* involved the Court's appellate habeas jurisdiction, not leave to file this original civil action. In *Mine Workers*, the underlying subject matter and parties were properly before the district court; the particular disputed question concerned statutory authority for ancillary injunctive relief. The Court also relied on *Shipp* and discussed a separate case involving doubtful basic federal jurisdiction. These decisions support an argument for narrowly necessary protection while the Court resolves a substantial original-jurisdiction question; neither adjudicates the fit of a foreign consul's present complaint under Article III or §1251(b)(1). Applying their principle here requires that case-specific determination.

**The power to determine jurisdiction does not establish entitlement to ultimate or continuing relief.** *Mine Workers*, 330 U.S. at 292, recognizes that an order would have to be vacated when want of jurisdiction is determined. At 294–295 it distinguishes punishment for disobeying an outstanding order from the plaintiff's entitlement to remedial relief: an erroneously issued injunction does not sustain a civil remedy merely because criminal contempt can survive. Its alternative holding would set aside the preliminary injunction and civil-contempt judgment if the statute barred the injunction. The contempt holdings therefore cannot be repurposed as authority to continue restraint after the relevant lack of power has been decided.

## 2. Statutory fit and independent constraints

- **The asserted original channel remains Article III and §1251(b)(1).** The validated text supplies original but not exclusive jurisdiction for actions or proceedings to which the specified ambassadors, ministers, consuls, or vice consuls are parties. It does not itself resolve standing, whether this action belongs to that class, discretionary exercise of original jurisdiction, the proper defendant, sovereign immunity, a domestic cause of action, or the permissible remedy. Under 1995 Rule 17.4 the leave motion and initial pleading place a case on the docket; docketing is not a grant of leave or a decision that jurisdiction exists.
- **Section 1651(a) is auxiliary.** Its authorization is for writs necessary or appropriate in aid of the Court's respective jurisdiction and agreeable to the usages and principles of law. The independently asserted jurisdiction must be identified; the statute supplies no freestanding jurisdiction to enforce an international measure and does not create the underlying duty or claim. *Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction v. United States Marshals Service*, 474 U.S. 34, 40–43 (1985), treats the Act as residual authority to fill gaps in otherwise proper judicial power. A statute specifically addressing the issue controls; inconvenience or a preference for another procedure does not justify an ad hoc writ. Its reservation of possible exceptional prisoner-transport circumstances does not create a general emergency exception to statutory limits.
- **Sovereign immunity remains a distinct legal question.** The validated packet preserves *Principality of Monaco* and the actual current *Seminole Tribe*, *Coeur d'Alene*, and *Ex parte Young* framework. Neither *Shipp*, *Mine Workers*, nor §1651 supplies a waiver of immunity. A preservation rationale cannot avoid identifying a substantial basis for proceeding against the particular defendants. Conversely, the earlier civil action's characterization of completed notification failures does not by itself decide the newly pleaded threatened violation. Whether that new asserted duty is domestically enforceable remains disputed.
- **The governing procedure remains original civil procedure.** A-771 does not become an original habeas case or an appellate stay application merely because execution is the threatened event. Rule 17 supplies the applicable framework and permits the civil rules to guide matters beyond prescribed pleading and motion form. The full Court's authority is the question here; an in-chambers injunction formulation is not automatically the full Court's standard. No absent filing, notice defect, default, or waived opposition period is inferred from the research record.

## 3. What a preservation analysis must distinguish

The supported affirmative argument is that an actually pending original action presents a substantial jurisdictional question which the Court cannot responsibly resolve before destruction of its subject, and that limited restraint is necessary to preserve the Court's ability to decide that question. The opposing argument may challenge the substantiality of the jurisdictional basis, the proper defendant or immunity route, whether any such additional deliberation is necessary, or whether the proposed restraint exceeds what the inquiry requires. Both are analytical possibilities, not newly recovered party submissions.

The reasoning must identify the threatened loss to this domestic proceeding and the particular unresolved jurisdictional issue. Grave consequences or the existence of an ICJ proceeding alone do not supply that showing. These authorities permit necessary consideration; they do not require delay whenever jurisdiction is disputed, prescribe an automatic pause, or establish a universal duration. Any chosen action needs its own supported scope and ending event under Engine §10.

A short restraint serving this jurisdictional inquiry is analytically distinct from a preliminary injunction premised on success on the asserted domestic claim, and from restraint lasting through the ICJ merits proceeding. The latter requests need their own lawful authority and applicable interim showing. This supplement does not erase the likelihood, irreparability, and equitable/public-interest issues identified in the validated packet. It does not treat *Steel Co.* as an affirmative source of interim power or permit a final merits decision on assumed jurisdiction.

The §2283 analysis below is an independent constraint. A court cannot evade it by describing affirmative civil restraint as administrative, invoking §1651 alone, or treating the authority to determine jurisdiction as an unlimited fourth statutory exception.

## 4. Section 2283: scope, supported paths, and exact unresolved predicates

**The requested restraint concerns enforcement of a state judgment.** Section 2283 prohibits a federal injunction staying state-court proceedings, subject to three exceptions: express authorization by Act of Congress, necessity in aid of the issuing court's jurisdiction, and protection or effectuation of its judgments. *Hill v. Martin*, 296 U.S. 393, 403 (1935), construed the predecessor's coverage to include the entire judicial process through enforcement, including action by ministerial officers and supplemental or ancillary proceedings. *Atlantic Coast Line Railroad Co. v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers*, 398 U.S. 281, 287 (1970), applies that understanding to §2283: directing the injunction to parties or prohibiting use of a completed proceeding's result does not avoid the statute. An affirmative order forbidding officers to carry out Breard's judicial death sentence is therefore the kind of restraint to which this prohibition speaks. Its short duration does not remove that character. Whether these particular applicants fall within the recognized nonparty limit must also be addressed.

**Independent nonparty status is a coverage question, not an assumption.** *Hale v. Bimco Trading, Inc.*, 306 U.S. 375, 377–378 (1939), permitted an independent federal suitor to challenge enforcement of a statute despite a state mandamus proceeding, to which the federal suitor was a stranger, commanding officials to enforce it. *County of Imperial v. Munoz*, 449 U.S. 54, 58–60 (1980), reaffirmed that route but required resolution of whether the federal plaintiffs were truly strangers not bound as parties to the state litigation. The absence of their names from the state caption did not decide the question; the Court remanded where their relationship to the state litigant and litigation had not been resolved. *Hill* likewise covers privies as well as named parties. Thus Paraguay's and the consul's asserted own rights support a distinct argument for the *Hale* route, rather than next-friend treatment, but do not conclusively establish it. **The exact unestablished predicate is whether each applicant is legally bound as a party or privy to the Virginia criminal proceeding, considering the actual relationship and participation relevant to that inquiry.** The authorized packet neither establishes such privity nor supplies a contrary binding determination. An adjudicator must decide the issue on supported facts or choose a supported alternative path; no participation, representation, or waiver is invented here. *Hale* does not eliminate other jurisdictional, equitable, immunity, or remedial restrictions.

**The Consul General's pleaded §1983 route has an established statutory exception.** *Mitchum v. Foster*, 407 U.S. 225, 237–243 (1972), holds that §1983 is an Act of Congress within §2283's expressly-authorized exception. The classification is established for §1983 actions; it is not necessary to prove anew that Congress intended an exception for every asserted §1983 right. But *Mitchum* does not decide whether the particular claimant has a right enforceable through §1983, whether the named officer is an appropriate defendant, or whether the requested relief meets the applicable interim and equitable standards. Its holding expressly preserves equity, comity, and federalism, including the principles applicable to interference with state criminal proceedings.

For A-771, the validated record identifies the Consul General's official-capacity §1983 claim based on the alleged newly threatened duty. **The unresolved legal predicate is whether the asserted federal duty supplies this claimant with a right enforceable through that cause of action against these officers, assessed at the level required for the particular interim action.** This is a disputed legal issue, not evidence that a required document was not filed. The present source validation neither decides treaty/Charter/ICJ enforceability nor converts a §1983 label into an adjudicated cause of action. *County of Imperial*, 449 U.S. at 60 n.4, rejected invocation of *Mitchum* where the complaint did not rely on or even mention §1983; that defect is distinct from a pleaded §1983 theory whose legal sufficiency is contested here.

**Paraguay's treaty and comity claims require their own route.** *Mitchum* does not automatically attach the Consul General's statutory exception to Paraguay's independent treaty or comity theories. It explains that an asserted express-authorization statute must create a specific federal right or remedy enforceable in equity whose intended scope would be frustrated without the ability to stay state proceedings; literal mention of §2283 is unnecessary. The supplied §§1251 and 1651 identify a forum and auxiliary writ authority, not such a separately established substantive right or remedial scheme. No controlling source supplied here establishes the treaties, UN Charter, or ICJ measure as an Act-of-Congress exception. Comity by itself cannot replace an exception: *Atlantic Coast Line*, 398 U.S. at 286–287, expressly rejects treating §2283 as a flexible comity policy. The federal-government plaintiff exception discussed in *Mitchum*, 407 U.S. at 235–236, concerns the United States or a federal agency asserting federal interests; Paraguay's sovereign status is not that exception. This leaves the independent-nonparty argument and the properly supported jurisdiction-aid inquiry, without deciding either.

**Jurisdictional preservation must fit the statutory necessity limit.** *Atlantic Coast Line*, 398 U.S. at 294–296, rejects inherent authority to disregard §2283 merely because a state proceeding conflicts with an asserted federal right, even a clearly established one. A connection to federal jurisdiction is insufficient. The necessary-aid exception concerns state interference so serious that it impairs the federal court's flexibility and authority to consider or decide the case. Concurrent litigation or disagreement with a state decision did not establish that impairment there. At 296 the Court separately recognizes its own broader potential appellate jurisdiction over federal questions in state proceedings and, correspondingly, broader authority to preserve that jurisdiction. This is jurisdiction-specific authority, not a blanket exemption of this Court from §2283.

The supported A-771 argument is that execution would irreversibly defeat a concrete object of an actual original action before the Court can decide a substantial original-jurisdiction issue. Its proponent must identify that object, the jurisdictional question, why decision cannot adequately precede the threatened event without restraint, and why the scope of restraint is necessary to preserve the domestic adjudication. The opponent may contend that the asserted original channel or relevant immunity route is insubstantial, that the issue is ready for decision, or that the order protects an international proceeding without identifying a threatened exercise of domestic jurisdiction. *Shipp* and *Mine Workers* give support for necessary preservation during a genuine jurisdictional inquiry; *Atlantic Coast Line* prevents their use as an unbounded statutory bypass. These are supported legal arguments, not holdings that A-771 satisfies the necessary-aid exception. The independent-nonparty and §1983 paths remain distinct alternatives.

**No existing judgment supplies the third exception on this record.** Protection or effectuation of a judgment requires an actual relevant adjudication, with the protected matter having been decided; *Atlantic Coast Line*, 398 U.S. at 288–293, refuses to enlarge a prior order beyond what it resolved. The pending A-771 leave motion is not such an adjudication. An ICJ measure is not a judgment of the domestic court issuing the proposed injunction. No existing domestic judgment in the supplied packet establishes an applicant's entitlement to prevent execution. No such judgment is invented.

**Distinct consequence for A-738.** The validated packet identifies a civil certiorari proceeding, No. 97-1390, and an alternative request for an affirmative §1651 injunction preserving Paraguay's and its officials' asserted own treaty/consular rights. Staying a federal judgment denying civil relief does not itself restrain enforcement of Virginia's separate criminal sentence. An order doing the latter requires its own affirmative authority and the §2283 coverage/exception analysis above. The independent-nonparty question concerns the applicants' relationship to the **Virginia criminal proceeding**, not merely their status as parties to the federal civil action. The packet also identifies a Consul General §1983 theory in that earlier civil action, so *Mitchum* is potentially relevant to that claimant and that actual claim; its applicability is not dependent on importing A-771's new ICJ theory into A-738.

For A-738's necessary-aid argument, the concrete jurisdiction to protect is the Court's appellate review of the civil judgment. The particular threatened loss to that review and to relief lawfully available in that case must be identified. *Atlantic Coast Line*'s discussion of potential review of **state** decisions does not by itself establish such a nexus in a petition seeking review of the separate **federal civil** judgment. A putative separate route for review of the Virginia criminal judgment cannot be silently attributed to these civil applicants. Neither a civil petition nor §1651 alone supplies an injunction, and no favorable civil judgment supporting the third exception is identified. The already validated review/reversal and injunction standards remain additional questions. **A-767 remains separate:** *Mitchum*, 407 U.S. at 235 n.16, identifies federal habeas legislation, including §2251, as statutory authority for the corresponding habeas stay; the presence of that separate habeas request does not transfer its statutory basis to either civil application.

## 5. Historical-statutory warning and reading receipt

*Shipp*, 203 U.S. at 573, additionally discussed then-Revised Statutes §766, under which further state proceedings were void pending the habeas appeal. **That automatic-stay provision is not imported into 1998 law.** The authorized eligible §2251 text instead makes a stay discretionary and expressly preserves the validity of state proceedings when no stay is granted. In any event §2251 concerns a pending habeas proceeding; A-771's original civil pleading does not obtain its benefit merely through association with a separate habeas request. *Mine Workers*' civil application of *Shipp* is important precisely because its preservation principle was not confined to that obsolete automatic-stay arrangement.
