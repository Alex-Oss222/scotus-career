### Supported alternatives and the decisive disagreement

**Path A — affirm this reviewed application.** Thomasson permits a predictive inference that the member must genuinely be allowed to contest; it does not require acceptance of his denial or a favorable outcome whenever good service is established. The published majority's record-based credibility characterization is supportable from the denial, cross-examination, and the agency's finding and consideration of rebuttal. The review board's discounting of general character evidence can express a judgment about its probative reach rather than a categorical refusal to credit any rebuttal. On that reading the existing record supports actual consideration and an unrebutted conduct-related inference. It does not support a rule making attraction conclusive.

The strongest objection is that the agency's expressed reasons, unlike the appellate explanation, do not distinguish attraction from likelihood or explain disbelief of Richenberg's sworn undertaking. Receiving evidence can coexist with making rebuttal ineffective. The affirming Justices answer that the published reasons and proceedings need not be read as adopting a conclusive status premise; the finding and evaluation of the offered evidence are consistent with the majority's account. They do not invent an express demeanor finding or hold that every appellate rationale may substitute for an agency reason. On the granted constitutional question, the sparse explanation does not itself establish a constitutional violation or a universally required additional finding. Whether the APA would independently demand more remains outside their holding.

**Path B — reverse on this application and remand to remove the discharge.** The contrary reading gives controlling significance to what the review board actually explained: attraction repeats the orientation premise, while the treatment of character witnesses deprives the sworn promise of obedience of its relevant support. The hypothetical responses add no sufficient connection to a likelihood of prohibited conduct on this record. At that applied scope, the disadvantage lacks the required relationship and the presumption has not functioned as a genuine individualized inquiry. This path applies ordinary rationality and Thomasson's boundaries without imposing completed-act, individual-harm, or governmental actual-purpose requirements.

The strongest objection is that this assessment reweighs evidence, makes a denial and service record effectively dispositive, and replicates the Thomasson dissent despite a current record containing additional equivocation. The reversing Justices answer that the defect concerns the particular inference and actual grounds, not a categorical evidentiary rule: neither desire nor hypothetical lawful conduct supplies the missing explanation for rejecting the promise to obey. They preserve genuinely probative evidence and lawful military predictions in other applications. Their own prior Thomasson dissent provides direct support for the as-applied conclusion; its broader dissatisfaction with that earlier application is not treated as controlling law.

**Path C — vacate for bounded clarification or reconsideration.** If the existing reasons are insufficient to identify which legal premise the agency used, a remand can require application of the actual Thomasson distinction without deciding ultimate retention. Arnold's published alternative and the controlling reservation of conclusive status exclusions support this possible accommodation. Its strongest objection is that it would add a mandatory explanation procedure absent an established basis, or merely invite a later rationalization of an already unlawful decision. A permissible version therefore concerns this unresolved application of the governing legal distinction; it cannot announce a general constitutional checklist, assume APA reviewability, require a new evidentiary hearing, or treat hypothetical reasons as existing findings.

No Associate selects Path C as the primary disposition here. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg can join its specified narrower version as an accommodation if the operative mandate vacates the reviewed judgment, requires genuine reconsideration under the conduct/likelihood distinction, and does not permit the same orientation-only reasoning to validate the discharge without that work. They would retain their preferred as-applied conclusion separately. The five affirming Associates do not join that accommodation on the current record because they find no established defect requiring it. A new, supported factual premise establishing categorical rejection of rebuttal would require fresh modeling, not an automatic transfer of their votes.

A facial invalidation, heightened orientation tier, compulsory individualized-harm proof, or final judicial retention finding is unnecessary and not supported as the common rule. No current coalition is manufactured by combining these different remedies or rationales.

## Trest v. Cain — December 9, 1997

### Threshold and exact entering law

[Lambrix, criminal-procedure Holdings](../../../state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md#the-court-may-decide-the-accepted-nonretroactivity-issue-before-the-unresolved-default-defense), lines 281–288, and [Standards, Sequencing nonretroactivity and procedural default on habeas review](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#sequencing-nonretroactivity-and-procedural-default-on-habeas-review), lines 1589–1603, effective May 12, 1997, make default and Teague distinct nonjurisdictional limits with no inflexible order. Lambrix's remand, Holdings lines 302–320, concerned an actually preserved defense and requires its real adequacy, independence and absence of excuse before denial. It supplies no command to discover an omitted defense.

[Granberry, 481 U.S. 129, 131–136](../research/preflight-c/granberry.txt), is surviving pre-divergence law about omitted exhaustion. Its appellate discretion, informed by comity, federalism, efficiency and the record, rejects both automatic waiver and mandatory dismissal in that setting. It is an analogy for distinguishing institutional interests from obligation, not an already decided rule for every discretionary procedural-default issue.

Pre-divergence Coleman permits a supported anticipated-default determination when an adequate independent rule forecloses the otherwise unexhausted claim, subject to the applicable exceptions. It does not establish those predicates here. The [simulated Gray entry](../../../state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md#gray-v-netherland-518-us-152), lines 226–274, does not contain the historical omitted-defense formulation quoted by the lower dissent. That formulation is not imported. Schlup's distinct gateway does not supply an innocence finding or current entitlement to relief.

[Lindh](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md#the-chapter-153-amendments-at-issue-do-not-govern-this-pre-enactment-noncapital-application), lines 6841–6853, and [Standards, Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#temporal-reach-of-ordinary-aedpa-habeas-amendments), lines 1684–1697, effective June 23, 1997, govern this September 30, 1991 noncapital application. ### Alternative paths and strongest objections

**Path A — vacate and remand after rejecting a mandatory duty.** Default's nonjurisdictional character does not alone answer every issue of judicial initiative, but it removes the proposed jurisdictional necessity. Neither the supplied statute nor surviving doctrine supplies an independent obligation to raise this omitted defense. Granberry shows that comity does not itself entail an absolute command even in the related exhaustion setting. The [published Fifth Circuit reasons](../research/preflight-c/trest-lower.txt) repeatedly characterize review as barred and the result as compelled by Sones; footnote 2 confirms that the parties did not discuss that case. The decision contains no discretionary assessment responsive to the omitted defense. A bounded remand therefore corrects the compulsory premise without adjudicating the underlying default.

The strongest objection is that the default rule protects state finality more strongly than exhaustion and that the apparent Mississippi deadline might justify intervention even without a party request. That objection could support permissible discretion; it does not establish a mandatory obligation. A clear deadline also would not by itself resolve all adequacy, application, excuse, or predicate-conviction-review issues. The current narrow question permits those matters to remain open.

**Path B — reject the duty but affirm as a lawful discretionary application.** Granberry supplies a possible institutional analogy; Coleman recognizes supported anticipated default. These authorities could make appellate initiative defensible on some record without treating default as jurisdictional.

The decisive objection here is that this would substitute an unexpressed discretionary basis for a judgment resting on obligatory exclusion and would treat Trest's failure to meet an unasserted defense as dispositive before its premises received adversarial development. The received record establishes neither intentional State relinquishment nor all the Mississippi rule and exception predicates. The eight Associates decline to affirm on that supplied alternative; their rejection does not establish a categorical prohibition of appellate initiative.

**Broader no-power or automatic-forfeiture path.** The State's omission and Trest's lack of opportunity make that possibility worth distinguishing, but the supplied question does not require a universal rule. Granberry rejects automatic waiver in its own setting; Lambrix involved preservation; simulated Gray supplies no general omitted-defense holding. The eight therefore reserve whether and when courts may raise default, the consequences of intentional relinquishment, and any general notice rule. The record does not establish a deliberate waiver merely from silence. No holding about such a waiver is needed.
