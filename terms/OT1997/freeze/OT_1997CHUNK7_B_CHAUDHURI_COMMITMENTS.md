## Result and operative propositions

All eight would affirm rejection of the challenge to the actual moments of silence. They would vacate the prayer-specific merits adjudication and remand for resolution of the live-controversy and legally available relief predicates, with the existing Tanford rule governing any surviving official-prayer claim. That disposition does not decide that every former-prayer claim is moot or that any damages defendant is liable. It preserves the distinction between threshold directions and a new constitutional merits adjudication of the discontinued prayers.

1. A university may designate a period of silence for the civic solemnization of its functions when it leaves each person's thought and private religious or nonreligious observance to that person, without prescribing prayer, adopting a devotional exercise or requiring religious affirmation. The actual designation, president's stated purpose and absence of established university direction of the audience recitations support that conclusion here. The 1988 counsel letter and succession from prayer are relevant evidence, but do not by themselves convert the implemented silence into official worship. No general immunity for an exercise called silence follows.
2. Neither apprehension about university service nor the mere presence of private religious expression establishes compelled observance on this record. The uncontradicted nonmonitoring and no-penalty representations, dean's affidavit and continuing right to abstain do not support a finding that university officials penalized religious nonparticipation. This is not a categorical rule that employment pressure can never exist at a university or that only formal discipline counts.
3. Before adjudicating the former prayers or awarding relief for them, the lower courts must establish the surviving claims and defendants' capacities, the legally available cause of action and relief, and any continuing prospective controversy under voluntary-cessation principles. A demand for damages cannot manufacture a damages action against a State or an official-capacity defendant whom section 1983 does not make a damages person. Conversely, cessation does not by itself eliminate a real prospective controversy or a legally available individual-capacity damages claim. Arizonans supplies these boundaries, not a finding of mootness on this different record.
4. If a former-prayer claim survives that inquiry, Tanford supplies the controlling current rule: university selection of a religious leader and incorporation of actual invocation or benediction in its official program are governmental worship; adult status, optional attendance, sincere solemnization, nonsectarian terms and permission to abstain do not cure that sponsorship. Whether this yields compensable liability is separate. Immunity depends on the law when the challenged conduct occurred, not retrospective use of Tanford.

## Adversarial comparison and remedy limits

**Former prayers:** Reaching the merits now would efficiently apply directly controlling Tanford to the official selection and program incorporation already established. Its strongest objection is antecedent: the packet does not establish which legally available claim remains after cessation. Remand addresses that precise gap rather than avoiding settled constitutional law. Dismissing the entire former-prayer component as moot would avoid an advisory merits decision, but fails because the supplied record establishes neither unavailability of every damages claim nor cessation sufficient to eliminate prospective controversy. The selected vacatur and bounded remand preserve both possibilities. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas's different views of the underlying prayer merits do not authorize deciding that dispute without a live claim.

**Silence:** Treating the 1988 letter and transition as evidence of organized devotional continuity is a lawful competing analysis. It fails on this actual implementation because the University supplies silence rather than prayer, the president states civic solemnization, no religious thoughts or professions are prescribed, and complicity in the audience recitations is conceded unsupported. Conversely, an unconditional silence safe harbor would ignore Agostini's operational inquiry and is rejected. The ruling concerns this exercise.

The remand is through the Sixth Circuit for ordinary proceedings. It requires no particular evidentiary hearing, no production order, no damages amount and no new injunction. The Court does not reopen Title VII, privately initiated audience expression or an unpresented independent claim. Capacity, viable damages cause, immunity and any prospective relief remain to be decided on their actual predicates. Five Associates endorse Tanford's underlying rationale; three accept the current-law instruction without a new merits join. Do not count all eight as newly adopting the official-prayer rule.
