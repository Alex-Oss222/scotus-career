# Hudson — neutral validation of sanction provisions

**Status:** Validated for independent modeling together with the [frozen Hudson packet](OT_1997CHUNK2_NEUTRAL_HUDSON.md), [lower-record addendum](OT_1997CHUNK2_NEUTRAL_HUDSON_LOWER_ADDENDUM.md), and [statutory source supplement](OT_1997CHUNK2_NEUTRAL_HUDSON_STATUTES_SOURCE.md). No commitments, same-case Supreme Court reasoning, disposition, or lineup were received.

Neutral preflight read the source supplement and its three linked official enactment/Code-history extracts. The 1978 enactment supports the express civil designation, $1,000-per-day original ceiling, participating-person coverage, broad definition of violation, assessment factors, notice/hearing/review structure, and §93 compromise authority. The official amendment notes support the 1982 discretionary assessment wording for both statutes and §504's added compromise authority. Good faith is an amount consideration; these provisions do not make every monetary violation willful. The original per-day ceiling does not establish the number of violation days or a lawful hypothetical maximum in any petitioner's case.

The 1989 amendment notes establish a material version boundary: increased monetary tiers ordinarily apply to later conduct, with a narrow earlier-conduct exception requiring, among other things, no already-issued agency notice. The February 1989 monetary notice predates enactment, so its actual assessments cannot be analyzed as though the later enhanced tier structure automatically governed. October settlement dates alone supply no contrary rule. The source does not establish that all early-1980s conduct occurred after the 1982 amendment; where that distinction matters, the 1978 and amended language must retain their actual dates. The supported 1989 assessment/compromise mechanism is not a retroactive finding about underlying conduct.

The §1818 history reproduces former subsection (e)(2)'s personal-dishonesty or willful-or-continuing-disregard language, with harm and unfitness qualifications. It also establishes that the 1989 §903(a) changes apply to violations and activities after August 9, 1989. This supports rejection of an assumed universal willfulness element, but the quoted former paragraph is not the entire pre-1989 removal statute and must not be represented as every possible ground or as the proved ground in these consent proceedings. Post-1989 numbering cannot silently replace the original authority for earlier lending conduct. The actual consent restriction and permission qualification remain those in the validated record.

The statutory features are inputs to the competing classification analyses, not an enacted answer to the Double Jeopardy question. Civil wording is relevant but not conclusive under received law; good-faith/gravity considerations do not alone establish punishment or exoneration. Halper's retained field, Ursery's stated scope, the separate debarment ruling, $44,000 actual sanctions, and the $72,000 evidentiary cost finding remain distinct. Missing original code editions or administrative instruments do not negate the supported enactment history or create litigant default; any outcome requiring an unverified additional statutory predicate requires that precise source or a supported narrower ground.

No new noncase-law change or Court action is created by reading these pre-existing sources. The supplement is suitable for release with these version and completeness limits, and no withheld-material exposure occurred.
