The material jurisdiction discussion is Part II, **pp. 47–50 and notes 7–8**, with the concluding portion of note 8 continuing on p. 51. The responsive public dissent is **Stevens, pp. 72–78**, joined by Brennan, Marshall, and Scalia. Powell's Part II was joined by Rehnquist, White, Blackmun, and O'Connor. Among the current eight Associates, O'Connor joined that jurisdiction holding; Stevens and Scalia dissented. Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer were not members of that Court.

The majority treated the finally resolved federal disclosure issue as reviewable despite further proceedings. Its analysis invoked Cox's category addressing the absence of effective later review, examined the prosecution's possible outcomes, and considered the irreversible disclosure of confidential child-protection records. The main text and notes must be read together. Note 7 considers a possible later appeal after a new-trial order, acknowledges that law-of-the-case principles do not themselves bar Supreme Court review, and nevertheless regards repetition of the already resolved federal question through state appellate proceedings as serving no useful finality purpose in the unusual circumstances. Note 8 addresses why the Court did not require a contempt route before review, distinguishing the procedural setting and confidentiality interest from ordinary discovery appeals.

Stevens's dissent accepted Cox's limited categories but disputed that Ritchie's record met them. It identified possible review after a contempt order or a new-trial order; state law-of-the-case treatment would not prevent eventual Supreme Court review. It also disputed using possible mootness, disclosure concerns, or case-specific efficiency to relax finality. Scalia joined that dissent. This prior disagreement supplies dated public legal positions; neither side's prior vote automatically dictates a position in a later case.
