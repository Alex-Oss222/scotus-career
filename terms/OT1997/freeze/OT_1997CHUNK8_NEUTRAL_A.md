  2. Whether avoiding an Establishment Clause violation supplies a constitutionally sufficient reason for excluding private religious worship from the forum.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit upheld the district’s exclusion of worship services from school facilities.
* **Relief Requested:** Bronx Household seeks reversal and equal access subject to neutral operational rules.
* **Material Record:** The district permitted broad after-hours uses addressing education, community, culture, character training, and ethical education but denied a church’s proposed worship services and religious instruction. The activities were privately sponsored, outside instructional hours, and could be identified as nongovernmental. The record facts below are from the Second Circuit’s opinion, 127 F.3d 207 (2d Cir. 1997); bracketed pin cites are to the numbered paragraphs of the Public.Resource.Org text linked in Section I.E.
  - **Parties and school:** The Bronx Household of Faith, an evangelical Christian church, and its pastors, Jack Roberts and Robert Hall, sued Community School District No. 10, its board president and the Board of Education of the City of New York over the refusal to permit the church to use the gymnasium-auditorium of M.S. 206B, the Anne Cross Mersereau Middle School, for weekly religious worship services. [¶1] M.S. 206B serves students aged 9 through 12 in grades 5 through 7. [¶12]
  - **Prior rentals:** The church had previously rented the M.S. 206B building twice, in 1994 and 1995, for programs of sports and games, and once in 1995 for a banquet. [¶13]
  - **The request:** Pastor Hall asked to rent the gymnasium each Sunday “for the purpose of conducting church worship services.” He wrote that the services would include “hymn singing, communion, Bible reading, Bible preaching and teaching,” and he cited overcrowding at the church’s present meeting location. [¶13]
  - **The denial:** A District 10 official denied the request under the SOP provision barring after-school use of school facilities by outside organizations for religious services or instruction. The application was denied again after the pastor asked the superintendent to reconsider. [¶13]
  - **New York Education Law § 414:** Section 414 does not list religious worship among the uses of school property it authorizes. It allows districts to permit uses including educational instruction; social, civic and recreational meetings and entertainments, and other community-welfare uses, that are “non-exclusive and open to the general public”; civic forums and community centers; and graduation exercises of nonprofit schools “provided that no religious service is performed.” [¶3]
  - **SOPs 5.6.1 and 5.6.2:** The New York City Board of Education’s Standard Operating Procedures give primary use of school premises to Board activities and next preference to community, youth and adult group activities. They permit use “[f]or the purpose of instruction in any branch of education, learning or the arts” (SOP 5.6.1) and for “social, civic and recreational meetings and entertainments, and other uses pertaining to the welfare of the community” (SOP 5.6.2). [¶¶4-6]
  - **SOP 5.9:** SOP 5.9, a Board of Education policy that governs District 10, provides: “No outside organization or group may be allowed to conduct religious services or religious instruction on school premises after school. However, the use of school premises by outside organizations or groups after school for the purposes of discussing religious material or material which contains a religious viewpoint or for distributing such material is permissible.” [¶¶4, 11, 12]
  - **Uses in 1994-95:** The parties agreed that during the 1994-95 school year District 10 facilities were used for tutoring programs; Boy Scout training, which Judge Cabranes’s opinion describes as including “ethical education”; Girl Scout training “in skills and character”; Pius XII Beacon programs including after-school tutoring and “character training, with no religious content”; a Grace Lutheran Church program on Black History Month; Greek language and history classes for young people offered by the Greek Orthodox Community of Riverdale; concerts; workshops on drugs and AIDS; and adult GED classes. [¶12; Cabranes, J., ¶57 & n.1]
  - **Stipulation:** The parties stipulated that “Community School District 10 has consistently followed Policy 5.9 by never renting school facilities to non-school groups or organizations, including Bronx Household of Faith, for the purpose of conducting religious worship services and religious instruction on school premises.” [¶15]
  - **Scope of the record:** The record covered school uses during a single school year. It disclosed no request to use the premises for discussing religious material or secular matters from a religious viewpoint. [¶32; Cabranes, J., ¶49]
  - **District court:** The district court (Preska, J.) granted summary judgment to the defendants on cross-motions. It found a limited public forum and found restrictions reasonably related to preserving access “primarily for educational purposes,” and it dismissed the remaining claims without analysis. [¶2]
  - **Second Circuit majority:** Judge Miner, writing for the court on a panel with Judges Van Graafeiland and Cabranes, held M.S. 206B a limited rather than open public forum and held SOP 5.9’s prohibition of religious services and instruction reasonable and viewpoint neutral. The court reasoned that “it is reasonable for state legislators and school authorities to avoid the identification of a middle school with a particular church” and to consider “the effect upon the minds of middle school children of designating their school as a church.” It found the non-exclusive-use provisions of no significance. [¶¶23, 28-33]
  - **Establishment Clause and other claims below:** The court stated that “there is no need for the Board of Education to rely on the Establishment Clause in limiting the use of the public school forum.” It rejected the Establishment Clause, Free Exercise and Equal Protection claims, held the RFRA claim moot after City of Boerne v. Flores, and affirmed. The affirmance covered both the services and the instruction parts of SOP 5.9 as applied to the church. [¶¶28, 35, 39, 40, 42, 43, 63]
  - **Judge Cabranes:** Judge Cabranes concurred in part and dissented in part. Stating that he was constrained by circuit precedent and the parties’ factual stipulations, he agreed that the forum was limited, and he concurred in upholding the ban on religious services as reasonable and viewpoint neutral, finding “no real secular analogue” to services and describing that ban as a form of content discrimination. He dissented from “that part of the judgment upholding the ban on religious instruction.” He reasoned that the District allowed “secular instruction related to ‘ethics’ and ‘character,’ paradigmatic contexts for religious instruction,” and that the instruction ban discriminates on the basis of viewpoint under Lamb’s Chapel and Rosenberger. He noted that the parties had stipulated that the church sought the gymnasium for “religious worship services,” so any difficulty in separating services and instruction from other religious speech was not presented. [¶¶45-46, 56-57, 61-63]
