The complaint is broader in legal theories than the original preflight's short account made explicit:

1. The first claim challenges approval of the plan and directing or permitting below-cost sales accomplished through clearcutting under **NFMA, NEPA and the APA**. The NEPA reference must be retained. It is not itself proof that a required statement was omitted, participation denied, or another discrete procedure violated.
2. The second claim challenges directing or permitting below-cost sales under **public-trust duties**. Its cause of action, substantive validity and ultimate remedy remain undecided; neither a public-trust duty nor its absence is established by a ripeness disposition.
3. The third claim challenges the **regulations for identifying land suitable for timber production**, alleging failure properly to identify economically unsuitable land under NFMA and the APA. It must not be silently reduced to a challenge to a particular timber sale. The asserted regulations and their use in the plan are part of the present substantive planning dispute.

Requested relief includes a declaration against the plan and timbering it authorizes, an injunction against further timber harvesting and below-cost sales pending revision, costs and fees, and other appropriate relief. The requested injunction is relief sought, not proof that the plan itself presently authorizes cutting or that a project has already received final approval. The clean packet's distinction between plan adoption and subsequent project authorization remains controlling as a supported record premise.

## Consequences for ripeness and statutory scope

All three described claims challenge the substantive basis for selecting land and permitting anticipated logging, clearcutting or below-cost sales. The regulation challenge makes the strongest present-review path more substantial: an asserted defect in a completed general rule may be legal in character and may predate an individual project. It does not, without its actual present effects, resolve ripeness. The existing *Toilet Goods* distinction between finality and ripeness applies to that argument as well as to the plan. The retained ability to challenge an applied planning or suitability premise at a concrete project stage is material to the narrow disposition; review is not postponed until trees are cut.

No new substantive construction of NFMA, NEPA, the APA or public-trust law is required to dismiss these presently described claims as unripe. In particular, the fact that NEPA can impose procedural duties does not transform every claim invoking it into an established procedural deprivation. Conversely, the Court must not announce that a genuine NEPA procedural claim necessarily awaits logging approval. The complaint recitation identifies no particular omitted environmental statement or other denied procedure. That source limit is the basis for preserving, rather than adjudicating, the distinct procedural question.

The Court's treatment of all three pleaded theories must be explicit. Dismissal concerns their present substantive plan/regulation challenge, without prejudice. It does not hold that land-suitability regulations can never be challenged directly, that a public-trust claim exists or fails on the merits, or that a properly supported procedure claim is premature.

## Later recreational-harm assertions

The merits brief's assertions concerning motorcycles, heavy machinery, road closures and hiking trails present a materially different possible timing premise: an independently operative plan provision might affect current recreation without a later timber authorization. The extraction establishes that these assertions appeared in that brief. It does **not** establish that the activities happened, that the plan finally authorized them, that a particular member suffered the harm, or that an earlier court received and adjudicated that theory.

The published account's report that no earlier presenting court document was identified is not converted into an independent finding that every earlier paper has been searched, an intentional waiver, or a conceded absence of harm. The clean lower majority and concurrence analyze logging/clearcutting and plan formation, including their consequences for recreation; they do not establish a separate adjudicated challenge to motorized access or a duty to close roads or construct trails. Harm to recreation from logging is not the same alleged injury as present motorized access or an unperformed trail improvement.

The supported disposition therefore addresses the three described substantive logging/suitability claims. It neither accepts the later assertions as adjudicated facts nor dismisses a distinct present-recreation claim on its merits or for want of ripeness. No universal new preservation rule is announced. The Court can state that the different assertions supply no established basis for reversing the threshold disposition of the dispute presented by the complaint and lower judgment, while leaving a properly presented claim and its prerequisites for appropriate proceedings.

If a proposed decision instead depends on finding these later assertions preserved, affirmatively forfeited, or part of an independently pleaded procedural claim, the minimum additional source is the relevant Second Amended Complaint text (paragraphs 13–60 and requested relief) plus the specific District Court or Sixth Circuit submission said to present that theory.
