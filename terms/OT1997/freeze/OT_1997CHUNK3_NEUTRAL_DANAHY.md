## Complete lower record and posture

Brieant wrote for Meskill, Calabresi and himself. The entire published opinion, including six numbered footnotes and the visiting-judge footnote, has been read. There is no separate writing. The circuit vacated the district court's denial of qualified-immunity summary judgment and remanded consistently with an immunity disposition for the appealed First Amendment claims. It expressly did not definitively classify all seven positions as policymaking/confidential offices.

Vacco became New York Attorney General January 1, 1995. He sought greater criminal-enforcement emphasis in the Medicaid Fraud Control Unit, required at-will professional staff to reapply, and delegated reappointment decisions to First Deputy William Flynn (not sued) and Acting Deputy Buscaglia. Deputy for Administration Page sent nonreappointment letters in mid-November 1995. The suit began March 1996 and sought money damages against Vacco, Buscaglia and Page individually. Other claims existed but were outside this appeal; the published disposition does not erase them.

The seven appellees were Danahy, Supervising Special Investigator; Burke and Bissram, Senior Special Investigators; Matthews and Angiletta, Special Investigators; Mungo, Senior Special Auditor Investigator; and Henesey, Investigative Clerk. Three additional named plaintiffs were not appellees. None held classified civil-service status or had entered through competitive examination. Danahy was Republican, two appellees Democrats, and four unenrolled. The case concerns alleged patronage, not a finding that all plaintiffs belonged to the opposing party.

The unit was a specialized statewide prosecutor's office investigating Medicaid fraud, patient abuse, and related health-care crimes. The opinion quotes then-current 42 C.F.R. §§1007.11 and 1007.13 concerning investigation, prosecutorial cooperation, and skilled staffing. Its described fraud prosecutions illustrate the office's work, not each plaintiff's personal involvement in every case. According to the record recitations, all seven worked closely with special assistant attorneys general on confidential investigations, audits and case preparation. Specific cited admissions include Bissram's surveillance/undercover work, Burke's undercover operations, Henesey's billing analysis, extensive recipient/provider interviews and custody of evidence, and Danahy's investigators’ work with confidential informants.

The circuit treated those duties and defendants’ knowledge of them as undisputed for its legal analysis, assumed the alleged patronage motive and nonpolicymaker status, and found reasonable officials could regard the jobs as confidential within the exception. That appellate characterization is not a fresh factfinding license: any material defendant- or employee-specific factual dispute must stay plaintiff-favorable in a legal immunity appeal. The record quoted in the opinion contains an administrative description for Danahy and statutory descriptions for Bissram, Burke and Mungo; it does not contain all seven descriptions or establish that each defendant consulted all descriptions.

**Important qualifications from the footnotes:** Civil-service exemption resulted from the temporary character of the positions, a circumstance the panel thought not necessarily apparent to a reasonable official. At-will/exempt status is therefore evidence bearing on objective perception, not conclusive constitutional classification. Later Gordon (1997) and McEvoy (1997) clarified job-description methodology; the court expressly reserved immunity for discharges occurring after Gordon. No new finding that these plaintiffs had partisan duties follows from a job title containing “investigator.”

## Entering law and issue sequence

The January 15 public baseline is provisional for this later event; intervening effective Chunk 3 holdings require a narrow refresh. 1. **Reviewability.** Johnson, Behrens and Clanton allow the legal sufficiency of a personal immunity defense on proper assumed facts, not reweighing evidence about duties, knowledge, participation, or motive. A legal conclusion that particular assumed duties qualify may be reviewable; calling a contested factual job account “law” cannot make it undisputed. If the court can decide legal immunity on the plaintiff-favorable facts, the defense may be resolved without final classification or trial. If a purported legal ground depends on appellate factual selection, dismiss that unavailable appellate portion and preserve ordinary proceedings below.
2. **Substantive affiliation protection.** Elrod–Branti, as actually formulated before 1995, asks whether affiliation is appropriate for effective performance of the particular office. A policymaker/confidential label, at-will appointment, technical expertise, or generalized administrative desire for loyalty is not dispositive. Public defenders' client confidences in Branti show that ordinary confidential information alone is insufficient; prosecutors' public discretionary function may support a materially different conclusion. Work within that function and actual trust relationships matter. Protection of an employee's political association remains distinct from Pickering’s speech balancing and from a constitutional entitlement to continued employment.
3. **Conduct-time notice.** Harlow/Anderson supplies objective, particularized immunity at the mid-November 1995 decisions. Elder requires relevant pre-existing authority; Lanier permits obvious coverage without a factual twin but does not treat an abstract association right as notice of every disputed application. No mandatory merits-first sequence follows. Regan (1993), Vezzetti (1994), Savage (1988), and then-existing circuit precedents described below are pertinent context. Gordon, Vona, McEvoy, Bavaro, O’Hare and the 1996–98 decisions do not supply retrospective notice. Genuine uncertainty can support immunity while leaving the ultimate constitutional rule intact; the mere existence of an exception supplies no automatic defense.
4. **Separate elements and relief.** Crawford-El preserves ordinary competent motive proof, actual personal responsibility, causation and an actual preponderance same-decision defense. A possible desire for stronger prosecution is not proof that the same dismissals would occur independent of protected affiliation. But those proof rules do not defeat an independently dispositive notice defense. The case concerns personal damages; immunity does not validate every dismissal or automatically govern entity/prospective relief. Fletcher’s functional prosecutorial-immunity framework does not give absolute advocacy immunity to personnel decisions by title.

## Supported alternatives and counterarguments

**Affirm legal immunity on the reported undisputed assumptions.** The specialized office, close prosecutorial work, sensitive discretion and known investigative functions could permit reasonable 1995 officials to view these positions as falling within Branti's confidential/policymaking exception even if ultimate classification remained debatable. The panel’s route assumes patronage and nonpolicymaker status; its defense is not proof that no discrimination occurred. The strongest objection is that technical confidentiality and civil-service exemption do not demonstrate partisan necessity, particularly for a clerk or routine investigator. This path must explain the actual discretion or trusted policy relationship supporting reasonable perception for each materially distinct position; generalized institutional importance alone is insufficient.

**Reject a categorical or insufficiently particularized immunity ground.** Branti's functional rule, nonreappointment holding and distinction between professional confidences and partisan concerns could make reliance on labels, party change and at-will status insufficient. If plaintiff-favorable duties establish purely technical work with no pertinent policy/confidential political role, a court could reject legal immunity or require proper lower-court application. The strongest objection is that the actual reported duties go beyond mere labels and that disputed constitutional classification in 1995 may leave room for reasonable error. Rejecting a legal defense would not establish ultimate liability, damages, personal causation, or a right to a trial.

**Resolve the interlocutory boundary by claim or disputed premise.** A supported legal ground may be reviewed while an employee- or defendant-specific factual dispute remains outside §1291. The same legal question need not have the same answer for all seven; the existing common record may also support a common disposition if the reasoning actually applies to each. Missing job descriptions do not automatically demand their production, prove default, or require a trial. A remand must identify the live legal question or unsupported inference, not prescribe unnecessary factfinding merely to repair a research archive.

These are neutral analytical possibilities, not attributed missing party briefs. The published opinion attributes the officials’ policymaker/confidential and immunity grounds and the plaintiffs’ patronage claim. No concession, new cause of action, or final fact is invented.
