## Actual judgment, grounds and mandate by component

**Astle and Blank — affirm their personal qualified-immunity summary judgments.** Harlow/Anderson's inquiry is whether these defendants' own acts violated a sufficiently apparent federal obligation during the 1988–1989 process. Davis v. Scherer prevents treating a state timing violation as sufficient by itself. Elder requires all relevant conduct-date law; Lanier permits obviously covered applications without an identical licensing case. Later decisions explain present methodology but supply no retroactive notice.

The earlier judicially sustained revocation and later administrative expungements did not adjudicate automatic reinstatement. Astle sought Blank's advice concerning that unresolved relationship; Blank consulted Turner and the state regional official. The reported direction to decide went to **Turner**. The record does not establish that Astle or Blank personally received and defied that instruction or had final power to resolve the conflict. Their actual roles, consultations and uncertain federal application support the particular immunity disposition. Asking a supervisor is not an independent universal defense, and this holding does not excuse an official who knowingly controls and refuses a plainly required decision.

The strongest competing path correctly observes that prolonged inaction blocks the hearing available after denial, and consultation does not answer every process claim. That concern survives as a possible underlying deprivation; it does not establish these two defendants' damages liability on the supported role/notice record. The Court need not resolve the ultimate federal process claim to affirm their immunity. State regulations required investigation within 75 days after a valid application and notification within 30 days after completion, with a hearing upon denial. Those commands inform the case but do not themselves establish entitlement to certification or each official's federal liability.

**DuRose officially — affirm vacatur and the bounded municipal remand.** Denial of his summary-judgment motion was not judgment as a matter of law for the plaintiffs. The trial court's mistaken description of that earlier ruling could not substitute for evaluating the trial evidence under Rule 50. Kentucky v. Graham makes this official-capacity claim the county/entity claim. Monell/Pembaur require an attributable policy or authorized final decision causing an actual federal deprivation; there is no respondeat-superior shortcut.

McMillian requires identification of the entity and function-specific final policymaker through applicable law and legally effective usage. Operational discretion, title, employment and lack of immediate supervision do not alone answer that inquiry. Its Alabama holding identifies no Oneida policymaker. Collins separately requires the underlying deprivation. Personal qualified immunity does not defeat municipal liability.

The district court must evaluate its actual trial record and explain any legally supported Rule 50 disposition. It may find judgment warranted for plaintiffs, judgment warranted for the county, or factual issues requiring a jury. None is selected in advance, and a new trial is not automatically required. The complete trial evidence is not reproduced; that source limit supplies no finding against either side. The $300,000 official-capacity judgment and dependent $70,324.47 fee/cost award cannot stand on the mistaken premise, but this Court adjudicates no proper amount or independent entitlement to fees.

**DuRose personally, post-expungement — affirm clarification and lawful adjudication.** The order concerning the original pre-expungement pleading could not dispose of a distinct claim added in the later amended complaint. An informal letter claiming that all personal claims had already been dismissed does not supply the missing formal disposition. The trial court must identify whether a ruling occurred, state its basis if it did, and otherwise rule on the pending request. Pleading-stage treatment cannot demand proof as though trial had occurred; summary judgment requires the actual record and its own standard. No personal liability, knowledge of the consultation chain or authority over every subordinate act is inferred.

**Other components — preserve their actual posture.** Leave to amend the pre-expungement claims remains, including the affected claims against DuRose, Hendricks and Kraus, without a finding that an amended claim has merit. Both spouses asserted the pre-expungement claims; the later process claims concern Juanita. Turner's favorable personal-capacity jury verdict and the unappealed Perales official declaration are outside this Supreme Court review and remain undisturbed. The Article 78 adequate-process contention remains undecided for appropriate consideration below. ALPA creates no universal rule requiring or forbidding such recourse.
