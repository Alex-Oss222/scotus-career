## Supported record and lower rulings

Leonard Jeffries was a tenured City College/CUNY professor and chair of its Black Studies department. His July 20, 1991 Albany address discussed public-school curriculum and historical racial oppression and contained derogatory statements about Jews. The lower opinion treated its subjects as matters of public concern. Received sanitized recitations additionally describe an off-campus event unaffiliated with CUNY and speech neither assigned nor controlled as an official duty. Those latter descriptions are received record recitations, not independently recovered transcript findings.

President Bernard Harleston and Chancellor W. Ann Reynolds arranged Board consideration of a one-year chair term rather than the usual three years. Neither was a trustee or voted. At its late-October 1991 meeting, the Board approved a one-year term ending in June 1992; no exact October day is supplied. Nine trustees voted for the one-year term, four for immediate removal and one abstained. Blanche Bernstein, one of the four immediate-removal voters, died during trial; claims against her were discontinued. Fifteen defendants remained. A separate objective date extraction also reports a replacement recommendation announced March 20, 1992, three days before the Board meeting at which trustees voted to replace Jeffries effective July. These dates distinguish the events; they do not add a claim or establish that every defendant caused every action.

The first group of jury answers found action because of the speech, no actual disruption of effective operation, and motivation by a reasonable expectation of disruption. Keep those findings distinct. No actual disruption is not the same finding as no reasonable forecast. A reasonable forecast is not itself the final constitutional balance.

Later answers identified Harleston, Reynolds, Edith Everett, Herman Badillo, Sylvia Bloom and Harold Jacobs as acting because of the speech and as not taking the same action absent it. The jury also found, as to those six, malicious intent to violate rights or injure Jeffries, or callous/reckless disregard of First Amendment rights. The disjunctive answers cannot be converted into an additional unanimous factual finding about a single motive. Three of these six were immediate-removal voters, one was the abstaining trustee and two were nonvoting officials. The other nine defendants were the one-year-term voters; the published opinion states that the jury did not attribute the same retaliatory animus to them.

CUNY bylaws and testimony supported the lower court's description of the chair as ministerial rather than policymaking. Jeffries retained his tenured professorship. This establishes the distinction between the challenged chair position and retained faculty role; it does not independently resolve the constitutional significance of all actual chair responsibilities.

The district court ordered two years' reinstatement as chair and entered reduced punitive awards against the six defendants after rejecting qualified immunity. The 1994 panel sustained reinstatement but found the punitive interrogatories irreconcilable and required retrial on that issue. The **1995 judgment now under review reversed the district-court judgment and directed judgment for defendants**. It relied on the reasonable-disruption prediction and its own legal balance of that forecast against the speech, and separately treated the nine one-year votes as an independent cause despite Harleston and Reynolds's role in presenting the proposal. The opinion also regarded the answers concerning the six defendants as irreconcilable and stated that such a conflict ordinarily called for retrial, which it deemed unnecessary because of its causation ground. These are the lower court's reasons, not controlling current law or inevitable conclusions for this Court.

An amicus urged special protection of university academic freedom. The lower court rejected that argument by distinguishing the chair appointment from the retained faculty position. No other amicus or party concession is established.

## Law entering the case

The accompanying [entering-law clarification](../entering-law/OT_1997CHUNK1_JEFFRIES.md) identifies exact controlling components, Standards titles, effective dates and source locations. Its propositions are summarized here so that the packet's decisional choices remain self-contained.

**Protected employee/citizen expression and the employment balance.** The First Amendment and §1983 supply the constitutional claim. *Pickering*, 391 U.S. 563; *Connick*, 461 U.S. 138; and *Givhan*, 439 U.S. 410, preserve content, form and context, public-concern speech, and the government's legitimate interests in performing its work. Public employment does not make every statement official speech. Off-campus delivery does not erase legitimate employment consequences either. Analyze this actual address and the chair's actual role; neither a general official-duty exclusion nor a special absolute academic-speech exemption is established by the supplied law. *Settle*'s student-assignment rule supplies no categorical authority to treat this public address as controlled curriculum. Academic freedom may inform the public interest and functional analysis, but tenure and a chair appointment are distinct interests.

Reasonable forecasts of operational disruption may justify action without waiting for actual harm. The speech's public value and the actual relationship remain part of the legal balance. Dislike of a viewpoint, controversy or embarrassment cannot simply be renamed a service need. Equally, public concern and an offensive statement's connection to a larger public discussion do not immunize all employment consequences. Supported effects on performance, necessary working relationships and actual institutional functions can matter. No new evidentiary checklist, actual-disruption prerequisite or least-severe-sanction requirement enters from these authorities.

**Waters's precise authority.** Its 1994-05-31 safe harbor has seven express supporters: a genuine, reasonable account of speech protects discipline only when the governing speech balance permits discipline on that account and the employer actually relied upon it. Six support the separate proposition that an unreasonable, false account cannot defeat actually protected speech bearing the required causal relationship to the action. Seven support its limited motive-based remand. There is no complete controlling investigative framework under *Marks*. This record primarily presents the legal sufficiency of a forecast and actual causation; it does not establish an employer mistake about the words spoken. Reasonableness about facts or prediction cannot substitute for a legal conclusion that the actual speech was disciplinable.

**Employment benefit and actual cause.** Retention of tenure and lack of an entitlement to a three-year chair appointment do not alone defeat a retaliation claim concerning the shortened term. *Perry v. Sindermann*, 408 U.S. 593, and *Umbehr*'s 1996-06-28 application preserve the distinction between property entitlement and unconstitutional conditions. *O'Hare*, also 1996-06-28, separates functional affiliation requirements from speech balancing. The chair title supplies neither a policymaker exception nor a guaranteed appointment.

Under *Mt. Healthy*, 429 U.S. 274, Jeffries must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role in the adverse action. Defendants may establish by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred absent that protected conduct. *Waters*, *Umbehr*, *O'Hare*, and the unanimous *Crawford-El* components of 1997-06-16 preserve this actual counterfactual. A conceivable lawful explanation is insufficient. Competent direct and circumstantial evidence are available under *Kimberlin* and *Crawford-El*; no special clear-and-convincing motive burden applies. These rules do not establish causation from mere official title, malice, attendance or association with a decision.

**Independent defenses and relief.** *Harlow*, 457 U.S. 800, and *Anderson v. Creighton*, 483 U.S. 635, retain particularized conduct-date immunity for personal damages. Later decisions cannot themselves supply earlier notice. The initial chair-term action occurred in late October 1991; any distinct March 1992 replacement action must be assessed at its own date if within the preserved claim. The 1994–1997 decisions do not retrospectively supply notice for either. This packet makes no final clearly-established-law determination. Qualified immunity is distinct from the underlying violation and from otherwise available prospective relief. Punitive answers cannot cure missing constitutional protection, causal injury or personal responsibility. Entity/capacity attribution and each remedy require their own predicates. No automatic reinstatement, damages, new trial or immunity follows from a general speech holding.

*NTEU*'s 1995-02-22 prospective-restraint rule requires a justification commensurate with the actual burden of its broad honoraria ban; it does not automatically move that entire burden structure into this individual appointment dispute. *Settle*, 1996-06-25, preserves genuine grounds and actual cause within its student-assignment setting. Neither creates a universal employment investigation code. ## Competing lawful paths and their strongest limitations

These are analytical alternatives from the supported record and law, not claims about unrecovered briefing.

1. **Sustain the judgment on the substantive balance.** A court could conclude that the reasonable forecast, genuinely relied upon and connected to the chair's actual responsibilities, outweighs the public interest in this speech. Actual disruption need not have occurred. If the challenged speech was legally disciplinable, speech motivation and the punitive answers do not by themselves establish a protected-speech injury. The strongest objection is that a verdict finding a reasonable expectation does not identify the constitutionally sufficient harm or perform the legal balance; ministerial duties and the public, independent setting can weaken the institutional justification. This path requires the actual balance, not an automatic reasonable-belief defense.

2. **Vacate because the substantive ground does not justify final defense judgment.** The Court could determine that the appellate treatment insufficiently accounts for the public concern, private-citizen setting, actual chair functions or relation of predicted harm to service. A supported legal error can warrant remand under the correct standard without deciding final protection or liability. The strongest objection is that *Connick* and *Waters* permit prediction and that the appellate court did purport to balance, rather than merely invoke a forecast. A contrary result must identify its actual legal or record deficiency; absence of realized harm, offense at the words or general academic status alone cannot decide the case. No supposed investigation duty should be substituted for the disputed balance.

3. **Sustain an independently sufficient causation defense.** Even if relevant speech was protected, an established independent lawful Board decision or failure of personal causal responsibility could support judgment for some or all defendants. Nine votes for the shortened term, separated from the six defendants identified by later answers, materially support examining that route. Its strongest limitation is that absence of the same retaliatory animus does not itself prove that those nine would impose the same shortened term absent protected speech. Nor do nine votes automatically sever the causal effect of officials who procured the proposal. Conversely, presentation of the proposal, an unsuccessful immediate-removal vote or an abstention does not automatically establish causal responsibility. The inquiry must distinguish the nine successful voters, three remaining immediate-removal voters, abstaining trustee and two nonvoting officials. It must explain actual lawful cause or the same-decision showing, not infer it from arithmetic alone.

4. **Remand for bounded causation/verdict treatment.** If neither appellate ground supports final judgment, unresolved personal contribution and incompatible verdict answers may require ordinary further proceedings. The lower opinion's own conflict assessment supports this possibility. Its strongest limitation is that verdict answers must be considered under their actual instructions and procedural form, and some apparently different answers may address distinct questions. This packet cannot declare a definitive Rule 49 inconsistency, waiver or mandatory new trial without the missing forms and preservation record. Rejection of a final-defense ground does not automatically revive every district-court award or establish Jeffries's entitlement to judgment. A narrowly reasoned remand can leave the lower courts to harmonize answers if legally possible, determine the consequence if not, and address independently preserved defenses.

All paths preserve severability where the record and actual appellate posture support different treatment of actors, components or remedies. Reinstatement requires a live and legally available chair remedy; the passage of time alone does not establish mootness, an expired injunction, continued eligibility or entitlement to a fresh term. The damages controversy cannot simply be equated with the availability of prospective relief. No unsupported threshold defect should be created from the absence of original materials in the research collection.
