## Conclusion and precise judgment

Each of Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer independently supports this formal disposition on the same grounds already frozen:

**Reverse the Fifth Circuit's judgment insofar as it affirms summary judgment for the school district on the appealed Title IX claim, and remand for ordinary further proceedings.**

The reversal establishes that the reviewed summary judgment cannot stand on the legal basis those Justices reject. It does not enter judgment establishing the district's liability, direct an award, resolve disputed evidence or require a trial. Subject to the governing mandate and ordinary procedures, the district may ultimately prevail if the claimant fails to establish the applicable elements or a supported defense defeats relief. Unappealed claims and defendants remain outside the disposition.

The original vacatur/remand formulation remains an available expression of their requested relief. In this particular setting, the four do not reserve the correctness of the legal premise on which they object to summary judgment: they reject an exclusive higher-management-notice requirement and seek adjudication under the already identified attribution alternatives. Formal reversal therefore accurately expresses their legal-error determination while preserving the same further-proceedings destination. This is a specific compatibility conclusion, not a rule that reversal and vacatur are interchangeable in all cases.

## Rationale and mandate limits

Kinman remains controlling unless a final proposition-level majority lawfully changes it. The four continue to maintain their published disagreement with its exclusive recipient threshold. Their agreement in a formal reversal/remand does not itself overturn Kinman, adopt a new actual-awareness inference, find this record sufficient under Kinman, or establish that the school deliberately disregarded a known danger.

The narrower judgment-agreement provision in RECONCILED_B carries over to this precisely bounded formal reversal: each may agree that the present Title IX judgment should be set aside and the claim returned for ordinary proceedings while withholding the narrower reasons or recipient instruction governing that return. Their own published Kinman judgment positions support that limited agreement. They retain their contrary attribution grounds. No common rationale follows merely from the common formal disposition, and no additional join is inferred for another Justice's reasoning. A materially different mandate — immediate recipient liability, conclusive termination of the claim, a new prerequisite beyond existing Kinman, or adjudication of unappealed claims — is outside this clarification.

Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain the proposed avoidable-consequences defense: the district must prove adequately publicized internal remedies likely to provide redress without undue risk, effort or expense; unreasonable failure to use those measures bars recovery only to the extent it consequently caused avoidable harm. A paper policy or nonreporting alone does not suffice. Stevens reserves that defense. Nothing in the choice of the word reverse changes those respective positions or makes the proposed defense controlling law.

All ordinary proof, causation and remedial limits in RECONCILED_B remain. No mandatory trial, damages, administrative enforcement, new regulatory cause of action, automatic teacher-status attribution, additional factual premise or new historical departure is adopted.
