## Event and controlling distinctions

Ordinary review under §1254(1) concerns the Ninth Circuit's December 17, 1997 en banc judgment in No. 95-99025, 132 F.3d 463. That judgment vacated the panel opinion, reversed the district court, and directed a habeas writ requiring a new trial. It did not order only a hearing or an acquittal, and it supplied no fixed retrial deadline. The operative federal petition was filed in August 1995. No additional Supreme Court docket, grant, argument, execution setting or party filing is invented.

The following rules and limits govern every commitment below, including a Justice who previously dissented from them:

1. **Transition is provision specific.** Lindh's June 23, 1997 holding covers a pre-enactment noncapital application and preserves separately directed provisions. Its chapter 153/chapter 154 comparison supplies a reason to extend its construction to a pending capital application outside qualifying chapter 154, but that extension is a question here, not a holding already entered. Chapter 154's pending-case clause operates only upon its counsel-system qualifications. None is established for Arizona. Felker's post-enactment authorization proceeding does not turn this August 1995 application into a post-enactment application or establish the reach of every amended merits, hearing or successive provision.
2. **Former §2254(d)(8) retains its own work.** A state credibility finding is presumptively correct unless the applicable statutory exception is established; the relevant exception asks whether the finding is fairly supported by the record as a whole. A different plausible inference does not itself remove the presumption. Conversely, the existence of a state finding cannot conclusively exclude all new evidence or foreclose examination of a genuine exception. Marshall v. Lonberger supplies the deference principle; the former statute supplies the whole-record exception. The state judge's failure to preside at the original trial is not itself a reason to disregard the later findings after observing the postconviction witnesses.
3. **The gateway is a probability inquiry about reasonable jurors.** Under the January 23, 1995 module **Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims**, reliable new evidence must establish, on the whole probative record, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. This is not the reviewing judge's doubt, ordinary prejudice, probable acquittal by some jurors, Brady materiality, or affirmative freestanding innocence. Trial admissibility does not delimit the evidence; the tape offer and the adverse family accounts must be assessed with the exculpatory evidence. Reliability, timing, motives, inconsistency and corroboration matter. Neither recantation nor impeachment nor a prisoner source is categorically disqualified.
4. **Abuse and other gates remain distinct.** The State's clear and particular abuse pleading, identifying prior history and new claims, precedes the prisoner's response burden. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate. Former §2244(b)'s prior-hearing alternatives and both exceptions retain their actual terms: an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and satisfaction that it was not deliberately withheld or otherwise abusive. New evidence does not automatically establish a new ground; lack of deliberate withholding does not eliminate all abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when those conditions and other applicable gates are satisfied. No discretionary refusal may rest on a mistaken gateway standard. No general mandatory-review rule after every gateway showing is supplied here.
5. **Three attribution theories are separate.** Actual possession or knowledge of favorable information by prosecutors or police acting for this prosecution falls within Kyles, even if the trial prosecutor personally lacked the information. A proposed obligation to obtain a known central witness's corrections file before using him is an acquisition rule beyond that established case-team rule. Imputation of all knowledge in every government office is broader still and is expressly outside current Kyles. The lower opinion's inconclusive statement about individual prosecutors' possession establishes neither possession nor absence of case-team knowledge. A police officer's knowledge of Dunbar's informant status establishes no finding that this officer knew every significant item in the DOC file.
6. **Brady has independent elements.** Under **Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility**, effective April 19, 1995, the prisoner must establish favorable evidence, suppression attributable to this prosecution, and collective reasonable-probability materiality. The lawful preparation, investigation and impeachment uses of the proven suppressed evidence are considered together with the whole record; no item must independently be material. Knowing a file exists is not knowing every item in it. Conversely, information actually known to the defense cannot simply be relabeled suppressed. The April 18, 1978 letter therefore matters without establishing that counsel knew the file's entire contents.
7. **A completed Brady violation needs no second prejudice screen.** Kyles incorporates the necessary prejudice in materiality. It requires neither a second harmlessness assessment of the same suppression nor a showing that acquittal is more likely than conviction. Brecht, O'Neal and Roy retain their actual category-specific holdings; they supply no general Kotteakos rule to defeat an established Brady violation here. Passing Schlup proves none of the Brady elements, and satisfying Brady materiality proves no Schlup gateway.
8. **Teague cannot be silently supplied or silently bypassed.** If the acquisition duty were made necessary to the writ, whether it was dictated at the actual finality date and whether a properly presented nonretroactivity defense bars it would require separate resolution. Caspari's preserved same-rule defense, Schiro's discretion concerning a late defense, and Lambrix's absence of an inflexible ordering rule retain their conditions. The supplied record establishes neither the exact finality date for this inquiry nor presentation of a Teague defense to the proposed acquisition rule. No waiver, preservation, or new-rule holding is inferred. The existing case-team rule is not converted into the broader acquisition rule by calling both Brady.
9. **No freestanding innocence writ follows.** Herrera supplies a record-specific insufficiency holding and leaves a uniform freestanding test unresolved. The conflicting accounts, possible joint participation and physical evidence do not support an affirmative declaration of Carriger's innocence. The present lower judgment rests on Brady after a gateway. No Justice's commitment below substitutes a freestanding writ for those missing predicates.
10. **Relief tracks the ground.** If a lawful gateway and a completed conviction-level Brady violation are established, conditional new-trial relief is the available remedy, with any reasonable retrial period fixed by the responsible lower court. If the gateway fails, this otherwise-abusive Brady ground supplies no writ. A remand correcting legal error or resolving genuinely material attribution is bounded by those questions and the applicable factual-development rules; it does not automatically require a hearing, unrestricted discovery, acquittal, immediate release or a stay. Unreached mitigation, competence and resentencing-counsel claims remain for their own lawful dispositions and do not disappear with the Brady rationale.

## Component commitments

It is not a finding of possession or an unconditional merits holding. If the attributable suppressed set is narrower, its collective materiality must be assessed on that actual set.

## Enhanced alternatives by component

### Applicable law

**Former-law path.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would decide that the chapter 153 merits and factual-treatment amendments at issue do not govern this already pending application. Each joined Ginsburg's Lindh opinion. Its decisive statutory contrast is between chapter 154's express pending-case clause and the omission of that instruction from the ordinary amendments enacted alongside it. That comparison does not depend on whether the already filed application concerns a capital sentence. The proper extension is to the ordinary chapter 153 treatment of this application, not to an exemption from all of AEDPA.

**Strongest contrary path.** Lindh expressly resolved noncapital proceedings, and Congress separately regulated capital habeas in chapter 154. A court must therefore test a capital case against the actual statutory provisions rather than enlarge Lindh by a label. The five answer that chapter 154's special reach requires a qualifying counsel system; capital status alone cannot supply it. No qualification is established here. The original federal application also preceded enactment, unlike Felker's authorization proceeding. Their joins stop at these identified ordinary amendments and do not decide every later procedural event or the operation of every successive provision.

**Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** Their published join in Thomas's Felker temporal concurrence identifies the activity regulated by the particular provision and rejects a universal timing answer. Scalia's Lindh dissent, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, supplies more specific personal support for applying amended §2254(d) to a still-pending adjudication: it regulates present relief authority, and chapter 154's distinct State-qualified scheme does not, in that view, reliably imply chapter 153's contrary reach. The controlling majority rejected that statutory inference in Lindh's noncapital setting; their dissent cannot erase that holding. Here they would not resolve the separate capital question merely to deny a claim that fails under former law. The strongest objection is that a habeas opinion should identify governing law before discussing the gateway. Their sufficient disposition expressly assumes the former regime, applies its controlling probability standard, and relies on no restrictive amendment; the outcome is independent of the disputed transition. They would not join a broad declaration that a pre-enactment filing alone disables every AEDPA restriction. If a later disposition requires affirmative relief or new fact development, that necessary provision's temporal operation must first be decided. Their published present-relief-authority rationale supplies a competing §2254(d) path, not a vote here that amended §2254(e), every successive rule, or every Title I provision governs. This reservation is not a present holding that the new statute applies.

### State findings and gateway

**Sustain the lower gateway.** The strongest supported path combines Dunbar's detailed confession without immunity, its framing explanation, the later confession report, the file's pattern of shifting blame, and the dependence of much of the physical evidence on Dunbar. The supposedly corroborating proof would look different to a reasonable juror aware that the principal witness had repeatedly manufactured accounts. His inaccurate explanation of how he acquired crime-scene knowledge and fear of capital prosecution when recanting strengthen the defense. The lower majority also recited the correct probability formula and expressly applied it in its final gateway paragraphs. Those features make the case stronger than a bare recantation.

**Counterargument.** The record permits guilt by joint participation rather than exclusive responsibility. Joyce's account, Larry White's understanding, Patience's report and Stephanie's account cannot be treated as exclusively exculpatory. The tape reconstruction is a probative offer excluded after withdrawal of the challenge and a relevance ruling, not a judicial finding that the science was unreliable. Its weaknesses and defense-testing limits remain relevant, but it cannot be erased for inadmissibility. The state judge observed the confession and its retraction. A finding that an alternative interpretation is equally plausible does not show that the state finding lacked fair support. Finally, the majority's footnote 4 expressly substituted confidence in the actual verdict for the hypothetical-juror question; its treatment of participation evidence may reflect that substitution. Correct introductory and concluding words cannot cure a materially different operative inquiry.

**Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer select corrected assessment, not present gateway success.** Stevens authored, and the other three fully joined, both Schlup's probability rule and its bounded legal-error remand. Each also joined Felker's later, record-specific refusal to find that gateway met despite substantial new scientific criticism. They therefore reject both automatic passage whenever evidence undermines a trial witness and automatic failure whenever contrary evidence survives. Here the lower court used incompatible descriptions of its task and did not adequately connect the inculpatory family evidence, tape offer and state findings to a complete properly instructed-juror assessment. Those are concrete defects warranting renewed consideration. The strongest objection to remand is that the complete published record already requires rejection. Their answer is confined: meaningful exculpatory evidence remains, its role is interdependent with contested credibility and physical-proof inferences, and the required inquiry has not reliably been performed under a single correct standard. A remand permits an adverse conclusion; it is not a commitment that the gate will open.

**O'Connor selects gateway failure.** Her full Schlup join prevents substituting a stricter innocence burden, and her Kyles join prevents confusing a substantial Brady claim with failure of the gateway. Her Herrera plurality and concurrence, however, separately examine delayed and inconsistent innocence evidence against surviving physical proof rather than treat serious doubt as decisive. Her later Felker join likewise accepts failure under the actual probability standard. On this supplied record, even allowing Dunbar's confession probative weight, the evidence of joint action and the tape evidence permit a reasonable properly instructed juror to find participation beyond a reasonable doubt. The repeated false accounts and motives give fair support to the state credibility finding. The strongest remand argument is the lower majority's legal error and untested tape reconstruction. She finds that correction does not require an additional factfinding opportunity on this gateway: the reliable new material does not reduce the supported adverse assessment to the probability required. This is not a rule that one possible guilty juror always defeats Schlup, nor a finding that every defense account is false.

**Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas select gateway failure under current law.** Kennedy authored the earlier stricter-standard dissent, with the joins specified in the slice; Scalia and Thomas supplied their separate qualified-refusal ground. Those dissents are not the operative standard. Their shared later Felker join demonstrates acceptance of a record-specific insufficiency conclusion under controlling Schlup. Here each accepts the confession and impeachment for assessment rather than excludes them categorically, but finds the adverse family evidence, tape, association and postcrime handling of property sufficient to sustain reasonable guilty assessment to a degree inconsistent with Carriger's demanding probability burden. The best objection is that much of the physical case came through a discredited witness. Their answer rests on the independent participation evidence and the difference between showing that Dunbar was guilty and showing that no reasonable juror would find Carriger guilty. They would not join the lower dissent's categorical statement that a repudiated recantation can never qualify, its demand for an affirmative innocence verdict by twelve imagined jurors, or an assumption that every item in the tape offer is conclusively established.

**Separate findings remain separate.** The four remanding Justices do not themselves hold the state credibility finding unsupported. The four rejecting the gate regard it as fairly supported and independently assess the expanded record, including the later cellmate account. None treats the finding as conclusive on evidence and events it did not assess. No Justice's position changes the statutory exception into de novo credibility review merely because the appellate court prefers another view.

### Materiality, acquisition, actual attribution and nonretroactivity

**Conditional full-file materiality path.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer apply the Kyles opinion they joined; Souter authored it. The file supplies more than additional convictions: a pattern of blaming others, indications of violence and armed conduct contradicting the presentation of a truthful nonviolent burglar, and leads for preparation and examination of the investigation. Those uses directly concern the indispensable witness and the prosecutor's emphatic reliance on his honesty. Collective evaluation can undermine confidence without proving that Carriger was elsewhere or excluding joint participation. This is an independent, lesser prejudice inquiry than Schlup. The confession and later material may illuminate the gateway and record; the trial-disclosure counterfactual must still concern lawful uses of evidence that should have been disclosed then, not a fiction that testimony first produced years later was available in 1978.

**Strongest nonmateriality path.** The jury knew six burglaries, immunity and a gun-form falsehood; counsel was alerted to the file and informant history; the tape, keys and conduct supply corroboration. Additional bad-character material may be partly cumulative or inadmissible. The five answer that the supported lawful uses concern the exact framing defense and the quality and independence of the investigation, not merely a larger tally of wrongdoing. Kyles rejects both item-by-item dismissal and legal sufficiency as the complete materiality answer. They nevertheless withhold an operative violation because the suppressed set and the legally attributable duty remain unresolved. Their conditional conclusion cannot be applied to a materially smaller set without renewed collective assessment.

**Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not reach a materiality disposition.** Their Kyles dissent accepted cumulative analysis while finding the surviving corroboration persuasive on that different record. It supports close attention here to the tape and defense knowledge, not a categorical conclusion that impeachment of an already impeached witness is immaterial. The strongest pro-relief argument is the specific habitual framing evidence and its effect on investigation. Each accepts that this can be materially different from additional burglary convictions. Their gateway disposition makes the merits choice unnecessary; none supplies a vote for treating the full file as nonmaterial, a requirement of probable acquittal, or a second harmlessness screen. Any later lawful materiality decision must distinguish evidence actually known to the defense and assess the collectively useful suppressed remainder.

**Existing case-team path.** All eight accept the current Kyles rule as governing. If prosecutors or investigating police acting for this case held or knew the pertinent favorable information, the prosecutor's personal ignorance does not excuse disclosure of material evidence. There is no need to invent a new all-agency rule to enforce that responsibility. The strongest objection is factual: the source does not find the relevant possession or knowledge, and an investigator's familiarity with Dunbar as an informant is not knowledge of the complete prison file. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer therefore support a bounded attribution determination only after a lawful route to merits is established. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas leave that factual question unresolved because this gateway fails; they do not adjudicate the absence of team knowledge.

**Known-central-witness acquisition path.** The strongest argument for the lower rule is limited and concrete: a prosecutor choosing to make a known career criminal the centerpiece of a murder case can obtain an identified corrections file and should not assure the jury of the witness's credibility while remaining avoidably ignorant of that file. Brady and Giglio protect trial fairness, not merely prosecutorial good faith. This rationale does not require searching every agency in every prosecution.

**The eight decline to sustain the present writ on that acquisition rule.** Even the narrowed proposal changes the operative trigger from information held by those acting for the prosecution to information that an outside repository could supply. Kyles's present affirmative duty stops at the former; it expressly disclaims unrestricted investigation and universal imputation. A witness's criminal history and the importance of his testimony do not establish that the repository acted for this prosecution. Stevens and Souter preserve the possibility that a properly presented, factually distinct practice of deliberately avoiding case-team information would fall within existing Brady; no such practice is found here. Ginsburg and Breyer insist that the constitutional duty be tied to the actual organizational relationship, not the convenience of access alone. O'Connor requires that same link before importing the file as suppressed. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject enlarging the obligation merely because the State could have acquired more impeachment. These are limitations on the current sufficient ground, not a factual finding that Arizona lacked the evidence.

**All-government imputation fails for every Justice.** Government custody somewhere is not enough. The proposed witness-specific duty must not be rejected or approved by substituting that much broader theory for it. Conversely, rejecting all-government imputation does not by itself decide whether the narrower acquisition theory is constitutionally sound. No present writ is supported on the narrower theory here because it is a disputed extension with unaddressed collateral predicates, whereas the lower court treated the duty as already supplied by Kyles.

**Nonretroactivity and narrower alternatives.** No Justice would cure that problem by inventing a preserved Teague defense or silently holding it waived. The four remanding commitments permit the actual case-team theory to be resolved under existing law if the gateway is satisfied; they do not direct adoption of a new duty. If acquisition alone remains indispensable, the court must determine the actual presentation and finality predicates and then the new-rule question before relief. A rule requiring new evidence acquisition regulates trial procedure; neither a substantive exception nor a watershed exception follows merely from the importance of impeachment. The contemporaneous exceptions remain available only on their actual terms. O'Connor's Caspari authorship and Stevens's narrower presentation position in that case are preserved: the majority's subsidiary-defense rule governs when its preservation and same-rule conditions exist, but those conditions are not supplied here. Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer's Lambrix joins preserve flexible sequencing of actual nonjurisdictional defenses without inventing one.

## Justice-specific join barriers and remedial limits

- **Stevens:** Will join former-law treatment, rejection of verdict-confidence as the gateway, full probative consideration, the bounded remand, existing team responsibility and conditional full-file materiality. Will not join present gateway failure as conclusively established, a categorical no-innocence-right declaration, automatic credibility rejection of recantations, or an acquisition-duty writ unsupported by its own collateral predicates. His authored Schlup and joined Kyles support the positive propositions; his Herrera and Felker writings preserve a possible freestanding claim without converting it into this remedy.
- **O'Connor:** Will join the bounded statutory extension and reject the gate on this record under controlling probability law. Will not join a stricter substituted quantum, a rule requiring affirmative freestanding innocence to obtain Brady relief, or an assumption that knowing a prison file exists defeats every suppression claim. Her Kyles join supports conditional materiality while her Herrera and Felker positions support keeping later innocence proof and procedural entitlement distinct. A legal-error remand is generally permissible; she regards the complete present gateway submission as insufficient and therefore opposes further gateway proceedings here.
- **Scalia:** Will join rejection under the governing gateway and preservation of actual case-team responsibility as current law. Will not join an unrestricted acquisition obligation, imputation of all State files, or mandatory reconsideration simply because the lower opinion contains an erroneous formulation where the record independently fails the proper gate. His prior statutory-refusal position retains both statutory qualifications and does not supply a new finding of deliberate withholding. He does not make his categorical Herrera view a necessary ground of this disposition.
- **Kennedy:** Will join the record-specific gateway rejection, not an automatic rule about recantations or a stricter quantum as current law. His Herrera concurrence with O'Connor preserves the distinction between execution of established innocence and the present unproved claim; his Schlup dissent and later Felker join require testing what the new evidence establishes about this prisoner's participation. He will not join a finding that the disputed tape offer is scientifically conclusive or that the State actually lacked the DOC information.
- **Souter:** Will join correcting and remanding the gateway and requiring genuine attribution before a writ. His authored Kyles does not permit personal prosecutorial ignorance to sever actual police knowledge; equally, its defined case-team relationship is not universal governmental possession. He will not join a definitive no-gateway finding based merely on the continued existence of inculpatory evidence, or a rule disregarding impeachment and investigation uses. His Herrera and Felker positions preserve possible independent innocence relief without granting it here.
- **Thomas:** Will join current-standard gateway failure and leave unrelated AEDPA temporal questions undecided. His Felker temporal concurrence requires identifying the regulated event, and his Schlup and Kyles dissents do not authorize replacing current law. He will not join a discovery obligation based solely on the State's theoretical capacity to obtain the file or a finding that the gateway itself compels a writ. His freestanding objection remains an additional reserved view, not a replacement for the operative gateway analysis.
- **Ginsburg:** Will join the limited capital extension of Lindh, legal-error remand, distinct attribution findings and conditional cumulative materiality. She will not join blanket prospective exemption from AEDPA, assumed chapter 154 qualification, an appellate credibility choice disguised as the §2254(d)(8) exception, or an agency-wide discovery obligation without an identified prosecution relationship. Her authored Lindh and full Schlup/Kyles joins support the specific selected grounds; no pre-appointment Herrera vote is attributed to her.
- **Breyer:** Will join the same statutory extension, correction of the gateway and bounded attribution inquiry. His Schlup, Kyles, Lindh and Felker joins permit serious record assessment and a materiality finding while rejecting automatic relief from disputed evidence. He will not join reliance on speculative investigative fruits, conversion of the new confession into evidence that could have been disclosed at trial, or an automatic hearing because further inquiry might be useful. No pre-appointment Herrera vote is attributed to him.

For the four remanding commitments, the work permitted is: apply the correct probability standard to the full probative record; explain the statutory treatment of state findings; decide any genuinely necessary factual development under its own governing requirements; and, only if a lawful route to merits exists, determine actual prosecution-team and defense knowledge and the collective materiality of the resulting suppressed set. A successful gateway does not dispense with independently applicable exhaustion, nonretroactivity or other claim conditions. For the four gateway-rejecting commitments, the disposition defeats this otherwise-abusive Brady rationale, not every unaddressed claim. Both paths leave the proper lower court to dispose of mitigation, competence and resentencing-counsel claims to the extent independently available. Neither path itself issues a writ, retrial clock, release order, acquittal, new sentence or execution protection.
