## Supported alternatives, counterarguments and join barriers

### 1. Impairment and reproduction's classification

**Supported path A:** Treat HIV infection as a physical impairment even before its most serious symptoms, then determine the remaining statutory elements separately. Section 36.104 expressly includes HIV disease whether symptomatic or asymptomatic within impairment. Its major-life-activities examples are illustrative. Reproduction falls within the statutory language even though it is not performed daily and not everyone chooses it. The text imposes no economic-activity restriction.

**Strongest counterargument:** Arline does not decide the status of an asymptomatic HIV carrier, and its tuberculosis plaintiff had a demonstrated record of substantial impairment. Neither an agency disease list nor the importance of procreation can establish that this plaintiff satisfies every part of §12102(2)(A). The answer is to preserve the three separate inquiries, not to treat Arline's reservation as an exclusion or the regulatory impairment definition as a conclusive disability rule.

**Supported path B:** Assume reproduction's inclusion and resolve the plaintiff's individual entitlement to summary judgment first. This is the narrower position Scalia and Thomas take. Their Yeskey and Joiner joins support preserving an unresolved element and limiting a mandate to what the record and legal question establish. They will not join a categorical exclusion of reproduction, but do not adopt its classification merely to complete an unnecessary proposition. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer regard the classification as a properly presented legal issue warranting resolution.

**Counterargument to path B:** Individual evidence cannot be evaluated intelligently without explaining the legal function it concerns, and the classification is within the granted disability question. That supports the six affirmative classification joins; it does not manufacture additional joins from an assumption.

### 2. Individual substantial limitation

**Supported affirmance path:** A serious medical restriction on having a child without exposing that child to infection can substantially limit reproduction even when conception remains possible. The supplied evidence reports untreated transmission risk around 25% and treated risk around 8%, together with Abbott's specific unchallenged testimony that HIV controlled her decision against childbearing. These are this record's historical evidentiary figures, not a judicial risk threshold or a rule for all individuals. The statute does not make proof of a pre-infection intention to conceive a condition of protection. The general answer about life functions can be accepted as her description of ordinary functioning without treating it as a retraction of the specific testimony.

**Strongest counterargument:** Abbott moved for summary judgment. Her broad negative answer can support a competing inference about the importance or actual limitation of the identified function in her own life; a factfinder could distinguish an abstract medical risk from a substantial individual limitation. An uncontradicted statement of a decision is not automatically conclusive proof of the statutory degree of limitation. The five affirmance commitments rest on the narrower view that the specific testimony supplies the individual connection and the broader lay characterization disputes no underlying medical or childbearing fact. They do not rest on a presumption of disability or on discrediting her answer.

**Supported vacatur path:** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would leave the individual determination open because those passages do not compel the same inference when ordinary summary-judgment protection is given to Bragdon. Their objection is the finality of the plaintiff's proof, not a requirement to attempt conception, a rule that only a person actively planning parenthood is protected, or a medical finding that the risk is small. O'Connor's Anderson and Celotex joins reinforce a distinction between a viable claim and a claim established as a matter of law. Scalia's and Thomas's Yeskey and Joiner joins likewise preserve separate elements and the difference between a supported inference and compelled judgment.

**Strongest counterargument to vacatur:** Requiring an additional showing of subjective reproductive desire can silently add words to the ADA and discount a functional limitation solely because the plaintiff has adapted her choices to it. The three vacatur commitments therefore impose no such element. They permit coverage upon proper application to the individual's record and do not foreclose the record-of or regarded-as alternatives if properly presented below. The unknown pre-infection plans do not establish either absence of limitation or a litigation default.

**Join boundary:** The five affirmance Justices will join an actual-disability holding confined to the supported individual record. They will not join a rule that diagnosis alone always establishes disability. The three vacatur Justices will not join the final individual application or an equivalent per se rule. No Associate supplies a controlling alternative holding under clauses (B) or (C). The lower supplement now establishes that the United States advanced a regarded-as contention and that the circuit did not decide it; it does not establish every necessary proposition for this Court to resolve that alternative. The five actual-disability joiners need not reach it; the three seeking vacatur leave available properly presented alternative grounds for lower-court consideration. No unpreserved new theory is invented.

### 3. Objective direct threat and the proposed provider deference

**Supported objective path:** Under §12182(b)(3) and §36.208, exclusion must rest on an individualized, objectively supported assessment of a significant risk to others that cannot be eliminated through the statutory modifications or aids. The regulation requires reasonable judgment relying on current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence, considering the risk's nature, duration, severity, probability and potential mitigation. A provider's status does not supply a legal presumption that these requirements are met. A subjective honest belief is insufficient. The relevant information is what was available when treatment was refused, not a later scientific correction supplied as contemporaneous notice.

**Strongest counterargument:** A provider must make a practical clinical judgment amid uncertain knowledge; judges should not replace supported professional assessment with hindsight or a demand for definitive proof of actual transmission. Arline reserved private-physician deference, and the regulation expressly refers to reasonable judgment. The answer is to take the provider's objective evidence and justified medical inferences seriously, including uncertainty where supported, without transforming a statutory condition into immunity for any reasonable private belief. No Associate requires a confirmed previous dental transmission as an invariant prerequisite or rules that an unconfirmed case has no evidentiary relevance.

**Supported competing formulation:** A reasonable private medical judgment may receive persuasive weight commensurate with its foundation and fit. All eight accept that formulation as evidentiary consideration; they reject it if “deference” means a special legal privilege that settles the exception whenever the provider expresses a reasonable view. Public-health judgments also remain subject to their medical basis and actual scope. Arline's guidance concerning public-health expertise does not make a professional association the CDC or make either institution's label conclusive. Stevens and O'Connor retain their Arline commitments; Scalia's earlier dissent does not require ignoring the ADA's express defense; Kennedy's Joiner opinion and the other Associates' joins require attention to the connection between material and conclusion on either side.

**Join boundary:** No categorical judicial deference to the defendant; no categorical rejection of private expertise; no conclusive public-health presumption; no requirement of zero risk; no rule that admission of an expert either proves direct threat or necessarily prevents summary judgment. The granted question does not require a new general appellate standard for every direct-threat determination or a new ruling allocating every trial burden.

### 4. Application within Question 3 and remedy after the complete lower-opinion supplement

**Actual lower method.** The First Circuit rejected a conclusive official-consensus rule and permitted qualified contrary medical proof. It nevertheless attached a continuing, intermediate presumption of correctness to the collective public-health judgment; its footnote 6 says the presumption remained available to the factfinder after contrary evidence appeared. The CDC text expressly recommended precautions to reduce risk. The court acknowledged that the text did not expressly establish safety or the absence of need for additional measures and inferred those propositions. The dental association separately stated that treatment with appropriate controls was safe. The later Marianos statements were limited because their relevant public availability was not established. The court also described Bragdon's firsthand sharp-injury report as anecdotal lay public-health testimony and discounted it on the ground that he was not an infectious-disease expert. It considered all eight proffers separately and together; a failure to consider cumulative evidence is not a permissible criticism.

**Bounded-remand path selected by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.** The defect is the legal treatment of medical judgment, not a finding that Bragdon's combined evidence must win or reach trial. Arline's respect for reasonable public-health judgment requires identification of the actual medical judgment and its supported scope. It does not authorize a court first to infer an official judgment beyond the agency's express proposition and then give that inferred judgment continuing presumptive legal force against the provider. The association's affirmative policy is important evidence, but its agreement with a judicial inference from the CDC cannot itself turn the inference into an established CDC judgment. A court may assess the inference's strength ordinarily; official status cannot do its evidentiary work for it.

The lower court's treatment of Bragdon's observations aggravates that method error. A witness may competently describe sharp injuries he experiences during his own procedures without being an infectious-disease expert. A scientific claim about the probability or significance of HIV transmission requires its separate foundation. The existing account supplies the actual distinction that the lower legal method failed adequately to maintain; no additional injury frequency or transmission probability is inferred. These six do not hold that those observations alone rebut a medical judgment or create a triable issue. They require their proper legal treatment as potential inputs to an otherwise supported medical inference.

This method mattered because it furnished the court's baseline for rejecting the private assessment and testing the proffers. The lower court's independent and cumulative discussion does not by itself remove the effect of that baseline. Finding it harmless would require the separately ungranted whole-record sufficiency determination. The six therefore vacate only the federal direct-threat determination and return it for reconsideration without the unsupported presumptive force and with personal observations distinguished from expert risk conclusions. The lower courts may reach the same summary-judgment result if ordinary rules warrant it. No trial, admission ruling, new evidence, confirmation requirement or numerical threshold is ordered.

**Strongest counterargument to the remand path.** Arline itself directs normal respect for reasonable public-health judgments, and the lower court rejected conclusiveness, allowed recognized private expertise, and discussed cumulative evidence. Its intermediate presumption can be understood as retained probative weight rather than a new burden; the private association's express policy and CDC protocols together can support the inference of safe routine care. The six answer narrowly: supported expertise may retain substantial probative weight, and an implicit scientific judgment may be proved. But the prescribed continuing presumption here was attached to a proposition the court itself supplied by inference, while the treatment of firsthand provider facts blurred ordinary competence and infectious-disease expertise. That combination warrants correcting the Question 3 method before accepting its application. This is not a universal ruling that every evidentiary presumption or every inference from an agency protocol is forbidden.

**Affirmance path selected by Stevens and Ginsburg.** They agree that provider status creates no special entitlement to deference and accept every evidentiary distinction just stated as a general constraint. They read the actual lower method differently. The court's intermediate presumption left the collective judgment available as evidence after rebuttal, permitted a contrary supported expert judgment, and imposed no conclusive rule. The association's express medical policy together with contemporaneous CDC infection-control guidance furnished a supported collective position. On that reading, the court gave expertise weight without eliminating the provider's opportunity to contest it. Its sharp-injury discussion rejected an unsupported leap from observed injury to statutory significant risk, rather than forbidding personal observation as such. They therefore identify no material Question 3 error requiring vacatur. They do not independently decide Question 5, infer absence of countervailing evidence from unavailable originals, or declare every treatment safe.

**Strongest counterargument to affirmance.** The opinion's express continuing-presumption language cannot be reduced to a statement that all evidence has some weight; the CDC inference and the lay-testimony wording show how the framework could affect the treatment of contrary material. Stevens and Ginsburg retain their affirmance only under their stated limiting reading of what that framework actually did. They will not join an opinion affirmatively endorsing an institutional label as sufficient foundation, a rule that an implied CDC conclusion automatically binds the factfinder, or exclusion of firsthand treatment observations for want of scientific credentials. They disagree with the six about whether the recovered lower opinion committed that legal error, not about a private provider's right to prevail whenever he acts in good faith.

**Justice-specific basis and scope.** Stevens's Arline join directly supports normal weight for public-health judgment, and his current-term Joiner separate position supplies a specific objection to expanding a legal grant into cumulative-record reconsideration. Ginsburg's Joiner join permits scrutiny of an inferential connection without requiring a study identical to the disputed treatment; her scope-register commitments support preserving the judgment when the granted legal challenge supplies no error. Kennedy's own Joiner opinion makes the actual connection, rather than an authoritative label, decisive and supports a limited mandate preserving unresolved questions. O'Connor's Arline join respects the actual reasonable official judgment, while her Anderson and Joiner joins prevent a presumption from resolving disputed underlying facts or bypassing inferential foundation. Scalia and Thomas rely on the statutory conditions and their Daubert/Joiner commitments against substituting status for support. Souter's joins in those same decisions support separating the actual proposition, inference and remaining merits, with the mandate limited accordingly. Breyer's Joiner concurrence values expert help while maintaining the judge's screening responsibility; it supplies no mandatory procedure or special institutional presumption. Shared earlier joins establish constraints, not mechanically identical outcomes on the disputed interpretation of this opinion.

The method issue is fairly included in Question 3 because the claimed entitlement to private-provider deference cannot be answered in application without deciding the legal force the lower court assigned to the competing public-health judgment. It does not establish a general appellate standard under separately ungranted Question 4, and no independent determination of the eight proffers' combined triability under Question 5 follows. No optional plain-error expansion is invoked. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas can join the risk rule and remedy while also seeking vacatur of individual disability; their coverage objection is not jurisdictional and the federal claim remains for resolution on remand.

**Temporal limit.** The ordinary inquiry concerns September 16, 1994 knowledge. The lower court expressly reserved an extreme later breakthrough overwhelmingly vindicating the provider, especially for prospective relief, while finding no such event here. No Associate adopts an exceptionless rule forbidding attention to current circumstances when framing prospective relief, and none finds that the reserved exception is triggered. The present legal holding decides the conduct-date assessment and leaves that hypothetical remedial issue open.

**Remaining evidence boundaries.** Seven possible dental-worker transmissions are not seven confirmed occupational transmissions. Forty-two documented cases among other health-care workers are not forty-two dental cases. The 1991 instrument study's call for research and testimony about absent positive spray evidence do not settle probability. The FDA blood-donation precaution does not itself establish a significant dental-treatment risk. A 1995 explanation cannot supply September 1994 knowledge. The CDC guideline, association policy, Marianos affidavits and post-refusal materials retain distinct institutional, temporal and inferential limits. The private expert Kuvin existed; the recovered opinion describes why it treated his identified later material as unavailable at the refusal date. None of these observations independently resolves the proffers' combined sufficiency.

**Original-record condition for a broader path.** The complete lower reasoning is recovered and neutrally validated; its method is no longer treated as unknown. The selected remand does not depend on reconstructing an exhibit's unpublished contents or finding an unreported admissibility ruling. A proposal to decide a particular scientific study's fit, the exact meaning of an unreproduced policy sentence, or the whole record's triability would need the relevant original material and, for Question 5, an independent scope basis. Missing originals remain research limits, not proof of default or absent evidence. No such broader path is committed.

## Operative propositions the eight Associates can jointly adopt

### A. Objective grounds for these initial seizures

An individualized vehicle stop to investigate insurance compliance requires objective grounds sufficient to justify that seizure. On this record, the old refusals to produce insurance information, considered with the intervening time, changed vehicles, valid inspection stickers and absence of fresh evidence of noncoverage, did not supply reasonable suspicion for stops 3–10 or 12. Anticipated refusal after a new stop cannot itself furnish the antecedent authority to make that stop.

Prouse requires sufficient objective justification for discretionary document-check stops; it is not confined to arbitrary selection of strangers. The known person and prior encounters are circumstances to consider, but do not eliminate the suspicion requirement. Bostick distinguishes permissible questioning from detention and rejects refusal alone as an automatic justification for seizure. Its consensual-encounter setting does not decide every consequence of violating a valid statutory production duty. The present decision rests on the actual insurance record, not a categorical rule that silence can never have evidentiary weight or that a valid inspection sticker conclusively establishes current insurance.

Whren preserves the independently observed violations supporting stops 1, 2 and 11, while requiring separate grounds for materially greater restraint. Ornelas supplies the current allocation of legal and factual review: the ultimate sufficiency of the undisputed initial-stop circumstances is independently reviewed, with protection of actual historical findings and genuine consideration of grounded officer inferences. No new observation or inference of actual noncoverage may be invented. Actual coverage is not substituted for Macaluso's knowledge. The renewed September refusal following stop 11 did not, on these circumstances, supply sufficient grounds for stop 12 two-and-a-half months later; the license/registration refusal and disputed alcohol observation occurred after initiation and cannot retroactively justify it.

No fixed staleness period, universal rule about insurance cards, general exemption from traffic enforcement, probable-cause requirement for every investigative stop, or constitutionalized state evidentiary rule is adopted. Stops are judged individually, not by counting total encounters or finding that Macaluso disliked Diamondstone.

### B. Fifth Amendment and the state insurance obligation remain distinct

The privilege can protect answers sought in a civil or informal setting when they expose the person to criminal responsibility. Lefkowitz v. Turley preserves that rule. The Vermont decriminalization and the actual nature of this insurance sanction, rather than the label alone, defeat this privilege claim on the supplied record. The earlier 1987 rulings concerned the earlier legal setting and do not settle the post-1989 question.

Byers supports the distinction between generally applicable financial-responsibility regulation and compelled criminal incrimination, but its separate rationales are not silently converted into a general majority exemption for regulatory questioning. No criminal use of this insurance information or disguised criminal sanction is supported here. The potential DUI accusation at stop 12 is not proof that compelled insurance information exposed Diamondstone to that accusation. No general DUI-interrogation privilege is decided.

The absence of this privilege does not establish grounds for a seizure. Nor does permission to consider silence establish that silence alone satisfies Vermont's insurance-liability requirements. The reported traffic-court decisions rejected that evidentiary sufficiency; the defendants' characterization of Administrative Procedure 38(D) and (E) does not become a holding that every refusal was an independently continuing offense. The Supreme Court need not resolve an unpresented state-law construction to decide these stops.

### C. Notice is defendant-specific and event-specific

Harlow and Anderson remain governing law. Immunity asks whether the particular defendant's conduct violated a federal right sufficiently established at the time, assessed objectively on the properly supported account of that defendant's information and acts. It does not turn on hostility, a claimed good heart, rank, or a rule that every unconstitutional seizure necessarily forfeits immunity. Anderson also does not demand a factual twin.

The traffic-court rulings supply neither issue preclusion against these defendants nor an independent federal right to damages. Nevertheless, an officer's receipt of a judicial explanation that the very asserted stop predicate is stale bears on whether continued reliance on that predicate can be objectively reasonable under already existing Fourth Amendment law. Davis v. Scherer excludes automatic forfeiture for a different state-law violation; it does not make the actual communications irrelevant to the federal inquiry. Prouse supplies the preconduct stop principle. Bostick existed before all twelve stops; its voluntary-questioning context limits the analogy. Subsequent simulated decisions state the current method but cannot supply notice retroactively.

The February 18 ruling and the June staleness ruling are not interchangeable. The former rejected the citation's evidentiary basis and addressed ambiguity as to which incident was charged; the latter directly addressed the legitimacy of continued stops. The source's June 20/June 30 discrepancy must remain disclosed. The stable sequence puts that ruling after stops 3–6 and before stops 7–10. ## Eastern Enterprises v. Apfel

### Questions and operative alternatives

The review concerns the constitutional retroactive tier-three liability assessed under section 9706(a)(3), not a renewed statutory assignment claim, other assignment tiers, unassigned beneficiaries, or the separate 1992 plan. Eastern employed these retirees before 1966 and participated in the early benefit undertaking. It also retained subsidiary ownership until 1987 and received substantial dividends; direct withdrawal in 1965 is not equated with severance of every economic relationship. Conversely, those later relationships are not proof of a lifetime promise or an established indemnity construction.

**E1 — Channel and remedy.** The due-process challenge to an enforced federal payment obligation is justiciable in the district court, with actual assessment supplying injury. Its existence does not depend on the Government's abandonment of a jurisdictional objection. The Tucker Act compensation question must be addressed independently for a takings ground. The three Justices choosing takings invalidity would permit declaratory/injunctive relief against this application because the challenged statutory scheme imposes ongoing payments to the privately operated fund and supplies no compensatory feature that would preserve its intended allocation through a Treasury award; their conclusion is about this statutory enforcement setting, not a general permission to enjoin a compensable appropriation. The remaining five do not use compensation jurisdiction to avoid the due-process merits or impose a claim for damages never sought. They reject the asserted takings ground at the antecedent property/regulatory distinction and do not decide an entitlement to Treasury compensation.

**E2 — Takings predicate and application.** The invalidity path treats the substantially retrospective extraction from Eastern's assets for this bounded class as an extraordinary regulatory burden examined under the economic-impact, reasonable-expectations and character factors applied in Concrete Pipe and Connolly. Large projected payments alone are not enough. The long retrospective reach, relationship between the pre-1966 undertaking and much later benefit guarantees, and concentrated assignment of their expense matter together. The competing path holds that an obligation to pay money from unspecified general assets does not identify an appropriated fund, parcel, earned interest, or other particular property interest; the objection belongs principally in due process. Phillips's ownership rule for actual interest does not make every payment obligation an appropriation. Concrete Pipe's rejection of a taking through a contextual analysis does not compel recognition of a taking for every general liability; at minimum its analysis supplies an independently sufficient rejection where applicable, not a categorical victory for either party here.

**E3 — Retroactive due process.** Carlton requires a legitimate purpose rationally served by the retrospective operation itself; reliance does not automatically defeat legislation, nor does revenue collection automatically validate it. Concrete Pipe sustains proportionate pooled-benefit allocation in its actual participation setting. The validity path finds a rational allocation to the employers whose workers earned benefits within the shared undertaking, with actual former employment and participation rather than assignment of strangers. A private contractual duration limit cannot alone constitutionalize immunity from later adjustment. The invalidity path finds the particular remote and severe imposition insufficiently connected to what this pre-1966 employer undertook or could reasonably have anticipated; employment and participation cannot by themselves justify assigning later lifetime obligations without limit. Neither path depends on a proved binding lifetime promise, an exact damages total, a fixed maximum retrospective period, or an invented fact about retained corporate control.

## Proposed common rationale and authority joins

All eight Associates directly support the following propositions at the stated scope; there is no aggregation of incompatible rationales and no proposed Marks holding.

1. **Identify the governmental function.** Agostini's actual updated Lemon inquiry governs: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine resolves conduct within its scope; an established permissible relationship resolves that relationship; a legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives ordinary inquiry into governmental attribution and function. Purpose, effects and institutional relationships are evidence within that one inquiry, not revived independent prongs. A school's genuinely academic teaching and performance of sacred music is distinct from organizing worship, prescribing religious profession or observance, adopting doctrine as public truth or soliciting adherence through public authority. Neither content and location alone nor an asserted artistic label decides the distinction. A secular goal cannot justify prohibited devotional means.
2. **The reported abstention option has a bounded function.** Sherman preserves protection against required words and gestures while distinguishing civic or academic activity from worship. The option supports rejection of the original compelled-singing theory. It is not an answer to a sufficiently alleged official-worship claim or necessarily to actual practical religious pressure. Lee's two grounds and Ingebretsen remain at their actual scopes; no demand for formal punishment, quantified pressure, conversion or successful religious persuasion is added to the applicable establishment rule.
3. **Actual conduct and ordinary procedure matter.** Settle permits genuine curricular judgment while rejecting constitutional immunity from an educational label. Its actual-ground distinction, and Crawford-El's ordinary-proof and discovery limits, forbid treating a possible lawful explanation as an established actual reason or using a categorical proof barrier to erase material evidence. Neither decision makes every motive allegation sufficient, directs unlimited discovery or imposes a special classroom investigation code.
4. **The amendment ruling leaves proof and defenses separate.** Foman and the date-effective Rules govern amendment; Leatherman rejects an invented municipal heightened pleading rule without relaxing Monell/Canton. Diamondstone retains Harlow/Anderson's particularized, objective conduct-time defense and requires individual causal responsibility. Present constitutional doctrine does not itself supply notice for 1994–95 conduct. Lee and other then-existing authority must be examined for the actual challenged act and official; later Settle, Sherman, Agostini, Ingebretsen, Tanford, Chaudhuri and Diamondstone cannot create earlier notice.
5. **Relief tracks the surviving claim.** Arizonans and Spencer require a continuing personal controversy and legally available relief; they do not extinguish a real damages claim upon graduation or create one against an unavailable entity/capacity. Correcting a futility premise changes neither damages availability nor immunity automatically.

## Entering rules and issue divisions

**A. Actionable environment and actual-authority attribution.** The current Standards and Tests entry *Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment* operates from Andrade, February 26, 1997, and Harrison, September 15, 1997, for the express burden and apparent-authority distinction. Its current-wording authority is Faragher, September 8, 1997. Ellerth must prove by a preponderance that actual delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment. Rank, employment and proximity alone do not suffice. Higher-management notice, alter-ego status, hiring/firing power, an express threat, a completed employment decision and tangible economic loss are not invariably required. Personal purpose and breach of an employer's prohibition do not by themselves defeat actual aid. Ordinary scope, negligence and apparent authority remain independent inquiries. Apparent authority separately requires an employer-attributable manifestation, reasonable belief and reasonable reliance; those are not added elements of the actual-power route.

The entry *Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite* operates from Harris, November 9, 1993, and Oncale, March 19, 1997, for same-sex coverage and causation. The plaintiff must separately establish conduct because of sex, objective severity or pervasiveness sufficient to alter employment conditions, and actual subjective perception of abuse. Psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, lost productivity and economic loss are unnecessary; sexual content or gravity alone does not conclusively prove discriminatory causation. The district court's treatment of severity/pervasiveness supplies the reviewed posture, not a new Supreme Court factual finding.

**B. Prevention, policy and nonuse.** Faragher and Harrison reserve a general complete defense based on genuinely effective protection and unreasonable nonuse, including elements, burden, relation to tangible employment action and distinct remedial limits. They establish neither universal liability for supervisors nor an affirmative-defense formula. A written prohibition, noncomplaint and eventual correction are not independently conclusive. There is no universal reporting or dissemination duty, policy immunity, negligence per se or shifted attribution burden. Gebser does not resolve the Title VII reservation. Its three-Justice avoidable-recovery proposal is an earlier published position, not controlling law.

**C. Judgment and relief.** The challenged categorical barriers can be decided without deciding constructive discharge, damages, a complete defense or every surviving summary-judgment ground. The 1993–94 conduct is outside Landgraf's pre-enactment bar on new remedies, but attribution does not establish compensatory or punitive relief, fees, jury entitlement or an amount. If section 102 relief later becomes material, its separate conditions remain: compensation excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other section 706(g) relief; punitive damages require malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights; either party may demand a jury when available section 102 compensatory or punitive relief is sought, and the jury does not hear the caps. No such entitlement is adjudicated here.

## Frozen proposition-level commitments

**P1 — No submission or completed-retaliation prerequisite.** An employee who rejects sexual advances and suffers no completed tangible retaliation may still pursue a Title VII hostile-environment claim. The notice inquiry concerns each challenged decision or omission before the May 13, 1994 injury. Gutierrez-Rodriguez v.
