## Common rule, application, and competing ground

Admission and exclusion of scientific expert testimony receive the same abuse-of-discretion review. Legal errors remain independently correctable; historical findings receive their proper protection and permissible case-specific evidentiary judgments receive deference. Careful review of a dispositive exclusion is allowed, but its contribution to summary judgment supplies no different standard. Rule 56's treatment of disputed merits facts does not turn the antecedent admissibility decision into an issue of fact reserved to the jury.

The original Rules permit examining whether an expert's reasoning reliably connects the data to the offered proposition. Accepted terminology, credentials, or a stated weight-of-evidence method do not foreclose that inquiry. Conversely, it is not enough to reject the testimony that no single study independently proves the conclusion, that a study uses animals, that an association lacks statistical significance, or that another possible cause exists. The foundation must fit promotion or acceleration as actually offered; sole causation is not required. No later Rule 702 amendment, invariant matching-study condition, or ultimate medical finding is adopted.

For the seven, the described infant-mouse injections, concentrated doses and different tumors, together with the identified human-study exposure and inference limitations, permit the trial judge's conclusion that this PCB-only inference lacked sufficient fit. The two nonsignificant reported excesses, the Norwegian study's particular mineral-oil exposure, and the Yusho co-exposures are considered as the described limits of these proffers, not categorical scientific disqualifications. The seven credit that the experts invoked a combined method and other materials; they do not find every possible bridge disproved or infer absent testimony from an incomplete source extract. Their appellate conclusion is that exclusion was within the permissible range, not that admission was necessarily forbidden or that PCBs cannot promote lung cancer.

Stevens accepts the proper review allocation but would have the Eleventh Circuit examine the application. The grant concerns that allocation, and his objection to deciding more rests on whether the combined method and the record were adequately assessed. Work by at least thirteen researchers and WHO materials cannot be dismissed merely by showing that the separately described studies were individually inconclusive. He does not find the testimony necessarily admissible; his mandate leaves a lawful exclusion available after the appropriate review. History directly confirms this objection and supplies no basis for counting him as concurring in the seven's judgment.

## Separate-writing function and remedy

**Breyer:** The difficulty of assessing science does not excuse the trial judge's screening duty. Scientific methodology, tentative knowledge and the move from general risk to individual causation can require particular care. Existing assistance, including court-appointed experts under Rule 706 and lawful management of the foundation inquiry, can help discharge that duty. His full join does not depend on appointing an expert, convening a new hearing or establishing a mandatory panel. Rules 403 and 703 retain their separate functions. The provisional statement that no additional institutional-science holding was necessary remains correct; the direct historical evidence supports a separate explanatory concurrence, not a new controlling procedure. No other Associate's join in that concurrence is inferred.

**Stevens:** A separate partial concurrence and dissent preserves agreement on review allocation and disagreement with this application and judgment. Its operative alternative is appellate reconsideration of PCB-only admissibility under the proper standard, not automatic admission or a research-driven order to supplement the record.

**Seven-Associate mandate:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit's rejection of the permissible PCB-only exclusion and remand for further proceedings. Preserve its unchallenged finding of evidence supporting furan/dioxin exposure; the District Court had not explicitly assessed causation admissibility on that assumed exposure. That remaining question prevents reinstatement of the complete defense summary judgment. No causation, damages, compulsory admission, new expert appointment or predetermined result on the alternative theory follows.
