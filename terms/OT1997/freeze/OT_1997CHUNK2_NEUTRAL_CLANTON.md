## 7. Clanton v. Cooper — December 29, 1997

### Event, record, and question

The complete Tenth Circuit opinion, 129 F.3d 1147, lower docket 96-7082, decided November 17, 1997, reviews Cooper's appeal from denial of qualified-immunity summary judgment. It affirms the surviving denial while rejecting three subsidiary warrant theories. Federal claims against Cooper alone remain; all other defendants and the state claims were dismissed. No cross-petition or expanded grant reviving those disposed components is established. The case concerns individual damages liability, not a new criminal prosecution or suppression motion.

**Supported nonmovant account and dates:** The opinion expressly presents disputed facts in Clanton's favor. After a July 26, 1993 trailer fire, Oklahoma fire-marshal agent Cooper interviewed witnesses including Clanton. In late August her nephew Bobby Eaves supplied a signed statement that his brother Michael had told him he and Clanton set the fire. On August 26, Cooper questioned Michael, who first denied involvement. Clanton's account is that Cooper falsely asserted physical evidence, threatened twenty-five years unless Michael confessed, suggested a confession implicating Clanton and an insurance motive, and promised light treatment if Michael complied. Michael signed a Miranda waiver and gave a recorded confession. The waiver does not by itself establish voluntariness, and neither the allegation nor the later recantation establishes coercion as an adjudicated fact.

Cooper signed the probable-cause affidavit August 31, 1993; a judicial warrant issued that day although no criminal information had been filed. Warrant information entered NCIC September 2. Customs arrested Clanton in Houston around September 13 on her return from Europe. After Houston police asked for instructions, an NCIC message attributed to Cooper falsely described possible homicide involvement and no bond, although the Oklahoma warrant set bond at $5,000. The accepted account attributes one to three days' detention to this message and release on personal recognizance after the homicide assertion was disproved. Arrest under the warrant and any additional detention caused by the later message are distinct causal events.

Michael recanted September 23, 1993. The state court quashed Clanton's warrant September 27, citing absent filed charges/indictment and the recanted accomplice statement. Those state reasons do not themselves establish a federal constitutional violation. A different state ruling later found Michael's confession voluntary, and he pleaded guilty September 28, 1994, receiving a two-year suspended sentence. No charge against Clanton was reported filed. The later voluntariness ruling and plea do not automatically bind Clanton; the record does not establish the elements of a preclusion defense.

**Claims and lower reasoning:** (1) The knowing/reckless material false-affidavit claim survives under Franks; the circuit refuses Cooper's factual-sufficiency challenge under Johnson. It rejects automatic constitutional invalidity from uncorroborated accomplice hearsay, absence of pre-warrant filed charges, or absence of a state grand-jury indictment. (2) The NCIC claim was not specifically pleaded, but the District Court treated it as within the general Fourth/Fourteenth Amendment allegations and found factual disputes about composition, authorization, review and transmission. Cooper denies additional injury and notes Texas's independent bond authority. The circuit permits the causal inference of extended detention and applies Franks by analogy without demanding an NCIC-specific precedent. (3) The coerced-confession theory invokes Clanton's own Fourteenth Amendment protection against governmental use of an involuntary statement to deprive her liberty. The circuit distinguishes third-party rights from her own injury, examines false evidence representations together with threat and promise, and treats the District Court's coercion conclusion as beyond interlocutory review. The last step presents a genuine legal/factual allocation question, not an immutable source fact.

### Supported competing paths

Cooper may distinguish aggressive but lawful interrogation from coercion or falsification, assert lack of materiality or attributable detention on the legally assumed facts, and raise any genuinely unsettled conduct-time constitutional extension. He cannot obtain interlocutory fact selection merely by denying Clanton's account; she cannot defeat immunity merely by characterizing every discrepancy as intentional.

Lawful paths include dismissal of factual-sufficiency portions of the appeal; rejection of immunity on a supported, clearly established material-deception or direct-liberty theory with remand and no liability finding; or claim-specific immunity where the assumed conduct is constitutionally insufficient or lacked particularized 1993 notice. A necessary factual-scope remand is distinct from a final merits ruling. The surviving NCIC theory requires its own falsehood, materiality, causal and notice analysis. The state voluntariness finding's effect cannot be assumed without identifying parties, issue, procedure, and the governing preclusion rule. The rejected subsidiary warrant theories remain rejected unless a supported scope basis permits review: probable cause may rest on sufficiently reliable hearsay under Gates; the Fourth Amendment does not universally require filed charges before a warrant; the federal grand-jury indictment guarantee is not imposed on the States. Section 1983 does not convert a state-law warrant defect into a federal deprivation. Rejection of a third-party privilege theory does not automatically eliminate an independent deprivation of Clanton's own liberty.
