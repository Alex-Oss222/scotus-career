## Campbell — material authority, no altered operative commitment

Campbell's Part I applies Powers' concrete-injury, relationship and enforcement-impediment requirements to selection of a Louisiana voting grand-jury member. Six Associates adopt that route; O'Connor and Kennedy are among them. That holding does not dispense with the impediment requirement. The excluded citizens face the described practical enforcement barriers. In Miller, the father actually brought his own claim and failed to appeal his dismissal without a reported unusual impediment. O'Connor and Kennedy therefore continue to distinguish the father's unpursued equality claim from the juror-rights route they now expressly support in Campbell. Neither may describe Campbell as barring third-party standing generally; neither needs to reverse a Miller commitment to honor its actual rule.

Campbell's unanimously adopted Part II allows the defendant to litigate his own due-process objection to the composition of the body that indicted him. It reserves the substantive due-process entitlement, violation and remedy. It reinforces the need to keep personal injury and access to adjudication separate from substantive success. All eight Miller commitments already recognize the daughter's concrete denial. The disagreement over whether her own claim invokes the father's sex-equality standard is not decided by Campbell's jury-composition standing rule. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer may use Campbell as a consistent example of reaching a person's own right without third-party litigation, but it is not a new holding that every direct adverse consequence permits assertion of another person's equality right. Their Miller own-right and scrutiny conclusions remain independently based on the actual classification and Virginia/Kelley/Klinger.

Stevens's merits route remains available without adopting Campbell as a citizenship or substantive-equality precedent. Scalia and Thomas's Campbell recognition of personal standing is consistent with their refusal to announce a universal Article III bar in Miller; their objection to the requested citizenship remedy remains distinct from denying that the applicant has concrete injury. Campbell supplies neither a naturalization remedy nor an automatic remedy following threshold success. Its preservation ruling adds no new Miller forfeiture fact. No Justice's Miller vote, join or remedy changes because of Campbell.

## Atlantic Mutual — no citizenship or equality rule imported

Atlantic requires actual statutory authority and interpretive room before deference, and preserves the statutory computation and exceptions. It decides neither citizenship transmission nor constitutional sex scrutiny, third-party standing or judicial correction of an invalid condition. The reported agency acceptance of Miller's paternal citizenship and sufficient presence is an objective record predicate, not a new Chevron holding, an agency concession of constitutional invalidity or a source of judicial remedial power. All eight Associates' Atlantic joins are consistent with the addendum's refusal to invent a support requirement under the former route. No change to their Miller commitments follows.

## Beach — distinguish expiration under a valid statute from the constitutional challenge

Beach enforces §1635(f)'s termination of a federal rescission right, including the express agency extension, and distinguishes that termination from limits on affirmative actions and the distinct damages remedy. It expressly avoids a universal rule about every deadline. The Miller decree remains untimely under both statutory alternatives; none of the Associates treats it as timely merely because other prerequisites are accepted. The dispute is whether the unequal deadline is constitutional and, if invalid, what correction is lawful. Beach does not adjudicate either question, turn constitutional correction into ordinary equitable tolling, or require a new Miller remedy.

Stevens continues to sustain the deadline on his merits ground. O'Connor and Kennedy continue on their claimant/limited own-claim ground. Scalia and Thomas retain their independent limitation on the requested citizenship remedy. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain the directed constitutional-remedy position explained in the factual addendum; it does not extend a valid deadline by recoupment or equity and is not foreclosed by Beach's statutory holding.
