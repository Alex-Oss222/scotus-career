### Scalia and Thomas: correction of an unnecessarily restrictive provisional reservation

The frozen provisional file correctly refused to count two judgment votes as joins in an unformulated overruling holding. History nevertheless supplies direct support for an additional affirmative position: both would accept express rejection of Halper's freestanding civil multiple-punishment method in favor of a rule requiring successive criminal prosecutions, while maintaining that a nominal civil designation cannot immunize proceedings actually criminal in character. Scalia's historical Hudson concurrence, joined by Thomas, expressly accepts the Court's different formulation because it preserves that requirement. Their earlier simulated Ursery concurrence presents the same objection; no changed premise defeats it.

Accordingly, retain their judgment-only relationship to the six-Associate rationale, but do not carry forward a categorical refusal to endorse Halper's rejection. This reconciliation corrects that unsupported restriction. It creates no Court holding overruling Halper, no third concurrence vote, and no join in the common rationale. A materially different proposed opinion remains subject to the ordinary formulation-specific join rules. No new neutral ground is involved: replacement of Halper and these Justices' competing constitutional account were expressly tested in the frozen packet and commitments.
