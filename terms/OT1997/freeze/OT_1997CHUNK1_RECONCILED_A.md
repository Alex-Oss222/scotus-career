Souter's historical dissent expressly addressed the silent-charter/default distinction; Breyer's did too, while emphasizing City of Rome's precedential force and the submitting authority's identification burden. Young prevents ambiguous submission from silently approving materially different arrangements. Lopez rejects a state-compulsion exemption. Their [combined operative module](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#administration-and-enforcement-of-voting-changes-requiring-preclearance) supplies no contrary categorical rule. Their historical exact-case reasoning, on the same material predicates, therefore outweighs the frozen prediction that this charter distinction would persuade them.
