## Event

Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes, No. 96-779. Argued October 8, 1997; decided May 18, 1998. On review of the Eighth Circuit's judgment, 93 F.3d 497, reversing judgment for the Commission on Forbes's First Amendment challenge to exclusion from a televised candidate debate.

The state broadcaster planned a selective, moderated debate program. Its spring 1992 selection category covered major-party candidates and other candidates with strong popular support. Forbes later qualified for the ballot, requested inclusion and was denied. Staff cited lack of campaign organization, appreciable voter support and press recognition as a serious candidate. The jury found that political pressure and disagreement with his views did not cause the exclusion. The question is whether this actual program created a forum requiring his admission and whether the exclusion was constitutionally permissible.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses and directs entry of judgment for the Commission on the reviewed forum claim.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

The case returns for implementation of judgment for the Commission on this claim. No prospective publication requirement, fixed admission formula or compelled future broadcast is imposed.

## Holdings

### 1. This selectively composed candidate debate is a nonpublic forum

**Holding and operative rule:** A public broadcaster's candidate debate is a nonpublic forum when its actual program retains editorial selection and has not been opened generally to every qualified candidate. Exclusions must be reasonable in light of the program's journalistic purpose and viewpoint neutral; neither public ownership nor the label journalism supplies immunity from those requirements.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. That six-Justice coalition controls both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The candidates express their own political views in this debate, so the broadcaster cannot treat their speech simply as the State's message. Yet a selective invitation program is not a general opening of airtime. Cornelius and Perry Education distinguish selective access from a designated public forum and preserve reasonable, viewpoint-neutral management.

The Commission planned a limited, moderated exchange and selected individual participants. Ballot eligibility did not itself open the program to every candidate. Rosenberger protects viewpoints within the opportunity actually created; it does not enlarge every opportunity into general access. Bronx likewise protects comparable private expression in a broad community forum while preserving genuine program boundaries. Its rental setting does not remove the broadcaster's supported selective function here. Denver Area supplies no categorical broadcasting classification or government editorial immunity.

The holding concerns this program, not every public broadcast or every candidate debate. A debate genuinely opened to all qualifying candidates would present a different access question. The State may neither disguise viewpoint exclusion as program definition nor rely on its own classification without examination of actual operation.

**Precedent treatment:** Cornelius and Perry Education supply the selective-access rule; Rosenberger and Bronx preserve viewpoint equality within genuine boundaries; Denver Area is not expanded beyond its distinct components.

### 2. The supported exclusion is reasonable and viewpoint neutral

**Holding and operative rule:** A genuine assessment of public support and journalistic significance may justify exclusion from this selective debate when it reasonably serves the program's purpose and is not a pretext for viewpoint discrimination. The established criteria and application satisfy that rule here; the Constitution imposes no additional prerequisite that future criteria be published.

**Controlling explanation:** Limited discussion time supports selecting a manageable group that will provide a useful exchange. Reasonableness does not require the best or only conceivable debate design. The advance selection category and staff's supported assessment of organization, voter support and press recognition bear on the program's purpose. The jury's express rejection of political pressure and viewpoint disagreement confirms the absence of those asserted improper causes; it does not by itself substitute for examining the criteria's reasonable relationship to the program.

The contrary standards argument rightly identifies the danger of officials controlling political access through vague judgments of seriousness. It does not establish an additional constitutional requirement of a numerical formula, universal written policy or prospective publication for this otherwise reasonable, neutral selection. Courts retain authority to test genuine viewpoint neutrality and pretext.

Ballot qualification alone neither establishes strong public support nor defeats a legitimate selective format. This holding does not authorize incumbent favoritism, sham criteria, selective administration, exclusion because a viewpoint is unpopular or denial from a program actually opened on terms the candidate satisfies. It decides no damages amount, official immunity, election remedy or future broadcast obligation.

**Precedent treatment:** The nonpublic-forum reasonableness and viewpoint rules are applied to the established record without adding a new publication or formula requirement.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Cornelius and Perry Education:** Applied to actual selective access and reasonable, viewpoint-neutral restrictions.
- **Rosenberger:** Preserves private viewpoints within the actual program rather than compelling universal access.
- **Good News/Good Sports and Bronx Household:** Preserve actual comparators and genuine forum boundaries; the planned selective debate differs from broad rental access.
- **Denver Area:** No universal public-broadcast classification or government editorial immunity is drawn from its separate components or incompatible rationales.

## Law After Decision

This selective debate may use reasonable, genuinely viewpoint-neutral judgments about public support and journalistic significance. Forbes's exclusion satisfies that standard on the established record. Judicial review for pretext and discrimination remains; no universal candidate-access, published-criteria or numerical-threshold rule is created.

## Separate Writings

Stevens, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, would sustain the appellate reversal on a narrower ground than an automatic right of every ballot-qualified candidate to participate. The State has created a platform for candidates' competing private political speech, and sufficiently definite, reviewable selection standards are necessary to constrain its discretion. Cornelius's access principles, Shuttlesworth's objection to uncontrolled permit discretion and Rosenberger's neutral-allocation requirement inform that conclusion. The spring category existed, but major-party status and strong popular support leave too much room for officials' judgments about seriousness. The jury's rejection of improper motive does not answer this distinct objection to the governing standards. The dissent permits reasonable selection, prescribes no particular polling number, finds no viewpoint bias contrary to the verdict, and would order no automatic damages or future broadcast.

## Procedure After Action

The lower courts must implement judgment for the Commission on Forbes's reviewed First Amendment forum claim. No additional criterion-publication requirement applies. The abandoned statutory-access claim is not revived, and no separate damages, immunity, costs or future-election determination is made.
