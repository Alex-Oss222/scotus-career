## Zeran

The two separable questions are the classification of notice-based distributor liability under the original §230(c)(1) and the temporal reach of the original §230(d)(3). The temporal clause was then subsection (d)(3); the definitions were then subsection (e). Modern renumbering and later amendments are excluded. Landgraf's threshold temporal-direction inquiry, its provision-specific consequences analysis, Rivers, and Hughes Aircraft are copied in the law slice. The general word immunity does not by itself establish the treatment of completed conduct.

A January 4, 1996 action was against KRXO, a different defendant. Review is from judgment on the pleadings, with well-pleaded allegations accepted rather than found after trial. ## Caron

The issue is the federal effect of a state restoration under §921(a)(20), not a general determination of the constitutionality of firearms restrictions. The exact federal definition, unless clause, §922(g)(1), and enhancement provision must be kept distinct. Beecham identifies which jurisdiction's law supplies the restoration inquiry. Love preserves its personal, incorporated arms holding without deciding every convicted-person restriction. Staples and the later Bryan explain their own offense-specific mental-state questions; neither creates an unstated restoration exception.

The control extraction reports restoration by operation of Massachusetts law, not an individualized pardon. The parties agree that civil rights were restored, apart from the federal effect of the surviving firearms restriction. Massachusetts allowed rifles and shotguns under its identification-permit regime for convictions more than five years old, but restricted possession of handguns outside the home or business. It did not impose a complete handgun-possession ban. The relevant provisions are Massachusetts General Laws chapter 140 §§121, 123, 129B, 129C, 131 and chapter 269 §10, as then effective.

The initial district sentence counted one California and three Massachusetts convictions. The First Circuit's earlier en banc decision, 77 F.3d 1 (1996), rejected a categorical requirement of an individualized restoration; on remand the district court excluded the Massachusetts convictions, 941 F. Supp. 238, 251–254. The reviewed First Circuit ruling counted them because of the continuing firearms restriction. December 1993 seizures included six rifles and shotguns and ammunition, with four §922(g)(1) convictions. The reviewed restoration question must not be replaced by an unsupported finding about a particular seized handgun, a complete ban, or an unpresented constitutional claim.

## Gebser

**Kinman v. Omaha Public School District, May 27, 1997, is controlling entering law and was omitted from the preparatory brief.** Its actual five-Justice recipient rule requires notice to a corrective official of harassment or its substantial risk and deliberate, unreasonable disregard causing injury or continuation. The full holding, limits, separate writings, and distinct judgment coalition are copied in the slice. Constructive notice, ordinary teacher authority, policy breach, and the wrongdoer's own knowledge do not alone satisfy that rule. The additional circumstantial-awareness formulation lacked majority support; the four-Justice agency and negligence alternatives remain noncontrolling. Franklin's previously reserved attribution question has therefore already been addressed. No inference may silently turn either separate theory into current law.

Controlled objective source extraction reports that in October 1992 parents of two other students complained to the principal about classroom comments. The principal met with the teacher, received an apology and assurance against repetition, cautioned him, and informed the guidance counselor; the complaint was not transmitted to the superintendent, who was the Title IX coordinator. Gebser did not herself report the relationship. Police discovery in January 1993 led to the teacher's termination. A complaint about classroom comments is not automatically actual notice of a sexual relationship or its substantial risk, and the adequacy of response is a separate issue under Kinman.

The preparatory packet's categorical description of missing policies requires qualification. The controlled source record identifies conflicting evidence: a superintendent affidavit describes a policy (App. 44a–45a), while deposition testimony describes no formal policy (Record 332, exhibit 2, pages 42 and 44); the superintendent did not remember whether a handbook notifying students of procedures had been distributed (App. 72a–73a). Gebser testified that she likely would have reported if she had known how, while describing fear and bewilderment (App. 64a). These remain testimony and source descriptions, not findings by this Court. The official duties to adopt and publish prompt equitable grievance procedures, 34 C.F.R. §106.8(b), and supply nondiscrimination notice, §106.9(a), are distinct from a factual finding that they were met and from private compensatory liability.

The district court entered summary judgment. Petitioners appealed the Title IX claim only. Constitutional claims, individual liability, administrative enforcement, and injunction standards cannot be reopened simply because the record references them. The identified joint appendix is Internet Archive item `micro_IA40385014_0076`, `04. Joint Appendix.pdf`; control is extracting any necessary additional fact without exposing withheld Supreme Court substance.

## Bajakajian

Austin and Alexander establish the fines inquiry at the actual level of their joins and expressly leave a comprehensive excessiveness formula unresolved. They do not turn instrumentality alone into the constitutional test. The retained Kurth Ranch and current Hudson punishment rules are distinct Double Jeopardy authorities; their standards are not automatically interchangeable with Excessive Fines analysis. The conduct, property relation, and penalties must support any further rule adopted here. The prior packet's proposed list of proportionality considerations is an analytical issue framing, not a preexisting mandatory list.

The controlled objective extraction reports a guilty plea to willful reporting failure; a separate §1001 false-statement count was dismissed by agreement, not resolved by acquittal. Forfeiture was tried to the judge. The district court found a lawful source and debt and no connection to another crime. Section 5322(a)'s statutory maximum of $250,000 and five years differs from the applicable Guidelines maximum of $5,000 and six months. The actual sentence was three years' probation, a $5,000 fine, and $15,000 forfeiture.

The Government appealed solely to obtain the entire $357,144. Respondent did not cross-appeal. The validity of the $15,000 disposition and authority to substitute that partial forfeiture are not themselves presented. The Ninth Circuit majority treated instrumentality as dispositive and did not reach proportionality; the concurring judge regarded the currency as an instrumentality but considered the full amount excessive. These distinct grounds must be preserved. Reporting failure is not a finding that the money was contraband or unlawful proceeds; dismissal of the false-statement count is not a finding that no lie occurred.
