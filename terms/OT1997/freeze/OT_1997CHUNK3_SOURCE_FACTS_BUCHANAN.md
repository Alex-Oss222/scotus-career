## Convictions, evidence, and proceedings

- On September 15, 1987, Douglas McArthur Buchanan, Jr., killed his father, stepmother, and two younger half brothers. In 1988 a jury in the Circuit Court for Amherst County convicted him of capital murder under the multiple-murder provision, Va. Code §18.2-31(7), four first-degree-murder charges, and four firearm offenses. It fixed death for capital murder, life for each first-degree murder, and imprisonment for the firearm offenses. The state supreme court vacated the redundant first-degree-murder conviction for the father's killing and otherwise affirmed. The state court also conducted the prescribed sentence review. Support: 522 U.S. at 271, 274; 103 F.3d at 346, ¶¶1–7.
- In the separate capital sentencing proceeding, the prosecution sought death on the statutory vileness aggravator. Two days of testimony included seven defense witnesses and eight prosecution witnesses. Defense testimony described his mother's death from breast cancer, his father's remarriage, and his parents' efforts to prevent contact with maternal relatives. A psychiatrist attributed extreme emotional disturbance at the time of the crime largely to stress from the family's response to his mother's death. Two prosecution mental-health experts generally agreed with the life events but disputed their effect on the crimes. These are accounts of testimony, not additional findings of the extraction. Support: 522 U.S. at 271–272.
- The prosecutor's opening acknowledged a troubled childhood and told jurors they would have to balance favorable matters against the crimes. In closing, the prosecutor said proving vileness did not require a death sentence, acknowledged mitigating evidence, and argued the circumstances nevertheless warranted death. Defense counsel explained mitigation, discussed the four factors below, and sought a life sentence. Support: 522 U.S. at 271–272 (record references App. 25–29, 43–44, 57–61, 64–66).
- The trial judge refused the requested additional instructions described below based on Virginia authority against singling out mitigating factors. Buchanan and the Commonwealth agreed to the pattern instruction, which was given without objection. That procedural fact does not erase the distinct request for additional instructions. Support: 522 U.S. at 272–274 (App. 39–40, 73–76).
- Jurors were told the foreman should sign the verdict form matching their choice. The death form certified a unanimous vileness finding and stated that, having considered mitigation evidence, the jurors unanimously fixed death. The judge read the returned death verdict and polled every juror. After the separate sentencing hearing prescribed by §19.2-264.5, the court imposed the jury's sentence. Support: 522 U.S. at 274 (App. 77).
- State collateral proceedings in the Amherst County court and Virginia Supreme Court were unsuccessful. Buchanan then sought federal habeas relief under 28 U.S.C. §2254; the district court denied relief and the Fourth Circuit affirmed on December 30, 1996. The federal appeal raised five groups of claims: mitigation instructions, excluded mitigation hearsay, ineffective assistance concerning possible guilty pleas, adequacy of state appellate review, and the failure to give a second-degree-murder instruction. Support: 103 F.3d at 346–351, ¶¶1–7, 17–40.

## Complete material instruction content

The full instruction reproduced at App. 73–74 contains the following five paragraphs. The fifth, requiring unanimity for death, appears in the fuller record quotation at 522 U.S. 280–281. This transcription is retained internally because the precise syntax is the disputed legal instrument; public rendering need not quote it at length.

> You have convicted the defendant of an offense which may be punishable by death. You must decide whether the defendant should be sentenced to death or to life imprisonment.
>
> Before the penalty can be fixed at death, the Commonwealth must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that his conduct in committing the murders of Douglas McArthur Buchanan, Sr., Christopher Donald Buchanan, Joel Jerry Buchanan and Geraldine Patterson Buchanan, or any one of them, was outrageously or wantonly vile, horrible or inhuman, in that it involved torture, depravity of mind or aggravated battery to the above four victims, or to any one of them.
>
> If you find from the evidence that the Commonwealth has proved beyond a reasonable doubt the requirements of the preceding paragraph, then you may fix the punishment of the Defendant at death or if you believe from all the evidence that the death penalty is not justified, then you shall fix the punishment of the Defendant at life imprisonment.
>
> If the Commonwealth has failed to prove beyond a reasonable doubt the requirements of the second paragraph in this instruction, then you shall fix the punishment of the Defendant at life imprisonment.
>
> In order to return a sentence of death, all twelve jurors must unanimously agree on that sentence.

Support: 522 U.S. at 272 n.1, 280–281, reproducing App. 73–74. No editorial parsing of these clauses is supplied.

Buchanan requested four particular-factor instructions: no significant prior criminal history; extreme mental or emotional disturbance; significantly impaired capacity to appreciate criminality or conform conduct to law; and age. The first three instructed that, if found, the factor mitigated against death and must be considered in choosing death or life; the age instruction stated age was a mitigating fact to consider. A further requested instruction directed consideration of the offense's circumstances, Buchanan's history and background, and any other mitigating facts, in addition to the specified factors. Support: 522 U.S. at 273 & nn.2–3 (App. 74–76).

## Material statutory provisions and actual judgment below

- Va. Code §18.2-31(7) covered killing more than one person as part of the same act or transaction. Section 19.2-264.3 provided the separate sentencing procedure and the vileness route invoked here; the particular aggravator submitted and the burden are set out in the complete instruction above. Support: 522 U.S. at 271–272 & n.1; 103 F.3d at 346, ¶¶3–4.
- Section 19.2-264.4(B), as reproduced in the opinion, allowed admissible evidence of offense circumstances, defendant history and background, and other mitigation, without making its listed factors exclusive. The four invoked statutory factors were no significant prior criminal activity; extreme mental or emotional disturbance during the offense; significantly impaired capacity to appreciate criminality or conform conduct; and age at the offense. The reported statutory extract omits other listed factors not invoked in these requested instructions; this receipt does not represent it as a verbatim reproduction of the entire subsection. Support: 522 U.S. at 273 n.2.
- Sections 17-110.1 and 17-110.2 required state supreme court sentence review, including whether passion, prejudice, or another arbitrary factor produced death and whether the penalty was disproportionate. Support: 522 U.S. at 274; 103 F.3d at 350–351, ¶¶35–38.
- The actual Fourth Circuit judgment was affirmance of denial of habeas relief. On the instruction claims, it held that Virginia's allowance of all relevant mitigation satisfied individualized sentencing and that state law required admissibility of mitigation, not instructions listing particular factors; it therefore also rejected the derivative due-process claim. These are lower-court grounds, supplied for review rather than adopted here. Support: 103 F.3d at 347–348, ¶¶8–16.
- The lower court separately rejected the hearsay-exclusion and ineffective-assistance claims, rejected the challenge to state sentence review, and treated the federal second-degree-instruction claim as unexhausted because only the state-law ground had been raised on direct appeal. The last procedural ruling concerned the second-degree-instruction claim, not the requested mitigation instructions. Support: 103 F.3d at 348–351, ¶¶17–40.
