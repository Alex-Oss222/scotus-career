## 147. Clifton v. Federal Election Commission — No. 97-786

### Reach and result

All eight Associates would decide the supplied merits question within the private petitioners' established review interest, affirm the First Circuit's limited declaratory disposition rather than restore the district court's broader invalidation, and leave the electioneering-message issues on remand. No FEC cross-petition seeking reversal of the relief already obtained by the plaintiffs is established. That limit independently bars enlarging the FEC's rights in this review; it does not imply that every Associate would approve every aspect of the initial appellate reasoning in an agency appeal.

The federal appellate remand does not itself remove jurisdiction under §1254(1), which permits review before or after judgment. It does make a comprehensive decision on undecided residual provisions inappropriate here. None would manufacture a mootness, standing or ripeness defect for the already adjudicated candidate-contact conditions. None would decide a new restriction beyond the reconstructed question or issue an additional injunction without an established predicate.

### Component grounds at the agreed level

**C1 — Independent inquiry and genuine collaboration.** An independently chosen inquiry to a candidate about public positions or votes is not, merely by occurring, a contribution or a candidate-directed expenditure. The statutory candidate connection concerns the expenditure, not the bare fact that the speaker gathered information from a candidate. Actual cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion concerning campaign spending remains separately regulable according to its predicates. The record does not establish that any particular oral conversation crossed that boundary.

**C2 — Extent of the present declaration.** The existing relief against compulsory written-only communications and equal space/prominence stands. General enforcement and rulemaking authority does not justify assuming that every independently edited guide involving a candidate inquiry is a prohibited contribution. The narrow statutory reading is reinforced by Hurley and Tornillo's protection of editorial choice and the importance of direct discussion of public issues. This ground does not decide whether Congress could enact every conceivable specifically tailored safeguard or whether a proven contribution could be regulated. It creates no exemption for disguised campaign collaboration.

**C3 — Residual electioneering clauses and corporate category.** Do not expand the judgment to strike the whole regulation, declare every electioneering clause valid, or supply a replacement definition. The district court has not decided the clauses' operation, ripeness and severability. Nor does the record establish MRLC's precise funding characteristics. Day prevents treating the bare acceptance of any corporate contribution as automatically fatal to MCFL protection, but does not establish that this organization's actual funding is insignificant or nonconduit. The remand preserves that distinction and any legitimately necessary inquiry.

### Alternative paths and remedy limits

The strongest argument for a broader invalidation is that candidate-information gathering and distribution are independent issue advocacy, so all added constraints exceed §441b. That fails as a categorical proposition: Buckley, Colorado Republican and Akins preserve contribution treatment for actual candidate-connected financing, and the residual content clauses have not received an authoritative operational construction. The strongest objection to affirming the narrower judgment is that an undefined restriction chills speech now. That concern is serious but does not establish a severability result or eliminate ripeness and scope issues; the remand may address legally justified interim relief on its own record.

The strongest agency argument, developed in the Bownes dissent, is that written exchanges create a verifiable record and prevent an unrecorded quid pro quo while leaving other lobbying available. Current law and the actual rule's coverage prevent that argument from converting every public-position inquiry into a contribution. More fundamentally, the present petition does not authorize restoration of agency restrictions already removed below. The limited agreement is therefore compatible with Stevens's and Ginsburg's greater willingness to accept appropriately fitted contribution prophylaxis. No fiction of unanimous agreement on a universal First Amendment rule is necessary.

The mandate leaves genuine-coordination regulation, express-advocacy provisions not successfully challenged, and distinct statutory membership exclusions in their lawful positions. The district court must address the unresolved electioneering provisions under their own terms and ordinary severability rules. No new campaign-finance liability, nonprofit-status finding, injunction, enforcement moratorium or exemption is entered by this commitment.

## 148. Federal Election Commission v. Maine Right to Life Committee — No. 96-1818

### Common threshold commitments

All eight Associates would reach the facial statutory issue. MRLC's established ongoing communications, actual cessation of some speech after the rule took effect and credible application risk supply a present controversy. It need not obtain advance approval for each communication under §437f, whose advisory process is permissive. McCarthy's actual statutory-design inquiry supports this conclusion; its later prison-specific statutory modification does not amend FECA. Scalia and Thomas would rest this conclusion on the statutory text and the inability of advisory review to invalidate the governing regulation, without adopting an open-ended balancing entitlement to bypass every adequate administrative process. No separate decision about Corbett's independent standing is necessary where the association supplies the same requested relief. The Attorney General's dismissal and rejection of additional unneeded relief remain undisturbed.

### The two merits paths

**M — Vacate the facial statutory invalidation and remand.** Buckley and MCFL require express electoral advocacy, not an exclusive dictionary of listed slogans. Section 100.22(b) is within the statutory boundary only as its own restrictive terms require: a communication taken as a whole, with merely limited external context, whose electoral portion is unmistakable and unambiguous and for which reasonable minds cannot differ between encouragement of election/defeat and another action. Genuine issue discussion, even hostile or favorable discussion of a candidate near an election, remains outside that category when it has a reasonable nonelectoral meaning. Timing, expected electoral effect, policy agreement or the regulator's preferred interpretation cannot substitute for the textual conditions. A court must test those conditions independently; agency expertise does not control the constitutional boundary.

On this interpretation, the lower court's categorical conclusion that paragraph (b)'s contextual route necessarily exceeds the express-advocacy construction is too broad. The lower court did recognize MCFL's marginally less direct exhortation as express advocacy; it did not demand one literal slogan. The disagreement concerns whether the regulation's cumulative conditions preserve that required expressive boundary. Vacate the statutory declaration and remand the unadjudicated constitutional vagueness challenge and any properly presented application issues. Do not declare the rule constitutional in all applications, order enforcement, or find any particular MRLC publication prohibited. Subsection (a), genuine contribution rules, nonprofit constitutional protection under Day/MCFL and the distinct membership exclusions remain separate.

**B — Affirm statutory invalidation.** Buckley and MCFL's express-terms construction protects speakers from a definition under which external events can convert an unchanged discussion of character, policy or qualifications into prohibited electoral speech. Paragraph (b)'s invitation to infer election advocacy from limited context supplies the very uncertainty the narrowing construction avoided; the accompanying explanation confirms that the agency contemplated character and qualification discussion whose classification changes with election proximity. A reasonable-person formula does not cure that expanded boundary. Affirm the declaration that paragraph (b) exceeds statutory authority, leaving subsection (a) and lawful contribution regulation intact and avoiding an unnecessary standalone constitutional-vagueness holding.

### Alternative-path verification and remedy

M has five non-Stone supporters; B has three. Those are provisional commitments, not a Court tally. M's supporters do not join an agency-friendly construction merely because the FEC promulgated it: the statute's independently determined boundary is controlling. They reject the inference that all candidate discussion becomes advocacy at election time and do not import a later functional-equivalence doctrine. Their construction is tied to this rule's actual words in 1998. The lower opinion itself explains the linguistic case for the definition but considers a stricter bright-line construction binding; that disagreement is the legal question presented, not an undisputed fact.

B's supporters do not demand that every electoral appeal literally say “vote for.” Subsection (a)'s direct electoral phrases, slogans and contextual identification remain intact. Their disagreement is whether paragraph (b)'s extra route permits the regulator to supply an electoral directive from timing and context where the speaker did not express one. M's answer is that the rule's cumulative conditions forbid that inference whenever a reasonable issue/action alternative remains. The dispute therefore changes judgment and cannot be hidden as unanimous agreement about vague labels.

M would vacate the declaration and remand rather than enter judgment that the FEC may enforce every challenged application. B would affirm the bounded statutory declaration. Neither path changes the threshold rulings, compels a referral, makes a finding of prohibited MRLC advocacy or automatically determines the different voter-guide regulation in Clifton. No controlling same-day dependence is assumed.

## 149. Federal Election Commission v. Williams — No. 97-601

### Common components and disposition

All eight Associates would vacate the appellate judgment that categorically terminated the action and remand. The unanimous provisional disposition rests on correction of an unsupported legal shortcut, not a finding that the Commission was diligent or its action timely. The $10,000 penalty and ten-year injunction are not reinstated by that disposition. Remaining appellate defenses and remedial objections remain open.

**W1 — Section 2462 applies.** In the absence of a different congressional limitations enactment, the five-year limit covers a judicial action initially imposing a FECA civil penalty as well as collection of an already assessed penalty. The default claim accrues with the actionable violation, not whenever the Commission completes its own investigation or decides probable cause. The agency's discretionary enforcement sequence cannot indefinitely postpone accrual. No general discovery rule for public penalties is adopted.

**W2 — Concealment and diligence require the right inquiry.** Equitable fraudulent-concealment analysis is distinct from that default accrual rule. The Commission must establish concealment that prevented timely discovery despite reasonable diligence, the resulting interval and the basis for applying it. Holmberg supplies the received equitable principle; Klehr preserves reasonable diligence and rejects misleading conduct alone as sufficient when ordinary inquiry would disclose the facts. Beggerly's statute-specific QTA result does not dispose of this claim.

The appellate majority's categorical conclusion that reports listing nominal contributors necessarily provide legally sufficient notice of the hidden source is erroneous. A reporting obligation is not proof that the secret payor was revealed, and accurate names of intermediaries can coexist with the concealment alleged. Conversely, allegations that contributions were made in another's name do not themselves prove that discovery was prevented or excuse all agency delay. The lower court must examine what information existed, what reasonable inquiry would show, and when the operative facts became discoverable. Fletcher's view that diligence is established is not adopted as a finding.

**W3 — Mandatory administrative periods.** The Ninth Circuit expressly reserved whether compelled notice/conciliation intervals affect the clock because they could not cure lateness on its rejected accrual calculation. Remand permits that preserved question to be addressed if needed after W2. No automatic 125-day allowance, tolling of the whole investigation, suspension beginning with any complaint, or conclusion that the October 19, 1993 complaint was timely is adopted. The statutory minimum and actual nonoverlapping periods must be distinguished from optional delay and maximum permitted conciliation. This is a reserved merits issue on remand, not an instruction that a particular extra interval must be awarded.

**W4 — Injunctive relief.** The appellate blanket dismissal rested on the same time-bar judgment. Vacatur removes that categorical premise without reinstating the ten-year injunction. Cope's concurrent-remedy principle prevents merely relabeling an otherwise barred retrospective claim, but it does not answer whether every independently justified governmental preventive remedy is a penalty. The actual injunction must satisfy the governing statute and ordinary prospective-equity requirements. Past liability alone does not establish a current violation or likelihood of recurrence; no such finding is supplied here. The Court need not decide a universal rule about all government injunctions under §2462.

### Alternative-path verification

Affirmance would be supportable if an adequate record and the proper standard established lack of concealment, discoverability with reasonable diligence, or lateness after all legally permissible intervals. The lower majority instead made disclosure of the nominal names categorically conclusive. Its public-report reasoning cannot do that factual work. An alternative affirmance requiring facts not established in the supplied record would repeat the same mistake in a different form.

Immediate reversal reinstating the enforcement judgment is also too broad. It would accept Fletcher's diligence assessment as a controlling finding and answer the reserved compulsory-process question without the necessary adjudication. The record permits correction of the legal error, not selection of the agency's preferred discovery date and toll. The eight commitments share this intermediate, legally sufficient result because Klehr and Beggerly require exactly the distinctions the categorical lower ruling erased; no assumption about the desirability of campaign-finance enforcement supplies a vote.

The remand is to the court of appeals for appropriate consideration or further proceedings, preserving the district judgment's unresolved appellate review and every properly presented remaining defense. Ordinary mandate procedure applies. There is no new penalty assessment, new hearing entitlement independent of genuine factual need, fixed tolling interval, discovery deadline or injunction duration.
