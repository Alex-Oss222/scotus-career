## Common threshold and law

All eight accept ordinary review of the Ninth Circuit's recall judgment. Felker's section 2244(b)(3)(E) bar concerns a grant or refusal of permission to file a second or successive district-court application; it does not itself bar review of the court's action on its first-application mandate. All accept some exceptional power to recall a mandate, constrained by governing statutes/rules and finality. They do not treat either an assertion of inherent power or the capital sentence as sufficient to justify exercise.

The January 1990 first application is governed by former section 2254(d) for the identified merits and factual-treatment provisions under Carriger's binding extension of Lindh. No California chapter 154 qualification is established. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas's prior reservations do not make that current holding unsettled. The distinct July motions and their later evidence do not silently become part of the August 3 first-petition recall. No AEDPA provision is categorically suspended, and a genuinely successive filing retains the statute's separate conditions.

## Four support affirmance on the genuine-process-repair ground

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the challenged recall as within the circuit's restrained discretion on this supported record. A court of appeals may exceptionally recall its first-habeas mandate to repair a demonstrated breakdown in its own internal consideration, where the correction genuinely restores that lost judicial process rather than admitting a new prisoner application or simply reconsidering an ordinary disagreement. The record must support the institutional failure and the relation of the correction to it; the asserted seriousness of the underlying claim or impending execution does not suffice alone. The court's timing and explanation, extent of reopening, and reliance interests bear on whether the asserted repair is genuine and restrained; these are reasons for the judgment, not a new mechanical checklist.

The circuit attributed the lost en banc consideration to internal misunderstandings, a clerk transition and a judge's failure to notice the panel decision, and it limited the renewed merits consideration to the first application's claims and evidence. The March statement that no active judge requested a vote does not disprove the explained failure to complete consideration. The 53-day interval after the mandate and stated decision to await state review are substantial concerns, but they do not, without an invented earlier knowledge chronology, demonstrate that the institutional account was a pretext. The correction occurred within the same first-application adjudication and before the proposed execution. These four would defer to the court's supported account of its internal breakdown at the level appropriate to exceptional-power abuse review.

Hazel-Atlas establishes exceptional appellate power to protect judicial process, while its fraud predicate is distinguished; no fraud is invented. Ohio Power demonstrates that a court's own procedural operation can exceptionally yield without an automatic innocence prerequisite. Carlisle forbids evasion of an actual rule, but no supplied rule categorically bars the genuine institutional repair identified here. Lonchar protects legally required habeas process without making every discretionary rehearing mandatory; the power to repair this actual judicial failure does not depend on a universal prisoner entitlement to en banc review.

These four would not install Schlup or Sawyer as a prerequisite for this process-repair recall. Their gateways concern otherwise barred claims, and the repair here returns to the first application without treating it as such a barred claim. Ordinary Strickland prejudice remains distinct from innocence. Their affirmance on the recall question does not independently find either innocence gateway satisfied or recast the first petition as a freestanding innocence action.

## Four support reversal of the merits-reopening recall

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse. They accept narrowly exceptional corrective power, including a genuinely defective mandate or corruption of judicial process, but conclude that the identified lapses here amount to missed discretionary consideration after panel adjudication and rehearing, rather than a defect preventing a valid first-application judgment. The mandate embodied the panel's decision; recall then reinstated substantial habeas relief and reopened murder review. The fact that the court initiated the second look and excluded new claims does not alone distinguish it from ordinary reconsideration of completed merits.

They would require, for this use of recall to reopen completed merits of state-prisoner habeas review, the demanding miscarriage-of-justice showing appropriate to the attacked conviction or capital eligibility. This is an express extension of the established gateways to this merits-reopening use of recall, justified by the finality and comity interests after federal review has ended; it is not a claim that Schlup or Sawyer already governs every recall. Genuine process repair, fraud, a mandate not expressing the judgment, and other materially distinct corrective situations are not universally subjected to an innocence prerequisite.

The supported internal mishaps and 53-day postmandate delay, including the decision to await state review, do not establish that the missed discretionary review required undoing completed merits. State proceedings remained distinct and did not prevent the circuit from protecting its own process. This conclusion does not depend on an invented exact date of judicial knowledge, a finding that every active judge consciously rejected review, or a numerical deadline for all recalls. It rests on the character of the described failure and the scope of the remedy chosen.

On the first-petition evidence, these four would find neither required innocence showing established. New impeachment must be assessed cumulatively and can matter; the existing trial impeachment already exposed substantial criminal history, informant activity, benefits, dishonesty and changed accounts. The additional specific information weakens those witnesses further but does not compel every reasonable juror to reject their accounts. Root's opinions undermine several forensic inferences and the timing of some bruises, yet do not supply an alternative cause for the crushing injury or establish that sparse sperm resulted from douching, much less establish consensual intercourse. Semen supports intercourse, not force by itself; the restraint evidence, altered clothing, gag and remaining confession accounts nevertheless permit a reasonable adverse assessment when considered together. The inconsistent left/right description of one wrist plays no role. Thompson's sleep account is evaluated as testimony, not accepted as a reconstructed fact.

For the rape conviction, the question is whether reliable new evidence with the whole probative record makes it more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict beyond a reasonable doubt; these four conclude it does not. For an eligibility-only challenge, Sawyer independently requires clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the rape special circumstance and other required eligibility predicates; that showing also is not made. They do not use mitigation or ordinary Strickland prejudice as a substitute, demand that new evidence postdate the mandate, or add AEDPA due diligence to Schlup. Leitch's later parole account is excluded from this assessment because the circuit expressly excluded that motion's evidence from the recall under review.

## Former state findings and merits boundaries

All eight retain the distinction between a qualifying state factual finding and the federal gateway judgment. Former section 2254(d) requires the actual finding, requisite state merits hearing and adequate written indicia; paragraph (8) requires the produced pertinent state record as a whole. Marshall protects historical and credibility determinations subject to the statute's exceptions. A different inference or later evidence alone does not erase that protection, and an earlier finding cannot conclusively decide new evidence it never assessed. No decisive state finding on every new impeachment item or Root opinion is identified in the packet. Neither side invents one, treats the federal panel as a state factfinder or uses the original verdict as conclusive on the augmented evidence.

The four reversing can reach their gateway-insufficiency conclusion without manufacturing a protected finding: their assessment rests on the described whole evidence and the burden they would adopt. The four affirming do not order a new gateway hearing or declare that every state finding loses force on recall. A lawful route to merits still requires Strickland's deficient performance and reasonable-probability-of-prejudice elements, with applicable former-law factual treatment. The recall question's disposition does not replace those elements with a confidence or innocence slogan.

## Exact operative consequences

For Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: affirm the challenged recall on the presented recall question. The en banc decree's separate components remain effective: affirmance of the District Court's rape-conviction and rape-special-circumstance writ relief and death-sentence vacation; further review of the first-degree murder conviction; and vacation of the then-entered execution order. This is not a murder acquittal, a new Supreme Court grant of habeas relief on independently reviewed Strickland merits, or an invented conditional-retrial deadline. No particular new hearing or newly set execution protection follows.

For O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: reverse the August 3 recall disposition and direct reinstatement of the June 11 mandate denying relief in the first application. The dependent rape-related writ affirmance, death-sentence vacation, murder-review remand and en banc vacation of the old execution order cannot remain effective through the invalidated recall. This does not itself schedule an execution, revive an expired date as an immediately executable command, decide a later independent application, or erase a distinct lawful source of protection. No underlying ineffective-assistance merits ruling is necessary once recall is rejected.
