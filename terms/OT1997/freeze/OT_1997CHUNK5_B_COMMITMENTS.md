## 1. Aktepe v. United States — March 10, 1998

### Questions, alternatives and limits

Review is of summary judgment on political-question grounds, not an adjudication that negligence occurred or an established Feres bar. The Public Vessels Act and Death on the High Seas Act do not themselves answer constitutional justiciability. Baker's issue-specific inquiry, as preserved by Nixon, controls. Gilligan bars judicial design and continuing supervision of military training but expressly leaves specific-injury damages controversies distinct; it supplies no automatic damages exception or universal military bar. Dalton's statutory preclusion cannot be transferred to an unrelated maritime waiver. Weiss, Loving and Richenberg preserve legal review at their actual scopes.

**Path A — affirm on this claim's inseparability from training policy.** The complaint challenges communications, training and drill procedures. Selecting when to disclose a drill, what commands must mean and whether an operator must question an apparent firing order can require choosing the balance between realistic combat preparation, command obedience and safety. The absence of an independently fixed, supplied operational command matters to that inquiry; it is not proof of litigant default. The strongest objection is that the attack was intended to be simulated and the immediate injury came from a concrete miscommunication, so retrospective care may be assessable without designing the exercise. This path survives only as a record-specific conclusion about the duties actually proposed; alliance participation, military status, embarrassment and lack of judicial expertise do not alone suffice.

**Path B — vacate and remand for a genuinely issue-specific operational inquiry.** The uncontested distinction between simulated attack and unintended live discharge supplies a concrete starting point for separating negligent execution from the choice of training policy. Gilligan's express reservation and Nixon's confined rule oppose converting military subject matter into immunity. The strongest objection is that a court cannot simply posit a safer procedure, call it operational and thereby avoid making military policy. This path therefore directs examination of the existing claims and record, not a finding that any particular written protocol existed or was violated, guaranteed discovery, liability or compensation. Claims requiring policy selection remain nonjusticiable; independently available threshold defenses remain open.

## 3. W.P. v. Verniero — March 16, 1998

### Scope and common legal premises

The reviewed merits judgment is the W.P. class appeal, Third Circuit No. 96-5416. No. 96-5132 is the separate E.B. jurisdictional case. All eight take the supplied W.P. merits questions as reachable through the authorized review, preserve the E.B. judgment as background only, and do not manufacture a petition, cross-petition or expanded grant. The class concerns pre-October 31, 1994 offenses and Tier 2/3 notification, not registration alone, Tier 1 or post-enactment offenses.

The controlling classification method is civil legislative intent followed by the clearest proof of punitive purpose or effect, assessed through supported combined operation. Hudson preserves that inquiry; Hendricks invalidated its actual prisonlike commitment rather than establishing civil confinement as a nonpunitive benchmark. No personal-monetary Halper accounting is transferred to notification. Punishment under Ex Post Facto and the separate Double Jeopardy predicates must not be merged.

The published state-law privacy restraint is substantive: disclosure must serve a legitimate and substantial interest with minimized invasion. It is not merely a promise of procedures. Sandin's sufficient state-constraint analysis supports examining that real limitation; its prison context is not a universal noncustodial threshold. Paul v. Davis's distinction between reputation alone and a protected legal interest does not erase this separate state-law constraint. All eight accept this limited protected-interest ground for the process question, without deciding an independent federal constitutional privacy right.

### Enhanced alternatives

**Civil classification.** The actual scheme grades risk, permits individual factual and atypical-case challenges and requires tailored audiences. It directly imposes no residence, work, movement or licensing prohibition. Public protection can justify disclosure without exact risk prediction. The strongest objection is that government-initiated broadcasting of conviction and predicted dangerousness resembles historical shaming and predictably produces cumulative social and economic burdens. Civil classification must answer that objection through actual tailoring and the supported record; it cannot disregard all private consequences categorically or assume every safeguard works perfectly. The nineteen reported experiences, six compelled notifications and 135-notification figure are not a single causal denominator.

**Punitive classification.** Affirmative official publicity, a criminal-history trigger, a dangerousness designation and foreseeable enduring burdens can cumulatively establish punishment even without confinement. The strongest objection is that this rationale risks equating stigma and deterrence with punishment despite the clearest-proof requirement and the actual encounter/risk limits. A punitive conclusion must identify the combined operation, rather than simply transplant Hendricks's outcome or convert Becker's historical-shaming argument into a finding of undisputed purpose. Ex Post Facto relief on the retrospective class would not require deciding Double Jeopardy offense identity.

**Process, two distinct choices.** State persuasion on both tier and notification plan removes the special risk of making the affected person disprove the State's affirmative justification for irreversible disclosure; the State already marshals an initial case. A clear-and-convincing quantum further allocates error against unnecessary disclosure, drawing on Mathews, Addington and Santosky. Its strongest objection is that underclassification threatens potential victims, the measure does not confine or terminate family status, and existing hearing safeguards matter. A State-preponderance alternative preserves meaningful proof without treating the two error risks as sufficiently asymmetric to require heightened certainty. Its strongest objection is that ordinary equipoise may leave an enduring unnecessary public disclosure despite the State's continuing monitoring alternatives. Allocation and quantum are assessed separately; neither civil classification nor a protected interest alone decides them.

## 6. United States v. Robinson — March 23, 1998

### Competing paths and component boundaries

**Direct-market path:** The Hobbs Act's enacted obstruction/delay/affecting-commerce element is applied to robbery directed at operating businesses' working capital used to buy interstate inventory and cash the identified checks. The actual depletion directly interrupts those commercial functions. American Life League supplies the direct-interference mechanism; Lopez permits aggregation after that mechanism is identified and does not require a substantial effect from each individual act. The strongest objection is that calling all money business capital could disguise the prohibited chain from any theft to reduced consumer spending. This record's identified business withdrawals and impaired restocking/check-cashing supply the boundary; no household-money, remote-goods-history or generalized crime-cost theory is adopted.

**Limit/remand path:** A generic depletion allegation or reliance solely on multiplying local crimes would be insufficient under Lopez. It could require reversal or reconsideration if the only nexus were remote spending potential. Its strongest objection here is the already supplied, concrete connection to ongoing interstate commercial operations and the statutory element. This alternative does not survive on the actual granted objection: the lower court's overbroad arithmetic explanation is unnecessary to affirm these convictions. No constitutional entitlement to a substantial individual effect is established.

The offense nexus remains an element, not subject-matter jurisdiction. The facial objection fails because the statute lawfully reaches this defined direct-interference class; no rule validates every imagined application. As-applied commerce objections to the three robbery components fail on that class and record. The conspiracy challenge fails insofar as it uses the same substantial-individual-effect theory against an agreement directed at those business robberies; no general overt-act or distinct conspiracy-elements rule is added. The supplied firearm objection is wholly derivative and fails with the predicate-commerce challenge. Independent indictment, instructions, sufficiency, aiding-and-abetting or firearm defects are not invented. Salinas's agreement distinction does not replace the applicable Hobbs Act elements.
