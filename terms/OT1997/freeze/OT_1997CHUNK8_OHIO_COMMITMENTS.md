## Common bounded judgment and grounds

All eight non-Stone Justices provisionally support vacating the Sixth Circuit's judgment and remanding for dismissal of the presented substantive forest-plan challenge without prejudice as unripe. The remand must displace the District Court's merits summary judgment rather than leave a substantive adjudication approving the plan. None reaches the merits of the NFMA claims or orders plan revision, plan vacatur, an injunction against logging, or any present project approval.

Each Justice supports the following propositions at the stated scope, subject to the individual join limits below:

1. **This substantive challenge is premature.** Under the fitness-and-hardship inquiry, the challenge to the plan's amount and methods of future logging is unripe where the plan itself authorizes no cutting, later site and method decisions must precede cutting, those decisions remain subject to public participation and judicial review, concrete application would materially assist review, and the present record establishes no sufficiently weighty adverse consequence from waiting for that application. This is a record-specific application, not a rule that forest plans or all programmatic actions are categorically immune from immediate review.
2. **Final adoption does not settle ripeness.** The adoption's planning significance is real, but a completed agency planning decision does not by itself require immediate judicial decision of every substantive objection. The Court can assume final action for this disposition without deciding that statutory issue. *Bennett*'s consequential biological opinion remains reviewable on its own established premises; retained discretion is not by itself a sufficient reason to reject review here or elsewhere.
3. **Later review must remain meaningful.** The supplied record permits affected persons to contest a later project decision before cutting, including an applicable plan premise on which the authorization depends. No present hardship equivalent to *New York*'s required siting preparations or *Pic-A-State*'s existing criminal constraint is established. This conclusion neither requires environmental injury to occur first nor guarantees relief, excuses statutory prerequisites, or decides an unidentified future limitations defense.
4. **The holding does not decide an independent procedural injury.** The present challenge asserts that the plan permits excessive logging and clearcutting through arbitrary planning choices. Calling the process biased does not establish a separately presented denial of notice, an environmental statement, comment, or another identified statutory procedure. The Court does not decide a hypothetical procedural claim. *Lujan* continues to permit protection of a threatened concrete interest without requiring certainty that the required procedure will change the ultimate agency choice. This reservation creates no new procedural cause of action or automatic standing.
5. **No substantive NFMA construction is adopted.** In particular, the Sixth Circuit's exceptional-circumstances characterization of even-aged management is not recited as statutory text, and the optimum-method and resource-protection requirements remain distinct. Their proper application is not adjudicated in this unripe suit. Neither court's merits judgment is allowed to survive as the disposition of the present case.

These are the grounds these commitments will join, not a proposed assignment or an instruction to make Stone join them. The threshold basis is sufficient without finding that these members lack all Article III injury, that every plan lacks APA finality, or that optional administrative appeals must be exhausted.

## Adversarial paths and common response

**Path A: dismiss the present substantive challenge as unripe.** Its strongest support is the conjunction of further reviewable decisions, useful site-specific facts, no cutting authority in the plan itself and no demonstrated loss of a meaningful opportunity for judicial review. The strongest objection is that the plan has already been adopted, directs future management unless amended, and allegedly encodes the very arbitrary preference the suit challenges. The conclusion rests on the particular legal and practical effects established here; the continued existence of discretion alone cannot answer that objection. *Toilet Goods* supplies the pre-divergence distinction between final action and a fit dispute. Current *Texas* applies the same fitness-and-hardship inquiry without closing review of a concrete future controversy.

**Path B: sustain present review of the final plan.** Its strongest version is Batchelder's: the final plan directs management and the asserted statutory defect concerns that completed planning choice, without reliance on undocumented agency motives. *Bennett* gives this argument real force because subsequent implementation discretion did not remove a present, consequential constraint. Its barrier here is the absence of the equivalent supported present effect and the fact that the challenged future harvesting remains subject to the described project decisions. The present challenge is therefore resolved on Path A. A different claim challenging a present legal restriction or a supported procedural deprivation would need its own assessment; no Justice votes to dismiss such a claim by an invented categorical rule.

The agency's opportunity to apply or revise the plan and the usefulness of a concrete record support fitness. They are not free-standing immunity, an automatic deference rule, or an added exhaustion requirement. *Darby*'s limitation on mandatory superior-agency appeals remains intact. The fact that the plan was administratively affirmed in 1990 is fully accounted for.

## Justice-specific commitments and join tests

### John Paul Stevens

**Judgment and reach:** Vacate and remand for dismissal without prejudice as unripe; no NFMA merits adjudication. Join common propositions 1–5 in full.

**Decisive own public authority:** Stevens delivered the unanimous current-term *Texas* opinion, which required a sufficiently defined intervention and substantial present hardship before reaching statutory coverage and distinguished genuinely consequential future obligations. His joins in *Bennett* and *Pic-A-State* preserve immediate review where an actual present constraint is established. His statutory-ground position in *Steel Co.* is not converted into assent to an unnecessary Article III holding here.

**Two-path assessment:** Final plan adoption and the concurrence's management-direction point make Path B materially stronger than a request concerning a wholly unspecified program. They do not eliminate the project's independent choice of place, method, environmental analysis and authorization or establish irretrievable injury before review. The distinction is sufficient under the rule Stevens already adopted in *Texas*.

**Join barrier and narrower alternative:** Will not join a categorical rule that environmental plans never affect concrete interests, a finding that the members have no standing merely from missing reproduced affidavits, or an adverse ruling on a nonexistent procedural count. A narrower fitness-and-hardship disposition answers those objections. No separate writing is needed if the common formulation is retained.

### Sandra Day O'Connor

**Judgment and reach:** Vacate and remand for dismissal without prejudice as unripe. Join common propositions 1–5 in full.

**Decisive own public authority:** O'Connor's controlling *New York* opinion allows review of a future command that requires substantial present preparations; her joins in *Texas*, *Bennett*, and *Pic-A-State* distinguish conjectural future application from current operational consequences. Her *Steel Co.* join expressly preserves legitimate nonmerits threshold sequences rather than demanding adjudication of every other threshold issue first.

**Two-path assessment:** The challenge is not premature solely because logging is future. Path B would prevail over that simplistic reason if the plan imposed a present burden comparable to *New York*'s siting obligation. The supported record instead permits further choice and timely review before cutting; no comparable present obligation or unavoidable loss is established. Path A therefore best fits her own distinction.

**Join barrier and narrower alternative:** No rule requiring an affected person to suffer logging damage or violate law before suing; no new exhaustion demand. With those limits and the meaningful-review reservation, she joins the shared rationale and remedy without a separate writing.

### Antonin Scalia

**Judgment and reach:** Vacate and remand for dismissal without prejudice as unripe. Join common propositions 1–5 in full.

**Decisive own public authority:** Scalia's *Lujan* opinion supplies the controlling stage-specific concrete-interest rule and its procedural-right qualification; his unanimous *Bennett* opinion treats a completed determination with legal consequences and a supported water-loss link as reviewable. His *Steel Co.* opinion excludes contested merits adjudication without established Article III jurisdiction, while his *Texas* join supports this nonmerits disposition.

**Two-path assessment:** His own *Bennett* holding prevents rejecting Path B merely because an implementer retains choice. Unlike its completed legal constraint, the facts supplied here do not authorize any timber cutting or establish that a site-specific decision has imposed the claimed forest-use loss. Finality may be assumed without resolving the different ripeness question. Path A thus needs no wholesale repudiation of final plans as agency action.

**Join barrier and narrower alternative:** No generalized citizen right to compel lawful forestry administration and no claim that procedural labeling eliminates concrete injury. Equally, no fabricated absence of personal use or noncontrolling expansion of *Lujan*'s absent-agency analysis. He joins the concrete, limited rule and has no separate-writing function on those terms.

### Anthony M. Kennedy

**Judgment and reach:** Vacate and remand for dismissal without prejudice as unripe. Join common propositions 1–5 in full.

**Decisive own public authority:** Kennedy delivered the unanimous *Pic-A-State* opinion recognizing a ripe challenge to a criminal restriction already stopping existing business; he joined *New York*, the controlling *Lujan* components, *Bennett* and current *Texas*. His *Steel Co.* join retains the nonexhaustive sequencing reservation.

**Two-path assessment:** The plan's influence cannot be dismissed as unreal, and *Bennett* prevents demanding certainty that relief will change every later decision. But this record lacks the immediate constraint that made *Pic-A-State* ripe and permits review of a concrete authorization before forest injury. Path B's plan-level legal formulation alone does not supply the missing present effect; Path A is the supported application.

**Join barrier and narrower alternative:** He will not join an absolute requirement that a threat be consummated, an assumption that statutory discretion always defeats redress, or a merits ruling approving the plan. With ordinary pre-enforcement review preserved, full joinder requires no separate writing.

### David H. Souter

**Judgment and reach:** Vacate and remand for dismissal without prejudice as unripe. Join common propositions 1–5 in full.

**Decisive own public authority:** Souter's unanimous *Humane Society v. Babbitt* opinion requires a supported connection between the challenged act, concrete interests and useful relief, but expressly avoids categorical rules and preserves procedural protection of concrete interests. His joins in *Bennett* and *Texas* supply the immediate-constraint and future-implementation distinction. His statutory disposition in *Steel Co.* does not establish agreement with its constitutional rationale.

**Two-path assessment:** The plan is more connected to subsequent logging than the certificate amendment was to the earlier transfer in *Humane Society*; that case is not a substitute causation holding. Path A instead follows the separate ripeness inquiry: reviewable project choices and facts remain, and no supported immediate consequence requires their anticipation. Path B does not prevail merely from completed plan adoption.

**Join barrier and narrower alternative:** No categorical third-party or intervening-discretion bar; no declaration that participation in plan adoption automatically cures any procedural violation. The procedural and standing questions are left at their actual record boundaries. This supports the common rationale without a separate writing.

### Clarence Thomas

**Judgment and reach:** Vacate and remand for dismissal without prejudice as unripe. Join common propositions 1–5 in full.

**Decisive own public authority:** Thomas joined all controlling *Lujan* components, *New York*, *Bennett*, the constitutional jurisdiction/redress holdings in *Steel Co.*, and unanimous *Texas*. These are specific adopted rules concerning concrete claims, supported legal consequences and avoidance of premature merits adjudication.

**Two-path assessment:** The strongest Path B argument is the final plan's direction of later management. His *Bennett* join means that legal consequence cannot be ignored. Here, however, final project authorization and the complained-of use remain unresolved; treating alleged bias in plan design as the eventual substantive forest injury would resolve the very contingencies *Texas* leaves for concrete review. Path A is sufficient without a separate standing judgment.

**Join barrier and narrower alternative:** No reliance on the lower majority's institutional-motive speculation; no NFMA merits decision; no conversion of a general interest in lawful administration into a procedural injury. He accepts the procedural reservation only with the concrete-interest requirement explicit. No separate writing is necessary.

### Ruth Bader Ginsburg

**Judgment and reach:** Vacate and remand for dismissal without prejudice as unripe. Join common propositions 1–5 in full.

**Decisive own public authority:** Ginsburg joined the complete *Texas*, *Bennett*, *Pic-A-State*, and *Humane Society* holdings. Her statutory-ground disposition in *Steel Co.* counsels against attributing an unnecessary constitutional standing conclusion to her, without changing the controlling law.

**Two-path assessment:** Batchelder's narrower final-plan argument gives Path B a supported legal basis and avoids speculation about the agency. Yet the final plan can be consequential for management while the particular substantive injury remains contingent on independent, reviewable project choices. The present sources do not establish that participation or judicial review will be lost by waiting. Path A is therefore supported, with the plan-level procedural distinction retained.

**Join barrier and narrower alternative:** No blanket prohibition on review of plans, no dismissal of an unestablished procedural count, and no finding that the lower court's acknowledged recreational interests are legally noncognizable. The common scope answers those barriers, making a separate writing unnecessary.

### Stephen G. Breyer

**Judgment and reach:** Vacate and remand for dismissal without prejudice as unripe. Join common propositions 1–5 in full.

**Decisive own public authority:** Breyer joined current *Texas*, *Bennett*, *Pic-A-State* and *Humane Society* in full and the redress holding, but not the constitutional sequencing part, of *Steel Co.*. These adopted positions permit assessment of actual administrative effects without conflating jurisdiction, finality, and merits.

**Two-path assessment:** Path B has practical force because a plan frames later administrative choices; the mere possibility of amendment cannot be an automatic answer. Path A nevertheless allows the agency's further project work to establish the concrete location, method and effects while preserving review before cutting. On this record the improvement in decisional facts and the absence of a demonstrated present adverse consequence justify waiting under *Texas* and *Abbott*.

**Join barrier and narrower alternative:** No inflexible rule that courts must always wait for implementation, no dismissal based solely on the theoretical possibility of amendment, and no mandatory new exhaustion requirement. The common case-specific rationale meets those limits; no separate writing is needed.
