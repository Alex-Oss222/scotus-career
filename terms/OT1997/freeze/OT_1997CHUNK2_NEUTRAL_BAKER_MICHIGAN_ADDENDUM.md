## Supported Michigan propositions

**Ordinary collateral-estoppel prerequisites.** Nummer v. Treasury Department, 448 Mich. 534, 542, 533 N.W.2d 250, 253, quoting Storey v. Meijer, 431 Mich. 368, 373 n.3, 429 N.W.2d 169, 171 n.3, identifies a same-parties/full-opportunity-to-litigate condition and mutuality of estoppel. Lichon v. American Universal Insurance, 435 Mich. 408, 427–428, 459 N.W.2d 288, 298, reaffirms mutuality and cites Howell v. Vito's Trucking & Excavating, 386 Mich. 37, 191 N.W.2d 313. These recitations support the reported ordinary conditions; they do not supply a complete list of exceptions, determine every form of privity, or establish that no qualification exists.

**Reported nonparty injunction applications.** Bacon v. Walden, 186 Mich. 139, 144, 152 N.W. 1061, 1063, treated a defendant who was not a party to an earlier injunction suit as not legally affected or bound by its decree. Detroit v. Detroit Railway, 134 Mich. 11, 15, 95 N.W. 992, 993, treated nonparty status as sufficient to defeat the proposed controlling effect of the earlier injunction determination in that case. These are concrete reported state-law applications relevant to the Bakers' nonparty status. They are not a newly inferred rule immunizing every person acting with, through or on behalf of a bound party.

**Burden.** The recitation of Detroit v. Qualls, 434 Mich. 340, 357–358, 454 N.W.2d 374, 383, and E & G Finance v. Simms, 362 Mich. 592, 596, 107 N.W.2d 911, 914, places persuasion on the party invoking preclusion. This is a legal allocation, not a finding that GM failed to file a paper, conceded an issue or lacks an argument because an original source was not retrieved.

**Modification channel.** The contemporaneous account of Michigan Court Rule 2.613(B) directs parties seeking modification of an injunction to the entering court. The complete historical text, exceptions and amendment history are not supplied. The source therefore supports that described procedural direction, but does not independently establish an outsider's obligation to seek modification, the decree's preclusive effect on a stranger, another court's evidentiary duty, or an absolute jurisdictional prohibition in every setting.
