## Result and operative ground

All eight would affirm the Pennsylvania Supreme Court's priority judgment, on a bounded construction of the actual collision between 26 U.S.C. section 6323(a) and 31 U.S.C. section 3713(a)(1)(B). Where the Government's federal tax lien is not valid against an existing-property judgment lien protected by section 6323(a), the Government cannot use section 3713 to obtain payment of that same tax claim from the encumbered property ahead of that protected judgment creditor merely because the taxpayer's estate is insolvent. Section 6323's specific tax-lien allocation governs this conflict; section 3713 otherwise retains its operation.

The established lien predates the federal notices and attaches to the identified real estate, not an after-acquired asset. The Government's acknowledgment of section 6323 protection establishes the protected-creditor predicate. It does not concede that every separately claimed federal-insolvency exception based on greater specificity, possession, execution or title transfer is met. The Court need not resolve that distinct exception because the specific statutory protection itself resolves this priority conflict. Thus no remand to decide that unused ground is required.

## Explanation and precedent treatment

The Government's strongest reading distinguishes invalidity of its tax lien against the creditor from the independent insolvency payment command. That distinction is legally intelligible, but it cannot preserve the operative protection Congress gave this judgment lien if the same Government takes this property's entire value first solely by relabeling the collection claim. In this insolvent estate containing no other asset, the asserted payment priority would defeat the specific allocation on the very property the allocation protects. Reading the provisions together gives effect to that allocation without excluding every tax debt from section 3713 or recognizing a universal lien exception.

Morton v. Mancari supplies the duty to harmonize provisions and give the specific scheme its actual work; it does not justify implied repeal from general policy. Randall illustrates specific statutory distribution displacing general priority at a defined conflict, without deciding this tax category itself. Kimbell Foods identifies Congress's rejection of unlimited tax-lien preference and the notice scheme's role; its lending/solvent-debtor setting means it is corroborative statutory context, not a controlling holding on insolvent estates. Emory and Key retain the demanding inconsistency requirement; the concrete property-specific conflict satisfies that requirement here.

McDermott is distinguished, not limited: a judgment lien had not attached to the later-acquired land before federal notice there. Here the protected lien already attaches to the existing property, and section 6323 protection is acknowledged. Holywell and Hill preserve attention to the exact statutory category and prevent commercial expectations or equitable hardship from supplying an exemption independently of text. No decision is overruled.

## Alternatives and remedial limits

Giving section 3713 independent control would require reversing the lower court's statutory construction and then addressing whether its separately unadjudicated federal-perfection question remains material. A direct adverse judgment premised on failure of that exception would be unsupported because the lower court did not decide it. Recognizing an independent perfected-and-specific lien exception instead would require defining and applying its federal requirements; this packet does not establish every proposed title, possession or execution predicate. Neither route is necessary when section 6323 resolves the concrete collision.

Affirmance sustains the authorized transfer in lieu of execution against the Government's competing priority claim. It supplies no new collection order, tax computation, personal-liability award under section 3713(b), factual hearing or judgment about additional property. Section 3713(a)(1)(A)'s separate living-debtor triggers and section 3713(a)(2)'s title-11 exclusion are not altered. No general exclusion of federal tax debts from all section 3713 proceedings is adopted. No separate writing is required if these bounds are retained.
