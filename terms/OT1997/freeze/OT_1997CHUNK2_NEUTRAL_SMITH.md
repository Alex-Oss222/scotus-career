## Validated facts and actual review question

Mattox, a member of a narcotics task force, entered Smith's mother's front yard after a tip identified people with cocaine there. On Smith's assumed account, he raised a baseball bat threateningly, ignored the initial order to drop it, then dropped it after a threatened shooting and fled. In the driveway he first feigned further flight, then submitted to Mattox's direction to get down. While Smith lay docile and unresisting, Mattox placed a knee on his lower back and pulled the left arm into cuffing position. Smith complained of discomfort; a grunt and blow followed, breaking his arm and requiring surgery for multiple fractures. The opinion expressly reports **no sign of anger** on this account. Neither injury alone nor a claim of malice is the legal premise.

Mattox sharply disputed compliance and testified that Smith continued resisting and reached for his gun. That dispute is preserved for ordinary proceedings; this legal appeal does not choose Mattox's account. Smith sued Mattox, the city and police chief under §1983. The district court entered judgment for the city and chief but denied Mattox's immunity summary judgment. The appeal raises **only whether the plaintiff-favorable conduct was clearly established as unlawful**. It is not an invitation to determine disputed evidentiary sufficiency, supervisory liability or municipal liability.

Smith's attempted warrant/probable-cause theory was not alleged or hinted in the amended complaint. The panel expressly declined to treat excessive force as damages for an unpleaded unlawful search or arrest. That theory cannot be revived here from a general reference to the Fourth Amendment. The panel recognized some restraining force could remain reasonable after threats and flight but considered the assumed effort, blow and severe injury while wholly unresisting within an obvious-violation category. Its assessment is the lower rationale under review, not this Court's conclusion. The panel preserved reconsideration of immunity if later established facts differ.

## Entering law and exact standards

**Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386 (1989)** supplies the Fourth Amendment objective-reasonableness inquiry for arrest force: the particular circumstances, including crime severity, immediate threat and active resistance or flight, viewed from the reasonable officer's contemporaneous perspective rather than hindsight. Subjective evil or good intention does not determine reasonableness. These considerations are contextual, not a tally; the earlier threat and flight remain relevant but do not establish that any later degree of force was necessary. The neutral packet's **Graham v. Collins** lead concerns capital punishment and supplies no arrest-force rule. Hudson v. McMillian's Eighth Amendment prisoner-force rule and Fletcher v. Kalina's prosecutor-function immunity do not replace Graham or create a presently necessary additional component.

**Harlow and Anderson v. Creighton** preserve particularized objective clearly-established-law immunity. **Elder (February 23, 1994), unanimous**, requires independent review of all relevant existing authority without admitting new claims/evidence or importing later notice. **Lanier (March 31, 1997), unanimous**, expressly identifies the same legal-clarity threshold for §242 fair warning and civil immunity: relevant lower-court law and an obviously covered application of an established rule can suffice without a factual twin; abstract rights, bad purpose, a fixed case count or an unforeseeable later enlargement cannot. Criminal elements and burdens are not imported into this civil case.

**Johnson v. Jones (June 12, 1995), Breyer for all nine**, excludes factual-sufficiency appeals while preserving separable legal immunity questions. **Behrens (February 21, 1996), Ginsburg**, has unanimous Part II on assumed facts; its seven-vote successive-appeal holding and eight-Associate claim-specific rule supply no factual selection or jurisdiction over unrelated matters. An unstated assumption may be identified favorably to the nonmovant without adjudicating truth. No repeated appeal is established here.

**Crawford-El (June 16, 1997), unanimous**, preserves ordinary competent evidence and independent notice defenses; its motive proof rule does not add a motive element to objective force. ## Supported alternative paths and remedies

A denial-of-immunity path can find that gratuitous, substantial arm-breaking force against an entirely submitted arrestee was obviously beyond Graham's lawful bounds in 1993, despite the absence of a factual twin and despite justification for some restraint. It must explain why this assumed degree and timing of force, not severe injury or general excessive-force law alone, supplied particularized notice.

An immunity path can accept the identical favorable facts yet conclude that the immediate threat, flight and transition to cuffing left the required force insufficiently clear under preconduct law. It cannot assume actual continued resistance or gun-reaching, make a matching case mandatory, or treat all force after earlier resistance as lawful. A distinction between constitutional unreasonableness and sufficiently clear unlawfulness remains available; immunity can be resolved on the latter without inventing a universal merits-first rule.

A jurisdictional dismissal is appropriate only to an actual factual-sufficiency component, not merely because the evidence is disputed. A lawful remand may preserve later fact-dependent immunity and ordinary proceedings. Denial at this stage proves no violation, damages or final liability; granting immunity on this claim decides no city/chief liability and supplies no warrant-based ruling. No mandatory trial, special interrogatory, settlement or new evidentiary hearing is ordered by this packet.
