## Actual decisions below

Each employing agency included an alleged false statement concerning the employee's own misconduct as an additional basis for adverse action. The MSPB retained underlying-misconduct determinations but set aside falsification charges under its legal rule protecting denials. It also ruled that such denials could not enhance the underlying penalty or impeach an employee, and, in the Barrett/Roberts setting, could not require reporting misconduct when doing so would implicate the reporting employee. Support: 522 U.S. at 264–265; 89 F.3d at 1577–1580, ¶¶1–22.

On July 16, 1996, the Federal Circuit affirmed the Board in the five-employee consolidated matter. It held that an agency could not separately charge or enhance punishment for an employee's denial of a charge or its underlying facts. The court expressly distinguished fabricated affirmative explanations, altered documents, false statements about other employees, and the agency's investigatory mode before charges; it did not purport to immunize every false statement. It grounded the denial rule in procedural due process and the opportunity to respond, distinguished a law/fact division in denials, and relied on *Grubka*. These are the lower court's legal grounds, not rules adopted by this receipt. Support: 89 F.3d at 1581–1586, ¶¶23–60, particularly ¶¶42–54.

The separate McManus appellate judgment affirmed the Board's rejection of the false-statement charge and its refusal to use the statements for impeachment. The employee-specific underlying conduct and final individual sanction are not reproduced in the sources read. No inference about these facts or any grant of immunity is made. Support: 522 U.S. at 265, citing 66 M.S.P.R. at 568.

## Complete material statutory framework

The 1994 United States Code texts were retrieved directly from GovInfo: [§ 7513](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title5/html/USCODE-1994-title5-partIII-subpartF-chap75-subchapII-sec7513.htm), [§ 7701](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title5/html/USCODE-1994-title5-partIII-subpartF-chap77-sec7701.htm), and [§ 7703](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title5/html/USCODE-1994-title5-partIII-subpartF-chap77-sec7703.htm). The following preserves all of § 7513's operative components, and the relevant proof and judicial-review provisions; unrelated fee and case-management clauses of § 7701 are not a proposed issue.

- **§ 7513(a):** Under OPM regulations, an agency may take an action covered by the subchapter against an employee only for cause promoting the efficiency of the service.
- **§ 7513(b)(1):** At least 30 days' advance written notice stating specific reasons, with an exception when there is reasonable cause to believe the employee committed a crime punishable by imprisonment. No finding that this exception applied to these respondents is supplied.
- **§ 7513(b)(2)–(4):** A reasonable period of at least seven days to answer orally and in writing and furnish affidavits and documentary evidence; representation by an attorney or other representative; and a written decision giving specific reasons at the earliest practicable date.
- **§ 7513(c):** An agency may by regulation provide a hearing instead of or in addition to the opportunity to answer.
- **§ 7513(d):** Appeal to the MSPB under § 7701.
- **§ 7513(e):** The agency must maintain the proposed-action notice, written answer or oral-answer summary, decision and reasons, action order, and supporting material, and furnish them to the Board or affected employee upon request.
- **§ 7701(a), (b)(1):** The appellant receives a hearing with transcript and representation; a Board, designated judge, or employee decides after written representations and opportunity for hearing. Removal cases must be heard by the Board, an experienced hearing employee, or an administrative law judge. Copies go to parties and OPM.
- **§ 7701(c):** In ordinary misconduct cases, agency action requires a preponderance of evidence; the substantial-evidence standard separately applies to specified performance/SES cases. Even otherwise supported agency action cannot be sustained if the employee shows harmful procedural error, a prohibited personnel practice, or legal invalidity.
- **§ 7703(c):** Federal Circuit review is on the record; agency action is set aside if arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of discretion, or otherwise unlawful; procedurally deficient; or unsupported by substantial evidence. The statute separately preserves trial de novo on discrimination facts in designated discrimination cases; nothing in this receipt imports such a claim into the reviewed legal question.
- **§ 7703(d):** OPM's Director may seek review of a Board decision determined to involve an erroneous civil-service interpretation with substantial policy impact. If the Director did not intervene before the Board, reconsideration must first be sought and denied. The Federal Circuit has discretion to grant the review petition; the Board and parties may appear. No missing docket evidence is treated as proof of noncompliance.

The Federal Circuit also reproduced then-5 C.F.R. § 5.4 (1995): when OPM, MSPB, the Special Counsel, or their authorized representatives require testimony or records about civil-service matters, agencies must make employees and pertinent records available, and employees/applicants/eligibles must provide information, testimony, documents, and material whose disclosure law or regulation does not prohibit. The relevant text is reproduced at 89 F.3d ¶¶47–49; the original historical regulation was not separately fetched.


## Issue-specific law and limits

**Constitutional text and employee procedure:** The Fifth Amendment provides that no person “shall be compelled in any criminal case to be a witness against himself” or “be deprived of life, liberty, or property, without due process of law.” The employees' protected employment interests were undisputed below. Loudermill,470 U.S.532 (1985), preserves constitutional procedures independently of state-created descriptions of an entitlement and ordinarily requires notice, explanation of the employer's evidence, and an opportunity to respond before termination, in conjunction with adequate later process. It does not decide this federal statutory denial immunity. The actual CSRA text above supplies at least seven days to answer and furnish evidence, representation, reasons, appeal, preponderance proof for ordinary misconduct, and independent harmful-error/unlawfulness protection; none of these commands literally says knowingly false statements must be immunized. Whether that immunity is implicit remains an interpretive issue to resolve rather than a reported legislative finding.

**Truthfulness and self-incrimination are distinct:** Bryson,396 U.S.64,72 (1969), rejects a general privilege to answer an official inquiry by a knowingly and deliberately false statement; its criminal false-affidavit setting and statutory elements remain distinct from administrative discipline. Garrity,385 U.S.493 (1967), bars use in a criminal prosecution of statements obtained under threat of losing public employment. Gardner,392 U.S.273,278–279 (1968), bars discharge solely for refusing to waive the immunity that protects compelled testimony and its fruits; refusal to answer questions specifically, directly, and narrowly concerning official duties without a required waiver presents a different setting. These rules establish no right to convert an unsuccessful denial automatically into a knowing lie, and no permission to punish a valid privilege invocation. No respondent-specific immunity, waiver, criminal threat, or refusal-to-answer event is supplied here. The legal issue can be decided without manufacturing one.

**Prior simulated law:** Gaudin(June 19, 1995), Scalia for all nine, requires the criminal jury to decide an actual offense's materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt. It preserves judges' statutory-meaning and sufficiency roles and does not impose a criminal jury or criminal burden on these civil-service proceedings. Lanier(March 31, 1997), unanimous, requires conduct-time fair warning under § 242, rejects later-law substitution, and preserves criminal elements separately from civil clarity. It neither decides ordinary civil-service discipline nor creates immunity for knowing falsity. Any employee-specific notice question must use the rule in effect when that employee acted and the actual charge; today's legal clarification does not supply missing earlier notice by itself. The ordinary misconduct proof burden remains statutory preponderance, with § 7703's appellate review standard distinct.

**Effective current-term law:** Jeffries(November 17, 1997) and Richenberg(December 8, 1997) require fidelity to actual legally disciplinable conduct, the real agency account, and genuine consideration where their rules apply; they establish no universal investigation checklist or an immunity for every denial. Valot(January 5, 1998) protects lawful nonsham use of the established unemployment-benefit process from retaliation solely for its prescribed cost, while expressly preserving rejection of fraudulent/ineligible claims and independent employment decisions. It does not decide these internal investigatory statements or revive its abandoned procedural theory. Hudson(December 10, 1997) requires the actual civil/criminal classification inquiry; occupational severity or overlap alone does not turn an administrative action into criminal punishment, and it does not decide a due-process defense here. Exact public limits and joins are retained in the slice; none expands the present question to an unasserted retaliation or double-jeopardy claim.

## Supported arguments and lawful paths

These are arguments reconstructed from the supported dispute and effective law under Engine § 14; they are not invented quotations, concessions, or papers.

1. **Reject the constitutional and statutory denial immunity:** The right to notice and a meaningful answer permits factual dispute, explanation, evidence, silence where lawfully protected, and challenge to the charge; it need not include deliberate falsehood. The CSRA preserves employee process and proof safeguards without expressly conferring denial immunity, while truthfulness can serve efficient administration. Bryson supplies a bounded contrary premise to a generalized right to lie. The strongest employee response is that escalating a charge solely because a defense fails may penalize the exercise of the very answer right and shift agency proof; that concern requires independent proof of knowing falsity and lawful penalty assessment, not an assumption from the adverse underlying finding. Correction of the categorical rule leaves those matters to ordinary agency/Board adjudication.
2. **Retain a narrower protection for legitimate contest or construe the statutory charge carefully:** A simple dispute over a legal characterization, uncertain memory, mistaken statement, or good-faith factual denial is not established deliberate falsification. Employees can argue that the statutory opportunity to answer would be frustrated by mechanical charge duplication or use of an unproved falsity as impeachment. The strongest Government response is that the lower rule protects denials even after knowing falsity is independently established and treats equivalent deliberate deception differently by phrasing. A narrower rule must identify the actual protected conduct and statutory foundation; it cannot be generalized into immunity for knowing lies or mere disagreement with credibility findings.
3. **Affirm a supported component or remand distinct applications:** The published employee matters and companion do not require one identical final disciplinary result. If the controlling legal rule is corrected, return for legally proper consideration of each charge, proof, nexus to service efficiency, preservation, and penalty. Retain independently unreviewed time-report determinations and do not automatically restore removals/demotions. If a genuinely distinct statutory or constitutional defense remains supported, it may be preserved for that employee without adopting the categorical denial rule. No blanket reopening, mandatory hearing, automatic finding of falsity, new criminal prosecution, immunity waiver, or final sanction follows.

A valid self-incrimination claim remains a separate conditional path requiring an actual factual predicate. It is not created by an unavailable personnel file, and it cannot be rejected as a litigant default because this preparer lacks that file. McManus's unreproduced underlying conduct does not block review of the identified common legal rule but prevents an invented employee-specific factual or final-penalty disposition.
