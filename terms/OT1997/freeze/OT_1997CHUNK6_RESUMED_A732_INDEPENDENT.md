### Claim-specific law that every commitment preserves

1. Article 36(1) protects communication and consular access, requires advising the detained person without delay of paragraph (1)(b)'s rights, requires consular-post notification on the person's request and forwarding communications without delay, and permits arrangements for representation while requiring consular officers to refrain if the person expressly opposes action. Paragraph (2) permits receiving-State law to govern exercise of these rights subject to its express full-effect condition. Neither the domestic-law clause nor that condition may be omitted. The stipulated failure to advise establishes neither prejudice nor an individual judicial remedy.
2. Coleman (1991), Carrier and Reed preserve adequate independent default, external cause and prejudice, and legal novelty at their proper scopes. Counsel's unfamiliarity with an existing treaty is not automatically objective unavailability. Trest's no-duty-to-invent-default holding does not defeat the default actually asserted here. The Fourth Circuit rejected cause without reaching prejudice; the district's contrary-counterfactual assessment is not an appellate finding. The affidavit concerning the plea offer, counsel's advice, refusal and testimony leaves the effect of consular assistance contested.
3. District Claim IV expressly rejects Slayton default because the federal due-process argument was raised on direct appeal. It then denies the claim on merits, AEDPA and Teague grounds. The appellate-treatment dispute is genuine: respondent reads the appellate default paragraph as resolving the claim; petitioner distinguishes that paragraph's substantive proportionality claim from the process claim. The express direct-appeal quotation independently supports the distinction. No modeled Justice defaults Claim IV merely by importing Claim III C's label.
4. Section 17-110.1(C) requires determination of passion, prejudice or another arbitrary factor and comparison for excessiveness or disproportionality against similar cases, considering both crime and defendant. Section (E) permits accumulation of records within a chosen period, requires use of available records as a guide, and requires accumulated records to be available to circuit courts. Permission to collect is not a command to collect every prosecution. The state court said it compared reviewed capital cases, including life sentences, and especially cases with both aggravating predicates; that is its reported method and conclusion, not independent proof that all comparable cases were included.
5. The petition's 33-conviction, 19-nondeath, 14-death and two-appealed-life figures are allegations from the described database. The later date range and newspaper-derived descriptions carry the packet's limits. Offense categories and counts alone neither prove comparability nor establish a federal proportionality rate. Pulley rejects a freestanding universal comparative-review requirement. Hicks and Evitts concern meaningful substantive/adjudicatory interests; Doe and Woodard prevent treating every mandatory step as a substantive entitlement. Woodard's adjudication/mercy distinction leaves the judicial function material, but creates no exhaustive-database duty.
6. The post-AEDPA filing invokes applicable chapter 153 provisions. Section 2254(d) requires an actual state merits adjudication of this claim before its deferential restriction applies; a federal court's merits discussion is insufficient. Section 2254(e)(2) has a distinct failure-to-develop trigger, its alternative rule/fact predicate, and its cumulative clear-and-convincing guilt requirement. No blanket bar or hearing entitlement is inferred. Chapter 154 qualification is not established. Exhaustion, nonretroactivity, default and statutory limits retain separate predicates.
7. Teague's finality comparison remains functional and claim-specific under Lambrix, Gilmore and Caspari; the statutory comparison rule requested cannot be declared old merely by citing fairness. O'Dell's watershed alternative is confined to its future-dangerousness, legal-parole-ineligibility and denied-correction conjunction. Neither contemporary exception is erased, but this comparative-method claim does not automatically satisfy either. The exact finality, preservation and state-merits predicates needed for an ultimate ruling remain reserved. No Justice treats a later Woodard rule as prefinality law.

## Enhanced alternatives and their legal consequences

**Treaty path T1 — deny on insufficient review/reversal prospects.** Ordinary procedural default can coexist with the treaty's domestic-law clause and full-effect proviso. Availability of the treaty and the relevant facts supplies a substantial answer to novelty/cause, and the stipulated notice failure alone does not prove prejudice or a judicial remedy. The strongest contrary argument is that a State's own failure to inform may prevent timely exercise of the right the treaty guarantees, so mechanically enforcing that resulting failure may defeat the proviso. A denial on this path must assess that argument; it cannot say that domestic procedure invariably prevails over treaty rights or that counsel's advice proves consular advice irrelevant.

**Treaty path T2 — protect review of the actual full-effect/default question.** There is a significant possibility that the lower court's ordinary-default treatment insufficiently accounts for the treaty's express condition when failure to give required notice bears on timely invocation. Correcting that legal premise could require further claim-specific consideration even without establishing entitlement to a writ. The strongest contrary arguments are the treaty's acknowledgment of domestic law, the objective availability of its text to counsel, and the independent need for a legally available remedy and material prejudice. This path therefore supports only a stay tied to petition review, not categorical immunity from default or a finding that the missing consular advice would have changed the case. Legal novelty alone is a weaker alternative and is not adopted as an established cause finding.

**Process path P1 — deny on insufficient review/reversal prospects.** The state court actually conducted comparative review, (E) permits a bounded accumulation, and an untested disparity in the appealed pool does not establish a substantive entitlement to a different universe or an arbitrary deprivation. AEDPA and a genuinely new database rule present additional obstacles. The strongest contrary argument is that a meaningful statutory protection against a disproportionate death sentence is judicial adjudication of a protected life interest, not a discretionary benefit, and a systematically selected pool can nullify that protection. Neither Doe nor Pulley answers that federal theory simply by calling the dispute state law.

**Process path P2 — protect review of the distinct federal adjudicatory claim.** The district's express preservation finding and the distinct theory create a significant possibility that the appellate disposition failed to address the actual federal claim or that the lower courts treated all statutory-method defects as noncognizable. Hicks/Evitts offer an old-rule route focused on arbitrary deprivation of an existing substantive adjudicatory protection; Woodard confirms the relevance of the function without supplying retroactive law. The strongest contrary arguments are (E)'s permissive language, actual review including life cases, uncertain legal comparability of the statistical sample, and independent Teague/AEDPA barriers. The route warrants examination, if at all, without requiring every capital record or deciding that this method was unconstitutional. Reversal prospects concern a legally consequential correction/remand, not a guaranteed writ.

**Required-process path L.** Lonchar protects process actually required by law that lawful expedition cannot complete. It does not establish an automatic first-petition stay or an additional hearing to complete research. Here the application can be assessed under Coleman/Barefoot on the supported materials. The grant positions below use that actual stay test; no position rests on an invented unfinished district duty or a general requirement to delay while a difficult case is considered.

## Relief and proposition compatibility

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer provisionally support a stay of execution until the Court disposes of petition No. 97-8214, unless modified or dissolved sooner. Any continuation after disposition of that petition requires a further order. Their common ground is T2 under Coleman/Barefoot: reasonable probability of four-member review, significant possibility of reversal concerning the treaty/default inquiry, and execution's irreversible harm. A grant would prevent execution during its stated duration; it would neither decide the petition nor continue automatically through every later proceeding. Stevens and Souter additionally find P2 independently sufficient. Ginsburg and Breyer do not join that additional sufficiency conclusion. All four may join a statement that Claim IV is separate, is not defaulted by importing Claim III C, and remains unresolved on its actual predicates.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas provisionally support denial on T1 and P1, with the precise limits stated above. A denial would issue no Supreme Court stay under A-732, but would not dissolve an unidentified order, authorize execution independently, resolve a companion application, deny certiorari, or settle the ultimate treaty and due-process questions. Their common denial formulation can acknowledge the treaty proviso, the genuine separate process claim and the uncertainties while concluding neither produces the necessary significant reversal prospect.

These are eight individual commitments, not a nine-member tally or controlling holding. The four-member review-prospect requirement does not supply the fifth vote needed for a Court stay. No conditional change of vote is recorded merely because another Justice prefers a result. A draft finding the consular violation automatically prejudicial, declaring all treaties immune from default, imposing exhaustive data collection, treating all methodological objections as nonfederal, or making first-petition status sufficient for a stay is incompatible with the commitments.

Every Associate can give the short signed explanation required for the actual referred relief by adopting only the identified shared grounds and any recorded separate qualification. This stage supplies no Chief Justice statement or certification and assigns no author. The legally significant grant/deny divide remains for reconciliation and assembly; it is not resolved through an invented temporary order.
