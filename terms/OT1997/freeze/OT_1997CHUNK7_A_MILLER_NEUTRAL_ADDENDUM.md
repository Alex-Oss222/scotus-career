## The statutory routes remain distinct

Miller belongs to the transitional cohort permitted to elect the pre-amendment §1409(a). That former subsection required legitimation before age twenty-one; the written-support condition was added in 1986 and is not an additional prerequisite under the older subsection available through that election. “Required only legitimation” describes the former §1409(a) condition, not elimination of the separate citizenship-transmission requirements in §1401. The reported agency acceptance of citizenship and sufficient physical presence remains material to those requirements.

The amended §1409(a) separately includes clear-and-convincing blood relationship, paternal nationality at birth, a written agreement to support until age eighteen unless the father is deceased, and one of its specified recognition events while the person is under eighteen. Those amended requirements must not be combined with the former age-twenty-one option into a hybrid regime. The supplied text establishes eligibility for the election; it does not separately identify a particular formal document exercising it. That document's absence from this research extract does not establish that the option was waived or unavailable.

The physical-presence versions also remain distinct: the original materials identify the former ten-total-years/five-after-fourteen requirement and the amended five-total-years/two-after-fourteen requirement. The agency acknowledgment is that the father's presence was sufficient to transmit citizenship, not merely that he met a newly invented numerical factual minimum. Neither a different version nor an invented shortfall may replace that accepted predicate without supported contrary material.

## Clarification of the original packet's reservation

The original caution that the postdeadline decree alone does not establish every statutory condition remains true as a general limit. It must not be used to treat paternal physical presence as unresolved: the published record already reports the agency's acceptance of its sufficiency. Biological paternity likewise is already undisputed, while the lack of a formal clear-and-convincing finding remains expressly identified. An unestablished written-support promise is not an indispensable missing prerequisite under every available route, because the transitional former-law route lacks that added condition.

The substantive review standard, whose equality right is asserted, third-party standing, the constitutional status of the recognition deadline, and the legal consequences of any constitutional ruling remain for independent analysis under the original entering-law authorities. This clarification chooses none of those questions. No additional research gap prevents modeling on the supplied objective record.

## Neutral limits

The agency acknowledged sufficient paternal physical presence; it must not be treated as an unestablished fact merely because the underlying original letter was not separately recovered. Biological paternity is undisputed, although the source expressly identifies no formal clear-and-convincing finding. This does not decide the constitutional classification, the applicable equality remedy, or every conceivable statutory qualification. The older-law election and absence of a separately established support promise must not be collapsed into a finding that such a promise was indispensable under every available route. The already supplied ten/five versus five/two presence versions and age-twenty-one versus age-eighteen distinction remain separate statutory matters.
