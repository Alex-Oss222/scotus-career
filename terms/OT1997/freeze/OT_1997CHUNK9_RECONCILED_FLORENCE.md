## Adoptable grounds and express join boundaries

**Black merits.** An officer who actually restrains a person's ability to protect herself, and personally directs or carries out her involuntary transfer into a serious danger the officer actually appreciates, may not deliberately and unreasonably disregard that danger when the officer has authority and a real opportunity to respond. Injury attributable to that defendant's conduct remains necessary. A reasonable protective response does not become unconstitutional because it fails. Negligence, constructive knowledge, assistance in a genuinely voluntary departure, or knowledge possessed only by another officer does not satisfy the rule.

DeShaney and Youngberg supply the restraint-based principle; Daniels excludes negligence; Collins distinguishes actual compulsion. Present Farmer, Doe and Lewis law preserves actual awareness, individual responsibility, reasonable response and genuine opportunity. Lewis's pursuit rule is not extended merely because a truck or earlier chase appears in the events. The inquiry is not a mechanical number of seconds. No general duty to prevent private violence, investigate every warning, arrest Kritis or rescue every endangered person is adopted. Scalia and Thomas join the direct-restraint application, not a free-standing judicial-shock standard; Thomas does not convert his Farmer judgment concurrence into a full Eighth Amendment join.

**Black notice.** Harlow and Anderson retain particularized conduct-date immunity. Before February 18–19, 1994, DeShaney, Youngberg, Daniels and Collins supplied the restraint/direct-deprivation distinction; Meador and Heflin applied custodial deliberate indifference. Walton's absence of police custody distinguishes its immunity result. Nishiyama's intentional unreasonable exposure to known danger is relevant contemporaneous warning, but its gross-negligence wording and contested noncustodial reach are neither adopted nor indispensable. The present result rejects a categorical immunity defense to knowing coercion, without deciding that every officer actually had the assumed information, opportunity, authority or role.

Farmer, Elder, Foy, Lewellen, Gazette, Kneipp, Pinder, Doe, Lanier, Smith and Lewis do not create retroactive notice. Even Elder's February 23, 1994 decision postdates the conduct. Later law may explain present review methodology and existing doctrine; it cannot supply a missing earlier prohibition. Neither an exact factual twin nor a general bodily-security abstraction controls by itself.
