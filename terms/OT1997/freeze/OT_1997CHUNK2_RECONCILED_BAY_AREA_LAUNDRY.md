## Operative propositions and remedy

Withdrawal creates liability under §1381, but a collection cause does not arise until assessment, a payment schedule and demand have occurred and the employer fails to make a payment then due. Section 1451(f) supplies the longer of six years from accrual or three years from actual or constructive knowledge of the cause, with the statutory fraud/concealment qualification only if its actual predicates are implicated. Knowledge of withdrawal does not create a collection cause before a payment violation.

Each unpaid installment has its own accrual date and limitations period. An expired first installment does not extinguish later timely claims. Statutory default and an election to accelerate are distinct from the first missed payment; when the plan validly exercises acceleration, the period for the accelerated debt runs from that exercise. Neither delinquency, the April cure warning, nor the complaint's alternative request proves an earlier acceleration. Whether the complaint itself accomplished acceleration remains undecided.

The duty to demand payment as soon as practicable remains operative, as do properly available objections to delay. No unreasonable-delay immunity, concealment finding, equitable tolling, or mandatory exhaustion before scheduled payments is created. Concrete Pipe's neutral adjudication and lawful rebuttal rules remain intact; this case neither validates the assessment conclusively nor resolves the uncompleted arbitration.

**Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's limitations judgment and remand. The Fund may pursue the later installments timely under these rules, but may not recover the first $345.50 installment. The judgment awards no sum, orders no full-debt payment, and grants no automatic prospective injunction. Other properly remaining assessment, acceleration and remedial questions retain their ordinary predicates.
