## Almendarez-Torres v. United States — No. 96-6839 — March 24, 1998

### Reconciled rules and judgment components

**P — penalty construction and bounded constitutional conclusion:** Section 1326(b)(2) authorizes enhanced punishment for the alien described in subsection (a) whose deportation followed an aggravated-felony conviction; it does not create an additional offense requiring that prior conviction to be charged in this indictment. Congress may make that prior-adjudication, recidivist status a sentencing factor in this setting. The reciprocal cross-references, recidivist subject and punishment structure provide the statutory ground. McMillan's unchanged-maximum setting and Watts's ordinary sentencing holding do not by themselves resolve this maximum increase; the distinct recidivism basis must do that work. Affirm the Fifth Circuit's sentence judgment.

**E — statutory element construction through constitutional avoidance:** The statute fairly permits treating the prior aggravated-felony conviction as the element authorizing the higher range. The increase from the ordinary two-year maximum to twenty years creates serious constitutional doubt, and the available element construction avoids deciding the constitutional boundary. Hamling then requires the omitted allegation in the indictment. Set aside the sentence affirmance and remand for lawful sentencing on the charged offense. This does not decide that every maximum-increasing fact, or every recidivist provision, must constitutionally be an element.

## United States v. Scheffer — No. 96-1133 — March 31, 1998

### Reconciled components

**S — common constitutional ground:** The right to present a defense permits evidentiary restrictions justified by legitimate trial interests and proportionate to their purposes. The Constitution does not require individualized admissibility proceedings for this polygraph credibility proffer despite Rule 707's categorical form. The rulemakers' supported reliability concern, in the distinct setting of expert certification of truthfulness and with the accused's own factual account available, supplies sufficient justification. Daubert and Joiner interpret the general Rules; they do not constitutionally mandate that regime for every military rule. Rule 707(b) continues to preserve otherwise admissible statements made during an examination. Reverse the CAAF's constitutional judgment and return for consistent proceedings.

**B and C — additional category-specific justifications:** In this polygraph setting, preserving court members' responsibility to judge credibility against a scientifically presented certification of the accused's truthfulness is an additional adequate justification for a categorical rule; so is avoiding the recurring collateral litigation over examiner qualifications, question design, countermeasures and interpretation. These positions permit this rule, not all exclusion of expert evidence bearing on credibility or any defense evidence that consumes time. They are separately joined only as identified below.

**F — opposing constitutional ground:** A total prohibition prevents a showing that a significant defense proffer is sufficiently reliable. Government administration of this examination and the proffer's relation to knowing ingestion make the opportunity consequential. The meaningful-defense protection in Egelhoff and Rock requires lawful foundation review here, without compelling ultimate admission, acquittal or a new trial before that inquiry. Affirm the constitutional rejection of the per se obstacle and preserve lawful further proceedings.

**T — statutory construction and avoidance:** Court can fairly mean the lawful tribunal, including its jury. Reading §504(c) to preserve jury determination of the damages facts and amount avoids an unnecessary constitutional ruling. This path does not adopt C's contrary statutory premise or decide the constitutional question.

## United States v. United States Shoe Corp. — No. 97-372 — March 31, 1998

**J — jurisdiction:** Section 1581(i)(4), read with (i)(1) and §4462(f)(2), gives the CIT exclusive jurisdiction over this administration/enforcement challenge to the HMT exaction. Congress directs customs-duty treatment for jurisdiction; the law also provides revenue from imports. The constitutional challenge attacks the legislatively fixed exaction, not a discretionary Customs valuation or classification decision. Passive collection and a form response do not make it a §1514 protestable decision merely because the company filed a document called a protest. Section 1581(a) therefore is not the necessary channel here. No untimely-protest cure or new deadline exception is adopted.

**M — export-tax merits:** IBM excludes nondiscrimination as a defense to a real federal tax upon exports. Pace preserves genuine compensation for government services, with workable approximation, but a charge must fairly correspond to the exporter's use of the services and facilities rather than tax protected cargo under a service label. This 0.125-percent value-only levy attaches to export loading; earmarking its receipts for useful harbor work does not supply the missing service-related measure. It is an export tax as applied here. Affirm the existing export-refund judgment.
