## Questions and separable commitments

Section 1254(1) supplies the review channel. The Fund seeks reversal of a final federal appellate limitations judgment. No supported jurisdictional or vehicle defect prevents deciding the presented accrual and installment questions.

1. **Accrual:** Reject accrual at withdrawal. Withdrawal establishes liability under §1381, but an action to collect a payment cannot arise before the employer fails to make a payment due under the statutory demand and payment provisions. Sections 1399 and 1451 distinguish the debt, payment obligation, and enforceable delinquency. Section 1451(f) allows the longer of six years from accrual or three years from actual or constructive knowledge of the cause; knowledge of withdrawal cannot start a shorter overriding period.
2. **Installments:** Each unpaid installment gives rise to a claim when it becomes due and is not paid. An untimely action for the first installment does not extinguish claims for later installments within their own statutory periods. Optional acceleration is distinct: a supported election following statutory default can alter when the remaining debt becomes due. Neither missed payments, the April 1987 cure warning, nor the complaint's alternative demand establishes an earlier acceleration.
3. **Judgment and relief:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's limitations judgment and remand for proceedings consistent with these rules. Its withdrawal-based dismissal cannot stand, and the District Court's alternative first-default bar cannot dispose of all later installments. This permits adjudication of timely installment claims; it neither awards a sum nor orders payment of every installment or the entire assessed debt. Any actual acceleration, remaining assessment defenses, arbitration issues, and entitlement to prospective relief require their own lawful predicates.
4. **First installment:** Do not decide its precise due date or timeliness in this review. The petition framed the later-installment conflict as dispositive even assuming the first was barred, while later submissions advanced a receipt-based sixty-day argument. The later-installment holding answers the presented question without selecting an unverified receipt date or converting those submissions into an established waiver. The mandate neither declares the first installment timely nor directs reopening of a matter otherwise foreclosed by the actual procedural record.

## Alternative lawful paths and strongest objections

**Withdrawal-based accrual.** Its strongest support is §1381's creation of liability and the danger that delayed assessment lets a sponsor postpone limitations. Its decisive difficulty is that §1399 makes demand and payment maturity separate prerequisites to a collection violation: at withdrawal the employer has not yet failed a payment obligation the plan has demanded. The discovery clause need not create a cause before one exists to retain independent work in cases involving later discovery. Every Associate rejects this path under the statutory sequence and Milwaukee, while preserving the actual “as soon as practicable” duty and any properly presented delay defense.

**One period for the whole debt at first nonpayment.** This could follow if the debt necessarily matured then. Its strongest argument is that withdrawal liability is one underlying obligation and installment scheduling merely regulates payment. But Congress separately makes acceleration optional following statutory default. Automatic whole-debt accrual would give initial nonpayment an effect the acceleration provision reserves to a further lawful event and election. Every Associate rejects that rule for this unaccelerated schedule. A proved earlier acceleration would materially change the application and requires a neutral factual refresh if newly established.

**Separate accrual.** Its strongest objection is extended exposure to collection proceedings and the risk of artificial delay. It nevertheless follows actual payment maturities and leaves both the demand-timing duty and genuine acceleration intact. It does not restart an expired installment claim, allow indefinite suit on an already matured entire debt, or create equitable tolling. The six-year rule is a floor under the enacted longer-of formulation, not a reason to disregard a separately applicable later knowledge period.

**Resolve the first installment now.** The sixty-day provision and the demand's receipt-based alternative are potentially material. Against immediate resolution stand the unverified receipt date and the petition's narrower dispositive framing. Those are actual factual and review-scope issues, not a shortage of research files treated as default. Leaving that installment undecided does not avoid the supported answer on later installments. None of the eight joins a finding that February 1, 1987 necessarily controlled or that every claim was timely.
