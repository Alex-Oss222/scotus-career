## Propositions and scope

**P1 — Excluded jurors' equal-protection rights.** Campbell's race does not prevent him from challenging purposeful racial exclusion in the judicial selection of a Louisiana grand-jury member. Powers supplies the required concrete injury to the defendant from discriminatory selection of the tribunal, his relationship with the citizens selected to act in his prosecution, and excluded citizens' substantial practical obstacles to litigating individual exclusion. McCollum confirms that the ordinary third-party-standing conditions operate in jury selection without requiring shared race. The foreperson is selected from the venire before the other members are drawn and has full voting powers: this is the selection of a member, not merely appointment to clerical office within a previously constituted body. This proposition is supported by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. It neither presumes discrimination nor provides general standing to vindicate every citizen's rights.

**P2 — Campbell's own due process.** Irrespective of his race, Campbell may challenge purposeful racial discrimination in the selection of a member of the grand jury that indicted him as an injury to the fairness and integrity of his own criminal process. Hobby does not defeat that route. Hobby addressed selection of a federal foreperson from an already selected grand jury and the office's ministerial duties; Louisiana's initial choice supplies a full member of the accusatory body. The distinction is the composition of that body, not a finding that every foreperson duty is substantive. All eight Associates support this personal-right route and the remand; no third-party-standing premise is necessary to it.

**P3 — Proof and remedy.** Reverse the Louisiana Supreme Court's judgment rejecting Campbell's challenge for lack of standing, and remand for consideration of the discrimination claim under the applicable proof rules and further proceedings. The alleged absence of any Black foreperson from January 1976 through August 1993, despite more than twenty percent Black registered voters, is uncontested as recited; it is not a Supreme Court finding that purposeful discrimination is established. The intermediate appellate court considered the evidence inadequate and contemplated an evidentiary hearing. The remand permits necessary fact development and legal assessment without itself adjudicating discrimination, ordering an unconditional new trial or quashing the indictment now. Any ultimate relief must follow the adjudicated violation and applicable remedial law. All eight Associates support this disposition.

**P4 — Unresolved Sixth Amendment issue.** Do not decide the fair-cross-section theory. Initial presentation below is not enough to establish the missing appellate presentation; the packet's specific procedural recitations establish that limitation. This reservation is not an inference from unrecovered research files. All eight Associates join the reservation.

## Supported accommodations

A common opinion may state P2, P3 and P4 for all eight without importing P1's rationale into those joins. Six Associates support a separate P1 portion. Scalia and Thomas may concur in the judgment or join the personal-due-process portions while withholding the excluded-juror-rights portion; no accommodation presently supplies their join in P1. Kennedy will join P1 if juror equality rests on individual citizenship and the actual standing requirements rather than presumed group representation. The distinction between these grounds must survive assembly even though the disposition is common.
