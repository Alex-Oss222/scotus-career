## 1. United States v. Kirk — February 14, 1998

### Component commitments

**Result:** All eight Associates would affirm the judgment sustaining Count One against the granted Commerce Clause challenge. The common ground applies Hale's possession/transfer integration rule to prohibited stock produced by local conversion. ### Alternative paths and scope

The strongest reversal path preserves Hale but distinguishes possession produced by wholly local conversion from a weapon acquired in a sale, arguing that no transfer element or admission establishes this conviction's connection to the market. Its strongest counterargument is the regime's direct concern with additions to prohibited stock: exempting retention of locally converted completed weapons would leave the very additional stock the prohibition controls available for concealment and circulation. All eight reject making a demonstrated prior sale a universal constitutional prerequisite. That condition would narrow the existing class-and-regime rule into a transaction rule it expressly declined to adopt.

The strongest objection to affirmance is that treating every marketable object's possession as necessary to a national market would erase Lopez's boundary. The adopted ground answers it through the particular enacted transfer/stock restriction and material impairment from excluding this completed prohibited stock. It approves no free-standing possession ban justified only by marketability, remote movement, gun crime, fear, insurance, or productivity. It does not decide every conversion-parts, readily-restorable, or wear-induced application of the incorporated definition. The statute's government-related exception and its separate exception for lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986, remain intact; manufacture before that date alone is not enough, and no exception is established here.

Lopez is applied, not overruled: connection precedes magnitude and courts may not insert a missing offense element. Hale supplies the controlling anti-evasion/material-impairment rule; Rybar supplies a recent application, not an additional interstate-crossing or proved-sale element. Parker's cross-border support obligation is a distinct mechanism and supplies no substitute machinegun rule. Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope without performing the dispositive work here.

**Remedy and limits:** Affirmance concerns only the preserved Commerce challenge to Count One. ### Operative rules, alternatives, and preserved distinctions

**Parole and protest letters.** The strongest broader-remand argument invokes Sandin's rejection of mechanical mandatory-word analysis and the substantive public-safety/eligibility criteria in Texas law. The answer is substantive, not verbal: those conditions limit when release may be granted, but do not materially restrict the Board's authority to deny the initial release sought here. No release has already conferred the community liberty protected in Young. Conversely, the absence of entitlement does not decide whether a distinct First Amendment penalty is permissible. All eight will join the disposition only with that separation.

The protest-letter judgment can rest on ordinary rational review without deciding that no classification could ever exist absent an antecedent group. The strongest objection is the trial finding that some statements are inaccurate, vindictive, or unrelated to its preferred criteria. That finding does not establish that every receipt or consideration of a statement lacks a legitimate relation to parole judgment, or that the broad exclusion remedy follows. The decision does not approve knowing official falsification, racial discrimination, punishment of protected litigation, use of a judicially invalidated conviction in defiance of a binding order, or every conceivable use of an individual false statement. Those distinct claims are not established by this protest-letter class judgment.

Article 42.18 §8(f)(2)'s mandatory victim-related notice, permission for written and oral participation, absence of a numerical ceiling on written speakers, and mandatory consideration of the victim-impact statement are separate commands and permissions. Section 8(e)'s file-inclusion requirements do not command reliance on every included allegation. Section 18(a)'s confidentiality is not a finding that all claimed accuracy defects are immaterial under every possible constitutional theory.

**Retaliation and access.** A State's discretion to deny a benefit does not permit denial because of independently protected conduct. The claimant must identify that conduct, show the challenged adverse act and its substantial or motivating connection, and establish the ordinary injury and attribution predicates. The officials may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without the protected conduct. A potentially sufficient lawful parole reason, without an actual counterfactual showing, does not satisfy that defense. Ordinary competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available; no clear-and-convincing or categorical direct-evidence requirement is added.

Lewis governs a claim that inadequate assistance actually or imminently obstructed a qualifying nonfrivolous legal claim. It does not require a prisoner alleging retaliation for a completed protected filing to prove that the filing failed. Johnson v. Avery and Wolff preserve the significance of assistance when necessary to recipients' meaningful court access; the State may supply effective alternative arrangements and enforce valid penological restrictions. No free-standing entitlement to serve as another prisoner's chosen lawyer follows. The remand must distinguish that necessary-access possibility, Johnson's own nonfrivolous filings, frivolous activity, and any separately grounded communication or petition theory actually within the case. It may not declare that every assistance activity is protected or that all assistance is constitutionally irrelevant.

The strongest complete-affirmance path relies on the Fifth Circuit's correct rejection of blanket protection and on genuinely inadequate trial findings. It fails only where its directions import a categorical Lewis restriction or displace Mt. Healthy's burden allocation. Those legal corrections do not erase the legitimate need for further findings. The strongest reinstatement path relies on repeated mentions of litigation and the trial court's general chilling finding. It fails because the record does not connect identified protected activity and attributable Board retaliation to Johnson's parole decision, establish the necessary representative injury, or support the decree's statewide scope. Missing source originals prove no default; the published appellate account itself identifies the missing findings that make remand, rather than appellate merits factfinding, appropriate.

**Equality and remedy.** The rights-dependent writ-writer equality theory remains distinct from protest-letter process and independent ordinary-equality theories. Comparable treatment and purposeful adverse effect must be established under their proper legal predicates; no universal statistical-proof requirement is imposed. Likewise, a conceivably rational ordinary classification is not an actual Mt. Healthy defense to a proved protected-right penalty. Class certification does not establish standing or a statewide wrong. Systemic relief requires a supported common practice and affected group and must fit the proved violation, respecting valid security interests and applicable prospective-relief law. The holding adopts neither a universal Monell requirement for every state-officer injunction nor an entitlement to purge all litigation references. Here the challenged Board-wide practice itself requires proof of Board attribution.

**Mandate:** Affirm the protest-letter dismissal and fee vacatur; vacate only the legally incompatible writ-writer directions; remand the retaliation and writ-writer equality claims for reconsideration consistently with the stated rules and actual record. The existing directive for further proceedings survives otherwise. No parole, release, fresh trial, compulsory discovery, liability, damages, fee award, or statewide injunction is ordered. Unappealed ex post facto, tentative-parole-month/progress, nonresident-furlough, and corporate-entity immunity dispositions stay closed. No personal qualified-immunity ruling is made.

### Operative grounds supported by the six affirming Associates

**Statutory authority.** Section 1395ww(h)(2)(A)'s instruction that the Secretary determine a hospital's average amount recognized as reasonable under the Act for direct GME costs in its fiscal-1984 reporting period permits the reaudit regulation's determination of an accurate allowable-cost base for the later statutory formula. It does not unambiguously command permanent use of every expense an intermediary once reimbursed. The Secretary's construction is reasonable in the context of an annual-cost system becoming an enduring base; the express authority concerning resident numbers does not negate the actual command to determine the average lawful cost. Brown requires the textual inquiry before deference, and its rule against administrative addition of a contrary statutory condition is preserved.

**Retroactivity and finality.** This rule applies the governing base-year cost principles to the antecedent data used for later payments. It neither demands repayment of the closed 1984 reimbursement nor changes what costs were allowable in 1984. Reusing old facts in prospective calculations does not alone attach a forbidden new legal consequence to completed conduct. Later already-paid years may be corrected only if each is independently still open under the governing reopening rules. The ordinary three-year finality of a particular reimbursement remains effective; finality of that payment does not, on this record, conclusively settle every historical cost fact for a distinct statutory calculation. Landgraf and Bowen retain their new-burden limits; the decision does not supply general retroactive legislative-rulemaking authority.

**Reasonableness and reliance.** The agency explained that errors formerly affecting an annual payment would otherwise become enduring formula inputs and that competing Medicare changes delayed scrutiny. That is an actual administrative rationale, not a substitute invented on review. The rule permits correction in both directions: it excludes unallowable/misclassified GME amounts, permits requested inclusion of costs wrongly classified as operating expenses, and permits the corresponding upward prospective-payment operating adjustment where specified. The later-period physician-time documentation allowance, which Regions used, addresses the asserted loss of base-year records. These features support the rule's reasonableness; they do not establish that every actual disallowance or allocation is accurate or that every reliance objection is immaterial.

**Preclusion.** The intermediary's February 28, 1986 notice fixed the annual reimbursement within its lawful finality rules. No PRRB adjudicatory hearing decided these cost issues. On this established posture, the payment-finality regulations and that notice do not create adjudicated issue preclusion binding the new base determination. This is not a categorical rule that administrative adjudications can never preclude later issues, nor permission to reopen a completed Article III judgment.

The strongest objection to affirmance is that Congress selected what had been recognized, deliberately choosing an administratively fixed historic baseline to secure finality. Scalia and Thomas find that textual reading decisive and would invalidate the disputed reaudit authority, with remand for lawful calculations under the existing recognized base and applicable resident-count/inflation rules; no precise payment award follows. **Operative holding:** Under §§1331 and 1441, the assertion that a prior federal judgment precludes a complaint presenting only state-law claims is a defense and does not transform that complaint into one arising under federal law. Complete statutory preemption is different: it concerns whether federal law supplies the claim's controlling legal character, not whether an earlier federal judgment defeats an otherwise state-law claim. An artful-pleading label or the asserted completeness of claim preclusion cannot supply the missing original jurisdiction.

**Application and alternatives:** The mortgage holders seek recognition/enforcement of Louisiana mortgage rights or alternatively damages under Louisiana law; the parties are Louisiana parties. The respondents invoke the bankruptcy orders to defeat those rights. Even if the orders conclusively extinguished them, that would answer a defense, not establish removal jurisdiction. The strongest contrary path reads Moitie footnote 2 as allowing removal whenever federal claim preclusion wholly bars a later state claim. The eight decline that extension: relabeling an essentially federal claim and asserting preclusion of an independent state claim are different. Moitie's ordinary preclusion rule is preserved; the footnote supplies no general exception for this defense. No finding that these petitioners prevail on the mortgage or may disregard the bankruptcy orders follows.

Peacock supplies the need for independent statutory authority in a later action and preserves lawful enforcement within its proper sphere. Chicago had substantial federal claims on the face of the complaints; it cannot supply supplemental jurisdiction here with no original federal anchor. Caterpillar preserves a completed judgment only when actual original jurisdiction exists by trial and judgment; all-Louisiana citizenship and a federal defense provide no such cure. Matsushita, Richards, and Baker retain their own recognition, binding-authority, and execution limits; none makes recognition law a universal removal grant.
