# Calderon v. Thompson — neutral preflight addendum

**Scope:** Supplement to [the original neutral validation](OT_1997CHUNK7_CALDERON_PREFLIGHT_VALIDATED.md), which remains unchanged. This addendum validates the newly supplied [chronology and conditional-writ extract](../research/chunk7_neutral_facts/CALDERON_CHRONOLOGY_ADDENDUM.md). It supplies no commitment, vote, assignment, present-case Supreme Court holding, or decision on whether recall was justified. The same April 29, 1998 cutoff and isolated neutral reading boundary apply. No withheld material or full present-case Supreme Court opinion was opened.

## What the new source supports

The prior validation left the more detailed internal chronology and finer conditional-writ terms unverified. The new extract materially narrows those limits:

1. **January notice and March request.** The published account attributed to Judge Kozinski's lower-court dissent reports that the panel notified the full court on January 17, 1997, of its intention to reject the en banc suggestion. On March 12, an off-panel judge requested an opportunity to make a belated call for an en banc vote and described a communication failure between outgoing and incoming law clerks. Another judge supported the request and questioned whether the January notice had circulated or had been missed. The panel-opinion author rejected the request, invoking the earlier notification and August 1996 circulation of the suggestion. These are reported correspondence and procedural acts, not a new adjudication of every judge's knowledge or responsibility.
2. **A later recall initiative.** The lower en banc majority reported a court-initiated request to consider recall shortly after the June 11 mandate and before the mandate was spread in the District Court. That reported request must be distinguished from the March request for a belated en banc call, the prisoner's July recall motion, and the August 3 recall itself. The extract supplies no exact June request date, completed vote at that point, or automatic suspension of the mandate.
3. **Conditional relief.** The Ninth Circuit directed the District Court to enter a partial writ unless California elected to retry Thompson within a reasonable time. The previously supplied decree identifies the rape conviction, rape special circumstance, vacation of the death sentence, further review of the murder conviction, and vacation of the execution order separately. Read together, the sources establish conditional partial-writ relief concerning the granted rape-related relief, while murder remains subject to the separately directed further review. They do not establish a present murder acquittal or a complete grant of every habeas claim.

No original correspondence, panel-response text beyond the reported explanation, or additional factual finding has been recovered. The second judge's question does not establish that notice actually failed to circulate, particularly alongside the source's reported January notification. It also does not establish that every judge knowingly declined review. The reasonable-time condition identifies the State's election to retry; it must not become an invented fixed deadline for election, commencement, or completion of trial. No compliance, expiration, newly activated clock, or consequence beyond the stated conditional direction is inferred.

## Significance for both supported recall paths

**Court-process repair remains a distinct possible basis.** The March correspondence provides a concrete reported institutional malfunction and an actual effort to obtain consideration after the ordinary opportunity had passed. The later court-initiated request is reported to have preceded the prisoner's July recall motion. These details strengthen the need to distinguish the court's own reconsideration of its first-application judgment from a new application relying on Leitch's later account. They do not establish that procedural repair was required or that the actual recall fell within a permissible exceptional power.

The strongest objection is now more specific: the asserted difficulty was brought to the panel in March, before the June mandate, and the panel expressly rejected a belated call. It is therefore incomplete to describe the case as an unnoticed clerical lapse first discovered immediately before execution. Independent modeling must address why the interval before the mandate and the subsequent delay did or did not defeat an exceptional corrective justification. The reported June initiative and the majority's stated wait for state proceedings supply the competing explanation; they do not establish that every delay was necessary or that correction had already been granted. The sources establish neither a statutory evasion motive nor a finding that lawful en banc review was impossible throughout the interval.

**A substantive-injustice rationale remains separate.** These chronology facts can bear on the permissible exercise of recall power and on whether institutional repair explains the action. They do not prove or disprove innocence, Strickland prejudice, or a constitutional violation. If a Justice would require a Schlup or Sawyer showing for this category of merits reopening, the model must articulate the proposed recall rule and assess the correct evidence and component. No supplied entering holding makes that gateway an automatic prerequisite to every recall. Conversely, a potentially substantial constitutional claim does not itself excuse any defect in recall authority or establish the gateway.

**Remedy remains component-specific.** A proposed affirmance, reversal, or limited remand must identify its effect on the conditional partial writ, murder-review direction, death-sentence treatment, and execution-order vacatur. Undoing recall does not itself authorize execution, prove that execution occurred, or adjudicate all independently available claims. Preserving recall does not by itself resolve every underlying merits question or authorize the operator to choose a retrial deadline.
