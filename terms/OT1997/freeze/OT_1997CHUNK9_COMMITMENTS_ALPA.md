## Questions and common limits

The question is compulsory exhaustion of the offered impartial fee arbitration by nonmembers who did not agree to arbitrate. It is not the ultimate chargeability of an expenditure, the audit's professional adequacy, discovery scope, fee amount, or whether Hudson governs every NLRA arrangement. The litigants either declined arbitration or participated under protest. Their appearance does not supply contractual assent. The 67 arbitration participants who did not sue are not added to this judgment.

All eight accept that the presently asserted obligation has no express statutory exhaustion command; an exhaustion obligation, if justified, would be judicial rather than a term assented to by these pilots. They distinguish binding contractual arbitration from nonbinding preliminary exhaustion. No Justice treats the arbitrator's award as entitled to contractual finality against these plaintiffs. Hudson's requirement is a prompt opportunity before an impartial decisionmaker, not arbitration in every case; its footnote 21 rejects preclusion in subsequent §1983 litigation. The applicability of Hudson's protections to this RLA arrangement is the presented premise rather than a newly adjudicated universal private-union rule.

## Competing lawful paths

**Independent judicial access:** Affirm the rejection of compulsory arbitration. Hudson protects objectors and does not itself require them to use the offered procedure. First Options' antecedent-assent principle excludes a contractual basis, while McCarthy requires a separate assessment of any judicial exhaustion ground. Here an outsider adjudicates rather than completing a statutory agency or plan's authoritative internal decision; no congressional allocation gives that decision deference. Useful expertise and possible settlement do not justify a mandatory additional adjudication before the independent judicial proceeding these employees retain. The strongest objection is that useful nonbinding exhaustion need neither contractual assent nor preclusion. This path answers that objection by the particular source, institutional fit, and duplication at issue, not by declaring every nonconsensual exhaustion requirement unlawful.

**Protective procedure first:** Reverse the categorical rejection of exhaustion, while preserving independent judicial adjudication afterward. Hudson itself calls for a prompt impartial process; a reasonable requirement to use a compliant process can identify disputed expenses, permit error correction and avoid unnecessary judicial disputes even when its result has no preclusive force. McCarthy permits sound judicial discretion without a statutory command. The strongest objection is that an option established to protect unwilling payers becomes an additional burden on their access to court, without an authoritative administrative decision to complete. This path answers that the initial process is confined to the particular prompt, impartial fee procedure and may eliminate or narrow disputes; it does not authorize a union to impose any private procedure it chooses, make arbitration binding, or evade judicial review. Actual compliance and practical adequacy remain indispensable.

## Proposition and remedy checks

Six Associates—Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg—support affirming the rejection of compulsory arbitration for these nonconsenting objectors. O'Connor and Breyer support a bounded preliminary-exhaustion rule and reversal with remand. These are non-Stone commitments, not a final Court tally. All eight reject contractual assent from protest and any automatic preclusive treatment of this award.

The six share the sufficient proposition that neither Hudson's protection nor the actual judicial-exhaustion justifications require these employees to undergo this private arbitration before suing. Scalia and Thomas do not independently adopt a general discretionary balancing formulation; an eventual common opinion must preserve that limit or expose the narrower join. No shared disposition decides a specific fee, audit, expenditure, class certification, discovery order, absent objector's rights, or the unpresented NLRA constitutional question.

The alternative-exhaustion position concerns a lawful first step, not a restoration of the district court's deference to the award. Existing participants have already used the process. Any consequence for nonparticipants requires a compliant available procedure; no new arbitral award, new deadline or forfeiture is invented. The source packet supports adjudication of this question without unrecovered briefs or policies. A ruling on particular unquoted policy terms or disputed consent would require further neutral source work.
