## What is actually established, and what is not

The FBI acknowledged a 23-page Lindblom file and five pages mentioning the Foundation in other files. At least some released redacted material describes Lindblom's associations. The district court reviewed withheld material in camera and described investigations of other people with whom the plaintiffs had incidental, apparently innocent contacts. These are actual records, not a speculative future plan to compile a file. Because original collection was not challenged, its pertinence to an authorized activity when collected is assumed; no finding follows that every record has now lost all lawful connection or that any current retention violates the Act.

The Foundation offered the Lies and Hallett affidavits describing possible reputational consequences, applicant deterrence and employee employment difficulty. The missing causal step is how prospective grantees or employers would learn of the FBI references in a manner supporting the claimed injury from retention. No reported FOIA practice, actual grantee response or other mechanism supplies that step. The existence of FOIA, plaintiffs' own publicity and an imagined third-party request do not, without the necessary evidence, fill it. The Court does not adopt a universal rule that an injured person forfeits standing by publicizing governmental conduct.

Lindblom supplied no distinct constitutional-standing argument or affidavits, no asserted impaired personal employment prospects, and relied on the Foundation's alleged harm. His departure from that Foundation does not itself moot a statutory challenge to the FBI's still-maintained records about him. His statutory personal privacy theory is different from the derivative constitutional injury actually presented; nothing here declares that a concrete injury can support only a statutory theory in every case or that a person must suffer lost employment to challenge a personal information invasion.

**Original monetary request:** The published opinion expressly states that the original suit sought monetary damages as well as injunctive and expungement relief. The source does not allocate that request to a particular surviving claim or establish preservation, abandonment, subsection, amount, intentional-or-willful conduct or actual damages. The appellate abandonment sentence concerns FOIA and Fifth/Ninth Amendment claims, not all monetary relief. The statutory discussion's focus on expungement cannot be converted into a finding that no money claim remains. Conversely, the original collective demand is not proof of a preserved, legally viable Privacy Act damages claim. Both limits are essential to the remand and to rejecting a whole-action redressability dismissal based solely on the equity question.

## Threshold sequence and rules each Justice accepts here

**A. Separate the parties, injuries, theories and relief actually under review.** The Foundation asserts only a constitutional claim. Lindblom asserts a constitutional theory and a statutory claim. His own retained associational records can implicate a personal interest distinct from the Foundation's reputational theory. A court must identify the actual surviving relief and claim-specific monetary history rather than invent a replacement plaintiff, officer, amendment request, damages demand, concession or abandonment. The existing records, allegations and actual procedural rulings provide the starting point; absence of their originals from the research archive is not a litigant's failure of proof.

**B. Apply Article III to the statutory claim on its own supported interest.** Lujan and actual Lee require specific summary-judgment support, with favorable reasonable inferences, for concrete personal injury, traceability and likely redress. The actual retention of records describing Lindblom's own protected associations presents a materially different candidate injury from an undifferentiated wish that government obey the law. Congress can recognize a concrete personal information interest; a bare assertion of an (e)(7) violation nevertheless cannot establish every constitutional element. The question is the supported present invasion of that actual interest, not the legal merits disguised as injury and not a presumed future leak. This Court does not finally decide the full statutory standing issue on an unexplained remedial premise.

The statutory merits may be contested without making the federal claim insubstantial. A court cannot defeat standing simply by deciding that every lawfully collected record may be retained; that is the disputed merits construction. Conversely, stating that expungement would physically end the alleged retention does not establish judicial power to order it. Where the causal theory is the FBI's own continuing possession, no independent third-party awareness is inherently necessary, but a legally available redress route still matters. Article III injury, legal entitlement and redress are not interchangeable labels.

**C. Identify the actual §552a(g) cause and adverse-effect predicate.** Section (e)(7) supplies a substantive duty with three alternatives: express statutory authorization, individual authorization, or pertinence to and scope of an authorized law-enforcement activity. The Act's civil-action machinery must then be respected. Under (g)(1)(D), other noncompliance must occur in a way having an adverse effect on the individual. That phrase is distinct from the adverse agency determination in (g)(1)(C) and from intentional-or-willful conduct and actual damages in (g)(4). The court must assess whether the supported personal privacy invasion is an adverse effect under the actual statutory route. The bare violation is not automatically sufficient, and the Foundation's failed reputation evidence is not automatically dispositive.

Doe v. FBI rejected direct (e)(7) relief bypassing (g)(1)(D) on an actual, unchallenged no-adverse-effect finding. No such finding is established here. Its alternative law-enforcement conclusion did not decide the present continuing-pertinence dispute. Nagel distinguished ordinary amendment exhaustion from an (e)(7) claim and recognized lawful remedial possibilities, but did not adjudicate every civil-action predicate. Clarkson located possible expungement through the amendment provisions, expressly described the limited statutory injunction categories, and reserved actual jurisdictional facts. These cases are date-eligible circuit authorities to reconcile with the statute, not interchangeable Supreme Court holdings or proof that Lindblom wins or loses.

**D. Establish the actual remedy and sovereign consent; do not bootstrap one from the duty.** A (g)(1)(A)/(g)(2) amendment order depends on the relevant (d)(2)–(3) request/refusal/review route and applicable exemptions. The report neither establishes satisfaction of those predicates nor establishes that no qualifying request occurred. A FOIA request is not automatically an amendment request. The access route in (g)(1)(B)/(g)(3) authorizes production in its own setting, not a general future-retention injunction, and it does not revive the abandoned FOIA challenge.

For a (g)(1)(C) or (D) suit, (g)(4) expressly provides monetary liability upon intentional-or-willful action, actual damages resulting from the violation and the stated minimum/cost provisions. No present award or finding follows; no later Doe v. Chao or Cooper gloss is imported. Whether any such money claim remains must be established from the actual action and appeal. The original general monetary request is neither discarded nor allocated by speculation. The Act's express remedial channels cannot be bypassed simply by calling the desired relief declaratory or directly invoking (e)(7).

Reich and the exact §702 preserve consideration of independently available prospective equitable review of unlawful official action, with the ordinary legal/equitable predicates and any express or implied statutory displacement. Section 702 is a nonmoney sovereign-immunity waiver, not by itself an adequate cause of action or permission to disregard the Privacy Act's remedial choices. Reich's actual implementing-officer suit does not silently replace the FBI with an unpleaded officer here. Any eventual mandatory decree must identify the responsible officer or successor by name or title. The Court does not now decide that the Act always precludes an independent route or that §702 always supplies one; that genuinely presented route and the displacement question must be addressed if necessary on the actual surviving action. Meyer forbids a direct agency Bivens damages invention and keeps waiver distinct from cause. It does not bar a genuinely authorized statutory damages claim.

**E. Apply the real exemptions to the real route.** The July 1997 §16.96 rule lists the Central Records System, its specified exemptions and their statutory (j)/(k) limits. The agency's rule alone does not establish the classification and coverage of every disputed record. A lawful (d) exemption can defeat a derivative (g)(1)(A) amendment cause; it is not erased because expungement would be desirable. Neither (j) nor (k) authorizes exempting the (e)(7) substantive duty. Doe/Tijerina's distinction between exemptible amendment duties and nonexemptible duties prevents treating a general (g) exemption as automatic freedom from the latter. This does not mean all remedies are automatically available. The FOIA exemptions previously used to withhold information are not substitutes for the Privacy Act exemption analysis. No agency waiver is assumed.

**F. Reach the contested retention construction only if the preceding necessary predicates support it.** Steel Co.'s actual June 2, 1997 holding forbids assuming Article III power to decide this genuinely disputed statutory merits question. Neither Norton nor Avrech has already controlled this construction. Not every (g) element is made constitutional jurisdiction by the statute's grant of district-court jurisdiction. The holding leaves appropriate ordering among genuine nonmerits thresholds intact; this remand does not impose a universal algorithm requiring every conceivable element to be adjudicated after another ground already disposes of a claim. It does require a lawful route to an actual merits decision. No Justice uses corrective jurisdiction to announce a statutory retention rule.

## Constitutional component and its strongest alternative

**Principal ground, all eight:** Affirm jurisdictional dismissal of the Foundation's constitutional theory for the unsupported retention-to-third-party-knowledge link, and Lindblom's presented derivative constitutional theory for want of his own supported injury. Meese permits supported reputational/electoral injury without naming every lost supporter or proving a completed loss. It involved an actual public governmental designation and evidence connecting that designation to consequences. Here the affidavits do not provide the needed route by which outsiders learn of the FBI references. Laird's subjective-chill rule and Lujan/Lee's stage-specific requirements apply without imposing certainty, a numerical probability or a particular document. The Court need not adopt the panel's broader suggestions about inherently speculative reputational injuries or all self-publicized harms.

**Strongest alternative:** Vacate for fuller consideration of reputational harm under Meese because the panel asked for named employees/grantees and treated plaintiffs' own publicity as decisive. That would correct an overbroad categorical rule if it were necessary to the result. It is rejected here because the independent missing causal mechanism remains even when supported reputational injury is fully recognized and the affidavits receive reasonable favorable inferences. The hypothetical FOIA practices in Tatel's opinion and the majority footnote are illustrations, not evidence in this case. A remand to see whether an imagined mechanism can be found would not follow from Lee. A different source-supported mechanism would require reconsideration; none is supplied by this record.

The Foundation's separate statutory standing is not created: it asserted no statutory claim. The constitutional dismissal does not decide whether the FBI may retain these records consistently with the First Amendment, whether a different adequately supported direct privacy claim could proceed, or what relief such a claim could support. The monetary clarification does not provide a concrete constitutional injury missing from these presented theories, and it does not supply an agency Bivens cause.

## Enhanced statutory paths and remedy consequences

**Path 1 — limited corrective remand before retention merits: principal for all eight.** The appellate opinion decided contested retention law while the distinct statutory injury, adverse-effect route and actual available relief were not independently resolved. The specific monetary-history clarification removes any sound premise that the court can simply treat this as a verified equity-only action and dismiss the whole statutory case if one injunction route fails. This is a substantive threshold problem: an actually maintained personal file may implicate a statutory privacy interest; lawful redress depends on distinct channels and exemptions; and the existing lower ruling did not settle them. Vacatur removes the statutory merits disposition pending that determination without granting relief or creating a new claim.

Its strongest objection is that a second threshold proceeding rewards failure to prove standing or postpones a pure legal question that the Court can decide itself. The response is bounded: the source reports no adverse statutory-standing finding or waived remedy resolving these points, no conclusively absent amendment history, and no adjudication of the particular (g) route. The Court does not invent missing evidence or promise a hearing. Ordinary resolution on the existing lawful record may suffice, including dismissal where a real predicate fails. The remand corrects an actual unresolved necessary premise of the merits judgment; it is not an instruction to complete the research archive.

**Path 2 — recognize present personal privacy injury now, then remand only for the remaining statutory/remedial predicates.** This is a lawful narrower-remand alternative. The FBI's acknowledged retention of Lindblom's own associational information supplies concrete conduct affecting an identified person, and Congress has specifically protected that informational interest. Lujan permits statutory recognition of concrete injuries; Nagel treats unlawful recordation as a personal concern rather than merely a generalized grievance. Third-party employment harm need not be required. On this path, however, a recognition of injury in fact alone would not determine adverse effect, full Article III redressability, the amendment/exemption route or preserved money relief.

The strongest objection is premature conversion of a substantial personal-interest theory into a conclusive finding about the invasion and applicable legal route, where the court below never separated those questions and the report does not establish all their premises. Each Justice chooses the threshold remand without this additional holding. It leaves the concrete personal-information theory genuinely open and rejects categorical barriers, rather than declaring every statutory violation an injury. Neither the physical existence of a record alone nor a possible downstream leak becomes a universal standing rule. This alternative remains legally available on a sufficient actual threshold record; it is not a present finding or instruction to find injury.

**Path 3 — affirm a threshold dismissal because no lawful statutory relief is available.** The text creates a substantial argument against a free-standing injunction under (g)(1)(D), and an applicable (d) exemption can foreclose an amendment route. The strongest reason for immediate dismissal is that the statute carefully specified relief and §702 preserves other statutes' prohibitions. The strongest objection is decisive to that whole-action disposition on this report: no conclusive applicable amendment exemption/request determination or independent-equity disposition has been made, and the original monetary demand is real but its surviving claim-specific scope is unresolved. The Court cannot infer both that money relief disappeared and that every equitable route fails. This path may be proper after the necessary actual determinations; it is rejected as the present comprehensive judgment. It does not justify deciding retention merits on assumed standing.

**Path 4 — resolve retention merits now on a supposedly automatic statutory right of action.** Nagel's language about expungement and the actual personal file make this superficially attractive. But Doe, Clarkson's own footnote, the exact (g) scheme, the exemption provisions, Reich and Meyer require more than a substantive prohibition plus a desired order. Steel Co. bars using an assumed route to adjudicate contested merits. All eight reject this path now. No existing Supreme Court decision controls the merits so as to invoke the preserved exceptional sequence.

**The two actual retention constructions remain unresolved, not ignored.** The FBI's strongest merits reading treats pertinence as a quality of a lawfully gathered historical record and emphasizes authorized law-enforcement activity broader than one open investigation. Closing an investigation need not destroy continuing historical usefulness. Its strongest textual objection is that (a)(3) defines maintain to include keeping as well as collection and (e)(7) uses that defined term, while Congress used collection/compilation language elsewhere. A categorical once-lawful/always-retainable rule needs to account for that independent operation.

Lindblom's strongest reading applies the pertinence/scope condition during continued retention, protecting against unnecessary information inventories independently of dissemination restrictions. Its strongest objection is the breadth and continuity of legitimate enforcement activities and the danger of converting pertinence into present investigative necessity. The reading need not require closure-triggered destruction, a periodic universal file audit, proof of immediate necessity or public disclosure of classified reasons. Conversely, the FBI's separate (b) dissemination restrictions do not themselves erase an (e)(7) retention limitation. Statutory or individual authorization and any genuine preservation obligation are distinct possibilities requiring an actual basis, not assumed concessions. Neither construction receives an operative vote because no Justice reaches the merits on the present threshold posture.

**Discovery component:** Do not order the classified records released, reopen FOIA, or reverse the discovery ruling as a stand-alone abuse-of-discretion holding. The statutory merits-dependent discovery assessment returns only to the extent a necessary threshold/remedy issue or a later lawfully reached merits question changes what is material. Existing confidential/in camera treatment may be used as lawful. The lower court determines any genuinely necessary limited inquiry under ordinary procedure; the mandate commands no additional evidence, deposition, hearing or new pleading. A finding that the district court reviewed the materials does not establish an unperformed Privacy Act exemption or adverse-effect analysis.
