## Decisive individual grounds, alternatives and join barriers

**Stevens.** His Moulton join, Michigan v. Jackson opinion and Patterson dissent make claim 9's separation of willing conversation from waiver decisive. He joins the narrower remand shared with Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer without requiring adoption of his broader Patterson position. Discussion of allegedly falsified records that support the alibi is not removed categorically from the indicted case merely by calling it a work-record discussion. The court must distinguish the state order's meaning from deliberate elicitation, actual invocation and a knowing, intelligent and voluntary constitutional choice. The strongest affirmance argument is that both reviewing courts reported waiver and no relevant invocation, with deference owed to historical findings. That does not, for Stevens, answer a mixed legal conclusion whose stated support is willingness to speak and a possibly erroneous separation of subjects. He does not infer a Jackson invocation from appointment or the order; a real invocation predicate must be established before applying its rule.

His Kyles join and authorship of Carriger's separate-elements treatment support remand of claim 15. A finding that investigators had no **credible suspect** does not by itself decide whether they possessed and withheld favorable investigative information with lawful impeachment or preparation value. The strongest opposing path reads that finding as a factual rejection of the precise allegation rather than a categorical Brady rule. He preserves the finding but rejects using that shorthand as a complete constitutional answer without the claim's actual information and suppression/materiality predicates. No favorable item is found to exist. His Schlup and Carriger opinions also explain why a correct gateway would be essential if needed, but those decisions preserve independent merits denial and do not demand an advisory gateway inquiry. His Wright concurrence join and contemporaneous Strickland rule support the common denials; on claim 7, Griffin's prohibition concerns use of silence, and the supported paragraph does not establish that use. He joins the narrow finality-era result for claim 10 rather than a rule that every extension is new. His prior mitigation positions do not justify deciding a moot death claim.

**O'Connor.** Her Patterson majority join permits an appropriately informed postindictment waiver; her Davis separate position does not erase the controlling possible-request rule, whose factual trigger remains absent for claims 11–13. She would affirm claim 9 because the lower opinion expressly determines voluntary Fifth and Sixth Amendment waiver after treating legal questions independently, and the supplied claim establishes neither an operative prior invocation nor an invalidating waiver fact. Her strongest objection to the remand position is that appellate brevity and unrecovered recordings do not themselves establish use of a willingness-only test. She will not join a proposition that talking alone always waives counsel, that other-offense investigation immunizes deliberate elicitation about pending charges, or that counsel's limited notice agreement waives Lucas's constitutional rights. A genuine contrary invocation or inadequate-warning fact would require different treatment.

Her Kyles join and Carriger Part III join, however, make claim 15's legal shortcut insufficient. A state finding excluding a credible alternative perpetrator can be respected while considering other lawful uses of actual withheld information. Carriger's conditional materiality treatment reinforces that separate question; it supplies no finding of suppression or materiality here and no duty to obtain every government file. She joins remand to determine the existing claim under those elements, not a new witness-file acquisition theory. The strongest affirmance ground is the underlying hearing and absence of convincing rebuttal; it preserves the historical finding, but cannot give a mixed Brady conclusion the presumption's protection. Her Keeney opinion explains why a hearing may sometimes be required yet does not order one without its predicates. Her Fretwell opinion supplies the express limit against the lower court's universal present-law prejudice formulation; the alternative grounds nevertheless resolve claims 5–6. Brecht/O'Neal's demanding prejudice rules do not require a harmlessness holding when claim 7 fails on the antecedent constitutional question.

**Scalia.** His Davis refusal to require clarification and his Wright principal-opinion join do not supersede their narrower controlling holdings. He accepts the actual no-communicated-request facts and the ordinary Jackson sufficiency disposition, rather than seeking to change the governing tests. His Patterson majority join supports claim 9 affirmance at the reported valid-waiver ground. The strongest contrary argument identifies a real difference between the state court's order and constitutional waiver, but the lower court itself acknowledged that difference and expressly resolved the constitutional issue. In his view, the reported record permits that conclusion; source nonrecovery supplies no reason to presume that warnings, comprehension or other necessary conditions were absent. He does not find any unreported warning or initiation fact and will not join a general equation of cooperation and waiver.

On claim 15, his Carriger dissent join emphasizes that supported state findings and the particular submission can permit a definitive denial without another inquiry. He reads the lower disposition as rejecting the factual premise of Lucas's specific alleged credible-other-suspect information, not as promulgating an exclusion of all information concerning investigated persons. The strongest remand argument is Kyles's lawful-use distinction. He accepts that rule but finds no established different suppressed favorable set requiring adjudication in this presented claim; he does not find such information absent solely because original files were unrecovered. His opposition to a further remand does not invent a gateway bar for claim 15. His own Bancorp opinion supplies partial equitable vacatur of death-only judgments, and his Spencer join leaves conviction custody distinct. He accepts the narrow claim 7 no-error ground; neither the historical Brecht alternative nor a new general harmlessness rule enters his commitment.

**Kennedy.** His Patterson majority join and Withrow Court-opinion join support separate constitutional consideration without making representation an absolute bar to talking. He affirms claim 9 on the published valid-waiver determination, while refusing either a general no-counsel-needed rule or a presumption that the November order itself invoked an additional federal protection. The strongest remand path is that impeachment of the alibi concerns the charged crime and therefore requires a real Sixth Amendment waiver. Kennedy accepts that legal proposition; he finds the reported waiver disposition sufficient for this challenge rather than treating the other-subject characterization as the exclusive ground. The actual historical findings retain former-law protection, but the legal conclusion is not accorded automatic deference.

His Carriger dissent join supports claim 15 affirmance on the existing factual rejection. **Souter.** His Withrow and Davis opinions require the actual constitutional predicate rather than a shortcut from a state opportunity, formal label or postrequest cooperation.
