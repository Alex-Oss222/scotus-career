**Event:** April 22, 1998; No. 96-1060.
**Stage and scope:** Affected independent modeling on the new neutral factual/statutory addendum, before renewed historical reconciliation or Stone assembly. The original Miller neutral packet, entering-law slice and independent commitments remain available; the original freeze is preserved. No historical comparator, reconciliation, private Stone material or control review was received. No author is assigned. Research cutoff and participation remain unchanged: all eight Associates participate, with nine seated Justices and five required for judgment.

This addendum replaces only the original commitment file's treatment of accepted eligibility predicates, the former-law route, and the grounds and remedy affected by those corrections. Its current rows below govern those matters. Original legal conclusions and join limits not contradicted here remain intact. It is not a Court action or a historical reconciliation.

## Corrected common premises

1. The State Department accepted Charlie Miller's United States citizenship and sufficient physical presence to transmit citizenship, and reported adequate evidence of access to the mother at the probable time of conception. Those are administratively accepted predicates, not missing proofs or new Supreme Court findings. Do not demand reconstruction of individual periods of presence or substitute a different statutory version to create an unreported deficiency.
2. Biological paternity is undisputed. There is no reported formal clear-and-convincing finding; that distinction does not establish disputed paternity, inadequate evidence or administrative rejection on that basis.
3. The administrative denial rests expressly on §1409(a)(4). It does not include an independent rejection for deficient citizenship, physical presence, paternity or a support promise.
4. The transitional former-law route is available and required legitimation before twenty-one, without the written-support condition added in 1986. The amended route's blood-proof, support and recognition conditions cannot be added to the former route. The separately applicable §1401 transmission predicates remain distinct and, as relevant here, administratively accepted.
5. The Texas decree supplies the reported legitimation and biological/legal parent-child relationship, but its July 1992 entry follows both available age deadlines. No earlier recognition is added. The absence of a separately recovered document electing the former law establishes neither waiver nor an additional factual blocker; no new election formality is invented.

The original passages suggesting that unproved physical presence, an unestablished support promise or a general absence of findings on all eligibility prerequisites independently prevents relief are withdrawn at that scope. The substantive equality question, whose right is asserted, and the constitutional remedial-power question remain open for independent decision; the new facts do not resolve them automatically.

## Remedial analysis now adopted by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer

Their original view correctly distinguished constitutional correction from equitable naturalization, but the original unexplored remaining-eligibility reservation is no longer supportable. The present record permits a remedy directed to the actual obstacle. Section 1401 supplies citizenship at birth through a qualifying citizen parent; the applicable former §1409 route makes legitimation the additional condition, with a deadline. The decree establishes the stated legal relationship, and the agency accepts the relevant transmission predicates. Removing the constitutionally defective age limitation therefore leaves an existing statutory route to apply rather than requiring a court to invent an unrelated naturalization program or import the maternal one-year presence period.

Heckler v. Mathews requires recognition that equality can be repaired by withdrawing or extending a benefit; it does not command withdrawal. Califano v. Westcott supports extension where that course best respects the statute's benefit-conferring structure, while leaving the judicial obligation to examine the actual statute intact. Here the narrower correction removes the invalid deadline from the claimant's available paternal route, retaining legitimation and the independent transmission prerequisites. Withdrawing maternal citizenship protection instead would unsettle a separate statutory protection for a different class and would not give effect to the general citizen-parent route with its valid prerequisites. These Justices select the former correction as the better fit with the statutory scheme; they do not attribute that conclusion to an administrative concession or announce that extension is always mandatory.

Pangilinan's prohibition on dispensing citizenship by equity does not, for these three, bar a constitutional judgment followed by application of the statute remaining after the violation-linked correction. They would reverse and remand for entry of appropriate declaratory recognition under the former-law route, with any ordinary implementation necessary to give effect to its available election. They would not order an evidentiary hearing about the accepted paternal qualifications, require a written-support promise belonging to the amended route, or decide an unpresented passport-processing dispute. This remedial selection is their independent legal conclusion, not an inference that every constitutional defect automatically establishes citizenship.

## Adversarial limits and current coalition

The strongest objection to the three-Justice remedy is Scalia and Thomas's position that deleting the deadline enlarges Congress's citizenship class even when all other relevant predicates are accepted. The three reject that characterization because they apply the residual citizenship-at-birth statute after a constitutional correction; Scalia and Thomas retain it because the original statutory grant expressly included the deadline. The factual addendum eliminates an extraneous factual objection but does not erase this legal disagreement.

Stevens's merits position still rests on the lawful role of timely recognition, not doubt about biological fatherhood. O'Connor and Kennedy's claimant position still rests on the father's absent hindrance, not a lack of redressable concrete injury. No changed threshold fact supports importing the three reversal supporters' own-right theory into those positions.

The Associate judgment division remains five for affirmance and three for reversal. The affirmance rationales remain distinct: Stevens on narrow merits; O'Connor and Kennedy on their claimant/limited own-claim route; Scalia and Thomas on remedial power. No five-Associate rationale or Marks synthesis is created. The three reversal supporters now have a common directed-remedy position rather than an unresolved-prerequisite remand. Their change is confined to the supported effect of the corrected record and the independently stated remedial choice.
