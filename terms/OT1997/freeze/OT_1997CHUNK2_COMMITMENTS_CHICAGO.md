## Threshold, propositions, and formal judgment

The review channel is §1254(1) review of the Seventh Circuit's judgment following the District Court's merits disposition. Section 1447(d) does not prevent this review: the case is not an attempt to review a district court's covered §1447(c) remand. The appellate court's jurisdictional direction following review of a federal merits judgment is the judgment under review. Quackenbush and Things Remembered retain their actual remand-review limits; no general exception for erroneous jurisdictional remands is created.

**Power to adjudicate.** These complaints plead substantial federal constitutional claims, including facial challenges independently cognizable under §1331. The fact that Illinois provides an administrative-review form and limits factual review does not remove the federal character of the pleaded federal claims. The removed civil actions contain claims within original federal-question jurisdiction. Section 1441(a)'s original-jurisdiction condition is satisfied; it does not require that every joined claim independently qualify.

**Related state claims.** Section 1367(a) supplies jurisdiction over the state administrative-review and constitutional claims arising from the same designation, permit denials, property, and procedure. They form the same Article III case as the federal claims. Record-confined review and deference to agency findings describe the judicial task; they do not supply an unexpressed categorical exclusion from §1367(a). The district court is exercising judicial jurisdiction over a removed civil action challenging agency action, not appellate jurisdiction over a state-court judgment. No ruling on federal district-court review of actual state-court judgments follows.

**Discretion and abstention.** Power does not require retention. Section 1367(c)'s enacted grounds and the Gibbs values remain applicable, as do properly supported abstention grounds with Quackenbush's remedy limits. The Seventh Circuit did not decide the challenges to retention. The District Court's consideration of economy, convenience, fairness, and comity does not make every §1367(c) or abstention objection disappear, but neither does ICS's assertion about unsettled Illinois law establish a compulsory remand.

**Disposition.** Reverse the Seventh Circuit's jurisdictional judgment and remand to that court for further proceedings consistent with the existence of original and supplemental jurisdiction, including the properly presented challenges to discretionary retention and abstention. Do not reinstate or affirm the District Court's entire merits judgment. No ruling is made on the substantive validity of the ordinances, due process, equal protection, takings, economic hardship, evidentiary sufficiency of the Commission's decisions, or the distinct Lakefront action.

## Enhanced comparison of the live alternatives

**Recognize original and supplemental power, then review retention.** The strongest objection is functional: Illinois has created an appellate proceeding in which factual additions are forbidden, and ordinary federal trial jurisdiction may not extend to performing a State's prescribed appellate task. The answer sufficient for the frozen commitments is that the federal court is hearing an initial judicial civil action, with pleaded federal claims and an express grant over the connected state claims. Congress's original-jurisdiction requirement attaches to the civil action containing the federal anchor; §1367(a) expressly supplies the other claims. A different standard of review is not a different Article III case. This ground requires neither de novo factual adjudication nor a freestanding federal administrative-appeal jurisdiction.

**Exclude record-confined state review from statutory power.** This is a serious interpretive alternative, not a frivolous argument. It would preserve the State's integrated review process and distinguish independent facial federal litigation from appellate scrutiny of agency findings. Its strongest difficulty is identifying an enacted exclusion applicable despite a substantial original federal claim and common factual controversy. Peacock's lack of any current original claim cannot supply that exclusion; Quackenbush's discretionary restraints do not establish lack of power. On the supplied record, none of the eight adopts this statutory construction. A draft need not disparage the structural concern; it must answer it at this level and preserve the specific federalism mechanisms.

**Affirm remand through §1367(c) or abstention despite jurisdiction.** ICS actually urges these alternative grounds; they remain legally possible. The strongest argument favors first state judicial construction and protection of a coherent local administrative process. The current packet does not establish that all necessary predicates and claim-specific relief classifications compel remand as a matter of law. The District Court did consider the governing values, and the Seventh Circuit did not decide those objections. Their ordinary appellate resolution therefore remains necessary; this Court should neither invent their rejection nor assume their success. No new universal findings checklist, presumption against retention, or required state-first sequence is adopted.

**Affirm the District Court's merits result immediately.** Recognition of power removes the circuit's ground but does not decide the unaddressed retention arguments or the constitutional and statutory merits. No Associate joins unconditional reinstatement. Conversely, a remand for further consideration is not itself a conclusion that discretion was abused.
