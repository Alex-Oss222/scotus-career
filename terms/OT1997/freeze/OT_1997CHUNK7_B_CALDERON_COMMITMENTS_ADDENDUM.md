## Changed factual basis and result

The eight provisional results remain four for affirmance and four for reversal, but the affirmance ground must now confront actual premandate notice and an actual rejected request. It may not depend on the earlier absence of those details. The following supported distinctions replace that incomplete premise:

- The published account attributed to Judge Kozinski's dissent reports January 17 notice of the panel's intention to reject the en banc suggestion. It then reports the March 12 belated-call request, the described clerk-transition communication failure, another judge's support and question about notice, and the panel author's rejection invoking earlier circulation and notification. The question whether notice was missed does not establish that notice was never circulated. The account also does not establish every judge's subjective knowledge or responsibility.
- Thus a request to correct the alleged failure was known and rejected before the June 11 mandate. This was not an unnoticed lapse first discovered on the eve of execution. The interval after the March request is a real finality objection; unavailable finer knowledge dates do not neutralize it.
- Separately, the en banc majority reports a court-initiated recall request shortly after the mandate, before it was spread in the District Court and before the prisoner's July recall motion. That is evidence of an independent institutional initiative, not proof that the mandate was already recalled, stayed or ineffective. The March request, June initiative, July prisoner motion and August 3 recall remain separate events.
- The August 3 action retained its limitation to first-petition claims and evidence. The stated wait for state proceedings explains the circuit's chosen sequence but does not establish that every day of delay was necessary.

The evidence assessment, separate crime/eligibility gateways, former-section-2254(d) safeguards, ordinary-review channel and exclusion of Leitch's later account remain as recorded in the original freeze. The new chronology does not itself establish or refute innocence or ineffective assistance.

## Revised assessment of the two lawful recall paths

**Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain affirmance.** They recognize that the March request was rejected and that the court had time before the mandate to address the problem. Their ground is not ignorance of the problem or a right to belated en banc review. The reported clerk-transition failure and actual belated-call effort identify a concrete institutional consideration problem; the court's reported June initiative, preceding the prisoner's new-evidence motion, gives independent support to its characterization of the eventual action as correction of its own first-application process. The panel author's refusal to permit a belated call is material, but does not by itself abolish the full court's exceptional power over its mandate. An extraordinary corrective judgment can remain available even after a procedural opportunity was knowingly refused, as Ohio Power's exceptional reopening illustrates; that authority does not make every refused rehearing correctable.

These four consider the full sequence a sufficiently specific institutional explanation to remain within restrained abuse-of-discretion review. The months after the March request, the June mandate and the State's execution scheduling weigh against reopening. The independent June initiative, first-application record limitation and explanation for awaiting the state proceeding weigh in its favor. They accept the latter as sufficient here without finding the delay necessary, the March rejection unlawful, or all judges blameless. The ultimate inquiry is exceptional exercise of retained judicial power, not whether the lower court selected the best available timing. They would not substitute a universal innocence prerequisite for that assessment.

Hazel-Atlas supplies exceptional appellate protection of judicial process while leaving its fraud predicate distinct. Ohio Power confirms that the ordinary finality of a court's own procedural decisions is not an absolute abolition of corrective power. Carlisle forbids circumvention of an actual governing rule; it does not establish a supplied rule removing this power after a belated-call rejection. Lonchar protects required process but does not make en banc review required. Accordingly, their rationale remains a bounded judgment about the circuit's own exceptional correction, not a newly declared entitlement of Thompson to en banc consideration.

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain reversal, with stronger chronology support.** The January notification and actual March request and rejection show that the asserted difficulty was available for institutional attention before the mandate. Issuance of the mandate after that sequence materially distinguishes the case from correcting a mandate mistakenly issued without an opportunity to address a known internal failure. The June initiative is important and must be acknowledged; it defeats any simple characterization of the recall as caused solely by the prisoner's July motion. But an internal proposal shortly after issuance is not completed recall and did not suspend the mandate. Delaying the actual action until August while the State proceeded increases the finality concern.

These four regard the August action's operative restoration of rape-related relief and renewed murder review as merits reopening after a completed first application, despite its institutional initiative. They do not need to find deliberate statutory evasion, dishonest reasons or that every judge affirmatively rejected review. The reported sequence and remedial scope suffice to reject the proposed independent process-repair justification. Their original expressly new rule for this category of merits reopening therefore remains: an appropriate miscarriage-of-justice showing is required, with Schlup governing offense innocence and Sawyer governing an eligibility-only challenge. The original whole-record conclusions that neither showing is met remain unchanged. They preserve genuinely different correction grounds rather than claiming innocence is required for every possible recall.

## Conditional remedy now verified

For the four supporting affirmance, the preserved en banc disposition directs the District Court to enter the partial rape-related writ unless California elects to retry Thompson within a reasonable time. That condition must accompany descriptions of the relief concerning the rape conviction, rape special circumstance and resulting death-sentence vacation. The separate first-degree-murder further-review direction and vacation of the then-entered execution order remain as supplied. The affirmation does not turn conditional relief into immediate unconditional release, murder acquittal, a complete grant of every claim, or a newly selected retrial clock. No fixed date for election, commencement or completion, elapsed reasonable-time finding or compliance determination is supplied.

For the four supporting reversal, reinstatement of the June 11 mandate removes the August recall's dependent conditional rape-related writ direction, death-sentence vacation, murder-review remand and execution-order vacatur. It creates no fresh execution date or affirmative authorization to execute, does not make an expired date self-executing and does not adjudicate a distinct independently available application or protection. The original mandate rather than a newly invented conditional-writ term determines the restored first-application posture.

Their general insistence on actual statutory and remedial predicates is consistent with the existing B commitments but supplies no additional holding or vote in those matters.
