## Validated record and correction of issue framing

Valot, Hansen and Roosa worked as substitute bus drivers for at least three years under successive nine-month contracts, without summer employment or an assurance of renewal. The Board ordinarily voted against automatic renewal, then considered the superintendent's recommendations for the next school year. In June 1993, the superintendent said he expected to recommend them but could not promise the Board's decision. They applied for and received unemployment benefits. He recommended all three for the next year, but on August 16 the Board hired the other recommended drivers and declined these three.

The Board **admitted** that their benefit applications and receipts caused the refusal; this is stronger and more precise than an unspecified inference of motive. Because the district reimbursed benefit payments dollar for dollar, it asserted fiscal protection and discouragement of perceived system abuse. Plaintiffs offered repayment after the refusal; the Board declined. The Board later changed its practices to give reasonable assurance of employment, invoking the between-terms statutory ineligibility rule. That later change cannot retroactively establish that these plaintiffs had assurance or were ineligible in summer 1993. The district court's suggestion of ineligibility is a stated lower rationale, not a verified finding defeating the lawful applications.

The complaint contained petition/redress, administrative-access, equal-protection and due-process federal counts, plus an Ohio public-policy count. Plaintiffs and amici treated retaliation for seeking benefits as an access/petition injury. They had actually obtained benefits; successful access does not itself answer retaliation. **Plaintiffs expressly limited due process to substantive due process, both by stipulation and appellate oral concession.** There is no preserved procedural-due-process claim to revive. Lack of an entitlement to renewed employment is relevant to the context but does not automatically answer every retaliation theory. The packet's property-interest formulation cannot create a new contractual renewal claim or restore the disclaimed procedural theory.

The district court granted defendants summary judgment, denied plaintiffs' partial liability motion, and declined the Ohio claim under §1367(c)(3). The Sixth Circuit affirmed. Engel treated petition claims like employee speech, applied a public-concern threshold, and alternatively found the employer's fiscal balance sufficient; he rejected substantive due process and equality under rational review. Ryan concurred in the result on a different predicate-right analysis, treating an application for benefits as outside protected petitioning and rejecting conversion of state benefits into a fundamental right. Merritt agreed with other components but dissented on petitioning, following San Filippo's protection for good-faith, arguable invocation of an established grievance mechanism. The petition analysis therefore must not be described as one shared rationale of all three judges. Their respective reasons are legal positions under review, not Supreme Court law.

Individual defendants asserted qualified immunity; the panel expressly **did not reach it** because it affirmed on merits. Ryan's passing Monell observation was not an adjudicated alternative ground; he expressly set it aside. Board attribution, each individual's participation, capacities, remedies and notice remain distinct. No party concession of every person's liability or of a policymaker predicate is invented.

## Entering law and material standards

Perry v. Sindermann and Mt. Healthy distinguish lack of a benefit entitlement from retaliation for independently protected conduct; the antecedent protection must actually be established. Pickering, Connick and Rankin supply the existing public-employment speech framework, with content/form/context, the appropriate public-concern inquiry and concrete service interests. California Motor Transport and Smith v. Arkansas State Highway Employees are pre-divergence petition/access sources identified below, but their contexts do not themselves settle whether every private benefits application receives distinct Petition Clause protection. San Filippo (3d Cir. 1994) is a date-eligible lower-court alternative for current merits, not controlling Supreme Court authority or 1993 notice.

**Umbehr and O'Hare (June 28, 1996):** Seven-Justice holdings protect qualifying existing contractor speech/affiliation and preserve the distinction between benefit entitlement and constitutional conditions. The claimant proves protected conduct and a substantial or motivating causal role; government may prove an actual same decision by a preponderance. A conceivable lawful reason is insufficient. Their contractor/affiliation holdings do not conclusively answer the distinct employee-benefit-petition question. **Crawford-El (June 16, 1997), unanimous**, forbids heightened motive proof and derivative categorical discovery barriers while preserving ordinary evidence, actual elements, independent defenses and notice. The Board's admitted purpose does not alone establish that the conduct was constitutionally protected.

**Jeffries (November 17, 1997):** Souter's Parts I and III have Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Part II adds Stone-Zsela. It preserves actual employee-speech balancing, individual causal attribution, a proven counterfactual and conduct-date immunity. Absence of tenure entitlement and a predicted cost/disruption do not automatically decide protection. Its public-concern address does not establish a public-concern finding for these personal applications or a general private-petition rule. No compulsory trial remedy is imported.

**Collins (February 26, 1992), unanimous**, rejects an extra freestanding governmental-power-abuse element in §1983 but requires the federal wrong and entity attribution separately. Its voluntary-workplace safety holding supplies no general constitutional benefit entitlement and does not excuse an independently proved right violation. **Albright (January 24, 1994)** has a seven-vote judgment with no controlling rationale; Ryan's reference to its plurality cannot be elevated into a new general specific-source or state-remedy bar. **Sandin's** prison-liberty holding does not supply an employment renewal entitlement or revive disclaimed procedure; the original packet's inclusion does no operative work on the preserved issues.

**Beach (June 1, 1993)** permits conceivable legitimate economic grounds without formal findings, preserving Moreno/Cleburne limits. **Heller (June 24, 1993)** requires a rational relation to the actual disadvantage and separately preserves justified family-participation distinctions. **Romer (May 20, 1996)** applies ordinary review to the actual class disability without a new tier, mandatory least-restrictive-means test or necessary finding about every actor's subjective motive. Fiscal stewardship is a potentially legitimate interest; whether this precise retaliatory disadvantage bears the required relationship and whether protected petitioning changes the framework remain legal questions. No automatic strict scrutiny follows solely from asserting a right, and no animus finding is manufactured.

**Chicago (December 15, 1997)** now expressly distinguishes supplemental power from §1367(c) discretion. Subsection (c)(3) permits declining related state claims after all original claims are dismissed; it does not remove power or compel dismissal/retention. A change to the federal disposition may require reconsideration of that rationale without awarding the Ohio tort claim. No state-law merits adjudication occurred. **Joiner (December 15)** concerns scientific evidence and abuse-of-discretion review, not a new permission to choose disputed merits facts at summary judgment; no expert-admissibility question is shown here.

Standards copied with full text and component authority: **Political retaliation in existing government service relationships** (Umbehr/O'Hare, June 28, 1996); **Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity** (Crawford-El, June 16, 1997); **Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review** (Elder, February 23, 1994); **Voluntary public employment and substantive workplace safety duties** (Collins); **Rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications** (Beach, June 1, 1993); **Equal protection of commitment proof and family participation** (Heller's distinct components). These titles do not create an additional element outside their actual scopes.

## Supported paths, burdens and remedy limits

A petition-protection path may distinguish filing a lawful, arguable benefits claim through an established governmental mechanism from ordinary employee expression, reject punishment solely for that invocation, and explain why the absence of renewal entitlement does not answer the claim. It must identify the protected petition interest without constitutionalizing every benefit program, treating entitlement as perpetual, or making every request immune from valid anti-fraud and service rules. If it applies a balancing framework, it must confront both the actual fiscal cost and whether suppressing a lawful claim can itself justify retaliation. Motive, constitutional protection, individual responsibility and conduct-date immunity remain different questions.

The competing path may apply public-concern employment doctrine to these private financial applications, or determine that the specific application is outside the petition right claimed, or sustain the action under a lawful service balance. Each requires its own legal explanation; lawful benefits cannot be relabeled fraud without proof. An independently sufficient rational-basis disposition of substantive due process/equality does not silently adjudicate the petition claim. Conversely, a protected-petition ruling does not establish every distinct constitutional theory.

A narrow reversal/remand can preserve rejected/disclaimed claims and require ordinary resolution of the surviving federal theory, attribution, immunity and actual relief, with reconsideration of the state claim's retention rationale. An affirmance can leave the state claim outside federal adjudication without deciding its merits. Immediate plaintiffs' liability judgment, reinstatement, backpay, punitive damages, a new hearing, compulsory trial or immunity for the Board is not established by this packet. Individual-damages immunity may remain even if a current merits rule favors protection; no right may be declared clearly established merely by citing post-August 1993 law.
