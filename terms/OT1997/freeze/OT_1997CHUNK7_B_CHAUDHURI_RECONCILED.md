## Rules, competing grounds, and separate-writing function

**Official prayer — five Associates:** A public university may not select religious leaders and incorporate actual invocations or benedictions as its own program. Mature listeners, voluntary attendance, nonsectarian wording, solemnizing purpose, and permission to abstain do not cure that official worship. Tanford supplies this precise rule; Agostini supplies the current single inquiry; Lee and Ingebretsen supply the preserved official/private boundary. The established invitation, content guidance, and devotional prayer meet that rule without inventing a complete university-authored script. The strongest contrary position is the permissible adult ceremonial relationship recognized by the three Tanford dissenters. The five reject that exception for the same official devotional function Tanford resolved. No separate finding of practical pressure is necessary.

**Silence — eight Associates:** A university may designate this period of silence to solemnize its functions when it prescribes no prayer, devotion, religious profession, or content of private thought. Sherman distinguishes civic acts from worship; O'Connor's Wallace distinction explains why silence can leave conscience to each participant. The 1988 counsel letter and replacement of prayer are relevant evidence, but the implemented silence, the president's stated civic purpose, and absence of established official direction of the audience recitations do not establish governmental worship. This is a conclusion about the actual operation, not a categorical safe harbor for every practice called silence. Genuine private expression need not be suppressed merely because it is religious; the supplied question does not reopen an independent private-speech claim.

**Pressure and reach:** The record does not support penalized religious nonparticipation. Chaudhuri's university-service assertion does not overcome the uncontradicted nonmonitoring/no-penalty account and dean's affidavit. This does not make formal discipline necessary for every coercion claim or rule out employment pressure on a different record. Title VII is outside review; no new free-exercise, private-expression, or unrelated judgment is adjudicated beyond the supplied questions.
