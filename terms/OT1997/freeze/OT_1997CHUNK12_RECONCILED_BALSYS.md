## United States v. Balsys

### Independent legal foundation and scope

All eight Associates reach the foreign-prosecution privilege question in the subpoena-enforcement proceeding. Balsys is a resident alien questioned in the United States. His status does not exclude him from the Fifth Amendment's protection. Deportation is civil, but a civil or administrative inquiry may seek answers incriminating in a criminal case; its label therefore does not independently decide the privilege. No domestic criminal exposure is asserted. The real and substantial risk of prosecution in Lithuania and Israel is established below and uncontested; Germany is not silently added. The Government's limitations representation is not a new judicial calculation of every domestic limitations period.

**Balsys A — Sovereigns bound by the privilege.** The privilege permits resistance to compelled testimony threatening incrimination in a criminal prosecution governed by the constitutional protection. The ordinary threat of prosecution by an independent foreign sovereign supplies no such privilege where no domestic criminal exposure is asserted. Murphy's state/federal holding prevents whipsawing among sovereigns bound to respect the same constitutional rule; Kastigar demonstrates how use and derivative-use protection keeps the witness and prosecuting authorities in the position silence would have preserved. Those decisions do not establish that a foreign sovereign must honor American immunity or that domestic courts can impose it. Zicarelli reserved rather than decided the real-risk foreign question. Verdugo-Urquidez's trial-right distinction assists the analysis without denying a domestic resident's constitutional status or treating a different Fourth Amendment holding as controlling this case by itself.

**Balsys B — Domestic compulsion against foreign penal exposure.** The contrary construction locates the protected act of resistance in domestic compulsion of testimonial self-incrimination. The constitutional text does not expressly confine a criminal case to a domestic one. Murphy's history, rejection of the old separate-sovereign approach, and account of the privilege's personal protections support applying it when a domestic witness faces a proved real and substantial foreign criminal danger. Kastigar's coextensiveness principle then prevents an ineffective domestic immunity promise from replacing the right to remain silent. The conclusion regulates the American command, not the foreign court. Zicarelli's reservation leaves this issue open; Verdugo-Urquidez concerns a foreign search of a nonresident's premises, unlike domestic compulsion of a resident.

**Balsys C — Cooperation limit.** OSI's liaison mission, the Lithuania memorandum and earlier sharing with Israel do not establish foreign direction of this subpoena, a joint prosecution, or use of domestic process as a proved foreign proxy. The A coalition leaves a materially different case of such concerted use open rather than creating and applying an undefined cooperation exception here. The B coalition does not need to prove such a relationship because ordinary real and substantial foreign exposure suffices on its view. No existing Israeli disclosure treaty, foreign immunity, secrecy assurance, or factual finding of coordinated prosecution is invented.
