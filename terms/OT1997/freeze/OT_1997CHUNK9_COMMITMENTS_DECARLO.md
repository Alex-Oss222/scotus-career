## Judgment components and common legal conclusions

All eight Associates would **affirm the court of appeals' disposition insofar as reviewed**. This means affirming the qualified-immunity summary judgments for Astle and Blank and preserving the separate municipal-liability and personal-claim remands concerning DuRose. It does not reinstate the district court's official-capacity damages judgment. Review lies under §1254(1) following the completed appellate trial/cross-appeal disposition; the case is not an interlocutory appeal from an immunity denial.

**1. Astle and Blank.** The question is the apparent federal unlawfulness of these defendants' own acts during the 1988–1989 application process, not whether the county complied with New York deadlines or whether indefinite administrative inaction is desirable. Harlow and Anderson preserve objective protection where the particular federal obligation was not sufficiently clear. Davis v. Scherer separates violation of state administrative requirements from clearly established federal rights. Elder requires all relevant conduct-date law, while Lanier rejects a factual-twin requirement without substituting abstract process principles for an uncertain application.

The prior judicially sustained revocation and later administrative expungement did not themselves adjudicate an entitlement to reinstatement. Astle confronted that unresolved relationship and sought Blank's advice; Blank escalated the question through Turner to the state regional official. The record places Klump's direction to make a decision with Turner; it does not establish that either Astle or Blank personally received and then defied that direction or had final authority to resolve the conflict. Their own uncertainty and actual consultations, combined with the absence of a sufficiently clear conduct-time federal rule resolving this application against their particular roles, support immunity. That is the sufficient ground, not a universal safe harbor for asking a supervisor.

New York required investigation within 75 days after a valid application and notice within 30 days after completion, with a fair hearing following denial. Those duties inform the setting but neither determine an entitlement to certification nor establish every defendant's constitutional culpability. The Court need not decide the ultimate federal process claim to resolve these two individual damages defenses. No later authority retroactively establishes notice; no categorical rule requires an identical licensing case or bars obviously covered procedural violations.

**2. DuRose in his official capacity.** A denial of a defendant's summary-judgment motion does not establish the plaintiff's entitlement to judgment as a matter of law. The trial court therefore could not substitute its mistaken description of the earlier order for an assessment of the trial evidence under Rule 50. An official-capacity action here is the county/entity action described by Kentucky v. Graham. Monell and Pembaur require an attributable policy or custom causing an actual federal deprivation; employment, job title and operational discretion alone do not suffice, and personal qualified immunity does not defeat a municipal claim.

Collins preserves the distinction between the underlying federal right and attribution. McMillian's function-specific final-authority inquiry controls the method, but its Alabama sheriff holding does not identify Oneida's policymaker. The familiar trial court must evaluate the actual evidence under those rules. It may find a legally supported judgment for either side or factual questions requiring a jury; the Court selects none of those alternatives. The packet lacks the complete trial record needed for a new dispositive county-policy, causation or liability finding. The $300,000 judgment and dependent fee award cannot stand on the mistaken prior-ruling premise; neither amount is independently adjudicated here.

**3. DuRose's post-expungement personal claim.** A ruling on the original pre-expungement pleading cannot silently dispose of a distinct claim added later. The informal letter does not supply the missing formal, reasoned disposition. The court must identify the actual procedural posture and decide the added claim under its applicable standard. Rule 12 treatment does not permit rejection merely because the pleaded individual theory has not yet been proved; summary judgment requires its actual record. This is a clarification and lawful-adjudication remand, not a finding that DuRose knew the consultation chain, controlled every subordinate act or is personally liable.

**4. Preserved scope.** The lower court's opportunity to amend the pre-expungement claims, including DuRose's, remains undisturbed at its actual pleading stage and carries no finding of merit. Turner's favorable personal-capacity jury verdict and the unappealed Perales declaration are outside this review; no component is revived merely because the municipal remand survives. The pending Article 78 adequate-process contention remains for appropriate consideration below and is not resolved universally or against either party here.

## Enhanced alternatives and individual commitments

On Astle/Blank, the competing remand path argues that consultation cannot justify prolonged inaction which itself prevents the hearing available only after denial. That is the strongest objection and a real possible federal claim. It does not overcome their particular damages immunity on this record: the relevant federal entitlement and each subordinate's responsibility must be clear, the earlier revocation was not automatically undone by expungement, and Turner's later instruction cannot be attributed to them without evidence. The selected path neither endorses the county's inaction nor establishes immunity for a person who knowingly controls and refuses a plainly required decision.

On the county claim, the competing outright-dismissal path invokes weak attribution and possible adequate state process. Both are preserved issues; neither permits a new Supreme Court finding from an incomplete trial record. Conversely, a claim of obvious missed deadlines cannot restore the damages judgment without the separate federal-right and Monell findings. The bounded remand is therefore the supported disposition, not a promise of plaintiffs' success or a compulsory new trial.
