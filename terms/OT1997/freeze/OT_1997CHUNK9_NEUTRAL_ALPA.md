## Supported record and lower judgment

ALPA represents Delta pilots under the Railway Labor Act. Its 1991 agreement with Delta established an agency shop effective January 1992. Nonmembers were required to contribute to collective representation; their objection is to the chargeable proportion, not a claimed agreement to arbitrate. ALPA initially discounted fees about 8% using 1990 expenses, then about 17% using 1991 expenses, and finally determined that 19% of 1992 expenses were nongermane. It credited or rebated objectors with interest. The 153 plaintiffs disputed ALPA's calculations and procedures.

ALPA treated protests as requests for arbitration under its own Policies and Procedures, over the litigating pilots' objection. AAA appointed an arbitrator from a labor-relations panel. Some pilots had already sued before the agency shop became effective. The district court refused to enjoin arbitration; the arbitrator refused to postpone it, and counsel entered a conditional appearance. The lower opinion reports 158 arbitration participants, of whom 91 became parties to this suit; 67 participants did not challenge the award. Another 62 plaintiffs did not participate. Thus the litigants before the court were nonparticipants or participants under protest. The sanitized objective extract reports 174 timely objectors. Those figures concern different groups—objectors and participants—and are not treated as a factual conflict or silently made identical.

The arbitrator largely sustained ALPA's allocations and declined requested discovery. In court ALPA resisted a request for documents concerning 20 sample projects, offering its entire 1992 expense records instead. The district court granted judgment largely for ALPA and ruled that arbitration had to be exhausted, relying on the arbitrator's findings on recordkeeping and overhead.

The D.C. Circuit reversed the exhaustion requirement. It distinguished an impartial arbitrator from the final internal authority of a governmental agency or benefit plan; it found no assent or statutory basis to defer to the arbitrator. It rejected ALPA's attempt to distinguish the circuit's NLRA decision in *Abrams* from this RLA case. Its separate rulings included: political government-relations expenditures were to be separated from safety-related collective bargaining; the 1990/1991 notice challenge lacked injury on the reported facts; auditors need not decide legal germaneness; professional audit adequacy remained contestable; and recordkeeping and overhead required independent findings. It left discovery management below. These are lower-court rulings, not Supreme Court holdings or a reason to expand the authorized question.

## Entering law and competing paths

**Surviving received authority.** *Hudson*, 475 U.S. 292 (1986), requires an adequate fee explanation, a reasonably prompt opportunity to challenge the fee before an impartial decisionmaker, and escrow for reasonably disputed amounts. It does not identify arbitration as the exclusive impartial forum. Its majority expressly denies preclusive effect to the arbitrator's decision in a subsequent §1983 action. White's concurrence, joined by Burger, favors exhaustion when a union supplies compliant arbitration. That is a separate writing, not the majority's rule. *Hanson*, *Street*, *Allen*, *Abood*, *Ellis*, *Beck*, and pre-divergence *Lehnert* supply the received union-security, chargeability, and objector-protection background at their actual scope. The parties' agreement that Hudson safeguards apply to this RLA arrangement is reported below; it is not an independent new holding about every private union or the NLRA.

*AT&T Technologies*, 475 U.S. 643 (1986), and *Gateway Coal*, 414 U.S. 368 (1974), supply the agreement basis of contractual arbitration. The frozen Standards module **Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability**, effective May 22, 1995, requires judicial determination of assent absent clear and unmistakable delegation under ordinary contract law. Appearing solely to object does not itself establish assent. Breyer's antecedent-assent rule has all nine joins; the eight Associates also join its conditional actual-delegation review proposition. Its arbitration-review rule does not itself answer whether a distinct exhaustion obligation can arise by law.

**Judicial exhaustion.** Simulated *McCarthy v. Madigan*, March 4, 1992, preserves statutory primacy and sound judicial discretion consistent with the statutory scheme when no command applies. Its actual agency-authority, expertise, correction, fact-development, efficiency, available-relief and access considerations matter; it does not create an automatic no-exhaustion rule outside its damages-only BOP setting. Its governing framework has Blackmun, Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; Scalia and Thomas supported the particular judgment only on the unavailable-compensation ground. The Standards module **Judicial exhaustion and damages-only federal prison medical-care claims** retains that framework outside a controlling statutory command; the PLRA amendment concerns confined-prisoner litigation, not these pilots.

**ALPA's supported path.** Hudson compels provision of an impartial expert procedure; a reasonable exhaustion requirement could allow that procedure to narrow facts, correct errors and reduce duplicative litigation while preserving later judicial review. White's concurrence and the reported *Lancaster* circuit position support the argument. Its strongest obstacle is source and fit: a compelled protective opportunity is not necessarily a duty imposed on nonconsenting claimants, and this neutral arbitrator is neither the union's senior policymaking official nor a statutory administrative tribunal. Efficiency alone does not identify the scope of lawful compulsion or deference. An exhaustion path must specify whether judicial review remains independent and what lawful source imposes the prior step; it cannot assume a binding award or consent from protest.

**Pilots' supported path.** They never assented, the reported statutory framework establishes no compulsory-arbitration gate for this fee dispute, and Hudson's protection gives a prompt option without surrender of the judicial forum. The strongest counterargument is that assent principles address contractual binding arbitration, while courts sometimes impose nonbinding exhaustion as judicial administration. The model must address that distinction through McCarthy and the actual procedure, not dismiss it by relabeling exhaustion as contractual waiver. The limited question does not require deciding every exhaustion doctrine, consenting party, future fee amount, or fee-calculation claim.

**Remedy.** Affirmance on exhaustion would preserve independent judicial treatment and the lower court's remaining bounded directions; it would establish no particular fee refund, discovery plan, or substantive chargeability result. Reversal must identify the exact consequence for already completed arbitration and those who did not participate, without inventing forfeiture, assent, statutory deference or a new award. A narrower correction/remand is available if an impermissible deference rule is distinct from a lawful preliminary process. No path automatically binds absent objectors or creates a class action.
