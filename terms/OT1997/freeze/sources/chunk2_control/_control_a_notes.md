## Hudson, 522 U.S. 93 (1997), December 10; No. 96-976

Judgment: affirmed, 9–0 on result. Court opinion Rehnquist joined O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, pp.95–105. It disavows Halper's method, restores legislative-intent / punitive-purpose-or-effect inquiry under Ward and Kennedy with clearest-proof threshold and facial statutory assessment, and treats the OCC sanctions as civil. It does not decide Blockburger or an Excessive Fines claim. Question reproduced in n.5 is punishment classification, not a separate grant of a same-offense issue.

All separate writings: Scalia, joined Thomas, concurrence p.106, fully joins but explains successive-prosecution rather than freestanding multiple-punishment understanding. Stevens, alone, judgment concurrence pp.106–112, would resolve on distinct elements under Blockburger; rejects gratuitous revision of Halper and warns against eliminating protections against civil-labeled punishment. Souter, alone, judgment concurrence pp.112–114, accepts much of Kennedy/Ward with context-sensitive clearest-proof caveats and resolves through Blockburger as part of that scheme; no formal join in Stevens's opinion. Breyer, joined Ginsburg, judgment concurrence pp.115–117, accepts Kennedy/Ward and finds neither facial nor actual sanctions criminal but rejects misleading clearest-proof language and reserves as-applied assessment rather than limiting review to the statutory face. No dissent or nonparticipation.

## Bay Area Laundry, 522 U.S. 192 (1997), December 15; No. 96-370

Judgment reversed/remanded; Ginsburg unanimous opinion pp.195–210; no separate writing or nonparticipation. Six-year accrual follows scheduled nonpayment after assessment/demand; each installment has a separate period absent exercised acceleration. Permissive acceleration is not automatic; n.5 says accelerated debt runs from exercise, and does not decide whether this complaint itself accelerated. Petition representations prevent revival of the first $345.50 installment despite contrary merits/argument timing claims. Remaining timely installments may proceed. Source scope does not equate missed installment with actual acceleration, nor abolish as-soon-as-practicable duty or laches objection in arbitration.

## General Electric v. Joiner, 522 U.S. 136 (1997), December 15; No.96-188

Judgment reversed/remanded, 8–1. Rehnquist Court opinion, Parts I–II unanimous (pp.139–143); Part III joined O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer (pp.143–147). Abuse of discretion governs admission/exclusion alike; outcome-dispositive exclusion does not alter that standard. Part III upholds the PCB-based exclusion but leaves the unchallenged furan/dioxin exposure ruling and resulting admissibility question open. It does not reinstate defendants' entire summary judgment or hold admission necessarily impermissible.

Breyer alone concurs pp.147–150, fully joining and emphasizing available court assistance, hearings, Rule16, Rule706 experts and other tools for scientific gatekeeping. Stevens alone concurs in Parts I–II and dissents in Part III and the judgment, pp.150–155; would remand to the court of appeals to apply the proper standard. Concern: inadequate briefing/record for Supreme Court application, weight-of-evidence methodology, and improper focus on each study individually rather than reliable collective reasoning. His final paragraph explicitly disclaims concurrence in the judgment. Runtime lineup accurate; do not describe nine votes for judgment or make his position a general rejection of abuse-of-discretion review. No nonparticipation.

## Chicago/ICS, 522 U.S.156 (1997), December 15; No.96-910

Judgment reversed/remanded, 7–2. O'Connor Court opinion pp.159–174, joined Rehnquist, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer. Federal constitutional claims support original federal-question jurisdiction and §1367 includes same-case state administrative-review claims despite deferential record review. Original diversity jurisdiction over an administrative-review action brought alone is expressly not decided. Discretion under §1367(c) and valid abstention remain; their application here is left to the Seventh Circuit. No ruling on constitutional validity of the ordinances or entitlement to demolition permits.

Ginsburg, joined Stevens, sole dissent pp.175–191. Would affirm jurisdictional remand because cross-system appellate review requires clearer congressional authorization; distinguishes direct federal rights actions from appellate review on a state/local record, relies on history, statutory structure and federalism, warns about implied diversity expansion and insufficient retention/abstention guidance. Its footnotes recognize §1367 can apply to removed cases, Pennhurst's state-sovereignty limits, distinction of IDEA's separate federal statutory regime, and domestic-relations analogy. No other writing or nonparticipation.
