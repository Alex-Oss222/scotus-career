## Fidelity Financial Services, Inc. v. Fink — 522 U.S. 211–221

**Identity:** No.96-1370; argued November 3,1997; decided January 13,1998; Eighth Circuit 102 F.3d334 **affirmed**.

**Writing and lineup:** Souter delivered the opinion for a unanimous Court: Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer all joined. One Court opinion, no separate writing, no noted nonparticipation. Judgment 9–0.

**Scope of actual holding:** A creditor invoking the §547(c)(3) enabling-loan exception must complete the acts that state law requires for perfection within the federal 20-day period after the debtor receives possession. A longer state relation-back period does not change when perfection occurs for this federal exception. Section547(e)(1)(B)'s “when” and “cannot acquire” definition refers to completion of the perfection acts, not a retroactive legal date. The opinion additionally relies on §546(b)'s omission of §547, the function of the 1994 extension from10 to20 days, and the former Bankruptcy Act's express but bounded allowance of state relation back. The Heflin–Sasser colloquy did not enact a new definition and conflicted with statutory text/history.

**Qualifications:** Footnote1 leaves open whether Fidelity's mailing itself sufficed under Missouri law. Required acts can include acts of governmental employees as well as creditors; do not recast the rule as merely “creditor mails within20 days.” Footnote3 identifies an additional consequence of Fidelity's interpretation: if perfection related back for §547(e), transfer timing under §547(e)(2)(A) could place the transaction outside the90-day preference period. The Court does not independently decide all §547(b) elements. Footnote4 treats the ambiguous Senate colloquy in the light favorable to Fidelity for argument's sake.

**Runtime comparator validation:** Identity, disposition, unanimous Souter authorship, and supplied holding are confirmed. No lineup correction. Supplement the main summary with the state-law-mailing reservation and complete-acts qualification above; avoid treating a quoted syllabus as the entire opinion.

## Baker v. General Motors Corp. — 522 U.S. 222–251

**Identity:** No.96-653; argued October15,1997; decided January13,1998; Eighth Circuit86 F.3d811 **reversed and remanded**. Judgment unanimous9–0; rationale divided5/1/3, not a9-Justice Court opinion.

| Writing | Author and joins | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Court opinion, pp.225–241 | Ginsburg; joined in full by Rehnquist, Stevens, Souter, Breyer | Five-Justice controlling opinion; no partial joins. |
| Concurring in the judgment, pp.241–242 | Scalia alone | Judgment only; enforcement-versus-recognition ground. |
| Concurring in the judgment, pp.243–251 | Kennedy; joined in full by O'Connor and Thomas | Judgment only; Michigan preclusion-law ground, objections to breadth of majority analysis. |

**Court opinion:** Recognition/preclusive force of a valid judgment is exacting; no general public-policy exception permits disregarding sister-state judgments, and equity decrees are not excluded categorically. Enforcement mechanisms remain governed by evenhanded forum law. The Michigan decree settles the Elwell–GM claims but cannot govern admissibility of evidence in the Bakers' separate Missouri case, where Michigan had no authority over the parties/claims. The decree could restrain Elwell from volunteering testimony but could not shield him from the other jurisdiction's subpoena in this case. The separately negotiated promise not to sue over court-ordered testimony reinforces that analysis. This is not a holding that all injunctions lack interstate recognition or that forum disagreement with another State's policy suffices.

**Material footnote limits:** Footnote9 expressly acknowledges that the Court had not yet ruled on the full-faith-and-credit due a state antisuit injunction barring a party from litigation in another State; it surveys practice rather than deciding every such decree. Footnote10 records a party assertion, undisputed by GM, that no particular trial question/answer drew attorney-client, work-product, or trade-secret objection. Footnote11 explains why GM's argument that only Elwell was bound meant simple nonparty issue-preclusion rules alone did not answer the argument as the majority understood it. Footnote12 expressly preserves issue preclusion if the Bakers themselves had been parties in Michigan and actually litigated the privilege question; do not convert the Court's holding into a general “Missouri judicial-process integrity” exception.

**Scalia concurrence:** Sister-state judgments furnish conclusive evidence of matters adjudged but do not execute themselves extraterritorially. GM sought Missouri execution of Michigan's injunction; full faith and credit did not require Missouri to prohibit testimony any more than to hold Elwell in contempt. A judgment can be a basis for an action or defense elsewhere and may be domesticated for enforcement under forum law; the Constitution/statute itself does not supply that execution. This narrow ground suffices, without joining the majority's wider discussion.

**Kennedy concurrence (O'Connor,Thomas):** Agrees no public-policy exception allows disagreement with the underlying law. Objects to the majority's apparent new exceptions for acts within another State's exclusive province and interference with other litigation; cross-border land decrees and antisuit injunctions require caution and are unnecessary to decide. Begin with the judgment's effect under rendering-state law. Michigan mutuality/full-opportunity rules would not bind the Bakers, who were strangers to the proceeding. Michigan Court Rule2.613(B), routing modification to the issuing judge, is a procedural comity rule, not a rule of preclusion binding Missouri. GM bore and failed to carry the state-law preclusion burden. Looking to practical effect, GM sought to preclude the Bakers' litigation of admissibility; even on GM's claimed Elwell-only theory, Elwell was a witness and was not relitigating a claim. This state-law ground resolves the case without a broad equity-decree exception.

The supplied separate-opening excerpts are incomplete; the Kennedy excerpt ends before the operative Michigan-law rationale. ## Rogers v. United States — 522 U.S. 252–261

**Identity:** No.96-1279; argued November5,1997; decided January14,1998. **Certiorari dismissed as improvidently granted**,6–3. The Eleventh Circuit judgment is left standing; this is not a Supreme Court merits affirmance or approval of omitted-element harmless-error doctrine.

| Writing | Author and joins | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Opinion announcing decision, pp.253–259 | Stevens; joined by Thomas,Ginsburg,Breyer | Four-Justice plurality supporting DIG; no majority merits holding. |
| Concurring in the result, p.259 | O'Connor; joined by Scalia | Two additional votes for DIG based on ambiguous presentation, not agreement that the instruction was adequate. |
| Dissent, pp.260–261 | Kennedy; joined by Rehnquist and Souter | Three votes against DIG; no resolution of omitted-element harmless-error merits in this dissent. |

**Stevens plurality:** The granted omitted-element/admission question was not fairly presented. It viewed the requested instruction as potentially demanding knowledge of statutory legal classification and the charge as actually requiring knowledge that the device was a silencer. It read the registration-language passage as disclaiming need for knowledge of registration law under Freed. Footnote5 interprets the charging colloquy; that disputed interpretation must not become neutral objective fact. Footnote7 limits the plurality's reading to knowledge of a device having all silencer characteristics; knowing possession of a bag or a generic dangerous item would not satisfy Staples, and trial courts should use clearer language.

**O'Connor/Scalia concurrence:** Ambiguity about whether the instructions required knowledge of silencer characteristics sufficed to make presentation of the intended constitutional question unclear. Concurred in dismissal without joining the plurality's affirmative construction that the charge was adequate. Shared its request for clearer instructions. The exact label is **concurring in the result**.

**Kennedy/Rehnquist/Souter dissent:** Read the trial court's charge and colloquy as requiring knowledge of possession but not knowledge of characteristics, consistent with pre-Staples circuit law. Criticized the plurality's unbriefed construction as effectively sustaining convictions on a newly generated theory while avoiding the important granted issue. Dissented from dismissal. Do not infer that these three had resolved the underlying harmless-error question in either direction.
