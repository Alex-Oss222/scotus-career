## Validated neutral record and review scope

On August 17, 1994, Diane Beasley bought a 1994 Ford and gave Fidelity a purchase-price note secured by the car. Fidelity mailed the Missouri perfection application on September 7—day 21. Beasley sought Chapter 7 relief about two months later and the case was converted to Chapter 13. Trustee Fink sought to avoid the lien as a preference. The bankruptcy court avoided it; the district court affirmed; the Eighth Circuit affirmed on the view that federal perfection occurs when the creditor completes the state-required acts.

The reviewed issue is whether the enabling-loan exception can be satisfied through state relation back where the creditor acts after the federal 20-day period but within Missouri's 30-day period. The controlling record supplies mailing, not an independently verified delivery date. The sufficiency of mailing under state law was not raised; do not convert September 7 into an invented delivery or make an unraised state-law mailing question the ground of judgment. No evidence establishes a step earlier than day 21.

## Entering law, statutory operations and burden boundaries

The applicable §547(c)(3)(B) period is **20 days after the debtor receives possession**, not a later amended period. The enabling-loan exception also requires a security interest securing new value given at or after signing a security agreement describing the property, given by or for the secured party under that agreement, given to enable acquisition, and in fact so used; this appeal supplies no separate adjudication of disputed predicates outside the timeliness issue. Section 547(e)(1)(B) concerns perfection against a simple-contract creditor's judicial lien on property other than real property. Missouri §301.600(2) defines the documents/delivery needed for a motor-vehicle lien and its relation back to creation upon delivery within 30 days. State creation and priority, the federal definition and exception, and the separate §547(b) preference elements must not be collapsed.

The source recites §546(b)(1)(A)'s enumerated trustee powers—§§544, 545 and 549. Its omission of §547 is text; the legal consequence is an interpretive argument, not a freestanding finding. Trustee proof of the preference elements remains separate from proof of an applicable exception. Other defenses, loan invalidity and an exact delivery date are not independently at issue on this supplied record.

The complete opening holdings and standards copied in the slice supply these precise boundaries:

- **Barnhill (March 25, 1992):** Federal transfer law operates on state-created property interests; an ordinary check transfers the debtor's account interest upon honor for §547(b). Section 547(e) cannot relate back before that interest passed. Seven joined; §547(c) timing was expressly not decided. This is an analogy concerning distinct statutory functions, not a pre-decided enabling-loan rule.
- **BFP (May 23, 1994):** Souter's five-Justice holding requires an independent federal reasonably-equivalent-value inquiry despite state-compliant real-property foreclosure, in realistically comparable forced-sale conditions. State regularity is evidence rather than conclusive compliance. All other avoidance predicates and recovery protections survive; no universal rule displacing state property law is adopted.
- **Rash (June 16, 1997):** Ginsburg and seven others require adjusted replacement valuation for retained-use Chapter 13 collateral under §§506(a),1325(a)(5)(B). The statutory setting governs; this neither decides preference timing nor establishes a universal bankruptcy valuation rule.

Standards: **Ordinary-check transfer date for preference avoidance** (present operation Barnhill, March 25, 1992); **Reasonably equivalent value in a forced real-estate sale** (BFP, May 23, 1994); **Replacement valuation for retained Chapter 13 collateral** (Rash, June 16, 1997). Full authority, scope and limits are mechanically copied. Butner and McKenzie remain available at their preserved distinction between state property interests and federal bankruptcy consequences; they cannot override the enacted federal exception. No current-term holding identified in the received neutral projection changes these provisions.

## Supported lawful alternatives

The trustee's path treats the federal period as requiring completion of the acts making the lien effective against the specified judicial creditor within 20 days; a state retrospective priority rule would not extend that period. It must explain why the federal statute measures the actual perfection acts, give effect to the property-law reference, and preserve state-law creation/priority for purposes not displaced by the exception.

Fidelity's strongest path reads 'perfected' through the state-law priority consequence: timely completion within Missouri's 30-day window deems the interest perfected upon creation, so the federal 20-day condition is satisfied on its own terms rather than enlarged. It must explain why that reading does not make the specified federal period depend on each State's longer window, and address §547(e)'s judicial-lien definition and §546's distinct enumerated powers. State relation back cannot simply be treated as the conclusion under debate.

Both paths must apply the actual 20-day regime. A construction ruling may affirm the preference judgment on the reviewed issue or reverse/remand for remaining lawful application, but may not invent an exact delivery, deem every enabling loan avoidable, or decide other transactions. Any genuinely necessary unprovided transaction or statutory-effective-date premise must be isolated; the reported source expressly identifies this as a 20-day case, and no contrary source is established.
