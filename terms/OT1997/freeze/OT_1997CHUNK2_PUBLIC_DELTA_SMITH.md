## Event

Smith v. Mattox, review of the Eleventh Circuit's decision in No. 96-6648, 127 F.3d 1416. Decided January 12, 1998, on the legal qualified-immunity issue presented by the denial of summary judgment. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is stated.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated on submission and at decision. No Justice was absent.

## Public Action

The judgment sustaining denial of Mattox's qualified-immunity summary judgment on the presented legal question is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: none. The case returns for ordinary lawful proceedings on the actual facts, remaining elements and defenses. ## Holdings

**1. The separable legal immunity question is reviewable on the proper assumed account.** The Court may decide whether the plaintiff-favorable conduct was clearly established as unlawful without resolving whether that conduct occurred. It may not select between accounts of compliance, continued resistance or gun-reaching, decide credibility or review evidentiary sufficiency. Part I is unanimous.

Johnson and Behrens preserve this legal/factual division, and Clanton confirms that disputed evidence does not eliminate review of a genuine legal defense. Section 1254 does not enlarge the circuit's §1291 authority. No separate factual-sufficiency component requiring dismissal is established here. The Court accepts the proper assumptions solely to decide this appeal.

**2. The particular assumed force was obviously outside the lawful bounds in June 1993.** Under Graham v. Connor, force during arrest must be objectively reasonable in the actual circumstances from the officer's contemporaneous perspective, including the severity of the crime, immediate threat and active resistance or flight. The considerable additional arm force and reported blow against Smith while wholly unresisting on the assumed account were apparently unlawful under that pre-existing rule; an identical earlier handcuffing-injury decision is unnecessary. Part II is unanimous.

The earlier bat threat, initial refusal, flight and feigned renewed flight justified caution and some restraining force. Submission did not establish completed cuffing or make all further force unlawful. But the assumed account describes docility during the maneuver, an arm pull, a complaint of discomfort, and a grunt and blow followed by multiple fractures requiring surgery. Those circumstances together, rather than the fracture alone, exceed the remaining latitude for reasonable error. The competing reliance on a dangerous encounter cannot insert continued resistance, gun-reaching or an unassumed safety mistake into this account.

Harlow and Anderson require particularized notice, not merely invocation of a general right to bodily security. Graham supplied the force rule before June 11, 1993. Elder and Lanier explain the present inquiry into relevant authority and obvious coverage; neither supplies retrospective notice. No exhaustive finding about the absence of all earlier authority is necessary, and the Court adopts no exclusive factually-similar-case route. Subjective anger is neither assumed nor required. Injury alone, bad purpose and hindsight do not determine the result.

**3. Denial on these assumptions leaves the received defense and ordinary proceedings intact.** Harlow and Anderson remain the governing objective qualified-immunity framework. Mattox may establish a materially different supported account, including continuing resistance or necessary force, through ordinary proceedings and may obtain lawful fact-dependent reconsideration of immunity. The complete retained-framework holding in Part III has all eight Associate Justices; all nine support the bounded affirmance and these ordinary-proceedings limits.

Elder requires relevant preconduct law on the preserved issue without importing new claims or evidence. Crawford-El preserves ordinary competent proof and independent defenses but adds neither a motive element nor retaliation's burden sequence to Graham. The Court does not reach unpleaded warrantless-entry or probable-cause theories, convert force damages into unlawful-arrest damages, reopen the city or police chief's judgments, or create a separate Fourteenth Amendment claim. Failure of this legal defense establishes no ultimate constitutional violation, causation, supervisory liability, damages or entitlement to trial.

## Precedent Treatment

Graham v. Connor supplies the objective arrest-force rule and is applied to the complete assumed encounter. Harlow and Anderson retain particularized objective immunity while permitting an obvious application without a factual twin. Elder preserves independent review of relevant conduct-date authority. Lanier supplies the common legal-clarity approach without transferring criminal elements or later notice. Johnson, Behrens and Clanton retain the legal/factual review boundary. Crawford-El preserves ordinary proof and defenses within the actual elements. No precedent is overruled or replacement defense adopted.

## Law After Decision

The existing force and notice rules exclude categorical immunity merely because no earlier case used the identical handcuffing maneuver or injury. The particular complete-nonresistance account here meets the obvious-application standard despite the earlier danger. The holding creates no general prohibition on all force after submission, presumption from a fracture, hindsight test or liability finding. Objective Harlow/Anderson immunity remains governing law.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that earlier menace does not authorize the assumed gratuitous injury after submission and that Mattox cannot obtain reversal by substituting a disputed account of resistance. The controlling application rests on the alleged absence of resistance, controlled position and additional blow after complaint, not the fracture alone. He also favors a contextual defense placing the justificatory burden on the official and rejecting a duplicative excuse for deliberate violations. That proposal is not adopted: the Court retains Harlow and Anderson, and Crawford-El did not replace them. Stone-Zsela preserves actual opportunities, reasonable mistakes, contrary supported facts and all remaining liability and remedial requirements.

## Procedure After Action

The circuit judgment is affirmed, leaving the District Court's immunity denial operative on the presented assumptions. Ordinary proceedings may resolve genuine historical disputes and any remaining elements or defenses. The disposed city/chief claims and unpleaded entry/arrest theories remain outside this decision. No compulsory trial or automatic return to this Court is ordered.
