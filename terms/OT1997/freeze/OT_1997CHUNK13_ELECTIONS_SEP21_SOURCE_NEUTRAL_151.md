## A. Case, record, and posture

- **Case:** *Jordahl v. Democratic Party of Virginia*, 122 F.3d 192 (4th Cir. 1997).
- **Supreme Court petition:** No. 97-859. **Term:** October Term 1997. **Simulation entry:** petition for certiorari, proposed for grant. The simulation's merits date has not been fixed.
- **Question presented, reconstructed from the appellate opinion:** Whether federal district courts can entertain a § 1983 suit asserting that a political party's resort to state-court injunctions unconstitutionally suppressed nonendorsing voter guides, and whether a live pre-enforcement claim survives changes to the state election-disclosure statute.
- **Judgment below:** Fourth Circuit affirmed dismissal of the federal claims for want of jurisdiction or justiciability and the decision not to retain supplemental state claims. It did not adjudicate the asserted First Amendment violation.
- **Record:** The Virginia Leadership Council, Vern Jordahl, and Mary-Beth LaRock distributed or planned nonendorsing voter guides in 1989 and 1993. The Democratic Party of Virginia obtained three state-court orders restricting distribution unless disclosure or registration requirements were met. The orders expired or were dissolved. Virginia repealed the original Fair Elections Practices Act in 1993 and amended its replacement in 1996. In federal court, Count One attacked the claimed system of prior restraint; other § 1983 counts sought relief for injuries attributed to the three injunctions. Additional state-law abuse-of-process, malicious-prosecution, and trespass claims remained.
- **Relief sought:** Damages, declarations, and injunctive relief on federal claims, with related state-law relief.
- **Threshold:** Article III live controversy and ripeness, *Rooker v. Fidelity Trust Co.* / *District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman* limits on collateral federal review of state judgments, private-party state action under 42 U.S.C. § 1983, supplemental jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1367.
- **Procedural anchors:** Fourth Circuit decided July 28, 1997; the challenged state orders had ended by the time of federal review.
- **Participation:** No recusal or other participation fact established in the consulted record.

## B. Law entering the case

The controlling rules are the First and Fourteenth Amendments, Article III, the civil-rights statutes, and the Court's decisions in force when the petition is considered. In the existing Court record, *McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission* (1995) protects an individual's truthful, independently produced political leaflets against categorical identification requirements, and *Johnson v. De Grandy* (1994) preserves the distinction between an independent federal claim and an impermissible appeal of a state judgment.

### Issue 1: Live challenge to election speech restrictions

- **Governing law:** U.S. Const. art. III; *Renne v. Geary*, 501 U.S. 312 (1991), on uncertain future enforcement; *Virginia v. American Booksellers Ass'n*, 484 U.S. 383 (1988), on credible pre-enforcement threats; *McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission* (1995), on core political leaflets and exacting review of compelled identification. The Court's pre-enforcement decision in *Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno* (April 28, 1997) likewise requires a concrete present constraint or realistic enforcement threat, not an already completed controversy.
- **Open point:** Whether the claimed continuing threat under the amended Virginia law was concrete enough to make Count One facial or pre-enforcement litigation justiciable, without equating a former injunction with future enforcement.

### Issue 2: Review of state-court injunctions and damages

- **Governing law:** *Rooker*, 263 U.S. 413 (1923), and *Feldman*, 460 U.S. 462 (1983), reserve appellate review of state judgments to the proper appellate channel. The Court's *Johnson v. De Grandy* (1994) decision permits a genuinely independent federal enforcement claim without implying unrestricted collateral review. *Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co.*, 457 U.S. 922 (1982), governs when joint use of state process becomes attributable state action under § 1983; ordinary private litigation alone does not establish that element. Section 1367 governs supplemental claims.
- **Open point:** Whether allegations of independently wrongful resort to judicial process can be resolved without determining the injunctions legally erroneous, and whether the political party can satisfy the separate state-action element.

## C. Decision-material litigation positions

- **Below:** Count One was moot as applied to repealed or amended statutes and premature as to the new law; the damages counts were effectively efforts to reverse state injunctions through federal district court. The court declined supplemental state-law jurisdiction.
- **Petitioners' strongest case:** Voter guides were core political speech, earlier injunctions interrupted distribution at election time, the ongoing threat was credible, and damage claims against those obtaining process should not be treated as appeals from judgments that no longer operated. An independent facial challenge may be different from a request to invalidate a particular order.
- **Respondents' strongest case:** No present application or concrete enforcement threat under materially amended statutes; the damages theory depended on declaring each state injunction unlawful; the party's use of ordinary litigation may not itself constitute state action. State-law tort issues belong in state court after federal dismissal.
- **Concession/limit:** Political speech's importance does not itself establish jurisdiction, state action, or a viable defendant.

## D. Lawful paths and uncertainty

1. Affirm for no live Count One controversy and lack of district-court appellate authority for judgment-dependent counts, without discussing speech merits.
2. Vacate in part where a federal damages claim identifies coercive misuse of state process to silence independently protected voter-guide speech, separable from a request to overturn the state injunctions; remand for the pleading, attribution and injury inquiries.
3. If federal claims remain dismissed, leave related state claims to the authorized forum. The pleadings' separation of independent injuries from judgment-caused injuries is material.
