**P3 — Judgment and limits:** Affirm the appellate judgment reinstating the prosecution, permitting ordinary further proceedings on the existing charge. Only post-October 25, 1992 willful failures may constitute this offense. Every element must be proved beyond reasonable doubt; ability-related and other lawful defenses remain. This decision determines no guilt, sentence, restitution amount, or treatment of older arrears in a future restitution order.

**S1 — Bribery construction:** Section 666(a)(1)(B), with subsection (b), does not additionally require proof that the bribe itself affected or was paid from federal funds. Congress selected an institutional federal-benefits condition and a separate covered-business/transaction condition. The proposed tracing/impact element is absent from that complete formulation. This construction preserves every express predicate; it does not make federal receipt somewhere in a county sufficient to prove every other element. For the supplied bribery concerning operation of a jail housing a federal prisoner under a federal agreement, no outer-boundary constitutional ruling is necessary. The model does not hold that every conceivable local bribe may constitutionally be federally prosecuted.

**S2 — RICO agreement construction:** Section 1962(d) requires a knowing agreement to further an undertaking that, if carried out, would constitute a violation of an identified §1962 subsection. For the charged §1962(c) object, the enterprise, interstate-commerce relation, conduct-through-pattern requirement, and actual pattern meaning remain. It does not require every conspirator personally to agree to perform two predicate acts, personally direct the enterprise, or perform every substantive act. The agreement must encompass the substantive criminal objective; employment, friendship, awareness, and association alone do not establish it. Reves's substantive direction rule is neither overruled nor erased: the agreed undertaking must contemplate the substantive violation, while the defendant's conspiracy liability rests on the necessary knowing agreement. No general aiding-and-abetting holding is made.

**S3 — Remedy:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit's judgment on the two presented statutory grounds. Rejecting the added elements requires no new-rule instruction or harmless-error remand here. This is not an independent factual sufficiency review and supplies no holding that an unidentified charge or instruction was proper in every respect. The substantive acquittal stays intact. No count, conviction, or sentence beyond the reviewed judgment is reinstated or enlarged.

**Adversarial alternatives:** The strongest bribe-nexus construction invokes protection of federal funds, limits on federal criminal jurisdiction, and criminal clarity; its difficulty is that the enacted text already supplies the institution/transaction boundaries while corrupt conduct in this federally connected jail gives no need to decide a remote application. It fails to preserve the distinction between agreeing to a criminal enterprise undertaking and performing the substantive crime; no such additional personal-act requirement appears in subsection (d). An agreement limited to lawful conduct would be insufficient, but that is a different limit from two personally promised predicate acts. Shabani and its Nash/Singer rationale support interpreting the settled agreement concept; their §846 holding is not simply imported as a universal conspiracy code.

## Foster — December 2, 1997

**Review and reach:** No. 96-670, argued October 6, 1997. Reach the congressional-election timing conflict. The district court granted state officials summary judgment; the Fifth Circuit reversed. The current judgment object is that appellate rejection of Louisiana's final October-election feature, not an assumed affirmance of a particular injunction. The exact decree terms are unavailable and are not changed or reproduced.

**Authorities checked:** [Voting Rights Coalition, February 26, 1996](../../OT1995/output/OT_1995CHUNK4.md#voting-rights-coalition-v-wilson-petition-identifier-no-95-673), O'Connor's unanimous congressional-election authority and administration qualifications; canonical Thornton/Bryant and [its public separate positions](../../OT1994/output/OT_1994CHUNK6.md#us-term-limits-inc-v-thornton-bryant-v-hill-nos-93-1456-93-1828), May 22, 1995; [Freightliner, April 18, 1995](../../OT1994/output/OT_1994CHUNK4.md#freightliner-corp-v-myrick-no-94-286), with Scalia's judgment-only position correctly limited. The operative Standards modules are **Federal election registration duties through state agencies** (February 26, 1996) and **Motor-vehicle standards and independent actual conflict** (April 18, 1995). Neither predetermines this timing construction or supplies an NVRA notice condition for this suit.

The official then-operative text was checked: [2 U.S.C. §7](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title2/html/USCODE-1994-title2-chap1-sec7.htm) establishes the Tuesday next after the first Monday in November of even-numbered years as the day for election of Representatives; [§1](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title2/html/USCODE-1994-title2-chap1-sec1.htm) connects the regular Senate election to the regular election at which a Representative is chosen; [§8](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title2/html/USCODE-1994-title2-chap1-sec8.htm) permits state prescription of the time for filling the specified vacancies, including failure to elect at the prescribed time. The successful final selection of an October majority winner is not such a failure.

**F1 — Authority and conflict:** Article I, §4 permits Congress to prescribe the congressional-election time. Sections 1 and 7 conflict with Louisiana's rule insofar as an October majority conclusively elects the congressional winner and leaves no selection for the prescribed November election day. Calling that final selection a primary does not alter its legal effect. An “election” may contain preliminary steps; it cannot be treated as still incomplete after state law has conclusively chosen the officeholder with no later electoral selection.

**F2 — Scope and remedy:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit's rejection of that final October-election feature. Preserve compatible preliminary procedures and the open-ballot form; decide no blanket prohibition on every act before election day. Section 8's actual vacancy/failure-to-elect conditions remain distinct, and no comprehensive runoff holding follows. Further implementation, if required, must conform to the established timing conflict; no replacement calendar, new deadline, completed-officeholding disturbance, whole-system invalidation, or invented injunction term is prescribed. Presidential timing is outside the case.

The strongest contrary path treats election as a broad multistage process and emphasizes retained state administration. It fails on the decisive supplied premise that an October majority has already achieved final legal selection and November contributes no selection act. The strongest broader preemption route would displace the whole primary system; actual-conflict principles and Article I's shared administration do not justify that additional reach. No general hostility to primaries, open ballots, or state election administration is adopted.
