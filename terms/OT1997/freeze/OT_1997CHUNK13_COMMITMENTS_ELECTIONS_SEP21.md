## 151. Jordahl v. Democratic Party of Virginia, No. 97-859

### Reach and component rules

**J1 — prospective Count One:** Affirm dismissal without deciding the constitutional merits of any publication, past injunction or present disclosure provision. The ended injunctions, replaced/amended statutes and absence of identified intended conduct facing a realistic current enforcement threat leave no live challenge at this record's scope. Caldwell's June 5, 1998 authoritative exclusion of solely issue advocacy from the current provisions strengthens that conclusion; it is not an adjudication that every plaintiff's past or planned activity was solely issue advocacy. The 1989 express vote-against pamphlet remains a different fact. Pic-A-State (April 28, 1997) and American Booksellers preserve suits before enforcement when a concrete threat exists. No actual-prosecution prerequisite or automatic mootness-by-amendment rule follows. The damages counts are analyzed separately and do not become moot merely because an order ends.

**J2 — damages Counts Two, Three, Seven and Eight:** The seven-Justice affirmance position below treats the pleaded demand to declare these particular injunctions erroneous and compensate their direct effects as a request for lower-federal appellate reassessment of state judicial decisions. Rooker and Feldman supply that jurisdictional boundary. These were adjudications applying law after opportunity to object; their prospective terms do not make them legislative acts. Ending their prospective effect does not itself convert this particular complaint into an independent federal wrong. The holding must be confined to that pleaded theory: no universal rule about all dissolved or reversed orders, no but-for causation test, and no jurisdictional bar merely because a damages action touches state litigation.

The distinction is material. Dennis permits an independently alleged corrupt procurement claim against private conspirators notwithstanding judicial immunity; Lugar governs the separate attributable-state-action question. Neither makes all winning private litigants state actors. De Grandy (June 30, 1994), Part I-B, permits the United States' independent enforcement claim without appellate review of a judgment against it. These sources preserve original claims; the published Jordahl record identifies no independent fraud, deception or corrupt conspiracy. Do not turn this absence into a finding that DPV's conduct was constitutionally lawful. Preclusion, attribution, liability, damages, immunity and limitations are not decided.

Heck (June 24, 1994) is a bounded §1983 cognizability decision concerning an outstanding conviction in the specified custody setting. It is not a jurisdictional Rooker-Feldman rule. The lower court's assimilation of the two is rejected. Spencer (March 3, 1998) preserves Heck's reservations and distinguishes independent injury. Neither case affirmatively grants federal appellate power over the orders here, and neither supplies a universal civil-injunction favorable-termination requirement.

**J3 — state Counts Four, Five, Six and Nine:** On the seven-Justice J2 disposition, affirm jurisdictional dismissal without prejudice because no original federal jurisdiction remains to support §1367(a). Correct the appellate description: the district court's no-original-jurisdiction ground is not simply discretion under §1367(c)(3) after a federal merits dismissal. Decide no state tort, filing deadline or recovery. On Stevens's J2 remand, vacate the derivative state-claim dismissal for reconsideration; federal jurisdiction would permit, not command, supplemental adjudication, and all applicable §1367 grounds remain open.

## 152. Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith, No. 96-1867

### Common sufficient ground and tested competing path

**F1 — review:** All eight reach the federal question. The Florida Supreme Court's expressly coextensive treatment of the state constitutional claim supplies no adequate independent state ground; §1257 review of the federal holding is available. No independent interpretation of Florida's Constitution is necessary.

**F2 — federal merits:** All eight affirm the judgment on this bounded ground: allocating a limited party-expense benefit according to an objective five-percent registered-support criterion, after lawful fees have been collected and without denying a candidate ballot access or restricting private political spending, does not impose a severe associational burden on the demonstrated record. Anderson/Burdick requires examining the actual burden and precise legitimate interest. Buckley, Part III, permits requiring demonstrated support for election funding and distinguishes withholding a subsidy from excluding a candidacy. Supporting established public participation without publicly subsidizing every splinter organization is a legitimate interest; protecting named incumbents from competition or suppressing a viewpoint is not. The open registration criterion bears a reasonable relationship to stable party support. The candidate's separate ballot qualification does not establish an automatic entitlement to a party subsidy.

This is not adoption of the lower court's unqualified language about discouraging minor parties, a general rational-basis exemption for public money, or a rule that every five-percent measure is valid. Florida uses registered membership rather than Buckley's prior vote and does not establish Buckley's recipient expenditure ceilings or post-election payments. Those differences prevent treating Buckley as an identical scheme, but they do not establish the missing substantial burden in this limited party-expense allocation. No finding is made that minor-party fees were directly transferred to rivals.

**Strong alternative:** Reverse because state support systematically magnifies existing parties' advantage after minor candidates already satisfy the access rules, making anti-factionalism an insufficient excuse for discriminatory political assistance. This path is strongest if the allocation substantially suppresses a minor party's competitive capacity or conceals viewpoint favoritism. The present objective and changing support criterion, lawful-fee concession, retained ballot access and absence of such a demonstrated burden distinguish that claim from an exclusion or a selective viewpoint penalty. Forbes and Finley are useful checks, not substitute election-funding rules: neither makes every subsidy permissible, and an objective criterion does not cure an independently proven unconstitutional burden.

**F3 — remedy:** Affirm the federal judgment; award no rebate or refund and prescribe no new formula. The competing invalidation path would require lawful remedial consideration rather than automatic judicial extension of every payment. No Justice decides an unpresented challenge to fee collection, candidate access, a special source-of-funds exaction or a materially different future scheme.

## 153. Libertarian Party of Illinois v. Rednour, No. 97-25

### Components and competing paths

**I1 — threshold:** All eight reach the continuing challenge. The recurring party classification and access requirements survive the completed 1996 primary under Norman's election-continuity principle. Apparent insufficiency of particular submitted petition sheets is not used as an independently decided standing ground. No completed election is reopened.

**I2 — association and access:** All eight affirm under Anderson/Burdick's examination of the entire actual access system. A State may demand meaningful support in each relevant electorate. Trustee-based statewide status need not automatically become congressional-district status where the party has not met the district criterion. The general-election petition route, with the statewide cap and special redistricting provision kept distinct, remains an available route without a constitutional entitlement to use a state primary. The different primary and general-election thresholds serve different stages and electorates. Jenness permits meaningful support requirements; Norman preserves independent qualification and forbids disabling qualified components because others failed. This record establishes neither a forbidden party-name barrier nor Norman's cross-electorate disqualification.

The holding depends on the actual scheme and supported burden. It does not validate every five-percent threshold, every full-slate rule or every cumulative deadline. The 1996 accepted-petition count cannot prove that all candidates met the full signature threshold; the separate 1992 exception cannot prove ordinary-year accessibility. The 1994 qualifications and the practical alternative are relevant but not conclusive. The published record presents no supported genuine dispute of an impossible cumulative burden requiring a trial. The large numerical difference alone does not make primary and general-election pathways constitutionally interchangeable.

**Strong alternative:** Reverse or remand because the combined district signatures, slate obligation and deadlines, despite statewide demonstrated support, severely impede effective party association. This would follow on a supported severe-burden record, and neither Timmons nor Jenness creates categorical immunity. Here it depends on treating statewide support as automatically district-specific or on a cumulative-burden factual premise not established in the adjudicated record. Timmons (April 28, 1997) permits proportionate regulation within actual available alternatives, not exclusion to protect the two existing parties. The present record supports the bounded affirmance without that forbidden interest.

**I3 — congressional qualifications:** All eight reject this challenge. These modicum-of-support and nomination procedures do not add a personal qualification for service in Congress. Under controlling Thornton (May 22, 1995), genuine time, place and manner regulation remains distinct from a substantive personal exclusion disguised as ballot administration. Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt that application with its controlling exclusivity premise. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas agree on the genuine-procedure ground without newly endorsing the exclusivity rationale they did not join in Thornton. No claim that any burden called procedural is automatically permissible follows.

**I4 — remedy:** Affirm; no 1996 primary redo, automatic district-established status, substitute signature number or new statewide qualification. No separately unpleaded equal-protection cause is adjudicated. Future properly supported access challenges and Norman's qualified-component protection remain available.
