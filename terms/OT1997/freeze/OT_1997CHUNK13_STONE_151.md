# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## B. Judgment and remedy

- **Stone's vote:** **Vacate in part, reverse the categorical jurisdictional treatment of independent speech-suppression claims, and remand.** Affirm the dismissal of Count One to the extent it attacks repealed provisions without a concrete threat under the replacement enactment. Reverse the proposition that every § 1983 damages claim concerning the party's procurement and enforcement of state injunctions necessarily asks a federal district court to review those judgments. Remand the damages claims for separation of any independently actionable coercive conduct from impermissible collateral attacks, and for a separate state-action inquiry.
- **Remedy:** No present award of damages and no federal order vacating the expired Virginia injunctions. Permit properly pleaded, independent federal claims to proceed only if the plaintiffs establish action under color of state law, a protected-speech injury and a valid cause of action. Reconsider supplemental state-law jurisdiction as appropriate after the federal claims are sorted.

## C. Issue positions

### Issue 1: A political party cannot obtain immunity for suppression by calling it election regulation

- **Reach:** The First Amendment supplies the substantive interest; Article III still limits prospective relief.
- **Proposed holding:** Voter guides comparing candidates, including guides that endorse no candidate, are protected political expression. *McIntyre* forecloses treating an ordinary independent guide as unprotected merely because the sponsor has not registered or identified its authors in a manner demanded without an overriding, narrowly fitted interest. A political party's wish to control what voters see is not itself such an interest. Past injunctions do not automatically make a challenge to a substantially changed statute ripe.
- **Application:** The three injunction episodes interrupted speech when voters could use it. Those episodes cannot be treated as constitutionally harmless simply because they expired. At the same time, the plaintiffs have not shown a sufficiently concrete new threat arising from the revised statute to sustain Count One as a forward-looking challenge to that statute.
- **Boundary:** *McIntyre* does not prohibit all campaign-finance reporting, narrowly tailored fraud laws or valid disclosure rules for materially different organizational conduct. Nor does the importance of voter guides create a present Article III controversy without credible future enforcement.

### Issue 2: Independent coercive-process injuries can be litigated without appellate review of a state judgment

- **Reach:** District-court jurisdiction, conduct under color of state law, and the First Amendment claim are distinct elements.
- **Proposed holding:** *Rooker* and *Feldman* bar a district court from undoing the merits of particular state judgments. They do not bar a properly pleaded claim that a participant independently used governmental coercion to punish or prohibit constitutionally protected speech, where relief does not depend on declaring the state judgment void. Under *Lugar*, state involvement must be proven; merely filing an adverse lawsuit is not enough. State judicial machinery cannot confer categorical immunity if defendants are actually joint participants in unconstitutional state action.
- **Application:** The alleged concerted pursuit of last-minute orders shutting down election guides warrants an inquiry into what conduct and injury the federal counts actually assert, not an assumption that an injunction's existence defeats every civil-rights theory. On remand, the district court must separate allegations requiring rejection of the judgments from independent conduct that can be evaluated while leaving them intact. It must then decide whether the Democratic Party was a state actor in the legally relevant conduct, whether any separate federal claim was adequately pleaded, and whether the record supports relief. Stone decides none of those factual matters in the plaintiffs' favor now.
- **Boundary:** A litigant retains its own right to petition a court. Neither an unsuccessful claim nor an injunction later dissolved establishes malicious prosecution, abuse of process or § 1983 liability by itself. State-law tort claims remain governed by their distinct elements.

## D. Certiorari position

- **Approved vote:** **Grant**, as expressly stipulated for this simulation.
- **Institutional ground:** Prevent the state-judgment doctrine from swallowing independent protection for time-sensitive political speech, without making lower federal courts appellate tribunals for state decisions.
