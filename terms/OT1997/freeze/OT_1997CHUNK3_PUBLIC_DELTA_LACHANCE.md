## Event

LaChance v. Erickson, No. 96-1395, with the Rule 12.4 McManus companion. Plenary merits decision, January 21, 1998; argued December 2, 1997. On review of King v. Erickson, 89 F.3d 1575, covering Walsh, Erickson, Kye, Barrett and Roberts, and the separate judgment reported at 92 F.3d 1208 concerning McManus. The questions concern constitutional and Civil Service Reform Act immunity for knowingly false factual denials, and the dependent charge, penalty, impeachment and reporting dispositions.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

Both appellate judgments are reversed insofar as they adopt categorical denial immunity and its dependent charge, penalty and impeachment bars. The Barrett/Roberts reporting disposition is set aside only to the extent it depends on that erroneous categorical premise. The matters return for lawful case-specific proceedings; the Court selects no employee's final sanction.

## Holdings

### Due process protects a meaningful answer, not proved knowing falsity

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fifth Amendment's opportunity to answer an agency misconduct charge does not immunize a knowingly false factual denial from an otherwise lawful employment falsification charge or penalty consideration. An unsuccessful defense is not itself a knowing lie: the agency must establish the actual false statement and requisite culpability under the governing employment rules, while preserving good-faith factual contest, mistakes, uncertain recollection, legal disagreement and procedural nonadmission.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion has all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Loudermill requires notice, an explanation of the employer's evidence and a meaningful opportunity to respond, with adequate later process; it does not presume that an inaccurate statement is knowingly false. Bryson rejects a general privilege to meet an official inquiry with deliberate falsehood, while its criminal statutory elements and proof requirements remain distinct. A denial's grammatical form does not make proved deception necessary to meaningful participation. The concern that an innocent employee will be punished twice merely for losing is answered by proof of the actual falsification charge and lawful process, not immunity after deliberate factual deception is established. A separate supported determination may use evidence also relevant to the underlying misconduct; no different-source or corroboration requirement is created. Insistence that the agency prove its case and disagreement about legal characterization are not automatically false assertions of fact. Nor does absence of an oath create the categorical immunity at issue. These are employment proceedings, with no criminal jury, criminal burden or universal materiality element added. Genuine self-incrimination questions remain separate.

### The Civil Service Reform Act supplies safeguards without denial immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 7513 and 7701 do not confer categorical immunity for a proved knowingly false factual denial. Their cause, procedure, proof and review safeguards continue to govern every particular charge and sanction; rejection of immunity establishes no employee's falsity, service nexus, regulatory liability or final penalty.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion is joined by all seven other Associate Justices, providing eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejection of the statutory immunity claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 7513(a) permits covered action only for cause promoting service efficiency. Section 7513(b) ordinarily requires at least thirty days' advance written notice of specific reasons; its exception requires reasonable cause to believe the employee committed a crime punishable by imprisonment, not a finding made here. The employee receives a reasonable period of at least seven days to answer orally and in writing and furnish affidavits and documents, representation, and a written decision with specific reasons at the earliest practicable date. The agency may provide a hearing by regulation under subsection (c); that permission is distinct from Board hearing and representation rights under §7701. Subsections (d) and (e) preserve Board appeal and maintenance of the notice, response, decision, action order and supporting materials, available to the employee or Board upon request.

Ordinary misconduct requires preponderance proof under §7701(c); specified performance and senior-executive matters retain their separate substantial-evidence rule. Otherwise supported action still cannot be sustained if the employee shows harmful procedural error, a prohibited personnel practice or legal invalidity. Section 7703(c)'s record-based review for unlawfulness, procedural deficiency, arbitrariness or abuse, and lack of substantial evidence is appellate review, not the agency's proof burden; designated discrimination facts retain their distinct de novo treatment. Section 7703(d)'s OPM substantial-policy-impact and erroneous-interpretation predicates, reconsideration prerequisite when the Director did not intervene, and discretionary appellate review remain intact. None of these protections requires immunity for deliberate falsehood. The employee retains a meaningful answer and review while the agency bears its actual burden. The Court finds no notice exception, review-prerequisite failure or new disciplinary ground in these cases.

### Penalty consideration and impeachment remain conditional

**Holding and operative rule:** A proved knowing false denial may receive otherwise lawful consideration in selecting an employment penalty or assessing credibility; the categorical bars resting on denial immunity cannot stand. Such use is permitted, not compelled, and an adverse finding on the underlying misconduct alone establishes neither falsification nor an entitlement to enhance a sanction or impeach the employee.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela additionally joins its penalty-consideration proposition and its actual-proof and lawful-procedure qualifications, but agrees only in the disposition removing the distinct impeachment bar.

**Controlling explanation:** The protected chance to contest an allegation does not require an adjudicator to disregard a properly established deliberate deception. Yet relevance, proof of the actual charge, procedural fairness and a lawful penalty basis remain necessary. The statutory decisionmaker must distinguish a knowing factual falsehood from an honestly held denial, ambiguity, memory failure or legal nonadmission. The same safeguards prevent automatic escalation based merely on an employee's unsuccessful defense. The Court therefore removes the categorical penalty and impeachment bars, including the identified McManus impeachment ruling, without selecting an adverse inference or sanction and without displacing unrelated evidence rules.

### The reporting branch and self-incrimination require their own predicates

**Holding and operative rule:** The Barrett/Roberts reporting disposition is set aside only insofar as it rests on categorical immunity from providing information because it would implicate the employee; further proceedings must address the actual duty and every supported defense. Rejecting a right to lie neither compels waiver of a valid privilege nor decides a genuine compelled-incrimination claim, and it creates no general duty to confess.

**Authority:** Part IV of Souter's opinion has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela joins only the proposition preserving separate compelled-incrimination review and prohibiting compulsory waiver of a valid privilege; he agrees only in the reporting disposition itself.

**Controlling explanation:** Garrity bars criminal use of answers compelled by a threat of public-employment loss. Gardner forbids discharge solely for refusal to waive protection against criminal use of compelled testimony and its fruits, while distinguishing questions specifically, directly and narrowly concerning official duties without a required waiver. The reproduced 5 C.F.R. §5.4 concerns information required by the designated civil-service bodies or authorized representatives and retains its exception for disclosure prohibited by law or regulation. These actual conditions cannot be replaced with either an unrestricted duty to report or automatic immunity whenever information may be self-implicating. No respondent-specific compulsion, criminal-use threat, refusal, immunity or waiver is established here. The Court selects no silence penalty or adverse inference and establishes no universal prohibition on otherwise lawful civil inferences. Barrett and Roberts's unreviewed false-time determinations remain undisturbed; the dependent reporting and investigatory-falsification issues return for lawful consideration.

## Precedent Treatment

- Loudermill is applied to preserve meaningful notice and response, adequate later process and the difference between inaccuracy and knowing deception. It supplies no immunity for a proved lie.
- Bryson supplies the bounded principle against deliberate falsehood as a response to an official inquiry; its criminal statutory elements and proof conditions are not transferred to employment discipline.
- Garrity and Gardner remain operative for compelled testimony, criminal-use protection and impermissible waiver demands, with Gardner's official-duty distinction intact. No respondent-specific privilege claim is adjudicated.
- Gaudin remains a criminal element-allocation holding, not a command for an administrative jury or criminal burden. Lanier preserves conduct-time warning in its stated setting; today's decision supplies no missing prior notice for an individual charge.
- Jeffries, Richenberg and Valot retain their actual-conduct, meaningful-rebuttal, lawful-process and bounded-remedy rules. None confers categorical denial immunity or creates a general investigation checklist.
- Hudson retains its distinct civil/criminal classification inquiry and supplies no complete due-process answer here. Lee's supported-duty and threshold-remand rulings establish neither employee falsity nor an individual reporting obligation.

## Law After Decision

A meaningful employment answer procedure and the CSRA's safeguards do not categorically protect a knowingly false factual denial. The actual charge, its culpability, proper administrative burden, service nexus, lawful procedure and sanction must still be established. Negative phrasing and absence of an oath do not establish immunity; failure to prevail, mistake, uncertainty and procedural nonadmission do not automatically establish falsity. The Court creates neither a criminal false-statement rule nor a duty to waive a valid privilege or confess.

Lawful consideration of proved falsity for penalty or credibility is permitted, with no mandatory enhancement, adverse inference or final sanction. The reporting branch depends on its actual rule and supported defenses. McManus's missing individual factual particulars are not supplied from the other employees, and Barrett/Roberts's unreviewed false-time determinations remain outside the correction.

## Separate Writings

## Procedure After Action

Both matters return to the Federal Circuit for further proceedings under the corrected legal rules. Walsh's falsification and dependent penalty questions return without automatic removal or a Court-selected replacement for the reported 90-day suspension. Erickson's corresponding issues return without automatic removal in place of the 15-day suspension. Kye's actual statements, knowledge and penalty require lawful review without converting her suspicion about her son into a proved assertion or automatically replacing the 45-day suspension with removal. Barrett and Roberts's investigatory-falsification and dependent reporting issues return, preserving the unreviewed false-time findings and selecting no replacement for their reprimands or automatic restoration of demotions and 30-day suspensions. McManus's distinct falsification and impeachment rulings return without a newly found underlying event or final sanction.

The reported Board sanctions retain their procedural status while the authorized adjudicators determine the lawful consequences of the corrected rule. The Court orders no blanket reopening, compulsory hearing, additional evidence production, criminal referral, privilege waiver, final knowing-falsity finding or automatic penalty enhancement. This Supreme Court review is complete; no return date or retained jurisdiction is established.
