# 159. United States ex rel. McKenzie v. BellSouth Telecommunications, Inc.

*Stone pre-vote case brief. Alternate-history petition and merits treatment, not an actual Supreme Court merits decision.*

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. Status

- **Version:** OT1997 Stone-only forecast, version 2. **Approval:** **PROPOSED AND APPROVED** in the simulation, not a historical Court disposition.

## B. Judgment and remedy

- **Stone's vote:** **Vacate the affirmance of the qui tam dismissal and remand that claim for the statutory inquiry; affirm the Sixth Circuit's revival of the retaliation claim.**
- **Remedy:** Restore only the possibility of a qui tam suit, subject to proof of direct independent knowledge, voluntary submission to the United States before filing, any applicable public-disclosure issue, and underlying fraud elements. Permit retaliation discovery and trial on its own statutory conditions. No immediate damages, punitive order, or company-wide injunction.

## C. Issue positions

### Issue 1: A court cannot add an unprinted original-source deadline

- **Reach:** Statutory jurisdiction/threshold first.
- **Received circuit rule and Stone's proposed change:** The Sixth Circuit required a relator to alert the government **before public disclosure** and adopted a broad “based upon” rule. Stone rejects that **circuit construction** where it moves the enacted deadline: § 3730(e)(4)(B) says voluntary delivery to government **before filing the relator's action**. He would not add a prior-publicity condition or equate every independently known fact with a complaint derived from another source. He does not purport to erase the separate textual requirements of direct and independent knowledge or voluntary government disclosure before suit.
- **Proposed holding:** “Based upon” must be assessed by whether the allegations actually derive from public disclosures in the legally relevant sense, not solely whether accusations about similar misconduct overlap. An actual original source with direct independent knowledge does not lose statutory status just because another person informed the public sooner, provided the statutory government-submission requirement is met.
- **Application:** McKenzie's dispatcher experience and early internal objections are evidence she may have independently known how repair reports were falsified. The court nevertheless established that allegations in her complaint closely resembled other public suits and news items. Neither fact alone settles which information her federal fraud allegations actually derive from or whether she voluntarily informed the government **before filing** as the statute specifically requires. Her under-seal filing and subsequent government service cannot simply be assumed to satisfy a prefiling duty. Remand is necessary.
- **Boundary:** The decision creates no private suit from a statute lacking one, no exemption for copied complaints, and no presumption that BellSouth defrauded the government.

### Issue 2: Corporate liability and employee protection are statutory

- **Reach:** The separate retaliation cause survives if the pleaded protected conduct, employer knowledge, and adverse response can be proved.
- **Proposed holding:** The § 3730(h) employment remedy does not rise or fall solely with jurisdiction to maintain the government's distinct qui tam claim. Internal actions that reasonably signal contemplated reporting or a potential FCA claim can be protected under the then-effective statute.
- **Application:** The allegations of persistent objections plus drawing supervisors' attention to litigation involving comparable federal fraud provided enough notice at dismissal stage. Whether the reactions actually caused covered retaliation remains to be determined.
- **Boundary:** Stone gives BellSouth no corporate privilege against enforcement and imposes no penalty because it is large. Statutory misconduct and causation, not corporate wealth, determine liability.

## D. Certiorari position

- **Approved vote:** **Grant** in the simulation.
- **Reason:** Resolve a statutory circuit split affecting both public recovery and the deliberately limited qui tam channel.
- **Status:** Final Stone-only position.
