## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate in part and remand. Affirm insofar as the judgment dismisses Bauchman’s claims for injunctive and declaratory relief as moot; insofar as it rests on the conclusion that selecting, rehearsing, and performing religious choral music, including in religious buildings, for academic purposes does not violate the Establishment Clause; and insofar as it sustains dismissal of the separate Free Exercise and compelled-expression theories, to the extent the record establishes a genuinely grade-neutral opportunity not to perform. Vacate insofar as the judgment affirms the denial of leave to amend the Establishment Clause claim as futile, including its treatment of the teacher’s alleged conduct before the 1994-95 school year as irrelevant.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** On remand the lower courts must evaluate the proposed amended complaint’s Establishment Clause allegations, and any evidence properly before them, under ordinary pleading, amendment, discovery, and proof rules. They may not treat the choir’s educational purposes, or secular purposes a court identifies on the pleadings, as a reason to disregard allegations of separately identifiable official devotional activity, and they may not treat the teacher’s earlier conduct as categorically irrelevant to what the 1994-95 activity did. The allegations are allegations, not findings. The district court’s alternative untimeliness ground, which the court of appeals did not decide, remains for the lower courts. Graduation moots prospective injunctive and declaratory relief; the damages claim can keep the controversy live, subject to Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona and to the identity and capacity of each defendant and any immunities, which remain for the lower courts. The remand determines no liability, damages, qualified immunity, or municipal or supervisory responsibility; it leaves undisturbed the choir’s study and performance of religious music for academic purposes; and it does not review the disposition of the Utah constitutional claims, which is outside the grant.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Mootness and continuing damages controversy

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone holds Bauchman’s prospective claims moot after graduation but reaches the remaining granted questions because her damages claim concerns completed conduct and can keep the controversy live.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Graduation ends Bauchman’s personal stake in prospective injunctive and declaratory relief concerning her participation in the school choir, absent continuing exposure or a certified class. A damages claim for completed alleged constitutional injury, against a defendant legally answerable in damages, is distinct and keeps that part of the controversy alive; it does not revive the moot prospective requests.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Bauchman’s graduation moots her claims for prospective injunctive and declaratory relief; her damages claim can keep the controversy live concerning the completed conduct, subject to Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona and to the questions of defendants, capacity and immunity reserved for remand.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section I records that Bauchman enrolled in a private school for 1996-97 and graduated in June 1997, that the court of appeals dismissed her claims for injunctive and declaratory relief as moot, and that she seeks damages under § 1983. An injunction or declaration would no longer affect her school experience, but a valid damages judgment could redress a completed constitutional injury if one is proved. Arizonans holds that an individual prospective employment-rule challenge becomes moot when the employee voluntarily leaves covered employment without an intention to return, realistic recurrence or a surviving claim, and that a general relief request cannot manufacture nominal damages against a State or official-capacity defendant that is not a damages person under § 1983; it leaves a legally available individual-capacity claim as a different question. Bauchman’s departure from West High School is analogous. Lee v. Weisman’s continuing-exposure ground has no application after graduation; Hodge v. Jones confirms that a surviving damages dispute does not maintain separate prospective requests; and Settle v. Dickson County School Board confirms that graduation proves no universal mootness and leaves actual capacities, municipal attribution, immunity and remedies open. Section I records that the defendants raised qualified immunity and supervisory liability, that the court of appeals did not reach those arguments, and that its opinion does not state the capacity in which each individual defendant was sued.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide whether damages can actually be recovered: the capacity in which each defendant is sued, qualified immunity, municipal or supervisory responsibility, and the available remedy remain for the lower courts. No certified class, continuing school consequence, or claim by current students is decided.

### Issue 2: Religious music and religious venues under the Establishment Clause

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Establishment Clause challenge to the selection, rehearsal, and performance of religious music, including performances in religious buildings. The proposed amended complaint’s allegations that the teacher used choir activities to advance religious belief concern a separately identifiable activity and are addressed under Issue 4.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Under the Lemon test as updated in Agostini, purpose, effects, and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry into the governmental act and the function it actually performs. Teaching and performing religious music for academic purposes is constitutional. A public-school choir may rehearse and perform works containing devotional texts for legitimate musical and educational purposes, and neither the religious lyrics nor the fact that a performance occurs in a church alone converts that academic activity into governmental worship.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Selecting, rehearsing, and performing religious choral music, including in religious buildings, for legitimate musical and educational purposes is permissible secular academic instruction and does not itself violate the Establishment Clause. Neither religious lyrics nor a church venue alone converts that instruction into governmental worship.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section I records the court of appeals’ recognition that a significant percentage of serious choral music is based on religious themes or text; sacred works form a substantial part of the Western choral repertoire. Settle v. Dickson County School Board distinguishes objective academic study of religion from governmental religious instruction, and preserves Lee’s and Sherman’s limits on official worship and coercive participation without creating a blanket prohibition on academic religious subjects. Agostini directs the court to identify the governmental act and the function it performs, and treats religious identity and physical location as relevant in context but not individually decisive. Section I records an elective a cappella choir offered for credit, a repertoire alleged to include a preponderance of Christian devotional music, and performances at the Church of the Madeleine, the First Presbyterian Church and Temple Square. Where the governmental act is the choir’s study and performance of choral works, its function is music instruction; devotional lyrics and religious venues are evidence to be weighed in context, but alone they do not make the program the school’s own worship. What changes the analysis is official organization, adoption, direction or religious supervision of worship, or solicitation of religious adherence through public authority, as Agostini, Lee, Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District and Tanford v. Brand confirm; those allegations are addressed under Issue 4.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Religious repertoire is not unconstitutional because it is religious, a church venue is not automatically worship, and no general bar on religious music in public-school instruction follows. This holding does not answer allegations of separately identifiable official devotional activity, which Issue 4 addresses, and it does not find that the purposes the court of appeals identified on the pleadings were the defendants’ actual purposes. The separate pressure grounds of Lee and Ingebretsen are not independently applied here.

### Issue 3: Free Exercise and compelled expression

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the separate Free Exercise and compelled-expression theories challenging required rehearsal or performance subject to the grade-neutral opt-out.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A neutral curricular assignment involving religious music does not compel religious exercise or ideological affirmation when the student has a genuine, grade-neutral opportunity not to perform the challenged music and is not required to profess belief or engage in worship.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The dismissal of the separate Free Exercise and compelled-expression theories stands to the extent the record establishes a genuinely grade-neutral opportunity not to perform.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section I records Bauchman’s own allegation that, when she objected in connection with the Choir’s Christmas concert series, Torgerson gave her the choice of not singing songs she found offensive and told her that nonparticipation would not adversely affect her Choir grade. To the extent that opportunity is genuine, the school requires musical study rather than religious profession. Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21, applying West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette, bars officials from requiring affirming words or gestures, punishing refusal, or requiring exit as the price of declining, but holds that a practice permitting penalty-free abstention supplies no compelled-affirmation ground. These theories turn on compulsion and are distinct from the Establishment Clause allegations addressed in Issue 4, which turn on what the official activity does; as Sherman itself recognizes, protected abstention does not cure actual school worship.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This position does not sustain dismissal on any other basis. A sham opt-out, lost credit, stigma, exclusion from the program, compelled profession of belief, or an actual worship requirement would present a different case, and nothing here forecloses the Establishment Clause allegations addressed in Issue 4.

### Issue 4: Leave to amend the Establishment Clause claim

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches Question 4, whether the court of appeals erred in affirming the denial of leave to amend the Establishment Clause claim as futile, including by treating the teacher’s alleged conduct before the 1994-95 school year as irrelevant. The proposed amended complaint’s allegations are reviewed at the pleading stage only.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A secular educational purpose does not immunize a separately identifiable official devotional activity. Under the Lemon test as updated in Agostini, government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious observance, or solicit religious adherence through public authority, and a secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Evidence of institutional practice, teacher direction, content, and history bears on what the governmental activity actually does: purpose and effects help identify the governmental function, and institutional relationships show who directs it. A public-school teacher’s use of classes to promote religious adherence is prohibited if that official religious sponsorship is proved. An otherwise legally sufficient unresolved allegation of such conduct is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure, not dismissed because the program also serves educational purposes.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The court of appeals erred in affirming the denial of leave to amend the Establishment Clause claim as futile. Specific allegations that a public-school teacher selected music and venues to promote religion and used choir activities to advance religious belief cannot be dismissed, and an amendment presenting them cannot be held futile, merely because the choir also serves educational purposes. The teacher’s alleged conduct before the 1994-95 school year is not irrelevant as a matter of law; it may be evidence of what the official activity did during that year.
- **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section I records that the proposed amended complaint, with eight affidavits, alleged for the first time that Torgerson selected religious songs and performance sites for the purpose of promoting religion; alleged the advocacy, promotion, endorsement and proselytizing of his religious beliefs and practices, including requiring students to attend and participate in events during which religious worship occurred; alleged that in 1992-93 he required the choir to perform approximately once each month at LDS worship services, as part of roughly two decades of conduct before 1994-95; and set out each administrator’s alleged responsibility. Bauchman also alleged that Torgerson berated and ostracized students who dissented from his religious advocacy and criticized her before classmates after she and her parents objected. Those are allegations, not findings. They describe separately identifiable official conduct, namely selection of music and venues to promote religion, promotion of the teacher’s religious beliefs through the class, and the connection of choir activities with religious observances, which, if proved, would be official solicitation of religious adherence or organization of worship forbidden under the Lemon test as updated in Agostini; the alleged religious purpose is evidence of that function, not a freestanding ground. Agostini holds that a secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means; Tanford v. Brand applied that limit despite a sincere ceremonial purpose; Lee holds that a desire for solemnity does not authorize the State to undertake religious presentation; and Ingebretsen forbids a school’s organization, adoption, direction, or religious supervision of worship within its own event. In reviewing the original complaint, the court of appeals identified plausible secular purposes on the pleadings itself, while stating that it could not evaluate direct statements from the defendants about their purpose; in affirming the futility ruling it held the earlier conduct irrelevant and found the remaining 1994-95 allegations insufficient. A purpose a court supplies on the pleadings cannot answer specific allegations of religious purpose and devotional use, and a teacher’s history of directing the same program bears on what that program did while Bauchman was enrolled. Because the futility ruling, including its alternative summary-judgment basis, treated earlier conduct as categorically irrelevant and assessed the 1994-95 allegations without it, it rested on legal error; under Foman v. Davis such a ruling supplies no justifying reason for denying leave, so the denial was an abuse of discretion and the court of appeals erred in affirming it. Fair-notice pleading under Conley v. Gibson and the liberal amendment standard of Rule 15(a) and Foman v. Davis govern the remand, and Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit bars a heightened pleading requirement for any municipal claim while preserving Monell’s policy-or-custom and causation requirements. Judge Murphy, concurring in part and dissenting in part below, would have held that the proposed amended complaint stated an Establishment Clause claim and objected to treating past acts as irrelevant; his view is the lower-court dissent, not authority.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not find that the alleged proselytizing or worship occurred, that the amended complaint adequately pleads a claim against any particular defendant, or that any defendant is liable. Whether the allegations are adequately pleaded and proved, the alternative untimeliness ground, whether any properly supported summary-judgment motion succeeds after discovery, the scope of discovery, and the relevance of any particular earlier act are for the lower courts under ordinary rules. Earlier conduct is not itself Bauchman’s injury; it bears on the 1994-95 activity as evidence. Issue 2, not this issue, governs religious repertoire and church venues as such, and the separate pressure grounds of Lee and Ingebretsen are not independently applied.
