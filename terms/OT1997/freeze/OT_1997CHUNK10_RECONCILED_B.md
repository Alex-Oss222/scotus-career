## 1. United States v. Bestfoods — No. 97-454

**Result and rule:** Preserve all eight model commitments to vacate the Sixth Circuit judgment and remand. The derivative and direct routes remain distinct. Corporate ownership and ordinary parent oversight do not themselves impose subsidiary liability; derivative liability requires the applicable grounds for disregarding separateness. Direct liability attaches to the parent's own operation of the facility without requiring a pierced veil or formal sole/joint-venture operation. Specify the operative facility conduct as management, direction or conduct of operations **specifically related to pollution, hazardous-waste leakage/disposal, or environmental compliance**, not any general participation in the subsidiary's business. The model's word “particularly” must not become a broader general-business-control test.

Dual officers/directors presumptively act for the subsidiary in its business. The Government must rebut that attribution for the parent conduct it alleges; ordinary monitoring, finance, budgets and general policy are insufficient. Departure from ordinary corporate norms, especially parent-serving conduct contrary to subsidiary interests, can bear on attribution. Parent-only environmental activity warrants examination, not an ultimate liability finding. No new universal burden, corporate code or state-versus-federal veil-law choice is added.
