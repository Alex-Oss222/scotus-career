## Grounds and limits that assembly must preserve

**Six-Associate rationale.** The well-pleaded, substantial federal constitutional claims place these civil actions within §§1331 and 1441(a). Related state administrative-review and constitutional claims concerning the same property, designation, permit denials and procedure form the same Article III case under §1367(a). The absence of independent original jurisdiction over each state claim is the reason to examine supplemental jurisdiction, not an automatic defect in removal of the qualifying action. Limiting factual review to an agency record describes the review task and does not supply a categorical exception to the enacted grant. The holding addresses these mixed federal/state complaints; it decides neither an action containing only a state administrative appeal nor an independent diversity route.

Section 1367(c) separately permits declining a claim that raises novel or complex state law, substantially predominates, remains after dismissal of all original-jurisdiction claims, or presents exceptional circumstances with other compelling reasons. Economy, convenience, fairness and comity inform the lawful inquiry; they do not erase the statutory conditions. Established abstention doctrines remain available on their actual predicates. Quackenbush's distinction between outright surrender of discretionary/equitable relief and a justified stay of independent legal-damages claims must be respected if implicated. None of those doctrines is found to compel or forbid retention on this record at this stage.

**Ginsburg/Stevens ground.** General original- and supplemental-jurisdiction language does not sufficiently establish authority for the particular appellate review that Illinois law assigns to its courts: judicial reconsideration confined to the local agency record, with agency facts presumptively correct. An independent action asserting federal rights remains distinct. Section 1367 applies to removed cases in its proper domain, but a factual relationship does not resolve the different statutory function presented here. Their objection is to power in this proceeding, not a holding that district courts should exercise available power and then abstain. Concern about the implications for diversity and state administration supports their reading; it does not turn a consequence they criticize into a holding by the six Associates. The six Associates' reservation of diversity and retention does not answer their threshold objection or secure their join.

**Review channel.** Section 1254(1) permits review of the Seventh Circuit judgment following the District Court's merits judgment. This is not appellate correction of a district court's covered §1447(c) remand. The positions create no exception to §1447(d) for an allegedly erroneous jurisdictional remand and do not alter Things Remembered or Quackenbush's remand-review rules.
