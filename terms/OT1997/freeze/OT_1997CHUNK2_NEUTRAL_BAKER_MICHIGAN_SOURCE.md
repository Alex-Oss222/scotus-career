The authenticated published source reproduces these earlier Michigan authorities:

- **Nummer v. Treasury Department**, 448 Mich. 534, 542, 533 N.W.2d 250, 253 (1995), quoting **Storey v. Meijer, Inc.**, 431 Mich. 368, 373 n.3, 429 N.W.2d 169, 171 n.3 (1988): the quoted collateral-estoppel prerequisites require the same parties to have had a full opportunity to litigate and mutuality of estoppel. The source does not reproduce the full opinions or every qualification.
- **Lichon v. American Universal Insurance Co.**, 435 Mich. 408, 427–428, 459 N.W.2d 288, 298 (1990): the reproduced passage affirms that mutuality remains Michigan law, citing the court's earlier reaffirmation in **Howell v. Vito's Trucking & Excavating Co.**, 386 Mich. 37, 191 N.W.2d 313 (1971). The omitted footnote and complete treatment of exceptions are not represented as recovered.
- **Bacon v. Walden**, 186 Mich. 139, 144, 152 N.W. 1061, 1063 (1915): the reproduced statement says the defendant, who was not a party to the earlier injunction suit, was not legally affected or bound by its decree. This establishes the reported nonparty holding, not a universal conclusion about every relationship to a bound party.
- **Detroit v. Detroit Railway Co.**, 134 Mich. 11, 15, 95 N.W. 992, 993 (1903): the reproduced statement treats the defendant's nonparty status as a sufficient answer to the claim that an earlier injunction determination controlled the later case. The full earlier proceedings are not supplied.
- **Detroit v. Qualls**, 434 Mich. 340, 357–358, 454 N.W.2d 374, 383 (1990), and **E & G Finance Co. v. Simms**, 362 Mich. 592, 596, 107 N.W.2d 911, 914 (1961): the authenticated recitation identifies the party invoking preclusion as bearing the burden of persuasion. This is a reported legal proposition, not an independently read full-opinion extraction.
- **Michigan Court Rule 2.613(B)**: the authenticated contemporaneous recitation describes a direction to parties seeking modification of an injunction to present the request to the court that entered it. The complete rule text, any exceptions, and its complete amendment history are not reproduced here. The recitation itself does not establish a rule's preclusive effect, an outsider's obligation, or the disposition of another forum's evidence question.
