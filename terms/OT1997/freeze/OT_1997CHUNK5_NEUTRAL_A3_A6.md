## C. SUPPORTED ALTERNATIVES AND JUSTICE-WRITING LEADS

These are neutral analytical alternatives under Engine §14 except the party positions expressly identified in the source addendum.

* **Nonassimilation:** Lewis can argue that the same homicide is already punishable under federal murder law, leaving no relevant gap for the Assimilative Crimes Act. The strongest objection is that a general federal prohibition might coexist with a genuinely different state offense protecting a distinct interest or covering materially different conduct. An identity-of-elements rule, an identity-of-conduct rule and a statutory-purpose/displacement inquiry can differ; the packet does not select among them.
* **Assimilation:** The alternative is that Louisiana's child-victim category addresses a gap not fully occupied by the federal murder categories, although federal law punishes the killing generally. The strongest contrary argument is that substituting the State's murder grading and mandatory penalty would rewrite Congress's existing graded homicide scheme merely because state law describes an aggravating circumstance differently. Test the particular provisions, rather than assuming every overlapping federal offense either permits or forbids assimilation.
* **Actual remedial dispute:** The Fifth Circuit rejected assimilation but retained the conviction as federal second-degree murder. Lewis does not contest the legal correctness of retention on the jury findings. She challenges mandatory life: the federal provision permits a term of years or life. The United States concedes resentencing under the Guidelines if the conviction rests on the federal offense, with an upward departure initially determined by the sentencing court. The reported range is 168–210 months. Thus rejection of assimilation does not require acquittal, retrial or a sentence below life; affirming the federal conviction and remanding its sentence is a distinct lawful possibility. Upholding assimilation would require separate analysis of the state penalty's application.
* **Prior writings:** Wells distinguishes the statute's actual elements from assumed ones and preserves component-specific conviction and sentence consequences. Gaudin allocates established criminal elements to the jury; it does not undo an uncontested lesser federal conviction on its own. Williams v. United States, 327 U.S. 711 (1946), is the direct pre-divergence ACA lead for a state offense overlapping a federal enactment; its precise rationale should be verified if the model relies on it. The selected Wells and Gaudin author/join statements provide date-eligible Justice-specific evidence, not automatic positions on assimilation.

## C. SUPPORTED ALTERNATIVES AND JUSTICE-WRITING LEADS

These are analytical alternatives except the State's contextual submission identified below.

* **Bruton path:** An obvious blank or spoken deletion in a joint trial may itself point the jury directly toward a sitting codefendant, leaving the accusation functionally unchanged despite removal of the name. The argument focuses on the redaction's visible accusatory form rather than every inference produced by linking an otherwise neutral confession to later proof. The strongest objection is that the confession omits two names, other testimony describes six participants, and identifying Gray still requires context beyond the confession's words.
* **Richardson path:** The State can argue that the redacted statement does not name Gray and that the limiting instruction governs because any incrimination depends on linkage to other evidence. The strongest counterargument is that Richardson distinguishes a confession stripped of any reference to the defendant's existence from an obvious deletion that invites a juror to supply the omitted name immediately. Consider whether a line between obvious blanks and neutral replacements is legally supported; the packet supplies no generic approval of every substitute phrase.
* **Separate prosecutorial-linkage question:** The follow-up question connected Bell's information to Gray's arrest. The State submits that Gray was already a suspect and subject to an arrest warrant, and describes the question as a transition. No contemporaneous objection to that particular question is identified, while severance and admission objections were made. A conclusion based independently on that question must separately address scope and preservation; it cannot silently substitute for deciding the preserved redaction issue. Neither the State's explanation nor the implication asserted by Gray is adopted as a new finding.
* **Remedy:** An established confrontation violation remains distinct from Chapman harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt. No full-trial source supports a present harmlessness finding. Reversal or vacatur with ordinary error review can be narrower than an unconditional new-trial command; no acquittal follows from the admissibility question.
* **Prior writings:** Scalia's pre-divergence Richardson majority and Stevens's dissent are the direct competing Justice-writing leads; their actual rationale and joins must be verified if relied upon. Bruton remains the starting rule. The selected simulated Zafiro opinion of O'Connor preserves concrete confrontation risks despite the usual efficacy of instructions.
