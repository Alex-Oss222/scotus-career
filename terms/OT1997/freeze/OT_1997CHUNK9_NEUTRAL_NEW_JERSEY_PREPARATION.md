## New Jersey v. New York — No. 120, Original

### Identity, pleaded relief and components

Argued January 12, 1998; proposed decision May 26, 1998. Article III original action between States under §1251(a), on both States' exceptions to a Special Master's report. New Jersey invoked original jurisdiction in 1993; the Master conducted a trial. There is no lower appellate judgment to affirm or reverse.

New Jersey seeks sovereignty over the artificial additions outside the original island, a high-water original boundary and an injunction against New York's enforcement/assertion of jurisdiction there. The Master recommended New Jersey sovereignty over the fill; rejected New York's prescription/acquiescence and laches defenses; chose original mean low water; included a historic pier area; and proposed a practical adjustment placing the main immigration building and surroundings in New York. New York excepts on compact construction, prescription/acquiescence and laches. New Jersey excepts on high versus low water, the pier evidence and the power to adjust the line for convenience. These are distinct components; agreeing on the fill does not decide all six exceptions or the final survey/decree.

Federal filling from 1891 over about forty-two years added approximately 24.5 acres to the original three-acre island. The United States' ownership, reserved jurisdiction and operation are separate from interstate sovereignty. The eventual decree must preserve federal jurisdiction and cannot silently award property title, decide every municipal boundary or regulate an unpresented issue.

### Entering snapshots

The complete text of the congressionally approved compact is mechanically preserved in [the official statute extract](../research/chunk9_neutral_sources/1834_compact_statute_pages.txt), from [4 Stat. 708–711](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-4/pdf/STATUTE-4-Pg708-2.pdf). Articles First, Second and Third must be read together: midwater boundary except as particularly stated; retention of New York's present jurisdiction over the named islands and other then-jurisdictional islands; New York water/submerged-land jurisdiction subject to New Jersey's separate property, wharf/dock/improvement, aground/fastened-vessel and fishing rights. Article Third's vessel exception preserves New York quarantine, health and passenger laws, and the fishing right is conditioned on unobstructed navigation. Articles Fourth–Seventh preserve further distinct water and process allocations. The congressional proviso expressly preserves United States jurisdiction. No compressed paraphrase replaces those qualifications.

Central Railroad Co. of New Jersey v. Jersey City, 209 U.S. 473, 478–479 (1908), as identified in the authorized objective extract, establishes New Jersey's underlying sovereignty on its side despite the compact's particular New York jurisdiction. Actual Hess (November 14, 1994) requires examining the actual compact/entity relation for immunity; it supplies no result about this territorial boundary.

Georgia v. South Carolina, 497 U.S. 376 (1990), is the directly relevant pre-divergence authority, fully read. Its unanimous Part III applies prescription/acquiescence on a concrete history of sovereign acts, tax, conveyancing, policing and notice; inaction may matter over a sufficiently long period, but the decision relied on more than unexplained silence. Its Part IV reads the particular island-reservation language with boundary stability; Kennedy disagreed on that text's future-island scope. Part VII applies the accretion/avulsion distinction to federal dredging on a close actual record; Scalia, joined by Kennedy, disputed the factual application, not the continued governing distinction. Natural gradual erosion/accretion can move a water boundary, while an avulsive relocation does not. Man-made additions require their actual formation history and compact setting; “federal work” alone is not a universal legal classification.

Georgia's equitable handling of an unspecified maritime line does not itself authorize rewriting an established compact land boundary. Distinguish interpreting an unresolved line, giving effect to legally sufficient prescription and redrawing an otherwise established line for administrative convenience. ### Alternatives and the necessary factual discipline

**Compact path A:** Retention of “present” island jurisdiction protects the original island while the midwater allocation and New Jersey's underlying territorial rights remain; artificial fill on its side does not itself enlarge New York sovereignty. Strongest objection: an island allocation may describe a continuing geographic entity and practical jurisdiction, and the precise scope of retained jurisdiction must be derived from the compact rather than a default private-property rule.

**Compact path B:** The named-island reservation encompasses its lawful subsequent enlargement, drawing on the allocation's terms and structure. Strongest objection: “present” retention, the surrounding territorial allocation and the artificial character of the fill may be inconsistent with an expanding sovereign enclave. Kennedy's Georgia dissent concerns its different “all islands” text and natural processes; it is a serious textual analogy, not a controlling answer.

**Prescription/acquiescence:** Long, actual, adverse sovereign administration with sufficient notice and acquiescence could establish the claimed sovereignty independently of original allocation; federal administration or geographic labels alone may be equivocal. The contrary path preserves the compact boundary if the required adverse acts/notice/acquiescence are not established. The Master's rejection is not independent evidence of each underlying fact. The packet requires a sanitized factual supplement before any model chooses between these outcome-changing factual applications.

**Laches:** Delay alone must be distinguished from a legally cognizable interstate-sovereignty defense and from prescription's elements. A finding of prejudice, waiver or settled reliance cannot be invented. If a Justice recognizes a laches defense beyond the Master's view, the relevant delay/prejudice facts and source must support its application.

**Line, pier and practical adjustment:** High water versus low water is a distinct compact/boundary construction question. The pier exception requires evidence of the 1834 physical shore and whether its footprint was actual land, not merely a structure over submerged territory. A decree may provide for survey or resolution of a genuine evidentiary gap; it may not invent coordinates. Practical inconvenience supports considering a lawful implementation mechanism, but a substantive reallocation requires a legal source beyond convenience. A judgment on these exceptions may direct a later decree; it does not automatically close all retained original proceedings.

### Individual sources and readiness

Stevens and O'Connor joined Georgia's prescription, island-construction and avulsion holdings. Stevens's separate maritime-boundary view, joined by Scalia, proposed a different equitable line only where that maritime line was not fixed. Scalia joined Georgia's island-text holding and dissented from its avulsion factual application; Kennedy dissented on island-text construction and joined Scalia on avulsion, while joining the prescription holding. Their exact component joins must remain distinct. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined actual Hess's compact-specific inquiry and current Alaska's distinction between political status, title and territory. Thomas joined current Alaska and is bound by inherited original-jurisdiction law; no Georgia participation is attributed to the four Justices not seated when it was decided. These analogous joins do not prescribe a result in this different compact.

**Readiness:** Compact and jurisdiction components are framed. A final prescription/pier/laches factual application awaits the narrowly requested sanitized supplement. Modeling may evaluate legal alternatives conditionally but may not freeze a final unsupported factual outcome. The completed public record must distinguish any merits determination from remaining decree work.
