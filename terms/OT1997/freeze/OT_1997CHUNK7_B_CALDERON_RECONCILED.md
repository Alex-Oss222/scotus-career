## Reconciled results and historical comparison

The eight-Associate division remains four to affirm the exceptional institutional recall and four to reverse and reinstate the June 11, 1997 mandate. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer affirm; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas reverse. Each Associate's result matches that Justice's historical position. History is confirming evidence, not a substitute for the independently assessed grounds and current-law limits below. Neither the historical Chief Justice's vote nor historical authorship supplies anything in this handoff.

Both sides recognize exceptional appellate power over a mandate, subject to governing statutory/rule constraints and abuse-of-discretion review. They disagree about whether this action is a justified correction of the court's own first-application process or a substantive reopening requiring an innocence gateway. Neither side treats capital punishment alone as a sufficient justification, and neither equates a court-initiated first-record recall with every prisoner-filed successive application.

## Common threshold and record limits

Felker's section 2244(b)(3)(E) review bar governs a grant or denial of permission to file a successive district-court application, not ordinary review of this separate mandate-recall judgment. The recalled proceeding remained confined to the claims and evidence of the first application; the prisoner's July motion and Leitch's later parole account were expressly excluded. Calling action court-initiated would not permit evasion if its actual basis were a successive application, but that contrary basis is not established here. The distinct July motion's rejection does not decide the separate recall's lawfulness.

Thompson filed the first application in January 1990. Current Carriger extends Lindh's bounded temporal construction to an already pending capital application outside established chapter 154 qualification: the identified amended merits and replacement factual-presumption provisions do not govern. No California qualification is established. This is not blanket immunity from every AEDPA provision or from independently applicable constraints on later filings. Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas apply the binding rule despite their earlier reservations.

The chronology now includes January 17 notice; the reported March 12 belated-call request, second judge's support, and panel author's rejection; the June 11 mandate; a separately reported court-initiated recall request shortly afterward; the prisoner's July motion; and the August 3 recall. The March issue was known and rejected before the mandate. The June initiative preceded the prisoner's new-evidence motion but was not an entered recall or stay. The source does not establish every judge's first-knowledge date or private responsibility. The stated wait for state proceedings is an explanation to assess, not proof that the delay was necessary.

## Four Associates affirm: exceptional institutional correction

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer accept a restrained power to restore judicial consideration lost through a specifically supported internal failure in the court's first-application process. The court must have an actual institutional corrective basis, not merely prefer a different merits result or use its own initiative to admit a barred new application. The nature of the problem, connection of the correction to it, timing, scope, and reliance interests inform exceptional discretion; these are reasons for the judgment, not a newly mandated checklist.

The clerk-transition failure, actual March effort to obtain belated consideration, and separately reported June initiative provide that concrete institutional basis. The panel author's rejection and subsequent mandate are serious contrary facts; they remove any theory of an unnoticed lapse first discovered immediately before execution. These four nevertheless conclude that the panel's refusal did not extinguish the full court's exceptional authority. The independently initiated first-record correction and explanation for awaiting state proceedings suffice under restrained abuse review despite poor timing. They do not find the March rejection unlawful, the delay indispensable, or every judge blameless.

Hazel-Atlas establishes exceptional appellate protection of the judicial process, with its actual fraud predicate distinguished. Ohio Power illustrates exceptional power over the Court's own procedure despite prior finality; it does not make ordinary refused rehearing perpetually reopenable. Carlisle forbids circumvention of an actual rule, but no supplied rule eliminates the identified corrective power here. Lonchar protects required process without making en banc review mandatory. The State's finality interest is substantial but does not itself transform this genuine institutional correction into an otherwise barred claim.

These four therefore refuse to impose Schlup or Sawyer as a prerequisite to this ground of recall. Both gateways retain their own operation where a claim is otherwise barred. They make no finding that Thompson passes either gateway and do not independently resolve Strickland prejudice or endorse every en banc merits statement. The issue is whether the exceptional correction falls within the court's authority and discretion, not whether this Court would independently reach each underlying merits conclusion.

## Four Associates reverse: completed merits reopening

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas recognize exceptional process correction but reject that characterization on this record. January notification and the actual March request and rejection supplied an opportunity for institutional attention before issuance of the mandate. The mandate expressed the panel's completed adjudication. The subsequent recall restored rape-related relief and reopened murder review; it did more than correct a clerical mismatch, remedy fraud, or dispose of a rehearing while the mandate remained stayed. The June initiative confirms independent court action but did not suspend the mandate, and the later delay increased the State's reliance interests.

For this category of recall reopening the completed merits of state-prisoner habeas review, these four would expressly extend the appropriate miscarriage-of-justice gateway. That is a new recall proposition supported by finality and comity, not a claim that existing Schlup or Sawyer already controls every recall. Fraud, clerical correction, a mandate not expressing the court's judgment, and other genuinely different process defects remain distinct. They require neither proof of dishonest motives nor a finding that every active judge knowingly rejected review.

For the stand-alone rape conviction, reliable new evidence considered with the whole probative record must show that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict beyond a reasonable doubt. For an eligibility-only attack, clear and convincing evidence must show that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the necessary rape special circumstance and other state-law eligibility predicates. Schlup and Sawyer remain separate; ordinary Strickland prejudice, mitigation, and confidence in the actual verdict do not substitute for either gateway.

The four find neither showing met on the first-petition record. Additional Fink and Del Frate information must be assessed cumulatively: specific informant histories, benefits, dishonesty assessments, Del Frate's earlier association with Leitch, and correspondence between his account and newspaper reports matter beyond the substantial impeachment already presented. The added evidence further weakens their testimony but does not require every reasonable juror to reject it in the context of the other evidence. It is not excluded merely because it impeaches or concerns informants.

Root's contrary opinions contest the handcuff inference, timing of bruises, and implications of sparse sperm. They do not provide an alternative cause for the crushing injury, select douching over low sperm count, or establish consensual intercourse. Semen establishes intercourse rather than force by itself; restraint evidence, altered clothing, gagging, remaining confession accounts, and Thompson's inconsistent explanations still allow a reasonable adverse conclusion when the augmented evidence is considered together. The source's inconsistent left/right wrist descriptions supply no inference. The later Leitch account is excluded because the recall did not rely on it. No requirement that evidence postdate the mandate or satisfy AEDPA's distinct due-diligence condition is added to Schlup.

## State findings, substantive limits, and writing functions

Former section 2254(d) protects a qualifying state factual determination after the requisite merits hearing with adequate written indicia. Paragraph (8) requires examining the pertinent produced state record as a whole; Marshall preserves historical and credibility findings subject to those statutory conditions. A later competing inference does not automatically erase a finding, and a prior finding cannot conclusively determine new evidence it never assessed. Neither side invents a state finding about every impeachment item or Root opinion, treats the federal panel as a state factfinder, or makes the original jury verdict conclusive on the augmented record.

The four reversing assess the gateway without manufacturing such a finding. The four affirming neither order a gateway hearing nor eliminate applicable state-finding protection. If a lawful route reaches ineffective assistance, Strickland's deficient-performance and reasonable-probability-of-prejudice requirements remain separate. Current Carriger's remand on its distinct inconsistent record analysis is not a mandatory remand rule for every gateway dispute.

The opposing writings require substantive accounts of institutional repair versus merits reopening, the actual January/March/June sequence and later delay, the legal choice whether to extend innocence gateways, and the resulting remedy. A writing for the four reversing must retain the cumulative evidence analysis and different crime/eligibility burdens; a writing for the four affirming must distinguish abuse review of the corrective decision from a merits finding of innocence or ineffective assistance. No author, joinder beyond these stated grounds, or hidden conference exchange is supplied. There is no material Associate judgment departure from history. Current Carriger and Schlup require the express whole-record/state-finding limits stated here rather than transplantation of historical shorthand about impeachment or jury respect.

## Exact conditional consequences

For Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer: affirm the recall on the presented question. The preserved en banc action affirms the rape-conviction and rape-special-circumstance relief and associated death-sentence vacation, with the District Court directed to enter the partial writ unless California elects to retry Thompson within a reasonable time. The separate direction for further review of the first-degree-murder conviction and vacation of the then-entered execution order remain. No fixed election, commencement, or completion date, elapsed-time determination, immediate unconditional release, murder acquittal, or independent Supreme Court Strickland holding follows.

For O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: reverse the August 3 recall and remand with instructions to reinstate the June 11 mandate denying relief on the first application. That removes the recall-dependent conditional partial-writ direction, death-sentence vacation, murder-review remand, and execution-order vacatur. It does not set an execution date, make an expired date executable, decide a distinct later application, or displace an independent lawful source of protection. The four-to-four Associate division alone establishes no final Court judgment.
