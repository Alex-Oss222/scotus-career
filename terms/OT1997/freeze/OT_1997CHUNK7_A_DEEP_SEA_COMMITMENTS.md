## Common committed propositions

**I. In-rem jurisdiction without State possession.** The Eleventh Amendment does not bar this federal admiralty proceeding against a wreck that California neither possesses nor holds through an officer, solely because California asserts ownership. The Davis supplies the actual-possession distinction for a sovereign's asserted property interest; Ex parte New York (No. 2) involved a State-owned vessel in State possession and governmental service, which is absent here. A title assertion or statutory constructive possession is not the actual custody whose displacement would require process against the sovereign. This is a conclusion about this traditional in-rem action, not a general immunity exception whenever a plaintiff writes the name of property rather than the State in the caption.

**II. Jurisdiction and title are distinct.** The district court may exercise in-rem jurisdiction and decide the competing claims under the governing law without first accepting California's ownership assertion or requiring it to prevail on the ASA as an immunity prerequisite. This permission is no judgment that California lacks title. It directs no personal damages, officer coercion or execution removing property from State possession, and decides no case involving a State-possessed wreck. Coeur d'Alene's officer/title limits and Seminole's unresolved direct-State constitutional rationale do not displace the distinct historical admiralty rule. No Article I abrogation, waiver or new general state-amenability holding is needed.

**III. ASA predicates and maritime abandonment.** Section 2105 asserts United States title and transfers it to the State for an abandoned shipwreck (a) embedded in State submerged lands, (b) embedded in coralline formations protected by the State on its submerged lands, or (c) on State submerged lands and included in or determined eligible for the National Register. Abandonment remains a distinct predicate, informed by its maritime meaning: renunciation may be express or inferred from appropriate circumstances; mere passage of time, failure to recover previously inaccessible property, or an assertion of State ownership does not automatically establish it. The Court should not determine on this incomplete title record that all insured and uninsured cargo and the ship have one settled ownership or abandonment status. Nor should it approve a universal express-renunciation-only rule or declare every old wreck abandoned.

The public must receive adequate notice of the location of a wreck to which title is asserted. The Secretary of the Interior, after consulting the appropriate State Historic Preservation Officer, makes the written eligibility determination for the third category. Public-land wrecks are United States property and Indian-land wrecks belong to the owning tribe under §2105(d). Rights reserved under 43 U.S.C. §§1311, 1313, 1314 and 33 U.S.C. §§414, 415 remain reserved. Section 2106 excludes salvage and finds only for wrecks to which §2105 applies; it leaves federal shipwreck law otherwise unchanged and does not affect a proceeding brought before April 28, 1988. This action began in 1991. None of these qualifications is a present factual finding that the Brother Jonathan satisfies the predicate.

**IV. Disposition and next stage.** Vacate the appellate judgment and remand for further proceedings consistent with the absence of a possession-based immunity bar, with ASA applicability and competing title claims addressed independently under the correct statutory and maritime principles. Do not affirm a categorical title conclusion reached as a preliminary jurisdictional predicate. California's statutory claim, insurer-derived interests, uninsured cargo and the United States' asserted property interest remain for lawful determination. No ownership, distribution, salvage award, entitlement to a completed salvage operation or final National Register fact is adjudicated. Necessary lawful custodial preservation remains for the district court; the Court neither orders surrender to California nor converts DSR's custodial/exclusive-salvor status into title. Do not decide California §6313 preemption broadly where jurisdiction no longer depends on defeating that asserted State title. Any remaining preemption question must be tied to an actual title determination and the ASA's scope.

## Common alternative-path result

The claim-based immunity path would protect the State from a decree adjudicating the asserted property interest, but supplies no sufficient reason to dispense with the actual-possession distinction of the applicable maritime precedents. The opposite overbroad path—caption alone defeats immunity and therefore DSR wins title—also fails: form does not justify dispossession of a sovereign and jurisdiction decides none of the ownership predicates. All eight join the narrow jurisdictional holding and independent title remand. No Justice's frozen commitment supplies support for a final abandonment finding, a relaxed title burden, complete §6313 preemption or an unqualified salvage entitlement.
