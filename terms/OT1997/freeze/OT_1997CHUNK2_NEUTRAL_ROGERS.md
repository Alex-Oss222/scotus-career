## 1. Event, lawful channel, actual question, and requested relief

## 2. Supported record and boundaries of the admissions

The sanitized account establishes the following, with its source status preserved:

- A deputy arrested Rogers for driving while intoxicated and searched him and his truck. A bag in the truck contained a silencer. The lower opinion places a black bag beneath the driver's seat; another published objective recitation describes a canvas bag behind it. The exact placement and color are unresolved and do no work in this packet's knowledge analysis.
- Counts two and three alleged knowing possession of an unregistered silencer and of a silencer lacking a serial number, under §§5861(d), (i), and 5871. The item was unregistered and lacked a serial number. A separate §922(o) machinegun count was also tried.
- The jury heard Rogers's recorded postarrest discussion identifying the silencer and his familiarity with silencers. At trial he identified the exhibit as a silencer. He denied owning the bag or knowing how the bag and its contents entered his truck. Recognizing the object's characteristics and knowing that the object was present in his vehicle are distinct propositions. These statements are evidence, not a stipulated guilty verdict or an admission of every offense element.
- Defense closing acknowledged familiarity with silencers while disputing knowledge that the bag was in the truck. No express waiver of the jury-instruction claim is established. Acknowledging a physical characteristic does not establish knowledge of a registration duty, intentional surrender of jury determination, or abandonment of a separate possession defense.
- The jury convicted on all three counts. The sentence was time served, 33 months, three concurrent three-year terms of supervised release, and a $150 special assessment. The sentence is background; this packet makes no inference about completed supervision, mootness, collateral consequences, or a required new sentencing calculation.
- Staples was decided after this trial. The Eleventh Circuit reversed the machinegun conviction for insufficient proof that Rogers knew the weapon had been converted to fully automatic operation; the Government agreed that count should be reversed. The two silencer convictions were affirmed.

The lower court treated refusal of the requested knowledge-of-characteristics instruction as omission of an essential element. It classified the omission as trial error susceptible to harmless-error review and found harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt because of Rogers's repeated identification of the silencer. It distinguished characteristic knowledge from the contested possession question. It assumed without deciding that Staples also applied to §5861(i), and stated that the trial instructions followed then-governing circuit law. Those are lower-court legal conclusions and characterizations, not controlling simulated Supreme Court law or independent findings resolving the proper construction of the charge. No additional alternative holding or lower dissent is supplied by the sanitized record.

### Controlling holdings

**Staples, May 23, 1994:** The Government must prove knowledge of the characteristics making the conventional-looking rifle a complete-weapon machinegun under §5861(d), beyond a reasonable doubt; circumstantial proof is available. Knowledge of nonregistration, registration law, or illegality is unnecessary. Thomas's five-Justice opinion supplies the controlling rule; Ginsburg and O'Connor support the narrower factual-knowledge requirement separately. Its holdings reserve other NFA devices and independently defined parts, ultimate knowledge, and properly available error-review questions. Staples is not itself an already-entered silencer or §5861(i) holding. Applying its distinction here is a supported legal argument; extending its statutory construction or assuming that protection in Rogers's favor must be stated expressly. The seed's broader formulation is accordingly qualified by the actual simulated holding.

**Gaudin, June 19, 1995:** Scalia's unanimous opinion requires jury determination beyond a reasonable doubt of the actual materiality element. It reserves universal structural-error classification. Acknowledged or powerful evidence does not by itself rewrite the statutory offense or allocate an element to the appellate court.

**Sullivan, June 1, 1993:** Scalia's unanimous rule makes a charge vitiating the reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings structural, because there is no valid reasonable-doubt verdict to test. It does not classify every instructional defect as structural. No defect in Rogers's global reasonable-doubt standard is established by this packet.

**Roy, November 4, 1996:** Souter's unanimous Part II holds that the preserved omission of the defendant's purpose to facilitate robbery is trial error capable of Chapman review, not automatically structural merely because an element was omitted. Part III, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, and Breyer, permits necessary-equivalence findings and supported whole-record inferences bearing on actual contribution to the verdict. The court must ask whether the charge led the jury to consider the evidence supporting the proposed inference. Strong evidence, a hypothetical correctly instructed conviction, or an appellate substitution of an element finding does not suffice when the omission prevents a reliable actual-verdict connection. This was a preserved personal-intent habeas case; its specific remedial mechanism and collateral posture do not become Rogers's direct-review remedy. The possible application to an admitted characteristic element must be explained rather than treated as an already-decided universal category. Roy defeats an argument that all element omissions are automatically structural, while leaving room to examine a distinct defect and its actual effect.

**Olano, April 26, 1993, and Johnson, May 12, 1997:** Waiver and forfeiture differ. A genuinely forfeited claim requires error, plainness, substantial-rights impact, and a separate discretionary fairness/integrity/public-reputation inquiry. Johnson measures plainness at appellate consideration where settled contrary trial law changes during direct review. It assumes substantial-rights injury and refuses correction under the fourth condition on its particular materiality record and intervening-law circumstances. It does not find preserved error harmless, establish a universal overwhelming-evidence exception, require innocence, demand a second institutional wrong, or finally classify every omitted element. Its unanimous error/forfeiture and fourth-condition components do not erase Scalia's nonjoin in the plainness holding or the express classification reservation.

**Estelle, December 4, 1991:** The controlling instruction holding applies whole-charge and whole-trial reasonable-likelihood review to an ambiguous instruction. This requires examination of the actual contextual charge; it is not permission to resolve an unreproduced instruction in either direction.

**Izumi, November 30, 1993, with the later components preserved in the scope module:** Rule 14.1(a) imposes a strong prudential presumption against questions neither presented nor fairly included; exceptions require independent support. A vehicle disposition is legally available when its actual predicates are established. A dismissal as improvidently granted would leave the judgment below intact without a merits affirmance or substantive decision of the omitted-element issue. A procedural disposition's legal effect must follow its public form.

Pre-divergence authorities retained by these holdings include Chapman (the beneficiary's burden to prove no contribution beyond a reasonable doubt), Winship (elemental proof), Cupp and Boyde (contextual instruction review), Rose and Fulminante (bounded trial/structural distinctions), Yates (actual effect rather than hypothetical sufficiency), Morissette and Gypsum (contextual criminal fault), and Freed (registration knowledge distinguished from object knowledge). Carella's separate opinion supplies a noncontrolling actual-findings rationale identified in Scalia's published Roy position, not a controlling categorical omission rule. The packet relies on these authorities at the scope retained by the selected simulated law and does not import later omitted-element decisions or historical post-divergence holdings in place of the simulation.
