#### Mississippi v. Louisiana, 506 U.S. 73 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1158.

**Decided:** December 14, 1992.

##### An interstate complaint belongs to the exclusive original forum

**Holding:** Section 1251(a) denies a federal district court jurisdiction to grant relief on an actual controversy between two states, even when the complaint is joined to private litigation and this Court previously declined an original action. The appellate judgment is reversed insofar as it adjudicates Louisiana’s complaint against Mississippi, with instructions to direct dismissal of that complaint for want of jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous controlling holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- California v. Arizona, 440 U.S. 59 — applied; this Court is the exclusive federal forum for a state’s claim against another state.
- Illinois v. City of Milwaukee, 406 U.S. 91 — distinguished as to different defendants and applied to preserve original-forum discretion.
- Ohio v. Wyandotte Chemicals Corp., 401 U.S. 493 — preserved; discretion does not grant another court exclusive interstate jurisdiction.
- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 — preserved; case-specific discretion does not negate §1251(a).
- Wyoming v. Oklahoma, January 22, 1992 — distinguished; its original-forum exercise and separate objections supply no district-court authority here.

##### Private title questions remain distinct from binding interstate relief

**Holding:** Section 1251(a) does not bar an otherwise lawful private-title action merely because deciding title requires locating a state boundary; such adjudication does not bind the states on their boundary. Louisiana’s intervention that seeks no relief against Mississippi is unaffected, and the remaining title claims return for a determination whether the existing record permits fair resolution or additional proceedings are necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous controlling holding and remand limitation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Durfee v. Duke, 375 U.S. 106 — applied; adjudication between private parties does not bind the states on their boundary.
- California v. Arizona, 440 U.S. 59 — applied to the actual interstate controversy; it does not withdraw otherwise lawful private-title jurisdiction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no interstate boundary, private title, accretion, avulsion or sovereign acquiescence issue. Party agreement or convenience cannot create district-court jurisdiction over the interstate complaint.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the interstate adjudication and direct dismissal of Louisiana’s complaint against Mississippi for want of jurisdiction. Remand the remaining title claims to determine whether the record permits fair resolution or further district proceedings are needed; Louisiana’s nonadverse intervention is unaffected.

#### Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, 511 U.S. 375

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-263.

**Decided:** 1994-05-16.

##### Postdismissal contract enforcement requires a jurisdictional basis

**Holding:** After a Rule 41(a)(1)(ii) stipulated dismissal that neither incorporates settlement obligations nor retains jurisdiction, a court has no ancillary or inherent jurisdiction to enforce the separate settlement merely because it ended a federal action; an independent jurisdictional basis is required. Neither factual interdependence nor authority to manage proceedings supports jurisdiction when the settlement-breach facts are separate from the former claim and breach violates no obligation in the dismissal order; judicial awareness or approval of the agreement is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule, application, and reversal with remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Willy v. Coastal Corp., 503 U.S. 131 (1992): distinguished; collateral Rule 11 authority does not create jurisdiction over a new settlement contract.
- Julian v. Central Trust Co., 193 U.S. 93 (1904): distinguished by its express reservation concerning judicially conveyed property; broad language is not general settlement-enforcement authority.
- Moore v. New York Cotton Exchange, 270 U.S. 593 (1926): its factual-interdependence basis is inapplicable to these distinct controversies.
- Chambers v. NASCO, Inc., 501 U.S. 32 (1991): its authority over litigation misconduct does not convert every settlement breach into interference with judicial proceedings.
- McNutt v. General Motors Acceptance Corp., 298 U.S. 178 (1936): applied; the party asserting federal jurisdiction bears the burden of establishing it.

##### A lawful dismissal order may make settlement compliance enforceable

**Holding:** A court may enforce settlement obligations through ancillary jurisdiction when, within its lawful dismissal authority, its order incorporates those obligations or expressly retains jurisdiction over the settlement, making breach a violation of the judicial disposition. Under Rule 41(a)(2) the court may impose such terms in its discretion; under Rule 41(a)(1)(ii) it may incorporate or retain jurisdiction if the parties agree, but private consent alone, awareness of the bargain, or an attempted later addition after jurisdiction has ended does not create jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this qualification defining the boundary of the jurisdictional holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Julian's actual reservation-based holding remains effective; Rule 41's distinct mechanisms are applied without creating a mandatory retention rule. Rule 60(b) reopening is reserved and is not treated as contract enforcement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides jurisdiction, not breach, specific performance, damages, Rule 60(b) reopening or diversity for a new claim. Neither private consent nor postdismissal judicial rewriting supplies lost jurisdiction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and remanded for dismissal of the settlement-enforcement motion for lack of the asserted ancillary jurisdiction. No breach adjudication or reopening follows. State contract enforcement and a new federal action with an independent jurisdictional basis remain distinct.

#### Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., 513 U.S. 30

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1197.
**Decided:** November 14, 1994.

##### PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity

**Holding:** A bistate compact entity does not acquire the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity merely by exercising governmental power: its actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must supply good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred, with state-treasury exposure highly probative but not categorically indispensable. PATH is a distinct, financially self-supporting enterprise responsible for its obligations; these employee suits impose no judgment obligation on either State and do not treat either State itself as a defendant, so PATH lacks that immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Lake Country Estates, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency*, 440 U.S. 391 (1979), applies its entity-specific compact inquiry: exercising state power does not itself make a bistate corporation the States, and the compact’s actual structure matters.
- *Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), preserves the distinction between a separate public entity and an arm of a State; a governmental function alone supplies no immunity.
- *Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. v. Feeney*, 495 U.S. 299 (1990), is distinguished as deciding federal-forum consent while assuming, rather than deciding, PATH’s arm status. Its consent ruling is not an earlier holding that PATH is immune.
- *Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission*, 502 U.S. 197 (1991), preserves state-court FELA coverage without constructive waiver of federal-forum immunity; no abrogation or waiver rule is enlarged here.
- *Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy, Inc.*, 506 U.S. 139 (1993), preserves immediate review of a qualifying immunity denial without establishing that a particular entity is an immune arm. Its appealability holding does not decide PATH’s status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every compact entity nonimmune, make a debt disclaimer alone dispositive, decide a materially different entity’s practical dependence, abolish state sovereign immunity or hold that FELA abrogates federal-forum immunity. Negligence, causation, damages, the Boiler Inspection Act merits, every independently applicable nonimmunity limitations defense and hypothetical consent exceptions remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both the Hess and Walsh immunity dismissals are reversed and remanded. The Eleventh Amendment bar is removed; ordinary nonimmunity defenses and the separate actions' merits remain undecided.

#### Doe v. Taylor Independent School District

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 90-8431; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** October 16, 1995.

##### Immediate review of legal immunity questions and factual sufficiency

**Holding:** An immediate qualified-immunity appeal may decide a separable legal question on the plaintiff's properly supported assumed facts, but may not decide only whether the evidence is sufficient to establish those facts. A factual-sufficiency portion must be dismissed; an appellate court cannot resolve disputed knowledge or causation by choosing whose account is true and recasting that choice as law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular additional factual-only appeal is found to exist. The Court resolves neither credibility nor the inconsistent January/February descriptions of Caplinger's first notice.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the separable legal immunity judgment and remand; dismiss any purely factual-sufficiency portion. The distinct supervisory-right and individual-immunity holdings appear under Civil Rights, Doe, October 16, 1995, Fifth Circuit No. 90-8431.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Johnson v. Jones supplies the legal-question/factual-sufficiency boundary. Elder v. Holloway requires examination of all relevant law, not reconstruction of a more favorable factual record for the officer. Neither rule expands collateral appellate jurisdiction.

#### Pinder v. Johnson

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 93-2125; no Supreme Court docket stated in the Record
**Decided:** May 21, 1996

##### What may an appellate court decide on this separable legal qualified-immunity appeal?

**Holding:** Sections 1254(1) and 1291 permit review of the separable legal immunity issue on the plaintiff-favorable account governing that issue. This jurisdiction does not permit a determination of evidentiary sufficiency, disputed intent or causation, or claims excluded from the appeal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; unanimous controlling rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The reviewable question is whether the assumed conduct violated a right sufficiently established when Johnson acted. The district court's characterization of its disposition as one under Rule 12(b)(6), and the appellate court's summary-judgment terminology, do not authorize an unfavorable resolution of disputed facts or enlarge the legal issue before the Court. The Court does not rely on a second-appeal premise or convert a legal immunity appeal into fact review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court determines neither disputed causation nor ultimate constitutional liability. The substantive notice holding and its exact five-Justice coalition appear under Civil Rights, Affirmative danger and conduct-time qualified immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed individual-immunity judgment is affirmed, 5-4, on the conduct-time notice ground. No excluded claim or new factual question is decided.

#### Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-12.
**Decided:** March 27, 1996.

##### IGRA's binding good-faith adjudication is judicial despite later statutory steps

**Holding:** Adjudication of IGRA's federal good-faith negotiation dispute is a judicial function when its judgment conclusively determines legal relations and activates constrained statutory consequences. Later mediation and executive action, and the absence of a guaranteed compact, do not make that judgment advisory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Stevens's serious doubt and Stone-Zsela's nonjoin supply no additional support.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aetna and Nashville permit conclusive adverse declarations without an immediately executable coercive decree. Brimson supports judicial assistance to administration within its adversarial, conclusive, enforceable-order setting, without creating IGRA contempt power. Ferreira's revisable commissioner process is distinguished. Gordon's jurisdictional disposition supplies no delivered reasoned majority opinion requiring execution in every judicial case; Hayburn produced no Supreme Court merits decision, and its reported circuit communications remain historical materials. Wisconsin's partial-redress rule concerns the judicial capacity to afford meaningful relief. Morgan Stanley and Plaut: Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement of qualifying completed private securities limitations dismissals under §27A(b)'s objectively defined procedure while courts independently determine eligibility and adjudicate the claims. The holdings do not establish an unrestricted power to reopen completed merits judgments and do not determine whether IGRA's conclusive adjudication ceases to be judicial because mediation or executive action follows.

##### A connected officer may be sued prospectively under IGRA within its complete statutory limits

**Holding:** Ex parte Young permits prospective official-capacity relief against Governor Chiles for an ongoing violation of IGRA's good-faith negotiation duty, subject to IGRA's full sequence and remedial limits. The State-directed wording and detailed procedure do not exclude that route in this case; they do not authorize greater remedies through the officer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. No additional general clear-indication theory commands that coalition.

**Material application:** The Governor's representatives negotiated, supplying connection to the alleged violation without establishing that he alone can bind Florida to every term. Mandatory good faith differs from discretion over compact terms, and nonagreement alone does not prove bad faith.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A qualifying request by the Tribe with jurisdiction over the Indian lands triggers §2710(d)(3)(A)'s good-faith duty; suit under (d)(7)(A)(i) may begin only after 180 days. The effective-compact enforcement action under (A)(ii) and Secretary action under (A)(iii) remain separate. Tribal evidence of no compact and failure to respond or respond in good faith precedes the State's good-faith burden under (B)(ii).

An actual adverse finding triggers an order to State and Tribe to conclude a compact within the first sixty days. The court may consider public interest, safety, criminality, financial integrity and adverse economic effects on existing gaming, and shall treat direct-tax demands on the Tribe or Indian lands as evidence of bad faith. Failure to agree during that period requires last-best proposals to a court-appointed mediator, who selects the proposal best comporting with IGRA, other applicable federal law and the findings and order. Submission to both sides begins a second sixty-day period for State consent; consent produces an agreed compact. Nonconsent triggers notice to the Secretary, who shall prescribe procedures in consultation with the Tribe consistently with the selected proposal, IGRA and relevant state law. The findings/order criterion expressly appears in selection under (iv), not verbatim in the Secretary clause (vii).

State nonparticipation does not automatically select the Tribe's proposal. Failure to agree or participate during the ordered period produces statutory progression, not added contempt or an order compelling agreement; every separate obstruction sanction is not decided. Section (d)(4) creates no new taxing authority but preserves agreed regulatory-cost assessments under (d)(3)(C)(iii), separately from direct-tax demands' evidentiary effect. Absence of other taxing authority does not justify refusing negotiation.

Class III gaming still requires approved tribal legislation, a State permitting such gaming for some person, organization or entity, and an effective compact subject to applicable remedial provisions; licensing and regulation remain distinct. Under (d)(8), disapproval may rest on IGRA, other federal law not relating to jurisdiction over gaming on Indian lands, or federal trust obligations. Forty-five-day inaction means approval only insofar as consistent with IGRA, and Federal Register publication remains necessary. Section 2721 severability supplies neither missing predicates nor an alternative program.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical Governor dismissal, 5–4, without finding bad faith or granting a compact, mediator, default proposal, Secretary procedures, damages, game-scope declaration or license. The separate September 22, 1993 merits judgment and stayed appeal No. 93-5256 receive their own lawful treatment and are not vacated. The asserted Secretary substitute remedy, independent administrative authority, complete state-law compacting power, Tenth Amendment defense and mediator-appointments question remain unadjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Young reaches this connected officer and ongoing federal duty without a universal rule for detailed statutes. Edelman's retrospective-compensation boundary remains; prospective costs alone do not establish retrospective relief. Pennhurst is not triggered merely because the federal obligation incorporates state gaming law. Hafer's personal-capacity damages rule does not supply this action. Puerto Rico Aqueduct supplies appealability, not immunity. New York and Voting Rights Coalition do not decide the reserved IGRA constitutional defenses; X-Citement Video preserves appropriate respondent defenses without compelling their resolution here.

##### State amenability lacks a controlling rationale, despite agreement on direct-State statutory intent

**Authority of the judgment:** Florida's immunity-dismissal direction is set aside and remanded by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, 5–4. Stone-Zsela and Stevens would sustain direct-State amenability; Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate without deciding it. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm. No controlling State-amenability rationale exists under Marks: avoidance is not a logical subset of the affirmative constitutional grounds, and three unused conditional alternatives are not actual abrogation joins.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** IGRA unmistakably authorizes an action naming the State through its State-party action, evidentiary burden, State-and-Tribe order and successive State-directed steps. General federal-question jurisdiction, §1362 or scattered State references alone would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Six actual positions across the separate writings: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The other three's conditional acceptance is not counted. This agreement concerns statutory intent alone.

**Current force:** The clear-statement proposition controls; no majority sustains or rejects Florida's constitutional amenability or requires a particular avoidance sequence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State-removal judgment establishes neither jurisdiction nor its absence; lawful jurisdiction is necessary before any State merits adjudication. The State claim is not terminated by immunity, and no abandonment, mootness or indefinite stay is presumed. No express or constructive waiver, reciprocal tribal/state consent or delegated United States immunity exemption establishes this suit. The United States appears only as amicus.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Union Gas is not overruled: its damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion retain their decided force without making every plurality sentence binding. Hans remains effective; separate common-law theories do not become law. Fitzpatrick's distinct Fourteenth Amendment authority is unchanged.

#### Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136; review of 90 F.3d 1237.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question

**Holding:** A federal court cannot assume disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim. Section 11046(c) describes enforcement and relief without making every statutory liability element jurisdictional; the holding preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences and does not prescribe an exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins, including O'Connor and Kennedy's nonexhaustive sequencing reservation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether EPCRA authorizes suit over reporting violations cured before filing remains undecided. Norton and Avrech retain their distinct settings involving already-controlled merits.

##### Requested relief cannot redress this completed informational deprivation

**Holding:** Assuming past informational injury and traceability, Article III requires requested relief likely to repair that injury or prevent a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. When reports were current before suit and no continuing or imminent violation is alleged, a declaration of past lateness, Treasury penalties, monitoring, future report copies and litigation expenses supply no such redress.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support dismissal on statutory grounds without joining this constitutional rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than rejects informational injury. Standing is absent at commencement, rather than lost by later cessation. Deterrent penalties may redress a supported threatened injury; annual reporting duties alone do not allege one. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party costs on a final order, not independent compensation. A properly presented replacement-information loss claim, monitoring in a live action, recoverable prefiling expenses, associational requirements and statutory temporal reach remain open. Section 11022(f) gives the fire department no transferable automatic inspection entitlement for this plaintiff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand with directions to dismiss, unanimously as to judgment. No merits relief, standing discovery, amendment entitlement or newly inferred recurrence inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to distinguish a substantial federal claim from its ultimate success; a contested statutory element is not automatically jurisdictional.
- **Young v. Northern Illinois Conference:** Applied at that same distinction; its constitutional-defense holding is not expanded into permission to assume Article III.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Its controlling standing elements and stage-appropriate support remain governing. Its three-Justice absent-agency application is neither enlarged nor needed.
- **Church of Scientology v. United States:** Meaningful partial relief remains enough; no effectual relief of that kind is available on these allegations.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** Congress's representative-enforcement choice does not eliminate personal injury and likely redress.
- **Bennett v. Spear:** Supported pleading-stage causal and remedial links remain sufficient; its ongoing constraint differs from the completed reporting deprivation here.
- **Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona:** Applied for the live-controversy requirement and limited corrective authority to vacate without reaching merits.
- **Norton v. Mathews; Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech:** Their already-controlled merits and distinct jurisdictional settings remain intact; neither decides this unresolved EPCRA dispute.
- **Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation:** Its different statutory wording and enforcement scheme do not settle the merits question the Court leaves open.
- **Meghrig v. KFC Western:** Its RCRA cause-and-remedy limits remain statute-specific; it supplies no automatic EPCRA current-violation rule.

#### Idaho v. Coeur d’Alene Tribe of Idaho

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1474.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

##### A disputed federal tribal title question does not create a sovereign-lands exception to prospective officer relief

**Holding:** The Eleventh Amendment does not bar a claim for prospective declaratory or injunctive relief against officials actually connected to an alleged ongoing violation of federal tribal property rights merely because adjudicating that violation requires resolving a disputed federal title question or affects important sovereign interests. The decree must address proved unlawful future official conduct and may not bind the immune State's title, award damages or retrospective compensation, execute against state property or require the State itself to convey title.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Material application:** The pleaded land-regulating officials have a concrete connection to the alleged interference; a general law-obedience duty would not suffice. The actual request for exclusive use and exclusion of regulation is acknowledged, but the Ninth Circuit withheld a decree binding Idaho’s title and the case remains at the pleading stage.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither generalized state-interest balancing nor a special sovereign-lands exception bars the route. The Tribe must establish the federal right, ongoing violation and lawful remedy. No ownership, navigability, prestatehood disposition or separate United States action is adjudicated; no general Article I abrogation rule is announced.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm permission for the prospective-officer claims, 5–4; leave the State, agency and direct quiet-title or retrospective-relief dismissals undisturbed, 9–0. Further merits and tailored-remedy proceedings are permitted, without automatic injunction or property transfer.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Young supplies the connected-officer route; Tindal and Treasure Salvors distinguish officer conduct relief from sovereign title. Oneida supplies federal-question character. Edelman, Green and Pennhurst retain retrospective and state-law limits. Seminole’s actual IGRA officer holding is consistent but supplies neither a universal statutory route nor an abrogation rule, and no IGRA sequence is imported.

#### Raines v. Byrd

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1671.

**Decided:** June 26, 1997.

##### Whether individual Members may challenge abstract institutional dilution after fully effective votes

**Holding:** Individual Members of Congress whose votes were counted and given full effect lack Article III standing to challenge the Line Item Veto Act on the asserted abstract dilution of Congress's institutional power and alteration of future votes. Express statutory permission to sue removes the applicable prudential obstacle but cannot supply the missing concrete personal injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes. Souter supplies the seventh judgment vote on a separate ground.

**Material application:** The Members lost votes that were counted and identify no personal injury from a cancellation; neither House authorized them to represent Congress.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical bar to legislator or official injury is established. Personal exclusion, lost salary, nullified decisive votes, an authorized institutional claim satisfying Article III and concrete cancellation injury remain distinct. Legislative remedies create no exhaustion requirement; no independent traceability holding is added.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lujan supplies concrete personal injury, without enlargement of its noncontrolling redressability analysis. Akins's concrete shared informational injury is distinguished. Powell preserves personal exclusion claims; Coleman preserves complete nullification of decisive votes. Bender and Allen support the personal-stake and unauthorized-representation boundaries.

##### What disposition follows from the Members' lack of standing

**Holding:** Because these plaintiffs lack Article III standing, the District Court's constitutional declaration must be vacated and the complaint dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. The Court does not decide the Act's constitutionality, nondelegation, Presentment or the independent ripeness question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes. Souter supplies the seventh judgment vote on a separate ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the District Court's constitutional declaration and remand for jurisdictional dismissal, 7–2. Vacatur neither sustains the Act nor orders a merits remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Steel Co. requires resolution of disputed Article III power before disputed merits in this setting; statutory expedition and direct review do not supply constitutional injury.

#### Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1279.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether §1983 adds a freestanding governmental-power-abuse element for municipal employees

**Holding:** A municipal employee who establishes deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and municipal responsibility need not also prove an independent, freestanding abuse-of-governmental-power element. The existence of the constitutional wrong and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous statutory holding.

**Material application:** The Fifth Circuit’s additional statutory requirement cannot sustain dismissal merely because the city acted as an employer. Its rejection does not establish the underlying federal right or final municipal attribution; attribution is assumed only to resolve the constitutional question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employment alone does not establish municipal liability, and deficient training does not create a substantive constitutional right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The extra statutory element is rejected, but dismissal of the federal claim is affirmed on the separate constitutional grounds below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658, supplies municipal policy and causation requirements distinct from employment alone. *Canton v. Harris*, 489 U.S. 378, is clarified as an attribution doctrine, not a source of the underlying substantive right. *Oklahoma City v. Tuttle*, 471 U.S. 808, preserves the distinction between constitutional wrong and municipal responsibility.

##### Whether voluntary municipal employment or these pleaded safety omissions establish a substantive-due-process deprivation

**Holding:** The substantive Due Process Clause does not impose an independent general obligation on a municipality to provide its voluntary employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks of harm. Accepting public employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous duty limitation.

**Material application:** The complaint alleges known hazards and deliberate failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers. Those allegations are accepted at dismissal; they do not make voluntary employment a custodial restraint.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not excuse violation of other applicable federal rights. Actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury, a different properly pleaded affirmative-danger claim and procedural due process are not decided. State safety and tort remedies are not adjudicated, and knowing allegations are not reduced to negligence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal substantive-due-process claim. No federal compensation or supervision order is entered; independently available state-law remedies retain their own merits and procedural requirements.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services*, 489 U.S. 189, supplies the absence of a general protective duty while leaving distinct custody and affirmative-deprivation questions. *Youngberg v. Romeo*, 457 U.S. 307, is distinguished because voluntary employment is not involuntary custody. *Daniels v. Williams*, 474 U.S. 327, preserves the distinction between constitutional due process and ordinary tort law without redescribing these allegations as negligence. *Bishop v. Wood*, 426 U.S. 341, rejects a general constitutional guarantee against employment mistakes.

**Independent alternative holding:** The pleaded municipal failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers against known hazards do not constitute arbitrary or conscience-shocking deprivation under substantive due process in this voluntary-employment setting. Even assuming the Texas safety statute creates the asserted duties and a protected liberty interest, breach alone does not establish that distinct substantive constitutional wrong.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part C of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices control this alternative rejection. Stone’s agreement in the judgment does not join Part C.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** No deliberate bodily injury or direction knowingly sending Collins into a dangerous sewer is alleged. The public-program rationale recognizes competing social, political and economic choices as a legal presumption; it is not a finding that the city acted responsibly or that its budget justified these omissions. The alternative independently supports affirmance without deciding state remedies or a different affirmative-injury claim. *Rochin v. California*, 342 U.S. 165, is distinguished from these protective omissions. *Bishop*, 426 U.S. 341, and *Daniels*, 474 U.S. 327, preserve the distinction between state employment or tort duties and substantive constitutional arbitrariness. *Harrah Independent School District v. Martin*, 440 U.S. 194, distinguishes an assumed state-created interest from arbitrary deprivation.

#### Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-7247.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### Actual awareness and unreasonable disregard of substantial danger

**Holding:** A prison official violates the Eighth Amendment by disregarding a substantial risk of serious harm only if the official knows facts indicating that risk, actually appreciates the risk, and fails to respond reasonably. A danger that an official merely should have discovered does not establish deliberate indifference; an official who responds reasonably is not liable under this rule even if harm occurs.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this rule; Thomas joins only the judgment.

**Material application:** The official need not foresee the precise attacker, timing or mechanism when the known danger encompasses the prisoner's situation. No defendant is found to have the required knowledge, responsibility or causal role; reasonable but unsuccessful protection remains outside deliberate indifference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: applies its distinction between deliberate indifference and negligence without reopening the accepted conditions framework.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its separate objective and subjective requirements and specifies actual-awareness recklessness for this failure-to-protect claim.
- Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517: applies its recognition of custodial protection duties; it supplies no automatic liability from an assault.
- Hudson v. McMillian, February 25, 1992: preserves the nontrivial malicious-beating holding and leaves its unresolved broader force standards untouched; no universal malicious-and-sadistic requirement enters this conditions claim.

##### Circumstantial proof and reconsideration of summary judgment

**Holding:** Actual awareness may be proved by ordinary circumstantial evidence, including an obvious risk, and does not require an express warning by the prisoner. Obviousness permits but does not compel an inference: a genuine failure to appreciate the risk defeats the knowledge element, whereas deliberately refusing to confirm strongly suspected facts or their obvious inference may establish awareness; summary judgment here must be reconsidered without treating Farmer's silence as dispositive.

**Proposition-level authority:** The proof-and-remand portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight adopt its reasoning; all nine support vacatur and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The transfer officials may contest their knowledge, responsibility, causation and reasonable response. The Court does not combine different defendants' innocent information into collective knowledge, find the alleged assault proved, or decide qualified immunity. The event-date rule is Rule 56(f).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its actual culpability requirement without making a prisoner warning an additional element.
- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: preserves the negligence boundary while allowing ordinary evidence of deliberate indifference.

##### Current danger governs preventive relief

**Holding:** Prospective protection requires an established current substantial danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse that persists through the litigation and is likely to continue. The court may consider later developments bearing on present danger and knowledge; litigation notice cannot create past damages liability, and officials may defeat prospective relief by showing that the danger or culpable response has ceased and will not recur.

**Proposition-level authority:** The prospective-relief portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this rule and the bounded remand.

**Current-force modification:** The constitutional liability rule is unchanged. PLRA §802 now separately conditions prospective relief in a federal-law civil prison-conditions action, excluding habeas challenges to the fact or duration of confinement: the court must find the relief narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct the particular plaintiff's federal violation and the least intrusive necessary means, giving substantial weight to adverse public-safety or criminal-justice effects. Relief requiring or permitting officials to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violating state or local law, additionally requires that federal law permit that contrary relief, that it be necessary to correct the federal violation and that no other remedy correct the violation; the statute authorizes neither prison construction nor tax increases. Section 802(b)(1) applies amended §3626 to prospective relief granted before, on or after enactment. No existing order is terminated by admission, and the original constitutional holding does not alone establish these additional statutory findings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Terre Haute warden and Bureau Director were sued only in official capacities; the Oxford warden, case manager and two regional officials faced both-capacity claims. Equitable discretion and appropriate internal grievance procedures are not converted into a new mandatory exhaustion holding. No later statutory exhaustion regime is applied.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Helling v. McKinney, June 18, 1993: applies preventive protection against serious future harm and its direction to examine current conditions.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: retains the separate culpability requirement for the threatened conditions violation.
- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: preserves the presented individual-officer damages channel without creating a Treasury claim.
- Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14: preserves the individual-officer Eighth Amendment damages source; damages entitlement remains unproved.
- FDIC v. Meyer, February 23, 1994: applies the distinction between an individual claim and an unavailable direct agency constitutional damages action.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: preserves review of immunity using all relevant law existing when the conduct occurred; this decision supplies no retroactive notice in 1989.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for reconsideration of the Rule 56(f) request and summary judgment without an express-warning prerequisite. Discovery may be allowed or denied on a proper basis, and renewed summary judgment remains available. Knowledge, responsibility, causation, reasonable response, capacities and immunity remain individualized. No trial, damages or placement injunction is ordered; prospective relief must fit any established continuing danger.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §802, additional §3626(a)(1) findings and express prospective-relief transition. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

#### Doe v. Taylor Independent School District

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 90-8431; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** October 16, 1995.

##### Knowing, causally effective supervisory acquiescence in sexual abuse through school authority

**Holding:** A school supervisor violates a pupil's bodily-integrity right by actually knowing of sexual abuse through school authority or a substantial risk of it, deliberately and unreasonably disregarding the known danger despite authority and a reasonable opportunity to protect, and thereby causing injury or its continuation. A reasonable response is not actionable merely because injury occurs; awareness may be proved circumstantially, but warning signs do not compel it and what the official merely should have known is insufficient. The properly assumed Lankford facts describe this personal knowing acquiescence, for which preconduct law gave fair notice, so immunity does not bar this claim at this stage.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five direct votes.

**Material application:** The teacher's exploitation of official authority and access supplies the governmental connection although some acts occurred away from school. The alleged warnings and culpable nonresponse are assumed for legal review; earlier contact, later intercourse and continuing abuse remain distinct for causation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Negligent failure to discover misconduct, a reporting-law breach alone, supervisory office or vicarious liability does not suffice. No communicated approval, actual liability, damages, district liability, separate Title IX claim or additional equal-protection merits ruling is established. May 1987 authorities cannot give retroactive notice for earlier omissions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm Lankford's immunity denial, 5–4, and return the surviving claim for proof of actual awareness, culpable inaction and attributable injury.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ingraham supplies bodily security; Monell and Rizzo preserve personal attribution and causal linkage. Hafer confirms individual responsibility; Elder requires all relevant conduct-date law. Collins's voluntary-employment omissions do not decide knowing participation in deliberate bodily abuse. Farmer is an actual-awareness analogy, not retroactive notice. Albright supplies no controlling general rule displacing this claim. The earlier supervisory cases inform the specific notice inquiry without becoming interchangeable general duties; Jefferson and Lopez's later dates and distinct direct-action/supervisory settings limit their use.

##### Caplinger's immunity on the distinct particularized notice question

**Holding:** Caplinger receives qualified immunity because conduct-time law did not clearly establish that his particular supervisory conduct, on his own assumed information and responses, violated Doe's federal right. The Court neither decides that conduct's constitutional merits nor imputes Lankford's fuller knowledge to him.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela concurs only in the judgment on a separate merits ground; that ground is not controlling.

**Material application:** Caplinger had limited later information and undertook inquiries, an interview at which Doe denied sex, and warnings; a promised wider meeting did not occur. These circumstances are distinct from Lankford's alleged knowing acquiescence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No constitutional approval of Caplinger's response, broad school-official immunity, mandatory merits-first sequence, or resolution of the conflicting January/February notice descriptions follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm Caplinger's immunity, unanimous in judgment. The legal/factual appellate boundary is separately recorded under Federal Courts, Doe, October 16, 1995, Fifth Circuit No. 90-8431.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Elder and Hafer require individualized conduct-date examination. The May 1987 Jefferson and Lopez decisions neither supply earlier notice nor equate the superintendent's circumstances with the principal's.

#### Pinder v. Johnson

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 93-2125; no Supreme Court docket stated in the Record
**Decided:** May 21, 1996

##### Do protective assurances or custody of a private assailant establish a custodial duty to protect the threatened family?

**Holding:** Official knowledge of danger, promises of protection, and custody of the private assailant do not establish a custodial special relationship with a mother or her children. An alleged affirmative deprivation of otherwise available private protection remains analytically distinct from an omission of public protection; absence of custody of the threatened persons does not itself resolve that different claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale. Part I's separate appellate holding appears under Federal Courts, Qualified-immunity appeals and presentation.

**Controlling explanation:** DeShaney rejects a protection duty founded merely on official knowledge and assurances, but its discussion of increased vulnerability does not fully resolve this alleged affirmative course of conduct. The substantive-due-process inquiry concerns the official's own deprivation and the culpability attached to that deprivation, not simply the making of a statement. Daniels excludes mere negligence; the Court does not decide its reserved questions about recklessness or gross negligence. Pinder alleged that Johnson's assurance induced her to return to work rather than provide or arrange private protection. That favorable account must remain distinct from a general right to police protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court establishes no general constitutional duty to detain an assailant, warn a victim, or fulfill every protective promise. It does not hold that custody is indispensable to every affirmative-danger claim, or decide the constitutional merits of the assumed affirmative-deprivation theory.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- DeShaney rejects knowledge-and-promise protection duties without deciding this entire affirmative-increased-vulnerability theory.
- Daniels excludes merely negligent deprivation while leaving its reserved culpability questions unresolved here.

##### Was Johnson's alleged conduct sufficiently established as unconstitutional by March 10, 1989 to defeat qualified immunity?

**Holding:** Qualified immunity turns on whether all relevant law existing when the officer acted made the particular conduct's unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable officer, without demanding a factual twin. On the favorable allegations here, law existing March 10, 1989 did not sufficiently establish that this noncoercive undertaking, release of the assailant, and uncorrected exposure constituted Johnson's own substantive-due-process deprivation; he therefore receives individual immunity on the reviewed claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II's notice standard joined by all nine Justices; Part III's application and affirmance joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, a five-Justice majority. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent from the immunity disposition; their substantive theory is not controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not identical prior facts, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant law. DeShaney had rejected an omission-based duty while leaving the distinct affirmative theory incomplete. Bowers discussed active official wrongdoing but rejected release-based liability; Archie discussed obstruction of private aid in a materially different culpability setting and reached an adverse outcome; Jensen's discussion of a distinct danger did not preserve a knowledge-and-promise special relationship after DeShaney. Those sources did not sufficiently identify the assumed noncoercive interference here as the officer's own deprivation. A judge's disagreement, the number of judges on each side, or an officer's subjective belief in legality does not independently establish immunity. Later cases cannot retroactively supply the missing conduct-time notice.

**Material application:** The Court assumes that the assurance caused the mother to return to work and forgo alternative protection and that the remaining favorable allegations are true. It does not award immunity because actual reliance was absent, choose disputed facts against Pinder, or reject constitutional liability on the merits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is notice-specific and claim-specific. Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets do not supply retroactive notice; Love establishes no mandatory merits-first sequence. Neither broader affirmative-danger liability nor the ultimate culpability and causation merits is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's reviewed individual-immunity judgment for Johnson is affirmed, 5-4. No new constitutional merits judgment or damages award is entered. Official-capacity and municipal claims, equal protection, section 1985, state wrongful-death and survival claims, and other excluded matters remain outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Anderson supplies the objective, particularized notice inquiry without an exact-facts requirement.
- Elder requires review of all relevant contemporaneous authority rather than an isolated citation or subjective belief.
- DeShaney supplies the rejection of a general protective duty but does not itself resolve the assumed affirmative-deprivation merits.
- Bowers, Archie and Jensen are considered as conduct-time circuit-law notice sources; their distinct facts, outcomes and culpability limits are preserved, and they are not converted into Supreme Court holdings.
- Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets cannot establish notice for conduct preceding those decisions; Love does not impose mandatory sequencing.

#### Uhlrig v. Harder

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190.
**Decided:** February 18, 1997.

##### Conduct-time law did not sufficiently identify these administrators' acts as their own constitutional deprivation

**Holding:** Individual qualified immunity applies unless all relevant law existing when each official acted made the particular conduct's federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official; no identical prior case is required. Even accepting the estate's supported favorable knowledge and causal account, law at these administrators' 1991 acts, no later than February 23, 1992, did not sufficiently establish that the challenged hospital restructuring, followed by clinical placement and the reported warnings and training, constituted each administrator's own substantive-due-process deprivation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight votes. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a different, noncontrolling ground.

**Material application:** The favorable summary-judgment account includes knowing closure of a protective unit despite serious risk to an identified staff group. It does not merge each administrator's conduct with clinical placement decisions; warnings and professional procedures bear on the legal analogy, not findings of reasonable care, no awareness or no causation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The affirmative-danger merits, culpability standard and Tenth Circuit five-part test remain open. Neither circuit disagreement nor the absence of identical facts alone establishes immunity. Collins, Elder and Pinder cannot supply notice before their dates. Official-capacity, survivor-personal and state-tort claims are outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Unanimous affirmance ends the reviewed individual-capacity damages claim without further factfinding or trial; only the eight-Justice immunity rationale controls.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow and Anderson supply objective, particularized notice without requiring a factual twin. Elder requires all contemporaneous authority and no mandatory merits-first sequence. Pinder separates affirmative deprivation from nonprotection but does not dictate the result for different conduct dates. Collins, DeShaney and Daniels retain their bounded workplace, protection-duty and negligence holdings; the considered circuit danger decisions do not become Supreme Court rules.

#### United States v. Lanier

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1717.
**Decided:** March 31, 1997.

##### What law must supply fair warning under 18 U.S.C. §242

**Holding:** Section 242, read with authoritative constructions, must have made the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably apparent when the defendant acted. Neither a later decision nor willful bad purpose can supply missing conduct-time notice, and novel or unforeseeable criminal enlargement is forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Moral reprehensibility and state criminality do not replace a definite federal right and notice. Vagueness, lenity, and the bar on unexpected retrospective enlargement retain related but distinct functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* supplies definite-right and separate willfulness requirements; courts construe the criminal statute rather than create common-law offenses.

##### Whether notice requires a Supreme Court decision on fundamentally similar facts

**Holding:** Fair warning may come from relevant pre-existing lower-court authority and from an obviously covered application of an established rule without a factual twin. Neither a Supreme-Court-only requirement nor a fundamentally-similar-facts prerequisite is lawful; conversely, an abstract liberty or bodily-security proposition does not settle an uncertain application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** One lower-court decision or a fixed numerical consensus is not automatically sufficient. Actual conflicts, authority, particular context, and express reservations matter. No count's constitutional basis, notice, personal responsibility, or official nexus is resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Elder* supplies examination of all relevant existing authority. *Anderson* requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not repeated factual identity. *Pinder* and *Uhlrig* preserve unsettled-application and attribution limits; later holdings cannot supply earlier notice.

##### Whether the civil clarity standard substitutes for criminal elements

**Holding:** Criminal fair warning under §242 and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: pre-existing law must make the constitutional unlawfulness apparent. The common threshold does not displace proof of deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the criminal burden of proof, or any other statutory element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Civil immunity analysis cannot itself convict, and judicial office alone does not prove that every act occurred under color of law. No categorically stricter criminal clarity standard is adopted.

##### Whether due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody are categorically excluded

**Holding:** Appellate review may not exclude due-process rights from §242 categorically or treat the victim's lack of custody as automatic immunity for an official's own assault. Where a specific constitutional provision covers the claim, that provision governs; the Fourth and Eighth Amendments do not exhaust every potentially covered abusive official act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the en banc judgment and remand for count-specific consideration under the proper conduct-time notice rule and all remaining properly presented issues. The mandate does not reinstate convictions or sentence, order acquittal or retrial, or require a new evidentiary hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal constitutional-assault formula or factual assault finding is made. Constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation, omitted protection, and supervisory attribution remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* and *Price* preserve §242's due-process reach. *DeShaney*'s private-violence rule does not immunize direct official wrongdoing. *Graham* supplies the specific-provision rule. *Collins*, *Doe v. Taylor*, *Pinder*, *Uhlrig*, and *Nabozny* retain their distinct deprivation and attribution boundaries, without supplying notice retrospectively.

### Exclusive interstate jurisdiction and private boundary-related claims

**Governing question:** What instructions govern exclusive interstate jurisdiction and private boundary-related claims?

**Current rule:** Under §1251(a), a federal district court cannot grant relief on an actual controversy between two States, even if joined to private litigation or this Court previously declined an original action. Such a complaint must be dismissed for want of jurisdiction. An otherwise lawful private-title action may determine a boundary incidentally without binding the States on their sovereign boundary; a State's intervention seeking no relief against another State is not barred on that ground. Original-forum discretion does not transfer exclusive jurisdiction to another court.

**Authority by component:**

- An interstate complaint belongs to the exclusive original forum: Mississippi v. Louisiana — An interstate complaint belongs to the exclusive original forum, 1992-12-14. Stone-Zsela’s opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; unanimous controlling holding.
- Private title questions remain distinct from binding interstate relief: Mississippi v. Louisiana — Private title questions remain distinct from binding interstate relief, 1992-12-14. Stone-Zsela’s opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; unanimous controlling holding and remand limitation.

**Present operation effective:** 1992-12-14; Mississippi v. Louisiana, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Mississippi v. Louisiana, 506 U.S. 73 (1992).

### State immunity of bistate compact entities

**Governing question:** When a bistate compact entity shares its constituent States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity.

**Current rule:** Exercise of governmental power alone does not confer the States’ immunity. The entity’s actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must give good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred. Exposure of the state treasuries is highly probative but not categorically indispensable; a separate, self-supporting enterprise responsible for its own obligations cannot claim immunity merely from its public functions.

**Authority by component:**

- PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity: PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity, 1994-11-14. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly support this entity-specific rule and its application to both employee actions. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent; no fragmented-rationale analysis is needed.

**Present operation effective:** 1994-11-14; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This compact-entity inquiry does not establish a universal sole-treasury test, decide abrogation or consent, or equate immunity with governmental status for constitutional-rights purposes.

**Holdings navigation:** PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity.

### Qualifying State counsel systems for special capital habeas procedures

**Governing question:** Conditions for chapter 154 and its separate unitary-review route.

**Current rule:** Chapter 154 applies to state-custody §2254 capital cases only when the State satisfies its counsel-system conditions. Under §2261(b)–(c), a statute, highest-court rule or other state-authorized agency mechanism must provide competent appointed counsel, compensation and reasonable litigation expenses for indigent capital postconviction review after the conviction and sentence are upheld on direct appeal to the State’s court of last resort or otherwise become final for State-law purposes, with appointment-competency standards supplied by statute or court rule. The mechanism must offer counsel to all prisoners under capital sentence and require a court-of-record order appointing counsel for an indigent prisoner who accepts or cannot competently choose, finding an informed rejection after a hearing if necessary, or denying appointment on a nonindigency finding. Trial or direct-appeal counsel cannot serve unless both prisoner and counsel expressly request continuity. Under §2261(e), state/federal collateral counsel ineffectiveness or incompetence is no §2254 relief ground but does not bar replacing counsel on the court’s own or prisoner’s motion at any postconviction stage. Section 2265 separately qualifies a unitary-review system permitting collateral claims during direct review: a statute or highest-court rule must provide competent counsel, compensation and reasonable expenses including collateral-claim litigation, and the statute or highest-court rule must prescribe appointment-competency standards; an offer and §2261(c)-type order follow trial, and trial counsel cannot continue absent both parties’ express request. For a qualifying unitary procedure, §§2262–2264 and 2266 apply with direct/postconviction references read as unitary review. If the transcript is unavailable at the posttrial representation order, the 180-day filing period starts only when a transcript is supplied to prisoner or counsel.

**Authority by component:**

- Qualification, representation, unitary route and transcript deferral: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §107(a), 28 U.S.C. §§2261, 2265, enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

**Present operation effective:** AEDPA enactment, 1996-04-24.

**Effective transition:** Under §107(c), chapter 154 applies to cases pending on or after April 24, 1996, subject to its own qualification conditions. Enactment establishes no State’s qualification, particular missed deadline, waiver, execution, stay or entitlement.

### Capital habeas filing and claim scope under chapter 154

**Governing question:** Filing time, tolling and permissible claims when the special State system qualifies.

**Current rule:** A chapter 154 §2254 application must be filed in the appropriate district court within 180 days after final state direct-review affirmance of conviction and sentence or expiration of time to seek that review. The period is tolled from filing to disposition of a Supreme Court certiorari petition seeking review of the final state direct-review capital judgment, from filing the first state postconviction/collateral petition to its final state disposition, and for an additional period no longer than thirty days upon motion in the prospective federal district court and good cause for failing to file within the period. Under §2264, the district court considers only claims raised and decided on the merits in state court unless proper presentation failed because of unconstitutional or unlawful State action, a newly recognized Supreme Court federal right made retroactive, or a factual predicate not discoverable by due diligence in time for state or federal collateral review. It must rule on properly presented claims subject to §2254(a), (d) and (e).

**Authority by component:**

- 180-day period, distinct tolling clauses and claim-scope exceptions: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §107(a), 28 U.S.C. §§2263–2264, enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

**Present operation effective:** AEDPA enactment, 1996-04-24.

**Effective transition:** Under §107(c), chapter 154 applies to cases pending on or after April 24, 1996, subject to its own qualification conditions. Enactment establishes no State’s qualification, particular missed deadline, waiver, execution, stay or entitlement.

**Related current rules:** “Qualifying State counsel systems for special capital habeas procedures” supplies unitary-review substitutions and transcript-based deferral. Chapter 153’s one-year period and exceptions are not substituted for these distinct provisions.

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

**Holdings navigation:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. (1997-06-02) — Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question.

### Individual legislator standing for institutional dilution

**Governing question:** Whether individual legislators may challenge abstract dilution of institutional legislative power after fully effective votes.

**Current rule:** Members whose votes were counted and given full legal effect lack Article III injury on a claim of abstract institutional-power dilution or altered force of future votes. Express statutory permission to sue may remove prudential restrictions but cannot supply personal concrete injury. Losing a fully effective vote differs from complete nullification of votes sufficient to determine an outcome or a personal deprivation of office or pay.

**Authority by component:**
- Personal/institutional boundary and statutory authorization: Raines, Raines v. Byrd — I. These individual legislators lack a concrete personal injury, Scalia with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg (six), 1997-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** Raines, 1997-06-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal ban on official or legislator litigation, legislative-exhaustion requirement or categorical rule against an authorized institutional suit is adopted. Concrete cancellation injury and complete vote nullification retain their distinct Article III questions.

### Voluntary public employment and substantive workplace safety duties

**Governing question:** Whether a voluntary public-employment relationship creates a general substantive-due-process duty to provide workplace safety.

**Current rule:** Voluntary municipal employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care, and substantive due process imposes no independent general obligation to provide such employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks. A state-law safety duty, even assuming it creates a protected liberty interest, does not convert its breach alone into a substantive constitutional wrong. The underlying federal right and constitutional arbitrariness remain distinct from state-law entitlement and municipal attribution.

**Authority by component:**

- No employment-based general safety duty or custody: Collins v. City of Harker Heights — Voluntary public employment creates no general constitutional safety duty, February 26, 1992; Part B of Stevens's unanimous opinion.
- State entitlement does not alone establish arbitrary deprivation: Collins — These pleaded omissions do not establish constitutional arbitrariness, February 26, 1992; Part C joined by White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas with Stevens. Stone's distinct concurrence is not part of this component's authority.

**Present operation effective:** *Collins*, February 26, 1992.

**Open question:** Actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury, procedural due process, and a properly alleged affirmative-danger claim are not resolved. *Collins* creates neither a general affirmative-danger test nor a categorical rejection of one.

**Holdings navigation:** Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992) — Municipal liability and public employment.

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. ### Serious risks in prison conditions and deliberate indifference

**Governing question:** What objective danger, official culpability and response govern preventive prison-conditions claims and failure-to-protect liability.

**Current rule:** A prisoner may establish an Eighth Amendment violation through deliberate indifference to involuntary exposure posing an unreasonable risk of serious future harm without waiting for existing disease. For future-health exposure, the objective inquiry includes the seriousness and likelihood of harm and contemporary society’s unwillingness to tolerate the risk. For a substantial danger to inmate safety, an official must actually know facts indicating the risk, actually draw the inference and disregard it unreasonably. Constructive knowledge or negligence is insufficient. Reasonable protective action defeats deliberate-indifference liability even if harm occurs. Actual awareness may be proved circumstantially, including from an obvious risk, but obviousness permits rather than compels the inference. Deliberate avoidance of confirmation may support actual awareness; a specific prisoner warning, identification of the exact assailant or prediction of the precise time is unnecessary. Knowledge cannot be imputed collectively without proof of the individual official’s awareness. Prospective relief considers current danger and awareness through the litigation and whether unlawful disregard is likely to continue; an adequate showing of nonrecurrence may defeat that relief. Later notice can support prospective protection but does not retroactively establish awareness for damages.

**Authority by component:**

- Preventive protection does not require existing disease: Helling v. McKinney — Preventive protection does not require existing disease, 1993-06-18. White’s Opinion of the Court, Preventive protection does not require existing disease portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

- Actual-awareness standard, circumstantial proof, reasonable response and preventive relief: Farmer v. Brennan — Actual awareness and unreasonable disregard of substantial danger; Circumstantial proof and reconsideration of summary judgment; Current danger governs preventive relief, 1994-06-06. Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this rule; Thomas joins only the judgment. The proof-and-remand portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight adopt its reasoning; all nine support vacatur and remand. The prospective-relief portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this rule and the bounded remand.

**Present operation effective:** Farmer v. Brennan, 1994-06-06, for subjective culpability, proof and preventive operation; Helling’s objective future-health inquiry remains effective from 1993-06-18.

**Holdings navigation:** Helling v. McKinney, 509 U.S. 25 (1993); Actual awareness and unreasonable disregard of substantial danger; Circumstantial proof and reconsideration of summary judgment; Current danger governs preventive relief.

### 1998-02-25 — Alaska v. Native Village of Venetie Tribal Government

[Public Projection](../records/alaska_v_native_village_of_venetie_tribal_government_merits_1998-02-25.md#public-projection).

#### Event

Alaska v. Native Village of Venetie Tribal Government, No. 96-1577. Plenary merits decision, February 25, 1998; argued December 10, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Ninth Circuit, 101 F.3d 1286. Review concerns whether the former Venetie Reservation lands conveyed under the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act are a dependent Indian community under 18 U.S.C. §1151(b), supplying the territorial authority asserted for the nonmember school-construction tax. The District Court rejected that classification in Alaska’s action to enjoin collection; the Court of Appeals reversed.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate. No Justice is recused.

#### Public Action

The judgment of the Ninth Circuit is reversed.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Reverse, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lands at issue lack dependent-community status. The asserted territorial basis for the approximately $161,000 nonmember construction tax therefore fails. Further proceedings below must conform to that ruling; no new hearing, title adjudication or region-wide jurisdictional decree is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt both holdings and the bounded disposition. No separate writing issues.

#### Holdings

##### Dependent-community status requires a federal land relationship

**Holding and operative rule:** A dependent Indian community under 18 U.S.C. §1151(b) requires land set aside by the Federal Government for Indian use and federal superintendence over that community’s land relationship. Tribal recognition, Indian ownership and federal assistance to residents do not independently supply those territorial predicates.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this rule; no fractured-opinion synthesis is involved.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reads the statutory category through Sandoval, which recognizes federal protection and supervision of Pueblo lands despite fee ownership, and McGowan, which treats a federal Indian-use set-aside under continuing supervision as Indian country. The rule does not require federal fee title, a reservation label or a prescribed administrative form. It does require the federal relationship those cases describe. Political dependence and territorial jurisdiction are distinct: Cherokee Nation and Worcester do not make every parcel owned by a federally recognized tribe Indian country. The Ninth Circuit’s six-factor approach cannot excuse the absence of either statutory predicate by balancing cohesiveness, government services and general dependence. Hagen and Yankton Sioux preserve the difference between title and territory; neither opening land nor fee ownership alone determines status.

##### ANCSA removed both predicates from these lands

**Holding and operative rule:** These former-reservation lands, conveyed in unrestricted fee after ANCSA expressly revoked the reservation and without continuing federal supervision of their use or disposition, are not a dependent Indian community. The Tribe’s asserted territorial authority to tax this nonmember construction consequently fails.

**Authority:** All nine join the Court’s complete statutory construction, application and geographic limit.

**Controlling explanation:** ANCSA §1618(a) expressly revoked this reservation, §1603 extinguished aboriginal land claims, and the settlement authorized fee conveyances without continuing federal restrictions on transfer. The corporations’ §1618(b) election selected former-reservation land instead of monetary and other land benefits; it did not preserve the revoked reservation or impose a new Indian-use restriction. Their later conveyance to the Tribe likewise did not recreate a federal set-aside. Section 1636(d) protects land against adverse possession, real-property taxes and certain judgments while it remains unsold, unleased and undeveloped. Those conditional asset protections do not require federal approval of ordinary transfers or federal supervision of land use. Health, welfare, social and development programs benefit people without supplying the missing territorial supervision. The Tribe’s self-determination argument cannot substitute political recognition for the land relationship Congress ended. No allotment is at issue. The Court leaves other Native parcels, trust land, tribal identity, benefits, member-based powers and independently established consensual authority untouched.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Cherokee Nation and Worcester do not convert recognized political dependence into territorial jurisdiction over every tribal-owned parcel.

### 1998-05-18 — Montana v. Crow Tribe of Indians, No. 96-1829.

[Authoritative Public Projection](../records/montana_v_crow_tribe_of_indians_merits_1998-05-18.md#public-projection).

#### Event

Montana v. Crow Tribe of Indians, No. 96-1829. Argued January 14, 1998; decided May 18, 1998. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 92 F.3d 826, which required transfer to the Tribe of the entire disputed state tax collection from Westmoreland.

The state taxes on coal from the Tribe's ceded-strip minerals were held invalid. The present dispute concerns approximately $46.8 million in severance taxes collected during 1975–1982 and $11.4 million in gross-proceeds taxes during 1975–1987, before the respective registry deposits. The question is whether the established invalidity requires payment of all those receipts to the Tribe, rather than an inquiry into its entitlement and the proper equitable remedy.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

#### Public Action

The Court reverses the mandatory whole-collection award and remands for proceedings consistent with the distinction between tax invalidity and the Tribe's entitlement to restitution.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

No amount is awarded. The invalidity judgment and completed registry distributions remain undisturbed. Proper consideration of an independent partial restitution claim remains open; the decision orders neither a new compensatory award nor an automatic reopening of evidence or allowance of amendment.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Common rejection of automatic total recovery and express preservation of properly supported independent partial relief |
| Concurrence | Souter | O'Connor | Existing prayer encompasses partial restitution of excessive receipts; explains the additional property-based grounds for genuine consideration of that claim |

#### Holdings

##### 1. Invalidity of these taxes does not establish entitlement to every receipt

**Holding and operative rule:** An invalid state tax does not automatically entitle a tribe to all sums a nontribal taxpayer paid. The claimant must establish entitlement to the particular fund or remedy; lawful tribal taxing authority and a protected interest in the taxed resource do not alone transfer ownership of the whole state collection.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined in full by all eight Associate Justices. That coalition controls both holdings; the additional partial-restitution framework in Souter's concurrence is not adopted by the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The tax-invalidity judgment remains binding. Cotton Petroleum nevertheless distinguishes concurrent state and tribal taxing authority from taxation that unlawfully burdens the tribal interest. Rejection of these excessive taxes therefore does not establish that every possible state levy would have been unlawful or that the Tribe owns every dollar collected.

California separates the taxpayer's claim, economic burden and a different claimant's direct entitlement. Fulton and Reich require lawful correction in their proper claimant and procedural settings; neither commands payment of every invalid tax to any economically affected entity. Chickasaw Nation identifies statutory tax incidence but supplies no universal award of receipts to a different claimant.

The Tribe's resource interest and the United States' trustee role matter, but they do not prove the particular total award. Who paid, who bore the relevant loss, the actual agreements and preserved claims, and prior distributions must be examined. The taxpayer's failed or relinquished refund opportunity is not automatically the Tribe's claim or a release of it. Conversely, labeling the State's gain unlawful does not establish the amount belonging to this claimant. No compensatory measure is pleaded and proved for an appellate award here.

**Precedent treatment:** The earlier tax-invalidity judgment remains intact; Cotton Petroleum preserves concurrent taxation; California, Fulton, Reich and Chickasaw retain their claimant-specific and remedial limits.

##### 2. Equitable analysis must use the actual legal and procedural interests

**Holding and operative rule:** The remand must distinguish lawful tribal tax coverage, actual payment arrangements, independent claims against the State and funds already distributed. None of the approval dispute, taxpayer refund history or Tribe-taxpayer agreement categorically extinguishes a properly supported independent tribal claim on this record; further relief must follow its lawful basis and proven scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The 1988 ruling treated Interior's original reservation-tax approval as covering the ceded-strip minerals. Its contrary exclusion was legally mistaken. That ruling distributed registry funds under the 1982 agreement; it did not determine Westmoreland's liability before that agreement. Actual refusal to approve, the company's pass-through difficulty and its unwillingness to pay remain relevant practical facts, but they cannot be recast as a conclusive legal absence of tribal authority.

The United States intervened in June 1983. The severance and gross-proceeds taxes entered the registry at different times, and the completed distributions must be considered without awarding the same benefit twice. Neither the agreement's treatment of Westmoreland nor its refund difficulties establish a tribal release of the separate claim against Montana.

The contrary full-recovery theory neglects these distinctions; a categorical no-recovery theory would do the same. Proper consideration of independent partial restitution remains open in this action. The district court may take a lawful course under the Federal Rules, but this decision does not compel reopening proof, allowing an amendment, measuring compensatory injury or paying a fixed lesser sum. Equity must explain entitlement; it may not become an unexamined excuse to retain an unlawful gain.

**Precedent treatment:** California's distinction between derivative and independent entitlements governs without barring a properly established direct tribal claim; the earlier distribution ruling retains its actual, limited scope.

Established tax invalidity does not itself establish a tribe's right to the entire collection from another taxpayer. The court must examine the claimant, fund, agreements, prior distributions and lawful theory of relief. A properly supported independent partial claim remains open; neither a total award nor a complete bar follows automatically.

Interior's legally erroneous refusal and the agreement with Westmoreland do not dispose of that independent claim against the State. A dollar-for-dollar compensatory-loss measure is not the only possible basis for restitution of an improperly obtained benefit.

He would require genuine consideration of that existing claim: determine the excessive share, consider registry distributions and the remaining equities, and decide whether all, some or none of that share should be restored. This does not permit treating every state tax dollar as excessive or establish entitlement to an additional penny. The concurrence joins the Court's bounded opinion because it leaves that inquiry substantively open; its affirmative restitution framework goes beyond the Court's common holding.

#### Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit's compulsory transfer of the whole preregistry collection is reversed. Further district-court proceedings must respect the preserved independent claim and the actual scope of existing rulings and agreements. No refund claim is revived for Westmoreland, no registry distribution is disturbed, and no additional amount, amendment or evidentiary proceeding is directed.
