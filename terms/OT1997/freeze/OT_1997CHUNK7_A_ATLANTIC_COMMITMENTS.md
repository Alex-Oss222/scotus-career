## Common committed propositions

1. Affirm the Third Circuit. Section 1023(e)(3)(B) does not unambiguously confine reserve strengthening to increases caused by changed computation methods or assumptions. Treasury has authority to implement the transition, and its specified net-addition construction is a permissible interpretation of the undefined statutory term in this particular fresh-start exclusion. The commitment is to that statutory-authority and Chevron ground; it does not require an independently sufficient judicial holding that this is the uniquely correct reading.
2. For an unpaid loss reserve attributable to an accident year before 1986, the operative comparison is the reserve at the end of the taxable year beginning in 1986 against the immediately preceding year-end reserve reduced by claims and loss-adjustment payments during the 1986-beginning year attributable to that reserve. Reinsurance ceded in that year is treated as a loss payment. Net strengthening is excluded from the fresh start and treated as occurring in the first taxable year beginning after December 31, 1986. Reasonableness of the reserve and discretion over setting it are not additional statutory prerequisites to this computation.
3. Preserve both regulatory exceptions: exclude a nondiscretionary loss amount reported from a mandatory state or federal assigned-risk pool; exclude payments for reinsurance assumed during a 1986-beginning year and additions for a line of business's reinsurance assumed then, only up to the hypothetical reserve computed with the assumptions, other than assumed interest rates, used for that line's reinsurance assumed in a 1985-beginning year. Reinsurance assumed or ceded in a 1985-beginning year is treated as occurring in the succeeding year when its appropriate unpaid-loss reserve is not adjusted until that year. Section 845 separately applies to a reinsurance transaction having avoidance of the strengthening limitation as one purpose. Neither exception is asserted to apply here; no new avoidance finding is made.
4. Congress supplied a fresh-start exclusion while expressly removing reserve strengthening. Restricting that removal to formal methodology changes is not compelled by the words or the transition's operation: increases based on revised loss information can enlarge the excluded difference as well. Treasury reasonably treats the specified net addition as strengthening without conducting an artificiality or taxpayer-intent trial. The anti-inflation purpose corroborates this reading but does not create authority apart from the statute.
5. The $519,987 deficiency challenged on this construction survives. Affirmance supplies no new accounting facts, penalty, scienter determination or adjudication of a different exception, accident-year rule or taxable year.

## Tested alternatives and sufficient response

**Technical-term invalidation:** A truly settled and statutorily incorporated meaning limited to methods or assumptions would control despite Treasury's policy preference. The present term and transition do not establish that exclusive meaning. The taxpayer's undisputed absence of a methodology change therefore does not dispose of its statutory liability. The strongest objection to Treasury is that ordinary accurate updating may be wholly innocent; the statute removes strengthening from an exclusion and does not make wrongdoing its trigger. The actual exceptions remain operative rather than being replaced by a generalized innocence test.

**Unbounded agency discretion:** Rejected by every Associate. Smiley and Sweet Home require interpretive space and a permissible construction within it; MCI preserves enacted boundaries. Auer concerns interpretation of an agency's own regulation and supplies no substitute test here. Regions' statutory/finality distinction supplies a comparison, not a new authority to reopen settled years. No separate writing is necessary if the opinion uses the common propositions and qualifications; a proposed uniquely-correct-reading alternative is not frozen support for that additional rationale.
