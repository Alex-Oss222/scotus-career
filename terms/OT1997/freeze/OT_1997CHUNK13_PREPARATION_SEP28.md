## 156. Ino Ino — corrected scope

The state court's manager-license ruling expressly rests on Washington's constitution, 132 Wash.2d 122–123. The Court may decide reviewable federal claims but cannot erase that independent protection or sit to correct state constitutional analysis. State-law rulings rejecting federal relief do not immunize the adverse federal judgment from review.

The four-foot requirement means separation between the dancer and that dancer's customer, measured between their bodies rather than extended arms; the feared eight-to-nine-foot construction is not the law adjudicated. The stage rule requires eighteen-inch elevation and eight feet from patrons. The lighting standard is thirty lux horizontally at thirty inches above the floor on ten-foot centers, not thirty lux at every possible point. Hours are 2 a.m.–10 a.m. These qualifications are material, 132 Wash.2d 111–114, 125–134, 137, and footnotes 5, 8–10.

Actual undercover evidence of sexual contact and detection difficulties supports the operational regulations. The record's rejected economic forecast is not a finding that all venues inevitably must close. The declaration on reconsideration reported larger losses before than after compliance; no new compliance experiment or economic finding is reconstructed. The expression claims require constitutional review even when a distance rule survives it; the lower court's rational-basis-only stage analysis need not become the Court's rule.

Section 5.08.070(B)(2) prohibits a performance or pictorial representation displaying the specified anatomy from being visible outside the cabaret. The state court narrows it to adult-cabaret performances and their images, but does not limit it to obscenity or material obscene as to minors. That remains a separate federal content-based restriction, 132 Wash.2d 138–139. It is not simply a ban on an entertainer standing naked on a public sidewalk. The dramatic-works/nonobscene exception and the state court's construction of §5.08.070(A)(5) are distinct from B(2).

The licensing-information challenge as reported invokes state constitutional privacy; the federal speech review does not adjudicate a new free-standing informational-privacy claim. The postjudgment amendment and evidence rulings have independent procedural settings. The fee issues include both plaintiffs' §1988 eligibility and the City's state-equitable award for dissolving the temporary restraint, whose purpose the state majority describes as deterring preliminary-injunction requests, 132 Wash.2d 142–146. The state dissent expressly presents §1988 preemption and Maher; these are not invented new objections. The amount remains unliquidated on remand.

## 157. Newton County — material temporal and component corrections

The 1997 appellate judgment affirms two preliminary-injunction denials and, through asserted connected appellate review, affirms the August 5, 1996 partial summary judgment dismissing the separately pleaded MBTA claim. It also vacates a January 14, 1997 district-court WSRA interpretation entered while that question was on appeal. Other statutory merits were not decided by that appellate judgment. The Forest Service conceded that some nesting birds would be killed; this is not an unsupported prediction attributed only to plaintiffs, 113 F.3d 115–116.

The January 1998 official government brief reports that the comprehensive river plans were completed in October 1996 and that adjacent forest plans were amended then to incorporate them (footnotes 3 and 7). The historical missed September 30, 1995 deadline remains true; a continuing failure to complete plans is false on the reported later record. These are reported agency facts, not a new agency-performance finding by this Court. The same brief expressly opposes the hunter-only construction and asserts that private timber harvesting can be covered (footnote 6). Its federal-agency coverage argument is separate. It says the contractor-approval theory was not raised below (footnote 11), which is a government preservation contention, not a fabricated concession by petitioners.

Directly connected later lower-court research recovered the official April 1, 1998 judgment in No. 97-1852, 141 F.3d 803. That judgment affirms the final district-court judgment on distinct agency-record, WSRA site-effects, NFMA, NEPA, Clean Water Act, Wilderness Act and ESA grounds. It does not freshly adjudicate the MBTA issue. It reports that counsel said at the December 10, 1997 argument that approximately three-fourths of road work and harvesting was complete. Nothing reviewed establishes completion of the remaining work by September 1998. Neither continuing preliminary proceedings nor complete logging may be presumed.

The October 1996 plan completion eliminates the particular request to halt the sales until those plans exist. The later final judgment also supersedes the obsolete preliminary posture. Neither fact by itself eliminates a live challenge to the separate MBTA merits dismissal. The scheduled review of the 1997 judgment does not silently add the April 1998 final judgment or all its new statutory issues. ## 159. McKenzie — corrected factual and statutory limits

McKenzie alleges personal participation in dispatcher reporting practices beginning long before suit. Public reports and the Falsetti/Dorris allegations overlapped, and the appellate court observed nearly identical complaint passages; those observations do not decide whether each material factual allegation was independently known. The published opinion reports compliance with the sealed submission procedure and governmental declination. It does not independently establish every original-source fact or convert sealed filing itself into the separate required voluntary provision before filing.

The statute then in force requires direct and independent knowledge plus voluntary disclosure to the Government before filing. It does not say before public disclosure or require that the relator supply the newspaper's source. The §3730(b)(5) related-pending-action bar and §3730(e)(3) government-party bar remain distinct and are not adjudged satisfied from similarity alone.

Retaliation is independently pleaded. Her alleged internal complaints, presentation of a newspaper account of a similar federal qui tam case, harassment and threats permit further proceedings under the then-existing pleading standard. Internal dissatisfaction alone does not necessarily qualify, and no successful qui tam recovery is an element of the retaliation protection. Protected activity, employer knowledge, causation and actual relief remain distinct. No fraud, retaliation, disability-causation or damages finding is made.
