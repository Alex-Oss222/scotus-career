## Validated facts and complete lower posture

The class consists of present and future Suffolk County pretrial detainees. The litigation began in 1971; the 1979 consent decree approved a new jail with single-occupancy cells. Construction delay and population growth led to modifications in 1985, 1990 and 1994. The Nashua Street jail opened in 1990 and the Charles Street facility closed; the 1994 modification allowed limited double bunking. Historical allegations characterized the claim under the Eighth Amendment, but the operative constitutional treatment of pretrial detainees must observe Bell v. Wolfish and the protection against punishment before conviction. No actual double-bunking constitutional violation follows from the label or from the decree itself.

In July 1996, the Sheriff moved for PLRA termination of prospective relief. The Commissioner sought vacatur. The United States intervened to defend constitutionality. The district court sustained the statute, ended specific enforcement but left the decree and consensual obligations intact. All parties appealed. The First Circuit sustained the presented constitutional operation, affirmed termination of prospective relief and refusal to vacate, and directed modification to terminate the decree's future operation itself. It distinguished termination from retrospective annulment. These are lower dispositions and arguments under review, not this Court's conclusion.

Plaintiffs presented Article III final-judgment and Klein objections; due-process theories based on vested rights and contract impairment; equal-protection objections based on pretrial rights, access and rational relationship; and statutory arguments that prior findings sufficed or a further inquiry was needed. The panel treated the decree as modifiable prospective relief, the statute as changing remedial law while leaving actual findings to judges, and the federal government as a nonparty to the alleged contract. It rejected a presumption that federal legislation benefiting States is equivalent to federal contractual self-dealing.

On actual findings, the district judge concluded the existing findings failed the statute. Since June 14, 1994 the court had received and evaluated periodic reports concerning assaults, rape, disease and related conditions. Its cited record contained no evidence of noncompliance with the modified decree supporting the requested findings. The First Circuit deferred to the trial court's interpretation of its own findings and factual evaluation. Compliance with a decree and absence of a current federal violation are conceptually distinct; the published account is not an independent finding by this reviewing Court that every condition was lawful, nor a guarantee of conditions after termination.
