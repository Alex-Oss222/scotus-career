**Record verified:** M.S. 206B serves grades 5–7, ages 9–12. The church sought Sunday rentals because of overcrowding, for services comprising hymn singing, communion, Bible reading, preaching and teaching. It previously rented for sports/games and a banquet. SOP 5.6 admits broad educational, civic, social, recreational and community-welfare uses; actual users included secular ethical and character instruction. SOP 5.9 forbids religious services and instruction but permits discussion/distribution of religious material and religious viewpoints. The stipulated consistent exclusion proves the policy's practice, not its constitutionality. The record covers one school year, with no reported request for permitted religious discussion. The request is expressly for worship, so no theological-classification factual dispute is invented. The appellate court found no record indication that the proposed services were closed to the public and no admission-fee fact; exclusivity and fees do not supply alternative grounds.

**Components below:** The Second Circuit affirmed all claims; Cabranes agreed that worship could be excluded but dissented as to religious instruction because comparable secular ethical and character training was admitted. Both portions are within the authorization. The separately rejected free-exercise/equality claims need not receive new independent holdings to resolve access. The lower court's RFRA description supplies no new simulated Boerne holding, and no independent RFRA merits issue is added to the grant.

**Entering-law refresh:** Lamb's Chapel forbids excluding a religious perspective on an admitted subject even assuming a nonpublic forum, but expressly reserved the separate Sunday-worship application and all restrictions on religious use. Rosenberger forbids viewpoint exclusion in its actual printing program, not every subject exclusion or every expenditure. Pinette and Chabad reject categorical establishment defenses based on religious content/location; their bounded access and actual attribution qualifications remain. Agostini replaces independent Lemon prongs with its single inquiry: specific prohibition/distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, then unresolved attribution/function. Actual government organization of worship is forbidden; private authorization and location do not alone decide attribution.

Tanford and Chaudhuri forbid officially arranged devotional prayers as the university's own program but preserve independently chosen private forum speech; here the church, not school officials, arranges the event. Perry (May 4) requires actual justification for a nonprofit distinction in a traditional public forum; it neither turns this school into a traditional forum nor imports Ward tailoring into every limited-forum subject restriction. Its narrower lesson is to examine the actual distinction and harm rather than presume a class label answers the issue. Its unanimous joins do not predetermine the worship question.

**Alternative paths:** Instruction may be excluded as a distinct religious activity, or treated as a religious perspective on admitted teaching. The strongest exclusion argument is that discussion remains permitted; the strongest answer is that secular instruction in ethics/character is admitted, so confining the religious perspective to discussion places it at a disadvantage in the same activity. All eight Associates accept the latter answer within the comparable subject matter, without invalidating every possible curricular boundary.

For worship, one path treats the admitted community/ethical/educational activities broadly enough that the religious form of this private meeting cannot itself disqualify it; the opposing path distinguishes a recurring church service, including communion, as a separate activity that the forum never admitted. The strongest objection to equal access is that it collapses subject limits into viewpoint discrimination and imposes a church-use forum the school did not create. The answer supporting access is that the broad actual community and instructional uses cannot be defined by subtracting their religious expression, and private devotional form alone does not alter the speech's otherwise eligible function. The strongest objection to exclusion is that secular analogues need not reproduce a sacrament before a religious meeting can be comparable. The answer supporting exclusion is that the stipulated integrated service, rather than a disputed label attached to ethical discussion, permits a genuine activity distinction. These are materially different grounds and remain distinct in the commitment table.

Establishment does not supply a categorical bar to the described private Sunday use: no official speaker selection, school program placement, compelled pupil attendance or financing of devotional content is established. An actual future attribution, favoritism, domination or operational problem would require its own supported inquiry. Neither a universally mandatory disclaimer nor a finding that an unspecified sign solves every issue is warranted. Neutral scheduling, capacity, safety, access and genuinely prospective forum rules remain possible; a neutral rule must actually be neutral, not preserve secular comparators while excluding their religious perspectives.
