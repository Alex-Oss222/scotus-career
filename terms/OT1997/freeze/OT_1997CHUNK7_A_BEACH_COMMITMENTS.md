## Common committed rule, explanation and remedy

Affirm. When the right to federal rescission expires under §1635(f), a borrower cannot revive it by asserting rescission defensively in a later foreclosure action. The ordinary expiration is three years after consummation or sale of the property, whichever occurs first, even if material disclosures have not been made; the statutory agency-proceeding extension below remains part of that rule. Section 1635(f) terminates the right itself, not merely the time for filing an affirmative action. A defense label does not preserve a right Congress has ended.

The extension applies when an agency empowered to enforce the subchapter institutes a proceeding to enforce §1635 within three years of consummation, finds a violation, and the obligor's rescission claim rests in whole or part on a matter involved in that proceeding. In that event the right expires at the later of the ordinary endpoint—three years after consummation or earlier property sale—and one year following conclusion of the proceeding, any judicial review, or the period for judicial review. No qualifying agency proceeding is identified here. The holding neither deletes this exception nor extends it to every private suit or regulatory inquiry.

Reiter and Bull preserve same-transaction recoupment against an affirmative-action limitation, only up to the plaintiff's liability. That rule does not convert the expiring §1635 rescission right into an unlimited defense. Congress separately made §1640 damages assertable by recoupment or setoff in a collection action after its one-year affirmative-action period. This express damages preservation and §1635(f)'s right-expiration language supply distinct grounds for treating the remedies differently. Consumer-protection purposes do not erase Congress's selected endpoint for unwinding a secured transaction.

The 1986 refinancing preceded the 1991 cessation of payments and 1992 foreclosure by more than three years; on the stated record the federal rescission right had expired in 1989. Affirmance leaves the $396 actual-damages and $1,000 statutory-damages offsets untouched. It does not adopt the trial court's alternative residential-mortgage classification, adjudicate timely rescission notice or how a timely exercise is enforced, settle tender obligations, invalidate or validate the entire loan on another ground, or eliminate independent state-law remedies. No new money award, hearing or injunction is ordered.

## Alternative-path comparison and writing functions

The strongest recoupment path would preserve a same-transaction defense while denying affirmative recovery, following Reiter and the practical concern that a lender controls collection timing. Its decisive defect here is the right-expiration text, not the borrower's choice of procedural form. The agency extension shows that the specified endpoint is qualified in an identified way, while Congress's express damages defense shows how it preserved defensive remedies when intended. The strongest objection to expiration is that an undisclosed transaction might otherwise remain enforceable; TILA's preserved defensive damages and separate state-law remedies remain available on their own terms. None establishes perpetual rescission.

All eight Associates support the same sufficient rationale and full scope stated above. No separate writing is needed if that scope is maintained. They have not committed to a broader ruling on §1635 notice, tender, enforcement of a timely exercise, or all statutes of repose. These are conditional join barriers, not missing choices in the presented case.
