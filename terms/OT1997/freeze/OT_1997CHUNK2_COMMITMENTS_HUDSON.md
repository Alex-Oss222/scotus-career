## Reach, record, and controlling premises

All eight Associates participate; the packet establishes no contrary participation fact. The case is reviewable under the supplied federal appellate channel. The issue reached is whether the personal monetary penalties, considered with the banking restriction actually imposed, supply punishment sufficient to bar the pending prosecution. The earlier appellate ruling sustaining the nonparticipation sanction retains its procedural scope; addressing the combined-sanction submission does not create a new independent appeal from that ruling.

The following distinctions are material to every commitment:

- The actual settlements were $16,500 for Hudson, $15,000 for Baresel, and $12,500 for Rackley: $44,000 altogether. The original assessments and an imagined maximum derived from an unknown number of violation days are not the sanctions finally imposed.
- The District Court held an evidentiary hearing and found $72,000 in OCC costs of pursuing these defendants. The Tenth Circuit treated the $44,000 sanctions as rationally related to those costs under Halper. The $72,000 is a reported evidentiary finding, not an independently audited calculation. The lower-record addendum resolves the earlier absence of a supplied amount; missing underlying cost documents do not erase the finding or justify an automatic new accounting.
- The approximately $900,000 loss remains an allegation. The conflict between the lower transcription's FDIC description and the independently validated bank-loss account is preserved. Neither supplies a proved government-loss figure or supplements the $72,000 finding. The conflicting individual amounts in the lower opinion's opening account are not substituted for the validated consent amounts.
- Sections 93(b) and 504 expressly authorize civil monetary penalties, with the original $1,000-per-day ceiling, administrative assessment and review, and the supported discretionary compromise mechanism. Good faith, gravity, financial resources, and prior violations are assessment considerations; they neither establish a universal willfulness requirement nor decide constitutional classification. No later 1989 penalty tier is imported into the February 1989 notice concerning earlier conduct.
- The consent restrictions prohibit participation in banking institutions without written regulatory authorization. Their protective function and their actual burden both matter. The supplied historical §1818(e) language distinguishes personal dishonesty from willful **or continuing** disregard; it is not the complete original removal statute or a finding that every removal predicate was admitted. The 1989 changes cannot be made the governing authority merely because settlement followed their enactment.
- The first appeal rejected the claimed settlement waiver. Consent supplies no new waiver, admission to every allegation, or adjudication of criminal guilt. The combined-sanction question does not add an Excessive Fines or independent due-process claim.

**Entering law:** Halper, 490 U.S. 435, 442–50 & n.6, permits reasonable remedial approximation including investigation and enforcement expense and preserves an exceptional personal-penalty inquiry. Kennedy's concurrence, id. at 452–53, makes that inquiry objective rather than a search for subjective motives. Simulated Ursery, June 24, 1996, expressly preserves that field while applying rebuttable civil-intent/clearest-proof analysis to its specified forfeitures. Simulated Hendricks, June 23, 1997, applies the demanding classification inquiry to actual cumulative operation in its Ex Post Facto setting; its confinement outcome does not classify banking regulation. Kurth Ranch's combined-operation tax holding does not establish a universal government-cost ceiling. Dixon retains Grady as well as necessary-offense protection; Felix preserves the distinct conspiracy agreement and rejects mere evidentiary overlap as sufficient. None of those decisions supplies a current offense-identity finding.

The operative Standards selections are **Civil forfeiture and criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy** (June 24, 1996), **Punitive purpose or effect in nominally civil commitment** (June 23, 1997), **A punitive drug tax after criminal punishment** (June 6, 1994), and **Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt** (Dixon, June 28, 1993; retained Felix components, March 25, 1992). No current-term holding changes those rules for this case.

## Enhanced comparison of the material alternatives

### Affirmance under surviving law versus a classification remand

**Affirmance path.** Apply the rebuttable civil-classification inquiry without converting Ursery into an exemption for all personal monetary sanctions, apply Halper to the actual penalties and supported government costs, and consider the banking restriction as part of the actual combination. This preserves all relevant earlier holdings while sustaining the reinstatement of prosecution.

**Strongest objection:** These are personal exactions conditioned on alleged wrongdoing, accompanied by a substantial occupational restriction. The amount factors include gravity and prior violations, and the agency could intend deterrence. A below-cost comparison alone cannot demonstrate that every sanction serves a remedial function; adding otherwise distinct burdens could expose punitive operation. Stevens's Ursery dissent and the Hendricks majority require a genuine answer to that objection.

**Supported resolution:** The selected ground is not cost arithmetic alone. The statutory civil machinery, the actual monetary amounts and evidentiary costs, and the qualified restriction's protection of regulated banking activity supply the combined nonpunitive account. Halper itself permits investigative and enforcement expense and reasonable approximation; Kennedy's concurrence rejects subjective-purpose searching. The gravity and good-faith considerations do not establish an invariably criminal sanction, and no number of violation days or extra statutory predicate is supplied. The six Justices' actual-operation commitments therefore remain meaningful without requiring a punitive result in every case involving a personal burden. Scalia and Thomas reach the same disposition on their narrower conception of the Clause's relevant trigger.

**Remand path.** Vacate for a limited classification inquiry if the lower judgment rested on a legally inadequate treatment of the money, the combined restriction, or a supported unresolved accounting predicate. If punishment were established, further work on attachment and offense identity could also be necessary; remand would not itself establish a bar.

**Strongest objection and result:** The District Court already held an evidentiary hearing and found costs. The circuit actually applied an objective Halper relation, rather than dispensing with Halper or inventing a figure. No verified preserved allocation defect, cost exclusion, or additional punitive operation is supplied. None of the eight selects a further remand merely to recover documents missing from this research packet. This is not a finding that the litigants waived such an issue or that no underlying challenge could exist. It is the bounded commitment supportable from the validated record, which already permits the classification answer.

The Justice-specific application is explicit in the table: Stevens's punitive-property objection, O'Connor's protective-design account, Kennedy's objective test, Souter's personal-penalty distinction, Ginsburg's cumulative-operation commitment, and Breyer's actual-operation method have each been applied to the new cost finding. Scalia and Thomas's competing view of the constitutional trigger changes the rationale, not the judgment. A materially different preserved issue would require a corrected neutral handoff; it is not an authorized alternative result silently left for assembly to choose.

### Preserving Halper versus replacing it with a single classification rule

**Broader path:** Expressly reconsider Halper and require only the legislative-intent/clearest-proof inquiry for all nominally civil sanctions. The strongest argument is that civil remedies frequently deter, punitive effects do not divide cleanly from remedial effects, and exact compensation accounting can misclassify legitimate regulatory remedies. Scalia and Thomas's earlier successive-criminal-prosecution account provides a different constitutional criticism of an independent civil multiple-punishment doctrine.

**Strongest counterargument:** Halper already limits itself to exceptional personal penalties, allows rough approximation, and recognizes investigation and enforcement costs. Ursery expressly preserved this field, and Hendricks affirmed the need to examine actual operation rather than making a label decisive. Here the existing accounting supports affirmance, so an assertion of conceptual tension does not establish that abandoning the rule is required to decide the case. The retained rule and the broader regulatory classification inquiry can both be applied to these facts without conflict.

**Commitment:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer select the Halper-preserving explanation at the stated scope; no overruling proposition receives their frozen join. Scalia and Thomas maintain their distinct ground, but their two judgment-only commitments are not affirmative votes for an unformulated overruling holding. No precedent is treated as already overruled, and no change to Kurth Ranch or the retained Grady rule follows. This leaves any genuinely different proposed precedent treatment to the separately governed reconciliation process, not to arithmetic consolidation of incompatible reasons.

### Classification versus a final same-offense bar

**Potential bar path:** A punitive first disposition could make attachment, completed proceedings, and offense identity decisive. Stevens's Ursery separate opinion supplies an example of those independent requirements being established; Dixon and Grady remain relevant, and Felix preserves the distinct conspiracy agreement.

**Strongest counterargument and limit:** The monetary classification fails here under each selected sufficient ground. Independently, the court of appeals expressly left identity undecided, the verified grant concerns punishment, and the complete charging instruments are not supplied. A shared transaction is not a finding that all 22 counts are the same offense; Rackley's false-entry counts cannot silently be attributed to the other petitioners. No Associate commits to dismissing all or particular charges on an unsupported identity analysis. The District Court's reported identity conclusion is not converted into an appellate holding. The affirmance leaves the unnecessary attachment and identity questions undecided rather than deciding that a punitive civil proceeding could never trigger them.
