## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Interlocutory factual appeals and appellate sanctions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve interlocutory jurisdiction without deciding the constitutional merits or factual sufficiency.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Johnson v. Jones and Behrens preserve legal immunity review but not an immediate appeal of every evidentiary dispute. No broader immunity replacement is necessary to resolve this jurisdictional defect.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The privilege of immediate legal immunity review does not authorize a fact-only appeal, and a dilatory unsupported appeal may carry sanctions only through independent lawful authority.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone would dismiss for want of appellate jurisdiction and preserve supported double costs and attorney’s fees. The defendants must litigate the identified factual disputes in the trial court, not repeatedly ask the appellate court to select their version of events. Because the earlier mandate already identified that boundary, a supported finding of a repetitious improper appeal justifies compensation for the resulting burden. Assessment must use the appropriate submissions and opportunity to contest the amounts; it is not a damages finding on the underlying civil-rights action.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Failure of immunity does not itself prove the constitutional claim, causation, damages, supervisory liability, or a right to trial.

**Proposed Stone disposition:** DISMISS the interlocutory appeal for lack of jurisdiction and sustain the supported double costs and attorney's fees.

**Reasoning:** An official may pursue an authorized immediate appeal presenting a separable qualified-immunity question of law. That privilege does not permit repeated interlocutory appeals seeking to relitigate whether evidence supports the plaintiff's account. Where the appeal unnecessarily prolongs proceedings despite an established jurisdictional limit, properly supported sanctions are available.

**Doctrinal contribution:** Limits on immunity appeals; prevention of dilatory litigation; preservation of factual questions for trial.
