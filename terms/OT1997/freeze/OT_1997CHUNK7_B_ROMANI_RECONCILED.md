## Reconciled result

All eight Associates affirm the Pennsylvania priority judgment. Where the Government's federal tax lien is invalid against this antecedent judgment lien protected by section 6323(a), section 3713(a)(1)(B) does not permit collection of the same tax claim from the protected real property ahead of that creditor merely because the debtor's estate is insolvent. The later, specific, comprehensive tax-lien allocation governs this actual conflict. The rule neither repeals section 3713 nor exempts all tax debts or all previously secured claims from it.

Historical unanimity on judgment supports the independent result. Seven Associates historically joined the entire Court opinion; Scalia joined all except its discussion of failed legislative proposals. The independent draft instead uses enacted statutory structure, actual property and notice, and harmonization; it does not assign interpretive weight to unenacted proposals. That removes the particular methodological barrier without changing his historical substantive ground.

## Complete grounds and limits

The Government's strongest contrary argument treats section 3713's payment preference as independent from section 6323's invalidity of a tax lien against the protected creditor. That distinction cannot resolve this concrete collision: the only estate asset is the encumbered land, and paying the same tax claim first from its value would destroy the property-specific protection Congress provided. Harmonization gives that protection effect while leaving section 3713 operative elsewhere; it requires no implied repeal or categorical exception for all secured creditors.

The state judgment lien and section 6323 protected-creditor status are established. They do not concede the separately disputed federal-insolvency perfection or title/possession requirements. The selected statutory ground makes adjudication of that alternative unnecessary. A contrary section 3713-first construction would have to account for the unadjudicated alternative before ordering an adverse property disposition; the existing concession cannot decide it by inference.

McDermott remains controlling for after-acquired property; its lien had not attached to that land before federal notice. This land was already subject to the protected judgment lien. New Britain supplies the lienor/property/amount concept without erasing the distinct section 3713 question. Thelusson does not categorically decide every later perfected real-property lien: Conard explains the unperfected interest involved there. Key and Emory involved unsecured claims and do not decide this protected-property collision. Kimbell Foods confirms the enacted tax scheme's accommodation of competing interests but does not itself decide insolvent-estate distribution. Morton supplies harmonization, while Randall illustrates a specific statutory distribution displacing general priority at an actual conflict. Holywell and Hill preserve statutory categories, not equitable exemptions. No precedent is overruled.

The rule concerns section 3713(a)(1)(B)'s insufficient deceased-debtor estate in the administrator's custody. It does not alter subsection (a)(1)(A)'s separate living-debtor triggers, subsection (a)(2)'s title-11 exclusion, or subsection (b)'s distinct representative-liability requirements. Section 6323's protections for other classes and transactions retain their own predicates.

Affirmance sustains the authorized transfer in lieu of execution against this federal priority objection. It creates no personal-liability judgment, tax amount, new collection order, factual hearing, or adjudication of additional property. No separate writing is necessary if the common enacted-text rationale stays within these bounds. If required by an expanded opinion, Scalia's separate writing would explain that bicameralism and presentment give failed proposals no legal force, later legislative views cannot authoritatively reinterpret earlier law, and investigating unenacted bills imposes unjustified interpretive costs. No author is assigned here.
