## Common disposition, rule and limits

All eight Associate Justices would affirm the judgment on the two granted Fourth Amendment questions. Review under §1254(1) reaches the completed appellate judgment. Neither staleness nor the challenged category descriptions requires overturning the suppression ruling on this reported record.

**Staleness.** A warrant must have a sufficiently current factual basis, assessed from the actual information, the property sought and reasonable inferences under Gates; elapsed time is material but not an automatic deadline. Here verified completed downloads to a telephone number tied to Lacy's apartment, corroborated by telephone records, provide a foundation for the experienced agent's account that possessors retain valued files for long periods in secure places, commonly at home. That combination permits the magistrate to find a fair probability of continued possession after this ten-month interval. It does not permit indefinite retention to be assumed or make generalized habits sufficient without evidence of this person's acquisition. Sgro supplies the current-probability requirement; Gates supplies the practical totality and substantial-basis review of the issuing magistrate, preserved by Ornelas for warrants.

**Particularity.** A warrant must limit the things to be seized sufficiently to prevent general exploratory rummaging. The permissible specificity depends on what the established probable cause concerns and what investigators reasonably can describe when obtaining the warrant; inability to give a more exact description does not excuse an unbounded search unrelated to the supported crime. Here the reported affidavit ties the system and storage media to actual downloaded images and explains why their location and the equipment/software needed for accurate retrieval cannot be known in advance. The reported authorization also supplies objective offense-related limits for records, including the BAMSE connection. In this setting the generic equipment categories and linked records do not create a general warrant. Andresen and Coolidge supply the substantive protection; the circuit's three considerations are useful applications, not a newly mandatory Supreme Court checklist.

Pinson and Myers protect concealed information in a home, distinguish initial authority from later remedies, and reject retroactive authorization of a warrantless technological search. They neither dispense with a warrant here nor invalidate a properly supported and bounded warrant merely because computers hold the evidence. The resolution establishes no digital exception, universal equipment-seizure permission, or forensic examination protocol. It adjudicates neither an unquoted warrant clause nor item-level execution. Trial discoveries and admissions supply none of the warrant's original justification. Disks obtained from the separate storage room under consent are not relabeled as products of this apartment warrant.

## Enhanced alternative-path conclusions

The competing staleness path would find that generalized retention assertions cannot bridge ten months without individualized proof of continuing activity. Its strongest support is the danger that easily retained property becomes permanently searchable. The supporting path prevails here because verified receipt supplies the missing individual foundation, the nature of stored files bears directly on continued possession, and the inference is bounded to the actual interval rather than permanence. No finding of an ongoing distribution business is needed or made.

The competing particularity path would distinguish permission to search for specified images from seizure of an entire computer system and potentially unrelated papers. Its strongest support is the home's substantial privacy and the risk of using investigative convenience to erase category limits. The supporting path prevails because the published record reports probable cause for the system, explains the real storage/retrieval uncertainty, and reports objective document limits absent in Kow. A naked assertion that computers are useful would not suffice; the unavailability of a more specific equipment description and the offense connection perform independent work. Nothing authorizes unrelated future searches merely because equipment was lawfully seized.
