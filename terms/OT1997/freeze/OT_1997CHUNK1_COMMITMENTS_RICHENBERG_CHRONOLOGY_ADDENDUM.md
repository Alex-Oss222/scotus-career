## Controlling law and application

**Jeffries Part I** rejects the particular appellate employment balance. A reasonable forecast remains relevant but does not independently establish that the actual speech was legally disciplinable; lack of entitlement or retention of a different benefit does not categorically defeat retaliation. The actual service relationship and institutional interests matter. Prevention need not await realized harm, and Waters supplies no complete investigation code. These propositions are controlled by the direct joins of Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The strongest analogy favors closer scrutiny of Richenberg's actual discharge ground: military interests or a plausible forecast cannot make irrelevant expression a lawful predicate merely by attaching an evidentiary label. That constraint was already part of Thomasson and every original Richenberg commitment. The five affirming Associates rely on the existing conduct-related statutory inference, the likelihood definition, and their assessment that rebuttal received actual consideration. They do not rely solely on predicted disruption, lack of a right to military service, or a hypothetical agency rationale. The three reversing Associates find that this particular record fails the actual-connection and genuine-rebuttal boundary. Jeffries supplies no new fact deciding between those interpretations.

The role-sensitive civilian speech balance does not automatically replace the military rule. Thomasson specifically sustains a conduct-related inference with genuine rebuttal, without completed acts or individualized operational injury; Walmer retains its distinct admitted-conduct preliminary scope. Jeffries neither requires a military member to hold a special policymaking or public-contact position before lawful personnel rules apply nor converts military deference into immunity. It does not alter ordinary rationality, the likelihood definition, §654(b)(2)'s member-demonstration mechanism, or the statute's separate routes and exceptions.

**Jeffries Part II** requires actual defendant responsibility and an actual preponderance same-decision defense when protected conduct supplies a substantial or motivating cause. Its direct majority includes all five Part I joiners and the Chief Justice. This rule rejects substituting a conceivable lawful counterfactual for a proved independent causal ground in that retaliation setting. It does not convert §654(b)(2) rebuttal into a governmental same-decision burden, impose an actual-purpose requirement on ordinary rationality, or establish protected-speech retaliation in Richenberg. No new causal event, animus finding, or alternative agency reason is supplied.

**Jeffries Part III** orders bounded further proceedings without compulsory retrial or automatic restoration of office or awards. It is controlled by the same five Associates as Part I. This accords with the original Richenberg distinctions among setting aside this discharge, genuine reconsideration, an additional explanation, a new hearing, final reinstatement, and compensation. Jeffries decides no military APA reviewability or fact-review standard. Its individual personal-damages immunity analysis does not supply a new issue or disposition in Richenberg's present prospective-relief inquiry.

Jeffries's separate proposed categorical speech exclusions and compulsory retrial are noncontrolling. O'Connor's dissent, joined by Scalia and Thomas, is also noncontrolling: its own contextual acceptance of predicted institutional effects cannot become an automatic forecast rule in Richenberg.
