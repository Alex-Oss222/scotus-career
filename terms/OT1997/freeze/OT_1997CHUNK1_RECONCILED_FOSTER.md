## Reconciled disposition and exact proposition joins

**Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer retain affirmance and each join F1 and F2.** Their support has these precise limits:

**F1 — Congressional timing and actual conflict.** Article I, §4 authorizes Congress to prescribe the time of congressional elections. Then-operative 2 U.S.C. §§1 and 7 conflict with the Louisiana feature that conclusively elects a congressional majority winner in October and leaves no act of selection for the prescribed November day. The legal finality of the choice controls; calling it a primary does not postpone that choice. Preliminary electoral steps can precede the day, but a process is not still open after state law has finally selected the winner with no remaining election-day act.

**F2 — Bounded affirmance and remedy.** Affirm the Fifth Circuit's rejection of that final October-election feature. Compatible preliminary procedures and the open-ballot form survive; no comprehensive rule about every preliminary act or later runoff is adopted. Section 8's actual vacancy/failure-to-elect conditions remain distinct and do not describe a successful final October majority. The judgment supplies no replacement calendar, deadline, disruption of completed officeholding, whole-system invalidation, or invented decree term. Presidential timing is outside the adjudicated congressional question.

The eight names above apply to **both** propositions. There is no separately adopted legislative-purpose part, no inference of broader implied-preemption support, and no attribution of a historical author's assignment to this event.
