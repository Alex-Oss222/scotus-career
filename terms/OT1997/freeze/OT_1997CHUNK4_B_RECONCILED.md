## 1. Alaska v. Native Village of Venetie Tribal Government — February 25, 1998

### Components, sufficient rule and remedy

V1 is the meaning of dependent Indian community in 18 U.S.C. §1151(b). V2 is its application to these former-reservation ANCSA lands and the asserted territorial tax. A dependent community requires land set aside by the Federal Government for Indian use and federal superintendence over that community's land relationship. Federal recognition of a tribe and assistance to its members are not substitutes for those territorial predicates. The statutory category does not require United States fee ownership, a reservation label, or a particular administrative form.

ANCSA expressly revoked this reservation, extinguished aboriginal land claims, and conveyed fee title through state corporations without continuing federal restrictions on disposition or use. The §1618(b) election selected the land received in the settlement; it did not continue the revoked reservation or impose a new federal Indian-use restriction. Subsequent tribal ownership did not itself restore the missing federal relation. Section 1636(d)'s protections against adverse possession, real-property taxation and certain judgments while the land remains unsold, unleased and undeveloped preserve assets under their actual conditions; they do not make transfers depend on federal approval or subject ordinary land use to federal supervision. Health, social, welfare and development programs support people without supplying the missing territorial superintendence.

**Commitment:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's contrary Indian-country classification. Ordinary implementation below must conform to the absence of §1151(b) status for the lands at issue; no further factfinding on generalized assistance is ordered. The claimed territorial authority to impose this nonmember construction tax consequently fails. Do not decide every Alaska Native parcel, allotment, trust acquisition, recognized tribe's political status, member-based authority, or an independently established consensual source of authority. No private-title adjudication or general elimination of tribal government follows.

## 5. Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach — March 3, 1998

### Components, rule and remedy

L1 concerns §§1407(a)/1404(a) self-transfer; L2 concerns the preserved error's effect on the trial judgment; L3 preserves the distinct pretrial rulings and proper remaining appellate matters. Section 1407 authorizes common-fact coordination for pretrial proceedings. Unless the action has previously terminated, the Panel must remand it to the original district at or before those proceedings end; the Panel may separate and remand particular claims earlier. Changing the asserted venue basis does not terminate an action. The fact that the command expressly addresses the Panel does not let a transferee court prevent its performance. Subsection (f) authorizes only rules consistent with Acts of Congress; the cited practice cannot overcome the statute. Subsection (h)'s specific Clayton Act trial-transfer permission does not confer that power in this tort action.

The objection was repeatedly preserved, including remand requests and mandamus followed by direct appeal.
