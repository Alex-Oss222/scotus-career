## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Criminal procedure — noncapital sentencing and double jeopardy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Does double jeopardy bar a second attempt to establish a prior-conviction enhancement after the first proof was held insufficient?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Double Jeopardy Clause; distinguish an acquittal of an offense, ordinary noncapital sentencing, and the special capital-sentencing doctrine.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AFFIRM, preserving the latest supplied Stone choice.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone’s supplied position permits renewed proof of the noncapital enhancement. He would explain why the allegation concerns the punishment for the current conviction and why the failed proof is not an acquittal of a separate offense. The existence of evidentiary hearings and a burden of proof does not alone transform every sentencing determination into a second criminal prosecution. He would preserve the capital acquittal-like exception and avoid implying that a State may relabel an actual offense as a sentencing factor to escape jeopardy. Caspari supplies procedural history and a reservation, not a binding answer; the proposed merits answer must therefore be justified independently.
* **Treatment of Controlling Precedent:** Caspari rejected habeas relief under Teague without deciding the constitutional merits; it is not already a merits holding against Monge. Witte distinguishes sentence selection from separate prosecution within its stated bounds. The simulation’s Dixon retains Grady, but that same-offense rule applies only once the disputed proceeding is properly classified as a prosecution to which jeopardy attaches.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No alteration of an actual acquittal, no automatic enhancement, and no importation of later sentencing jurisprudence.
