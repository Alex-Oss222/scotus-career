## Reconciliation of material differences and limits

**Jordahl.** Stevens's J2 and J3 difference remains substantive. His own *Feldman* dissent, 460 U.S. at 488–490, supplies an original-collateral-claim objection distinct from his disagreement about the bar proceeding's judicial character. The ended injunctions and demand for damages from their private procurer are material to that objection. His contrary path neither assumes fraudulent procurement nor treats dissolution as constitutional exoneration. The other seven maintain the claim-specific appellate-review boundary on the published descriptions of these counts. Their acceptance does not extend to all consequences of all state orders; *Dennis*, *Lugar* and *De Grandy* retain their distinct functions, and preclusion, state action, merits, immunity and limitations remain distinct questions. The frozen rows explain why the seven reject this remand path individually. A certiorari denial supplies no reason to erase this disagreement or recast Stevens as historically aligned with an affirmance.

The shared J1 result respects the June 5, 1998 *Caldwell* construction of current Virginia law, the distinct express vote-against 1989 pamphlet, and the separate histories of the three injunctions. *Caldwell* does not retroactively adjudicate those injunctions or establish that every plaintiff engaged solely in issue advocacy. The current-threat ground does not depend on an invented post-Caldwell filing or concession. The J3 ground is §1367(a), not a discretionary §1367(c)(3) choice after a federal merits adjudication. *Heck* is not converted into Rooker-Feldman jurisdiction or a universal civil-injunction favorable-termination rule.

**Florida.** The eight common merits commitments depend on the actual limited program, the lawful-fee concession, the continuing ballot access, and the objective, potentially changing five-percent registration criterion. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg's objections to discretionary or viewpoint-dependent selection remain respected because the criterion is statutory and reviewable; their agreement does not accept political entrenchment as an interest. Ginsburg's limited *Finley* join remains limited. Scalia and Thomas's broader subsidy positions are not silently attributed to the other six; they accept the common sufficient narrower ground. *Buckley* supplies received election-funding authority without being represented as Stevens's personal historical join. The neutral record does not support an exact invented allocation formula or a finding that minor-party fees directly funded rival parties. The historical denial adds nothing to these Justice-specific reasons.

**Illinois.** The five non-Stone Justices adopting *Thornton*'s exclusivity premise must remain separate from O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas's genuine-procedure ground if the assembled opinion states the broader constitutional premise. Agreement that this procedure adds no personal qualification cannot be presented as their new endorsement of exclusivity. The existing *Thornton* holding retains its actual controlling support; it is not reconstructed from these present judgment votes. The ordinary petition denominators, statewide cap and distinct redistricting exception remain separate. Disputed 1996 petition acceptances do not establish full statutory compliance, and exceptional 1992 qualification does not prove ordinary-year access. The relevant-electorate distinction and absence of an established impossible cumulative burden answer the modeled alternative without making percentages, slate rules or prior isolated success categorical safe harbors.
