## The proposed rule and its genuine competing ground

The lower court's proposal is that, before making this known career criminal its central prosecution witness, the State must obtain and review his identified corrections record and disclose the material favorable credibility information under Brady and Giglio. That is an affirmative acquisition duty. It differs from both Kyles's duty to learn of information already held by those acting for the prosecution in this case and imputation of every government's office's files.

**The supported affirmative case for an extension:** The prosecution chooses the immunized witness and relies centrally on his credibility. A defined corrections file directly concerns that credibility; its pattern of blaming others and its contradictions of the State's nonviolent-truthful-witness presentation are more than a generalized hope of finding impeachment. Brady and Giglio protect the fairness of the trial, and Kyles makes clear that personal prosecutorial ignorance does not itself settle disclosure responsibility. A duty confined to this known witness and an identified file can be evaluated on its own scope without equating it to unlimited discovery or every agency's constructive knowledge. The fact that counsel knew the file existed does not establish that counsel knew its materially favorable contents.

**The supported substantive objection:** Brady's existing responsibility for information within a case's prosecution team does not automatically require the prosecutor to create or conduct every useful credibility investigation. The DOC repository's relationship to this prosecution remains unestablished. The proposed rule adds a duty to obtain evidence before it is held by those acting for the prosecution. Its justification must therefore explain why this defined witness-file relationship makes acquisition part of the State's constitutional trial obligation, and what bounds that obligation. The known history and the defense's own letter also matter to the distinction between State suppression and a defense investigation that did not occur. Those facts do not conclusively answer the distinction or supply an ineffective-assistance holding. The source establishes no categorical inability of the defense to obtain the file, no deliberately arranged avoidance by prosecutors, and no finding that all the relevant contents were already known by either side.

Neither argument establishes litigant misconduct from a research gap. No new accessibility, cost, control or file-sharing fact is assumed to make either path easier. The constitutionality of a bounded duty can be assessed without deciding that it applies to every central witness or to every government repository. The word bounded is not itself a justification: the identified prosecution choice and the file's relationship to fair trial preparation must do the legal work.

## The narrower path and the mandate

For Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, a corrected gateway inquiry legitimately precedes any present writ. That is the sufficient remand ground. If the gateway fails and no independently available route exists, there is no need to decide acquisition or Brady merits. If a lawful route to merits exists and actual case-team possession or knowledge is established for the material suppressed evidence, existing Kyles can resolve the claim without deciding the extension.

**Actual attribution is only a potentially obviating route.** Its presently inconclusive state is not proof that this route succeeds. A court must not require the prisoner to prove actual team possession as the exclusive way of proceeding while purporting to leave the acquisition theory open. If actual attribution is absent or insufficient and the bounded acquisition theory remains necessary and properly presented, it must be considered on its constitutional merits and on any actually applicable collateral conditions. The remand may permit supported attribution findings, but does not mandate extra factual proceedings solely to avoid an otherwise properly decidable legal question. It orders no automatic hearing or acquisition of all government files.

For O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, current-standard gateway failure independently defeats this otherwise-abusive Brady route. They can leave the acquisition duty undecided without implying that it could never govern a properly available claim. Their dispositions retain independent treatment of the other unaddressed claims. Should later proceedings lawfully reopen this particular merits issue on a different established basis, their reservations supply no predetermined constitutional rejection.

The primary remedial language is therefore clarified to preserve **both** supported merits theories after any required gate: existing case-team attribution and the proposed bounded acquisition obligation. Neither theory is established by the present record simply because the other is uncertain.

Neither classification is decided here. Nor does failure to establish a Teague objection establish the substantive correctness of the proposed duty.
