## Verified historical results and qualifications

### Geissal v. Moore Medical Corp., 524 U.S. 74

Full opinion and notes reviewed. Souter for a unanimous Court; no separate writing. Vacated and remanded, not an award of benefits or damages. Election timing controls; continuously existing other coverage, including coverage becoming primary, is not newly acquired coverage after election. Later-acquired coverage triggers the termination provision only subject to its condition-specific preexisting-condition protection. The 1996 HIPAA qualification was not effective when this dispute arose. The significant-gap comparison between plans has no statutory basis. Rule 54(b) judgment reached Counts I/II, not III/IV; do not turn the rejection of COBRA ineligibility into a separate new estoppel holding or invent full Aetna reimbursement.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-74.pdf.

### Hopkins v. Reeves, 524 U.S. 88

Opinion and all footnotes reviewed. Thomas for Rehnquist, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer, and himself; Stevens alone dissented. Reversed conditional habeas grant. Beck's capital-specific removal of existing lesser included offenses differs from Nebraska's generally applicable definition that felony murder has no lesser included homicide offenses. State discretion does not become a license to manipulate definitions arbitrarily. Court expressly left open whether jury-versus-judge sentencing alone would distinguish Beck (n7). Enmund/Tison culpability can be determined at sentencing or appeal under Cabana; it is not a new trial element. Manslaughter-definition arbitrariness argument was not presented first to Nebraska; second-degree murder requires intent that felony murder does not. Teague was raised first in the cert petition, and the Court chose merits rather than deciding it (n3). Stevens grounds: capital eligibility supplies the intent/moral-equivalent culpability whose absence justified the state distinction, making Beck applicable; n2 discusses Nebraska v. White's one-offense account of premeditated/felony murder and possible developing state law. Do not summarize him as merely favoring all lesser-related-offense instructions.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-88.pdf.

### Cass County v. Leech Lake Band, 524 U.S. 103

Full controlling opinion and notes reviewed, with Cornell cross-check for n5. Thomas for a unanimous Court; no separate writing. Reversed only the eight pine/homestead parcels; cross-petition on thirteen allotted parcels was denied. Freely alienable reservation land made so by Congress is ad-valorem taxable unless Congress clearly manifests contrary intent; tribal repurchase alone does not restore immunity. Taxability lasts only until trust restoration; seven of the eight were restored in 1995. Yakima's transaction-excise-tax rule is distinct and not adjudicated here.

Material source defect: the official govinfo PDF at printed p115 n5 visibly duplicates the Nonintercourse Act argument and drops the question-presented clause. This is not merely extraction noise; the rendered page `cass_footnote5.png` confirms it. Cornell's contemporaneous slip opinion has the coherent text: applicability of the Nonintercourse Act to reacquired land was not decided because outside the question presented (taxability if land remains freely alienable) and not addressed below. Preserve the reservation; do not construe the malformed official sentence as a holding of inalienability.

Sources: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-103.pdf; https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/97-174.ZO.html (n5); https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/97-174.ZS.html (unanimity).

### Dooley v. Korean Air Lines, 524 U.S. 116

Full opinion and notes reviewed. Thomas for a unanimous Court; no separate writing. Affirmed. DOHSA's express beneficiary, pecuniary-damages and limited-survival choices exclude adding the deceased's pre-death pain/suffering through general maritime survival law for a death covered by DOHSA. Limited section 765 survival substitutes the personal representative when an injured plaintiff dies during suit but preserves section 762 compensation. This does not decide whether general maritime law ever supplies survival outside this statutory setting (n2). Moragne's overruling of The Harrisburg and Gaudet's territorial-water recovery remain distinct; Higginbotham precludes overriding Congress's chosen high-seas damages, Zicherman supplies applicable domestic law and had expressly reserved this pain/suffering issue.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-116.pdf.

### New Mexico ex rel. Ortiz v. Reed, 524 U.S. 151

Full per curiam and footnote reviewed. Certiorari granted, reversed and remanded; no noted separate writing, tally, or named vote roster. Do not fabricate a disclosed 9-0 poll. ### Muscarello v. United States, 524 U.S. 125

Full opinion and dissent, including every footnote, reviewed. Breyer for Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, and himself; Ginsburg dissented with Rehnquist, Scalia, Souter. Affirmed BOTH No. 96-1654 (Muscarello, Fifth Circuit) and No. 96-8837 (Cleveland/Gray-Santana, First Circuit). Knowing possession and conveyance in an accompanied vehicle suffices, including locked glove compartment or trunk; readiness/accessibility is not additionally required, but the separate during-and-in-relation-to requirement remains. Personal agency/possession differentiates carrying from broader transport or shipment. Bailey's active-employment meaning of use remains independent. The ordinary meaning, statutory purpose and context resolve the issue without grievous residual ambiguity under the majority's lenity standard. Ginsburg's dissent does not demand literal bodily contact: n7 includes a gun in a nearby briefcase/handbag or at the saddle, approximating a pocket/holster. Its grounds are context, related firearms provisions distinguishing transportation from bearing at hand, Bailey's immediacy, lenity, and the Guidelines' continuing possession enhancement (so no punishment gap), rather than no sanction for drug-related gun possession. Her n12 explains why Poff's leave-the-gun-home remark concerned the original use prong, not unlawful-carry prong. Treat quoted historical Smith/Wells/Reno propositions against actual entering law during reconciliation, not as automatic current authority.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-125.pdf.

### Bryan v. United States, 524 U.S. 184

Full controlling opinion and both separate writings, including all footnotes, reviewed. Stevens for O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer, and himself; Souter also concurred alone. Scalia dissented with Rehnquist and Ginsburg. Affirmed. Section 924(a)(1)(D) requires knowledge of unlawfulness of the conduct, not of the specific federal licensing requirement. Knowing factual elements differs from willful illegality; context may require more (Liparota, tax statutes, Ratzlaf) and the latter decisions are not overruled. Rejection of licensing-knowledge requirement does not approve the later instruction saying no knowledge of lawbreaking was needed. Part V acknowledges that instruction's error and gives four reasons against reversal: no specific preserved objection beyond the rejected theory, whole-charge context, absence of the claim in the court of appeals, and the limited cert question. Souter would vacate if the specific objection had been raised and preserved; his actual join includes the Court's opinion. Scalia's dissent requires awareness that unlicensed dealing is unlawful, not ability to cite the exact statute or federal character. It rejects borrowing illegality from any unrelated offense, sees offense-specific knowledge supported by section 922(b)(3), and ultimately rests on lenity after unresolved ambiguity. The dissent's comparative presumptions are not its final independent rule.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-184.pdf.

### AT&T v. Central Office Telephone, 524 U.S. 214

Full controlling opinion, concurrence, dissent, and notes reviewed. Scalia for Rehnquist, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer, and himself (seven); Stevens dissented; O'Connor took no part. Rehnquist concurred alone. Reversed. Tariff exclusivity covers price-affecting services/privileges, including promised faster provisioning and allocation contrary to the tariff. Claims seeking preferences inconsistent with the tariff cannot evade the rule by sounding in tort or alleging willfulness; the tariff's willful-misconduct liability exception removes tariff limitations, not statutory limits. Section 414 preserves compatible remedies; section 202 discrimination remedies are separate, but COT's late attempted amendment was denied and not appealed (n1). Majority n2 relies on the question presented and respondent's failure to advance independent tort grounds/remand as an alternative; it does not adjudicate every possible independent tort. Rehnquist emphasizes this necessary derivative-claim premise: the tariff governs covered service terms, not all customer-carrier relations, slander, or genuinely independent interference. Stevens agrees with preemption of genuinely derivative claims but sees independent customer slamming, disclosure of markup, and possibly misappropriation of confidential data; he would remand for a new trial, not sustain the undifferentiated verdict wholesale. His n2 notes the verdict did not divide contract/tort damages; n4 conditions the database theory on evidence. His Nader/savings-clause argument preserves generally applicable tort duties incidentally affecting rates. Do not summarize Stevens as rejecting tariff exclusivity itself.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-214.pdf.

### United States v. Bestfoods, 524 U.S. 51

The root operator supplied source-review assistance after reading all 23 official PDF pages and notes in sequential complete ranges; this paragraph preserves that attribution rather than claiming the reconciliation context independently read every page. Souter for a unanimous Court, with no separate writings; vacated and remanded. Corporate ownership/stock control alone does not create derivative CERCLA liability absent veil piercing, but a parent may be directly liable for its own operation of the facility. The operation must specifically concern pollution, hazardous-waste handling/disposal, or environmental compliance. Ordinary investor oversight, finance, budget, and general policy supervision is insufficient. Dual officers/directors presumptively act for the subsidiary; government must rebut that attribution through departures from ordinary norms, particularly parent-serving acts contrary to subsidiary interests. CPC-only employee Williams's reported activities suffice for remand, not an ultimate liability finding. Notes: n7 Aerojet no-disposal/third-party defenses not before the Court; n9 derivative no-liability result unchallenged and state/federal veil-law choice reserved; n10 derivative liability may be operator as well as owner liability; n13 dual-hat presumption weakens as conduct departs norms; n14 existing findings insufficient for dual-hat responsibility, but additional evidence may be identified on remand. Do not collapse owner/operator, derivative/direct, or facility/subsidiary distinctions.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-51.pdf.

### National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, 130 F.3d 1041

The complete saved lower opinion, Henderson concurrence, Sentelle dissent, and every note were reviewed in four contiguous text ranges. No Supreme Court merits comparator exists. The D.C. Circuit affirmed summary judgment 2-1 on December 5, 1997; Wald and Henderson supported validity through distinct reasoning, and Sentelle dissented. Do not call Wald's entire opinion a majority rationale. Wald relied on channels protection, aggregate biodiversity/option value, and prevention of destructive interstate competition. Henderson rejected the channels theory and speculative future medicinal value, but accepted ecological interdependence and commercial-development effects. Sentelle rejected all proposed rationales as insufficiently tied to interstate commerce, without a jurisdictional nexus or stopping point; he distinguished regulation of commerce from regulation that itself affects commerce. The disagreement concerns historical Lopez's application, not a simulated Supreme Court rule.

Material boundaries: suit challenges section 9(a)(1)(B) as applied to the Fly, not the entire ESA. The section 10 incidental-take permit requires a conservation plan, public-comment opportunity, incidental taking, minimization to the practicable extent, adequate funding, and no appreciable reduction of survival/recovery likelihood (Wald n2). The substation already had a November 1995 permit; the intersection/corridor redesign was separate. Habitat harm's actual-killing/injury requirement remains stated in Henderson n1. Wald's bibliography/medical possibilities are argument, not established local commercial trade in the Fly; Henderson's concurrence does not establish that trade either. The lower text's abbreviated timeline has tension (its complaint date October 20, 1995 precedes a described November discussion, and it places a May 1993 notice after listing although the quoted final regulation was September 1993); avoid using these shorthand dates to create an independent timeliness or permit-default conclusion. Lower court merits reasoning supplies no SC lineup and no automatic current authority.

Source: https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/130/130.F3d.1041.96-5354.html.
