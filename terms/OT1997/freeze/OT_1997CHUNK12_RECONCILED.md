## Disability: operative rule, application and disagreements

### Five direct supporters: Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer

Section 12102(2)(A) requires an impairment, a major life activity and substantial limitation of that individual's activity. HIV disease can satisfy the impairment element before its most serious symptoms; section 36.104 expressly includes symptomatic and asymptomatic HIV disease and separately recognizes physiological disorders of relevant body systems. This is not a holding that every diagnosis alone proves every statutory element. Section 12201(a), except where the ADA otherwise provides, preserves at least the standards of Rehabilitation Act Title V and its implementing regulations. Arline's contagious-disease analysis does not convert its reservation concerning an asymptomatic carrier into exclusion from the ADA or an automatic disability conclusion.

Reproduction is a major life activity. The statutory term concerns significance and does not restrict coverage to public, economic or daily activities. The regulatory list introduced as examples is illustrative. Caring for oneself is not necessarily public or economic, and reproduction is not less significant merely because it is not continuously performed. The distinction between impairment of a reproductive system and a substantial limitation of reproduction nevertheless remains: neither item alone supplies the whole definition.

Abbott's specific, unchallenged testimony that HIV controlled her decision not to bear a child, considered with the supported perinatal transmission risks, establishes substantial limitation on this record. Complete inability to conceive is unnecessary. The reported approximately 25 percent untreated risk and approximately 8 percent treated risk are historical record figures, not a general legal numerical cutoff or modern medical guidance. Even considering the treated figure, the danger to the child remains substantial; the Court need not decide whether mitigating measures must generally be disregarded. The general negative answer about impaired life functions is preserved as her lay description of daily functioning; it does not retract the specific childbearing testimony or create a contrary medical account. A pre-infection intention to conceive, an attempted pregnancy or willingness to take the risk is not an additional statutory condition.

The strongest contrary argument is that a plaintiff moving for summary judgment must prove a limitation in her own life, and that the general answer and unknown prior plans may distinguish an abstract restriction from an actual one. The five reject an inference that an unknown prior preference defeats the specific unchallenged account. They do not resolve credibility against the opposing party or treat missing research as proof. Yeskey separates institutional coverage from individual entitlement but does not decide this statutory element; ordinary Rule 56 requirements remain intact. No automatic HIV-disability rule, record-of holding, regarded-as holding or ruling on every other activity follows.

The controlling supported application is perinatal risk and Abbott's own testimony. Historical discussion of partner-transmission studies, state criminal statutes, insurance and treatment costs may explain the comparator, but those matters were not supplied as an independently modeled current-case ground. They are not silently added as independently sufficient holdings. A proposed broader ground needs the proper clean neutral/model handoff. The five's existing sufficient ground does not depend on doing so.

Ginsburg may separately emphasize that HIV affects family relations, employment potential, self-care and the ability to obtain care, and that discrimination before visible symptoms cannot be justified simply by the disease's invisibility. Her historical broader view of actual or perceived limitation remains a noncontrolling explanation consistent with her full join. ## Direct threat: common rule and different grounds

### Common objective rule—eight Associates

Section 12182(a) prohibits disability discrimination by a covered public accommodation; a provider's professional office falls within section 12181(7)(F) when its operations affect commerce. No live commerce or office-coverage dispute is supplied. Section 12182(b)(3) permits exclusion when the individual poses a significant risk to others' health or safety that cannot be eliminated by modifications of policies, practices or procedures, or auxiliary aids or services. Any conceivable residual risk is not the same as a significant risk, and the statute demands neither an absolute guarantee of safety nor confirmed past transmission as an invariant prerequisite.

Section 36.208 requires an individualized assessment based on reasonable judgment relying on current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence. Nature, duration, severity, probability of injury and available mitigation perform distinct work. The relevant standpoint is the information available to the provider and the profession when treatment was refused. A good-faith subjective belief or a professional title cannot itself establish the statutory defense. The conditional reasonableness language in petition Question 3 is the rule proposed for evaluation, not a finding that Bragdon's assessment was reasonable.

A provider's supported medical analysis receives consideration according to its foundation and relevance. Arline's reservation about reliance on private physicians did not create personal immunity for the provider's state of mind. Scientific uncertainty may matter when supported; a requirement of objective evidence does not require impossible certainty. Daubert and Joiner preserve reliable foundation and an adequate connection to the proposition offered, not a universal study-matching or numerical-risk rule. Their admissibility, appellate-review and combined-inference holdings do not independently decide the substantive degree of dental risk or the whole record's summary-judgment sufficiency.

The hospital offer supplies no separate defense. The lower opinion expressly reports that Bragdon did not pursue a hospital-modification theory or establish comparative hospital safety; the record also did not establish privileges, available safeguards or a scientific basis for greater safety. Section 12201(d) does not compel Abbott to accept the offered accommodation or facility charge. These points do not establish a general prohibition on medically supported additional precautions in another case.

The lower court expressly reserved a hypothetical later medical breakthrough overwhelmingly vindicating the concern, particularly where relief has prospective operation. No such development is established here. The conduct-date rule is not expanded into an exceptionless rule about every later circumstance relevant to prospective relief, and no new exception is found triggered.

Official status does not prove what proposition the authority actually adopted or establish that the proposition resolves the particular risk. A professional association may supply respected scientific evidence but is not thereby a public-health authority. The actual foundation, institutional role, date and inference remain distinct.

The strongest affirmance argument is substantial: the First Circuit rejected a conclusive-consensus rule, allowed supported contrary expert opinion, considered all eight proffers separately and together, and gave the CDC and Association materials a reasoned reading. ## Operative propositions the eight Associates can jointly adopt

### A. Objective grounds for these initial seizures

An individualized vehicle stop to investigate insurance compliance requires objective grounds sufficient to justify that seizure. On this record, the old refusals to produce insurance information, considered with the intervening time, changed vehicles, valid inspection stickers and absence of fresh evidence of noncoverage, did not supply reasonable suspicion for stops 3–10 or 12. Anticipated refusal after a new stop cannot itself furnish the antecedent authority to make that stop.

Prouse requires sufficient objective justification for discretionary document-check stops; it is not confined to arbitrary selection of strangers. The known person and prior encounters are circumstances to consider, but do not eliminate the suspicion requirement. Bostick distinguishes permissible questioning from detention and rejects refusal alone as an automatic justification for seizure. Its consensual-encounter setting does not decide every consequence of violating a valid statutory production duty. The present decision rests on the actual insurance record, not a categorical rule that silence can never have evidentiary weight or that a valid inspection sticker conclusively establishes current insurance.

Whren preserves the independently observed violations supporting stops 1, 2 and 11, while requiring separate grounds for materially greater restraint. Ornelas supplies the current allocation of legal and factual review: the ultimate sufficiency of the undisputed initial-stop circumstances is independently reviewed, with protection of actual historical findings and genuine consideration of grounded officer inferences. No new observation or inference of actual noncoverage may be invented. Actual coverage is not substituted for Macaluso's knowledge. The renewed September refusal following stop 11 did not, on these circumstances, supply sufficient grounds for stop 12 two-and-a-half months later; the license/registration refusal and disputed alcohol observation occurred after initiation and cannot retroactively justify it.

No fixed staleness period, universal rule about insurance cards, general exemption from traffic enforcement, probable-cause requirement for every investigative stop, or constitutionalized state evidentiary rule is adopted. Stops are judged individually, not by counting total encounters or finding that Macaluso disliked Diamondstone.

### B. Fifth Amendment and the state insurance obligation remain distinct

The privilege can protect answers sought in a civil or informal setting when they expose the person to criminal responsibility. Lefkowitz v. Turley preserves that rule. The Vermont decriminalization and the actual nature of this insurance sanction, rather than the label alone, defeat this privilege claim on the supplied record. The earlier 1987 rulings concerned the earlier legal setting and do not settle the post-1989 question.

Byers supports the distinction between generally applicable financial-responsibility regulation and compelled criminal incrimination, but its separate rationales are not silently converted into a general majority exemption for regulatory questioning. No criminal use of this insurance information or disguised criminal sanction is supported here. The potential DUI accusation at stop 12 is not proof that compelled insurance information exposed Diamondstone to that accusation. No general DUI-interrogation privilege is decided.

The absence of this privilege does not establish grounds for a seizure. Nor does permission to consider silence establish that silence alone satisfies Vermont's insurance-liability requirements. The reported traffic-court decisions rejected that evidentiary sufficiency; the defendants' characterization of Administrative Procedure 38(D) and (E) does not become a holding that every refusal was an independently continuing offense. The Supreme Court need not resolve an unpresented state-law construction to decide these stops.

The traffic-court rulings supply neither issue preclusion against these defendants nor an independent federal right to damages. ## Eastern Enterprises v. Apfel

### Questions and operative alternatives

The review concerns the constitutional retroactive tier-three liability assessed under section 9706(a)(3), not a renewed statutory assignment claim, other assignment tiers, unassigned beneficiaries, or the separate 1992 plan. Eastern employed these retirees before 1966 and participated in the early benefit undertaking. It also retained subsidiary ownership until 1987 and received substantial dividends; direct withdrawal in 1965 is not equated with severance of every economic relationship. Conversely, those later relationships are not proof of a lifetime promise or an established indemnity construction.

**E1 — Channel and remedy.** The due-process challenge to an enforced federal payment obligation is justiciable in the district court, with actual assessment supplying injury. Its existence does not depend on the Government's abandonment of a jurisdictional objection. The Tucker Act compensation question must be addressed independently for a takings ground. The three Justices choosing takings invalidity would permit declaratory/injunctive relief against this application because the challenged statutory scheme imposes ongoing payments to the privately operated fund and supplies no compensatory feature that would preserve its intended allocation through a Treasury award; their conclusion is about this statutory enforcement setting, not a general permission to enjoin a compensable appropriation. The remaining five do not use compensation jurisdiction to avoid the due-process merits or impose a claim for damages never sought. They reject the asserted takings ground at the antecedent property/regulatory distinction and do not decide an entitlement to Treasury compensation.

**E2 — Takings predicate and application.** The invalidity path treats the substantially retrospective extraction from Eastern's assets for this bounded class as an extraordinary regulatory burden examined under the economic-impact, reasonable-expectations and character factors applied in Concrete Pipe and Connolly. Large projected payments alone are not enough. The long retrospective reach, relationship between the pre-1966 undertaking and much later benefit guarantees, and concentrated assignment of their expense matter together. The competing path holds that an obligation to pay money from unspecified general assets does not identify an appropriated fund, parcel, earned interest, or other particular property interest; the objection belongs principally in due process. Phillips's ownership rule for actual interest does not make every payment obligation an appropriation. Concrete Pipe's rejection of a taking through a contextual analysis does not compel recognition of a taking for every general liability; at minimum its analysis supplies an independently sufficient rejection where applicable, not a categorical victory for either party here.

**E3 — Retroactive due process.** Carlton requires a legitimate purpose rationally served by the retrospective operation itself; reliance does not automatically defeat legislation, nor does revenue collection automatically validate it. Concrete Pipe sustains proportionate pooled-benefit allocation in its actual participation setting. The validity path finds a rational allocation to the employers whose workers earned benefits within the shared undertaking, with actual former employment and participation rather than assignment of strangers. A private contractual duration limit cannot alone constitutionalize immunity from later adjustment. The invalidity path finds the particular remote and severe imposition insufficiently connected to what this pre-1966 employer undertook or could reasonably have anticipated; employment and participation cannot by themselves justify assigning later lifetime obligations without limit. Neither path depends on a proved binding lifetime promise, an exact damages total, a fixed maximum retrospective period, or an invented fact about retained corporate control.

They leave materially different supported facts and independent defenses open.

**Official path — affirm the personal speech-immunity judgment without deciding ultimate constitutional protection.** The concrete close working relationship and reported coworker distrust distinguish the case sufficiently from Pickering and the predominantly clerical role in Rankin. Connick and Givhan leave genuine room to consider prospective loss of trust and the circumstances of an internal confrontation. The preconduct cases do not make the immunity question clear enough to impose personal damages here. This path does not treat the presence of balancing as a defense, rely on probationary status, attribute fraud to Furuno, find malicious falsity, or invent a same-decision showing. It need not endorse every consideration in the Ninth Circuit's opinion.

**Strongest contrary ground:** Public-funding accuracy is a substantial concern, the disruption is described as minor, and internal reporting is affirmatively protected. A forecast built merely from a coworker's hurt feelings or the fact of criticism is not a legally adequate governmental interest; Givhan is a teacher–principal case and therefore prevents reliance on the relationship's label alone.

**Answer adopted by the official-path Justices:** Those points may prevail on ultimate protection, and no generic trust exception follows. But the personal-damages question is distinct. Existing law recognizes the relevant employer interests and leaves sufficiently unsettled their application to this acknowledged daily relationship and reported distrust. The officials' objectively reasonable legal uncertainty can support immunity without a merits holding that the nonrenewal was constitutional. This is not a ruling that every reasonable forecast wins the balance.
