# Objective record supplement for neutral validation

These are supported published recitations of underlying pleadings, proceedings and evidence, not new findings or a ruling on any Supreme Court question. The neutral reviewer must validate their materiality and framing before modeling. Original pleadings and appendices have not been independently examined. The source links identify provenance; the source opinions' dispositions, reasoning and lineups are withheld from modeling.

## Textron

The reviewed decision is 117 F.3d 119 (Third Circuit, 1997). The November 1995 complaint alleged that the employer concealed subcontracting plans during negotiations. It sought compensatory and punitive damages and a declaration that the collective agreement was voidable at the union's option. The agreement ran April 1, 1994–April 1, 1997, prohibited strikes, and required seven days' notice before subcontracting agreements. The complaint alleged no breach by either party. The published record account reports no threatened strike. The union's alternative federal-common-law jurisdiction assertion appeared in a brief footnote; that alternative was outside the supplied question about §301(a). An actual or threatened enforcement dispute must not be inferred from the requested declaration alone.

Source: [published record recitation](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/97-463.ZO.html), introductory facts, declaratory-request record description and note 1. Underlying appendix and complete briefs are not independently recovered.

## Montana v. Crow Tribe

The reviewed Ninth Circuit decisions are 92 F.3d 826, amended at 98 F.3d 1194 (1996). The requested disgorgement was approximately $58.2 million: $46.8 million severance taxes and $11.4 million gross-proceeds taxes. The payment periods are described as 1975–1982 and 1975–1987 respectively; the pleadings described recovery through the 1983 and 1988 payment boundaries. Registry deposits began January 1983 for severance taxes and November 1987 for gross-proceeds taxes. The current amended complaints sought restitution and interest, not alternative compensatory damages for actual financial loss. Earlier pleadings had broader requests; a 1993 proposed amendment should not be treated as an allowed current damages claim without further verification. The district court received conflicting evidence about market effects but made no finding on that issue. Amounts already deposited were distributed for the Tribe's benefit. These facts do not settle entitlement to preregistry payments.

Source: [published record recitation](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/96-1829.ZO.html), facts and notes 3, 7–8. Any precise scope of the rejected amendment should be checked if dispositive.

## Bousley

The reviewed decision is 97 F.3d 284 (Eighth Circuit, 1996). The indictment charged firearm use under §924(c)(1), not carrying. The published record account identifies no evidence that the Government refrained from charging carrying in exchange for the plea. No actually forgone more-serious charge is established by this supplement. The defendant appealed his sentence but did not challenge the plea's validity on direct review. His later collateral motion challenged the connection between bedroom firearms and garage drug activity. The district court relied on proximity and accessibility. The Government did not argue that failure to raise factual innocence initially waived that route. None of these recitations resolves the governing collateral-review or innocence standard.

Source: [published record recitation](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/96-8516.ZO.html), introductory procedural account and indictment description citing appendix pages 5–6. Original indictment and plea materials have not been independently read.

## Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal

The reviewed decision is 118 F.3d 628 (Ninth Circuit, 1997). Three earlier federal petitions were dismissed for unexhausted claims. The Ford claim was included in the March 1993 petition and dismissed as premature at the State's request; relief granted on another ground was later reversed, with the 1996 appellate mandate expressly preserving later Ford litigation. A March 1997 reopening request was denied before an execution warrant. After the warrant, Arizona courts found the prisoner competent. The renewed federal motion challenged the state conclusions and procedures; the district court dismissed for lack of jurisdiction on May 16, 1997. The applicant then requested appellate authorization. The Ninth Circuit held authorization unnecessary and transferred the petition back, rather than granting authorization on §2244(b)'s substantive gatekeeping criteria. The legal characterization of that action remains for decision.

Source: [published procedural recitation](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/97-300.ZO.html), introductory chronology; also 80 F.3d 1301, 1309 n.1 (1996).

## Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes

The reviewed decision is 93 F.3d 497 (Eighth Circuit, 1996). In spring 1992, staff and an Associated Press bureau chief planned five debates and approximately 53 minutes of questions and answers in each hour. Their stated selection category was major-party candidates plus other candidates with strong popular support. The two major candidates were invited June 17; Forbes qualified for the ballot with 2,000 signatures in August, requested access August 24, and was refused September 4. Staff testified to lack of organization, voter support and press recognition as a serious candidate. The jury expressly rejected political pressure and disagreement with Forbes's views as reasons for exclusion. That verdict does not independently establish every proposed objective-criteria requirement. The prior statutory access claim had been dismissed for nonexhaustion and was no longer pressed. No broader past selection practice is established here.

Source: [published record recitation](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/96-779.ZO.html), Part I; underlying affidavit paragraph 5 and trial record are described, not independently inspected.
