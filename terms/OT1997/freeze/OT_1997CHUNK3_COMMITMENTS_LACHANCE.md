## Components and shared operative conclusions

After independent assessment, all eight Associates reach the following bounded dispositions; their individually supported qualifications appear below.

**C1 — Fifth Amendment procedural-due-process denial immunity.** Reverse the categorical rule that an employee's opportunity to answer an agency charge constitutionally immunizes a knowingly false factual denial from an otherwise lawful independent falsification charge or penalty consideration. Loudermill protects notice, an explanation of the employer's evidence and a meaningful opportunity to answer, with adequate subsequent process. It does not require an employer to immunize deliberate factual deception. Bryson supplies the surviving principle that a claimed objection to an official inquiry does not generally authorize knowingly deliberate falsehood; its criminal elements and setting are not transplanted into these employment proceedings.

This ground does not equate losing an underlying charge with lying. A good-faith factual denial, mistake, uncertain recollection, dispute about legal characterization, or insistence that the agency prove its charge remains distinct from established knowing falsification. A procedural nonadmission is not automatically a false assertion of a concrete fact. The agency must prove the actual falsification charge, including its requisite culpability, under the governing administrative standard. “Independent proof” means a separate supported determination of the charge and its actual elements, not a new rule that the evidence must come from a different source than the underlying misconduct evidence. No criminal jury, criminal burden, universal materiality element or mandatory corroboration requirement is added.

**C2 — civil-service statutory protection.** Reject a categorical denial immunity inferred from §§7513 and 7701. Section 7513's right to at least seven days to answer orally and in writing, submit affidavits and documents, receive representation and specific reasons, and obtain the available hearing/appeal protections has operative force without protecting proved knowing falsehood. Section 7513(a) still requires cause promoting service efficiency; §7701(c)'s ordinary-misconduct preponderance standard and defenses for harmful procedural error, prohibited personnel practices and legal invalidity remain independent. Section 7703(c)'s appellate substantial-evidence and legality review is not the agency's proof burden. None of these texts guarantees the lower court's denial/fabricated-explanation immunity line. No particular charge's regulatory validity, service nexus, penalty or harmful-error finding is decided.

**C3 — enhancement and impeachment.** Set aside the categorical legal prohibition on considering a proved knowing false denial in lawful penalty assessment or using relevant supported falsity to assess credibility, including the identified McManus impeachment ruling. Rejection of that prohibition is permission for ordinary lawful adjudication, not a command to enhance any penalty or draw any adverse inference. A denial's unsuccessful outcome alone supplies no automatic enhancement or impeachment fact. Applicable proof, relevance, procedural fairness and other actual defenses remain for the proper decisionmaker. No unrelated evidence rule is displaced.

**C4 — Barrett/Roberts reporting allegations and self-incrimination.** Vacate the reporting-claim disposition only insofar as it rests on the same categorical premise that an employee cannot be required to provide information because it would implicate the employee. Preserve the distinct Garrity/Gardner protections. Compelled answers obtained under threat of public-employment loss cannot be used against the speaker in a criminal prosecution as Garrity holds; discharge solely for refusing to waive the protection against use and fruits is forbidden under Gardner. Its distinction for questions specifically, directly and narrowly concerning official duties without a required waiver remains intact. No present respondent is found to satisfy or fail those predicates. Correcting a due-process right-to-deny theory neither decides a privilege claim nor creates a general obligation to confess every possible offense. The reported false-time findings in Barrett and Roberts were outside the Federal Circuit review and remain undisturbed.

**C5 — judgment and remedy on each reviewed matter.** Reverse the two appellate judgments insofar as they endorse the categorical denial immunity and its dependent charge, penalty and impeachment bars; remand for further proceedings under the corrected legal rule. For the Barrett/Roberts reporting branch, vacate its dependent disposition and remand without resolving any actual privilege or specific reporting duty. The mandate is a legal correction, not reinstatement of every original adverse agency action. No compulsory hearing, new evidence demand, criminal referral, immunity waiver, final falsity finding or final penalty follows.

## Enhanced two-path comparisons

**Constitutional and statutory answer right.** The employees' strongest path would preserve protection for a simple denial because a meaningful chance to contest charges would be chilled if every failed defense generated another offense. The lower court attempted to avoid universal deception immunity by distinguishing denial from affirmative fabrication and precharge investigation. That distinction does not answer the case in which the denial is independently proved knowingly false: deliberately misleading the adjudicator is not made necessary to a fair opportunity to answer by its grammatical form. The governing statutory notice, proof and legal-error protections can protect legitimate contest without barring a separate supported falsification charge. The selected path therefore rejects categorical immunity while making the actual knowing-falsity predicate indispensable. An alternative that punishes every unsuccessful denial would fail the same procedural protections and is not selected.

**Penalty and impeachment.** A narrower employee path would prohibit mechanical enhancement and deny automatic credibility consequences, even if the constitutional categorical rule is rejected. That concern is supported and is preserved as a qualification. It cannot sustain the actual across-the-board bar where knowing falsity, relevance and a lawful penalty basis are established. The Government's strongest counterpoint is that direct deception may bear on trust and the service; it still must establish the actual charge and lawful sanction rather than treating a duty to answer as a duty to admit guilt. Correction leaves the appropriate consequence to the statutory process.

**Reporting and self-incrimination.** A broad Government path could treat the absence of a right to lie as resolving the reporting charges and authorizing punishment for silence. Garrity and Gardner make that extension unavailable. A broad employee path could treat any self-implicating answer as immune from administrative obligation or discipline; Gardner's official-duty distinction defeats that categorical proposition. Both privilege and reporting duty require their actual predicates. The selected judgment removes only reliance on the invalid denial-immunity premise, preserving a legally valid self-incrimination defense and the limits of 5 C.F.R. §5.4 as reproduced, including information whose disclosure is prohibited by law or regulation.

**Common rule versus final employee result.** Immediate reinstatement of all agency actions could be defended as restoring decisions previously set aside under an erroneous rule. It would improperly decide unreviewed facts, remaining legal defenses, nexus and penalty questions and would erase the distinct McManus record gap. Conversely, inability to choose a final sanction does not require affirming an invalid categorical rule. Bounded remand lawfully resolves the common issue and leaves each remaining question where the governing statutory process puts it.

## Eight independent Associate commitments

### John Paul Stevens

**Judgment/reach:** C1–C3 reversed as specified, C4 dependently vacated, C5 remand on both judgments and each affected employee component. Reach the constitutional answer question and reject the asserted independent statutory immunity; no threshold dismissal is warranted on the supplied facts.

**Own support and sufficient ground:** He joined White's full Loudermill opinion (1985), including meaningful pretermination response and the refusal to presume an inaccurate statement a knowing lie. That protection does not require immunity after knowing falsity is properly established. His Jeffries Parts I–III joins (November 17, 1997) require legally disciplinable conduct, actual responsibility and a remedy not automatically reinstating an earlier result. His Valot protected-process joins (January 5, 1998) protect lawful nonsham invocation while expressly preserving fraud/eligibility controls; using an answer process does not immunize a proved deceptive act within it.

**Adversarial conclusion and join barriers:** The strongest employee objection is a chilling escalation based solely on losing. His Loudermill and Valot commitments require that objection to be answered through actual charge proof, genuine opportunity to contest knowledge and lawful penalty assessment. They do not sustain categorical immunity for deliberate factual deception. His Richenberg dissent (December 8) insists on real effect for a rebuttal and rejects appellate replacement of reasons; it supports preserving employee-specific adjudication rather than automatic reinstatement. Join only with those qualifications. Gaudin and Lanier, which he joined, preserve their criminal scope; no criminal element or rule of retrospective notice is imported.

### Sandra Day O'Connor

**Judgment/reach:** Adopt C1–C5, rejecting constitutional/statutory immunity and remanding without automatic individual sanctions.

**Own support and sufficient ground:** Her full Loudermill join supplies a real opportunity to respond but no presumption that an inaccurate statement is deliberate deception. Her Richenberg opinion (December 8, 1997) distinguishes meaningful rebuttal from a requirement to accept every denial while demanding the actual agency account. That principle supports adjudication of deliberate falsehood without cancelling the employee's answer right; the military inference and scrutiny rules themselves do not govern the CSRA.

**Adversarial conclusion and join barriers:** A protected-denial exception may appear necessary to prevent an agency from punishing resistance to its accusation, but that concern does not justify immunity after proper proof of an independent knowingly false factual statement. The actual allegation, preponderance burden, service nexus, lawful process and appropriate sanction remain distinct. Her Jeffries dissent and Valot dissent do not supply a broad license to punish every internal defense; they addressed different protected-conduct questions. Preserve C4's actual self-incrimination conditions, do not treat disagreement with an agency as lying, and do not silently apply Gaudin's criminal jury requirement or Hudson's civil classification as a complete due-process answer.

## Authority and remedy compatibility

Each of the eight independently joins the proposition that procedural due process and the cited CSRA provisions do not categorically immunize an otherwise lawfully punishable, proved knowing false factual denial. Each joins preserving truthful or good-faith contest, actual charge proof, statutory process and review, and valid self-incrimination protection. The same eight permit ordinary consideration of proved falsity in penalty/credibility decisions without commanding any adverse consequence. They support the qualified C4 remand, not a new substantive reporting duty or a finding that a privilege was invoked, waived or inapplicable.

The disposition does not decide the agency allegations anew, overturn preserved underlying findings, automatically reinstate original discipline, or adopt a general criminal false-statement doctrine. It does not require a defendant to prove innocence, place on employees the agency's preponderance burden, or replace §7703(c)'s actual appellate standard. The distinct recorded Board sanctions and unpublished McManus record must retain their actual procedural status while the authorized bodies consider the legal consequences of the corrected rule; the Supreme Court selects none of those final sanctions itself.

No opinion may obtain the recorded full joins by omitting the distinction between unsuccessful denial and proved knowing falsity, treating adverse credibility as a compulsory penalty, or equating the right to answer with a duty to waive self-incrimination protection. These are operative qualifications, not optional dicta. No separate-writing or authorship prediction is necessary to preserve the complete commitments.
