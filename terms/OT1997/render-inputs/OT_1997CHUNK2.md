## Event

Hudson v. United States, No. 96-976. Argued October 8, 1997; decided December 10, 1997. Certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 92 F.3d 1026, reviewing reinstatement of a criminal prosecution after civil banking sanctions.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision. No Justice was recused or otherwise absent.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: none. The criminal prosecution may proceed. The Court orders no additional accounting hearing and decides neither guilt nor the identity of the administrative predicates and charged offenses.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins |
|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and III: classification framework, combined application, and disposition | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II: Halper's personal-penalty application | Breyer | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg |
| Concurrence in Parts I and III and the judgment | Stone-Zsela | None |

## Holdings

**1. Civil intent remains rebuttable through examination of actual punitive operation.** Congress's civil designation of these banking monetary sanctions and the regulatory character of the occupational restriction establish the intended civil framework. That designation may be overcome by the clearest proof that the sanctions' purpose or effect is criminal; their supported design and operation must be assessed together, without treating deterrence, severity, occupational burden or overlap with crime as individually conclusive. Part I controls through Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

Ward supplies the intent-and-effect inquiry. Ursery preserves both its demanding override requirement and the distinct field of personal civil penalties; Hendricks requires meaningful examination of actual operation. The monetary provisions expressly impose civil assessments through administrative procedures. Their good-faith and gravity considerations affect amount and do not establish a universal willfulness element. The applicable original monetary authorization cannot be replaced by the increased tiers enacted after the February 1989 notice. Mendoza-Martinez informs punitive-effect examination without requiring a new exhaustive checklist. Civil wording is relevant, but it cannot make actual punishment immune from constitutional review.

**2. These monetary assessments survive Halper's retained personal-penalty inquiry.** A personal civil penalty remains subject to Halper's requirement of a rational remedial relationship, including reasonable approximation; that rule is not overruled or replaced by a universal statutory-face inquiry. On the supported record, the actual $44,000 in total monetary assessments, compared with the District Court's finding of $72,000 in government costs pursuing these defendants, permits a remedial characterization and requires no further accounting. Part II controls through Breyer, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

The Government may recover legitimate pursuit costs without establishing that deterrence played no part in the regulatory response. The objective relation answers the present claim; the agency's failure initially to calculate its costs does not itself establish punishment. The initial $200,000 demand is not the obligation ultimately imposed. Nor are alleged banking losses substituted for proved government costs. This application creates no automatic monetary ratio, immunity for every penalty below an asserted cost figure, or universal compensation ceiling for other sanctions.

**3. The combined sanctions do not bar this prosecution.** The banking restriction, which permits participation with written regulatory authorization, protects positions of trust in a regulated industry. Considered with the actual financial assessments, its supported operation does not provide the clearest proof of criminal punishment; the subsequent prosecution therefore is not barred on the presented multiple-punishment theory. Part III controls through Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

The restriction's substantial occupational effect does not erase its protective function, and the Court considers that effect together with the monetary burden. Kurth Ranch involved an extraordinary arrest-linked assessment on confiscated contraband whose combined operation made it a second punishment; these sanctions have no comparable defining operation. Hendricks concerned prisonlike custody and the actual failure of a civil treatment structure, facts absent here. Those distinctions preserve both precedents. A different banking scheme or materially different operation could produce a different classification. Because criminal punishment is not established, the Court does not decide Blockburger or retained Grady offense identity, attachment in punitive civil proceedings, the Excessive Fines Clause, or independent due-process objections.

## Precedent Treatment

Halper remains binding in its exceptional personal-civil-penalty field and is applied to the supported monetary obligations and costs. Ursery's rebuttable civil classification and preservation of that field remain intact. Hendricks supplies cumulative actual-operation analysis without transferring its confinement outcome. Ward and Mendoza-Martinez retain their intent/effect and punitive-indicator roles. Kurth Ranch's extraordinary-tax holding is distinguished, not narrowed. Dixon, Grady, Blockburger and Felix retain their offense-identity rules; none is applied to determine the identity of these charges. Austin's distinct Excessive Fines protection remains available in an appropriate case. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Effective December 10, 1997, the identified OCC sanctions supply no multiple-punishment bar to this prosecution. Personal monetary sanctions remain subject to Halper's reasonable-remedial-approximation inquiry, while actual combined operation remains material under the civil-intent/clearest-proof framework. Neither regulatory labeling nor a monetary comparison alone creates a categorical safe harbor. No same-offense rule, fines rule, settlement-waiver rule or confinement rule changes.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I and III and the judgment. He applies Ursery and Hendricks without alteration: the combined design and actual operation of these monetary and occupational measures do not establish the clearest proof of criminal punishment. Their accountability and protective functions differ from Kurth Ranch's extraordinary exaction and Hendricks's prisonlike application. He does not join the separate independent Halper-accounting analysis and does not reject Halper's continuing force. His ground leaves different operational facts, excessiveness and offense identity open.

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurs in the judgment. Their account requires successive criminal prosecutions, and the genuinely civil banking proceedings do not supply the first such prosecution. They would reject Halper's freestanding civil multiple-punishment method, while allowing inquiry into whether a nominally civil proceeding is actually criminal. That theory does not command a majority and does not overrule Halper.

## Procedure After Action

This Supreme Court review ends. The reinstated criminal proceedings continue in the ordinary course, with properly remaining defenses preserved. The prior appellate nonparticipation ruling retains its procedural scope; consent supplies no newly found waiver or admission. No accounting, conviction, dismissal of individual counts, or scheduled return to this Court is ordered.

The complete original administrative instruments, indictment particulars and underlying cost-hearing documents are not reproduced; no additional statutory predicate, waiver or count-specific identity finding is inferred.

## Event

Bay Area Laundry and Dry Cleaning Pension Trust Fund v. Ferbar Corp. of California, No. 96-370. Argued November 10, 1997; decided December 15, 1997. Certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 73 F.3d 971, concerning limitations on withdrawal-liability collection.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision. No nonparticipation is established.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: none. The Fund may pursue timely later installments under the rules below, but may not recover the first $345.50 installment. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment excluding that first claim. The remand awards no money, directs no full-debt payment, and grants no automatic prospective injunction.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Breyer delivers the opinion of the Court. All eight other Justices join Parts I–II on accrual, installments, acceleration and the bounded remand. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg join Part III on the first-installment exclusion; Stone-Zsela agrees in that component's judgment only. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

**1. Collection accrual requires a payment violation.** Withdrawal creates liability under 29 U.S.C. §1381, but a collection cause does not accrue until the plan has assessed liability, set a schedule and demanded payment, and the employer fails to pay an installment that is due. Section 1451(f) supplies the longer of six years after accrual or three years after the plaintiff acquired or should have acquired actual knowledge of the cause; in fraud or concealment, an action may be brought within six years after discovery of the cause. No fraud or concealment finding is made here. Parts I–II are the unanimous opinion of the Court.

The statutory sequence distinguishes liability from an enforceable delinquency. A limitation cannot begin on this collection claim before the Fund can sue for the missed payment. Knowledge of withdrawal does not itself supply knowledge of an accrued cause, and the discovery provision need not be decisive in every collection case. Milwaukee distinguishes computational dates from actual demand and payment; its calculation holding does not already decide this accrual question. North Star concerns borrowing when Congress has supplied no period, whereas §1451 supplies its own. The Ninth Circuit's March 1985 withdrawal trigger is therefore erroneous.

The plan still must assess and demand payment as soon as practicable under §1399(b)(1). Proper objections to delay remain available on their actual grounds; concern about creditor delay does not authorize substitution of withdrawal-date accrual or a finding that every delayed demand is valid. Scheduled payments commence no later than sixty days after demand notwithstanding review or appeal, as the statute provides. No exhaustion requirement postpones those payments.

**2. Installments accrue separately unless valid acceleration changes maturity.** Each missed installment gives rise to a separate collection claim and its own limitations period. An expired first-payment claim does not extinguish later timely installments; after a legally effective election to accelerate, the period on the accelerated debt runs from that exercise. A later aggregate demand cannot revive installments already barred.

Congress authorized installment obligations and distinguished ordinary delinquency from default and acceleration. Nonpayment default requires failure to cure within sixty days after written notice; an alternative plan-defined default must indicate a substantial likelihood that the employer will be unable to pay. Even a default does not itself establish an election to accelerate. The December 1986 demand, April 1987 cure warning and continued nonpayment must retain their distinct functions. Neither the warning nor delinquency proves an earlier acceleration. Whether the February 1993 complaint's alternative full-debt request itself accomplished acceleration is left open. The lower courts must identify actual demand, due dates and any effective acceleration, without inventing a payment schedule or awarding every requested installment.

**3. The Fund cannot revive its first-payment claim on this petition posture.** Having obtained review on its representation that the conflicting installment rules determined the case and that the single-period rule barred its suit, the Fund may not now recover the first $345.50 installment while obtaining reversal for the later installments. Part III controls through Breyer, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

Other submissions argued that the first payment was timely under the sixty-day provision and the demand's receipt-based terms. Those arguments do not undo the premise on which the Fund presented the controlling conflict for review. Exclusion of the first claim does not decide the unverified demand-receipt date, impose a universal February 1 maturity rule, or establish that every alternative argument forfeits a claim.

## Precedent Treatment

Milwaukee is applied for the distinction between computational assumptions and actual payment, default and acceleration; its installment-calculation rule remains unchanged. Concrete Pipe's neutral adjudication and lawful factual and actuarial rebuttal burdens remain intact; the assessment is not conclusively validated here. North Star's borrowing rule is distinguished because Congress enacted the relevant limitation. Inter-Modal's distinction between substantive plan rights and enforcement predicates is consistent with this decision but supplies no preexisting withdrawal-accrual rule. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Withdrawal-liability collection accrues upon the actual missed due payment after assessment, scheduling and demand. Independently due installments retain separate periods, subject to any valid acceleration and the particular first-claim exclusion here. The as-soon-as-practicable demand duty, interim payment obligation, neutral adjudication, ordinary defenses and statutory fraud/concealment qualification retain their own predicates. No equitable tolling, automatic assessment validity, arbitration outcome or right to every requested remedy is established.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for installment-specific timeliness and the properly remaining assessment, acceleration and remedial questions. The first $345.50 claim is excluded. The Court does not resolve the uncompleted arbitration, calculate an award or order future payments by injunction. No jurisdiction is retained and no return date is set.

## Source Notes

The Ninth Circuit's reported judgment, 73 F.3d 971, and the published recitation of the December 12, 1986 demand, April 14, 1987 cure warning, February 9, 1993 complaint and petition representations support the chronology. The demand assessed $45,570.80 and offered 240 monthly installments of $345.50; the complaint alternatively sought the full debt or $25,375 in then-due installments plus prospective relief. Original instruments and pleadings are not reproduced. The decision relies on the recorded representations without finding a new concession, exact receipt date, pre-suit acceleration, or arbitration disposition.

## Event

City of Chicago v. International College of Surgeons, No. 96-910. Argued October 14, 1997; decided December 15, 1997. Certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 91 F.3d 981, concerning removal and supplemental jurisdiction over challenges to local landmark decisions.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision; no Justice was absent.

## Public Action

The Seventh Circuit's jurisdictional judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded to that court for further proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversal is supported 7–2 by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm the jurisdictional remand. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer adopt the controlling mandate returning to the Seventh Circuit for the remaining questions, including properly presented discretionary-retention and abstention arguments. Stone-Zsela would additionally direct the District Court to recognize jurisdiction and then determine whether to retain, stay or remand the state claims. The Court does not reinstate the District Court's entire merits judgment.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins |
|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I: original and supplemental jurisdiction and limits | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II: appellate mandate | Thomas | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer |
| Concurrence in Part I and reversal, with distinct remand instruction | Stone-Zsela | None |
| Dissent | Ginsburg | Stevens |

## Holdings

**1. These mixed complaints support federal jurisdiction.** Substantial federal constitutional claims on the face of the complaints supply original jurisdiction under §1331 and permit removal of the qualifying civil actions under §1441(a). Section 1367(a) supplies supplemental jurisdiction over the related state constitutional and administrative-review claims because they form part of the same Article III case. The state claims do not fall outside that grant merely because their review is confined to an administrative record. Thomas, Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer directly adopt Part I.

The complaints challenge the same property designations, permit denials and administrative procedures under federal and state law. Their common facts, injury and requested relief meet the enacted same-case relation, informed by Gibbs's common-nucleus analysis. Congress did not require each supplemental claim to supply independent original jurisdiction; that would defeat the function of supplemental jurisdiction. Illinois's restriction on new evidence and presumption that agency factual findings are correct describe the task the reviewing court performs, not a categorical exception to §1367. The cases concern local administrative decisions, not appellate review of a state-court judgment.

The structural objection that a federal trial court cannot perform state administrative review does not overcome the actual statutory grant in these mixed cases. Recognition of power leaves the governing state review standards intact and leaves restraint to its proper source. The Court decides neither an action containing only a state administrative appeal nor a diversity basis for such an action.

**2. Jurisdiction does not compel retention.** Section 1367(c) separately permits declining supplemental jurisdiction where the claim raises novel or complex state-law issues, substantially predominates, remains after all original-jurisdiction claims have been dismissed, or presents exceptional circumstances with other compelling reasons. Established abstention remains available on its own predicates. Part I's seven Justices adopt this distinction and its limits.

Economy, convenience, fairness and comity inform the lawful exercise; they do not replace statutory conditions or erase jurisdiction. Quackenbush distinguishes power from abstention and confines outright Burford surrender to equitable or otherwise discretionary relief; an independent legal-damages claim may be stayed on a justified showing but cannot simply be relinquished on that ground. Burford's actual requirements, including timely and adequate state review and either difficult state-law questions of transcendent public importance or disruption of coherent state policy on a matter of substantial public concern, must be met if invoked. No such application is decided here. The Seventh Circuit did not reach the alternative retention arguments, so Part II's six Justices return the case for their ordinary consideration. No constitutional merits or permit entitlement follows from recognizing jurisdiction.

## Precedent Treatment

Gibbs supplies common-case background and the values informing lawful discretion; §1367 supplies the enacted grant and conditions. Peacock is distinguished because its later full-debt action against a new debtor lacked an original federal anchor; its independent-jurisdiction requirement remains. Quackenbush's separation of power, appellate channel and remedy-specific abstention remains intact. Things Remembered's bar on review of covered district-court remands is unchanged: this case reviews the Court of Appeals' judgment after a district merits judgment. Steel Co. preserves jurisdiction-first treatment and supplies no additional supplemental power. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

A district court with original jurisdiction over these substantial federal claims also has supplemental power over closely related state administrative-review claims, notwithstanding their record-confined character. Power remains separate from §1367(c), abstention and the limits of the requested remedy. The ruling establishes no categorical administrative-appeal jurisdiction, diversity route, exception to §1447(d), mandatory retention rule or alteration of state review standards.

## Separate Writings

Justice Ginsburg, joined by Justice Stevens, dissents. The general jurisdictional grants do not sufficiently authorize transfer of the appellate function Illinois assigns to its courts: review confined to a local agency record, with its facts presumptively correct. An independent federal-rights suit is different from the particular statutory review proceeding brought here. A common factual nucleus and federal issue therefore do not answer the antecedent power question, and later discretion to abstain cannot supply missing authority. They would affirm the jurisdictional remand; their objection is to statutory power, not its discretionary exercise.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins Part I and reversal. He would direct the District Court to recognize supplemental jurisdiction and then decide, under §1367(c), abstention principles and ordinary remedial law, whether to retain, stay or remand the state claims. His additional district-court instruction does not command the Court; he neither requires retention nor determines that abstention or remand is warranted.

## Procedure After Action

The Seventh Circuit must proceed without the categorical jurisdictional bar and address the properly remaining contentions. The Court resolves no constitutional validity, taking, hardship, evidentiary sufficiency or demolition entitlement, and the separate Lakefront action remains outside the judgment. No state-court judgment is displaced and no Supreme Court jurisdiction is retained.

## Source Notes

The Seventh Circuit opinion, 91 F.3d 981, and the published account of the Cook County complaints, removals, administrative record limits and District Court judgment establish the posture. Original complaints, agency materials and briefs are not reproduced. The claim that Illinois appellate courts had not resolved the ordinance and state constitutional questions is a litigant's contention, not an exhaustive finding about state precedent. No new merits fact or compulsory abstention premise is supplied.

## Event

General Electric Co. v. Joiner, No. 96-188. Argued October 14, 1997; decided December 15, 1997. Certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, 78 F.3d 524, concerning review of scientific expert-admissibility rulings.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision; none was absent.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is reversed and the case remanded for further proceedings within the limits below.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 8–1. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: Stevens, who would vacate for appellate reconsideration under the proper standard. Kennedy, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer hold that the PCB-only exclusion was within the District Court's discretion. Stone-Zsela joins reversal without joining that particular application. The unchallenged furan/dioxin exposure ruling and remaining causation-admissibility questions survive; the complete defense summary judgment is not reinstated.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins |
|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–II: review and legal inferential-fit rule | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III: PCB-only application and bounded mandate | Kennedy | O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer |
| Concurrence in Parts I–II and the reversing judgment | Stone-Zsela | None |
| Concurrence, joining the Court's opinion in full | Breyer | None |
| Concurrence in Parts I–II and dissent from Part III and the judgment | Stevens | None |

## Holdings

**1. Admission and exclusion receive the same abuse-of-discretion review.** Appellate courts review a trial court's admission or exclusion of scientific expert testimony for abuse of discretion. Legal error remains independently correctable, historical findings receive their proper protection, and permissible case-specific evidentiary judgments receive deference. The importance of an exclusion to summary judgment supplies no different or asymmetric standard. Part I is unanimous.

Daubert left appellate review open. Koon and the task-sensitive reasoning of Pierce and Cooter & Gell support distinguishing correct law from permissible application; their other subject-specific rules do not transfer wholesale to expert testimony. Ornelas's independent review of ultimate constitutional suspicion and probable cause addresses another task. An appellate court may scrutinize a consequential exclusion carefully, but the Federal Rules' allowance of expert evidence does not justify a separate, particularly stringent standard for exclusions. Rule 56 protects the treatment of disputed merits facts; it does not remove the antecedent admissibility decision from the judge.

**2. Reliability includes the inferential connection to the offered proposition.** Original Rules 104(a) and 702 permit the judge to examine whether the expert's reasoning reliably connects the scientific material to the conclusion actually offered. Credentials, accepted terminology, or an assertion of a weight-of-evidence method cannot substitute for an adequate connection; screening that connection does not itself decide ultimate causation. Part II is unanimous at this legal level.

Daubert requires a preliminary foundation, established by a preponderance, of scientifically reliable knowledge fitting the factual question and assisting the factfinder. Principles, methodology and valid application cannot be separated by forbidding any inquiry into the inference drawn. But the rule requires neither a study identical to the plaintiff's circumstances nor independent proof of the conclusion from each study. Animal work, nonsignificant associations, competing causes and individually inconclusive materials may contribute to a supported combined inference. The offered theory here is promotion or acceleration, not necessarily sole causation. No later Rule 702 amendment or categorical statistical-significance requirement is adopted.

**3. The PCB-only exclusion was permissible on this proffer.** The described differences between the experimental materials and Joiner's exposure and disease, considered with the identified limitations of the human studies, permitted the District Court to find an insufficiently supported PCB-only inference. Part III controls through Kennedy, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The holding is that exclusion fell within the permissible range, not that admission was necessarily forbidden or that PCBs cannot promote lung cancer.

The animal work involved infant mice, concentrated injections and alveologenic adenomas; Joiner was an adult with small-cell carcinoma and lower alleged occupational exposure. Two reported human studies showed nonsignificant excesses whose authors did not connect them to PCB exposure. The Norwegian study addressed particular mineral oil and did not mention PCBs; the Yusho group had several potential carcinogenic exposures without an isolated PCB effect in the described findings. These limitations bear on the connection offered, rather than establishing automatic scientific disqualifications. The experts invoked a combined method, exposure information, interviews, medical records and additional research; the Court does not infer that no bridge existed merely because a source extract is incomplete. Considered at the proper discretionary level, the described fit deficiencies support the ruling.

The Eleventh Circuit's separate conclusion that evidence supported furan/dioxin exposure was not challenged in the petition. The District Court had not expressly assessed causation admissibility on that assumed exposure. Those questions require further proceedings and prevent restoration of the full defense judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

Daubert's flexible foundation, valid-application and preponderance rules are applied and supplemented by today's appellate-review rule; its distinction between admissibility and ultimate causation remains. Koon, Pierce and Cooter & Gell inform the allocation of legal correction and discretionary application. Ornelas is distinguished by its particular ultimate constitutional inquiry, not narrowed into a universal rule about mixed questions. Frye's mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite remains displaced. Rules 403, 703 and 706 and Rule 56 keep their separate functions. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Scientific expert admissions and exclusions receive uniform abuse-of-discretion review, including independent correction of legal mistakes. The reliability inquiry may examine the actual inferential connection while remaining distinct from ultimate causal proof and preserving legitimate cumulative methods. The present PCB-only exclusion is sustained within that discretionary range. No universal study-matching condition, categorical animal-study rule, compulsory expert-assistance procedure, technical-specialty rule, collateral reopening entitlement or determination of the alternative-exposure theory follows.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens concurs in Parts I–II but dissents from the application and judgment. The grant concerns review allocation, and the Eleventh Circuit should apply the correct standard to the actual cumulative method. Individually inconclusive studies do not necessarily defeat a combined inference; the described reliance on work by at least thirteen researchers and World Health Organization materials warrants attention to the whole method. He would vacate for appellate reconsideration, without commanding admission or finding causation.

Justice Breyer joins the Court's opinion in full and concurs separately. Difficult scientific questions do not excuse the judge's screening duty. Existing assistance, including a court-appointed expert under Rule 706 and lawful management of the foundation inquiry, may help assess tentative scientific knowledge and its application to an individual case. He requires no expert appointment, new hearing or mandatory panel; those suggestions are not controlling procedures.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I–II and reversal on the uniform-review and inferential-fit grounds. An expert's assertion cannot replace an adequately supported connection. He does not join the particular finding that this PCB-only exclusion was within discretion, and he preserves the unresolved exposure questions and the remainder of the tort action.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for further proceedings consistent with the review rule and sustained PCB-only exclusion. The unchallenged furan/dioxin exposure ruling remains, and causation admissibility on that theory remains open. No finding of causation, damages award, compulsory admission, automatic new trial or expert appointment is entered.

## Source Notes

The District Court opinion, 864 F.Supp. 1310, and Eleventh Circuit opinion, 78 F.3d 524, together with authenticated published record descriptions, establish the rulings, proffered promotion theory and study limitations. Full studies, complete depositions and the full record are not reproduced. The reported observations and experts' cumulative method are preserved without independent medical findings, invented numerical data, or an inference of litigation default from an unavailable source.

## Event

Tanford v. Brand, review of the Seventh Circuit's judgment in No. 96-3053, 104 F.3d 982. Decided December 22, 1997, on the merits of the challenge to Indiana University's official commencement invocations and benedictions. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is stated.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated on submission and at decision. No Justice was absent.

## Public Action

The Seventh Circuit's judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded for the lower courts to determine appropriate prospective relief against the official prayer practice, subject to the plaintiffs' continuing standing and the requirements for an injunction.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 6–3. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, who would affirm. Any prospective relief the lower courts find warranted, subject to the plaintiffs' continuing standing and the requirements for an injunction, must be limited to the University's selection or conduct of invocation and benediction prayers as official worship and must preserve independent prayer and lawful private expression. No damages, fee ruling, automatic hearing, mandatory new forum or general ban on religious speech is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins |
|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I: live controversy and official attribution | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer |
| Opinion of the Court, Parts II–III: constitutional application and relief | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer |
| Dissent, Part I: adult-setting ground | Scalia | Kennedy, Thomas |
| Dissent, Part II: historical accommodation and compulsion account | Scalia | Thomas |
| Separate dissenting statement | Kennedy | None |

## Holdings

**1. The live controversy concerns the University's own devotional program.** The continuing policy and Urbanski's concrete expectation of attending his 1999 graduation support prospective review. The University selects a religious leader and incorporates actual invocation and benediction prayers into its official ceremony; independent composition of the words does not make the exercises private equal-access speech. Part I is unanimous.

Lee's prospective-exposure principle supports the live claim without treating other plaintiffs as perpetual students or relying on ideological objection alone. The program places the invocation after the national anthem and the benediction at the close. The reproduced benediction invites prayer, addresses God, seeks divine assistance and concludes with Amen. The University need not have written a full script for the exercise to be its own: official selection and incorporation establish attribution. The limited instruction to offer an uplifting general message and the clergy's independent wording remain part of the record, not evidence of private-forum access.

**2. Official university commencement worship violates the Establishment Clause on this record.** A public university violates the Establishment Clause when it incorporates officially arranged religious prayer into its commencement exercises, as the University did by selecting a religious leader to conduct actual invocation and benediction prayers as components of its official commencement. The violation does not depend on whether attendance is compulsory, whether the prayer is sectarian, or whether the ceremony also serves secular purposes. This governmental organization of worship falls under the specific-prohibition route of Lemon as Agostini modified it; mature attendance, meaningful freedom to abstain, brevity, nonsectarian language and a sincere ceremonial purpose do not cure it. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt Parts II–III.

Agostini retains Lemon as the governing test while treating purpose, effects and entanglement as evidence within one inquiry, not independent prongs. A specific prohibition or distinct doctrine governs conduct it resolves; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives the attribution-and-function inquiry. Lee and Ingebretsen supply the official-worship prohibition applied here. The University selects and presents actual devotional exercises as its own commencement activity, so that specific prohibition resolves this case without a separate primary-effect test or a residual inquiry. Today's decision applies that principle to the previously unresolved university setting. The audience's maturity and genuine freedom to remain seated, leave or attend another ceremony bear materially on coercion, but do not change whose worship is presented. Most attendees remained seated, ordinary movement was common, and no penalty followed nonattendance. The Court accepts those facts without finding adolescent pressure or requiring a separate coercion showing.

The practice's long history and purpose of solemnity receive their actual weight but establish no permissible relationship authorizing this governmental devotional act. Marsh protects a distinct legislative-prayer setting; it does not establish an exception for every adult ceremony. Sherman permits a particular civic pledge and expressly distinguishes actual prayer; placing worship in a civic event does not convert it into civic acknowledgment. A secular ceremonial objective cannot justify the prohibited means. The exercises' brevity, and the view below that any advancement or entanglement was de minimis, do not determine whether the worship is officially organized. The ruling requires no sham-purpose, denominational-exclusion or excessive-entanglement finding. It applies Lemon as Agostini modified it and decides no other adult ceremonial arrangement.

**3. Relief must address the supported official practice and preserve private expression.** On remand, the lower courts shall determine appropriate prospective relief against the University's official selection and inclusion of these prayers, subject to the plaintiffs' continuing standing and the ordinary requirements for an injunction. Independently chosen private religious expression, neutral access to a genuine forum and nondevotional ceremonial remarks remain distinct; the judgment requires no new forum and no suppression of otherwise lawful viewpoints.

Ingebretsen supplies the remedy's official/private boundary. Any decree must reach the established institutional practice, not every religious reference, speaker or activity at the University. Private remarks by graduates, historical acknowledgment without devotional operation and a moment of silence are not prohibited by this holding. Any decree the lower courts enter must not prescribe new ceremonial content or treat these reservations as a ruling that arrangements not before the Court are permissible.

## Precedent Treatment

Lee and Ingebretsen's official-worship prohibition is applied to the actual university commencement program; their separate substantial-pressure rules remain unchanged and need not be applied to find a violation here. Engel and Schempp retain their official-worship protections despite nonsectarian wording and permission to abstain. Lemon remains the governing test as Agostini modified it: the specific-prohibition route resolves this governmental organization of worship, and primary religious effect is evidence rather than an independent prong. Agostini's onsite secular-service holding does not authorize governmental worship. Marsh is distinguished by its legislative context, and Sherman by its civic exercise. Pinette, Rosenberger, Good News and Mergens preserve genuinely private religious expression; their neutral-access holdings do not authorize governmental worship. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Effective December 22, 1997, a public university violates the Establishment Clause when it incorporates officially arranged religious prayer into its commencement exercises; the violation does not depend on compulsory attendance, sectarian content or the ceremony's secular purposes. The University's official selection and incorporation of these devotional prayers is unconstitutional even for the mature voluntary audience shown here. Lemon, as Agostini modified it, resolves this governmental organization of worship through its specific-prohibition route, independently of a coercion finding. The separate pressure protections, private-speech rules, legislative-prayer precedent and ordinary ceremonial acknowledgment retain their existing limits. The decision creates no general prohibition on religion at universities or on every religious feature of an adult ceremony.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia's dissent, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas in Part I, would affirm because this brief traditional adult ceremony, with genuine freedom to abstain and no demonstrated unconstitutional pressure, falls outside the established secondary-school prohibitions. All three joined Agostini's modification of Lemon. They regard this particular historical ceremonial relationship as permissible within that test, drawing on Marsh while recognizing that its holding concerns legislative prayer. They acknowledge that the exercises are official and devotional; they disagree with the Court over whether the official-worship prohibition or a permissible ceremonial relationship governs this adult setting. The Court rejects that extension of Marsh. Their position does not make every longstanding adult prayer practice permissible.

Justice Thomas also joins Part II of Justice Scalia's dissent, which emphasizes historical ceremonial accommodation and the absence of the compulsion required by their account. That position does not become a universal historical exemption or an opinion of the Court, and Kennedy does not join its coercion methodology.

Justice Kennedy separately explains that Lee protects against substantial practical pressure without requiring a legal penalty. The mature audience, optional attendance, ordinary movement, seated abstention and available exit or reentry do not establish that pressure here. Consistently with his full join in Agostini, he accepts the modified Lemon test but regards this concrete voluntary adult ceremony as a permissible relationship, declining the Court's application of the official-worship prohibition to this university setting. He does not call the prayers private or reject practical coercion as a constitutional concern.

## Holdings

**1. Immediate review separates legal immunity from historical facts.** An interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal may resolve a separable legal defense on the proper facts assumed in the nonmovant's favor, but may not decide factual sufficiency, participation, credibility or which disputed account is true. Ultimate constitutional voluntariness is not wholly historical merely because its test considers circumstances. Part I is unanimous.

Johnson and Behrens protect the assumed historical account without foreclosing genuine legal review. Section 1254 permits this Court to review the circuit case but does not enlarge the circuit's §1291 authority. Miller distinguishes the ultimate voluntariness question from historical facts; Ornelas preserves task-specific allocation rather than a universal rule for mixed questions. A court therefore cannot avoid a legal immunity question by labeling the entire coercion inquiry factual, or obtain factual review by attaching a legal label. The factual portions of Cooper's appeal must be dismissed; the distinct reviewable questions remain.

**2. The assumed material warrant deception defeats the asserted legal immunity defense.** On the proper assumed facts, an officer cannot obtain immunity for knowing or reckless factual falsehoods material to probable cause merely because a judge issued the warrant. Franks and Malley supplied the relevant pre-August-1993 rule. Part II-A's complete rule has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela additionally joins its application to the assumed deliberate material falsehoods and its subsidiary-theory limits.

The rule requires actual culpable falsity, personal responsibility and materiality. A truthful corrected account may still support probable cause, and a later recantation does not itself prove that the affiant knowingly fabricated evidence. Judicial issuance does not categorically sever the responsibility of an officer whose material deception procured the warrant. Cooper may contest the facts at the proper stage, but cannot secure interlocutory reversal by substituting his account for Clanton's. The Court does not find what the affidavit actually contained or place Bobby's derivative statement into it as independent probable cause.

The three subsidiary theories remain rejected: sufficiently reliable hearsay may support probable cause; the Fourth Amendment does not universally require filed criminal charges before a warrant; and the federal grand-jury indictment requirement does not govern state prosecutions. These propositions establish neither universal reliability of accomplice statements nor federal liability from a state-law warrant defect.

**3. Deliberate material detention deception does not require a prior case using the same communication system.** The asserted categorical immunity defense fails on the assumed personal communication of deliberate material homicide and no-bond falsehoods to procure otherwise unjustified additional unreasonable physical detention actually caused by that message. The established Franks/Malley prohibition on material official deception has an obvious application to this particular seizure; electronic transmission and a different custodian do not require a factually identical earlier NCIC case. Part II-B is unanimous.

The arrest under the original warrant and the later message's alleged extension of custody are distinct events. The Oklahoma warrant's $5,000 bond provision does not itself establish a federal release entitlement, and Texas's authority to set bond does not prove that the same detention would independently have occurred. The claim requires falsehood, culpability, materiality, attributable causation and an actual unreasonable physical seizure. An inaccurate message, bad motive, state-law breach or passage of one to three days alone is insufficient. If the same lawful detention would have occurred without the message, or Cooper had no material causal role, the pertinent defense remains available. Ordinary factual resolution must answer those questions; the Court decides no general duration of Fourth Amendment coverage through every stage of a prosecution.

**4. The independent coerced-confession theory requires proper legal review and conduct-date notice analysis.** The appellate disposition of the distinct third-party-confession branch is vacated because treating ultimate constitutional coercion as wholly outside interlocutory review fails to separate the historical assumptions from the legal defense. On remand the court must identify Clanton's own protected interest, the particular use and legal voluntariness of the statement, and whether the claimed violation was clearly established at the relevant 1993 conduct date. Part III has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela dissents from this disposition.

The Tenth Circuit did discuss Clanton's own due-process interest and notice. The defect is its overbroad treatment of the ultimate coercion conclusion, not total omission of those subjects. A claim involving an involuntary but not knowingly false statement is distinct from the preserved knowing-deception claims. Michael's personal self-incrimination privilege cannot be borrowed, but that does not categorically eliminate an independent injury to Clanton's liberty. Nor does Albright establish a universal Fourth Amendment displacement rule or a general state-remedy bar: its incompatible rationales supplied no controlling synthesis.

Jackson, Blackburn, Spano, Haynes and Fulminante supply the received voluntariness background. Frazier does not make an evidence lie conclusive, and Hutto does not make every promised benefit coercion. The alleged threats, promised leniency, suggested narrative and asserted physical evidence must be assessed together under the appropriate totality rule. The earlier third-party trial-use cases identified below, including LaFrance, Merkt, Chiavola and Bradford, do not automatically establish a particular pretrial-detention damages right in August 1993; their actual holdings and relevant circuit law must be evaluated. An identical factual precedent is unnecessary for obvious coverage, but an abstract liberty principle or bad intent cannot resolve a genuinely unsettled extension. No ultimate coercion, independent due-process right, clearly established violation or immunity is decided here.

**5. Existing proof and immunity rules remain distinct from ultimate liability.** Harlow and Anderson preserve objective, particularized conduct-date immunity. Elder requires consideration of all relevant pre-existing law on the preserved issue; later adjudications cannot retroactively provide notice, and no mandatory merits-first sequence prevents use of a genuinely dispositive notice defense. The eight-Associate opinion adopts these limits throughout; Stone-Zsela joins their application to the preserved deliberate-deception claims while proposing the separate change described below.

Crawford-El and Jeffries preserve competent direct and circumstantial proof, actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation without an invented heightened motive requirement. Their retaliation-specific burden allocation does not become the burden for every fabrication claim. Fletcher distinguishes evidentiary acts from advocacy and preserves the remaining liability requirements; it does not supply Cooper with prosecutorial immunity or itself provide 1993 notice. Failure of a legal immunity defense establishes no damages, supervisory liability, automatic discovery entitlement or right to trial.

## Event

Fidelity Financial Services, Inc. v. Fink, No. 96-1370. Argued November 3, 1997; decided January 13, 1998. The Court reviews the Eighth Circuit's affirmance of avoidance of Fidelity's lien and asks whether Missouri relation back satisfies the federal enabling-loan perfection deadline despite later performance.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision. No Justice was absent.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed on the reviewed enabling-loan defense.

## Judgment & Remedy

Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: none. The existing avoidance judgment stands against the asserted §547(c)(3) defense. No new hearing, mailing remand, damages award, title-transfer command or Chapter 13 plan-treatment determination is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

**1. The state-required perfection acts must be completed within the federal twenty days.** Under the applicable §547(c)(3)(B), the security interest must be perfected within twenty days after the debtor receives possession. A longer state relation-back period cannot satisfy that condition by treating later performance as earlier completion. All nine Justices join this holding and its application.

Section 547(e)(1)(B) identifies perfection for this property by protection against a simple-contract creditor's superior judicial lien. State law supplies the interest, the protection and the required acts; federal law supplies the time within which that protection must be achieved for the exception. Required acts include an act of a governmental employee when state law makes it necessary. Timely creditor effort or mailing is not a universal safe harbor.

Fidelity's competing reading treats eventual retrospective priority under Missouri's thirty-day period as satisfying the federal definition from creation. But a later conditional act is not completed protection within Congress's interval. Otherwise each State's longer grace period would substitute for the enacted twenty days. Missouri's ordinary creation and priority rules remain valid in their own sphere; this construction gives the separate federal exception its operative effect.

Section 546(b)(1)(A) corroborates this reading by specifying trustee powers under §§544, 545 and 549 while omitting §547. That structure is support for the §547 construction, not an independently sufficient holding or universal prohibition on state relation back. Barnhill's distinction between state interests and federal timing supports the analysis while its express reservation of §547(c) is respected. BFP and Rash likewise require attention to each federal provision rather than substitution of a different state-law consequence.

**2. The stated day-21 mailing cannot satisfy this exception through relation back.** Beasley acquired the car and gave the purchase-price note and security interest on August 17, 1994. Fidelity mailed the perfection application September 7, day 21. Even crediting mailing for the timeliness argument, the act came after the federal period. The longer Missouri relation-back rule cannot cure that lateness.

The Court does not find an exact delivery date or decide whether mailing alone satisfied Missouri's documents-and-delivery requirements; that state-law issue was not raised and is unnecessary. No earlier perfection step is established. The transaction's applicable period is twenty days, not a later amended interval.

**3. Other avoidance and exception requirements retain their separate operation.** The enabling-loan exception also requires a security interest securing the specified new value, given at or after signing a security agreement describing the property, by or for the secured party under that agreement, to enable acquisition and actually used for that purpose. This appeal decides no separate dispute about those conditions. The trustee's §547(b) preference predicates remain distinct from proof of an applicable exception.

Failure of the timing defense does not make every enabling loan avoidable or establish every preference element in another transaction. The Court affirms the existing judgment on the reviewed issue without invalidating the underlying loan, reopening adjudicated predicates or deciding unrelated defenses and plan treatment.

## Precedent Treatment

Barnhill retains its federal honor-date rule for ordinary-check transfers under §547(b); its reservation of §547(c) is respected and the present enabling-loan issue is decided on its own text. BFP retains the independent federal value inquiry for its foreclosure setting and does not supply a universal property-law displacement rule. Rash retains adjusted replacement valuation for retained-use Chapter 13 collateral, not preference timing. Butner and McKenzie preserve the role of state property interests within the applicable federal bankruptcy rules. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

For this twenty-day regime, a creditor must complete all state-required perfection acts within twenty days after possession to use §547(c)(3). Later completion within a longer state relation-back window does not qualify. State creation and priority remain operative outside the displaced timing consequence, and other preference and exception elements remain separate.

None.

## Procedure After Action

The Eighth Circuit's judgment sustaining avoidance of Fidelity's lien remains effective on the reviewed issue. No further proceeding is ordered to resolve the unraised mailing question, supply a delivery date, revalue collateral or determine plan treatment.

These limits create neither a factual default nor a need to decide an unnecessary issue.
