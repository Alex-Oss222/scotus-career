## Event

Clifton v. Federal Election Commission, No. 97-786. Plenary merits decision on submission, September 14, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 13. On review of the First Circuit's June 6, 1997 decision, 114 F.3d 1309, following the District of Maine's declaration, 927 F. Supp. 493. Rehearing dispositions occurred June 27 and August 8, 1997. No exact oral-argument date is established.

Robin Clifton and Maine Right to Life Committee challenge the limits on independent candidate inquiries, written-only guide contacts, equal space and prominence, and the unresolved electioneering-message clauses. The challenged regulations are 11 C.F.R. §114.4(c)(4)–(5), promulgated under the Federal Election Campaign Act, including 2 U.S.C. §441b. MRLC publishes abortion-related voting records and guides using general funds that include corporate donations. No finding establishes that an ordinary inquiry supplied a campaign service or that MRLC meets the nonprofit funding criteria. The private petitioners obtained substantial relief below and seek more; the appellate judgment preserved lawful coordination restrictions and remanded the unfinished clauses.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No recusal or nonparticipation is established.

## Public Action

The judgment of the First Circuit is affirmed within the limited declaration and remand described below.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Affirm the limited declaratory judgment and preserve the existing remand, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** None.

The declaration reaches §114.4(c)(4) only insofar as it bars mere candidate inquiries, and §114.4(c)(5) insofar as it imposes written-only contacts and equal space and prominence. The District Court must address the unresolved electioneering-message provisions' content, ripeness and severability. The broader original invalidation is not restored. No new injunction, enforcement moratorium, liability finding or nonprofit exemption is entered; a properly supported request for interim relief remains for the District Court.

## Holdings

**Authority for Holdings 1–3:** Parts I–III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the stated narrow propositions. Stevens and Ginsburg join Holding 2 within the private petitioners' present review and its limited statutory ground; they do not adopt a general constitutional ban on contact safeguards.

### 1. Asking a candidate about public issues does not itself finance the candidate

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 2 U.S.C. §441b, 11 C.F.R. §114.4(c)(4) cannot prohibit a publisher's mere inquiry to a candidate about positions or recorded votes as a contribution or candidate-directed expenditure. The required connection concerns campaign spending: actual cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion concerning the expenditure remains governed by its own statutory predicates.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that gathering information is not itself spending on a candidate's behalf. Buckley v. Valeo distinguishes independent expression from candidate financing; Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission requires a real candidate connection rather than a label substituting for that connection; Akins v. FEC likewise requires actual covered financing. MRLC's effort to learn the meaning of public votes supplies no finding that a candidate directed the guide's content, collaborated in distribution, or received an agreed campaign service. The Commission may investigate such arrangements when supported. Its concern that a survey could conceal collaboration cannot make every inquiry collaboration. This holding protects neither staged questions supplying campaign work nor a paid political advertisement disguised as an independent guide.

**Precedent treatment:** Buckley's contribution/expenditure distinction, Colorado Republican's actual-connection requirement and Akins's transaction-specific coverage remain in force and apply to this information-gathering setting.

### 2. The existing written-only and equal-space declaration stands

**Holding and operative rule:** In this private-petitioner review, the declaration invalidating §114.4(c)(5)'s compulsory written questions and replies and equal space and prominence remains in place. General rulemaking and enforcement authority does not authorize treating every independently edited guide involving candidate inquiry as a prohibited contribution or imposing these indiscriminate contact and editorial conditions on that premise.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's limited statutory ground respects the difference between a real contribution and dialogue used to compose independent speech. A written record can aid enforcement, but administrative convenience does not establish that an oral discussion transfers value to a candidate. Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo protects editorial allocation; Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston protects the speaker's selection of an expressive composition. Those principles reinforce the need for actual statutory authority before requiring a private publisher to give every candidate equal space and prominence. The petition seeks broader protection for the private plaintiffs; no established cross-petition seeks enlargement of the Commission's rights by restoring restrictions already invalidated. That review limit independently preserves the existing relief. The Court does not decide that Congress could never authorize a specifically fitted contact safeguard, that accurate disclosure could never be required under lawful authority, or that proved coordinated services are exempt. A government-authored guide or a condition on a properly defined public resource presents different questions.

**Precedent treatment:** Tornillo and Hurley reinforce editorial protection without becoming a universal prohibition on regulation of campaign contributions. Austin v. Michigan Chamber of Commerce is neither overruled nor treated as a delegation of every restriction on corporate speech.

### 3. The unfinished provisions and organizational status remain for the existing remand

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court does not expand the declaration to invalidate the whole regulation or uphold the unresolved electioneering-message clauses. Their operative meaning, ripeness and severability remain for the District Court; MRLC's entitlement to nonprofit constitutional protection remains undecided on its actual funding characteristics.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court cannot supply an operative definition the appellate record leaves unsettled or decide severability from the label alone. The existing remand permits those questions and any properly supported interim-relief request to be decided under their own standards. Day v. Holahan prevents automatic denial of MCFL protection solely because an ideological nonprofit accepts some corporate money; it does not establish that MRLC's funding is insignificant, noncommercial or free of conduit features. The Court therefore neither grants an organizational exemption nor assumes commercial-treasury status. Genuine-coordination restrictions, express-advocacy provisions not successfully challenged, and distinct statutory membership exclusions retain their lawful scope.

**Precedent treatment:** Day and MCFL require actual organizational and funding characteristics; Austin's surviving corporate-treasury rule remains available in its materially different setting. No prior decision is overruled.

- Colorado Republican: applies its requirement of an actual expenditure connection; party identity or a bare contact cannot replace it.
- Akins: applies actual statutory financing predicates without expanding its distinct membership or political-committee rulings.
- Tornillo: applies protection of editorial allocation as support for the narrow statutory construction.
- Hurley: applies protection of chosen expressive composition at the same limited level.

Actual coordinated campaign financing remains separately regulable. Neither the meaning and severability of the electioneering-message clauses nor MRLC's nonprofit status is settled by this decision. The rule concerns the challenged guide regulations; it supplies no decision of the separate §100.22(b) litigation.

## Event

Federal Election Commission v. Maine Right to Life Committee, Inc., No. 96-1818. Plenary merits decision on submission, September 14, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 13. Review of the First Circuit's October 18, 1996 affirmance, 98 F.3d 1, of the District of Maine's judgment and reconsideration ruling, 914 F. Supp. 8. No exact oral-argument date is established.

The question is whether 11 C.F.R. §100.22(b)'s context-sensitive definition of express advocacy exceeds 2 U.S.C. §441b as construed in Buckley v. Valeo and FEC v. Massachusetts Citizens for Life (MCFL), and whether the association had to request an advisory opinion before bringing this pre-enforcement challenge. MRLC publishes recurring newsletters, candidate surveys and issue advocacy, and accepts some corporate money. The District Court found actual cessation of speech after the rule took effect October 5, 1995, with no disputed material adjudicative facts. It invalidated paragraph (b) on statutory grounds, reserved standalone vagueness, dismissed the Attorney General, and denied further unnecessary relief.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No recusal or nonparticipation is established.

## Public Action

The judgment sustaining categorical statutory invalidation of §100.22(b) is vacated and the case remanded. The threshold conclusion permitting this action without compulsory advisory review is sustained.

## Holdings

### 1. This live challenge does not require an advisory opinion first

**Holding and operative rule:** An association whose recurring issue communications are subject to an effective FEC regulation and whose speech has actually ceased because of its credible application may seek pre-enforcement review of the rule's legal reach. Section 437f's permissive advisory-opinion procedure is not a compulsory exhaustion requirement for this challenge and cannot furnish an agency determination invalidating the governing rule.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine join the statutory-design and actual-controversy ground; no broader exhaustion-balancing proposition is adopted.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that MRLC need not seek advance official permission for each prospective communication before presenting this facial legal claim. The trial findings establish ongoing speech activity and actual cessation after the regulation became effective, rather than a hypothetical speaker's objection to a dormant rule. McCarthy v. Madigan makes the enacted administrative scheme central; FECA makes this advisory route available without making it mandatory. The remedy sought is a legal decision about the regulation itself, which an advisory opinion cannot invalidate. The association supplies a sufficient controversy for the relief sought, so the Court need not decide Corbett's independent recipient-standing theory. Nor does this conclusion disturb dismissal of the Attorney General, against whom no supported enforcement threat was shown. A materially different administrative scheme or an entirely hypothetical future communication is not decided.

**Precedent treatment:** McCarthy's statutory-primacy principle applies; its broader discretionary balancing is unnecessary here. Its later prison-specific statutory modification does not impose exhaustion under FECA.

### 2. The rule's cumulative restrictions preserve the statutory express-advocacy boundary

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 2 U.S.C. §441b, 11 C.F.R. §100.22(b) does not exceed the statute merely because it permits limited context rather than an exclusive list of electoral slogans. Its coverage requires a communication considered as a whole, only limited reference to external events, an unmistakable and unambiguous electoral portion suggestive of only one meaning, and no reasonable difference over whether it encourages election or defeat of a clearly identified candidate rather than another kind of action; a reasonable nonelectoral interpretation excludes coverage.

**Authority:** Parts II–III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court are joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from the statutory disposition and do not join this construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Buckley v. Valeo and FEC v. Massachusetts Citizens for Life require express electoral advocacy without prescribing a single verbal formula. The lower court itself recognized that MCFL included a marginally less direct explicit appeal; the disagreement is whether paragraph (b)'s contextual route necessarily crosses the narrowed statute's boundary. Its cumulative conditions need not do so. Election proximity, expected electoral effect, policy agreement, hostility toward a candidate, or an agency's preferred reading cannot replace the required unambiguous electoral direction. Limited context may inform the communication's meaning, but cannot displace a reasonable call to issue debate or some other action. The contrary concern that unchanged issue speech will be reclassified whenever an election approaches is answered at this statutory level by enforcing every restriction together, not by deferring to the Commission. A court must independently test the communication against them. This interpretation rejects categorical statutory invalidity; it neither decides the unresolved constitutional vagueness claim nor holds the rule valid in every application. It finds no MRLC publication unlawful and does not order enforcement.

**Precedent treatment:** Buckley and MCFL retain their narrowing construction and protection of genuine issue discussion; the rule is permissible only within that boundary. Akins v. FEC's insistence on actual statutory conditions and independent judicial resolution is applied, without enlarging its distinct coverage holdings.

### 3. The unresolved constitutional challenge returns for adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical declaration that §100.22(b) exceeds statutory authority is vacated, and the case is remanded for the unresolved vagueness challenge and any properly presented application issues under the restrictive construction stated above. Subsection (a), separate contribution rules, distinct membership exclusions, and nonprofit protection based on actual characteristics are not invalidated or generally approved by this disposition.

**Authority:** Part III has the same five-Justice merits coalition. The four dissenters would leave the bounded statutory declaration intact instead.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court removes the statutory premise of the judgment below, not the separate questions that premise made unnecessary to decide. A statutory construction alone cannot establish constitutional clarity in every application. No injunction against an otherwise unsupported enforcement threat, replacement rule, or judicial supervision of rulemaking follows. Day v. Holahan and MCFL require attention to an organization's actual political resources and financing; the acceptance of some corporate contributions does not establish MRLC's ultimate category, and no such determination is needed here. Lawful contribution enforcement and the Attorney General's dismissal remain separate. The remand permits the unresolved claims to be adjudicated without treating today's decision as a directive to prosecute.

**Precedent treatment:** Day and MCFL retain their fact-dependent nonprofit protection. Austin v. Michigan Chamber of Commerce remains within its surviving corporate-treasury scope; nothing here approves every corporate restriction.

## Precedent Treatment

- McCarthy: applies statutory primacy to the permissive FECA advisory process; no broad exhaustion exception is created.
- Buckley: retains protection of issue discussion through the express-electoral-advocacy construction; no exclusive vocabulary is required.
- MCFL: retains its express-advocacy construction and distinct nonprofit protection based on actual characteristics.
- Akins: applies actual statutory predicates and independent judicial review without extending its membership or political-committee holdings.
- Day: preserves actual nonprofit protection despite some corporate funding; no finding about MRLC's full status is made.
- Austin: neither overruled nor enlarged into general approval of every corporate restriction.

## Law After Decision

Paragraph (b) is no longer categorically invalid on the statutory ground adopted below. Every cumulative condition remains binding: limited context cannot replace an unmistakable electoral direction or defeat a reasonable nonelectoral meaning. The Court leaves constitutional vagueness and concrete applications open. The decision changes no distinct membership exclusion, contribution predicate or nonprofit-status rule, and decides no separate voter-guide regulation.

## Separate Writings

Kennedy, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Thomas, concurs in Part I and dissents from Parts II–III and the vacatur. He would affirm the declaration that paragraph (b) exceeds the statute while leaving standalone vagueness undecided. Buckley and MCFL protect an ascertainable express-terms boundary before publication: the speaker must supply the electoral direction, rather than having the regulator infer it from election proximity and external events. The Commission's treatment of character and qualification discussion permits precisely the shifting classification that the narrowing construction prevents. The no-reasonable-alternative formula does not cure that enlargement of delegated authority.

The dissent does not demand one canonical slogan. An explicit command, including a functional phrase unambiguously instructing a vote for identified candidates, remains different from policy persuasion with an electoral implication. Ordinary context may identify the candidate or explain express words; it may not supply the absent electoral command. Corporate funding cannot itself transform issue speech into a contribution or express exhortation. The dissent preserves subsection (a), actual candidate contributions, genuinely coordinated expenses and corporate-treasury regulation within lawful categories. It proposes no replacement regulation, general ban on context-sensitive interpretation, corporate exemption, or additional injunction.

The vacatur authorizes no automatic enforcement or finding against MRLC. Ordinary mandate procedure applies; no special deadline or new factfinding requirement is imposed.

## Event

Federal Election Commission v. Williams, No. 97-601; Ninth Circuit No. 95-55320. Plenary merits decision on submission, September 14, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 13. Review of the Ninth Circuit's December 26, 1996 decision, 104 F.3d 237, reversing a final Central District of California judgment. No exact oral-argument date in this Court is established.

The questions concern 28 U.S.C. §2462's application to FECA civil penalties, ordinary accrual, fraudulent concealment and diligence, compulsory administrative intervals, and the related injunction. The lower record describes twenty-two contributions by nominal donors involving Williams's advances in a campaign ticket promotion from autumn 1987 through January 1988. Hooton's administrative complaint arrived September 12, 1988; a reason-to-believe finding followed September 13, 1989. The published opinion gives no exact notification date. Conciliation ran May 24–July 20, 1993; the FEC sued October 19, 1993. The District Court imposed a $10,000 penalty and ten-year injunction and entered final judgment March 7, 1995 after dismissal of the remaining count. The court of appeals reversed on limitations without resolving the remaining defenses.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No recusal or nonparticipation is established.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for appropriate consideration or further proceedings under the proper concealment and diligence standard.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Vacate and remand, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** None.

The FEC retains the burden of establishing concealment, reasonable diligence and the legally relevant interval. The remand preserves the compulsory-process question if necessary and all properly presented unresolved appellate defenses. It reinstates neither the $10,000 penalty nor the ten-year injunction. Any penalty must survive the limitation inquiry and remaining defenses; any prospective remedy must independently satisfy statutory and equitable requirements.

Parts I, II and IV have nine direct votes. Part III's reservation has eight; Stone-Zsela's affirmative tolling rule has one and is noncontrolling.

## Holdings

### 1. The ordinary five-year penalty period applies

**Holding and operative rule:** Absent a different congressional limitations enactment, 28 U.S.C. §2462 governs a FECA civil action initially imposing a penalty as well as one collecting an assessed penalty. The claim ordinarily accrues when the actionable violation occurs, not when the FEC chooses to complete its investigation or makes a later administrative determination; no general agency-discovery accrual rule applies.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that enforcement includes imposition of the civil penalty itself. The distinction urged between assessment and collection would leave initial punitive enforcement outside the general deadline although Congress supplied no separate FECA penalty period. United States v. Walsh and 3M Co. v. Browner supply persuasive applications of that reading. An agency's discretionary sequence cannot postpone accrual indefinitely. The Court decides no issue under the statute's separate condition concerning the offender or property being found within the United States during the same period so proper service may be made. Different enacted tolling provisions or independent continuing violations retain their own rules; the continuing effects of a completed contribution do not themselves establish a new violation. This ordinary accrual rule does not resolve proven fraudulent concealment or the separately reserved compulsory-process question.

**Precedent treatment:** No Supreme Court accrual rule for every limitations statute is announced. The fixed statutory period remains distinct from the concealment inquiry described below.

### 2. Nominal donor reports do not conclusively disclose the hidden source

**Holding and operative rule:** Fraudulent-concealment tolling requires the FEC to establish concealment that prevented timely discovery despite reasonable diligence, when the operative facts were or should have been discovered, and the resulting legally justified interval. Reports identifying nominal contributors do not, as a matter of law, disclose an alleged undisclosed reimburser or establish when reasonable inquiry would reveal that source.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine direct joins at this evidence-based ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Holmberg v. Armbrecht's fraud principle and Klehr v. A. O. Smith Corp.'s requirement of reasonable diligence. Neither a general reporting obligation nor accurate identification of intermediaries proves that the concealed source was revealed. The alleged device may hide the very fact the agency must discover. Conversely, deception inherent in an offense does not automatically toll every claim, and investigatory authority does not excuse lack of diligence. The lower court must examine available information, warning signs, reasonable inquiry and discoverability, including the agency's conduct after the September 12, 1988 insider complaint. That complaint is evidence of actual notice, not conclusive proof that earlier discovery was impossible. Klehr rejects misleading conduct alone where reasonable inquiry would disclose the claim, without demanding extraordinary investigation overcoming otherwise successful concealment. United States v. Beggerly's Quiet Title Act holding rejects additional tolling under that statute's own notice-based period; it does not decide this distinct concealment question. The appellate majority's categorical notice shortcut cannot stand, but Judge Fletcher's affirmative diligence assessment is not adopted as a finding.

**Precedent treatment:** Holmberg and Klehr supply concealment and diligence requirements. Beggerly retains its statute-specific rule and is distinguished from this question; its separate concurrence supplies no established exception.

### 3. The compulsory-process issue remains open on remand

**Holding and operative rule:** After the correct concealment and diligence inquiry, the court of appeals may address the preserved question whether compulsory notice or conciliation under former 2 U.S.C. §437g affects the limitation period, if that question matters to timeliness. The Court awards no fixed interval and decides neither categorical compulsory-process tolling nor the action's ultimate timeliness.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela supports consideration of the necessary interval question but separately adopts a legal rule requiring exclusion of proved compulsory waiting periods; he does not join the Court's reservation of that rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Ninth Circuit did not decide this question because its January 1988 calculation remained late even if 125 days were credited. Once the premise that reports conclusively disclosed the hidden source is removed, that arithmetic cannot dispense with the preserved legal argument. An assumed maximum does not establish a statutory entitlement to that amount. Any inquiry must distinguish a required minimum and actual nonoverlapping periods from a permitted maximum conciliation period, optional investigation and internal delay. The Court neither starts a suspension with the filing of any administrative complaint nor counts the entire investigation as compulsory. It leaves the appropriate statutory decision and its necessary chronology for remand, without fixing a new hearing or deadline.

**Precedent treatment:** Sierra Club v. Chevron U.S.A., Inc., a lower-court decision under a different statute, identifies the argument preserved below; its tolling conclusion is not adopted as the Court's rule.

### 4. Vacatur does not reinstate the penalty or the ten-year injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** The appellate judgment is vacated and the case remanded for appropriate consideration or further proceedings, preserving unresolved defenses and remedy objections. A time-barred retrospective claim cannot be rescued merely by relabeling its derivative relief as an injunction; even a timely penalty claim does not automatically justify a ten-year prospective restraint.

**Authority:** Part IV has all nine joins for the actual disposition, anti-evasion principle and independent prospective-remedy requirements. The Court does not classify every governmental preventive remedy under §2462.

**Controlling explanation:** Cope v. Anderson prevents equity from becoming a means of evading a bar applicable to the concurrent legal claim. It does not resolve every independently supported governmental prevention remedy. If the penalty remains stale after the proper inquiries, merely derivative injunctive relief cannot revive it. If the enforcement action is timely, unresolved defenses and the lawful penalty still require adjudication. The injunction separately requires statutory authority, a concrete basis for prevention and terms confined to unlawful conduct. Twenty-two past transactions alone establish neither a present violation nor likely recurrence. Removing the appellate court's categorical time-bar premise supplies no finding authorizing the existing $10,000 penalty or ten-year restraint and no permission to forbid vaguely similar lawful activity.

**Precedent treatment:** Cope's concurrent-remedy principle remains binding at its scope. No universal holding treats every governmental preventive injunction as a penalty or exempts it from statutory limits.

## Precedent Treatment

- Holmberg v. Armbrecht: applies the received equitable protection against fraud preventing discovery by a diligent claimant.
- Klehr v. A. O. Smith Corp.: applies reasonable diligence and the distinction between reasonable inquiry and extraordinary investigation; its separate civil RICO accrual questions are not imported.
- United States v. Beggerly: preserves the Quiet Title Act's statute-specific rejection of additional equitable tolling and distinguishes the present concealment claim.
- Cope v. Anderson: applies the bar against evading limitations by relabeling concurrent derivative relief; it does not classify all independent government injunctions.
- United States v. Walsh and 3M Co. v. Browner: the lower-court assessment/enforcement and ordinary-accrual analyses are persuasive here.
- Sierra Club v. Chevron U.S.A., Inc.: the different statutory tolling rationale is left for the preserved argument, not adopted as a controlling rule.

## Law After Decision

FECA penalty assessment is subject to the ordinary five-year federal penalty limitation, while proven concealment requires a real diligence and discoverability inquiry. Nominal contributor reports are not conclusive disclosure of a hidden payor. The Court leaves compulsory-process tolling open and grants no 125-day allowance, discovery accrual rule or finding of timeliness. Derivative equitable relief cannot evade a stale legal claim, and prospective prevention requires its own grounds.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I, II and IV and in the judgment, and agrees that the necessary interval question must receive proper consideration on remand. He would also decide the legal rule the Court leaves open: suspend the clock for notice and good-faith conciliation periods that federal law actually compelled and during which filing was unavailable, proved to apply to this action. That finite construction coordinates §2462 with §437g; it provides no extra time for discretionary investigation or voluntary internal delay.

The duration must follow the statute and established chronology, without duplicating overlapping intervals or treating a permitted maximum as mandatory. If the concealment inquiry supports a September 1988 discovery point, the October 19, 1993 suit is roughly thirty-seven days beyond five years and a legally required pause could matter. On the Ninth Circuit's January 1988 premise, even its assumed 125 days would not cure lateness. Stone-Zsela selects neither discovery date, finds no diligence established, and awards no interval. He joins the requirement of proof, the genuine penalty deadline, the rejection of nominal-report notice as conclusive, and independent scrutiny of any prospective injunction. His compulsory-waiting rule is not the Court's holding.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit for appropriate consideration or further proceedings. If the action is timely, unresolved defenses and lawful relief remain for adjudication; if the penalty claim is stale, it and any merely derivative injunction cannot be sustained. No new penalty, injunction duration, mandatory hearing, fixed tolling interval or investigation deadline is ordered.

## Event

Hadix v. Johnson, Sixth Circuit Nos. 96-2463 and 96-2582, 133 F.3d 940. Plenary merits decision on submission, September 14, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 13. On review of the Sixth Circuit's January 13, 1998 reversal and remand of the District Court's November 1, 1996 denial of termination. No separate Supreme Court docket or exact oral-argument date is established.

Michigan prisoners brought this conditions action under 42 U.S.C. §1983 in 1980. A 1985 consent decree addressed safety, sanitation, hygiene and protection from harm without a State admission of liability. The District Court denied termination solely because it considered the Prison Litigation Reform Act's 18 U.S.C. §3626(b)(2)–(3) inconsistent with separation of powers; the United States intervened under 28 U.S.C. §2403. The questions are whether that Article III objection bars application of the termination framework and what findings remain for the returned proceedings. The separate automatic-stay appeal is not before the Court.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at submission and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No recusal or nonparticipation is established.

## Public Action

The Sixth Circuit's judgment is affirmed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings applying the amended statutory termination conditions.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit's reversal and limited remand, and remand for consistent further proceedings, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** None.

The District Court's Article III bar remains rejected. It must determine the §3626(b)(2) predicate and amended §3626(b)(3) preservation conditions on the necessary record, ending unsupported future duties and retaining only supported relief. No decree provision is automatically granted continued force or terminated; completed lawful effects are not vacated. The separate stay proceeding and the unreviewed constitutional grounds are untouched.

## Holdings

**Authority for Holdings 1–3:** Parts I–III of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt the full, bounded statutory and Article III rationale.

### 1. Conditional prospective termination preserves the judicial task

**Holding and operative rule:** On the Article III objection presented, 18 U.S.C. §3626(b)(2)–(3) validly changes the conditions for continuing prospective prison relief while leaving judges to determine actual violations, statutory eligibility and remedial scope. It neither legislatively finds compliance nor retrospectively annuls completed determinations, payments or other lawful historical effects of the decree.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that consent does not freeze the law governing future judicial supervision. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail v. Rouse, Benjamin v. Jacobson and Gavin v. Branstad apply conditional prospective termination while preserving independent adjudication. Plyler v. Moore supplies the independent statutory route. The strongest contrary argument treats a final consent decree as an irrevocable adjudication of every future duty. That argument mistakes changing prospective law for deciding the original dispute under unchanged law. Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., and United States v. Klein preserve that distinction and the judicial task. Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. and Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. permit bounded reinstatement of specified securities limitations dismissals, supplying neither absolute finality nor unrestricted congressional reopening power. The statutory requirements remain for judges to apply; Michigan's original nonadmission of liability establishes neither current compliance nor absence of a violation. No due-process or equal-protection ground is decided in this Article III review.

**Precedent treatment:** Rouse, Benjamin, Gavin and Plyler are applied at their prospective-relief scope. The changed-law precedents preserve independent adjudication; Morgan Stanley and Plaut retain their separate, bounded reinstatement holdings.

### 2. The amended statutory findings must actually be adjudicated

**Holding and operative rule:** The movant must establish that covered prospective relief was entered without substantive findings of necessity, narrowness and least intrusiveness under §3626(b)(2); sufficient findings need no ritual statutory wording. If that termination route applies, amended §3626(b)(3) preserves relief only on written, record-based findings that it remains necessary to correct a current **and** ongoing federal-right violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn, and is the least intrusive necessary means.

**Controlling explanation:** Rouse, Benjamin and Gavin supply the amended conditions. The November 26, 1997 amendment applies to pending proceedings; the former current-or-ongoing text addressed by Lewis v. Casey and Plyler is not the present standard. Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail retains its distinct significant-change and suitably tailored modification rule under Rule 60(b)(5), but that threshold is not an extra prerequisite to the enacted termination route. Consent decrees are covered relief. Negotiated protections do not become independent federal substantive rights merely because they appear in a decree. Equally, a necessary corrective remedy may exceed the minimum substantive conduct rule when all required findings support it. Neither the lack of original findings nor a term's breadth alone proves current compliance or requires unconditional termination. Each retained obligation must satisfy the preservation conditions; unsupported future obligations must end. A case in which the amended conjunction alone excludes an actually supported violation protected by the former safeguard remains undecided.

**Precedent treatment:** Rouse's amended-text application controls, as applied in Benjamin and Gavin. Lewis and Plyler retain the actual original-text holdings they made. Rufo remains a distinct equitable route, neither displaced nor added to the statutory prerequisites.

### 3. The existing remand supplies the unperformed inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the District Court denied termination solely on constitutional grounds, the Sixth Circuit's reversal and return for the statutory inquiry are affirmed, with further proceedings consistent with the amended conditions. The court must permit the material proof needed to decide those conditions; this judgment orders neither immediate wholesale termination nor indefinite unconditional continuation of the decree.

**Controlling explanation:** Gavin requires the adjudication a constitutional-only denial left undone. Benjamin involved an actual legal refusal to receive relevant current evidence; no identical refusal is established here. Rouse involved substantive evaluation of findings and monitoring reports; those evaluated findings cannot be imported into this case. Ordinary lawful procedure must allow the evidence necessary for the real inquiry, but an unreproduced decree or the possibility of useful additional information creates no freestanding hearing entitlement. The Court decides no current Michigan condition or individual obligation. The automatic-stay issue belongs to separate appeal No. 96-1943; no new stay, expiration or statutory clock follows here. Due process and equal protection, mentioned in the lower court's footnote as grounds not before it, are not enlarged into holdings. Independent contracts, state enforcement, prisoner release, new-relief standards and complete dismissal of the original action are likewise outside this decision.

**Precedent treatment:** Gavin's missing-inquiry rule applies. Benjamin's necessary-proof rule is preserved without importing its factual predicate; Rouse's rejection of a speculative compulsory inquiry remains intact.

## Holdings

### 1. The removed applications no longer present a live constitutional dispute

**Holding and operative rule:** The obsolete §121.180(6)(a) definition-based reporting and §121.035(2) corporate-employee claims, §121.025 corporate independent/issue-spending claim, §121.190(1) issue-only identification claim, and KRL/Zoeller and independent-spending branches of the §121A.030(4) gubernatorial claim are moot because the amendments removed the challenged applications and no surviving injury or supported recurrence remains. The theoretical power to reenact does not preserve those disputes; a credible threat, continuing enforcement or legally available retrospective relief would require separate analysis.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the claims must be assessed individually against the law that now operates. The revised permanent-committee definition requires a primary purpose of expressly advocating election or defeat of identified candidates, slates or political parties and regular operation throughout the year; it excludes campaign committees, political issues committees, inaugural committees and party executive committees. Independent expenditures no longer fall within contributions. Section 121.035(3)'s permission for contributions concerning constitutional amendments, ballot public questions and issues of public importance is distinct from its nonprofit independent-expenditure permission, which requires that the nonprofit not derive a substantial portion of its revenue from for-profit corporations. Nothing here decides every nonprofit's eligibility under that condition. The changed text, removed applications and reported enforcement history support mootness, without a surviving damages claim or adverse current application. Arizonans requires a live claim throughout review. The mere possibility of reenactment is insufficient, but an express announcement is not the exclusive way a recurrence concern could be established. No merits conclusion about an obsolete restriction follows from dismissing its challenge.

**Precedent treatment:** Arizonans applies the requirement of a continuing controversy and the separation of live relief from dead constitutional questions. City of Mesquite's voluntary-cessation principle remains available on supported recurrence facts; it does not make bare legislative power sufficient or announced reenactment indispensable.

### 2. Mootness requires only the appropriate nonmerits treatment of the affected judgments

**Holding and operative rule:** Existing dismissals for want of a controversy remain nonmerits dismissals; underlying merits adjudications of the now-moot applications, including the former issue-advertisement application, are vacated and those claims are to be dismissed as moot. The relief is confined to those applications and does not erase still-live judgments or confer constitutional merits jurisdiction.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins only this limited remedial judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that jurisdiction and equitable treatment of a judgment are different questions. Bancorp permits a just disposition under §2106 when ordinary merits review becomes unavailable, while denying any automatic entitlement to vacatur. Here the legislature changed the law as the losing challengers pursued review; they did not settle or voluntarily abandon that review. Leaving obsolete constitutional merits determinations in place would retain decisions that cannot now receive the review sought. Arizonans supports clearing such merits rulings while preserving the nonmerits character of jurisdictional dismissals. Those equities justify the limited cleanup, but supply no reason to disturb the independently reviewed live claims. The Court neither approves the repealed scheme nor treats the original merits rulings as void when entered.

**Precedent treatment:** Bancorp supplies equitable §2106 authority and retains its settlement-specific limits. Arizonans applies corrective disposition without merits jurisdiction. Munsingwear supports judgment clearing when review becomes unavailable, subject to the equitable inquiry rather than an automatic rule.

### 3. Nonprofit status does not require a categorical exemption for corporate candidate gifts

**Holding and operative rule:** Kentucky may apply §121.025's direct candidate-contribution restriction to KRL's general corporate treasury without creating a categorical exemption merely because KRL is an ideological nonprofit. The rejection of that facial exemption does not authorize restrictions on protected independent advocacy or determine whether KRL satisfies an independent-expenditure funding condition.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a payment to a candidate remains different from an association's own expression. NRWC supports prophylactic treatment of the corporate contribution channel, and MCFL expressly distinguishes direct contributions from the independent spending it protects. The destination of the payment and the risk of candidate obligation supply the relevant ground; the State need not first prove that this corporation completed a corrupt transaction. KRLPAC supplies a separate voluntary path for candidate-directed support, while lawful independent advocacy remains distinct. Day protects an association according to its actual resources and structure; its nonprofit holding expressly left direct corporate gifts undecided. The contrary categorical exemption would erase that distinction. The Court does not find KRL to be a commercial treasury or conduit, decide its funding eligibility, or endorse every restriction that can be attached to incorporation.

**Precedent treatment:** NRWC applies to the contribution channel; MCFL's independent-expression protection remains intact. Day's nonprofit holding is distinguished according to the transaction it decided. Austin is neither overruled nor enlarged beyond its surviving corporate-treasury scope.

### 4. The paid candidate-advertisement identification rule survives this facial challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** The surviving facial challenge to §121.190(1)'s payer-identification requirement fails in the paid candidate-finance and organizational setting presented. The rule requires the payer's name and address, introduced by the paid-for-by designation, on newspaper and magazine advertising, posters, circulars, billboards, handbills, sample ballots and paid television or radio announcements expressly advocating election or defeat of a clearly identified candidate, slate or group of candidates; ordinary issue advocacy is not covered merely because it mentions a candidate.

**Authority:** Part IV of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Thomas dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that identifying the financer of organized paid candidate advocacy serves source-tracing and enforcement interests different from general curiosity about an ordinary issue leafleteer's identity. The address helps identify the legal payer and connect an advertisement to financial transactions; it is a burden requiring justification, not dispensable statutory language. Buckley's disclosure interests and McIntyre's express reservation of materially different campaign-finance and organizational settings permit this bounded facial judgment. The on-message command is more intrusive than filing a report, but the actual financer-identification function supplies the necessary connection here without limiting the amount or content of the independent expenditure. The State cannot presume corruption or coordination merely from an advertisement's independence or political identity. McIntyre continues to protect ordinary anonymous issue leaflets, and materially different individual applications remain open. So does a claim supported by a reasonable probability of threats or reprisals. No actual retaliation, coordination, general membership-disclosure authority or requirement of permission before publication is found.

**Precedent treatment:** McIntyre is distinguished on its reserved candidate-finance and organizational context without diminishing its individual-leaflet rule. Buckley supplies the distinct finance-disclosure interests. Colorado Republican preserves independence and forbids identity-based substitution for an actual candidate connection. Brown v. Socialist Workers preserves protection against demonstrated associational harms without creating a new blanket exemption.

### 5. The preserved challenges to the two direct $1,000 limits fail

**Holding and operative rule:** The preserved facial challenge to §121.150(6)'s $1,000 giving and acceptance limit in any one election is rejected under the direct-contribution distinction. The separate challenge to §121A.050(1)'s $1,000 slate limit in any one election is likewise rejected, preserving that section's knowing-acceptance and knowing-giving terms and the giving prohibition's express exception for independent expenditures as statutorily defined.

**Authority:** Part V of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; the coalition covers both distinct provisions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the surviving direct-transfer challenges can be resolved under Buckley's contribution rule without treating contribution limits as constitutionally unlimited. A direct payment to the candidate presents a corruption and circumvention concern different from financing one's own advocacy. The lower court reported that counsel had proposed the $1,000 figure and essentially conceded its constitutionality. That reported position confines the preserved controversy; it is not a general unseen waiver or a reason to accept an incorrect legal rule. The direct-transfer character and bounded claim supply the judgment. The Court does not move the slate provision's knowledge requirement or independent-expenditure exception into a different section. Nor does it turn a per-election limit into an annual one, establish $1,000 as a constitutional minimum or safe harbor for every office, or authorize limits meant to equalize political influence. Future demonstrated cumulative or prohibitory burdens remain distinct.

**Precedent treatment:** Buckley's direct-contribution distinction and fitted anticorruption rationale apply; its historical figure is not a constitutional floor. Day preserves scrutiny of meaningful association and cumulative burdens without equating contributions with independent expenditures.

### 6. The annual all-intermediary aggregate requires the inquiry the lower court bypassed

**Holding and operative rule:** A $1,500 ceiling on one person's annual contributions to all permanent committees and contributing organizations together must be assessed for meaningful collective political association and fit to an identified corruption or circumvention mechanism. Because the lower judgment sustained §121.150(10) without giving that inquiry its required force, the judgment on this component is vacated and remanded for application of Day to the existing record and ordinary remaining remedial questions.

**Authority:** Part VI of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: six Justices. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg dissent from this remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the ability to regulate a real candidate-funding conduit does not answer the fit of an aggregate that exhausts one allowance across unrelated recipients. Day requires meaningful opportunity to pool resources for political expression. California Medical permits prevention of circumvention through candidate-transfer channels, but its four-Justice low-protection reasoning is not a controlling categorical answer to every intermediary contribution. KRLPAC's candidate transfers identify a legitimate concern; they do not establish the fit of the law's complete combined-recipient reach. The lower court must consider actual recipient functions, the aggregate's breadth, the supported burden and the separate opportunity for direct candidate gifts under the proper standard. A higher figure than Day's $100 does not end the inquiry, and a general reference to smaller state campaigns does not supply it. Conversely, the Court finds neither every application invalid nor the State's evidentiary showing absent. This is a legal-standard remand, not a compulsory new trial, discovery order, new burden of proof, constitutional dollar minimum or direction to exempt all independent recipients automatically.

**Precedent treatment:** Day applies its meaningful-association and fit rule beyond the particular $100 ceiling while retaining its limits. California Medical preserves genuine conduit regulation without converting plurality reasoning into law. Citizens Against Rent Control protects collective expression; NCPAC preserves independent advocacy without immunizing candidate contributions.

### 7. Both gubernatorial receipt branches survive KRLPAC's facial challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** KRLPAC's facial challenge to both §121A.030(4) receipt limits is rejected: the Fund participant's permanent-committee receipts are limited to 25 percent of qualifying contributions received, up to $150,000 per election, while the nonparticipant is subject to the separate $150,000 ceiling. Direct candidate receipts present a source-specific dependency concern, and this facial record does not demonstrate obstruction of effective advocacy or association; concrete later burdens under either branch remain open.

**Authority:** Part VII of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate and remand both branches.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that candidate receipts from one organized financing channel differ from the annual pool across independent recipients addressed in Part VI. Buckley's contribution distinction permits a fitted response to dependence on that channel without requiring proof of a completed corrupt bargain. The percentage branch limits the share measured against the participant's own qualifying contributions; it remains operative at receipts below the maximum. The nonparticipant branch uses its separate fixed ceiling. The district court reported no indication that the provision had actually limited PAC contributions or was likely to do so on this record. That reported conclusion supports this facial judgment; it is not a statutory exception or an irrebuttable fact about every race. The contrary concern that earlier donors may exhaust a later association's opportunity is substantial but does not establish facial invalidity here. Neither the maximum-fundraising example nor the number of committees needed to reach a maximum is a premise of this judgment. The Court does not rely on consent to unknown funding conditions, disfavor of PAC views, or equalization. Later proof of ordinary fundraising disabled by the percentage at low receipts, selective foreclosure or prohibitory cumulative effects requires separate consideration.

**Precedent treatment:** Buckley's direct-contribution rationale applies at this source-specific and facial level. Day preserves meaningful association and rejects a permanent dollar safe harbor; its different annual-intermediary and responsive-financing components do not resolve these candidate-receipt rules. Colorado Republican preserves independent advocacy without converting direct candidate receipts into independent spending.

## Law After Decision

Kentucky's annual $1,500 all-intermediary aggregate has not been held facially unconstitutional; its sustaining judgment must be reconsidered under Day's meaningful-association and fit inquiry. The direct corporate-gift, paid candidate-identification and two direct $1,000 judgments remain effective only within the preserved facial questions and distinct statutory terms. Both gubernatorial receipt branches survive KRLPAC's facial attack, with the percentage operative below the maximum and later concrete burdens reserved. The decision creates no universal dollar threshold, blanket nonprofit immunity, permission to regulate issue speech merely mentioning a candidate, or complete rule for public-financing conditions. Obsolete constitutional merits rulings receive no continuing force through the limited mootness disposition.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; Stevens and Ginsburg join the annual-aggregate dissent only.** The three would affirm §121.150(10)'s annual-aggregate judgment on the preserved facial record. Buckley and California Medical permit preventive regulation of actual candidate-funding intermediaries, and KRLPAC makes candidate transfers. Day requires meaningful pooling and genuine fit, but its established $100 burden does not compel remand whenever a higher limit's practical effects have not been quantified. The valid direct candidate limit and anti-pass-through function suffice here, without declaring intermediary association less valuable or requiring proof of completed corruption. The dissent recognizes that the combined cap substantially burdens multi-organization association. It reserves developed proof of prohibitory cumulative operation, improperly covered independent advocacy and changed conditions, including inflation; its position creates no perpetual $1,500 safe harbor. Stone-Zsela alone further explains that the Fund-participant percentage connects the source ceiling to the slate's own qualifying receipts more closely than a flat maximum. It may bind at lower receipts; its facial justification rests on source-specific dependency and this record, not on a 151st donor. A small-receipt slate's later concrete fundraising disability remains open. That additional comparison is not part of the Court's holding.

**Kennedy, dissenting in part, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** These Justices would vacate and remand both gubernatorial branches for distinct fit analysis. They accept the difference between candidate cash and independent expression and the legitimacy of preventing candidate dependency. But a classwide receipt ceiling excludes later willing donors because others have already given, even though each donor obeys the base limit. Day and the contribution principles preserved in Colorado Republican require the added burden to have its own fitted justification. General hostility toward organized interests and an illustration assuming maximum fundraising and maximum gifts do not supply it, particularly for a percentage that binds earlier. They would remand without facially invalidating either branch, immunizing candidate contributions, or deciding the full constitutionality of voluntary public financing.

**Thomas, dissenting in part.** Thomas would vacate and remand the surviving identification judgment to address the statute's coverage of an individual's independent candidate handbill. Talley and the anonymous-pamphleteering tradition underlying his McIntyre position do not end when the pamphlet discusses a candidate instead of a ballot issue. Identifying organized campaign financing may justify distinct reports, but it does not automatically justify placing an individual's name and address on every protected handbill. He does not find that Zoeller actually distributed one, exempt every organization or medium, select an unverified statutory exception, or order a blanket injunction. His objection seeks proper treatment of the protected-pamphlet breadth, rather than facial invalidation of every identification duty.

## Event

Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County; Nos. 97-1115 and 97-1160. Plenary merits decision on submission, September 21, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 13. ## Holdings

### 1. Unequal competitive opportunity supports this review

**Holding and operative rule:** Regular bidders facing a supported likelihood of exclusion from equal competition have an injury even without proving that they would win a contract. The associations may seek common prospective relief where their members possess standing, the interest is germane, and the claims and relief do not require individual participation; facts may be stipulated, but jurisdiction may not.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion has all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela joins the equal-competition injury proposition and the distinction between subsidiary factual review and constitutional legal review; he does not add a distinct associational-standing rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Northeastern Florida Chapter, Associated General Contractors v. Jacksonville identifies the denial of equal bidding opportunity as the injury. United Food & Commercial Workers Union Local 751 v. Brown Group preserves the association requirements and their distinct constitutional and prudential roles. The associations' regularly bidding members and stipulated likely future exclusion establish the necessary injury for all three programs, and the common justification challenge permits common prospective relief. No lost award or injury from every hypothetical contract mechanism is required. This does not confer standing to litigate every distinct operational challenge. Under §1254(1), the Court reviews the final appellate judgment. Subsidiary historical findings receive clear-error review; constitutional sufficiency, the level of scrutiny and remedial fit require independent legal assessment.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Northeastern Florida applies unequal-competition injury; its standing rule does not decide the preferences' validity.
- Brown Group applies the association requirements at their stated scope; its standing rule does not decide the preferences' validity.

### 2. The local racial classifications remain subject to strict scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule:** Local racial and ethnic contracting classifications must rest on a supported compelling justification and narrowly fitted means. General social inequality, numerical imbalance and good intentions alone do not establish that justification; a supported local system of exclusion in which the County participates can permit fitted repair without invariably requiring a prior formal judgment of liability.

**Authority:** Part II has all eight Associates for strict scrutiny and the federal/local distinction. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the affirmative remedial proposition; Scalia reserves the permissible extent of racial remediation beyond the grounds needed to reject these programs. Stone-Zsela does not join this local rule.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co. governs local contracting preferences, and Hopwood v. Texas preserves supported justification, individual equality and narrow fit for state racial classifications. Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña sustained its distinct federal arrangement under Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC's important-objective and substantial-relationship standard. Adarand expressly preserved Croson; its federal rule cannot be transferred to Dade County merely because the County describes its purpose as corrective. The Court declines the proposed replacement of local strict scrutiny. This decision creates neither a right to proportionate contracts nor a prohibition on every properly supported local repair program.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Croson remains controlling for local racial classifications.
- Hopwood remains controlling for state racial classifications and fitted repair.
- Adarand retains its bounded federal application; it is neither overruled nor enlarged.
- Metro Broadcasting retains the different federal standard; it is neither overruled nor enlarged.

### 3. The BBE and HBE programs fail the independent narrow-fit requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** Before using these racial and ethnic contracting preferences, the County must seriously consider reasonably available race-neutral measures responsive to the exclusion it identifies and provide a supported explanation of their inadequacy. This record does not satisfy that requirement for either BBE or HBE; it requires considered alternatives, not trial and exhaustion of every conceivable measure.

**Authority:** Part III has all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela would vacate and remand under a different proposed local rule. Four Associates also find the racial evidence insufficient, but that additional ground does not control.

**Controlling explanation:** Croson's narrow-fit requirement and Hopwood's fitted-repair principle require attention to measures addressing the actual problem. The record identifies procurement information, financing, bonding, payment and discriminatory-treatment obstacles. The County's limited technical and financial assistance and conclusory treatment of alternatives do not show serious assessment of responsive neutral measures; HBE lacks such consideration. Flexibility, waivers, annual review and five-year continuation review cannot supply the missing inquiry. The possibility that an existing BBE program reduced observed disparities matters to evidence, but cannot cure this distinct tailoring defect. The Court therefore need not decide whether the combined racial evidence would otherwise establish a sufficient remedial predicate. It does not constitutionalize a statistical-significance threshold, numerical parity, a requirement of a prior discrimination judgment or a duty to exhaust every possible neutral course.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Croson applies to the unperformed alternatives inquiry without making corrective contracting preferences categorically invalid.
- Hopwood applies its fitted-repair principle to that inquiry without categorical invalidation of corrective preferences.

### 4. The WBE program fails the distinct intermediate-scrutiny inquiry on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** A sex-conscious contracting measure requires an actual important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the classification, with a demanding governmental burden. On independent assessment of the accepted findings and combined evidence, the County has not adequately substantiated the identified gender exclusion needed to justify this three-sector WBE preference; its invalidation is affirmed.

**Authority:** All nine accept the stated intermediate standard. Part IV's application and affirmance have five direct votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate and remand for a different independent assessment of the combined record.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley v. Board of Trustees, Klinger v. Department of Corrections and United States v. Virginia require actual important purposes and a substantial relationship, without importing racial strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. The Court independently examines the constitutional sufficiency of the evidence while retaining subsidiary findings not shown clearly erroneous. Firm-size adjustments largely explain award-dollar differences; the surviving WBE SIC 15 disparity, women's entry disparity and accounts of refusing to deal with female owners matter but do not adequately justify this program's allocation across all three construction sectors. The subcontract denominator and broader marketplace pool do not reliably identify the relevant procurement comparison. The conclusion does not rest merely on calling the trial judge's ultimate constitutional view permissible. Nor does it impose a numerical minimum or rule out sufficient nonstatistical proof. The court of appeals rejected strict racial tailoring for WBE and found its flexibility capable of satisfying substantial relationship if the evidentiary predicate existed. The present failure is that predicate, not the racial alternatives defect, an excessive 11 percent goal, or an obligation to exhaust neutral measures. A later supported and differently designed women's program is not decided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Kelley retains demanding intermediate scrutiny and rejects stereotyped or merely numerical justification; it supplies no strict-scrutiny or statistical-only prerequisite.
- Klinger retains that distinct intermediate standard and actual-justification requirement, without a statistical-only prerequisite.
- Virginia retains demanding intermediate scrutiny and rejection of stereotyped justification, without importing racial strict scrutiny or least restrictive means.

### 5. The existing injunctions remain confined to these programs

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgments and permanent injunctions against the challenged BBE, HBE and WBE programs are affirmed on their respective grounds. The relief addresses the operative programs and established likely future unequal exclusion; it neither prohibits unexamined future corrective measures nor awards a contract, damages or an individual discrimination judgment.

**Authority:** Part V has the eight-Associate coalition for BBE/HBE and the five-Justice WBE coalition identified above. Stone-Zsela would maintain the racial restraints temporarily during his proposed remand, rather than affirm their present permanent disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Concrete future exclusion, not past injury alone, supplies a preventive basis for relief. Hopwood's independent prospective-relief requirements and Federal Election Commission v. Williams's insistence on an actual preventive foundation are satisfied by the still-operative programs and stipulated future exposure. Williams supplies no campaign-finance limitation rule here. Lawful race-neutral outreach and procurement measures remain available. No court is directed to adopt a new preference program, and the separate evidentiary bases are not merged.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Hopwood supplies its independent prospective-relief limits without deciding a future unexamined scheme.
- Williams supplies the actual preventive-basis requirement without deciding a future unexamined scheme.

## Event

Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Johnson & Higgins, Inc.; No. 96-1743, petition captioned Johnson & Higgins, Inc. v. EEOC; Second Circuit No. 95-6216. Plenary merits decision on submission, September 21, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 13. Review of the Second Circuit's August 8, 1996 decision, 91 F.3d 1529, affirming an interlocutory injunction following partial summary judgment on ADEA liability. Monetary relief was reserved. No exact oral-argument date in this Court is established.

The questions concern independent EEOC enforcement and conciliation, employee status of working owner-directors, the age-based retirement rule and statutory exceptions, and the employment injunction's scope. Johnson & Higgins is a New Jersey corporation conducting an insurance and employee-benefits business. Its board ranges from 20 to 44 directors, with 35 at the investigation's beginning. Nomination by the chairman and election by two thirds of the board select existing senior employees; annual evaluation by six senior directors, compensation review and removal controls continue alongside substantial ownership and governance.

Directors must hold at least 500 shares, and cessation of ownership ends board service. Departure requires share sale; a ten-year agreement provides dividend-equivalent payments in exchange for consulting availability and noncompetition. Stock is predominantly director-owned, with a small fraction issued to other key employees since 1993. The unanimously adopted 1983 policy couples retirement from employment, officer status, directorship and equity at the end of the year age 62 is attained, or earlier when age 60 and fifteen board years coincide. Only the latter branch permits corporate-need extensions approved by the chairman and executive committee, year by year and no later than the end of the age-62 year.

The EEOC learned of the policy during another investigation. No current or former director sought this enforcement action, and affidavits opposed it. Investigation notice, a determination, a July 1992 conciliation meeting and invitation, renewed information requests and refusals preceded suit filed August 5, 1993. The reported releases lacked ADEA-specific and seven-day-revocation terms; no individual's monetary entitlement or release consequence is adjudicated here.

All nine participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No recusal or nonparticipation is established.

The Second Circuit judgment affirming ADEA liability and the bounded prospective employment injunction is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

The public enforcement and governing classification rules have nine joins, without converting the dissenters' conditional age-rule agreement into a coverage or liability vote. The maintained employment-retirement policy and incoming employee agreements to it remain restrained; rescission and submission of a compliant policy proceed on a date set below. Separately lawful board and equity arrangements remain possible. Monetary relief is reserved; no damages, reinstatement or board/ownership entitlement is awarded.

## Holdings

### 1. Private opposition does not disable the Commission's public enforcement action

**Holding and operative rule:** The EEOC may enforce the ADEA under 29 U.S.C. §626(b) without an affected individual's charge, consent or request for recovery, and §626(f)(4) prevents a private waiver from extinguishing that public authority even if the waiver is valid. The Commission must attempt informal conciliation, conference and persuasion before suit; the actual efforts here satisfy that condition without a duty to repeat an unproductive offer indefinitely.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 626(b) incorporates public enforcement powers and remedies; §626(d)'s charge and waiting requirements govern an individual's action, while §216(b)'s party-plaintiff consent requirement does not govern the Commission. General Telephone Co. of Northwest v. EEOC supports independent public enforcement in its Title VII setting, and Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp. preserves Commission authority despite an individual's separate arrangement. The ADEA's own text decides this case. Notice of investigation, a determination, the July 1992 meeting and invitation, and renewed September and December information requests gave the company the actual violation theory and opportunities to resolve it before the August 5, 1993 suit. Its resistance explains why repetition was unnecessary; categorical denial alone is not a general exception to the statutory attempt requirement. Opposing director affidavits therefore do not bar this suit. Oubre v. Entergy Operations preserves enacted waiver safeguards, but the Court need not determine each release's private effect because §626(f)(4) independently protects enforcement. No automatic private damages, recovery for unwilling persons, repayment or double-recovery ruling follows.

**Precedent treatment:**

- General Telephone supports independent agency enforcement in its statutory setting without replacing the ADEA text.
- Gilmer preserves independent agency enforcement despite an individual's arrangement, without replacing the ADEA text.
- Oubre's private-waiver holding remains distinct; this decision neither enlarges nor retreats from it.

### 2. Employee status depends on the complete relationship

**Holding and operative rule:** The circular employee definition in 29 U.S.C. §630(f) invokes ordinary common-law agency and requires consideration of all relevant incidents of the actual relationship. Corporate form, an employee label, director title, ownership, profit-based compensation or an exclusive three-factor formula cannot alone establish or defeat employment; a genuine proprietor remains outside employee coverage, while actual employment may coexist with ownership and directorship.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden supplies the ordinary-agency presumption and the all-incidents inquiry. Relevant matters include control over work and removal, skill, tools and location, duration, assignment and scheduling authority, compensation, assistants, business integration, benefits and tax treatment; in this setting, governance powers, equity and profit-and-loss arrangements must also receive their actual weight. NLRB v. Town & Country Electric permits compatible roles while preserving actual control and statutory boundaries. Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises likewise distinguishes a genuine relationship from an evidentiary label, and Robinson v. Shell Oil requires attention to the particular statutory setting. Those decisions do not classify every working owner as an employee. Incorporation does not create categorical coverage, and the company's partnership analogy does not erase a distinct employment relationship. ADEA incorporation of FLSA remedies does not import the FLSA's broader definition of employment.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Darden's common-law method applies to the ADEA's circular definition.
- Town & Country preserves compatible roles and actual service within its statutory boundaries; unrelated exclusions are not transplanted.
- Walters supports genuine-relationship inquiry at its statutory scope without importing unrelated Title VII holdings.
- Robinson supports attention to statutory context without importing unrelated Title VII holdings.

### 3. These directors continue covered employment alongside their ownership and governance

**Holding and operative rule:** On the complete undisputed relationship, the employee-directors remain ADEA employees while holding stock and exercising substantial collective governance. Their actual full-time operational service, individual review, compensation determination and removal controls establish employment that election to the board does not extinguish.

**Authority:** Part III has six direct votes: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate and remand classification and the dependent liability and injunction.

**Controlling explanation:** The directors are selected from existing senior officers and continue their ordinary managerial work. Six senior directors conduct annual reviews; compensation depends on performance and an annually determined profit percentage; each director submits a resignation exercisable by two thirds of the board. These controls operate on each individual despite collective ownership. The Court gives substantial weight to the contrary features: required shares, voting and appointment powers, authority over merger or dissolution, profit participation, mutual succession terms, compulsory share sale on departure and the ten-year consulting/noncompetition arrangement. Those features establish genuine ownership and governance, but do not eliminate the continuing operational employment subject to other senior actors' control. Darden requires this complete assessment, rather than acceptance of the lower court's categorical phrasing or exclusive selection of three indicia. The undisputed incidents permit decision now. No inactive director becomes an employee by title alone, and no rule places all partners, shareholders or full-time proprietors within the Act.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Darden applies its complete inquiry to the actual dual relationship without a per se rule for corporate owners.
- Town & Country preserves compatible roles in applying that inquiry without a per se corporate-owner rule.

### 4. A sensible reason for selecting age is not a factor other than age

**Holding and operative rule:** For a covered employee, compulsory retirement at the end of the year age 62 is attained, or earlier at the end of the year both age 60 and fifteen board-service years are attained, is an age-based employment decision. Succession planning does not make those express age conditions reasonable factors other than age under §623(f)(1); the corporate-need extension available only on the age-60/service branch does not remove the age trigger.

**Authority:** Part IV.A's statutory rule has nine direct joins. The six-Justice coverage coalition applies it to liability here; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join only on the express condition that employee status is established.

**Controlling explanation:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins distinguishes years of service actually independent of age from a decision motivated by age or using a criterion as its proxy. These alternatives expressly require age. The chairman's corporate-need finding, approved by the executive committee, permits only year-by-year continuation under the earlier branch and never beyond the end of the year age 62 is attained. The company's reasonable desire for succession therefore cannot satisfy a provision requiring a criterion other than age. It may pursue another legally permissible criterion or separate lawful governance retirement from covered employment. No heightened constitutional age tier is created, and no bona fide occupational qualification, foreign-law conflict or qualifying benefit/seniority-plan defense is established on this record.

**Precedent treatment:** Hazen Paper's actual-age/non-age distinction controls. Its willfulness rule is preserved; facial liability does not itself establish knowledge or reckless disregard or award liquidated damages.

### 5. The executive-retirement provision does not authorize these ages

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 631(c) permits compulsory retirement only for an employee who has attained age 65, following bona fide executive or high-policymaking employment throughout the two immediately preceding years, with an immediate nonforfeitable aggregate annual retirement benefit of at least $44,000 from the employer's pension, profit-sharing, savings or deferred-compensation plans, or their combination. This policy's ages 60 and 62 fail the age condition regardless of the unresolved benefit valuation; executive status alone supplies no exemption.

**Authority:** Part IV.B's statutory construction has the eight Associates. The five Associates affirming coverage apply it here. Stone-Zsela agrees in rejection of this exception without adding a ground; the three classification dissenters retain their coverage condition and do not join the ultimate liability judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The exception contains cumulative conditions, not a general freedom to retire any executive. For benefits paid in a form other than a straight-life annuity without ancillary benefits, or involving employee contributions or rollovers, §631(c)(2) requires the prescribed adjustment to the equivalent of a straight-life annuity without ancillary benefits under a plan without employee or rollover contributions. Neither the ten-year contract nor a general description of generous retirement benefits establishes those requirements. The age condition alone disposes of the asserted applicability to the maintained policy; no benefit amount or annuity valuation is found. The Court neither requires a proprietor to remain in governance nor extends the exception below its enacted age.

## Event

## Holdings

### 1. Past restraints do not alone establish a present controversy under changed law

**Holding and operative rule:** A prospective challenge requires identified intended conduct facing a realistic current enforcement threat; ended injunctions under replaced or materially amended provisions do not alone supply it. Count One must be dismissed on this record, without requiring an actual prosecution or deciding whether any publication or disclosure rule is constitutional.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno and Virginia v. American Booksellers Association permit review before enforcement where the threatened constraint is concrete. Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona requires a continuing personal controversy. Here all injunctions against these plaintiffs have ended, the statutes have materially changed, and no identified intended activity faces a supported current threat. The Party's failure to disavow every future suit does not fill that gap. Caldwell's authoritative exclusion of solely issue advocacy from the current Virginia provisions further weakens a threat resting on issue-only speech, without treating the express vote-against pamphlet as issue-only or deciding any past order. Amendment does not automatically moot every claim, and the importance of timely political speech cannot replace the absent current controversy. No rule limits proper defendants universally to the Commonwealth, bars every suit against a private enforcer, or disposes of accrued damages merely because prospective relief ended.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Pic-A-State applies the concrete-threat requirement and preserves review without prosecution.
- American Booksellers preserves credible pre-enforcement review; no such threat is established here.
- Arizonans applies the continuing-controversy requirement without extending its unavailable-damages holding to these private damages defendants.
- Caldwell supplies current Virginia construction only; it does not adjudicate the past injunctions.

### 2. These pleaded damages counts seek appellate reassessment of particular state judgments

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal district court lacks authority under Rooker and Feldman to adjudicate these counts insofar as their asserted federal wrong is the legal error of the particular state injunctions and their requested damages compensate those orders' direct effects. The rule is confined to that pleaded theory; it does not bar a claim merely because state litigation caused, preceded or bears on an independently actionable injury.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Rooker v. Fidelity Trust Co. and District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman reserve appellate reassessment of state judicial decisions to the proper appellate channel. The orders here applied law after an opportunity to object; prospective terms did not make those adjudications legislative acts. The published descriptions of the four counts demand a determination that the specific restraints were legally erroneous, without identifying a separate procurement wrong. Their later expiration or dissolution and the demand for damages against the procuring party do not change that pleaded wrong. Dennis v. Sparks preserves independent corrupt-procurement claims, and Johnson v. De Grandy preserves original federal enforcement without appellate review of a judgment against the claimant. Neither supplies a missing independent allegation here. The Court rejects a universal rule covering every consequence of a state order and a simple but-for causation test. It makes no finding that the Party's conduct was lawful and decides no preclusion, state action, immunity, limitations, personal responsibility or damages question.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Rooker applies the lower-federal appellate boundary to the actual wrong and relief asserted.
- Feldman applies the original-action versus individual-judgment distinction; prospective relief can remain judicial adjudication.
- Dennis preserves genuinely independent procurement claims; judicial immunity does not immunize private corrupt conspirators.
- De Grandy preserves its independent-enforcement holding and is distinguished from these pleaded attacks on judgments against the claimants.
- Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co. remains the separate state-attribution inquiry; merely winning or filing private litigation does not establish it.

### 3. Heck supplies no universal civil-injunction jurisdiction bar

**Holding and operative rule:** Heck's favorable-invalidation requirement concerns present §1983 cognizability in its bounded custody setting, not lower-federal appellate jurisdiction. It supplies no universal rule requiring favorable termination of every civil injunction before an independent damages action may proceed.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes on this explanation. The separate writings likewise reject a categorical bar but do not add joins to the Court's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Heck v. Humphrey coordinates §1983 with a real lawful opportunity to challenge an outstanding conviction in custody, while preserving independently cognizable injuries and its unresolved populations. Edwards v. Balisok applies that rule to invalidity-dependent disciplinary-credit claims. Spencer v. Kemna keeps Article III injury, statutory access and possible civil relief distinct and preserves the reservations for former prisoners and those without a realistic habeas opportunity. Those decisions neither convert cognizability into subject-matter jurisdiction nor authorize appellate reassessment of these orders. The lower court's assimilation of Heck to Rooker and Feldman is rejected. Affirmance rests on the actual claim-specific jurisdictional analysis, not a new civil-injunction favorable-termination rule.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Heck retains its bounded statutory prerequisite and independent-injury limits; it is not a Rooker-Feldman decision.
- Edwards retains the invalidity-dependent disciplinary-credit extension, without governing these civil orders.
- Spencer preserves the distinct inquiries and unresolved populations; no exemption or universal bar is created here.

### 4. The state claims lack the required supplemental-jurisdiction foundation

**Holding and operative rule:** Where the pleaded federal claims supply no original jurisdiction, §1367(a) supplies no supplemental jurisdiction over their related state claims. Counts Four, Five, Six and Nine therefore remain dismissed without prejudice, without an adjudication of tort merits or a discretionary retention decision under §1367(c)(3).

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part IV, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1367(a) requires a civil action within the district court's original jurisdiction before its supplemental grant operates. Count One lacks a current controversy and the pleaded damages counts demand appellate authority the district court does not possess. The district court's actual no-original-jurisdiction ground therefore supports dismissal. Section 1367(c)(3), which permits declining supplemental jurisdiction after claims within original jurisdiction have been dismissed, is not an interchangeable description of that defect. Nothing here requires a court possessing original jurisdiction to retain every related state claim. State tort elements, timeliness and recovery remain for a competent forum.

**Precedent treatment:** The Court corrects the Fourth Circuit's discretionary description while preserving the district court's §1367(a) ground; no earlier Supreme Court decision is displaced.

## Event

Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith, No. 96-1867. Plenary merits decision on submission, September 21, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 13. Review under 28 U.S.C. §1257 of the Florida Supreme Court's December 5, 1996 judgment, 687 So. 2d 1292, with rehearing denied February 17, 1997, approving the First District's judgment sustaining the filing-fee allocation. The question is whether Florida Statutes §99.103(1) (1993) violates federal political-association and equality protections by distributing approximately half of qualifying parties' candidates' filing fees to those parties' executive committees for party expenses, while excluding parties below five percent of the State's registered voters. The criterion is objective and potentially changing; it does not name the Democratic and Republican Parties. The published description supports that approximate allocation, not an exact dollar formula.

## Holdings

### 1. The federal question is reviewable

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1257 permits review of the federal constitutional holding where the state court expressly treats the relevant state constitutional protection as coextensive and supplies no adequate independent state ground sustaining its judgment. The Court may decide this federal challenge without announcing an independent construction of Florida's Constitution.

**Precedent treatment:** No earlier Supreme Court rule is altered; the ordinary federal-review and independent-state-ground boundaries apply.

### 2. This objective support criterion is constitutional at the demonstrated burden

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may allocate this limited party-expense benefit according to an objective five-percent registered-support criterion where lawfully collected fees, continued candidate access, unrestricted private political spending and the demonstrated record establish no severe associational burden, and the measure reasonably serves stable public participation. The holding permits demonstrated-support financing; it does not permit preserving named incumbents or suppressing political viewpoints as independent objectives.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes, including the stated limitations.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson v. Celebrezze requires comparing the character and magnitude of actual injury with the State's precise interests and their need for the burden. Burdick v. Takushi applies that inquiry to the complete electoral setting: severe burdens demand narrowly fitted compelling justification; reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens ordinarily may rest on important regulatory interests. A funding label does not excuse examination of the classification, but a comparative benefit alone does not establish severe restriction.

Buckley v. Valeo, Part III, permits demonstrated-support conditions on election funding and distinguishes withholding a subsidy from excluding a candidacy. Here the objective, potentially changing registration threshold reasonably measures stable party support. Supporting established public participation without subsidizing every splinter organization is a legitimate allocation interest. Wilson's individual ballot qualification does not establish an automatic entitlement to party-wide financial assistance. Norman v. Reed protects independently qualified candidacies; this provision does not remove one from the ballot.

The strongest contrary argument is that selective aid magnifies existing parties' advantages after rivals qualify. Such an allocation remains reviewable for substantial suppression, viewpoint favoritism or discriminatory administration. This record establishes no such burden beyond the limited benefit's existence. Florida's registration measure differs from Buckley's vote criteria, expenditure ceilings and post-election payments; those features are not imported here. The Court does not adopt unqualified rhetoric about discouraging minor parties, a general rational-basis exemption for funding, or validity for every five-percent threshold.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Anderson applies the actual-burden and precise-interest inquiry to this financial classification.
- Burdick preserves burden-sensitive scrutiny; its write-in presumption is not transferred to all subsidies.
- Buckley supplies demonstrated-support funding authority while retaining constitutional limits and political fluidity; the schemes are not identical.
- Norman preserves qualified-component ballot protection without creating automatic party-expense funding.
- Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes preserves review of genuine support criteria and viewpoint discrimination; its forum classification is not an election-finance rule.
- National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley supplies no universal subsidy immunity; its arts-program holding and divided spending rationales retain their actual scope.

### 3. Affirmance supplies no new payment or allocation entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the presented federal challenge fails, the judgment sustaining §99.103(1)'s limited allocation is affirmed without a rebate, refund or judicially prescribed formula. The lawful collection of candidate fees, access to the ballot and a materially different source-of-funds exaction remain outside the adjudicated challenge.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** Stone-Zsela would reverse because this mandatory candidate-fee allocation gives an institutional benefit to established parties while withholding the same form of support from a recognized smaller party whose candidate has already qualified and paid the lawful fee. Anderson and Burdick require examination of that injury and Florida's actual interest. The five-percent statewide-registration threshold measures accumulated party strength rather than this candidate's qualified race or an identified administrative cost of returning the fee. For him, preserving established organizations and avoiding factional alternatives do not adequately justify this particular exclusion; institutional advantage cannot be its own constitutional end.

Buckley permits genuine support-sensitive financing and is not overruled by that position. Norman reinforces that unrelated party-organization restrictions cannot nullify valid candidate qualification, though Stone-Zsela does not claim the challenged rule literally removes Wilson from the ballot. The party asserts the participation interests of its members and voters, not personal rights for every legal entity. No finding that minor-party payments were directly transferred to rivals is needed for his objection to the different benefits generated by the same type of lawful fee.

He would enjoin further distribution under the invalid classification after orderly implementation on remand. Florida could end the rebate, extend it evenhandedly to recognized parties with qualified candidates, or adopt a genuine support-sensitive measure justified by an independent constitutional interest. ## Holdings

### 1. The recurring access controversy remains reviewable

**Holding and operative rule:** Completion of the 1996 primary does not end this recurring challenge to continuing party classifications and nomination requirements. The Court reaches the access questions without reopening that primary or adopting apparent petition insufficiency as an independent standing ground.

**Authority:** Souter's Part I has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela also joins the recurring-controversy and no-reopened-election conclusions, without an additional petition-sufficiency rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Norman v. Reed preserves review of recurring election conditions that otherwise escape decision within a completed election cycle. These classifications continue to govern the party's congressional route, so the passage of the particular primary does not resolve the dispute. The Court does not independently adjudicate standing on a new assessment of the rejected sheets, or convert acceptance of other deficient petitions into proof of legal compliance. Review permits deciding the continuing rule; it does not require undoing a completed election.

**Precedent treatment:** Norman applies at its election-continuity scope without ordering a new election.

### 2. The actual congressional access system survives this limited challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Anderson and Burdick, Illinois may require meaningful support in the relevant congressional electorate and use different primary and general-election nomination routes where the complete actual system leaves a reasonable available route to the general ballot. On this record, trustee-based statewide status does not compel district-established status, and the combined requirements do not establish the severe or unjustified burden asserted; the holding creates no categorical safe harbor for percentages, slates or cumulative deadlines.

**Authority:** Souter's Part II is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson v. Celebrezze requires assessment of the injury's character and magnitude, the State's precise interests and their need for the burden. Burdick v. Takushi requires narrow fit to a compelling interest for severe burdens and ordinarily permits reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens justified by important regulatory interests. The inquiry considers the whole system, not a nominal alternative alone.

Norman v. Reed permits distinct-electorate support requirements while forbidding party-name barriers and loss of independently qualified components because another electorate failed. Statewide trustee support does not itself show congressional-district support. The different denominators and stages make the petition comparison more than a simple tenfold disparity. Jenness v. Fortson permits meaningful support requirements in an actual workable system; Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party likewise examines the available nomination and advocacy alternatives. Here the capped general-ballot petition route supplies an available alternative without an automatic constitutional right to a public party primary.

The substantial numerical difference and combined conditions deserve examination. The 1994 successes are relevant but not conclusive; disputed 1996 acceptances cannot prove full compliance, and the exceptional 1992 route cannot establish ordinary-year accessibility. No supported genuine dispute of the asserted impossible cumulative burden requires trial. Nor does the record establish Norman's disqualification of an independently qualified component through another electorate's failure. The strongest contrary argument requires a demonstrated heavier cumulative burden or automatic transfer of statewide status that this record does not support. Literal impossibility is not a universal prerequisite to a constitutional claim. Incumbent preservation is no independent justification, and a properly supported future burden challenge remains available.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Anderson applies the actual-burden, precise-interest and necessity inquiry.
- Burdick preserves complete-system review and the severe-burden boundary; its write-in presumption is not generalized.
- Norman preserves electorate-specific support and independently qualified components; it creates no automatic statewide-to-district status.
- Jenness permits a meaningful demonstration of support, not universal approval of five-percent petitions.
- Timmons applies proportionate regulation within actual alternatives, not an incumbent-protection interest or categorical approval of every nomination rule.

### 3. Genuine nomination procedures do not add a personal congressional qualification

**Holding and operative rule:** These genuine support and nomination requirements regulate access procedure rather than adding a personal qualification for service in Congress, so the distinct Qualifications Clause challenge fails. Article I's exclusive qualifications remain binding; a substantive personal exclusion cannot escape that rule merely by being labeled ballot administration.

**Authority:** All eight Associates directly join Part III.B's genuine-procedure conclusion. Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer additionally join Part III.A's application of the exclusive-qualifications premise, providing five direct votes for that explanation. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas do not join III.A. Stone-Zsela supports the claim's rejection without adding a ground; his vote is not counted for either rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton distinguishes substantive personal eligibility from genuine regulation of the time, place and manner of elections. Its rule prevents a State from withholding ordinary ballot access from an otherwise qualified congressional candidate because of a prohibited additional personal qualification. The requirements here concern demonstrated electoral support and nomination, not a candidate's personal eligibility to serve. That procedural classification answers this challenge, while the association analysis separately examines whether the actual burden is constitutionally justified. Calling a restriction procedural does not immunize severe access barriers or disguised personal exclusions. The five Justices joining III.A apply Thornton's controlling exclusivity premise; the three joining III.B alone need not abandon their disagreement with that premise to reject a challenge to genuine procedures.

**Precedent treatment:** Thornton is applied within its substantive-versus-procedural boundary; its existing six-Justice authority is not reconstructed from today's distinct votes.

### 4. The judgment supplies no replacement election system

**Holding and operative rule:** The access judgment is affirmed and the distinct Qualifications Clause claim rejected without reopening the primary, declaring automatic district-established status, substituting a signature number or granting a new statewide qualification. Independently qualified components retain Norman's protection, and supported challenges to different actual cumulative burdens remain open.

**Authority:** Souter's Part IV has all eight Associates on the affirmance and its grounds; Stone-Zsela agrees only with rejection of the distinct Qualifications Clause claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The supported record permits disposition of the actual claims without inventing a genuine factual dispute or designing an alternative nomination scheme. The Court's conclusion does not validate every complete-slate condition, deadline combination or future application. Nor does it decide the separate equal-protection claim that was not pleaded. A later claim must establish its actual injury, applicable interests and remedy rather than treating this bounded affirmance or isolated successful qualification as conclusive.

**Precedent treatment:** Norman retains its independently qualified-component rule and bounded election remedy; neither is displaced by this affirmance.

## Event

Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue, No. 97-858; Washington Nos. 62941-2, 62939-1 and 62940-4. Plenary decision on submission, September 28, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 13. Two cabaret corporations and three performers challenge Bellevue's adult-cabaret ordinances. The questions concern protected expressive conduct, the actual distance, stage, lighting and hours restrictions, substantial overbreadth, the separate outdoor-image prohibition, independent state licensing protection and two distinct attorney-fee claims.

Bellevue City Code §5.08.070(A)(6), as authoritatively construed, requires four feet between a nonstage performer and that performer's customer, measured between bodies rather than outstretched arms; it does not require separation from every person in the room. Section (A)(1) permits exposure of the female breast below the top of the areola, or the pubic region, anus, buttocks, vulva or genitals only on a stage at least eighteen inches above the immediate floor and at least eight feet from the nearest public patron. Section (D)(2) requires sufficient, equally distributed lighting throughout public areas so objects remain plainly visible, with thirty lux measured horizontally thirty inches above the floor on ten-foot centers, not at every possible point. Section (E) closes the cabaret to public operation from 2 a.m. to 10 a.m., leaving sixteen daily hours.

The adult-entertainment definition, §5.08.010(B), covers performances with specified anatomical exposure, predominant emphasis on sexual activities, or intended sexual stimulation conducted regularly or as a substantial part of the premises' activity. The regular-or-substantial condition belongs to the third branch, not all three. Section .070(F) preserves nonobscene plays, operas, musicals and other dramatic works; nonobscene classes, seminars and lectures held for serious scientific or educational purposes; and nonobscene exhibitions, performances, expressions and dances. Its stated exception concerns the sexual conduct described in RCW 7.48A.010(2)(b)(ii)–(iii): masturbation, oral-genital acts, bestiality, excretory functions, lewd genital exhibition, and violent or destructive sexual acts including mutilation, dismemberment, rape or torture. The Washington court construes the interaction with (A)(5) to prohibit only obscene conduct, not those subjects irrespective of obscenity. The incorporated obscenity criteria require prurient appeal under contemporary community standards, the specified patently offensive depiction or description, and absence of serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value when the work and its context are considered as a whole.

Section .070(B)(2) separately prohibits a performance, photograph, drawing, sketch or other pictorial representation displaying breasts below the top of the areola, pubic hair, buttocks, genitals or anus from being visible outside the cabaret. Its state construction limits it to adult-cabaret performances and their images; it does not add an obscenity or material-obscene-as-to-minors condition. The (A)(5) construction is not transferred to (B)(2).

Undercover evidence established sexual contact and difficulty detecting it in close, poorly lit settings. The accepted findings retain erotic communication at the actual distances. The economic forecasts partly assumed an incorrect eight-to-nine-foot body separation; the reconsideration declaration reported greater losses before than after compliance. No finding establishes inevitable closure or a guaranteed profit margin. The later evidence and amendment rulings, information-disclosure claim under Washington privacy law, and other state-only questions remain distinct.

The state court invalidated the fourteen-day processing gap for managers without temporary licenses under Washington Constitution article I, §5. A licensed manager must be present; temporary dancer licenses do not cure the manager gap. ## Holdings

### 1. Federal review leaves independent Washington protection intact

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court may review the adverse, determined federal questions under §1257 while leaving the independently sufficient state constitutional manager-license invalidation intact. The collateral fee accounting does not reopen the underlying federal merits or authorize this Court to correct Washington's own constitutional rule.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Michigan v. Long preserves an adequate independent state ground. The manager-license ruling expressly rests on Washington's greater protection against prior restraints and supports relief without a federal determination. The Court neither restores that fourteen-day licensing gap nor decides that federal law independently invalidates or permits every such interval. FW/PBS, Inc. v. City of Dallas retains its separate federal procedural requirements. Other adverse federal rulings remain reviewable; a state-law discussion does not insulate them. The state court has resolved those merits, leaving collateral fee accounting rather than the unresolved underlying merits presented in Jefferson v. City of Tarrant. Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith concerned coextensive state reasoning and supplies no power to revise this independent state protection. State privacy, postjudgment evidence and amendment rulings are not independently reviewed as federal claims.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Long applies the adequate-independent-ground boundary.
- FW/PBS retains its federal licensing rules without deciding the independently protected manager interval here.
- Jefferson is distinguished because underlying merits, rather than merely collateral fees, remained unresolved there.
- Florida v. Smith preserves federal review where state reasoning is coextensive; that premise is absent from the manager-license ruling.

### 2. The supported operational rules regulate conduct while preserving expression

**Holding and operative rule:** These actual spacing, stage, lighting and hours requirements survive federal review because they serve substantial contact-prevention and detection interests unrelated to suppressing the message, are sufficiently fitted to those interests and preserve meaningful expressive performance. Their exact customer, measurement, dimensional and time limits control; neither commercial operation nor erotic content removes the expression from First Amendment protection.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** United States v. O'Brien requires governmental authority, an important or substantial interest, a purpose unrelated to expressive suppression, and an incidental burden no greater than essential to that interest. Ward v. Rock Against Racism requires genuine fit and sufficient remaining channels without a universal least-restrictive-means test. Barnes v. Glen Theatre recognizes the expressive interest through distinct opinions; no single Barnes rationale authorizes dispensing with scrutiny of every cabaret-specific restriction. City of Renton v. Playtime Theatres permits attention to actual secondary effects, not hostility to the communication itself.

The supported contact incidents and visibility difficulties justify the actual body-to-body four-foot rule and elevated stage eight feet from patrons. A six-foot stage separation allowed a tall patron to reach a dancer. The specified lighting measurements constrain enforcement discretion while aiding observation. The hours rule addresses the late-night movement of patrons from closing bars and retains sixteen daily hours of performance. These provisions regulate the physical setting; the Court rejects the lower court's rational-basis-only account of stage dancing.

The record does not establish that these bounded rules eliminate practical expression. A business has no guaranteed profit margin, but proof that regulation destroys meaningful channels would matter; inevitable closure is not the exclusive measure of speech injury. The rejected forecast cannot be assessed as though the law required eight or nine feet between table-dance bodies. Madsen and Schenck require attention to each burden but address injunctions; their heightened remedial standard is not silently imposed on all legislation. No categorical immunity, morality-only justification or national distance safe harbor follows.

**Precedent treatment:**

- O'Brien supplies the operative expressive-conduct requirements.
- Ward supplies fitted manner regulation without universal least restrictive means.
- Barnes preserves expression and its actual divided rationales; it is not converted into categorical cabaret immunity or no-scrutiny treatment.
- Renton applies noncommunicative secondary-effects analysis without immunizing censorship.
- Madsen v. Women's Health Center preserves its provision-specific speech analysis and distinct injunction standard.
- Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York preserves context-specific fit without making all distances unconstitutional or valid.

### 3. The narrowed definition and conduct provisions survive substantial-overbreadth review

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented overbreadth challenge fails as to the adult-entertainment definition and dance-distance provisions when their authoritative state constructions and nonobscene-work exceptions are applied. Section .070(A)(5) is sustained only as confined to obscene conduct through the state's construction of (F); that construction supplies no missing exception in (B)(2).

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on this component.

**Controlling explanation:** Broadrick v. Oklahoma requires real and substantial overbreadth compared with a conduct regulation's legitimate sweep; New York v. Ferber preserves the protected-expression inquiry. The definition's hypothetical occasional or revue applications do not establish substantial overbreadth on the presented record of the live activity studied. The four-foot rule applies to the performer's customer rather than every member of the public. Section (F) protects the three stated nonobscene categories, and the Washington court reads its sexual-conduct exclusion with the statutory obscenity criteria rather than as an exception for all nonobscene depictions of the listed acts. That controlling state construction defeats the asserted nonobscene reach of (A)(5). The Court does not strike the defined live-entertainment category merely because every conceivable venue has not been separately studied. It equally does not endorse a categorical rule that every adult-entertainment definition is valid. The independent outdoor prohibition has a different actual scope and receives its own analysis.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Broadrick applies substantial overbreadth to these conduct provisions at their construed scope.
- Ferber preserves examination of protected expression; it supplies no general adult-cabaret exemption.

### 4. Visibility alone cannot justify the protected-image prohibition

**Holding and operative rule:** Section .070(B)(2) is invalid insofar as it prohibits protected nonobscene adult-cabaret performances or their images from being visible outside solely because the listed anatomy appears. Restricting the subject to cabaret entertainment does not convert its communicative effect into a secondary effect or supply missing obscenity, minor-obscenity, captive-home intrusion or actual-conduct conditions.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part IV, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Controlling explanation:** Erznoznik v. City of Jacksonville protects public displays from broad suppression merely because unwilling viewers can see them. Madsen preserves that principle even in its distinct injunction setting: distress at a visible image is not itself a sufficient ground for banning it. The City invokes children and other passersby, but (B)(2) contains no material-obscene-as-to-minors definition and no captive-home predicate. A picture of protected performance is representation, not the sexual contact addressed by the operational rules. Limiting the ban to cabaret performances does not cure that defect. The state construction of (A)(5) cannot be silently inserted into a different provision. Independent lawful public-nudity, obscenity, trespass and neutral-sign restrictions retain their own operation. State courts must determine severability and conforming relief; the Court writes no replacement display code and authorizes no violation of independently valid rules.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Erznoznik applies protection against broadly suppressing images visible to unwilling passersby.
- Madsen preserves its observable-images principle without exporting the entire injunction test.
- Renton is distinguished: the prohibited visibility is the communication itself, not an independently demonstrated secondary harm.

### 5. Plaintiffs' fee eligibility must be considered under the actual federal result

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical denial of plaintiffs' §1988 fees must be vacated for consideration of their actual federal success and the distinct eligibility and reasonable-amount inquiries. State-law success alone does not automatically establish entitlement, but it also does not categorically defeat fees where an available substantial, fee-eligible federal claim supports an award.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part V.A; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins this rule whatever federal success the Court's judgment supplies.

**Controlling explanation:** Farrar v. Hobby distinguishes enforceable success from the reasonable amount of fees; it does not guarantee a substantial award. National Private Truck Council, Inc. v. Oklahoma Tax Commission preserves Maher v. Gagne's qualified rule that an available substantial, fee-eligible federal claim may support fees without an express federal merits decision. The Washington court's categorical statement cannot replace that inquiry, and the present reversal of the protected-image prohibition supplies actual federal success that must be considered. Hensley v. Eckerhart requires attention to actual results and unrelated unsuccessful work. No fee total is fixed, no nominal damages are awarded, and not every unsuccessful challenge becomes compensable. The manager-license remedy remains state law; eligibility is not created solely because expressive activity was involved.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Farrar preserves eligibility separately from reasonable amount, including a permissible reduction to zero on the appropriate assessment.
- National Private Truck Council preserves the need for an available fee-eligible federal claim and rejects a categorical state-success exclusion.
- Maher preserves possible fees through an available substantial, fee-eligible federal claim without an express federal merits decision; state-law success alone neither establishes entitlement nor bars the award.
- Hensley governs actual success and unrelated unsuccessful work in determining a reasonable amount.

### 6. State equity cannot evade federal limits on defense-fee shifting

**Holding and operative rule:** A municipality cannot recover the expense of defending a substantial, nonfrivolous §1983 request merely because interim restraint was dissolved, by describing those defense fees as state equitable damages. The City's award is vacated to that extent for separation of federal defense expenses from any independently permissible state-claim expenses or actual procedural misconduct.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part V.B; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Christiansburg Garment Co. v. EEOC and Hughes v. Rowe protect nonfrivolous civil-rights claims from automatic prevailing-defendant defense fees. A rule designed to deter plaintiffs from seeking preliminary protection cannot circumvent that limit by changing the award's label. The mere decision to request interim relief is not misconduct. The Court does not erase every state equitable basis, decide an unpresented bond-enforcement claim, or treat the failure to post the disputed bond as itself resolving the federal issue. The state court must identify the legal source and attributable work. Its refusal to award the City's later appellate defense fees remains distinct from the remanded dissolution accounting. No fixed award, federal fee entitlement for the City or automatic recovery of every defense expense follows.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Christiansburg applies its limitation on prevailing-defendant fees without making loss of interim relief sufficient.
- Hughes carries that protection into §1983 litigation; state-law labels do not evade it.

## Holdings

### 1. Life custody preserves conviction claims, but the death-only requests are moot

**Holding and operative rule:** The continuing conviction and imprisonment keep the existing conviction-level claims live, while commutation removes the requested execution and death-selection relief in claims 1, 2, the death-only branch of 4 and claim 16 without a demonstrated surviving consequence. Those death-only requests must be dismissed; they are not converted into a new freestanding claim to release from life imprisonment.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I is joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** Carafas v. LaVallee preserves a conviction controversy, while Lane v. Williams distinguishes a challenge confined to ended punishment. Spencer v. Kemna keeps custody at filing and a continuing controversy separate. Lucas's conviction-based imprisonment continues, but no concrete consequence of the removed death selection is established. The Court therefore reaches the existing conviction branch of claim 4 and the other live claims without deciding the execution-only merits. Herrera v. Collins supplies no categorical rejection or uniform extraordinary standard for every freestanding innocence claim. Neither the commutation nor the report establishes an official judicial admission of innocence; their evidentiary significance is distinct. Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas retain their actual mitigation rules, but no live death-selection question requires applying them here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Carafas preserves the continuing conviction controversy.
- Lane distinguishes claims directed only to ended punishment.
- Spencer preserves claim-specific continuing injury without extinguishing present conviction custody.
- Herrera retains its record-specific holding; no broader innocence rule is adopted.
- Graham remains unchanged; its mitigation holding is not adjudicated on this moot claim.
- Johnson remains unchanged; the Court does not endorse the lower court's mitigation reasoning.

### 2. The death-only federal merits rulings receive limited equitable vacatur

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §2106, the corresponding death-only federal merits adjudications are vacated because executive commutation frustrated their review while review was being pursued, without established voluntary settlement or abandonment. The equitable disposition does not vacate the conviction, revive death eligibility or erase the live claim rulings.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II is joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela disagrees with vacatur.

**Controlling explanation:** U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership makes vacatur equitable rather than automatic. Its ordinary refusal after voluntary settlement does not determine this executive commutation, which removed the object of the death-only requests during pursued review. Clearing the now-unreviewable federal merits rulings is the just limited disposition on these circumstances. No voluntary withdrawal is established here. The Court does not decide those rulings' substantive correctness or give clemency the effect of a constitutional adjudication. This is direct disposition of the judgment before the Court, not a collateral reopening power.

**Precedent treatment:** Bancorp applies an actual equitable assessment under §2106; neither mootness nor executive clemency creates an automatic vacatur rule.

### 3. Former factual presumptions do not displace independent constitutional review

**Holding and operative rule:** The identified amended §2254(d) and replacement §2254(e)(1) rules do not govern this pending first federal application outside established chapter 154 qualification: former §2254(d) presumes qualifying state factual determinations correct, placing on the applicant the burden to establish their error by convincing evidence unless a statutory exception is established or otherwise appears. Constitutional sufficiency, waiver, fairness and Brady conclusions remain independently reviewed, and claims 3–7 may be denied on their sufficient independent grounds with gateway availability assumed rather than decided.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by all nine; the claim 15 application also has the five votes in Parts VII–VIII.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh v. Murphy and Carriger v. Stewart supply the applicable chapter 153 construction without deciding every AEDPA amendment. Qualifying historical or credibility findings follow a merits hearing before a competent state court involving the applicant and the State or its agent, evidenced by adequate written indicia. Paragraph (8) removes that presumption when the pertinent produced state record as a whole does not fairly support the finding. The applicant's otherwise-applicable burden is convincing evidence of factual error under former §2254(d), not the replacement §2254(e)(1) formulation. A different plausible inference does not itself defeat protection; neither may a finding conclusively decide later evidence it never assessed. Wright v. West preserves independent legal assessment rather than general deference to a reasonable state constitutional conclusion.

Trest v. Cain identifies default as nonjurisdictional, and Lambrix v. Singletary rejects inflexible sequencing of distinct nonjurisdictional defenses. The Court assumes the asserted gateway for claims 3–7 solely to deny them independently. It neither excuses default generally nor bypasses a jurisdictional defect. Coleman v. Thompson retains its adequate-independent-ground and exception requirements if default becomes decisive. Schlup v. Delo and Carriger govern any then-necessary whole-record probability inquiry; the verdict, corroborative character or trial inadmissibility cannot categorically dispose of reliable evidence. No advisory gateway finding is necessary now. The claim 15 remand has no established default bar in this record.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Lindh applies the covered chapter 153 temporal rule while preserving separately directed provisions.
- Carriger applies its pending-capital and distinct state-finding rules; it orders no automatic hearing here.
- Wright preserves independent constitutional assessment and Jackson sufficiency.
- Trest preserves nonjurisdictional default without requiring an invented defense.
- Lambrix preserves nonrigid sequencing without deciding this case's constitutional merits by itself.
- Coleman retains actual default predicates and separate cause/prejudice and miscarriage-of-justice routes.
- Schlup retains the demanding whole-record gateway; no passage or failure is decided.
- Bousley v. United States preserves factual innocence as distinct from legal insufficiency; its plea-bargain conditions are not imported.

### 4. Claims 3 and 4 do not establish constitutional insufficiency

**Holding and operative rule:** Texas's independent-corroboration requirement does not by itself supply a federal constitutional rule, and the trial evidence, including the admitted confession, permits a rational finding of the charged murder during aggravated sexual assault or attempt. Failure to supply the victim's personal name does not negate the proved death and offense elements or establish the existing conviction-level claim 4 theory.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by all nine; gateway availability for these claims is assumed.

**Controlling explanation:** Jackson v. Virginia requires evidence from which a rational jury could find every element beyond reasonable doubt; Wright preserves that test. The reported admitted account describes the charged murder in the course of actual or attempted aggravated sexual assault. Disbelief of the alibi alone could not supply a missing element, but that is not the complete trial record. A state corroboration objection does not automatically remove an otherwise constitutionally admissible confession from federal sufficiency review. Its constitutional objections receive their separate analysis. Likewise, the victim's unknown name is not an additional federal element where death and the charged crime are proved. The later augmented innocence presentation is not substituted for Jackson's trial record. The Court declares neither every confession sufficient nor every corroboration rule irrelevant to other properly presented claims.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Jackson applies element-by-element rational-jury review of the actual trial evidence.
- Wright retains that constitutional requirement without a general reasonable-state-decision deference rule.

### 5. The two appellate-counsel claims fail on their distinct grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** Claim 5 supplies no reasonable probability of a different lawful appellate result from an identity objection that the reported state law did not require; claim 6 does not establish professionally unreasonable issue selection under the contemporaneous, unsettled corroboration law. These are separate Strickland denials, not a general direction to measure every prejudice claim by present law or excuse every omitted unsettled argument.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by all nine; gateway availability for claims 5 and 6 is assumed.

**Controlling explanation:** Strickland v. Washington requires deficient performance and reasonable-probability prejudice, with professional performance assessed from counsel's perspective at the time. The supplied state-law account required no positive personal identification then or now, so the proposed claim 5 objection would not supply a different lawful result. Claim 6 concerns the omission before Gribble v. State clarified the corroboration issue. The demonstrated omission and contemporary law do not establish unreasonable performance; an argument need not already have prevailed to be professionally available, and a later clarification is not automatically unforeseeable. No additional omitted argument is supplied. Lockhart v. Fretwell preserves ordinary prejudice outside its displaced-substantive-rule setting, and the lower court's general present-law shorthand is not adopted.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Strickland supplies both required elements and contemporaneous performance assessment.
- Fretwell retains its bounded lost-benefit holding without replacing reasonable-probability prejudice generally.
- Gribble supplies the reported later state-law clarification, not an automatic federal excuse for all unsettled issues.

### 6. Claim 7 fails before any harmlessness inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** In the reported paragraph, the prosecutor's reference to Lucas's knowledge of the matchbook's owner does not ask the jury to infer guilt from failure to testify or offer that silence as evidence. Claim 7 is therefore denied on the antecedent absence of a constitutional violation, without a new harmlessness ruling.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part IV, joined by all nine; gateway availability is assumed.

**Controlling explanation:** Griffin v. California forbids use of the accused's trial silence as inculpatory proof. The prosecutor acknowledged inconclusive handwriting evidence and uncertainty whether the matches belonged to Lucas or the victim, then attributed knowledge to Lucas. The final reference can be troubling in isolation, but the reported paragraph explains why ownership does not determine guilt rather than asking the jury to use silence against him. No responsive defense argument, curative instruction or larger unproduced passage is supplied. The Court does not adopt the lower court's alternative substantial-and-injurious-effect analysis. Brecht v. Abrahamson and O'Neal v. McAninch retain their category-specific Chapman holdings; this no-error conclusion neither extends them universally nor selects a new test for Griffin error.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Griffin applies to the actual contextual use of silence, not every reference to personal knowledge.
- Brecht retains its protected-silence category; its historical alternative is not adopted here.
- O'Neal retains its personal-intent and conditional-relief holdings without a universal extension.

### 7. Claim 8's state impeachment error did not deny fundamental fairness

**Holding and operative rule:** Admission of Caulder's stale but not constitutionally invalid convictions, although erroneous under state evidence law, did not make the reported trial fundamentally unfair. The federal claim is denied independently of the state court's harmlessness formulation.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part IV, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** A state evidentiary error alone is not a federal due-process violation. Caulder was important to the employment alibi, but the stale convictions were not the only impeachment of its foundation. Coworker Emery described false attendance entries and kickbacks, and Lucas himself described that system. The Court considers the improper evidence with that independent record rather than treating the witness as unimportant or merely counting other inculpatory proof. Loper v. Beto concerned constitutionally invalid convictions; no such defect is alleged here. The reported use does not establish fundamental unfairness, and the state's harmlessness finding is not substituted for that constitutional inquiry or treated as resolving every conceivable cumulative claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Loper is distinguished at its constitutionally invalid-conviction premise; stale convictions do not automatically establish the same federal violation.

### 8. Claim 9 is denied on the reported personal constitutional waiver

**Holding and operative rule:** The February 1984 interview claim fails on the reported voluntary Fifth and Sixth Amendment waiver and absence of an established contrary invocation or invalidating waiver fact, assessed independently of the state order's construction. Counsel's notice agreement, willingness to speak alone or labeling the work-record subject as different cannot by itself substitute for Lucas's knowing, intelligent and voluntary constitutional choice.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part V, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Patterson v. Illinois permits a sufficiently informed postindictment waiver. Maine v. Moulton prevents an other-investigation label from excusing deliberate elicitation concerning pending charges, and Michigan v. Jackson requires its actual invocation predicate before its protection is applied. Discussion of the alibi's work records cannot be categorically placed beyond the indicted case simply by naming the subject. The relevant constitutional inquiry nevertheless was expressly decided below, with historical findings distinguished from legal conclusions. On this presented claim, the reported voluntary personal waiver is not overcome by an identified warning, invocation or coercion defect. Neither representation nor the protective order alone establishes such a defect; counsel's agreement alone would not surrender Lucas's rights.

The strongest contrary position regards the published explanation's willingness language and subject separation as inadequate. The Court does not read its explicit constitutional-waiver conclusion as merely either shortcut, and source nonreproduction or appellate brevity does not establish that necessary conditions were absent. No unreported warning words, initiation fact or finding of attorney consent to charged-offense questioning is supplied. Withrow v. Williams requires independent constitutional review; a state-order breach alone would not justify habeas relief. This disposition does not authorize all represented interviews or equate cooperation with waiver.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Patterson applies valid postindictment waiver without an automatic representation bar.
- Moulton preserves protection against deliberate elicitation about pending charges despite other-investigation labels.
- Michigan v. Jackson preserves its actual invocation requirement; none is invented from representation.
- Withrow preserves independent review and distinct claim requirements, rather than a bar based on earlier state opportunity.

### 9. Claim 10 requests a rule not dictated at finality

**Holding and operative rule:** At the reported 1990 finality, rules governing continuing courtroom restraints did not dictate categorical exclusion of a brief recorded custodial appearance showing handcuffs. Teague therefore bars that requested retroactive rule, which falls within neither then-existing exception.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part VI, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** Teague v. Lane requires the actual finality-era dictated-rule comparison, not a label of novelty or the bare existence of disagreement. A brief recorded image accompanying a confession differs from keeping the accused visibly restrained throughout trial. The requested categorical recording rule neither places conduct or a class of persons beyond the State's substantive punishment authority nor supplies a watershed procedure essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Lambrix preserves those requirements and rejects treating every application of an older principle as new. The Court does not approve every restraint image or reject a demonstrated whole-trial unfairness claim on a materially different record.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Teague applies the dictated-rule test and both contemporary exceptions to this precise requested rule.
- Lambrix preserves genuine finality-era comparison without an automatic novelty rule for extensions.

### 10. Claims 11–13 fail under their distinct attachment and invocation facts

**Holding and operative rule:** The June 22 and July 31 statements preceded formal adversarial proceedings in this offense, and the July 28 statement preceded such proceedings in the Abilene offense; the asserted Sixth Amendment protection had not attached for the respective statements. The supported findings also establish neither a clear nor a reasonable possible present request for interrogation counsel before them, so the distinct Fifth Amendment claims fail under the governing Davis rule.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part VI, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** McNeil v. Wisconsin makes Sixth Amendment attachment offense-specific; representation elsewhere does not attach it here. Arizona v. Roberson separately preserves a properly invoked custodial counsel right across offenses. Davis v. United States requires neutral clarification after a valid waiver when context conveys a reasonable possible present request: substantive questioning must pause and cannot resume while the request remains unresolved, and clarification cannot discourage counsel through pressure, promises or repeated persuasion. An unexpressed wish or unrelated lawyer reference does not trigger it. A clear request requires cessation under Edwards v. Arizona, and Smith v. Illinois prevents later answers from erasing it; further questioning then requires counsel or the suspect's own initiation and a valid waiver.

The reported findings cover more than absence of unequivocal words: before these statements Lucas neither verbally requested counsel nor otherwise indicated a present wish for counsel. The Court relies on that supported predicate, not later cooperation to undo an earlier request, and rejects the lower court's clear-request-only quotation as the governing rule. No initial-warning or initial-waiver claim absent from this presentation is adjudicated. Confession reliability, counsel protections and police-coerced involuntariness remain separate; disputed truthfulness and incentives alone do not establish coercion.

**Precedent treatment:**

- McNeil applies offense-specific Sixth Amendment attachment.
- Roberson preserves the distinct cross-offense effect of invoked interrogation counsel.
- Davis applies the possible-present-request trigger and mandatory clarification limits.
- Edwards preserves cessation after clear invocation and the conditions for renewed questioning.
- Smith forbids retrospective erosion of invocation by later answers.
- Miranda v. Arizona preserves its independent interrogation protections; no unpresented initial-warning issue is decided.
- Colorado v. Connelly preserves the coercion requirement without adjudicating a new coercion claim.

### 11. Claim 14's contextual completion did not deny fundamental fairness

**Holding and operative rule:** The reported responsive portions of the July 31 recording, containing implicit other-offense references after selected defense portions created a misleading impression, did not establish fundamental unfairness when considered with the instruction against using other crimes to decide guilt. Opening the door supplies no unlimited permission to introduce propensity evidence.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part VI, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** The constitutional question concerns the use and effect of the evidence, not a freestanding right to present a misleading extract. The reported responsive purpose, implicit character of the references and limiting instruction support denial of this due-process claim even assuming a state evidentiary error. No unproduced wording is supplied and no guilt inference from character is adopted. This is an independent absence-of-fundamental-unfairness conclusion, not a general harmlessness rule or an automatic conclusion that any evidence becomes admissible once a party selects part of a statement.

**Precedent treatment:** No earlier Supreme Court rule is displaced; the federal fairness inquiry remains distinct from state-law admissibility and a universal harmlessness test.

### 12. Claim 15 requires the distinct Brady inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** A supported finding that investigators had no credible alternative suspect does not, by itself, resolve whether they possessed and suppressed favorable information whose collective lawful defense uses were material. The denial of this existing claim is vacated for the actual favorable-information, prosecution-team attribution, suppression and collective-materiality inquiries, without finding any element satisfied or discarding a qualifying historical finding.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part VII, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Brady v. Maryland requires favorable material information actually suppressed. Kyles v. Whitley evaluates its collective lawful impeachment, preparation and investigative uses in the whole record: a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence, not proof that acquittal is more likely or that the remaining evidence is insufficient. The prosecutor must learn of favorable information held by police acting on the government's behalf in the case; personal ignorance does not remove it from review. Carriger preserves those elements separately from state factual presumptions.

The historical no-credible-suspect finding may retain former §2254(d) protection. Its constitutional consequence does not. An unsuccessful lead can have lawful defense uses without proving another person's guilt. The strongest affirmance reading treats the hearing finding as rejection of this precise factual premise. The remand permits that outcome if the actual claim and record support it, but requires the distinct elements rather than a credible-suspect proxy. It directs examination of the existing claim, not a search for hypothetical wrongdoing.

No undisclosed item, team possession or materiality is now established. Known information cannot be relabeled suppressed; attribution does not extend automatically to every government repository, and Carriger's proposed corrections-file acquisition duty is not adopted. If a complete material Brady violation is established, Kyles forbids a second duplicative harmlessness hurdle. No such complete violation is found today.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brady preserves actual favorable suppression and materiality as separate requirements.
- Kyles requires collective lawful-use assessment and actual case-team responsibility; no second duplicative prejudice test follows an established violation.

### 13. Claim 17 remains within Powell's limited exclusion bar

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented request to exclude confessions as fruits of an arrest allegedly lacking probable cause is barred under Powell because the supplied proceedings afforded a full and fair state opportunity and identify no concrete denial of that opportunity. The Court neither decides the arrest lawful nor extends this Fourth Amendment exclusion restriction to independent Fifth, Sixth Amendment or Brady claims.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part VI, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** Stone v. Powell limits this exclusion-based habeas remedy after a full and fair opportunity to litigate the Fourth Amendment theory. The present claim identifies no defect in that opportunity; the Court does not infer adequate process merely from unavailable transcripts. Withrow preserves separate review of Miranda and properly pleaded constitutional confession claims, which receive their own treatment here. A demonstrated opportunity defect would require a different Powell inquiry. No new coercion claim is created, and no approval of the arrest's merits follows.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Powell applies only its full-and-fair-opportunity exclusion rule.
- Withrow preserves independent confession protections and fair litigation of properly presented claims.

### 14. The Brady remand orders correct adjudication, not another hearing or a writ

**Holding and operative rule:** Claim 15 returns for application of its actual constitutional elements and former factual-presumption rule on the existing claim; no new hearing, discovery, production order, conditional writ, retrial or release is ordered. Any needed factual development must rest on a genuinely material inadequacy and the applicable hearing and procedural requirements, with §2254(e)(2)'s separate timing and trigger questions reserved unless they become necessary.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part VIII, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes for the remand and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes retains Townsend v. Sain's deliberate-bypass qualification for inadequately developed material facts on a properly presented claim; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a necessary hearing under that rule. But an existing hearing is neither conclusive proof of adequacy nor an invitation to repeat adequately developed issues. The lower court must identify the actual material gap, existing state and federal development and applicable independent barriers before ordering more proceedings.

Where §2254(e)(2) applies and the applicant failed to develop the factual basis, its hearing prohibition has cumulative conditions: the claim must rely on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court or a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts must establish clearly and convincingly that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt. This Court decides neither that provision's temporal application here nor whether its failed-development trigger is met. Lindh and Carriger's identified §2254(d)/(e)(1) rulings do not decide those separate questions. Correcting the Brady analysis establishes no constitutional violation or remedy now; any complete available violation must receive its own lawful prejudice and relief analysis.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Keeney preserves its properly presented-claim and material-inadequacy hearing rule without compelling repetition.
- Townsend retains the relevant deliberate-bypass qualification and distinct hearing grounds.
- Lindh preserves separately directed statutory provisions rather than a blanket pre-AEDPA exemption.

## Event

## Holdings
##### 1. Completion of the requested plans ends this prospective planning request

**Holding and operative rule:** A request to stop timber sales only until identified river plans exist is moot once those plans have been completed, when no surviving remedy or concrete recurrence is established for that request. On the equities here, the stale appellate merits treatment must be vacated and the request dismissed; the January 14, 1997 district interpretation remains vacated on mootness grounds.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by all nine Justices, supplies direct controlling authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Government reports that the plans were completed and incorporated into adjacent forest plans in October 1996. An order to await that completed work can no longer afford the particular relief requested. The September 30, 1995 deadline was missed, but that historical failure supplies no continuing controversy over whether to stop sales until the plans exist. No surviving damages demand or independent remedy is established for this request.

U.S. Bancorp permits an equitable disposition under §2106 after merits review becomes unavailable; it does not make vacatur automatic. Petitioners neither settled nor abandoned review. The prevailing agency's reported performance exhausted the request before the appellate merits adjudication. Arizonans for Official English permits correction of that stale adjudication without a new merits ruling. These circumstances justify vacatur and dismissal. They also require leaving the January 1997 district interpretation vacated, without adopting the proposition that any interlocutory appeal divests a district court of all related merits authority.

The Court does not excuse the missed deadline, approve the completed plans, decide whether plan preparation was a legal precondition to these sales, or resolve the territorial extent of planning duties. Distinct coordination, adjacency-protection and site-effects obligations are neither extinguished nor newly adjudicated.

**Precedent treatment:** U.S. Bancorp is applied as an equitable, rather than automatic, judgment-clearing rule; Arizonans for Official English is applied to correction of merits treatment after the particular controversy ended.

##### 2. The final adjudication displaces the former preliminary MBTA interval

**Holding and operative rule:** When final adjudication has ended the period for which preliminary relief was requested, an appeal seeking protection only during that former interval is moot even though an underlying claim may remain reviewable. The obsolete appellate affirmance of preliminary MBTA relief is vacated and that preliminary appeal dismissed; no old preliminary motion is revived.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** University of Texas v. Camenisch distinguishes temporary preservation of the parties' positions from final adjudication of their rights. The final district-court judgment and April 1, 1998 affirmance ended the pending-trial interval addressed by the July 29, 1996 preliminary order. The Court therefore clears the superseded appellate treatment of that interval rather than deciding whether an injunction should once have issued.

This disposition does not rest on an unsupported finding that every timber operation has finished. Nor does it vacate the later final judgment on other statutory or administrative-record grounds. The actual earlier dismissal of the MBTA claim is a separate component and receives its own review. Any request for future relief on that returned claim must establish a current legal and equitable basis; success in obtaining this limited vacatur supplies none by itself.

**Precedent treatment:** Camenisch is applied to the distinct duration and function of preliminary relief. U.S. Bancorp supplies authority for the confined equitable disposition without making vacatur of the independent final adjudications automatic.

##### 3. The entered dismissal was lawfully reviewable with the injunction denial

**Holding and operative rule:** An injunction appeal permits review of an actually entered and separately appealed dismissal of the same equitable claim when the identical dispositive legal obstacle makes examination of that dismissal necessary to meaningful review and resolving the obstacle requires no additional unresolved factual adjudication. Those conditions are met here; §1254(1) permits review of the MBTA component actually affirmed in the court-of-appeals case without a newly invented appeal or petition.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by all nine Justices. Breyer joins this actual Deckert-based formulation; his earlier judgment-only position in Clinton is not treated as a join to Clinton's rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The injunction was refused because the asserted MBTA claim was legally unavailable. One week later the District Court entered a categorical dismissal on that same obstacle, and plaintiffs appealed both orders. Accepting the obstacle would sustain both; rejecting it without correcting the entered dismissal would leave the same claim legally foreclosed. This identity of the legal issue and the equitable claim supplies the necessary connection.

Deckert permits examination of a legal objection defeating the very equitable claim while excluding unrelated interlocutory matters. Swint rejects review of a separate party's independently answerable liability question and reserves genuinely necessary review; this case involves the same entered legal foreclosure. Clinton v. Jones permits review of an integrated temporal-protection order, supplying a confined analogy rather than a general efficiency exception. Camenisch forbids converting a preliminary estimate into final merits adjudication. Here there is an actual dismissal, not an estimate relabeled as a judgment.

Common facts, efficiency and a merits issue's relevance to likelihood of success are insufficient by themselves. Additional independent legal or factual issues remain outside the proper appeal. Later final-judgment merger does not cure an earlier jurisdictional excess, and the Court does not rely on it to do so. Section 1254(1) also does not impose §1257's state-court finality requirement. The later final judgment is posture evidence, not an expanded grant encompassing all its other statutory rulings.

**Precedent treatment:** Deckert is applied to the same equitable claim's actual legal dismissal; Swint's separate-liability exclusion is preserved; Clinton's integrated-order principle is applied by bounded analogy; Camenisch's preliminary/final distinction is preserved.

##### 4. The separately dismissed MBTA claim has not been shown moot

**Holding and operative rule:** Completion of river plans and final adjudication of other statutory claims do not, without loss of all effective relief on the MBTA authorization challenge, moot review of that claim's separate legal dismissal. Review may correct the dismissal on this record, but any affirmative remedy on remand requires a present, independently supported basis and must cease if the claim is actually moot.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The controversy concerns identified sale authorizations and alleged injury to protected birds; the Forest Service conceded that some nesting birds would be killed. Final rejection of the claim does not itself extinguish the controversy over that rejection. Neither the reported plan completion nor the later final appeal decided the MBTA claim anew or established that no meaningful relief remains.

The December 1997 report that approximately three-fourths of road work and harvesting was complete proves neither completion by September 1998 nor an exact present amount of unfinished work. The Court infers neither continued cutting nor completed harvesting from the absence of a later report. Arizonans requires a controversy throughout review. Bennett preserves the distinct, stage-appropriate requirements of injury, causation and redressability. Ohio Forestry's premature programmatic challenge differs from these concrete sale authorizations; it supplies no blanket immunity from review and no exemption from the remaining conditions.

The Court decides a legal dismissal and orders no current operational relief. A supported showing on remand that no effective remedy remains must receive its own mootness determination. Conversely, uncertainty about precise harvest status cannot itself establish either mootness or entitlement to an injunction.

**Precedent treatment:** Arizonans and Bennett retain the continuing-case and stage-of-proof requirements. Ohio Forestry is distinguished at its absence of a concrete project authorization; its independent jurisdictional and review conditions remain intact.

##### 5. The APA authorization challenge requires its own statutory inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** A court cannot sustain dismissal of an APA challenge to the Forest Service's affirmative sale authorization solely because the MBTA supplies no implied private action, the sovereign is not subject to the asserted criminal punishment, or FWS has not demanded a permit. The lower courts must examine the challenged authorization under the applicable MBTA text and regulations, the APA's retained conditions and the actual preservation and remedial record; the present judgment establishes no MBTA violation or permit obligation.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, has five direct rationale votes. The four dissenters agree that an implied private action and a demand for FWS enforcement are distinct from APA review, but would sustain the agency-permit coverage ground; they do not join this remand holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Bennett separates an available APA route from a particular statute's private enforcement mechanism and retains §§702 and 704's actual conditions. The pleading challenges completed Forest Service authorizations as contrary to applicable law. It is not an action to punish the United States criminally or to compel FWS to prosecute. Heckler v. Chaney protects discretionary nonenforcement within its proper setting; silence by the enforcement agency does not transform every challenge to another agency's affirmative act into a nonenforcement suit. Lincoln v. Vigil likewise preserves enforceable statutory boundaries.

The penalty provision's list of covered persons and the contemporaneous permit regulations require consideration, but they do not alone answer what substantive constraints apply to the challenged authorization through the APA. Cooper and Will recognize a context-sensitive sovereign presumption; the criminal actor question cannot replace examination of the distinct legal act and asserted obligation. The contrary reading treats absence of sovereign punishment as conclusive of a different review question. That is insufficient to sustain this dismissal.

Sweet Home requires attention to the statute actually governing the alleged injury; its ESA definition of harm cannot be transplanted into the MBTA. The remand must address §703's operative prohibition, causal and regulatory limits and any properly presented preservation issue. The conceded prospect of bird deaths alone does not prove a violation. The Court decides neither the Act's reach to incidental killing nor private-logger or individual-officer liability, and assumes no contractor theory was preserved.

APA review retains injury and redressability, the provision-specific protected-interest inquiry, final agency action, no other adequate court remedy, applicable preclusion and committed-to-discretion limits, and relief other than money damages under §702. Any decree must satisfy the ordinary legal and equitable conditions. No treaty-based action, permit system or immediate operational decree is created.

**Precedent treatment:** Bennett is applied to the separate APA route and its conditions; Chaney and Lincoln retain their actual discretion boundaries; Cooper and Will are distinguished from a conclusive answer to this different APA authorization question; Sweet Home's statute-specific method is preserved without importing ESA coverage.

*Securities and Exchange Commission v. First Jersey Securities, Inc.*, No. 96-1862 (petition caption: *First Jersey Securities, Inc. v. SEC*). Review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Second Circuit's December 10, 1996 judgment, 101 F.3d 1450, affirming securities-fraud liability, disgorgement, prejudgment interest and the preventive injunction while reversing the Special Agent appointment. The defendants challenge the adverse liability and remedial components; no request to restore the appointment is within this authorized review. The questions concern preclusion, proved primary and controller responsibility, connected monetary relief and the supported risk of future violations.


## Participation

All nine Justices participate at submission and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No recusal or nonparticipation is established.


## Public Action

Affirm the Second Circuit judgment, 9–0; preserve its reversal of the investigative Special Agent appointment.


## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The Second Circuit's split judgment remains intact; the District Court's decree is not reinstated in full.

The proved primary fraud and independent §20(a) ground stand. Disgorgement is $22,288,099 after the $5 million settlement credit against $27,288,099 in calculated six-security gains; the joint and several obligation permits one recovery, not duplicate collection. Prejudgment interest of $52,689,894 stands, with interest on the credited $5 million ending at payment. The supported prospective securities injunction remains. The already-entered Special Agent reversal is undisturbed; no new investigation, uncharged liability or additional recovery is authorized.

## Holdings

### 1. The earlier proceeding does not release these later transactions

**Holding and operative rule:** A judgment concerning earlier transactions does not bar an enforcement action for later distinct transactions merely because the alleged method resembles the earlier misconduct; a settlement releases additional conduct only within its actual scope. The 1979 proceeding and 1984 settlement therefore do not bar this action concerning the 1982–1985 transactions, and permission to supplement the earlier complaint imposed no obligation to add every later wrong.

**Authority:** The eight Associate Justices adopt Kennedy's Part I: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of the defense without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lawlor distinguishes later transactions from a second demand for recovery on the same claim. Moitie preserves the finality of an actual judgment; it does not immunize later conduct. Baker likewise requires respect for a judgment's lawful effect at its real scope. The earlier proceeding concerned different transactions, and the actual settlement language did not settle the later transactions now proved. Similarity of practices cannot enlarge that release. The defendants' argument that the SEC could have supplemented the old proceeding confuses procedural permission with an obligation to sue there. The Court preserves the earlier judgment while rejecting the proposed extension of its effect.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Lawlor is applied to distinct later transactions.
- Moitie's finality rule is preserved.
- Baker is applied at the judgment's actual scope; no broader release is inferred.

### 2. Proven direct deception supports primary fraud liability

**Holding and operative rule:** A broker-dealer and the officer who knowingly directs its deceptive transactions are responsible for their proved primary securities fraud; office, ownership or assistance alone does not establish that violation. First Jersey's intentional concealment of its dominated markets, repurchases and coordinated splitting and resale plan, together with Brennan's knowing hands-on orchestration, supports the liability found under Securities Act §17(a)(1), Exchange Act §10(b) and Rule 10b-5.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt Kennedy's Part II; nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The findings establish deceptive dealing in the six securities, rather than liability inferred from Brennan's title or wealth. The firm's transactions carried an implied representation that the prices reflected a competitive market. Its concealment of its own domination and coordinated repurchase, unbundling and resale plan made that representation misleading to both selling and purchasing customers; its duty was to disclose the facts needed to make those representations truthful. Its contemporaneous repurchase prices supply a supported market benchmark in that setting. Illiquidity within the firm's unilateral control does not establish an independent explanation defeating those findings. No automatic five-percent or ten-percent markup rule is adopted. Central Bank preserves its confined private affirmative-assistance action, but direct participation in the actual deception supplies liability here without enlarging that action or importing its private reliance requirements into SEC enforcement. O'Hagan preserves the need for actual deception, culpability and a connection to securities transactions; this case does not extend its source-deception theory to status alone.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Central Bank's private accessory rule remains separate and is not expanded.
- O'Hagan's primary-deception requirements are preserved without extending misappropriation liability.

### 3. The proved controller ground independently supports Brennan's liability

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 20(a) permits the SEC, which falls within the Exchange Act's relevant definition of person, to enforce controller responsibility for the established §10(b) and Rule 10b-5 violation on proof of actual control and the deliberate culpable participation found here. Its defense requires both good faith and absence of direct or indirect inducement; Brennan cannot satisfy that defense on the supported findings.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt Kennedy's Part III; nine direct rationale votes. This is an independently sufficient statutory ground at the stated scope, separate from his primary participation.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 3(a)(9)'s definition includes governmental agencies, defeating the contention that the SEC cannot invoke this supported controller liability. Brennan exercised actual control and deliberately participated in the coordinated scheme. A compliance arrangement that deliberately failed to examine the relevant transactions does not establish good faith, and his active direction defeats the separate no-inducement requirement. The Court does not impose liability from passive shareholding alone, and the defense remains available to a controller who proves both good faith and absence of direct or indirect inducement. It need not settle every disputed culpable-participation pleading burden because that participation was proved. This Exchange Act controller ground does not become a rule supplying controller liability for every Securities Act claim.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Central Bank retains its distinct accessory domain and does not displace the express controller provision or its conjunctive defense.
- O'Hagan retains its distinct primary-fraud domain and does not displace the express controller provision or its conjunctive defense.

### 4. Equity may recover the connected gains once

**Holding and operative rule:** In the SEC's injunctive enforcement proceeding authorized by Securities Act §20(b), 15 U.S.C. §77t(b), and Exchange Act §21(d)(1), 15 U.S.C. §78u(d)(1), traditional ancillary restitution permits recovery of the reasonably approximated unlawful gains connected to the proved fraud, with credits preventing duplicate recovery. Joint and several responsibility is proper for First Jersey and Brennan's proved collaboration and benefit in this enterprise, not because every owner owes every corporate debt.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt Kennedy's Part IV; nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 20(b) and 21(d)(1) authorize the SEC's suit for injunctive enforcement; their words do not expressly grant disgorgement. Porter and Mitchell recognize ancillary restitution within that statutory equity unless Congress displaces it. Mertens construed a different, expressly limited ERISA remedial provision; it does not remove this traditional gain-recovery function. The six-security profit approximation was $27,288,099. Crediting the $5 million class-settlement payment leaves $22,288,099 to be disgorged. Brennan's deliberate collaboration, complete ownership, unrestricted access to the enterprise funds and corresponding enrichment connect him to those gains. The defendants' strongest objection—that equity cannot take unrelated wealth or impose another person's unrelated gain—states a real limit but does not describe these findings. The award removes the proved benefit once; it supplies no recovery of unproved transactions, unrelated profits, wealth as such or every recipient's gross receipts. Deterrence alone supplies neither the amount nor an additional punitive component.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Porter supplies traditional ancillary restitution within statutory equity.
- Mitchell supplies traditional restorative equity absent statutory displacement.
- Mertens is distinguished by its different statutory restriction, without altering its ERISA holding.

### 5. Retained-use value supports the interest award

**Holding and operative rule:** Prejudgment interest may approximate the value of retaining unlawful gains, subject to the actual credit, causal connection and equitable fairness of the award. On these findings, the $52,689,894 award using the IRS underpayment measure is sustained, with interest on the credited $5 million ending when that amount was paid.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt Kennedy's Part V; nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The $52,689,894 award is the prejudgment interest fixed in the July 17, 1995 District Court judgment, not a recalculation through today's decision. The defendants retained use of the remaining gains during that prejudgment period; interest on the $5 million paid in January 1987 ceased at that payment. A borrowing-cost measure addresses that benefit more directly than the lower rate the Government pays a lender; no identified legal or calculation error requires substitution of the Treasury rate. A large ratio to principal, or the passage of years while the benefit remains held, does not by itself transform retained-use compensation into punishment. The actual cessation of interest on the paid settlement credit prevents charging for money no longer retained. The Court makes neither the IRS rate nor interest during every possible litigation delay universally mandatory. Porter and Mitchell's gain-restoration principle supports this confined use-value component, not an unexplained surcharge.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Porter's restorative-equity principle is applied to the connected benefit of retained funds, without a universal rate.
- Mitchell's restorative-equity principle is applied to the connected benefit of retained funds, without a punitive multiplier.

### 6. Prevention requires a supported risk of recurrence

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Securities Act §20(b), 15 U.S.C. §77t(b), and Exchange Act §21(d)(1), 15 U.S.C. §78u(d)(1), a prospective securities injunction requires the proper showing of a supported risk of future violations; past misconduct or a defendant's refusal to confess does not alone supply that basis. The systematic intentional violations, relevant prior sanctions, ongoing market activity, retained personnel and continuing control support the injunction entered here.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt Kennedy's Part VI; nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The governing provisions authorize the SEC to seek restraint of a person engaged or about to engage in covered violations and provide for injunctive relief on a proper showing without bond. The actual findings connect the deliberate course of conduct to continuing means and opportunity for recurrence. The injunction therefore prevents supported future securities violations rather than punishing wealth, corporate office or defense of the suit. FEC v. Williams preserves the separate requirement for a genuine preventive basis and does not classify every governmental equitable remedy as a penalty. Neither an industry-wide ban nor relief against unrelated actors is ordered. The Court sustains this decree on its supported predicates without creating an automatic injunction from a completed fraud judgment.

**Precedent treatment:**

- FEC v. Williams is applied to independent preventive support, preserving its limits on automatic prospective relief.

## Event

*United States ex rel. McKenzie v. BellSouth Telecommunications, Inc.*, No. 97-850. Decided September 28, 1998, on merits submission; no separate oral-argument date is established. Review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Sixth Circuit's August 26, 1997 judgment, 123 F.3d 935, affirming dismissal of the qui tam claim while reversing dismissal of the separate retaliation claim. The questions concern the then-operative False Claims Act's public-disclosure/original-source conditions and the sufficiency of the pleaded retaliation claim. The qui tam allegations concern falsified telephone-repair reports said to avoid refunds to federal customers; no underlying fraud has been found. The reported refund arrangement requires a customer request after a line is out of service for more than 24 hours, subject to four distinct exceptions: diminished rather than wholly lost service, inaccessible customer premises preventing repair, an employee rather than customer report, or a customer-extended repair period. Those contractual conditions remain allegations about the underlying arrangement, not findings that refunds were owed or fraudulently avoided.

## Holdings

### 1. Original-source status has a prefiling condition, not a prepublication condition

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the then-operative §3730(e)(4)(B), a relator invoking the original-source exception must have direct and independent knowledge of the information on which the allegations are based and voluntarily provide that information to the Government before filing the action. The statute does not additionally require government disclosure before public disclosure, first disclosure to the Government, or responsibility for the publisher's source; the dismissal resting on that added deadline is vacated.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt Thomas's Part I; nine direct rationale votes.

**Statutory setting:** Section 3730(e)(4)(A) bars an action based upon public disclosure of allegations or transactions in a criminal, civil or administrative hearing; in a congressional, administrative or Government Accounting Office report, hearing, audit or investigation; or from the news media, unless the action is brought by the Attorney General or the person bringing it is an original source. This decision applies the original-source definition without adopting a comprehensive construction of based upon.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress supplied both knowledge conditions and a specific prefiling disclosure condition. The aim of preventing derivative bounty litigation cannot substitute an earlier publication clock. Direct knowledge and independent knowledge remain distinct requirements; neither employment nor a claim to insider status proves both. Lexecon supports applying the enacted conditions without inserting an additional procedural barrier. Brogan preserves written statutory limits rather than a free-standing policy exception. Oubre likewise requires performance of statutory protections rather than replacing them with an unprovided prerequisite; none of those decisions decides original-source facts here. The Sixth Circuit reasoned that the first person to sound the alarm alone deserves the reward, but its added chronology condition excludes persons who may satisfy Congress's definition. The Court does not reward copied allegations: genuine knowledge and voluntary disclosure remain to be proved.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Lexecon is applied to written statutory conditions without importing its separate venue rule.
- Brogan preserves the enacted boundary without importing its separate criminal holding.
- Oubre is applied to statutory conditions without importing its separate waiver rules.

### 2. Correcting the deadline does not establish jurisdiction or fraud

**Holding and operative rule:** The qui tam claim returns for application of the actual public-disclosure and original-source conditions; this judgment does not declare McKenzie an original source or establish jurisdiction. Even assuming the identified public disclosures trigger §3730(e)(4)(A), the exception requires the prescribed knowledge and voluntary governmental disclosure before filing, and dismissal remains available if those conditions fail.

**Authority:** The eight Associate Justices adopt Thomas's Part II: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with vacatur and factual remand but supplies his different additional construction in a separate writing.

**Controlling explanation:** The assumed public predicates are the reported Dorris state-court suit concerning South Central Bell's practices, the Falsetti federal qui tam suit involving a different BellSouth subsidiary, and their news coverage. The earlier workplace knowledge, substantially overlapping complaint passages, and timing and content of government communication require their own record treatment. Similar language is relevant evidence; it does not automatically decide the origin of every factual allegation. Conversely, dispatcher experience does not establish every original-source fact. Sealed filing and government service under §3730(b)(2) cannot simply be assumed to satisfy voluntary disclosure before filing, and government declination proves neither original-source status nor fraud. The extra prepublication deadline is sufficient to require vacatur, so the Court does not select a comprehensive construction of based upon. Steel Co. preserves jurisdictional inquiry distinct from substantive merits without repealing the provision's express jurisdictional form. Separately, §3730(b)(5) prohibits a person other than the Government from intervening or bringing a related action based on facts underlying a pending private FCA action. Section 3730(e)(3) bars a private action based on allegations or transactions that are the subject of a civil suit or administrative civil money penalty proceeding in which the Government is already a party. Neither distinct bar is adjudged applicable merely from similar allegations.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Steel Co. retains the distinction between jurisdiction and substantive liability without displacing this express statutory jurisdictional condition.

### 3. The employee's retaliation claim has independent statutory conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** The then-operative §3730(h) protects an employee against covered employment retaliation because of lawful acts in furtherance of an FCA action, including qualifying investigation or assistance before an action is filed; protection does not depend solely on a successful qui tam recovery or survival of the public-disclosure bar. McKenzie's allegations permit the required inferences of protected activity, employer knowledge and causation at the reviewed dismissal stage, so the Sixth Circuit's reversal and retaliation remand are affirmed.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt Thomas's Part III; nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** McKenzie alleged persistent reporting of the fraud beyond ordinary dispatcher tasks, presentation to supervisors of a newspaper account of similar federal qui tam litigation, and responsive threats and harassment. These facts permit an inference that BellSouth understood her conduct as connected to contemplated federal enforcement. The employer need not hear the precise statutory label when the alleged facts provide the required notice. Ordinary performance complaints without such a connection are not automatically protected. Robinson supports reading retaliation protection in its statutory context while keeping prohibited conduct, notice and causation distinct; its Title VII rule does not supply FCA elements. Discovery may disprove any predicate. The judgment finds neither actual fraud nor retaliation, disability causation or an entitlement to reinstatement, back pay or damages. The claim proceeds under its own statutory conditions rather than falling automatically with a distinct qui tam jurisdictional ruling.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Robinson v. Shell Oil Co. supplies contextual treatment of a retaliation provision while its Title VII coverage holding and limits remain distinct.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Stone-Zsela joins the Court's rejection of the extra prepublication deadline and its distinct retaliation holding.
