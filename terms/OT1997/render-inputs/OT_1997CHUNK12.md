## Event

Bragdon v. Abbott, No. 97-156. Argued March 30, 1998; decided June 25, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the First Circuit, 107 F.3d 934, affirming summary judgment for Abbott. Bragdon refused routine office dental treatment after Abbott disclosed HIV on September 16, 1994 and offered hospital treatment carrying an additional facility charge.

The granted questions concern Abbott's disability under the ADA and the legal treatment of a provider's judgment under the direct-threat exception. The separate independent-review and whole-record trial questions were not granted. State-law claims are outside the federal grant.

## Holdings

### 1. HIV can impair before visible symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity

**Holding and operative rule:** HIV disease is a physical impairment within the ADA before its most serious symptoms, and reproduction is a major life activity. A claimant under the actual-disability branch must still show that the impairment substantially limits that individual's activity; diagnosis or reproductive-system impairment alone does not establish every element.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the disability part. The other three accept or assume impairment for this case without joining its general rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 12102(2)(A) separates impairment, major activity and individual substantial limitation. The Title III regulation, §36.104, expressly includes symptomatic and asymptomatic HIV and recognizes physiological disorders of relevant systems. Section 12201(a), except where the ADA otherwise provides, preserves at least Rehabilitation Act Title V standards and regulations. Arline's contagious-disease analysis does not turn its reservation about asymptomatic carriers into an exclusion from this statute.

The illustrative activity list is not confined to publicly performed, economic or daily activities. Reproduction's significance is not diminished because it is not continuously performed. The contrary reading would convert examples into a restriction the text does not impose. At the same time, a reproductive-system impairment and a substantial limitation of reproduction are separate inquiries. Yeskey resolves institutional coverage at its own scope and supplies no automatic individual entitlement here. No record-of or regarded-as holding is made.

**Precedent treatment:** Arline's disease and individualized-assessment principles are applied within the ADA; Yeskey's distinction between coverage and individual entitlement remains.

### 2. Abbott's own reproductive activity is substantially limited on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** Abbott's specific unchallenged testimony that HIV controlled her decision not to bear a child, considered with the supported perinatal transmission danger, establishes substantial limitation of her reproduction. Complete inability to conceive, a pre-infection plan, an attempted pregnancy or willingness to accept the danger is not an additional condition.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice disability coalition directly adopts this application.

**Controlling explanation:** The reported risks were approximately 25 percent without treatment and 8 percent with treatment. Even the treated figure represents a substantial danger to the child on this historical record. These figures are evidence here, not a legal threshold or a conclusion about current treatment. The Court therefore need not resolve whether mitigating measures generally must be disregarded.

The general answer denying impaired life functions describes daily functioning and does not retract the specific childbearing account. The opposing argument correctly demands a limitation in this person's life, rather than an abstract medical risk. Abbott supplies that connection. Unknown prior plans do not defeat her specific unchallenged testimony or justify resolving credibility against her. Ordinary summary-judgment burdens remain; no automatic rule covers every person with HIV, every activity or every future medical condition.

**Precedent treatment:** The ordinary individual-definition and Rule 56 requirements remain; neither institutional coverage nor a diagnosis substitutes for the supported personal limitation.

### 3. Direct threat requires objective individualized assessment, not professional sincerity

**Holding and operative rule:** A covered provider may exclude a person only for a significant risk to others' health or safety that cannot be eliminated by modification of policies, practices or procedures or auxiliary aids or services. The provider must make an individualized reasonable judgment based on current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence, addressing the risk's nature, duration, severity, probability of injury and reasonable mitigation; sincere belief or professional status supplies no special legal deference.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly support this common objective method.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 12182(b)(3) and 36.208 distinguish significant risk from any imaginable residual risk. They require neither absolute safety nor confirmed past transmission as a universal prerequisite. The relevant standpoint is information reasonably available to the provider and profession when treatment was refused, September 16, 1994. Arline supports individual medical assessment but gives no private provider a subjective immunity. Daubert and Joiner require reliable foundation and fit; they do not decide the substantive dental risk or impose numerical quantification in every case.

The hospital offer creates no independent defense. The lower court reports that no comparative-hospital-safety theory was pursued or established, and the offer alone proves no privileges or safeguards. Section 12201(d) does not require Abbott to accept that accommodation or charge. The Court does not require service outside professional competence or bar precautions justified by objective evidence in another case. No later breakthrough overwhelmingly vindicating the concern is established; the possible relevance of such a development to prospective relief is not categorically foreclosed.

**Precedent treatment:** Arline's individualized inquiry is applied; Daubert and Joiner retain their foundation and fit roles without becoming substantive risk or automatic trial rules.

### 4. Public-health authority has founded, nonconclusive weight; the cited sources require bounded reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule:** Reasonable medical judgments of public-health authorities receive special but nonconclusive weight, subject to a credible scientific challenge. That weight attaches only to a supported judgment within the source's actual role, date and scope; a private professional policy is not automatically an official public-health assessment. The direct-threat judgment is vacated for reconsideration of the CDC and Association materials on those terms, without deciding whole-record triability.

**Authority:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join the public-health/source-remand part. Stone-Zsela joins the objective method and remand judgment, not this distinct ground; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas support the risk judgment on their contrary official-weight approach.

**Controlling explanation:** Arline supports respect for reasonable public-health judgment while allowing supported scientific disagreement. The First Circuit largely used the correct objective method, rejected conclusive official consensus, permitted qualified contrary evidence and considered all eight proffers together. Its continuing rebuttable presumption was not an irrebuttable command. The Court does not invalidate every such presumption or find that cumulative review was omitted.

The CDC's 1993 recommendation says universal precautions reduce risk; it does not expressly assess the remaining risk's level or say further measures are unnecessary. The circuit drew those inferences. Their foundation must be considered before official authority is treated as resolving the defense. The Association's 1991 policy affirmatively describes safe office care, but it is a private professional policy whose scientific and professional-duty bases must be distinguished. The record does not establish that it was merely ethical, scientifically worthless or incapable of an implicit medical judgment. These source questions can affect the granted deference issue without deciding whether all evidence already requires trial.

The remand requires ordinary reconsideration of the existing sources and other evidence, without requiring reconstruction of a policy history, automatic supplementation or a new expert proceeding. It establishes neither zero risk nor significant risk. Personal sharp-injury observations and a scientific risk inference are distinct; the Court makes no finding that competent firsthand testimony was actually excluded. The lower court may again find summary judgment appropriate.

**Precedent treatment:** Arline's founded public-health weight is preserved; Joiner's source/inference discipline is applied within the granted question, leaving independent whole-record sufficiency undecided.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Arline:** Supplies individualized assessment and founded respect for public-health judgment; neither its carrier reservation nor private-physician reservation creates an exclusion or subjective defense.
- **Yeskey:** Institutional coverage does not resolve this individual's disability; its reserved question remains distinct.
- **Daubert and Joiner:** Reliable foundation, fit and supported inference remain separate from substantive risk and whole-record triability. No automatic exclusion, expert appointment or numerical threshold follows.
- **Ordinary summary-judgment and question-scope rules:** Supported inferences and actual individual proof remain required; the granted method question does not itself enlarge review to the separately ungranted trial issue.

## Law After Decision

Abbott is disabled on the established reproduction record. The general rule recognizes impairment before visible symptoms without treating every HIV diagnosis as complete statutory disability. The direct-threat inquiry remains individualized and objective, with significant risk and reasonable mitigation assessed at the relevant time. Founded public-health judgments receive special but rebuttable weight; a private policy and an inferred official conclusion require their actual foundation. Ultimate risk, whole-record triability, other disability routes and state-law merits remain unresolved.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the disability and common objective-method holdings and supports vacatur for assessment of contemporaneous objective evidence and reasonable precautions. Professional sincerity cannot replace that support, but the ADA permits refusal when a significant risk that reasonable measures cannot eliminate is proved. He does not adopt the separate public-health special-weight or specific source-foundation rationale, decide ultimate risk, require treatment outside professional competence or declare every person with HIV disabled.

**Stevens, joined by Breyer, concurring.** They would prefer affirmance because the lower court applied the objective approach and thoroughly considered the evidence, including its cumulative force. They nevertheless join the entire Court opinion and the bounded source reconsideration, which leaves the lower court free to reach the same result. This is agreement with the remand entered, not an opposing vote or a trial mandate.

**Ginsburg, concurring.** She joins the cautious, informed remand and the complete disability analysis. HIV can affect family relations, employment potential, self-care and access to care before visible symptoms; invisibility does not justify discrimination. Those broader observations establish no additional individual finding, alternative regarded-as holding or automatic diagnosis-based rule.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissenting on disability.** They assume undisputed impairment but reject reproduction as the presented statutory activity: the illustrative functions concern repeated ordinary functioning, and naming reproductive-system disorders does not itself answer the separate activity question. Independently, even assuming classification, Abbott's post-diagnosis choice, unknown prior plans and general negative answer do not establish a present individual limitation to their satisfaction. They distinguish a risk affecting choice from demonstrated present loss and do not adopt utter inability as the standard. They would reject this actual-disability determination, without deciding every alternative claim or ordering a trial under the classification they reject.

**O'Connor, separately dissenting on disability.** Childbirth is not generally comparable to the representative recurring activities. She leaves open substantial limitation if reproduction were recognized and other intimate or family activities. Accepting impairment for this case is not an affirmative general impairment rationale or a finding that HIV is harmless.

**Scalia's risk-method part, joined by O'Connor and Thomas.** All three agree with objective, contemporaneous evidence and risk vacatur but reject special scientific weight from official endorsement in litigation between private parties. Credentials, foundation, fit and reasoning should stand on their own; review of an agency's own entrusted action differs. They view the circuit's continuing official presumption as an error requiring reconsideration, not merely a source-identification problem. Possible dental transmissions may have probative value without confirmation, and other occupational evidence cannot be rejected solely because its occupational name differs; actual exposure conditions and the link to this procedure still matter. The seven dental cases are possible, not confirmed, and the forty-two occupational cases are not dental cases. Their more favorable assessment of this evidence supplies no independent whole-record trial holding, proved significant-risk finding or mandated trial.

## Event

Clinton v. City of New York, No. 97-1374. Argued April 27, 1998; decided June 25, 1998. On direct appeal under 2 U.S.C. §692 from the District of Columbia's consolidated declaratory judgment, 985 F. Supp. 168. The questions concern the statutory review channel, each action's Article III standing and whether the challenged post-enactment cancellations violate Article I.

On August 11, 1997, the President canceled §4722(c) of the Balanced Budget Act and §968 of the Taxpayer Relief Act, each signed August 5. The former protected identified New York provider taxes from specified Medicaid recoupment consequences; the latter made a qualifying seller's tax deferral available for specified cooperative acquisitions. The New York plaintiffs face revived fiscal exposure. ## Holdings

### 1. Section 692 permits these organizational challengers' direct review

**Holding and operative rule:** In §692's particular expedited constitutional-review scheme, an adversely affected individual includes a separately injured juridical claimant such as the organizational plaintiffs here. That statutory permission supplies this direct-review channel but does not dispense with Article III standing or the statute's separate procedural requirements.

**Authority:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg directly join the statutory-channel part. Stone-Zsela and Breyer accept review without joining its complete rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress applied cancellation to governmental and corporate fiscal interests and provided prompt constitutional review of its operation. In this context, the eligible-plaintiff term encompasses separately injured organizational claimants. The contrary textual argument has force: ordinary usage often means a natural person, the Dictionary Act distinguishes persons and individuals, and the same Act elsewhere distinguishes businesses from individuals. Ordinary federal-question review is also available, so the construction does not rest on a false assertion that entities otherwise lack a remedy. The particular expedited design nevertheless supports their inclusion here, not a universal definition of the term or jurisdiction based merely on importance.

Section 692 still requires prompt delivery of complaints to both Houses, preserves their intervention rights and the House's ability to intervene without a special resolution, and requires notice of appeal within ten calendar days and the jurisdictional statement within thirty. It prohibits a single-Justice stay and commands expedition. The reported filings supply this review posture; no disputed compliance date is resolved. The sufficient direct route makes it unnecessary to decide an alternative certiorari application before circuit judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Raines's distinction between statutory authorization and actual injury remains; Steel Co.'s priority for disputed Article III questions is observed.

### 2. Removal of New York's statutory protection creates concrete injury

**Holding and operative rule:** Cancellation of the identified protection against Medicaid recoupment and consequent provider liability causes a present injury by removing that protection and reviving concrete fiscal exposure, even though waiver proceedings and ultimate payment remain unresolved. A declaration restoring the provision redresses that loss without determining the final debt.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join the New York standing part.

**Controlling explanation:** The relevant exposure follows existing law, rather than a generalized objection to government action. HHS had not denied the waiver applications, and the estimated $2.6 billion is not treated as an assessed debt. The injury is the removal of an enacted protection against identified liability. Raines rejected legislators' abstract institutional dilution while leaving concrete cancellation injuries open; this case presents the latter. Lujan requires a supported injury, causal connection and useful relief, not certainty about every later administrative decision. Restoring the provision repairs the particular loss. The unions' independent standing and every named plaintiff's status need not be decided once an adequate claimant in this action is established.

**Precedent treatment:** Raines is distinguished on actual injury; Lujan's stage-specific factual showing and ordinary redressability requirement are applied.

### 3. The actual acquisition opportunity supports Snake River's standing

**Holding and operative rule:** Withdrawal of a seller-side tax incentive creates a cognizable injury to a cooperative's actual, identified acquisition opportunity when supported commercial activity and seller interest permit the inference that the withdrawal impairs that opportunity. Restoration redresses the opportunity loss without guaranteeing a sale, price, financing or eventual tax deferral.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join this bounded opportunity holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Snake River was formed to acquire processing capacity, held identified discussions and encountered an owner's expressed interest in a tax-deferred transaction. The incentive specifically favored qualifying cooperative purchases. Those facts support an actual bargaining disadvantage, not a free-standing hope shared by anyone who might indirectly benefit from tax legislation. The declaration says Snake River ended the discussions; it does not establish a seller's refusal or completed bargain. Neither is indispensable to this supported opportunity inference.

Bryant recognizes economic opportunity injury without certainty of the eventual purchase; Investment Company Institute recognizes harm from changed competitive conditions. Lujan preserves reasonable inferences from the actual evidence and imposes no universal executed-contract prerequisite. Akins permits meaningful redress despite another actor's continuing lawful discretion. Simon and Allen require a concrete causal and remedial chain; the targeted incentive and identified activity provide it here. Consolidation supplies no standing from New York's separate injury. Cranney's independent injury is unnecessary and remains undecided; membership or office alone does not establish one.

**Precedent treatment:** Bryant and Investment Company Institute support opportunity injury at this factual scope; Lujan, Simon and Allen retain actual proof and causation requirements; Akins permits useful relief without guaranteed downstream action.

### 4. The President may implement enacted choices but may not cancel their legal force on a new policy judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** After the underlying spending and tax provisions have become law, Congress may not authorize the President to remove their operative legal force selectively on a later discretionary fiscal-policy judgment outside bicameral passage and presentment. The two challenged cancellations therefore have no legal effect; genuine contingent administration and discretion within Congress's enacted substantive choices remain permissible.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg join the common Article I and remedy parts.

**Controlling explanation:** Chadha requires repeal and amendment, as well as enactment, to follow Article I's lawmaking procedure. The President may return an entire bill before enactment; the challenged authority instead selects enacted provisions afterward. Its consequences are substantive: loss of Medicaid protection and loss of an enacted tax rule. The statutory name alone is not decisive.

The strongest contrary view reads the earlier Act and later fiscal laws together as one authorized contingent scheme. Field permits Congress to prescribe a consequence that follows an executive determination of a specified contingency. Here the President retains a policy choice whether to cancel even after making the required findings. The real category, timing, information and fiscal restrictions do not convert that discretionary legal nullification into compulsory implementation of the substantive decision enacted in the canceled provision.

Congressional consent does not cure the constitutional defect. Cancellation operates without another law; restoration then requires enacted disapproval subject to the President's ordinary veto. Neither branch may make its preferred revision conclusive merely because the other cannot assemble overriding majorities. Train preserves statutory spending commands while leaving actual delegated discretion available. Loving concerns bounded implementation; Morgan Stanley and Plaut concern Congress's own enacted adjustments with independent adjudication retained. None authorizes this executive repeal. Hechinger's congressional-agent setting is distinct.

The sufficient Article I ground leaves independent nondelegation and balance-of-powers theories, unpresented discretionary-budget severability and disputed lockbox applications undecided. Congress may authorize fiscal choices, contingent appropriations and lawful rescission proposals; no alternative mechanism is judicially prescribed.

**Precedent treatment:** Chadha's enactment/repeal requirement is applied; Field and Train preserve genuine implementation; Loving, Hechinger, Morgan Stanley and Plaut retain their distinct institutional limits.

## Law After Decision

The two cancellations cannot displace the enacted provisions. The cancellation Act's real limits do not cure their Article I defect: only entire qualifying amounts, new direct-spending items or limited tax benefits may be canceled; the President must find deficit reduction, no impairment of essential functions and no national-interest harm, consider legislative history, construction, purposes and specified or best available information, and transmit the required reasoned special message to both Houses within five calendar days excluding Sundays. Effect begins on both Houses' receipt. Enacted disapproval restores the identified item from its original effective date, and disapproval laws cannot themselves be canceled. Discretion remains permission, not a duty to cancel.

The limited-tax-benefit categories remain distinct: revenue-losing deductions, credits, exclusions or preferences for 100 or fewer beneficiaries in a fiscal year, and transitional relief for 10 or fewer. The first excludes equal treatment within the same industry or activity or for the same property/investment type, and differences based solely on business size/form, general individual demographics, amount or a generally available tax election. The second separately excludes retention of prior law for all binding contracts or other legally enforceable obligations existing on a date contemporaneous with Congress’s action specifying that date and technical corrections estimated to have no revenue effect. Related businesses and associations under §§707(b) and 1563(a), an employer's qualified plans and holders of one bond issue are each aggregated; an entity's owners or beneficiaries are not counted again. Revenue loss is measured in the first effective fiscal year or the first five fiscal years. If enacted congressional identification is present, cancellation is confined to the identified benefits; otherwise the definition governs. The bar on reviewing congressional identification does not bar this constitutional challenge.

Section 4722(c) protects provider taxes, fees or assessments defined in §1903(w)(3)(A) and collected by the State of New York from a health care provider before June 1, 1997, where a waiver of §1903(w)(3)(B) or (C) under (E) was requested or otherwise would have been required; if requested, administrative action, including judicial review, must have remained incomplete on July 23, 1997. It does not itself decide the final recoupment or provider assessment.

Section 968 applies to sales after December 31, 1997 of stock in a domestic corporation substantially all of whose activities consist of the active business of refining or processing agricultural or horticultural products, and which bought more than half those products during the one-year period ending on the sale date from the purchasing cooperative or its member farmers. The cooperative must fall under part I of subchapter T and market those products. Section 1042(g) treats it as the specified worker-owned cooperative for this purpose, substitutes 100-percent ownership for 30 percent, disregards employer-security status and §1042(c)(1)(A)’s domestic-C-corporation/no-readily-tradable-stock restrictions, and disapplies §1042(c)(2)(D)’s member-elected one-person/one-vote board requirement and (c)(7)’s exclusion of gain includible in a C-corporation seller’s gross income. Remaining conditions include an election filed by the statutory return deadline, including extensions, qualifying replacement-property purchase within the three-month-before through twelve-month-after period, verified consent to §§4978 and 4979A, and the seller’s at-least-three-year holding period. The ownership attribution rules, alternative each-class or total-value tests and §1504(a)(4) stock exclusion remain; the special rule changes the percentage, not those terms. Other qualified-security, replacement-property, underwriter, basis, recapture and limitations provisions retain their own operation without a new eligibility adjudication. Restoring this provision awards neither the seller's deferral to the cooperative nor any taxpayer's final eligibility, basis or recapture result.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring.** The constitutional division protects individuals as well as competing institutions. Selective cancellation can concentrate burdens on identifiable groups. Congress's willingness to surrender a part of its role therefore does not remove the individual-liberty concern. He joins the complete Court opinion; this additional explanation does not create a separate test or remedy.

**Scalia, joined by O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part; Breyer joins only the New York merits/execution part.** The special review statute's use of individual means a natural person. Ordinary federal-question review remains available, so an expedited route limited to people is workable. Cranney's presence cannot supply an unproved personal injury or transfer statutory eligibility to an entity. Scalia and O'Connor propose using a distinct certiorari-before-judgment route if its application and pending-case requirements are met; that proposal is not a grant in this case.

They accept New York standing but reject Snake River's showing. Preliminary discussions and expressed interest do not adequately establish a usable transaction or concrete loss: the cooperative ended discussions, and no adverse seller response or price is established. Simon and Allen demand a supported causal chain dependent on another actor; Bryant's compelled-sale setting and incentives are materially different. They require no universal executed contract or financing document, but would vacate the tax declaration and dismiss for lack of standing rather than decide the tax merits.

If the New York merits are reached, these Justices, with Breyer, treat the later choice as execution of authority contained in laws already enacted together. Field demonstrates that an authorized executive decision affecting a statute's operation is not necessarily forbidden repeal. For direct spending, Cincinnati Soap and established discretionary appropriations permit expenditure choices. Train forbids withholding contrary to a command but leaves Congress free expressly to confer discretion. The Act's subject, time, information and fiscal constraints sufficiently bound this spending choice. They recognize no inherent presidential cancellation power. Scalia and O'Connor withhold the tax merits; their shared execution discussion does not become a vote for the distinct tax delegation's validity.

**Breyer, dissenting; O'Connor and Scalia join only the presentment/execution part.** He accepts both controversies and exercise of direct review and would sustain both cancellations. His additional functional and delegation grounds emphasize the limited budget subject, express categories and exceptions, short exercise period, information duties, mandatory fiscal findings, presidential accountability and Congress's retained power to exempt items and enact disapproval. J. W. Hampton supplies the intelligible-principle standard, and Skinner rejects a stricter delegation rule merely because taxation is involved. He acknowledges thinner historical support for tax cancellation, the risk of selective burdens, absent subsidiary administrative rules and lack of ordinary agency-style review, but finds the actual combined limits sufficient for these applications. Actual Plaut, Hechinger and Loving preserve attention to the function retained by each institution; none alone decides this case. ## Event

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor's opinion announcing the judgment, joined by Scalia, Thomas.** These three Justices would treat the exceptional burden as a regulatory taking under the combined economic-impact, reasonable-expectations and governmental-character factors of Connolly and Concrete Pipe, which supply contextual review of pension obligations. ## Event

National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley, No. 97-371. Argued March 31, 1998; decided June 25, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 100 F.3d 671. ## Holdings

### 1. The consideration requirement is not facially invalid under the First Amendment

**Holding and operative rule:** In this competitively selective arts-grant program, §954(d)(1)'s requirement to consider general decency and respect for diverse public beliefs and values, alongside artistic excellence and merit, is facially permissible because it prescribes neither a categorical viewpoint exclusion nor fixed disqualifying weight. The consideration is mandatory; an invidious discriminatory application, a penalty on independently financed expression or coercive suppression remains separately reviewable.

**Authority:** The narrow facial-validity part is directly joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas support the judgment on a broader ground; their votes are not added to this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute requires consideration, so the Court cannot treat the words as optional or erase them through panel diversity. But attention to a consideration is not an instruction to deny every work that causes offense. Artistic grants require comparative judgments about excellence, merit, audience and suitability within a program that cannot support every applicant. These considerations can operate contextually without a mandatory viewpoint veto. The facial challenge therefore does not establish that the enacted criterion must operate unconstitutionally in the manner claimed.

Rosenberger protects viewpoints within a generally available private-publication benefit; it does not classify every expenditure as that benefit. Forbes permits genuine selection in its particular candidate debate while preserving review for viewpoint discrimination; it supplies no automatic immunity for arts officials. Denver Area keeps private editorial permission distinct from governmental burdens and yields no universal subsidy rule. Those distinctions permit this narrow conclusion without treating private art as the government's own speech.

The contrary argument correctly insists that consideration must do real work. It does not establish that every meaningful contextual judgment must become a prohibited disqualification. Scarcity alone would not excuse an actual invidious denial. The Court decides neither a particular award nor the legality of a condition suppressing speech financed outside this program. The separate obscenity prohibition in §954(d)(2) is not rewritten.

**Precedent treatment:** Rosenberger's program-specific viewpoint rule and Forbes's genuine-selection and viewpoint-review requirements remain intact; Denver Area is not extended into categorical grant immunity.

### 2. Comparative artistic standards do not establish facial vagueness here

**Holding and operative rule:** In this selective grant scheme, the decency and respect considerations, read with artistic excellence and merit, are not facially void for vagueness merely because comparative artistic judgment cannot be reduced to precise objective criteria. This conclusion concerns discretionary arts patronage, not a criminal prohibition, a license required to speak or an objective entitlement to payment.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join the narrow vagueness part. Scalia and Thomas independently reject the claim through their broader subsidy rationale. Stone-Zsela does not decide it.

**Controlling explanation:** The scheme necessarily asks decisionmakers to compare works according to artistic judgments. Precision appropriate to a coercive speech prohibition cannot simply be demanded of every such comparison without also disabling ordinary judgments of excellence and merit. The challenged considerations operate within that selective function and impose no criminal liability or permission requirement for creating art. The possibility that uncertainty can discourage applications does not make all aesthetic imprecision unconstitutional. Actual viewpoint discrimination remains distinct and reviewable; permissible imprecision is not permission to conceal an unlawful criterion. Souter's First Amendment objection addresses that separate alleged criterion rather than uncertainty inherent in awarding scarce artistic support.

**Precedent treatment:** The speech-protective concern with uncontrolled coercive discretion is preserved; its demands are applied in light of this program's actual grant function rather than transferred unchanged from criminal or licensing rules.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Rosenberger:** Its viewpoint rule remains tied to the private-publication opportunity actually opened; not every subsidy must be structured as that program.
- **Forbes:** Genuine selective judgment and review of viewpoint discrimination remain compatible. Public ownership and a selective purpose create no blanket constitutional immunity.
- **Denver Area:** Its distinct permission and compulsory-burden holdings remain distinct; none supplies a universal governmental-speech or subsidy exception.
- **Rust and Maher:** The four-Justice spending discussion uses their program-definition and funding-priority distinctions. That discussion establishes no broader holding authorizing viewpoint conformity throughout private arts patronage.

## Law After Decision

The challenged consideration requirement survives this facial challenge, and permissible imprecision in this comparative arts setting supplies no separate vagueness basis for invalidation. No categorical viewpoint veto, automatic-disqualification rule, required weight, panel-composition safe harbor or universal grant exemption is created. Invidious applications and conditions burdening independent speech retain their own constitutional questions. Section 954(d)(2)'s obscenity prohibition and otherwise lawful artistic and administrative criteria remain separate.

## Separate Writings

**Supplementary spending discussion by Breyer, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy.** These four Justices add that program-specific appropriations may define what government chooses to support without financing every activity. Rust and Maher illustrate distinctions between funding a defined program and prohibiting independent activity. The observation does not replace the narrower statutory construction or authorize every discriminatory arts award. Ginsburg withholds assent from this further discussion; it has no independent controlling force.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** Government may choose the art it subsidizes, including a preference for decency and respect, without thereby preventing a rejected artist from creating the same work independently. This broader account treats the statutory command as real and does not depend on reducing it to a harmless consideration. Rosenberger's broadly available printing benefit and Forbes's opened candidate opportunity differ from discretionary arts patronage. The concurrence likewise rejects importing speech-related vagueness doctrine from coercive regulation into ordinary grant selection as such. It supplies no approval of penalties on independently funded speech, disguised regulation, every possible unconstitutional condition or departures from the rules of a forum actually opened.

**Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela in the First Amendment part, dissenting.** Requiring officials to consider decency and respect for public beliefs directs attention to whether private art conforms to prevailing views. That criterion disfavors offensive, irreverent and challenging expression even if it is not dispositive in every case. Treating mandatory consideration as legally inconsequential would delete Congress's command, while panel diversity does not remove it. Rosenberger's protection of independent private viewpoints and Forbes's continuing viewpoint constraints cannot be evaded merely because artistic support is selective. The program supports private expression, not a governmental message. Genuine neutral judgments concerning merit, subject, audience, educational suitability, finances and administration remain available; invalidating the proviso would not require funding every applicant. These two Justices would retain facial First Amendment relief without deciding settled individual claims, unrelated remedial questions or public financing of religious exercise.

Souter alone adds that artistic comparative judgment necessarily permits imprecision and joins the Court's narrow rejection of facial vagueness. His objection is to the viewpoint criterion, not unavoidable uncertainty in assessing art. Stone-Zsela does not join this statement and leaves vagueness unresolved.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment and the facial injunction are reversed. The case returns for implementation of that disposition. No application-specific judgment or new grant decision is commanded, and the settled claims remain settled.

## Event

United States v. Balsys, No. 97-873. Argued April 20, 1998; decided June 25, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, 119 F.3d 122. The Government challenges that court's vacatur of an order enforcing an administrative deposition subpoena. The question is whether a real and substantial risk of prosecution by an independent foreign sovereign, without asserted domestic criminal exposure, permits refusal to answer under the Fifth Amendment.

Balsys is a resident alien questioned in the United States during an investigation of alleged wartime conduct and possible immigration misrepresentations. Lithuania and Israel risks are established and uncontested; Germany is not included in that finding. Neither wartime guilt nor deportability is adjudicated.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court reverses and remands. Independent foreign prosecution risk alone does not support the claimed privilege.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.
**Opposing:** Ginsburg and Breyer.

The subpoena may be enforced subject to distinct domestic-use, immunity, treaty or other preserved objections. The decision orders no conviction, contempt, deportation, immunity, unconditional disclosure or discovery concerning a hypothetical proxy prosecution.

## Holdings

### 1. Independent foreign prosecution risk alone does not supply the privilege

**Holding and operative rule:** A real and substantial fear of prosecution by an independent foreign sovereign, without more and without asserted domestic criminal exposure, does not permit a witness to refuse compelled testimony under the Self-Incrimination Clause. The rule concerns the scope of the threatened criminal use, not a resident alien's constitutional status or the civil label of the proceeding.

**Authority:** The common sufficient part has Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. These seven directly adopt the rule and application; no fractured-decision aggregation is required.

**Controlling explanation:** The privilege protects against compelled self-incrimination in the criminal system governed by the constitutional prohibition. The words concerning criminal cases are read within that system and its protection against compelled use. The historical same-sovereign background does not survive as a license for state and federal governments to evade the same constitutional rule. Murphy bars that domestic evasion after both levels are bound; Kastigar explains why effective use and derivative-use protection can substitute for silence. Neither holding empowers an American court to bind an independent foreign prosecutor or establishes a privilege against that prosecutor's ordinary separate proceedings.

Zicarelli decided the absence of a real danger and left this foreign-risk question open. Balsys supplies the genuine Lithuania and Israel danger missing there, but proving danger does not itself establish its constitutional coverage. The opposing reading places decisive weight on domestic compulsion and the personal dilemma it causes. Those concerns do not enlarge the protected criminal-use scope into an unconditional right to withhold testimony whenever any foreign law threatens prosecution. Verdugo's distinction between the Fifth Amendment trial protection and a foreign search assists this functional inquiry without excluding a resident from domestic constitutional protection.

A civil investigation can still seek answers protected against domestic criminal use, and lawful pretrial invocation is not categorically premature. Here no domestic criminal exposure is asserted. The Government's limitations representation is not adopted as a universal limitations ruling. The holding permits no deliberate false answer, overrides no distinct immunity protection and decides no actual treaty or transfer objection.

**Precedent treatment:** Murphy's domestic intersovereign protection and Kastigar's effective use/derivative-use immunity remain intact; Zicarelli's reserved question is answered at the independent-foreign-risk scope; Verdugo is used only for the specific constitutional distinction, not an alien-status exclusion.

### 2. The present cooperation record does not establish an attributable prosecution

**Holding and operative rule:** OSI's general liaison mandate, a Lithuania assistance memorandum and past evidence sharing with Israel do not on this record establish that this subpoena serves a foreign prosecution attributable to the United States. A materially different, sufficiently coordinated prosecution is not decided; no cooperation exception, mandatory discovery inquiry or rigid test is adopted.

**Authority:** All seven Justices joining the common sufficient part accept the bounded record application. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter additionally join the affirmative general reservation that sufficiently coordinated prosecution might implicate American constitutional restraints; the other two express only ordinary nondecision. The more detailed possible-extension discussion has only four supporters and is noncontrolling.

**Controlling explanation:** International assistance shows prosecutorial interest, but assistance and transfer of evidence alone do not make an independent sovereign's prosecution an American one. The record supplies neither foreign direction of this subpoena nor an established joint or proxy proceeding. An undeveloped possibility cannot create an exception in this case. All seven leave materially different facts unadjudicated and supply no finding about an Israeli disclosure treaty or foreign assurance not established here. The five joining the affirmative reservation additionally explain that this decision does not authorize circumvention of domestic constitutional restraints through a proved proxy arrangement. They leave whether and when attribution establishes protection to an actual case presenting it.

**Precedent treatment:** Murphy's protection against domestic evasion remains; no new extension to a cooperative foreign prosecution is established.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Murphy:** Retained for state/federal compulsion and criminal use within the shared constitutional system. Its wider discussion does not establish the claimed independent-foreign-risk privilege.
- **Kastigar:** Effective use and derivative-use immunity remains the constitutionally sufficient domestic substitute for silence; an American promise is not declared binding on a foreign tribunal.
- **Zicarelli:** Its real-danger requirement remains. The previously reserved foreign-risk issue is answered only to the extent presented here.
- **Verdugo-Urquidez:** Its Fifth Amendment/foreign-search distinction informs the analysis; its Fourth Amendment result does not remove a domestic resident's constitutional protection.
- **Brogan and Woodard:** Their actual lawful-privilege, false-answer and compulsion distinctions remain. Neither was a foreign-risk decision, and neither creates a right to lie or permits compelled domestic criminal use.

## Law After Decision

Foreign danger alone no longer supports the Second Circuit’s refusal to enforce this subpoena. Actual domestic privilege and immunity protections remain. The judgment creates no general immunity from questioning, universal duty to answer despite independent objections, restriction on legitimate foreign cooperation, or rule governing a proved American-attributable foreign prosecution.

## Separate Writings

**Supplemental portions by Kennedy, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter.** These four Justices add that the privilege's protection of personal values is conditional: effective domestic immunity can remove the justification for silence. Extending it to independent foreign risk could instead make some silence irreplaceable because an American promise cannot control a foreign tribunal. The possible gain in protection and cost to domestic investigation do not justify that extension. Stevens particularly emphasizes fairness in the American criminal process and the difficulty of allowing foreign criminal laws to constrain otherwise lawful domestic evidence gathering. These reflections are additional, noncontrolling grounds.

The same four leave open a more specific possibility: substantially similar prohibitions on international offenses, combined with American immunity or evidence gathering deliberately serving another nation's prosecution of the common offense, might present a prosecution serving the United States as well. Whether that argument should succeed remains undecided. These are illustrations, not a checklist, established exception or finding about Balsys. Stone-Zsela joins only the general attribution reservation; Scalia and Thomas join neither supplemental discussion.

**Breyer, joined by Ginsburg, dissenting.** American officials are compelling answers that concededly threaten real foreign prosecution. The constitutional reference to any criminal case, Murphy's rejection of the old divided-sovereign approach and its account of personal protection support the witness's refusal. The relevant command restrains domestic compulsion rather than prescribing foreign procedure. Kastigar requires a substitute coextensive with the privilege; an immunity promise ineffective against the established danger is therefore insufficient. Practical investigation costs cannot narrow the right.

Their proposed protection requires real and substantial danger and operates question by question, including answers furnishing evidentiary links; it creates no general privilege based on speculative foreign laws. Zicarelli remains controlling on genuine risk. Effective assurances might remove a danger, but none is found or ordered here. Legitimate investigation through independent evidence remains available. The dissent would affirm the vacatur of enforcement without deciding guilt, deportability, foreign evidence rules or a right to lie.

**Ginsburg, also dissenting.** She emphasizes that domestic governmental compulsion exposes the person to self-condemnation and the same threat to human dignity regardless of the prosecutor's nationality. The established Lithuania and Israel danger suffices on that understanding; she would not require an additional joint-prosecution showing. She joins Breyer's full dissent.

## Procedure After Action

The Second Circuit judgment is reversed. The case returns for lawful subpoena-enforcement proceedings consistent with the independent-foreign-risk holding and preservation of distinct objections. No new collateral proceeding or remedy is ordered.

OSI’s liaison order, the August 3, 1992 Lithuania memorandum and reported prior Israel sharing support only their stated contents. No Israel disclosure treaty, actual joint prosecution, confidentiality assurance or foreign immunity is established. The Government’s limitations position is not a new judicial calculation.

## Event

Bauchman v. West High School, Tenth Circuit Nos. 95-4084 and 96-4101; judgment below 132 F.3d 542. Plenary merits decision, June 26, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 12. On review of the Tenth Circuit's December 18, 1997 judgment. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is supplied.

## Holdings

#### I. Graduation ends the student-specific prospective controversy, but legally available damages remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Graduation and the absence of continuing exposure or a certified class moot Bauchman's requests for an injunction and prospective declaration concerning her participation in the school choir. A legally available damages claim against a defendant answerable for a completed injury may preserve that controversy, but cannot revive the prospective requests or create a damages remedy against an unavailable defendant or capacity.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans for Official English distinguishes a departed claimant's prospective challenge from a legally available claim for past injury, and rejects manufactured nominal damages against a State or official-capacity defendant unavailable under §1983. Hodge likewise separates damages from prospective relief. Bauchman has graduated and cannot return to district secondary schools; her mother's representative role supplies no independent parental controversy, and no sibling claim or class is pleaded. Lee's continuing-exposure ground therefore does not preserve these prospective requests. The damages demand permits consideration of the past-injury controversy without deciding each defendant's capacity, personal responsibility or immunity. Settle confirms that graduation neither eliminates every damages claim nor establishes entitlement to damages. Those questions remain below.

**Precedent treatment:** Arizonans and Hodge apply to separate forms of relief; Lee's continuing-exposure basis is distinguished; Settle preserves defendant-specific remedial inquiries.

#### II. Academic performance of sacred music is distinct from official devotional activity

**Holding and operative rule:** Selecting, rehearsing and performing religious choral music, including in religious buildings, for legitimate musical and educational purposes does not itself violate the Establishment Clause. Religious words, a church venue or a teacher's religious identity alone do not transform that academic activity into governmental worship; neither an artistic label nor a secular objective excuses separately identifiable official organization of worship, prescribed religious observance or solicitation of adherence through public authority.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's opinion; all nine Justices join the complete rule and its bounded original-complaint application.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon, as Agostini modified it, directs one inquiry into the governmental act and the function it performs. Purpose, effects and institutional relationships are evidence: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls conduct it resolves; an established permissible relationship resolves that relationship; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation proceeds through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. Settle protects genuine academic study without making curricular discretion constitutional immunity.

Sacred music forms a substantial part of the choral repertoire. Its academic study and performance may remain music instruction in a church. The original reviewed theory rested on repertoire and location without adequately alleging that the school was conducting worship or prescribing religious profession. Its dismissal and the corresponding reconsideration ruling stand at that scope. This does not turn the academic explanations supplied by the Court of Appeals on the pleadings into findings of the defendants' actual purposes. Nor does it require particular subjective-intent words in every complaint: an adequately alleged official devotional function is actionable without that formula. The reported ridicule remains context, not an adjudicated independent retaliation claim.

Lee, Ingebretsen and Tanford preserve the prohibition on official devotional activity even where another purpose is sincere. That distinct allegation is presented through the Establishment Clause amendment. The Court finds neither actual worship nor that all practical consequences of participation were voluntary, and independently applies no separate religious-pressure ground.

**Precedent treatment:** Agostini supplies the unified Lemon inquiry; Settle preserves genuine pedagogy without immunity for unconstitutional function; Lee, Ingebretsen and Tanford retain their distinct official-worship and pressure rules.

#### III. The genuine grade-neutral option defeats the original compelled-singing theory

**Holding and operative rule:** The original Free Exercise and compelled-expression dismissals stand to the extent the record establishes a genuine opportunity to decline the challenged songs without grade loss, required profession of belief or worship. The materially unchanged proposed Free Exercise and compelled-expression claims remain rejected; this conclusion does not govern a sham option, lost credit, exclusion, stigma, compelled profession or actual worship requirement.

**Authority:** Part III of Ginsburg's opinion; all nine Justices join this conditional and claim-specific conclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** Barnette protects against compelled affirming words and gestures. Sherman applies that protection while distinguishing penalty-free abstention in its actual civic setting from official worship. Bauchman's own reported account describes an offered option not to sing objectionable songs and an assurance that her grade would not suffer. At the reviewed scope, singing those songs was not compulsory, and academic performance is not by itself a religious profession. The Court decides no other basis for rejecting these theories and does not find every rehearsal, venue, group activity or practical consequence voluntary. Protected abstention does not cure school-organized worship; the amendment's Establishment Clause allegations remain separate.

**Precedent treatment:** Barnette and Sherman's compelled-affirmation rule are applied, without converting abstention into a general Establishment Clause defense or adding a formal-penalty requirement to Lee or Ingebretsen.

#### IV. A categorical exclusion of earlier concrete conduct cannot support the amendment's futility denial

**Holding and operative rule:** Earlier concrete official conduct may be relevant to an alleged continuing devotional use of the same school program; the Court of Appeals erred in treating all pre-1994–95 conduct as categorically irrelevant and sustaining denial of the Establishment Clause amendment on the resulting constitutional and futility analysis. The application must be reconsidered under ordinary pleading, amendment, discovery and proof rules, including Rules 15 and 59 and the unaddressed timeliness ground; neither amendment, unrestricted discovery nor trial is ordered.

**Authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The amendment alleged a continuing practice of using public teaching authority to promote religion, including earlier worship performances, religious exercises, admission inquiries and classroom devotional direction. Those concrete allegations differ from an inference based only on the teacher's faith. Their possible relevance to the program's 1994–95 function does not depend on Bauchman's standing to recover for other pupils' injuries. Agostini forbids using a secular objective to justify prohibited devotional means. A plausible academic explanation identified by a court cannot dispose of adequately presented allegations of a different actual function.

Foman permits supported reasons to deny leave, but the categorical exclusion supplies no lawful futility ground. The same error undermines the alternative summary-judgment forecast; no summary judgment was actually entered. A legally proper futility forecast remains available after the error is removed. Conley supplies then-operative fair-notice pleading. Leatherman bars heightened municipal pleading while preserving Monell and Canton's actual attribution and causation requirements. Settle and Crawford-El distinguish supported lawful grounds from merely possible explanations and preserve ordinary case management; their claim-specific burdens are not transplanted here.

The allegations remain allegations. Temporal connection, truth, weight, admissibility and each defendant's causal role remain open. Rules 403 and 404(b), including the distinction between a legitimate evidentiary use and character propensity, and proportionate discovery limits remain available. No particular incident must be admitted. Diamondstone preserves individual conduct-time immunity; later decisions cannot establish notice in 1994–95. The reported reprimand proves no administrator's receipt, knowledge, liability or loss of immunity. A genuinely independent lawful ground may still defeat the application, and any properly supported later summary-judgment motion receives ordinary consideration.

**Precedent treatment:** Foman and Conley govern amendment and pleading; Leatherman preserves ordinary municipal pleading without relaxing Monell/Canton; Settle and Crawford-El prohibit the particular categorical shortcut while preserving procedure; Diamondstone retains Harlow/Anderson's individual conduct-time inquiry.

#### V. The dependent state-claim direction must be reconsidered with the federal disposition

**Holding and operative rule:** The appellate direction to dismiss the state constitutional claims without prejudice is vacated only because it depended on the premise that no federal claim could proceed. On remand the lower courts must reconsider supplemental jurisdiction in light of the federal amendment disposition; they remain free to dismiss on ordinary discretionary grounds, and no Utah constitutional merits question is decided.

**Controlling explanation:** Correcting the granted federal amendment ruling removes the stated predicate of the dependent supplemental-jurisdiction direction. Reconsideration is a consequence of that federal remedy, not adjudication of an independent Utah constitutional question. Section 2106 authorizes correction of the dependent direction and appropriate further proceedings in a case lawfully before the Court; it does not enlarge the questions under review. Section 1367's ordinary discretion and comity remain. ## Holdings

#### I. Unfulfilled threats may support a hostile-environment claim under the existing actual-authority rule

**Holding and operative rule:** An employee who rejects sexual advances and suffers no completed tangible retaliation may pursue a Title VII hostile-environment claim, but must prove discrimination because of sex, an objectively and subjectively abusive environment sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions, and employer attribution. For the actual-authority route, the employee must prove by a preponderance that delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of that environment; rank, employment or workplace proximity alone is insufficient, while final hiring or firing power, a fulfilled threat, economic loss and higher-management notice are not invariably necessary.

**Authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight Associate Justices; nine direct joins to the rule, application and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Meritor requires an agency inquiry, rather than either automatic supervisor liability or exclusive reliance on management notice. Andrade, Faragher and Harrison give that inquiry a concrete actual-power connection and place its proof on the plaintiff. Harris supplies the separate objective and subjective environment standard; Oncale keeps discrimination because of sex distinct from offensive sexual content and employer responsibility.

Slowik's supported role in hiring and promotion, although subject to superior approval, and his alleged use of work-related authority permit inquiry into whether that power materially aided the asserted environment. The threats cannot be excluded merely because Ellerth refused, no retaliation followed, his purpose was personal, he was not the employer's alter ego or higher management lacked notice. Conversely, a title or opportunity to encounter Ellerth would not alone establish the connection. Ordinary plaintiff-favorable summary-judgment principles govern; the Court neither finds actual aid nor independently establishes all environment elements.

Ordinary employment scope, employer negligence and apparent authority remain distinct routes. Under Harrison, an apparent-authority theory requires an employer-attributable manifestation and reasonable reliance on that appearance; the supervisor's own assertion of power alone does not establish it. Those requirements are not additional elements of actual-power attribution. The Court decides no separate preserved quid-pro-quo claim by relabeling this one, no universal tangible-action definition and no rule that one unfulfilled threat always suffices.

**Precedent treatment:** Meritor's agency requirement is applied through Andrade, Faragher and Harrison's actual-power/material-aid rule; Harris and Oncale retain their independent environment and discrimination predicates.

#### II. Policy knowledge and nonreporting alone do not supply a complete defense

**Holding and operative rule:** Ellerth's knowledge that a harassment policy existed and her failure to report during employment do not alone eliminate earlier material aid from delegated authority or establish a complete defense. Whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse can establish a general complete defense remains open, including its elements, burden, relationship to tangible employment action and any distinct limitations on particular relief.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine direct joins. The three-Justice concurrence's additional recoverability proposal is not part of this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Faragher rejects the proposition that a paper prohibition, noncomplaint and eventual correction automatically erase attributable harassment, while reserving a genuinely effective protection defense. Harrison preserves that reservation and distinguishes the plaintiff's attribution burden from any proposed defense. A known policy makes prevention and avoidability relevant; it does not establish that the means of protection were practically available or effective, or that nonuse was unreasonable.

The employer's strongest argument is that effective prevention and unreasonable nonuse should limit responsibility. That question does not require a general formula here. Existing law already limits attribution to plaintiff-proved actual power materially aiding the wrong, rather than treating supervisory status as sufficient. Neither that rule nor the present record supplies an automatic negligence safe harbor. The Court does not find this policy effective or ineffective, its channels safe or unsafe, or Ellerth's conduct reasonable or unreasonable. No exact policy wording, prevention practice or reporting route is assumed.

The decision adopts neither a general complete defense nor categorical rejection of every defense; it creates no new burden shift, universal reporting duty, publication duty, tangible-action exception or rule erasing already incurred harm. Prevention, negligence, apparent authority and particular avoidable loss retain their proper significance when supported, without importing a new recovery formula into this holding.

**Precedent treatment:** Faragher's complete-defense reservation and Harrison's preservation of it remain unchanged; Meritor does not make a policy and noncomplaint conclusive.

#### III. The appellate reversal stands, with ordinary further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** The Seventh Circuit's judgment reversing employer summary judgment on the appealed attribution claim is affirmed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings under Parts I–II. The legal error in categorical nonliability establishes no final liability, damages or entitlement to trial; genuinely preserved independent grounds may be resolved under ordinary procedures, and the separately dismissed constructive-discharge claim remains outside this disposition absent established preservation.

**Authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The lack of higher-management notice or completed retaliation cannot foreclose the supported actual-power inquiry. That is enough to sustain the appellate rejection of summary judgment on the categorical ground presented. It is not a finding that Ellerth will prove an actionable environment, material aid or recoverable loss. Andrade illustrates correction of an attribution error without deciding all preserved alternatives; Faragher's restoration of an existing nominal judgment rested on supported bench findings not present here. Harrison likewise separates an agency error from independent claim and remedy questions.

The lower courts must resolve genuinely disputed facts and supported independent grounds in their proper posture. The Court mandates no trial, additional agency hearing, pleading amendment, discovery supplementation, liability finding or award. The conduct occurred in 1993–94, so Landgraf's temporal bar for pre-1991 conduct is not triggered merely by this case; the separate statutory conditions governing compensatory and punitive relief, fees and jury entitlement remain unadjudicated.

**Precedent treatment:** Andrade's bounded correction and Faragher's distinct findings-based remedy are applied at their actual scope; Harrison's procedural distinctions and Landgraf's temporal limit remain intact.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson:** Applied to require agency analysis rather than automatic supervisory imputation or a notice-only rule; policy and noncomplaint alone remain inconclusive.
- **Andrade v. Mayfair Management, Inc.:** Applied to actual supervisory power materially aiding harassment and to correction without automatic liability or trial.
- **Faragher v. City of Boca Raton:** Applied to the actual-power rule and the express complete-defense reservation. Its supported bench findings and nominal-judgment restoration do not establish facts or mandate a second agency proceeding here.

## Separate Writings

Recovery would be reduced or barred only to that proved extent.

They distinguish attribution from compensation, applying to employment the remedial position they expressed in Gebser without treating Title IX as controlling Title VII. Their rule would neither shift the employee's attribution burden nor create a special immunity for cases without tangible action or a categorical exception for cases with one. If all compensable loss were proved avoidable, loss recovery could be eliminated, but discrimination would not be erased and other relief and fees would not thereby be decided. No fact here establishes application of their proposal. The Court reserves adoption of these terms.

## Event

Monge v. California, No. 97-6146. Argued April 28, 1998; decided June 26, 1998. October Term 1997, chunk 12. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of California, reviewing 16 Cal. 4th 826, 941 P.2d 1121 (1997). The question is whether the Double Jeopardy Clause bars another attempt to establish the serious-prior-felony allegation after the first proof failed to establish its necessary personal-conduct qualification.

The jury convicted Monge of using a minor to sell marijuana, selling or transporting marijuana, and possessing marijuana for sale. The information gave notice of a serious-prior-felony allegation and a distinct prior-prison-term allegation. Monge waived a jury on those allegations. At the bifurcated bench proceeding, the State introduced a prison record establishing an assault conviction and imprisonment, but not necessarily the required personal use of a dangerous or deadly weapon or personal infliction of great bodily injury. The prosecutor's assertion about use of a stick was not evidence of that missing fact. The trial court found both allegations true and imposed eleven years: a selected five-year term doubled to ten, plus one year for the separate prison-term allegation. A three-year term on count two was stayed and a two-year term on count three ran concurrently.

The State conceded on appeal that the first record did not prove the serious-prior qualification beyond a reasonable doubt. The Court of Appeal found insufficiency and barred retrial. The California Supreme Court reversed that double-jeopardy ruling, permitting another proof proceeding. The seven-year unenhanced maximum on count one, fourteen-year qualifying-prior maximum, selected five-year term and independent one-year enhancement remain distinct.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Holdings

#### I. This noncapital recidivist-status determination does not receive offense-acquittal finality

**Holding and operative rule:** The Double Jeopardy Clause does not bar renewed proof of the serious character of Monge's prior conviction in this noncapital sentencing proceeding after the first proof was insufficient. Here the required personal-conduct qualification determines recidivist status for punishment of the current offense, rather than adjudicating a new offense or a capital acquittal-like determination; neither the availability of trial safeguards nor the increase in the punishment ceiling alone makes this particular determination an offense acquittal.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer; five direct joins to this classification and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** DiFrancesco distinguishes ordinary sentencing determinations from final acquittals of offenses. Bullington and Rumsey preserve acquittal-like protection for their capital adjudications; the Court does not erase that exception or make a statutory label conclusive. The present allegation concerns the serious character of an existing conviction as a basis for punishment of the current drug offense. Renewed proof neither prosecutes the old assault again nor reopens the current drug verdicts.

The strongest contrary argument is that the generic conviction does not establish the required personal use or infliction, and that this additional fact authorizes a higher maximum after a completed criminal-burden hearing. The Court accepts those premises. It nevertheless classifies this inquiry into the prior conviction's qualifying character as noncapital recidivist sentencing, not a new offense adjudication. California's jury, confrontation, evidence and reasonable-doubt safeguards govern the proceeding; their provision does not itself create federal acquittal finality. Legislative characterization alone could not remove protection from an actual offense.

Caspari left these direct-review merits open. Almendarez-Torres construed the federal reentry statute to make its prior conviction an aggravated-offense element; it adopted neither a general constitutional recidivism exception nor a general maximum-increase rule. Its statutory construction does not decide California's different classification. Witte and Watts address sentencing within conviction-authorized punishment and do not independently answer the increased-ceiling question here. The Court therefore decides this particular classification directly rather than treating those cases as foreclosing the argument.

The holding supplies no comprehensive classification of all maximum-increasing facts, mandatory minima, unchanged-range findings or prior-conviction procedures. It decides no independent indictment, jury or due-process claim, changes no actual jury waiver, and permits no relabeling of an actual offense to evade jeopardy.

**Precedent treatment:** DiFrancesco's sentencing/acquittal distinction is applied; Bullington and Rumsey retain their capital protection; Caspari's merits reservation is answered for this case; Almendarez-Torres, Witte and Watts remain within their actual statutory and sentencing limits.

#### II. Permission to prove the allegation again neither repairs the first record nor directs an enhancement

**Holding and operative rule:** The California judgment permitting another proof proceeding is affirmed because the disputed adjudication does not receive offense-acquittal protection, not because the State's first evidence was sufficient or its failure excusable. Any renewed proceeding must satisfy applicable law and safeguards; no true finding, automatic doubling, aggregate sentence, or invalidation of the independent prior-prison-term enhancement or drug convictions follows from this judgment.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion; the same five Justices join the complete remedy and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The State conceded that the original prison record failed to prove personal weapon use or personal infliction of great bodily injury beyond a reasonable doubt. A valid generic assault conviction remains distinct from the unproved serious-felony qualification. The Court makes no new finding about the assault and does not treat the prosecutor's assertion as evidence.

Burks, as applied in Hinds, bars a second prosecution opportunity to supply missing proof after actual insufficiency in a constitutionally protected offense adjudication. That consequence is mandatory when its predicate exists. The present judgment rests on nonattachment to this recidivist-status determination; it creates no trial-error exception, proof-repair permission after an offense acquittal, or authority to reopen a protected capital acquittal. Watts's different-burden evidentiary-use rule supplies no such permission either.

The State may attempt renewed proof only under governing state law. The Court neither requires it to do so nor predicts success, and it does not decide whether another independent defense or sentencing constraint limits that course. Count one's seven-year unenhanced maximum is not a fixed lawful aggregate sentence; the selected term, doubled term, independent year, stayed count and concurrent count retain their distinct treatment. Those matters remain for lawful proceedings below.

**Precedent treatment:** Burks and Hinds retain their mandatory finality consequence once acquittal protection attaches; no exception to those holdings is adopted. Dixon's retained Grady rule remains unchanged and does not independently establish the attachment disputed here.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, dissenting.** The additional personal-conduct fact was not established by the old conviction and was indispensable to authority for the greater punishment maximum. McMillan's unchanged ceiling and Witte's and Watts's ordinary sentencing uses do not answer this case. Neither is a general medical-liability rule or a finding about these appellants.

The favorable account assumes actual appreciation of a concrete danger and culpable contribution to its continuation. It assumes neither bad faith, desire to harm Camilo-Robles nor conscious choice to violate federal law. The defense's strongest alternatives—reasonable medical judgment, sound expert reliance, limited authority, inadequate personal information or absent causation—remain materially different supported accounts; they cannot replace the assumptions on this legal appeal. Conversely, warnings or professional imperfection do not prove the claimant's required account.

Gutierrez and Febus expressly retain actual or constructive notice in their supervisory formulations. Today's actual-information ground neither rewrites them into an exclusive subjective-awareness rule nor decides the full constructive-notice theory. Notice, constitutional culpability and affirmative causation remain separate. Farmer, Doe, Chipman, DeCarlo, Ringuette, Smith and Diamondstone illuminate present analysis at their own scopes; they do not create preconduct notice or a general duty to prevent every injury. No new medical checklist, compulsory report-reading duty, general rescue duty, independent substantive-due-process holding or separate station-house constitutional rule is adopted.

**Precedent treatment:** Harlow/Anderson remains controlling; Graham identifies the supported constitutional source; the two earlier circuit authorities inform particularized notice without becoming a nationwide test; later decisions preserve their setting and timing limits.

### IV.A. Hoyos's legal immunity request fails on his own assumed information and effective clearance

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming federal eligibility, Hoyos cannot obtain qualified-immunity summary judgment on the favorable account that his February 28 recertification culpably disregarded violent danger he personally appreciated and effectively contributed to the officer's unrestricted return and this injury. His advisory role and lack of legal rearming power do not categorically sever causation, but personal knowledge, culpability and the clearance's contribution must be proved.

**Authority:** Part IV.A of Scalia's opinion; all eight Associates join the complete application. Stone-Zsela supports the denial on his separate proposed defense and the shared individual-responsibility limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Hoyos allegedly knew the violence and September shootings and had received Sedra's warning before the February clearance. His acknowledgment that certification would most probably produce rearming and the reported routine reliance permit the assumed effective contribution; they do not establish it conclusively. Gutierrez's personal causal-link rule prevents automatic exoneration because advice precedes another official's decision, while its culpability requirement excludes ordinary medical error. Hoyos did not read Calderon. Rivera's reading is not imputed to him, and nonreading or departure from an interview practice alone does not establish a constitutional wrong. The assumed knowing, culpable clearance, not diagnosis or negligence, defeats the present legal request.

**Precedent treatment:** Gutierrez's causal-link principle and Harlow/Anderson's particularized inquiry are applied to the assumed professional role without a new certification-liability or report-reading rule.

### IV.B. Rivera-Gonzalez's actual reading and professional decision require separate treatment

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming eligibility, Rivera-Gonzalez cannot obtain qualified-immunity summary judgment on the favorable account of his own informed, culpable February clearance and affirmative contribution to the resulting unlawful coercion. The claimant must establish his particular awareness, culpable response and causal influence; neither a shared defense nor association with Hoyos establishes those elements.

**Authority:** Part IV.B; all eight Associates adopt the full application. Stone-Zsela supports this denial on his separate ground and the shared individual limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Unlike Hoyos, Rivera read Calderon and judged it insufficient to require investigation. That fact joins his own warning history and alleged informed clearance; it cannot be transformed into Hoyos's nonreading. A disagreement with Rivera's assessment is not itself recklessness. The legal denial depends on the assumed actual appreciation and culpably ineffective response, with effective advice contributing to this injury. Gutierrez supplies the personal-causation principle, and Febus preserves the need for a real link rather than an inference from status. Ordinary professional judgment and a materially different supported account remain available.

**Precedent treatment:** The same particularized framework is applied individually; Gutierrez and Febus supply neither collective knowledge nor liability from professional disagreement.

### IV.C. Diaz-Pagan's warning and absence do not erase a supported earlier causal omission

**Holding and operative rule:** Diaz-Pagan's qualified-immunity denial stands on the favorable account that, despite his own appreciation of the particular danger and effective protective authority, his culpable earlier omissions contributed to the unlawful encounter. Neither his limited immediate command nor vacation on the injury date automatically defeats that account; his actual warning, authority, opportunity and causal contribution must be assessed separately.

**Authority:** Part IV.C; all eight Associates join the full application, while Stone-Zsela supports the denial and shared personal limits on his separate ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Diaz-Pagan allegedly read Calderon, knew the violence and threats, and could affect assignments or protective steps. He warned Santiago; that is an actual response, not a fact to omit. The asserted remaining omissions include acquiescence in known rearming, failure to alert operational staff and departure on April 18 without warning his substitute. Gutierrez permits responsibility for a culpable omission within effective authority and opportunity even without physical participation in the assault. Febus forbids speculative linkage. The account therefore requires proof of an earlier effective omission, not liability from title, absence or a failure to prevent every injury.

**Precedent treatment:** The personal-authority and affirmative-link limits of Gutierrez and Febus govern; no general continuing duty or liability from vacation is created.

### IV.D. Santiago-Gonzalez's own assignment authority supplies the alleged causal role

**Holding and operative rule:** Santiago-Gonzalez's qualified-immunity denial stands on the favorable account that he knowingly and culpably retained the officer in public-contact duties despite his own concrete danger information and effective assignment discretion, with an affirmative connection to the injury. His superior rank alone supplies neither another official's knowledge nor causation.

**Authority:** Part IV.D; the eight Associates adopt the complete application. Stone-Zsela supports the denial and personal-attribution limits on his separate ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Santiago allegedly received Calderon, personally handled the shootings' aftermath, and could direct desk or administrative assignments. Those own-information and operational-power allegations differ from mere organizational proximity. Gutierrez's effective-assignment example supplies relevant earlier notice; Febus requires culpability and a causal link even where a supervisory omission is asserted. The claimant must establish a culpable deployment decision or omission that contributed to this encounter. A different supported account of information, authority, reasonable response or causation remains open; the Court finds none of those facts proved.

**Precedent treatment:** The earlier assignment-authority and causal-link principles are applied at Santiago's own alleged role, without vicarious responsibility for Diaz-Pagan or the arresting officer.

### IV.E. Toledo-Davila's reliance on the clearance is relevant but not conclusive

**Holding and operative rule:** Toledo-Davila's qualified-immunity denial stands on the favorable account that, with his own appreciation of the violent danger and effective rearming authority, his culpable automatic implementation of the clearance affirmatively contributed to the unlawful coercion. Reliance on experts or ordinary procedure is no categorical defense, but reasonable reliance on a sound evaluation remains materially different and no breach of a state regulation alone defeats federal immunity.

**Authority:** Part IV.E; the eight Associates join the full application. Stone-Zsela supports the denial and the individual information, authority and causal limits on his separate ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Toledo allegedly knew the violent history and the open shooting investigation, had sole legal power to rearm, and treated the clearance as automatic. His own act, rather than the superintendent's title, supplies the asserted connection. The reported regulation identifies authority; Davis prevents turning its breach alone into forfeiture of federal immunity. Expert reliance may be reasonable, but cannot settle this appeal by replacing the assumed culpable account. Because this injury involved seizure and beating rather than a gunshot, the connection between rearming and this coercion must still be proved. Neither possession of a weapon nor office conclusively supplies it.

**Precedent treatment:** Gutierrez and Febus require actual personal linkage; Davis keeps state compliance and the clearly established federal right distinct; Harlow/Anderson continues to protect supported reasonable error.

**Authority:** Part V's complete framework is joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela also joins the ordinary-proceedings, personal-proof and no-automatic-liability-or-trial limits, without adopting every retained-defense premise.

**Controlling explanation:** Hunter preserves judicial resolution under ordinary summary-judgment constraints; Johnson and Behrens preserve the distinction between a different supported legal issue and repetitive or factual-sufficiency review. Denial of this legal request neither determines what happened nor requires a trial regardless of remaining defenses.

Elder requires relevant law for each challenged act, not one undifferentiated notice date. Gutierrez's amended opinion was available from August 17, 1989; no precise 1989 duty is inferred where the event's day is unknown. ## Holdings

### I. The actual appeal sought factual selection rather than a separable legal immunity decision

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit properly dismissed this interlocutory appeal because the arguments actually pursued required choosing disputed defense evidence over the plaintiff's sworn account rather than deciding a separable legal immunity question on the proper assumptions. An otherwise authorized legal issue accepting those assumptions remains reviewable; neither the presence of factual disputes nor a prior appeal categorically defeats jurisdiction, and this first mandate expressly left proper immunity presentation open.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins to the complete rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** Johnson excludes an immediate appeal that asks whether the evidence supports the plaintiff's factual account. Doe, Behrens, Smith and Camilo preserve legal review on the proper assumed facts without choosing truth. Section 1254 does not enlarge the Court of Appeals' §1291 authority. If necessary assumptions are unstated, a reviewing court may identify them from material already before the District Court without reweighing evidence, or seek clarification when they cannot responsibly be identified.

Here the defendants relied on disputed treatment records, Mukherjee's affidavit despite Berryman's denial that he examined him, their own denials and later treatment by nonparties. Those contentions seek factual selection. Later care by others does not itself settle what these defendants earlier did. The conclusion rests on the affirmative judicial account of the actual arguments, not on an inference that the unrecovered three-issue letter was absent, waived or contained no legal issue. No hypothetical unidentified legal contention is rejected on its merits.

The preliminary Clerk's order withheld dismissal only at that time; it did not finally establish jurisdiction. Nor did the 1995 mandate decide immunity or prohibit its proper presentation. It identified factual conflicts and expressly reserved that defense. Behrens supplies no numerical one-appeal limit, while retaining the bar on repeated unchanged legal rulings and matters actually resolved by a mandate. The later factual appeal cannot become lawful merely because the motion itself was permitted.

**Precedent treatment:** Johnson, Doe, Behrens, Smith and Camilo apply the legal/factual review boundary without a categorical disputed-facts rule or an invented earlier immunity adjudication.

### II. Section 1912 did not authorize the award after the circuit dismissed the appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1912 permits a discretionary award to the prevailing party of just damages for delay and single or double costs when the Supreme Court or a court of appeals affirms a judgment. Because the Sixth Circuit dismissed this appeal rather than affirming a judgment, that provision did not authorize its double-cost and attorney-fee award; the award and its derivative amount-setting entitlement are vacated.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion; all nine Justices join the statutory rule and this disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute's affirmed-judgment condition limits the occasion for its discretionary remedy. The panel's account of unnecessary protraction may bear on a different sanctions inquiry, but it cannot replace the missing statutory trigger. Judicial discretion operates within the authority Congress supplied.

The strongest argument for preserving the award is that repeated factual contentions imposed an avoidable appellate burden. That does not establish authority under §1912 after a dismissal. Nor does this Court's present affirmance of the jurisdictional dismissal retroactively turn the circuit's earlier action into the affirmance on which its award depended. The Court does not decide the full measure of recoverable damages when §1912 actually applies and does not itself impose a new award.

**Precedent treatment:** Section 1912 is applied according to its affirmed-judgment condition; Cooter & Gell's source-specific treatment of appellate sanctions does not permit discretion to cure an unavailable source of authority.

### III. Sanctions may be reconsidered only under independently valid authority and process

**Holding and operative rule:** On remand the Sixth Circuit may, without being required to do so, reconsider sanctions under independently valid authority and its necessary conditions. Rule 38 permits an award to the appellee of just damages, including appropriate appellate attorney fees, and single or double costs only upon a determination that the appeal is frivolous, after a separately filed motion or notice from the court and a reasonable opportunity to respond; an amount-setting opportunity after an award is imposed does not by itself supply an earlier opportunity to contest entitlement.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion, joined in full by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins to the limited remand and every stated condition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Rule 38 amendment effective December 1, 1994 requires sanctions-specific process before imposition. A request embedded in a brief is not a separately filed motion; a jurisdictional show-cause order is not automatically sanctions notice. Roadway Express requires fair notice and opportunity before fee sanctions. But the reported account does not establish that no other sufficient motion, notice or opportunity occurred. Vacatur rests on the §1912 defect, not an invented absence of process.

The circuit may examine its actual record and provide any required process before exercising discretion. It must assess the appeal actually pursued, distinguish permitted immunity presentation from factual relitigation, and address the procedural sequence, including preliminary permission to continue. That permission is neither a permanent jurisdictional ruling nor automatic protection from sanctions. Dismissal alone does not establish frivolousness. No new evidentiary hearing is mandatory if a sufficient opportunity to respond can otherwise be afforded.

Inherent power is not automatically substituted for §1912. A fee award on that ground must independently satisfy bad-faith and fair-process requirements and the governing limits of Roadway Express and Chambers; the Court finds no such predicates established and changes no broader inherent-power rule. Section 1927 addresses counsel and does not itself validate this award against defendants. Ordinary Rule 39 costs on dismissal are distinct from double costs and attorney fees. Cooter & Gell preserves the separate appellate source of sanctions authority. Possible equivalent relief under another source does not validate an unexercised alternative power. No sanction, amount or counsel liability is imposed or directed.

**Precedent treatment:** Rule 38's operative conditions and Roadway Express's process requirements govern possible reconsideration; Cooter & Gell preserves source-specific authority, and Chambers retains its existing limits without automatic substitution or enlargement.

Assistant Superintendent Althea Jenkins initially placed Brewster at salary Step 15. Her December 2 and 7 memoranda identified deficient teaching-experience verification and a proposed Step 4 placement, invited response and preceded the December 13 Board action. Brewster and his representative met Jenkins December 15; she considered his administrative-experience argument and proposed comparator before confirming the reduction January 9, 1995. Initial entitlement and retrospective withholding are assumed. Exact withholding dates and amounts are not established.

The Court reverses Furuno's personal speech-immunity award, vacates the common speech-immunity ground for the other individuals, affirms personal salary immunity for prospective reduction and reported retrospective recovery, and remands within the stated limits.

## Holdings

### I. The separable legal immunity grounds are reviewable on the proper assumptions

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court may review the circuit's legal immunity judgment under §1254(1) on the facts properly assumed for the interlocutory appeal; it may not choose between disputed accounts or decide evidentiary sufficiency. Disputed facts do not categorically foreclose an otherwise authorized legal appeal, and an unadjudicated personal-evidence fallback cannot be resolved by relabeling it a legal defense.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this review boundary and its present application.

**Controlling explanation:** Mitchell preserves immediate review of a separable legal immunity question. Johnson, Doe and Behrens distinguish that question from the sufficiency or truth of an account of personal conduct. Smith and Camilo apply the boundary to proper assumed facts; Berryman turns on the factual arguments actually pursued. Section 1254 does not enlarge the circuit's §1291 authority.

This appeal presents legal protection even on Brewster's assumed account and therefore differs from Berryman's factual appeal. The Court accepts the appeal-specific Furuno motive concession, the absence of a same-decision assertion, the assumed salary interest and reported withholding. It does not accept the disputed performance account, absence of recoupment or another person's motive as established alternatives.

The circuit did not reach personal-evidence sufficiency for Jenkins, Clarke and the Board members on speech, or for Furuno, Clarke and the Board members on salary. The Court makes no such findings and invents no separately pending factual appeal to dismiss. A reviewing court may identify necessary assumptions from the existing record without selecting truth, or may seek clarification if those assumptions cannot responsibly be identified. Missing originals establish no absent evidence, waiver or default.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Mitchell preserves legal immunity review.
- Johnson excludes factual-sufficiency review.
- Doe applies that exclusion.
- Behrens preserves authorized assumed-fact review.
- Smith applies it to the concrete account.
- Camilo preserves defendant-specific assumptions.
- Berryman is distinguished by the actual factual arguments there.

### II. Contextual balancing neither grants nor defeats immunity automatically

**Holding and operative rule:** Personal damages immunity remains governed by Harlow and Anderson: all relevant preconduct law must make the particular assumed conduct's federal unlawfulness sufficiently apparent, without an identical-facts prerequisite. The presence of a contextual constitutional balance neither automatically establishes reasonable legal uncertainty nor automatically defeats the defense; each claim, actor and challenged act requires its own notice inquiry.

**Authority:** Part II has all eight Associate Justices for the complete rule. Stone-Zsela joins only the proposition that contextual balancing supplies no automatic immunity, making that proposition unanimous.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow preserves an objective defense and Anderson requires particularity. Elder requires all relevant authority on the preserved issue. Lanier and Smith confirm that an obvious application can suffice without a factual twin; their later dates cannot establish notice in 1994 or early 1995. Crawford-El changed neither the immunity framework nor the constitutional elements.

Pickering and Mathews require contextual judgment, but that fact cannot end the inquiry. Conversely, rejecting a general balancing-immunity formulation does not dispose of a sufficient case-specific ground. The Ninth Circuit examined the concrete employment relationship and independent salary-process alternatives; it did not simply declare all balancing law unclear.

December 1994 salary actions, January 1995 confirmation and recommendation, and any individually supported later action retain separate dates. Waters preceded those acts but supplies only its express majority propositions, not a complete investigation code. NTEU, decided February 22, 1995, supplies no December or January notice and its broad prospective-restraint burden does not automatically govern a later individual action. Jeffries, Gilbert, Lanier, Smith, DeCarlo, Ringuette, Camilo and Berryman govern present review within their actual scope, not earlier notice. The eventual June 30 termination does not move every challenged act to that date.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Harlow is retained.
- Anderson requires particularized notice.
- Elder preserves relevant-law review.
- Lanier rejects an exclusive factual-twin route without retrospective notice.
- Smith applies that method.
- Crawford-El preserves the existing defense.
- Waters remains limited to express majority propositions.
- NTEU remains bounded by its date and prospective-restraint setting.

### III. The assumed attendance-speech account defeats the common legal immunity ground

**Holding and operative rule:** On the assumed public-concern account, Furuno cannot receive personal damages immunity for nonrenewal motivated by Brewster's internal attendance-error report affecting school funding, where the reported minor coworker friction and general need for trust identify no materially different operational impairment; Pickering, Givhan and Connick made that particular retaliation sufficiently clearly unlawful when he recommended nonrenewal. His award is reversed on that legal ground, and the same common ground for Jenkins, Clarke and each individual Board member is vacated without deciding an unexamined personal defense or imputing Furuno's motive or participation.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Breyer, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Those five adopt the complete assumed-account protection and conduct-date clarity conclusions and both remedies. Stone-Zsela separately supports setting aside Furuno's award through vacatur and joins only the other-individual vacatur judgment, beyond his stated limited joins elsewhere.

**Controlling explanation:** Pickering protects substantial teacher speech about public-school finances while reserving materially different close relationships and operational circumstances. Givhan protects private teacher-to-principal communication while preserving manner, time and place. Connick permits reasonable anticipation of disruption and gives genuine close working relationships weight, but requires greater justification for substantial public concern. McKinley corroborates the difference between genuine operational concerns and criticism itself without transferring its distinct facts.

The strongest immunity argument is that this acknowledged daily relationship and reported distrust leave room for reasonable preventive judgment. Those considerations matter, but on this account they identify no special operational incompatibility beyond the error report and minor friction. An inference from criticism alone cannot supply the missing impairment. The holding requires no realized injury before an employer acts and protects neither every internal complaint nor every allegation about finances. The limited audience is not independently disqualifying. Later audit disproof establishes neither knowing or reckless falsity nor an earlier lawful motive. No fraud accusation made to Clarke is imputed to Furuno.

The appeal-specific motive concession and absence of a same-decision claim preclude replacing this account with established poor-performance causation. They do not decide ultimate facts. Waters requires genuine reliance on legally disciplinable speech; its limited rule and Jeffries's present role-sensitive approach supply no automatic forecast defense or retrospective notice. NTEU's entire prospective-ban burden is not imported. Each other defendant retains proper individual legal grounds and ordinary personal-proof requirements.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Pickering is applied with its close-relationship limits.
- Givhan preserves private speech and manner/time/place.
- Connick preserves preventive judgment with greater justification for substantial public concern.
- McKinley supplies corroborating preconduct circuit guidance.
- Waters preserves genuine lawful grounds at its limited majority scope.
- Jeffries informs present application without earlier notice.
- Mt. Healthy preserves actual cause and the distinct same-decision defense.

### IV. The actual salary process supports personal immunity for reduction and reported recovery

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming Brewster's protected initial salary entitlement and actual retrospective withholding, the individual officials receive personal damages immunity for the prospective reduction and the reported recovery implementing the same placement determination because preconduct law did not make insufficiency of this concrete notice-and-response sequence sufficiently clear. This ground affirms the personal immunity judgment without deciding complete constitutional adequacy, the correct salary classification, the validity of every collection method or the first date of wage loss.

**Authority:** Part IV is joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela would vacate and remand both salary implementations and joins neither this disposition nor this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathews weighs the actual private loss, error risk and public interest. Loudermill requires meaningful notice, an explanation of the employer's evidence and an opportunity to answer, with adequate later process; no trial-like advance hearing is invariably required. Mallen and Barry preserve justified provisional action and subsequent review. They do not make ordinary fiscal concern an emergency. Gilbert's later felony-suspension holding neither supplies earlier notice nor approves all salary withholding.

December 2 disclosed three verified teaching years, sought further verification by December 9 and warned of December 13 Board action. December 7 identified Step 4. Both invited contact. At the December 15 meeting, attended with representative Wiley Jones, Brewster presented administrative experience and Rosemary Carrillo as a comparator. Jenkins examined those records before her January 9 answer. Meeting promptness and Jenkins's impartiality were not challenged.

The substantial objection is that verification notices may fail to explain a classification dispute or warn of recovery from earned wages. A contract, meeting or possible restoration does not erase that objection. But these actual opportunities, followed by substantive comparator review, distinguish Sanchez's complete absence of any opportunity to be heard and leave an additional procedural requirement insufficiently clear for personal damages, including the reported recovery under the same determination.

The Court adopts neither fiscal urgency as an independent merits ground nor January 9 as the necessary first deprivation. Even if December 13 began the deprivation, the earlier memoranda and response opportunity remain a substantial alternative supporting this limited immunity conclusion. Unknown payroll timing prevents universal merits approval, not this ground. The sixty-day verification clause proves no timely compliance, blanket recovery right or waiver; neither it nor §45028 is finally construed. Later restoration cannot retrospectively cure missing process.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Mathews supplies contextual process analysis.
- Loudermill preserves meaningful response and adequate later process.
- Mallen permits only supported provisional urgency.
- Barry requires effective subsequent review.
- Sanchez is distinguished by the actual opportunities here.
- Gilbert preserves the initial/continuing distinction without retrospective notice.
- Harlow supplies the retained personal-immunity defense.
- Anderson supplies the particularized notice requirement for this sufficient immunity ground.

### V. Personal proof, claim boundaries and ordinary proceedings remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The speech remand requires separate treatment of each defendant's supported conduct, information, authority and causal contribution, with ordinary proof and independent defenses; the salary personal-immunity affirmance remains in force. Failure of immunity establishes no constitutional violation, causation, damages, supervisory liability or right to trial, and personal immunity decides no entity or official-capacity entitlement or excluded claim.

**Authority:** Part V has all eight Associate Justices for its complete proof, capacity and remedial limits; the five Part III joiners control their application to the speech remand. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the stated no-automatic-violation, causation, damages, supervisory-liability or trial proposition, which is unanimous.

**Controlling explanation:** Crawford-El preserves ordinary competent direct and circumstantial proof and the ordinary preponderance standard. Under Mt. Healthy, the claimant must establish protected speech and its substantial or motivating causal role; a defendant may prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. A conceivable lawful explanation is not that showing. The appeal-specific concession decides this legal appeal without becoming ultimate proof for every defendant.

Jenkins's employment notice does not supply Furuno's motive, and her salary-review role does not establish his salary participation. Clarke's receipt of a different allegation proves neither transmission to Furuno nor participation in attendance-speech retaliation. Her reported union-only attribution does not revive that excluded claim. Each Board member's approval or membership supplies neither another person's information nor a personal causal finding. Jeffries and Camilo preserve those distinctions; DeCarlo keeps entity attribution and personal defenses separate.

Ordinary proceedings may resolve actual facts, remaining elements and properly available individual defenses. A materially different supported account may permit lawful immunity reconsideration, subject to Behrens's settled-mandate and unchanged-legal-ruling limits. No personal-evidence fallback is decided here. The salary ruling does not restore the District Court's contrary denial. No compulsory trial, discovery or hearing, employment restoration, repayment or damages follows, and the excluded union, state and failure-to-train judgments remain undisturbed.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Mt. Healthy retains its actual same-decision burden.
- Jeffries preserves personal cause and ordinary remand.
- Camilo preserves individual knowledge, authority and causation.
- DeCarlo separates capacities.
- Behrens permits only otherwise authorized reconsideration without repetitive appeals of unchanged rulings.

## Law After Decision

A balancing test supplies neither automatic immunity nor automatic defeat of immunity. The Court applies retained conduct-time immunity separately: the particular assumed attendance-error retaliation defeats Furuno's legal defense and the common speech ground, while the actual salary notice-and-response sequence supports personal immunity for both prospective reduction and reported recovery.

The speech rule does not require actual operational damage, immunize all internal financial allegations or convert the appeal's assumptions into final liability findings. Salary immunity establishes neither complete process sufficiency nor a lawful classification or recovery entitlement. Personal roles, proper capacities, excluded claims and ordinary proof remain separate. Each controlling proposition has a direct majority at its stated scope; the five-vote Furuno reversal is not enlarged by Stone-Zsela's distinct vacatur vote or a Marks synthesis.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela would vacate and remand the immunity award on both claims for separate evaluation. He joins the legal-review boundary, rejects automatic protection merely because Pickering or Mathews balances factors, and agrees that failure of immunity proves no constitutional violation, causal contribution, damages, supervisory liability or entitlement to trial. His vote to vacate Furuno's award remains distinct from the Court's reversal; he agrees with the other-individual speech vacatur without joining the full application.

He proposes placing the justificatory burden on the official under a contextual reasonable-mistake defense, rather than retaining a duplicative excuse for deliberate constitutional violations. That is a proposed change; Crawford-El did not replace Harlow and Anderson. His proposal identifies no new proof quantum, mandatory factor list or finding that an official knowingly violated federal law. A defensible understanding depends on the actual governing law, responsibilities, knowledge and decision; contextual balancing alone supplies none.

On speech he would identify the actual communication, employment action, protected interest and supported operational justification. On salary he gives weight to the memoranda, experience-verification opportunity, meeting and comparator review because they bear on whether error could be corrected through adequate process; mistaken classification alone proves no denial of process. He nevertheless would return that claim for evaluation under his proposed approach, rather than affirm on the Court's retained-defense ground. One arguable salary interpretation cannot protect unrelated retaliatory nonrenewal. His position neither treats the actual notices as nonexistent nor rests on a finding that the circuit considered no concrete alternative. He directs no liability finding or compulsory trial on either claim.

Justice O'Connor, joined by Scalia and Thomas, agrees with legal review, the retained immunity framework, salary immunity and the individual/procedural limits, but would affirm the personal speech-immunity disposition for every individual. She does not decide that the nonrenewal was constitutionally permissible.

Pickering distinguished remote criticism from materially consequential close working relationships; Givhan protected internal speech while retaining manner, time and place; Connick allowed reasonable preventive management where trust matters. The conceded daily teacher–principal relationship and reported coworker distrust leave sufficiently genuine legal uncertainty for personal damages protection, even though the financial subject is substantial and the employee may prevail on the ultimate constitutional balance. Rankin's different, predominantly clerical role does not remove that uncertainty.

The dissent accepts that minor friction, hurt feelings or the existence of balancing cannot independently grant immunity. It also accepts private-speech protection, the public-concern assumption, the appeal's actual-cause limits and the absence of a same-decision assertion. Its disagreement is the particular conduct-time clarity conclusion. It neither imports a fraud accusation to Furuno, treats later disproof as knowing falsity, finds actual operational breakdown, establishes poor performance as an independent cause, nor relies on later cases as prior notice. Waters and her Jeffries position support attention to genuine institutional interests without making a forecast conclusive. No heightened proof rule or immunity replacement follows.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for proceedings consistent with the component judgment. Furuno's immunity award on the particular attendance-speech legal ground is reversed, leaving denial of that defense effective on the assumed account. The common speech-immunity disposition for Jenkins, Clarke and each named individual Board member is vacated; proper individual legal defenses and ordinary factual treatment remain available. The Court does not decide their unaddressed personal-evidence fallback or assign Furuno's role to them.

The circuit's personal salary-immunity disposition remains in effect for every individual on the common ground, covering both prospective reduction and reported retrospective recovery. Jenkins's actual process role does not establish the participation of Furuno, Clarke or any member. No wage calculation, withholding date, repayment or entitlement ruling follows. Actual surviving entity, official-capacity or equitable matters retain their own scope; none is created by this decision, and the excluded union-speech, state and failure-to-train matters are not revived.

No ultimate liability, damages, reinstatement, restoration of salary, costs, compulsory trial, discovery or hearing is ordered. Ordinary proceedings and genuinely supported independent defenses remain, subject to the settled mandate and rules for authorized review. Supreme Court review is complete; no automatic return date or retained jurisdiction is established.
