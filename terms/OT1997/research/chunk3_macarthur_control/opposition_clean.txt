STATEMENT
1. The Privacy Act of 1974, 5 U.S.C. 552a, regu-
lates the collection, maintenance, use, and dissemina-
tion of personal information by federal government
agencies. The Act provides, inter alia, that individu-
als may seek access to and amendment of records
pertaining to them. At issue in this case is
subsection (e)(7) of the Act, which states that an
agency shall “maintain no record describing how any
individual exercises rights guaranteed by the First
Amendment unless expressly authorized by statute
or by the individual about whom the record is
maintained or unless pertinent to and within the
scope of an authorized law enforcement activity.” 5
U.S.C. 552a(e)(7).?
2. Petitioner Lance Lindblom, then president of
the J. Roderick MacArthur Foundation, filed Free-
dom of Information Act (FOIA) and Privacy Act re-
quests with the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI
or Bureau) headquarters and its New York, Chicago,
and Washington, D.C., field offices for information
maintained about him. C.A. App. 21-26, 40-41. The
FBI located 23 documents responsive to petitioner’s
request. C.A. App. 111, § 19. Those documents in-
cluded a total of 36 pages, of which 13 were duplicates.
Ibid.; see Pet. App. 2a. The Bureau “released re-
dacted copies of several of the documents relating to

ARGUMENT
Petitioner contends that the information in his file,
while properly collected in the first instance, may not
be retained by the FBI unless it is pertinent to a
“current law enforcement activity,” Pet. 12, by which
he appears to mean an active or ongoing investigation.
That claim lacks merit. The decision of the court of
appeals is correct and does not conflict with any
decision of this Court or of any other court of appeals.
Further review is not warranted.

the language of the Privacy Act. Subsection (e)(7) re-
quires only that agency records describing expres-
sive and associational activities protected by the
First Amendment must be pertinent to and within the
scope of an “authorized law enforcement activity.”
