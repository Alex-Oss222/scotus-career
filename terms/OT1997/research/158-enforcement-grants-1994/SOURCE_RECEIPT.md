## Verified source scope

Section 77t(b) permits the SEC, in its discretion, to sue in a United States district court or United States territorial court when a person is engaged or about to engage in acts or practices violating or about to violate the Securities Act provisions or rules. Upon a proper showing, a permanent or temporary injunction or restraining order is to be granted without bond. The remainder of the paragraph concerns discretionary SEC referral to the Attorney General and criminal proceedings.

Section 78u(d)(1) likewise permits the SEC, in its discretion, to bring an injunctive action for the Exchange Act and rule violations at issue here, with a proper showing required and no bond. Its listed courts are the proper United States district court, the District Court for the District of Columbia, and United States courts in a territory or other place subject to United States jurisdiction. Its full text additionally enumerates specified self-regulatory rules and a criminal-referral route; §78u(f) separately limits actions to enforce self-regulatory rules. Those additional categories are not a new ground for this case.

These are express grants to seek injunctive relief. Neither requested paragraph expressly says “disgorgement” or “any equitable relief.” Identification of the grants therefore supports the existing statutory-equity premise without representing the ancillary-restoration construction under *Porter* and *Mitchell* as words in these provisions. The 1994 §78u(d) text contains paragraphs (1)–(3); it contains no paragraph (5). No later §21(d)(5) is used.
