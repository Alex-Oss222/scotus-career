## Historical decisions and every writing

### Atlantic Mutual — April 21, 1998

Scalia wrote for all nine Justices; no separate writing. The Court affirmed the Third Circuit. Industry evidence did not establish a settled technical meaning limiting “reserve strengthening” to changed assumptions or methods. The 1959 and 1984 life-insurance provisions did not establish that limitation either. Under Chevron and Cottage Savings, an ambiguous phrase admitted Treasury's reasonable treatment of net reserve additions, accommodating administration, fairness, and avoidance of manipulation of the exceptional fresh-start deduction. The Court expressly did not resolve the parties' disagreement about how Atlantic's four-claim hypothetical would operate; it considered the hypothetical commercially unrealistic.

Completeness caveat: the regulation has two actual exceptions, not an unspecified carveout. The verified CFR excludes nondiscretionary amounts added from mandatory state/federal assigned-risk-pool losses and specified payments/reserve additions for assumed reinsurance, subject to the hypothetical-reserve ceiling and assumptions. Its timing rule and §845 anti-avoidance provision also survive. Those text passages are in the neutral supplemental file; do not convert the holding into a claim that literally every reserve addition counts.

### Campbell — April 21, 1998

Kennedy wrote Parts I, II, IV, and V for all nine; Part III had Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer joining Kennedy. The judgment reversing and remanding was unanimous. Part III applied Powers's injury/relationship/hindrance framework to the defendant's assertion of excluded prospective grand jurors' equal-protection rights. Louisiana's selection chooses a voting grand juror, unlike merely designating a foreperson from an already constituted federal grand jury. Part IV recognized standing to assert the defendant's own due-process right, while leaving its full merits and remedy unresolved. Part V declined the fair-cross-section issue because appellate presentation was not established.

Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurred in the judgment and all parts except III. He would overrule Powers and also found its rationales particularly inapplicable to this grand-jury selection: no demonstrated injury from another person's exclusion, no voir-dire relationship, and realistic class-wide opportunities for excluded jurors themselves to sue. His footnote 3 expressly accepted own-right due-process standing while doubting eventual merits success. Do not turn that disagreement into opposition to remand or into a holding that discrimination was proved.

### Beach — April 21, 1998

Souter wrote for all nine; no separate writing. The Court affirmed. Section 1635(f) governs the duration of the federal rescission right, not merely the time to sue; expired rescission cannot be revived as federal defensive recoupment. Ordinary recoupment doctrine does not extend a right that Congress extinguished. Section 1640(e)'s express damages-recoupment preservation and the 1995 §1635(i) provisions confirm the distinction. The Court did not decide an independent state-law rescission right; no such Florida claim was before it.

Retain §1635(f)'s earlier-sale language and its specific agency-enforcement extension; the latter's three predicates and exact duration formula appear in footnote 1 and the neutral supplement. Damages recoupment has its own “except as otherwise provided by State law” qualification. Do not conflate that qualification with §1635(i)(3)'s preservation of state-law rescission recoupment.

### Miller — April 22, 1998

The judgment was affirmed 6–3 through three separate two-Justice rationales; no opinion of the Court adopted a single equal-protection framework.

- Stevens, joined by Rehnquist, sustained §1409(a)(4)'s timely formal-parentage condition. Reliable proof, opportunity for a parent-child relationship during minority, and ties to the United States supported it; Lehr supplied an affirmative-act analogy. He accepted consideration of the father's discrimination claim and distinguished confirmation of citizenship from discretionary naturalization. He did not decide the different physical-presence requirements, §1409(a)(1), or (a)(3), and declined the remedial question because he found no violation. The §1409 transitional election and age-21 alternative were material record qualifications.
- O'Connor, joined by Kennedy, denied third-party standing because the father could have pursued an appeal from his dismissal. The daughter's own claim received rational-basis review and failed. The concurrence expressly rejected Stevens's conclusion that the gender distinction survives heightened scrutiny. Thus the two Justices' judgment votes are not heightened-scrutiny merits votes.
- Scalia, joined by Thomas, found judicial relief unavailable: Congress's conditions on foreign-born citizenship could not be bypassed by severance or equitable conferral, particularly given §1421(d), the interlocking statutory provisions, and competing remedial possibilities. Crucially, footnote 1 accepted the daughter's third-party standing under existing precedents, despite disagreement as an original matter; this opinion does not join O'Connor's threshold rationale.
- Ginsburg, joined by Souter and Breyer, dissented. Her historical review traced nationality rules disadvantaging mothers and later rules assuming their caregiving role. Present favorable treatment of mothers still reinforced a sex stereotype where functional distinctions could serve the asserted objectives. She joined Breyer's constitutional analysis.
- Breyer, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, separately dissented. Government-obtained dismissal and venue transfer supplied sufficient hindrance; he also questioned treating ancestry-based discrimination against the child as exempt from heightened scrutiny. Citizenship at birth was distinct from discretionary alien naturalization. §§1409(a)(3)–(4) poorly fit the asserted objectives given parentage proof, DNA testing, and sex-neutral alternatives. He would sever those requirements under the INA's severability clause and recognize citizenship supplied by the remaining statute, distinguishing Pangilinan.

Factual/version caveats: fatherhood and sufficient United States physical presence were not disputed; the Texas decree came after age 21. The published source identifies the former §1401 10-year/5-after-14 requirement and amended 5-year/2-after-14 requirement. Do not treat the newer numbers as automatically the governing birth-date version. The father did not appeal his dismissal. Neither the old age-21 alternative nor the possible nonapplication of the later support requirement may be omitted.

### California v. Deep Sea Research — April 22, 1998

O'Connor wrote for all nine. The Court affirmed federal jurisdiction, vacated the remainder, and remanded. Actual possession matters in this in-rem admiralty setting: California's title assertion without possession did not bar the federal court from adjudicating competing claims. The Davis, The Siren, and related sovereign-vessel cases supplied the possession distinction; Treasure Salvors and the New York cases were read in their procedural/property settings, not as categorical bars to every state title adjudication. Abandonment required reconsideration under admiralty meaning; the Court did not determine abandonment, statutory title, or ASA preemption of California §6313.

Stevens concurred separately, reconsidering his own Treasure Salvors reasoning and favoring the broader historical view that the Eleventh Amendment does not bar in-rem admiralty adjudication. Kennedy, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, joined the Court but reserved reconsideration of the possession/nonpossession distinction; the concurrence did not convert the narrow holding into a permanent general immunity rule. The official ASA extract includes §2105's categories, transfers, exceptions/reservations, and §2106's separate salvage/finds, other-shipwreck-law, and pre-April-28-1988-proceeding provisions.

### Edwards — April 28, 1998

Breyer wrote for all nine; no separate writing. Affirmance rested on Guidelines relevant-conduct sentencing: the sentencing judge could determine kind and amount, and the same conduct would count whether part of the conviction offense or the same course/common scheme. The Court assumed without deciding the proposed cocaine-only interpretation of the general verdict and did not resolve the statutory or constitutional claims. The actual sentences were within the applicable cocaine-only statutory ceilings; no viable same-course objection was made, and the challenged argument was first raised on appeal. Watts and Witte were historical premises requiring comparison with their actual simulated counterparts. The saved Cornell opinion ends after “The judgment of the Court of Appeals is”; its substantive analysis is present, and the complete syllabus expressly reports affirmance. This terminal formatting omission is disclosed rather than filled by a claimed unread reporter page.

### Estate of Romani — April 29, 1998

Stevens wrote with Rehnquist, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The Court affirmed protection of the earlier perfected judgment lien against the later federal tax claim. It harmonized §3713 and the later, specific, comprehensive Tax Lien Act rather than finding an implied repeal. Thelusson was distinguished through its unperfected general lien and Conard's explanation; Key and Emory involved unsecured creditors. The historical broader question of §3713 against all prior secured liens was not comprehensively resolved.

Scalia concurred in the judgment and joined the opinion except its treatment of failed legislative proposals. Bicameralism/presentment, the inability of a later Congress to interpret earlier law authoritatively, and the costs of consulting unenacted legislative history were his substantive grounds. Do not describe him as withholding all reasoning. Government agreement that its tax liens were invalid against the earlier lien under §6323 is distinct from the disputed §3713 federal-choateness/title-or-possession question identified below. The neutral concession supplement deliberately does not declare that latter issue conceded.

### Calderon — April 29, 1998

Kennedy wrote with Rehnquist, O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas; Souter dissented with Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The Court reversed with instructions to reinstate the June 11, 1997 mandate. It accepted a genuine sua-sponte first-petition recall as outside §2244(b)'s literal successive-application prohibition, but limited substantive habeas-merits reopening through traditional finality principles informed by AEDPA. Ordinary clerical correction, fraud on the court, and a mandate stayed pending en-banc consideration were expressly distinguished. The historical rule required avoidance of a habeas miscarriage of justice, with Schlup's standard for innocence of the underlying offense and Sawyer's higher standard for death eligibility. It found the first-petition evidence inadequate under either; later Leitch evidence was not folded into that first-petition record.

Souter accepted the sua-sponte/first-record description and regretted the timing. He nevertheless favored ordinary deferential abuse-of-discretion review: en-banc administration, correction of procedural malfunction, and avoidance of constitutional error in a death case supplied legitimate factors. Whether the recalled merits ruling ultimately was correct differed from whether judges could reasonably think reconsideration necessary. AEDPA did not regulate this genuine first-petition recall, and its successive-application policies did not justify a new actual-innocence prerequisite. His footnotes recognized the limits on prisoner-filed recall motions and analogized discretion over legal-justification judgments to Cooter & Gell.

Objective supplements separate lower procedural chronology, first-petition evidence, and the new July Leitch assertion. Source judicial/executive assessments remain attributed assessments. The Cornell account inconsistently refers to the crushing injury as right wrist and later left wrist; the supplement preserves and flags that discrepancy rather than correcting it by guess. Historical postdivergence Supreme orders are not automatically admitted as simulated Court actions.

It reports argument on April 21 and the Seventh Circuit judgment at 116 F.3d 288. Later explanations of why the writ was dismissed are not date-eligible holdings or source-established reasons.

The lower opinion distinguishes the settled business-search claim, unappealed probable-cause dismissal, and live custody-policy challenge. The civil licensing character must not be recast as a criminal breach-of-peace case.
