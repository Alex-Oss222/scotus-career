# Clinton v. City of New York: factual source extraction

Objective factual and lower-court supplement only. Excludes the historical Supreme Court result, rationale and lineup. Source: official [524 U.S. 417-497](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-417.pdf), fully read including every writing and footnote by control. Do not pass raw source to independent modeling. Fresh neutral validation required. Evidentiary cutoff: June 25, 1998.

* The Line Item Veto Act was enacted April 1996 and effective January 1, 1997. Its cancellation categories are discretionary budget-authority dollars, new direct spending, and limited tax benefits. Under section 691 the President must consider specified legislative-history/purpose information, determine deficit reduction/no impairment of essential government functions/no harm to national interest, and notify Congress by special message within five calendar days excluding Sundays. Cancellation takes effect upon receipt; an enacted disapproval bill makes it null and void. The Act denies cancellation authority over such disapproval bills. Neither challenged cancellation was disapproved by enacted legislation. Both items had been signed into law before cancellation, and compliance with the statutory cancellation procedures was undisputed. (436-437.) These are separate statutory conditions; do not turn a discretion to cancel into a duty to cancel.
* New York's dispute involves Balanced Budget Act section 4722(c), enacted August 5, 1997, and canceled August 11. A 1991 statute reduced federal Medicaid subsidies by specified state provider taxes. HHS notified New York in 1994 that fifteen taxes were covered and that $955 million was then repayable; it allowed waiver applications. New York submitted applications that remained formally undecided. The State estimated exposure through March 1997 as high as $2.6 billion. Section 4722(c) deemed identified pre-June 1, 1997 taxes permissible where the specified waiver had been requested or would otherwise be required and, for requests made, administrative/judicial action was incomplete on July 23, 1997. (422-423; complete statutory quotation in n.2.) Do not assume HHS denied the requests or that the full estimated sum was finally assessed.
* Plaintiffs in the New York action were the City, two hospital associations, one hospital and two employee unions. Under existing New York provisions, adverse HHS action would trigger retroactive provider assessments; NYCHHC's estimated assessment was approximately $4 million for each relevant year. The lower court treated the cancellation as removing statutory protection against that contingent liability. (425-426; 431; relevant New York statutes reproduced at n.10.) The source does not establish that the unions independently suffered a distinct injury.
* Taxpayer Relief Act section 968 amended section 1042 to permit deferral of specified sellers' capital gains on sales to eligible farmers' cooperatives. The described qualifying refiner/processor is a domestic corporation substantially engaged in refining/processing agricultural or horticultural products, purchasing more than half those products in the prior year from the purchasing cooperative or its member farmers. Other qualifying conditions cannot be inferred from this summary; it is not a substitute for the entire tax provision. The provision was expressly designated among limited tax benefits subject to cancellation and was canceled August 11, 1997. (423-425.)
* Snake River is an Idaho cooperative of approximately thirty potato growers formed in May 1997, with acquisition of processing capacity among its purposes. Mike Cranney, a member and officer, also sued. Cranney's declaration reported a May discussion with Idaho Potato Packers' owner about a tax-deferred acquisition if the proposed legislation passed, the owner's expressed interest, and an earlier discussion by Snake River's general manager. Cranney stated that cancellation caused him to terminate the discussions; the declaration also described consideration of other Idaho facilities. (426-427; declaration quoted at 458-459; App. 111-115.) No quoted portion establishes an agreed price, a binding sale, or the seller's refusal after cancellation. The factual extraction deliberately avoids converting discussions into a completed or certain bargain.
* The two actions were consolidated in the District of Columbia. Both sought declarations that the Act and particular cancellations were invalid; neither requested an injunction against the President. Their complaints invoked both the Act's expedited-review clause and federal-question/Declaratory Judgment Act jurisdiction. The district court found at least one plaintiff in each action had Article III standing; it ruled the cancellations inconsistent with bicameralism/presentment and alternatively with separation of powers, 985 F. Supp. 168 (D.D.C. 1998). (425 n.9; 426-428; 455.)
* The government filed both a direct Supreme Court appeal and a protective D.C. Circuit appeal. Section 692 authorizes specified persons to challenge constitutionality in district court, requires prompt service to both Houses with intervention rights, provides a ten-day direct appeal window, and directs expedition. The statutory question whether corporate/governmental appellees are individuals was first raised on appeal. (428-429; 455.) These litigation events do not resolve the statutory route or authorize importing a historical Supreme Court action.
