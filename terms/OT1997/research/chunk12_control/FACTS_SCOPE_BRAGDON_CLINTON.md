## Bragdon v. Abbott, No. 97-156: granted questions

Source: official report, 524 U.S. 624, 648, 654, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-624.pdf . Local source: `USREPORTS-524-624.txt`.

The reported grant included the federal disability issue. For the direct-threat branch, the report reproduces three proposed questions and states that only Question 3 was granted. The following passage is mechanically copied; question numbers are the petition's numbers, and the last sentence identifies grant scope only.

```text
   The petition for certiorari presented three other questions
for review. The questions stated:

         “3. When deciding under title III of the ADA whether
      a private health care provider must perform invasive
      procedures on an infectious patient in his ofﬁce, should
      courts defer to the health care provider’s professional
      judgment, as long as it is reasonable in light of then-
      current medical knowledge?

         “4. What is the proper standard of judicial review
      under title III of the ADA of a private health care pro-
      vider’s judgment that the performance of certain inva-
      sive procedures in his ofﬁce would pose a direct threat
      to the health or safety of others?

         “5. Did petitioner, Randon Bragdon, D. M. D., raise a
      genuine issue of fact for trial as to whether he was war-
      ranted in his judgment that the performance of certain
      invasive procedures on a patient in his ofﬁce would have
      posed a direct threat to the health or safety of others?”
      Pet. for Cert. i.

Of these, we granted certiorari only on question three. 
```

The report at page 654 states that Question 5, whether the petitioner raised a genuine issue for trial, was not granted, and describes the resulting briefs and arguments as not concentrated on whole-record sufficiency in light of every summary-judgment submission. This is a published characterization of the briefing's scope, not independent review of the original briefs. It does not establish that any exhibit was absent, that any party defaulted, that evidentiary sufficiency was resolved, or that no legal consequence can follow from the granted question. The original petition, briefing, and joint appendix were not separately recovered for this supplement.

## Date-eligible jurisdiction and rule texts

The following texts were mechanically copied from official historical sources. The 1997 Code and the rules adopted July 26, 1995, effective October 2, 1995, are used; no later text supplies the rule. The saved full sources permit verification of surrounding qualifications.

### 28 U.S.C. 1254 (1997)

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1997-title28/html/USCODE-1997-title28-partIV-chap81.htm ; local `USCODE-1997-title28-chap81.html` and `.txt`.

```text
§1254. Courts of appeals; certiorari; certified questions






Cases in the courts of appeals may be reviewed by the Supreme Court by the following methods:


(1) By writ of certiorari granted upon the petition of any party to any civil or criminal case, before or after rendition of judgment or decree;


(2) By certification at any time by a court of appeals of any question of law in any civil or criminal case as to which instructions are desired, and upon such certification the Supreme Court may give binding instructions or require the entire record to be sent up for decision of the entire matter in controversy.
```

### Supreme Court Rule 11 (1995 rules)

Source: https://www.supremecourt.gov/ctrules/rules/Rules_1995.pdf ; local `Rules_1995.pdf` and `Rules_1995.txt`. The official PDF title page supplies adoption/effective dates.

```text
Rule 11. Certiorari to a United States Court of Appeals 
Before Judgment 
A petition for a writ of certiorari to review a case pending 
in a United States court of appeals, before judgment is en-
tered in that court, will be granted only upon a showing that 
the case is of such imperative public importance as to justify 
deviation from normal appellate practice and to require im-
mediate determination in this Court. See 28 U. S. C. 
§2101(e).
```

### Supreme Court Rule 18.12 (1995 rules)

The direct-appeal rule separately addresses disposition and consideration of appellate jurisdiction:

```text
12. After considering the documents distributed under 
this Rule, the Court may dispose summarily of the appeal on 
the merits, note probable jurisdiction, or postpone consider-
ation of jurisdiction until a hearing of the case on the merits. 
If not disposed of summarily, the case stands for briefing and 
oral argument on the merits. If consideration of jurisdiction 
is postponed, counsel, at the outset of their briefs and at oral 
argument, shall address the question of jurisdiction. If the 
record has not previously been filed in this Court, the Clerk 
of this Court will request the clerk of the court in possession 
. of the record to certify and transmit it.
```
