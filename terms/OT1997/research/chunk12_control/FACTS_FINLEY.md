# NEA v. Finley: factual source extraction

Objective facts and lower-court proceedings only; historical Supreme Court outcome, rationale and lineup excluded. Source: fully read official [524 U.S. 569-623](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-569.pdf). Every writing/footnote read by control. Raw source is withheld from independent modeling. Fresh neutral validation required. Evidentiary cutoff June 25, 1998.

* Section 954(d) is reproduced exactly, by mechanical copying, in FACTS_STATUTE_FINLEY.md. Keep section 954(d)(1)'s decency/respect considerations distinct from section 954(d)(2)'s separate express prohibition on funding obscenity and consistency with statutory purposes. The factual record does not itself resolve what statutory consideration requires or whether panel diversity satisfies that duty.
* NEA applications first go to advisory panels, then to the National Council on the Arts, which advises the Chairperson. The Chairperson has ultimate grant authority but cannot approve an application the Council recommends against. The 1990 statute separately requires diverse artistic/cultural perspectives, broad geographic/ethnic/minority representation and knowledgeable lay members on panels. (573; sections 955(f), 959(c).)
* In December 1990, the Council adopted a resolution seeking implementation of section 954(d)(1) through diversity of panel composition; the Chairperson made a similar statement. There was no promulgated official interpretation of section 954(d)(1). (577.) This describes the agency's conduct, not the legal adequacy of that implementation.
* Finley, Fleck, Hughes and Miller applied before section 954(d)(1) existed. An expert panel recommended the projects, initially and after reconsidering three applications at the Chairperson's request. The Council later recommended disapproval; denial notices issued in June 1990. The artists sued asserting political denial, failure to use statutory procedures, and Privacy Act injury from disclosure of application excerpts; they sought grant restoration/reconsideration and privacy damages. NAAO joined when they amended the complaint to challenge the new provision. (577-578.) Allegations of political motivation or confidentiality breach are not findings.
* The statutory and as-applied claims were settled with payment of the denied grants, damages and fees. The surviving litigation concerns a facial First/Fifth Amendment challenge to section 954(d)(1), not an adjudication that the four original denials violated the later-enacted standard. Two artists, Hughes and Miller, received subsequent grants in September 1991. (578; 586-587.)
* The district court entered summary judgment for the challengers and enjoined enforcement, 795 F. Supp. 1457 (C.D. Cal. 1992). It held the standard insufficiently definite and facially incompatible with protected speech, rejecting diversity of panels as adequate implementation. No stay was sought, so the challenged clause had not been applied since June 1992. A divided Ninth Circuit affirmed, 100 F.3d 671 (1996), on vagueness and viewpoint discrimination; rehearing en banc was denied with dissent, 112 F.3d 1015 (1997). (578-580.)
* The 1989/1990 appropriations restriction and certification requirement were different enactments/practices. Bella Lewitzky Dance Foundation, 754 F. Supp. 774 (C.D. Cal. 1991), invalidated the earlier certification requirement; NEA did not appeal. Do not merge that ruling with the facial challenge to the permanent 1990 amendment. (574-576.)
* The official recitation records that 1998 appropriations restricted individual grants primarily in favor of eligible organizations and state agencies, with subgrant constraints. Exact exceptions and transition depend on section 329, 111 Stat. 1600, which is not reproduced completely in this opinion. (574.) NAAO asserted representative standing through member organizations; the published recitation identifies its director's declaration about potentially controversial member projects. (617; App. 21-25.)
