APPLICATION FOR STAY OF EXECUTION 


Petitioner, Angel Francisco Breard, by counsel, moves this Court to issue an order 
staying Breard’s execution, which is scheduled for April 14, 1998. 

Breard, a citizen of Paraguay born in Argentina, filed his first, and only, federal 
habeas corpus petition on August 30, 1996, based in part upon Virginia’s violation of the 
Vienna Convention on Consular Relations (the “Vienna Convention” or the “treaty”) in 
connection with his case. Two weeks after Breard’s petition was filed, the Republic of 
Paraguay, :ts Ambassador to the United States and its Consul-General filed a federal 
complaint (denoted hereafter as the Paraguay suit) based upon the same facts as Breard’s 
Vienna Convention claim, seeking declaratory and injunctive relief against several officials of 
the Commonwealth of Virginia to vindicate rights conferred upon Paraguay and the Consul- 
General by the Vienna Convention. After the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed the 
District Court’s dismissal of both cases,’ the Circuit Court for Arlington County set April 14, 
1998, as the date for Breard’s execution. A copy of this order is attached as Exhibit A. 

The plaintiffs in the Paraguay suit filed a Petition for Certiorari in the Court on 


February 24, 1998. Republic of Paraguay v. Gilmore, No. 97-1390. On March 22, 1998 they 


filed an Application for Stay of or Injunction Against Execution pending Disposition of 


Petition for Certiorari. On March 11, 1998, Breard filed a Petition for a Writ of Certiorari in 


the Court. Breard v. Greene, No. 97-8214. On March 23, 1998, Breard filed in the Court of 


Appeals an Application for a Stay of Execution which was denied on Friday, March 27, 1998. 
A copy of the order of the Court of Appeals is attached as Exhibit B. 
Breard asks the Court to stay his scheduled execution to afford it the opportunity to 


consider both his Petition for Certiorari and that filed by Paraguay and its officials in a

A stay of execution should be granted if it is reasonably probable that four members 
of this Court would consider any of the underlying issues sufficiently meritorious for a grant 
of certiorari, there is a significant possibility that the decision of the Court of Appeals will be 
reversed, and irreparable harm is likely to result if the decision is not stayed. Barefoot v 
Estelle, 463 U.S. 880, 895 (1983). Breard satisfies this standard with respect to all of the 
questions presented in his Petition for a Writ of Certiorari 

Breard’s case raises an important issue not yet resolved by this Court concerning the 
unquestioning determination by the District Court and the Court of Appeals that the 
jurisprudential doctrine of procedural default overrides the dictates of the Vienna Convention. 
Breard argues in his Petition for Certiorari that the rights to consular notification and access 
granted by Article 36(1) of the Vienna Convention are not subject to the doctrine of 
procedural default because Article 36(2) of the treaty exempts its provisions from the 
operation of domestic laws that would deprive the consular notification requirement of “full 
effect.” 

As Judge Butzner of the Court of Appeals noted in his concurring opinion in this 
case,” the Convention affects Americans who travel abroad each year and who might be taken 
into custody by officials of another country. If America’s domestic procedural rules bar any 
legal remedy when an alien in this country is convicted and given what some nations deem a 


"barbaric" sentence of death without the knowledge or intervention of his consulate, the

The interpretation and application of Article 36 of the Vienna Convention present 
important questions that it is reasonably probable that four members of this Court will 
consider sufficiently meritorious for a grant of certiorari, and there is a significant possibility 
that the decision of the Court of Appeals will be reversed. This Court ought to be able to 
consider carefully the issues raised both in this case and in Paraguay’s case without the 
looming deadline of Breard’s execution. 

Assuming arguendo that Vienna Connection claims could be procedurally defaulted, 
Breard has asserted that the novelty of the claim provides cause for the default. Mr. Breard’s 
trial counsel, appellate counsel, and state habeas counsel, like nearly all other criminal defense 
and habeas lawyers in the country, reasonably were unaware of the treaty’s consular 
notification provision and would not have discovered it in the course of reasonable and 
diligent investigation. The continuing validity of, and application of, the novelty doctrine 
make it reasonably probable that four members of the Court will consider this issue 
meritorious for a grant of certiorari, and establish a significant possibility that the decision of 


the Court of Appeals will be reversed. See Reed v. Ross, 468 U.S. | (1984) 


Finally, Breard asserts that the Virginia Supreme Court’s method for performing the 
state-mandated proportionality review violates federal due process because it deprived him of 
his life and liberty interest in the procedure prescribed by statute for the review. This case 
gives this Court an opportunity to provide guidance concerning the extent to which the Due 
Process Clause requires compliance with proportionality review statutes. Because of the 
importance of this issue and the number of states employing similar statutes, it is reasonably 


probable that four members of the Court will consider the underlying issue sufficiently

The final factor for the Court to consider in order to grant a stay of execution -- that
