## Common entering-law controls

Use the exact copied holdings and standards in the entering-law slice; the following identifies their work without replacing their text.

- **Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency**, present operation effective *Behrens*, February 21, 1996: a properly authorized appeal can decide a separable legal immunity issue on the relevant assumed facts. It cannot select factual truth or reweigh sufficiency. A prior appeal is not a numerical bar, but no repeated unchanged legal ruling or resolved mandate issue becomes reviewable. *Johnson*, June 12, 1995; *Doe*, October 16, 1995; and current *Smith*, January 12, 1998, preserve the same boundary. Section 1254 review does not enlarge the circuit's §1291 jurisdiction.
- **Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review**, effective *Elder*, February 23, 1994: consider all relevant law on a preserved issue, without creating claims, facts or retrospective notice. Harlow/Anderson remain operative. *Lanier*, March 31, 1997, and *Smith* reject an exclusive factual-twin route; neither establishes conduct-time notice by its later date.
- **Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity**, present operation effective *Crawford-El*, June 16, 1997: ordinary competent direct/circumstantial proof and ordinary preponderance apply to the retaliation motive; no new heightened quantum or categorical derivative discovery bar. Protected activity, personal contribution and actual causation remain necessary. A genuine same-decision showing differs from a conceivable lawful ground. No immunity replacement or automatic trial follows.
- *Fletcher*, June 16, 1997; *DeCarlo*, May 28, 1998; *Ringuette*, June 11, 1998; and *Aponte-Matos*, February 26, 1998, preserve personal role, conduct-time notice, claim/capacity distinctions and the lack of automatic immunity from superior approval or compliance with a state rule. Their current applications do not supply earlier notice.

All dates below distinguish the law available when officials acted from law governing adjudication in 1998. Later lower-court cases quoted in the reported opinions are not retroactive notice merely because the lower opinion cites them. Postdivergence Supreme Court citations in those opinions must be replaced or screened against the supplied in-world holdings.

## Camilo-Robles v. Hoyos

### Corrected review posture and components

The First Circuit's June 29, 1998 judgment **affirmed** the district court's denial of four summary-judgment motions brought by five appellants. The docket span is Nos. 97-2260, 97-2261, 97-2262 and 97-2264. It awarded ordinary appellate costs to the appellee. The Supreme Court review is of that circuit judgment, not an unexplained direct review of a trial-court denial. The appeal preserves both the psychiatrists' claimed Puerto Rico statutory immunity and the five appellants' federal qualified-immunity positions; its federal-contractor eligibility discussion is a distinct issue, not proof that state action automatically confers immunity.

The underlying claims allege unreasonable arrest/detention and police violence under §1983, attributable to the appellants' own culpable participation through evaluations, rearming or deployment. The arrest/force facts implicate the Fourth Amendment as applied to state officials through the Fourteenth. Do not reproduce the lower court's suggestion that the Fifth Amendment constrains Puerto Rico police through the Fourteenth. Whether a genuinely distinct substantive-due-process claim is preserved, and whether any later custody stage requires a different source, must be identified before adopting a separate constitutional holding. *Graham* supplies the arrest-force framework; the abstract right to bodily security alone does not resolve the supervisory notice inquiry.

The district court set its schedule February 21, 1996; summary-judgment motions followed in December. Camilo-Robles opposed the psychiatrists on the merits and sought Rule 56(f) time as to the police officials, citing obstructed discovery. The court granted the extension without a time limit and referred discovery disputes. The magistrate first ruled August 26, 1997; the district judge denied summary judgment two days later in a two-page order before receiving all oppositions. The circuit reconstructed likely plaintiff-favorable assumptions from material already in the trial record, including the joint psychiatric motion, its opposition, other defendants' submissions and earlier evidence. It expressly excluded extra-record material referenced on appeal. Lack of an opposition was neither default nor an evidentiary admission. A clarification remand remained available if the assumptions could not responsibly be identified; factual sufficiency cannot be relabeled a legal issue.

### Supported factual account and individual attribution

On the favorable account, on May 13, 1994, Officer Miguel Diaz-Martinez tried to park in the judges' area at the Bayamon Judicial Center. Security guard Grancid Camilo-Robles told him not to. Diaz-Martinez put a hand on his gun, arrested and handcuffed him, shoved him into the cruiser, punched his stomach and slapped his face while taking him to the station, and detained him after removing belt and shoes. He was released uncharged approximately three hours later. These are summary-judgment assumptions, not a verdict.

Diaz-Martinez's reported history included at least eighteen disciplinary infractions involving violent/threatening conduct; an August 1989 incident in which he assaulted his wife, took a police shotgun, and held officers including the acting superintendent hostage; involuntary hospitalization and a schizophrenia diagnosis; a recommendation for civilian work; suspension in 1990 and expulsion in 1991. Following a successful appeal he was reinstated in May 1993. He assaulted a civilian on desk duty, received unrestricted psychiatric clearance in August 1993, and after rearming shot two unarmed residents on September 8, killing one. His weapon was taken away; he returned to desk work in November. On January 20, 1994 he threatened to kill another officer; he transferred to CIC six days later. On February 28 the psychiatrists again cleared him for unrestricted armed duty. The later August 1994 expulsion, manslaughter plea and sentence are subsequent history, not notice the appellants possessed before Camilo-Robles's injury. Mental illness alone does not establish dangerousness, constitutional culpability or liability; the concrete history and the defendant's actual information do the work.

### Distinct defenses and lawful alternatives

**Puerto Rico statute.** The psychiatrists invoked P.R. Laws Ann. tit. 26, §4105 (1994), which protects a health professional acting as a Commonwealth/dependency/instrumentality/municipal employee in the performance of professional duties against damages litigation for malpractice. Their contract purported to invoke the provision (misnumbering it §5105), while describing them as independent contractors. The lower court identified unresolved employee-status facts and held separately that a state immunity cannot defeat §1983 under *Martinez v. California*, 444 U.S. 277, 284 n.8 (1980). It did **not** decide §4105's effect on pendent Puerto Rico claims. This statutory defense, federal immunity and any state claims must receive separate treatment. No general federal absolute immunity for medical evaluation is established.

**Contractor eligibility.** The lower court treated contractual integration in police evaluations as state action and extended qualified-immunity eligibility. Current *Richardson*, June 23, 1997, separates state attribution, eligibility and a possible independent good-faith defense. Its commercial-prison holding expressly reserves brief assistance, adjunct service, close official supervision and governmental integration. One lawful approach assumes eligibility arguendo and resolves the particular legal immunity question; another reaches preserved eligibility through the actual historical/functional record; a third returns an inadequately supported eligibility issue without declaring either all contractors immune or none immune. The reported record establishes a professional contract and recurring evaluations, but not market competition, full-time commercial prison management or all terms of official supervision. Those features must not be invented. A separate private good-faith defense remains undecided under Richardson and cannot silently replace immunity.

**Culpability and notice.** *Doe* (October 16, 1995) supplies a current, bounded school-authority knowing-acquiescence rule; *Farmer* (June 6, 1994) supplies actual-awareness, permissible circumstantial inference and reasonable-response principles in prison conditions. Neither licenses a general constructive-knowledge supervisory rule, neither retrospectively gives these defendants notice for pre-May 13, 1994 conduct, and neither may be transplanted mechanically. Current *Chipman* reinforces individual awareness and genuine opportunity without adopting a general failure-to-protect rule. The lower opinion's actual-or-constructive formulation must therefore be separated from the actual-knowledge facts it identifies.

The strongest claimant path is a narrow application to each defendant's own admitted warning information, practical authority and causally effective decision or omission, preserving proof and later defenses. The counterargument is that even serious known danger does not establish the exact federal supervisory/professional obligation at the relevant conduct date, that medical judgment or reasonable reliance could be mistaken without deliberate disregard, and that rearming/public deployment must be connected to this seizure and beating. The strongest immunity path is individualized notice or objectively reasonable response on the proper assumptions; its counterargument is that no factual twin is required and an officer's known repeated violence plus direct rearming/deployment powers may make the particular conduct plainly unlawful. A narrower jurisdictional path dismisses a purely factual-sufficiency portion or seeks clarification where assumptions cannot be reconstructed. None permits choosing the defense account, pooling knowledge, finding liability from immunity denial, or commanding a trial regardless of remaining legal defenses.

## Berryman v. Rieger

### Corrected posture, record and preservation

The Sixth Circuit's July 6, 1998 judgment dismissed the defendants' interlocutory appeal for lack of jurisdiction and imposed double costs and attorney fees under §1912, leaving the Clerk to determine amounts after submissions and any necessary hearing as special master. No dollar award is supplied. A remand for ordinary proceedings is not a judgment that the doctors/nurses violated either constitutional provision.

Philip Berryman's September 1992 complaint alleged that a floor buffer knocked him down, injured his head/knees and caused continuing pain. He sued Dr. Dean Rieger, Nurse C. Kukla and Nurse Diane Holzheuer for intentionally denying treatment in retaliation for litigation against Nurse Linda Fox at another prison facility. The theories are **distinct**: Eighth Amendment deliberate indifference to serious medical needs and First Amendment retaliation for resort to court. Retaliation does not automatically fail merely because the retaliatory act would otherwise have been lawful. Conversely, asserting motive does not establish serious need, personal action, protection, cause or injury.

The January 26, 1995 appended opinion reversed the first summary judgment because sworn accounts materially conflicted. Defendants' records purportedly showed at least four visits and prescribed treatment. Berryman asserted total denial by these defendants and forgery of their records, not merely disagreement with a medical judgment. He described being kept in the waiting room. His affidavit attributed to Kukla a refusal because of his suit against her friend Fox and to Rieger a refusal because of those allegations, with a direction to seek Veterans Administration care. He denied treatment by Holzheuer and challenged the claimed nursing-supervisor progress entries. No direct retaliatory statement by Holzheuer is identified in the published account. Her distinct proof position cannot be filled by the other defendants' statements.

The first appeal left discovery and hearsay/business-record objections unresolved because the competing sworn account already prevented the particular summary judgment. Qualified immunity had not been raised in the original district motion; that first panel declined to decide it for the first time on appeal and expressly permitted proper presentation on remand. Therefore the later immunity motion was **not barred simply by the first mandate** and was not the relitigation of an immunity holding already rendered. The problem asserted in 1998 was the factual substance of the actual second appeal.

After discovery on remand, the defendants again sought summary judgment on immunity. The magistrate found factual disputes prevented the requested conclusion; the district judge adopted that recommendation. The circuit's preliminary jurisdictional show-cause order was followed by defendants' letter identifying three purported legal issues and a Clerk's order stating dismissal was inappropriate **at that time**. That was not a final jurisdictional adjudication. Their merits brief did not engage Johnson/Behrens; the panel examined jurisdiction again at argument.

The panel described defendants' actual contentions as reliance on the contested medical records; Dr. Mukherjee's affidavit despite Berryman's denial that he ever examined him; the defendants' own denials; and later treatment by nonparties. These points challenge the plaintiff's factual account. Later nonparty care does not by itself answer whether these particular defendants earlier refused care. The panel's dismissal rests on the actual appeal's demand to select accounts, not a rule that every case containing disputed facts is unappealable.

### Lawful competing paths and separate sanctions issue

A supported jurisdictional disposition can sustain dismissal if no argument remains that accepts the proper plaintiff-favorable assumptions and raises a separable immunity question. The strongest opposite path asks whether the asserted legal issues can be isolated notwithstanding unnecessary factual arguments; *Johnson*, *Behrens* and *Smith* preserve that possibility. It may not manufacture a legal issue absent from the appeal or reject the trial court's sufficiency premise. The reported opinion identifies the nature of the disputed evidence but does not reproduce the three-issue letter in full. Original appellate papers are the targeted source if the precise content is material to rejecting all possible separable legal issues. No assumption of their absence or waiver is justified merely by missing originals.

**Sanctions must be adjudicated independently.** The candidate's phrase “independently supported sanctions” is too conclusive. Section 1912 is titled damages and costs on affirmance and makes an **affirmed judgment** the textual trigger for discretionary just damages for delay and single/double costs. The circuit instead **dismissed** for want of jurisdiction. Its repetition/delay findings may support abuse, but do not themselves answer the statutory-trigger question. Nor does a later Supreme Court affirmance automatically cure the circuit's own original authority.

[28 U.S.C. §1912](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1912) states this condition; its historical note identifies the 1948 enactment without subsequent amendment. The official [1994 OLRC chapter result](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=1994&req=granuleid%3AUSC-1994-title28-chapter123) corroborated the same text in search output, but complete archival fetches returned errors. This is an explicit retrieval limit, not a missing statutory rule.

Appellate Rule 38 supplies an analytically distinct possibility: a frivolous appeal can support just damages and single/double costs after a separately filed motion or court notice and reasonable opportunity to respond. The [Rule 38 text and 1994 amendment note](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frap/rule_38) establish the December 1, 1994 procedural change; the later December 1, 1998 amendment changed only the caption and is not an August 1998 substantive amendment. The lower opinion does not identify a sanctions motion or a notice that sanctions were contemplated. Its **jurisdictional** show-cause order is not automatically notice of **sanctions**, and a future amount-setting hearing does not necessarily supply an earlier opportunity to contest entitlement. Missing notice recitation is not proof that notice never occurred.

*Roadway Express v. Piper*, 447 U.S. 752, 767 (1980), requires fair notice and opportunity to be heard before fee sanctions and requires the necessary bad-faith finding for inherent-power sanctions. Its old §1927 interpretation must not be imported without accounting for subsequent statutory amendment; §1927 is in any event a different provision directed to counsel, whereas this opinion imposes liability on defendants. Local downloaded `roadway_express.txt` and `frap38_history.txt` preserve the fetched sources. No new sanction against counsel is authorized by this supplement.

Lawful alternatives include sustaining a properly authorized and procedurally supported award, vacating only sanctions for failure of the identified authority or required process, or a limited remand identifying authority and permitting required process without treating a missing source as proved lack of notice. The strongest defense of sanctions is the concrete repetition of the same factual disputes after the earlier mandate and the panel's finding of unnecessary protraction; the strongest objection is that sanctionable-seeming conduct cannot dispense with statutory conditions and notice. Ordinary Rule 39 dismissal costs, double costs, fees, damages for delay and personal liability of counsel are not interchangeable.

For any merits discussion reached, *Estelle*, *Wilson* and applicable pre-September-1992 prison/court-access law must be identified separately from current Farmer/Crawford-El doctrine. The exact injury/treatment dates are not supplied; the September 1992 filing gives an outer bound, not an exact conduct date. No new Eighth Amendment or retaliation holding is needed to decide a truly factual appeal.

Date-eligible Justice-source leads, not modeled commitments: Breyer's unanimous *Johnson*; Ginsburg's *Behrens* legal-review explanation, including Stevens/Breyer's distinction between conditional review mechanics and successive authorization; Scalia's unanimous *Smith* assumed-facts rule; each Associate's *Crawford-El* join; and the differentiated Farmer positions where an Eighth Amendment ground actually becomes necessary. The sanctions question requires its own authoritative text/process analysis rather than deriving a position from immunity votes.

## Brewster v. Board of Education

### Corrected judgment, question and excluded matters

The Ninth Circuit on July 13, 1998 **reversed denial of qualified immunity** on the free-speech and salary-process claims and held the individual officials immune from monetary damages. It did not announce that contextual balancing can never clearly establish law: it expressly examined whether this was an exception, and note 9 identifies a total denial of any hearing as a plainly different due-process case. The candidate's “categorical immunity judgment” and question phrased as though balancing alone decided the case must be corrected. A neutral question is whether, on the properly assumed facts, the officials receive personal damages immunity on the reviewed attendance-speech claim and the distinct salary-procedure claim, including the lower court's actual alternative process grounds.

The district court dismissed five state claims, granted summary judgment on failure to train, rejected the public-concern character of Brewster's **union-grievance speech**, and denied immunity on the two surviving claims. The officials did not appeal their favorable union-speech ruling. No separate challenge that would reopen it is supplied. The relevant surviving speech concerns alleged attendance-accounting errors or falsification affecting public funding. The state claims and failure-to-train disposition do not revive automatically. The Board's presence in the caption does not give an entity personal qualified immunity or establish entity liability. The reported appellate relief concerns individual monetary liability; preserve any differently supported capacity or remedy without inventing a live equitable claim.

### Speech: facts, assumptions and individual roles

Brewster entered service as a first-year probationary elementary teacher in September 1994. He became a union grievance representative and filed teacher/union complaints. Principal Gary Furuno claimed classroom-management problems and difficulty with working relationships; he claimed he had decided by November to recommend nonrenewal. Brewster disputed aspects of the evaluation account, including Furuno's alleged favorable oral response after December observation; no written evaluation was supplied. These competing assertions are not findings that legitimate poor performance caused nonrenewal.

In late November Brewster told Superintendent Audrey Clarke that the attendance clerk was falsifying daily registers to increase Average Daily Attendance and funding. Brewster attributed to Clarke a statement recognizing the financial importance; she denied recollection. Brewster then confronted the secretary and told Furuno of purported **errors**. Furuno offered review and reported finding none, as did an independent audit finding no significant accounting error. Later correction of a charge does not itself establish knowing falsity or an earlier lawful motive.

Furuno recommended nonrenewal by letter January 23, 1995 and then to the Board. Assistant Superintendent Althea Jenkins advised Brewster of an opportunity to address the Board. At its February 28 meeting he first characterized the proposed nonrenewal as union retaliation. A March notice ended employment June 30. For this immunity appeal the officials **conceded** Furuno was motivated by attendance speech and **did not assert** that the same action would occur without it. The poor-performance counterfactual cannot therefore be inserted as an established ground supporting this appeal. The concession need not become an adjudicated motive finding for every later stage.

The district court characterized Furuno as the effective nonrenewal decisionmaker despite necessary Board approval. No reported evidence showed Clarke passed the fraud accusation to Furuno or acted on that accusation in nonrenewal; Brewster's stated Clarke theory concerned union activity. The Ninth Circuit assumed public concern without deciding it. It identified taxpayer relevance even if the errors were accidental. It then relied on the clerk's reported distrust/hurt, the acknowledged close teacher-principal relationship requiring trust, possible effect on duties, the limited internal audience and eventual incorrectness of the charge. The district court had called coworker disruption minor. These are the lower court's balancing considerations, not findings of actual operational breakdown, intentional false accusation or independent performance cause.

The lower court did not reach fallback evidentiary-sufficiency contentions about Jenkins, Clarke and individual Board members on speech (notes 1 and 5). Those persons cannot be assigned Furuno's motive by collective attribution. If the legal immunity ground is rejected, any remaining personal-role issue must be handled at its proper stage and within interlocutory jurisdiction.

### Salary: every material procedure and alternative ground

Brewster listed nine years as superintendent, one as principal, one as a graduate-level associate professor and six as teacher-principal. California Education Code §45028 required uniform credit for training/experience; the district counted full-time K–12 teaching, not administrative or college work. Whether its definition was lawful/uniform and Brewster's placement correct were disputed. Jenkins initially placed him at Step 15. His contract made starting salary depend on verified information and allowed verification within sixty days to apply retroactively. The parties disputed whether he supplied requested former-employer addresses.

Jenkins's December 2 memorandum reported verification of only three teaching years, requested further verification by December 9 and warned of Board action December 13. Her December 7 memorandum identified Step 4 as the proposed correction. Both invited contact with personnel staff. The Board acted December 13. On December 15 Brewster and union representative Wiley Jones met Jenkins. She offered restoration if he could establish that administrative experience previously earned comparable credit. He identified Rosemary Carrillo; Jenkins reviewed those personnel records and on January 9, 1995 told Brewster they did not establish that comparator and confirmed reduction.

The district court assumed correct initial placement and a property interest for summary judgment. The Ninth Circuit likewise assumed, without deciding, an interest from the contract or §45028. The officials disputed recoupment of earlier overpayments, but the district court found retroactive withholding occurred; the appeal must assume it did (note 6). Neither no property nor no recoupment can be silently substituted as an established alternative ground.

The lower court gave multiple independent process/immunity grounds: (1) the interest in stopping substantial potentially unrecoverable overpayment and the lesser private consequence than permanent dismissal left reasonable uncertainty about requiring an advance hearing; (2) treating January 9 as the final deprivation, the two notices, solicited response and December 15 meeting with representative and opportunity to offer evidence satisfied process; (3) even treating December 13 as the deprivation, the two earlier memoranda supplied notice, explanation and opportunity to respond, followed promptly by the meeting; and (4) at minimum no insufficiency was clearly established. The court's characterization of finality is legal reasoning to assess, not proof that no paycheck had already been reduced. It did not identify the exact dates or amounts of withholding.

Brewster did **not** challenge promptness of the December 15 meeting or Jenkins's impartiality (notes 7 and 8). The lower court distinguished Sanchez, where no opportunity to be heard existed at any time (note 9). It did not reach personal-evidence sufficiency as to Furuno, Clarke or the Board members on process (note 10). Jenkins's participation in salary review must not be exchanged for Furuno's role in nonrenewal.

### Competing lawful paths under current law

For speech, the employee path gives substantial weight to public-funding accuracy, the limited disruption and the protection of internal speech under *Givhan*, while questioning whether hurt feelings, uncommunicated accusations and later disproof justify the personal damages disposition. It must confront the conceded close working relationship and reasonable-forecast protection, and separately establish clarity in early 1995. The official path relies on the actual employment relationship and conduct-time uncertainty under *Pickering*, *Connick*, *Rankin*, *Harlow* and *Anderson*, not categorical immunity from the existence of balancing. It must confront the assumed motivating role, absent same-decision claim and the rule that public concern is not lost merely through internal communication.

Current *Waters* supplies only the express majority propositions on genuinely held reasonable accounts of disciplinable speech and unreasonable false accounts of actually protected speech; no complete investigation code controls. *Jeffries*, November 17, 1997, rejects dispositive reliance on a forecast or lack of job entitlement while preserving actual institutional needs and distinct individual immunity. Neither Jeffries nor Umbehr supplies early-1995 notice by its later date. *NTEU*, February 22, 1995, concerns a broad prospective restriction; its entire justification burden does not govern this individual action, and it cannot give notice for January or earlier conduct. *Valot*, January 5, 1998, does not reopen this excluded union-speech issue or transform every workplace grievance into its particular external-benefits petition holding.

For process, one lawful path sustains personal immunity on the actual notices/opportunity/meeting even if the lower opinion overstated general uncertainty. A distinct path reaches a narrower merits sufficiency conclusion on supported timing assumptions. A claimant path questions whether conditional future verification notices adequately notified the real administrative-experience issue or whether money was withheld before a meaningful chance to answer. That path must address both memoranda, the representative-assisted meeting, admitted opportunity to offer the comparator and the unchallenged promptness/impartiality; this is not a no-process case. If exact payroll timing becomes material, identify that missing fact rather than assuming either no loss before January 9 or a loss before any opportunity to respond.

Current *Gilbert*, June 9, 1997, permits limited initial suspension on independently reliable felony charges but separately requires justified continuation and meaningful prompt response; it creates no automatic fiscal-emergency requirement, no generic permission to cut pay, and no assumption that any meeting suffices. Its actual initial-suspension predicate differs from salary correction and supplies no 1994–1995 notice. *Mathews*, *Loudermill*, *Mallen*, *Barry* and the prior entitlement authorities remain the conduct-date sources. Distinguish current merits assessment from retrospective immunity. The combined disposition may differ by claim, defendant or remedy. Rejecting a general statement in the opinion does not dispose of an independently sufficient alternative ground.
