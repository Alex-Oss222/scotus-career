# Arteaga: additional neutral record facts from archived papers

This is an objective factual handoff. It contains no Supreme Court disposition, rationale, participation, or filing directive. Source identifiers refer to the archive's numbered bundles and PDF page numbers, not the handwritten pagination. The source originals remain control material. All four substantive bundles, including their embedded appendices, were read completely.

## Identity of the papers

- Bundle 2 is Arteaga's petition, dated August 17, 1997. Its cover names Pete Wilson and other California officials and combines habeas-corpus and certiorari labels. It includes the Wilson/Corrections appellate memorandum and district orders described below. Its allegations and requested relief range across several criminal, habeas, civil-rights, and filing-restriction matters; they should not all be assigned to the Wilson civil action merely because they appear in one submission. [2: PDF pp. 2-11.]
- The archive's bundle 3 label, 'Respondents Brief,' is misleading. Its actual cover identifies Arteaga's supplemental petition for habeas corpus; it also contains a first supplemental appendix submission and copies of earlier papers. It is not an opposition authored by a respondent. [3: cover and complete bundle.]
- Bundle 4 contains Arteaga's second and third supplements with embedded lower-court orders, his additional motions, and administrative correspondence. Bundle 5 is his reply to what he calls Governor Wilson's response dated November 24, 1997. The underlying response is a Corrections Department administrative letter: responding at the Governor's request, the writer says the purpose of Arteaga's submitted notice of an appellant's informal brief is unclear and forwards it to the Legal Affairs Division. The letter refers to Ninth Circuit No. 97-16705. It does not address the merits of the Wilson dismissal or admit Arteaga's substantive allegations. [4: complete bundle, especially PDF p. 12; 5: cover and complete bundle.]
- No separately identified respondent litigation opposition appears among these four recovered bundles. That describes this archive item, not everything that might have existed in a court file.

## Wilson's distinct lower-court posture

1. The July 21, 1997 Ninth Circuit memorandum resolves two distinct appeals together: No. 96-16222, Arteaga v. Pete Wilson and Daniel E. Lungren, from C 94-3046 FMS; and No. 96-16223, Arteaga v. California Department of Corrections and others, from C 94-1575 FMS. It identifies both as section 1983 actions dismissed for failure to amend pursuant to district-court instructions. The court affirmed under Rule 41(b), citing Ferdik v. Bonzelet, 963 F.2d 1258, 1260 (9th Cir. 1992). It said the district court had twice allowed amendment, explained the deficiencies, and warned that failure to amend would result in dismissal. It also denied requested judicial notice, consolidation of habeas petitions with the civil-rights action, and habeas relief within that action. These are lower-court rulings, not present merits findings. [2: PDF p. 8, visually verified.]
2. The district court's May 10, 1996 dismissal order recites that the two complaints were filed in 1994 and dismissed with leave to amend on October 17, 1994. The cases were stayed during premature appeals. After lifting the stays in January 1996, the court set February 29, 1996 for amended complaints. Arteaga requested a renewed stay on March 6 because he was occupied with state-court cases. On March 7 the court denied a stay but extended the amendment date to April 15. The dismissal order says no amended complaints had been filed by May 10. It specifies prejudice to filing the allegations in other unpaid complaints; preserve that qualification rather than converting it into an unqualified adjudication of every allegation on its merits. [2: PDF pp. 9-10, visually verified.]
3. The March 7 order separately denied a request for a prison 'red privilege card' allowing unrestricted library use, in place of the approximately two hours per day the order said Arteaga then received. Citing Lindquist v. Idaho State Board of Corrections, 776 F.2d 851, 858 (9th Cir. 1985), it distinguished access from unlimited access. It directed a separate amended complaint with the correct caption and case number in each action, and warned that noncompliance by April 15 would lead to dismissal absent extraordinary circumstances. [2: PDF pp. 10-11, visually verified.]

## What Arteaga actually argues about procedural failure

It would overstate the papers to say that Arteaga offers no explanation for procedural defaults. The original petition alleges continuing obstruction of court access, removal or withholding of litigation records, and a filing-review system that prejudiced his pending matters. Its questions expressly refer to Nos. 96-16222 and 96-16223. Its detailed discussion of a dismissal for failure to prosecute, however, concerns a different proceeding, Ninth Circuit No. 92-15341. It argues that noncompliance there was not willful and was caused by officials' interference. The petition incorporates other filings and seeks relief from imprisonment as well as relief from procedural obstruction. These are his allegations and arguments, not established facts. [2: PDF pp. 3-6.]

The supplements and reply continue that account. They allege officials deprived him of legal papers and access needed to litigate, claim that an order in the 1992 habeas appeal was withheld until 1997, seek restoration of that proceeding, identify the Wilson/Corrections appeals among the matters allegedly affected, and attack restrictions on filing and access. They also invoke Portman v. County of Santa Clara, 995 F.2d 898 (9th Cir. 1993), asserting that Arteaga can present issues for which Portman lacked standing. The papers repeatedly intermingle claims concerning different proceedings. No conspiracy, intentional obstruction, or underlying innocence allegation becomes a finding merely because it is accompanied by a pleading or administrative record. [3-5: complete texts; especially 3: PDF pp. 2-4.]

There is also a direct postjudgment challenge involving the Wilson case. A December 2, 1997 district order addresses motions filed in November in four civil cases, including C 94-3046 and C 94-1575. It rejects a petition for mandamus/prohibition/habeas as an improper effort to litigate closed cases; denies another request for government-funded transcripts because no new reason was supplied after an earlier denial; treats a coram-nobis request as a Rule 60(b) motion; and rejects the described 1992 difficulties obtaining litigation materials as a basis for reopening because those facts were known before judgment. It also denies reconsideration or vacatur and explains that additional motions do not themselves reopen the cases. Those are that court's reasons, not an independent resolution of the alleged interference here. [4: PDF pp. 3-4.]

The record therefore contains both an actual extension request in the two civil cases and broad allegations that interference caused litigation failures, plus later motions seeking to reopen the Wilson judgment. In the four complete bundles, I did not identify a timely amended complaint in C 94-3046 or C 94-1575, or a clearly particularized account tying a specific withheld document or incident to inability to meet the March-April 1996 amendment instruction. That limited observation does not support describing the petitioner as silent about noncompliance, treating every allegation as irrelevant without analysis, or assuming that missing archival material proves a litigant default. The May 1996 dismissal and July 1997 affirmance supply the historical lower-court finding of nonamendment.

## Sworn personal financial evidence

Bundle 3 includes an in-forma-pauperis motion and a signed declaration under penalty of perjury dated August 17, 1997. Visual inspection, necessary because OCR is materially wrong, establishes the following reported finances:

- Arteaga states that he is not currently employed. He gives October 18, 1988 as his last employment date and approximately $1,000 per month as his pay then. That amount is not claimed current income.
- For the preceding twelve months he answers no to income from business or self-employment, stocks/bonds/royalties, rents, pensions/annuities/life insurance, and Social Security or other government sources.
- He answers no to owning or buying a home, owning a car, and having a bank account. He reports no other source of income or money.
- He lists his children as dependent on him but says he cannot contribute to their support because of imprisonment. His characterization of that imprisonment as unconstitutional is his assertion.

These are affirmative sworn statements about Arteaga's own finances, not an absence of financial evidence. They are not an independently verified prison trust-account balance. The form does not provide a separately certified current balance or resolve any later change in resources. [3: PDF p. 24, visually verified in full.]

The motion's respondent line visibly contains 'Superior Court of Santa Clara County, California' crossed out, with no replacement respondent legible. It is reproduced in this petitioner's supplemental bundle. Its personal financial statements remain evidence concerning Arteaga, but the reproduced page alone does not establish a separately executed Wilson-specific IFP motion or the exact procedural linkage of the older form to each request now presented. Do not convert this caption limitation into a conclusion that his personal financial condition is unknown or that he failed to supply financial evidence.

A separate November 26, 1997 Eastern District order in No. 2:97-cv-01728, Arteaga v. Wilson, transfers a new civil-rights action to the Northern District under section 1404(a). It expressly states that the court has not ruled on the IFP request. It therefore is not evidence of a financial-eligibility denial. It concerns a different action from C 94-3046 and C 94-1575. [4: PDF pp. 4-5.]

No determination of financial eligibility, frivolousness, abuse, or the disposition of either current request is made in this factual handoff.
