## Newsweek

Newsweek seeks repayment of sales taxes paid during 1988–1990. Florida exempted newspapers but not magazines beginning January 1, 1988. The Florida Supreme Court held that classification unconstitutional in Department of Revenue v. Magazine Publishers of America, 565 So. 2d 1304 (1990), reaffirmed at 604 So. 2d 459 (1992). Newsweek invoked Fla. Stat. §215.26(1). After administrative refusal, the trial court entered summary judgment against Newsweek, and the First District affirmed at 689 So. 2d 361 (1997).

The appellate court relied on §72.011 (1987): a prepayment challenger could deposit the disputed sum in court, post bond, or obtain approval for another arrangement. Florida had long permitted constitutional tax-refund claims under §215.26: State ex rel. Hardaway Contracting Co. v. Lee, 155 Fla. 724, 21 So. 2d 211 (1945). Osceola v. Florida Department of Revenue, 893 F.2d 1231, 1233 (11th Cir. 1990), also treated that route as adequate. No particular timeliness or other independent refund-condition default is established by this extraction.

## Arteaga

Lorenzo Arteaga requests financial leave to file certiorari papers. The Ninth Circuit affirmed a dismissal with prejudice for failure to amend complaints as the district court directed. The lead petition and supplement assert innocence, false imprisonment, constitutional violations, and conspiracies among officials; they do not address the district court's dismissal ground. The reported proceeding includes a separate financial-leave motion in Arteaga v. Wilson but provides neither a separate printed docket nor Wilson's submission content or financial affidavit. This source does not establish that Wilson's papers repeat the lead petition's defects. It supplies no adjudication of financial ineligibility.
