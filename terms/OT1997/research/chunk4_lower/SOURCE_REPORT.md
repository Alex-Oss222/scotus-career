## Johnson v. Rodriguez, 110 F.3d 299 (5th Cir. April 23, 1997)

**Posture correction.** Nos. 95-50879 and 96-50382 are appeals by state officials in their official capacities from a bench-trial judgment and fee award. The certified class includes all present and future Texas prison inmates and is expressly limited to prospective relief. This is not a personal-capacity damages case. Qualified immunity is not a remand issue identified by the opinion. The named plaintiff's own injury, the Board's policy/custom, causation, and the proper scope of class relief are the central remand matters. See 110 F.3d at 302–03, 312–16.

**Exact lower disposition.** The protest-letter ruling is reversed, with instructions to dismiss that claim with prejudice. The writ-writer ruling is reversed and remanded for reconsideration or further proceedings under the opinion, including its retaliation **and equal-protection** analyses. The fee award under §1988 is vacated because the reversed judgment no longer establishes prevailing-party status. Do not describe the entire equal-protection controversy as finally dismissed while preserving only retaliation.

**Separate components and supported facts.**

1. **Protest letters / parole-file procedures.** Texas parole is discretionary. Article 42.18 §8(f)(2) requires victim-related notice, permits written and oral statements, does not limit the number of persons supplying statements, and requires consideration of the victim-impact statement. Section 8(e) requires particular material to be included in the file; inclusion and mandatory consideration are distinct. Section 18(a) generally makes the file confidential and privileged. The magistrate found differential treatment, inaccurate information in some letters, and no articulated verification policy; it prohibited accepting or considering protest statements and placing them in files. Those are trial findings and the trial remedy, not statutory commands. The Fifth Circuit found no antecedent discernible group purposefully targeted by the protest-letter provisions, and alternatively identified rational interests in victim participation, protection from reprisal, and avoiding formal adversarial parole hearings. It distinguished equal protection from an accuracy/hearing claim requiring a protected liberty interest. The absence of a parole entitlement does not itself dispose of a distinct retaliation claim.
2. **Writ-writer retaliation.** The appellate opinion distinguishes prisoners' nonfrivolous claims about their own convictions or confinement from frivolous or secondary assistance activity. It reads Lewis/Tighe to deny an independent access right in assistance to others; that legal reading requires independent testing under entering law. The magistrate had treated all litigation activity as protected. The Fifth Circuit requires identification of Johnson's protected activity, a formal or informal Board policy/custom authorizing punitive retaliation, and proof that the policy actually affected his parole decision and that parole would not otherwise have been denied. General file references, discussions of litigation, subjective perceptions, and old findings about other prison agencies do not by themselves establish those elements. Lack of comparative statistics is damaging but expressly not necessarily fatal to retaliation.
3. **Writ-writer equal protection and class remedy.** The opinion requires a supported comparison with otherwise similarly situated prisoners, discriminatory purpose/effect, and an official policy/custom; it notes the absence of comparative evidence and treats the asserted general effect as incidental. It nevertheless remands this writ-writer claim. Johnson must first show redressable injury supporting his representation of the class; any broader relief then requires particularized findings about contemporary members and the scope of actual constitutional injury. Relief must fit the proven violation. The opinion does not find that Johnson ultimately lacks standing; it calls for findings on remand.
4. **Closed matters.** Footnote 1 identifies unappealed, final dismissals of the ex post facto, tentative parole month/institutional-progress, and nonresident furlough claims, and final corporate-entity Eleventh Amendment immunity. They are not merits components reopened by this review.

**Strongest competing paths, as analysis rather than invented filings.** The officials can defend the protest-letter result by the absence of an entitlement or targeted classification and the state's legitimate information/victim interests, and defend remand or denial of systemic relief by missing plaintiff-specific causal findings. Johnson can argue that access and retaliation do not depend on a right to parole, that mixed litigation histories do not permit retaliation for identified protected filings, and that the assistance-to-others category cannot be rejected without addressing whether the recipients' access was otherwise adequately protected. The published record supports a narrow remand addressing those legal standards without finding new acts of retaliation. A contrary award of individual or systemic relief would need the omitted activity, causal, policy, and representative-injury findings. The opinion's generalizations about the merit of prisoner litigation are judicial observations, not findings establishing that Johnson's every relevant suit was frivolous.

**Material limits.** The original parole files and full trial record were not recovered. The opinion expressly says the record lacks evidence about the scope/results of Johnson's litigation and lacks identified causal findings. That supports preserving those questions on remand, not inferring either protected activity or default. The neutral packet's statement that Johnson himself wrote the protest letters is misleading: the challenged protest letters principally came from victims, officials, and members of the public opposing release.

## Aponte Matos v. Toledo Dávila, 135 F.3d 182 (1st Cir. February 3, 1998)

**Posture and result.** No. 97-1645; argued December 3, 1997. Plaintiffs appeal a series of summary judgments in a damages action under §1983 against Puerto Rico officers and under Bivens/§1331 against federal officers. Review is de novo, taking the evidence favorably to plaintiffs. The First Circuit reverses and remands **only the warrant-falsification claim against Ernesto Laboy-Escobar** and affirms the remaining disposition, with the jurisdictional limitations below. The material conduct was December 6, 1993; the warrant affidavit concerns an asserted December 3 observation. A Supreme Court rule announced later cannot automatically establish notice in 1993.

**Component map.**

| Component | Actual basis and limits below |
|---|---|
| Laboy's affidavit | Laboy said he saw two men exchange a firearm and enter the house. A resident's sworn statement says she was home and no man entered at that time; her husband supports her account that he was elsewhere. The fellow officer's affidavit reports only what Laboy told him, not an independent observation. The disputed observation was essential to probable cause. Franks supplies deliberate/reckless material falsehood requirements; negligence or innocent mistake is insufficient. Sworn conflicting evidence creates a triable dispute; the court does not find actual falsification or ultimate liability. |
| Other officers / fabrication conspiracy | No evidence showed their knowledge of or assistance in the alleged falsehoods. Participation in the subsequent search alone does not supply that link. |
| Plichta computer-search claim | **Not properly before the court:** final Rule 54(b) judgment entered May 29, 1996; notice of appeal filed May 5, 1997, beyond the applicable 60 days. The district court reasoned that the unsuccessful attempt to access files was not a search; the appellate court expressly did not address that merits conclusion. |
| Federal supervisor Ilario | Separate July 22, 1996 partial final judgment also not timely appealed. Do not substitute an evidentiary or supervisory-merits ruling for this ground. |
| House-search scope / questions about money | Timely appeal. No evidence showed a search of a place unavailable in a weapons search. Questions about hidden money, without compulsion to answer, did not defeat immunity on this record; the ruling does not grant unrestricted authority to search for money. |
| Failure to knock and announce | Decided on immunity: no clearly established constitutional notice requirement applicable to these officers in 1993 under the circuit's analysis. Neither the district court nor appellate court decides that the no-knock entry was constitutionally reasonable. Wilson (1995) and Richards (1997) do not by their dates alone answer 1993 notice. |
| Excessive force / execution | Fourth Amendment objective reasonableness, not substantive-due-process conscience-shocking analysis. The evidence included an ax entry, 10–15 officers, dogs, and a blockade. Plaintiffs described orderly conduct inside; no arrest, physical contact, or physical injury occurred. Colón allegedly pointed a gun and threatened the returning daughter who insisted on crossing the blockade; her version is assumed, and the court grants immunity based on the security function and circumstances. It does not establish a universal rule that physical injury is indispensable. |
| Puerto Rico supervisors | No affirmative link between Laboy's alleged fabrication and their encouragement, acquiescence, or deliberate indifference. A quota-like demand for three search warrants monthly and a much earlier judicial criticism of Laboy did not establish the required link. No respondeat-superior liability. |
| Counsel access | Claim not specifically argued on appeal and deemed waived (footnote 1). |
| State-law supplemental jurisdiction | Court declines an appellate instruction requiring the district court to exercise it; plaintiffs remain free to request discretionary exercise on remand (footnote 10). |

**Strongest competing paths.** The fabrication claim turns on the contradictory sworn eyewitness evidence and materiality, not merely a failed search or a suspected ulterior motive. Officials can dispute fabrication at trial and insist on individual involvement and conduct-date notice. Plaintiffs can challenge categorical reliance on physical injury for force, but this source supplies no new evidence beyond the assumed threat and surrounding search. The search-scope and knock-and-announce components must remain independent of whether Laboy's affidavit was false. The quoted original affidavit and resident's declaration are preserved in the full texts. The First Circuit expressly does not decide whether Plichta's independent tip about intended robbers and hidden money could alone justify a warrant, because the police did not seek one on that basis.

**Material limits.** No complete appendix, district-court Rule 54(b) orders, or notices of appeal were recovered. Published appellate recitations support the narrow timeliness dispositions and summary-judgment facts under Engine §14. A path overturning their certification or timing would require the relevant original orders/notices or an authenticated contrary recitation; none is supplied. The broad Archive name/docket search found no matching collection item. No inference of missing litigant filings or default follows.

## Shahar v. Bowers, 114 F.3d 1097 (11th Cir. May 30, 1997) (en banc)

**Source facts for fresh validation.** The en banc court affirms summary judgment for Bowers. Shahar seeks damages and injunctive employment relief. Her free-exercise, intimate-association, expressive-association, and equal-protection theories must remain distinct. The dismissal of her substantive-due-process claim was not contested on appeal (majority footnote 7). No separate free-speech claim was presented below (Tjoflat footnote 1). Bowers pleaded qualified immunity, but the district court did not reach it and he did not ask the appellate court to affirm on that alternative damages ground (Tjoflat footnotes 2 and 10). Thus the judgment is not a qualified-immunity judgment, and an eventual remand must preserve the defense/preservation question rather than invent its resolution.

The employment offer followed successful summer clerk work, was accepted, and anticipated a September 1991 start. Before that start, Bowers withdrew it by a July 1991 letter referring to the planned same-sex marriage and his concern that inaction would imply approval and impair the office. Shahar disclaimed civil marriage status and public marriage benefits. Her application identified her female partner as her future spouse. Her religious ceremony, guest list, name change, insurance, and house ownership are described in the opinion, but not all were known to Bowers when he acted. Majority footnote 21 expressly acknowledges later-acquired facts and invokes the requested employment relief. The majority's date recital says the ceremony took place in June, while its narrative describes June discussion of a late-July ceremony and the Godbold dissent says it occurred after withdrawal. Avoid assigning a precise ceremony date without further source verification; that discrepancy does not require choosing a disputed merits premise.

**Competing record accounts.** The majority assumes association rights without finally deciding their existence, applies Pickering, and gives weight to a legal employer's confidentiality, public representation, potential reassignment, and reasonable predictions of internal and public disruption. It does not find actual disruption. It distinguishes conduct from orientation for equal protection and assumes free-exercise protection before rejecting that claim under the same balancing approach. The evidence of employer reasons consists of the withdrawal letter and deposition recitations: perceived conflict with state legal positions, public credibility, sodomy enforcement, working relations, and judgment.

The dissents stress the sincere religious and enduring intimate relationship; no request for legal marriage; an invitation-only, out-of-state ceremony without news publicity; no established violation of law; Bowers's admitted lack of knowledge of sexual conduct; and his refusal to discuss his concerns with Shahar. They dispute the reasonableness of the predictions and reliance on prejudice, and distinguish actual law-enforcement impairment from assumed public hostility. Tjoflat separately agrees with judgment while rejecting the majority's assumed-right balancing method; he would deny protected intimate-association status and find no proof that the ceremony's religious character caused withdrawal. These are competing judicial grounds and readings, not independently established factual findings or Supreme Court outcomes.

**Neutral limits and paths.** It is accurate to say the published record identifies no actual workplace disruption and no established lawbreaking; it is inaccurate to turn that into a stipulation that the employer had no predictive or job-related reason. Review can test whether the accepted facts permit summary judgment under the governing employment/association and equal-protection standards. A remand can preserve unresolved motive, reasonable-investigation, and relief questions. Neither the protection of the relationship nor the adequacy of the prediction may be assumed solely to reach a desired disposition. A finding that Bowers knew the later insurance/name-change facts at the time would contradict the acknowledged source limitation. The full recitations provide bounded support without reconstructing new evidence or private conversations. Further source work would be needed for an exact ceremony date or to decide disputed motives as fact.
