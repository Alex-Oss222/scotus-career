## United States v. Ramirez

The source records a violent prison escapee, an informant described as reliable who believed he had seen the escapee at the house, and an agent's observation of a similar-looking man. Officers had information about possible guns in the garage. Roughly 45 officers executed the warrant; one garage window was broken while officers announced through a loudspeaker. Ramirez, believing burglars were entering, fired into the garage ceiling; police fired back and identified themselves. He surrendered, waived Miranda rights, admitted owning two guns and being a felon; a subsequent warrant was used to retrieve the guns. The escapee was not found. The lower courts suppressed on a heightened exigency rule for property damage. The actual amount and purpose of damage and the suppression causal question remain distinct. ## Lewis v. United States — correction to judgment below

The Fifth Circuit REJECTED assimilation of Louisiana's child-victim first-degree murder provision. It nonetheless retained the conviction because the jury's finding under the state offense necessarily included the elements of federal second-degree murder, and upheld the life sentence because it was within the federal statutory maximum. Thus the neutral packet's statement that the Fifth Circuit permitted assimilation is erroneous.

Lewis did not contest the legal correctness of retaining the federal second-degree conviction on those jury findings. She challenged the mandatory life sentence: unlike the Louisiana provision, federal second-degree murder permitted any term of years or life. The federal Guidelines range identified for this first-time offender and vulnerable victim was 168–210 months, with a possible upward departure to be determined by the sentencing court. The United States conceded that if the conviction rested on federal second-degree murder, resentencing under the Guidelines was appropriate and a district judge had to make the departure determination in the first instance. Do not turn that concession into a mandatory reduction below life or a requirement of retrial. The source does not establish a separately preserved prejudicial trial-error claim requiring a new trial.

Sources: objective posture and party submissions in 523 U.S. 155, 158–159, 172–173, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-155.pdf; judgment below 92 F.3d 1371. Raw same-matter Supreme Court source is control-only.

## Quality King

The record does not resolve whether the first purchaser was the British or Malta distributor, or whether title passed at carrier delivery or destination. ## Gray v. Maryland

Bell's confession named himself, Gray, and Jacquin “Tank” Vanlandingham; Vanlandingham died before trial. Gray and Bell were jointly tried. Oral reading substituted deleted/deletion for both other names; the written version used blanks separated by commas. Other witnesses described six participants; Gray denied participating, and Bell did not testify. The court instructed that the confession was evidence against Bell only. Maryland's intermediate appellate court set Gray's conviction aside; its highest court reinstated it.

There was a prosecution follow-up question linking Bell's information and Gray's arrest. The State's source position is that Gray was already a suspect and an arrest warrant issued before Bell confessed, and that the question marked a transition rather than established the confession's identification of Gray. No contemporaneous objection to that question is identified; the defense had sought severance and objected to use of the confession. Treat the source's account of the State's contextual position as a submission, not a new factual finding. ## Triestman

The order under review is a refusal to reconsider a court-of-appeals denial of authorization to file a successive §2255 motion. It is not a judgment on a separately filed §2241 petition. The Second Circuit's Part I held §2244(b)(3)(D) and (E) incorporated in §2255, including the express ban on appeal/certiorari from authorization grants/denials; it distinguished that ban from the court of appeals' own sua sponte reconsideration power. A Supreme Court appellate vehicle must therefore be addressed; an unfiled §2241 case cannot silently be substituted.

The lower opinion expressly reserved actual innocence and the Government's arguments that Triestman carried a firearm or aided/abetted carrying. The plea described possessing a firearm for protection at the drug laboratory and placing a pistol in a cabinet or drawer. The prisoner had filed §2255 motions, not a §2241 petition. The lower court recognized a limited §2241 avenue but did not adjudicate the ultimate claim or order release. Sources: `triestman.txt`, full opinion including all 24 footnotes read; 124 F.3d 361, https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/124/124.F3d.361.96-2563.1270.html. ## Hetzel

The jury returned $750,000; the district court reduced it to $500,000 because one supporting claim was legally insufficient. The Fourth Circuit upheld liability but found damages excessive and directed recalculation. On remand the district court awarded $50,000 and allowed a new damages trial when Hetzel rejected that award. The Fourth Circuit issued mandamus and stayed retrial, construing its mandate as requiring entry of final judgment. Thereafter the district court entered $15,000, matching the higher of two comparator awards identified by the court of appeals; an appeal from that later judgment was pending. Distinguish the challenged mandamus judgment from that pending damages appeal. Sources: objective procedural recitation at 523 U.S.208, 208–210 & nn.1–2, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-208.pdf. Raw same-matter source is control-only.
