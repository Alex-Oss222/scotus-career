## Separate-writing substance for reconciliation

Lewis: Scalia and Thomas reject the majority's open-ended inquiry into federal policy/field occupation. They read the original ACA offense concept through common-law offense categories, permit modern firmly established categories, and find federal law already covers murder. They reject both a rigid Blockburger test and Kennedy's modified test as poor fits for gap filling. Their express PartIV join makes the sentencing explanation controlling with eight historical votes, though the assimilation framework has six. Kennedy prefers a presumption of state-law assimilation grounded in federalism and a modified same-elements approach: ignore trivial wording/jurisdiction differences, do not equate greater and lesser offenses, and preserve Williams where Congress specifically sets the disputed element differently. Louisiana's child-age element was not set by Congress and therefore should be assimilated on his approach.

Gray: Scalia's four-Justice dissent preserves Richardson's facial-versus-inferential line and ordinary reliance on jury instructions. Two deletions, multiple participants, and only one other defendant made identification uncertain; obvious redactions are not powerfully incriminating as a class. He objects to hiding edits or rewriting confessions and stresses severance/evidence-rule protections beyond the constitutional minimum. FN2 disputes the significance and preservation of the prosecutor's follow-up question, citing the preexisting warrant and absent objection. This caveat must not be lost when summarizing the writing.
