Pro se plaintiff brings this action, by Order to Show Cause, 
under "42 U.S.C. §§ 1983 and 1985" for violation of her rights 
under the “Sth and 14th Amendments.” (See Complaint at ¥ 58g In 
essence, plaintiff is dissatisfied with defendants’ conduct 1n an 
ongoing dispute between plaintiff and her landlord, defendant 
Porzio. Plaintiff alleges, inter alia, that Judge Friia, a “City 
Judge" of the City of White Plains, “accepted the affidavits of 
service as grounds for in personam jurisdiction" despite 
plaintiff's contention that those affidavits contained various 
perjured statements. Plaintiff seeks to “stop" “sewer service" and 
tne “lying process servers [presumubiy cefenuantS Kuty aid 
Cheatem), lying lawyers who pay for it [presumably defendants 
Lehrman and Guterman) and judges who allow it (presumably defendant 
Friia)." Plaintiff also seeks "$3 million compensatory and 
punitive damages," costs, and “a stay of all proceedings in White 
Plains City Court during the pendency of this action." We grant 
plaintiff's request to proceed in forma pauperis, but deny her 
requests for injunctive relief and for appointment of counsel and 


dismiss her complaint for the following reasons. 


DISCUSSION 

When evaluating plaintiff's allegations this Court must accept 
as true the factual allegations in the complaint and construe all

Plaintiff's claims against defendants Porzio, Lehrman, 
Guterman, Ruto and Cheatem must also be dismissed. A claim for

acted under color of a state "statute, ordinance, regulation,

not generally liable. Rendell]-Baker v. Kohn, 457 U.S. 830, 838-42 
(1982); Flagg Bros, Inc. v. Brooks, 436 U.S. 149, 155-57 (1978); 
Moose Lodge No. 107 v. Irvis, 407 U.S. 163, 173 (1972); Adickes v. 
S.H. “Kress & Co., 398 U.S. 144, 152 (1970); see also Sturm v. 
Schrank, 43 B.R. 755 (S.D.N.Y¥. 1984) ("lawyers and other persons 
who participate in highly regulated professions do not, absent 
more, act under color of State law for purpose of liability under 


42 U.S.C. § 1983). As defendants Porzio, Lehrman, Guterman, Ruto 


and Cheatem are private parties who are not alleged to work for any

1983 is not “presented. j 
With regard to plaintiff's allegations of a conspiracy to 
deprive her of her constitutional rights under 42 U.S.c. § 1985, 
the claim is dismissed as to all defendants. Plaintiff has failed 
to allege facts sufficient to show the existence of any conspiracy 
designed to deprive her of her rights. Singer v. Fulton County 
Sheriff, 63 F.3d 110 (2d Cir. 1995) ("Although the pleading of a 
conspiracy will enable a plaintiff to bring suit against purely 
private individuals, the lawsuit will stand only insofar as the 
plaintiff can prove... the violation of a federal} right."); see 
Qstrer v. Aronwald, 567 F.2d 551, 553 (2d Cir. 1977). Where, as 
here, the claims of conspiracy are vague and provide no basis in 
fact, they must be dismissed. pPolur v. Raffe, 912 F.2d 52, 56 (2d

Finally, insofar as plaintiff seeks review of Judge Friia's 
rulings in Poria v. Buell, which is currently pending in the City 
Court of White Plains, the complaint is dismissed. The Supreme 
Court has held in both Rocker v. Fidelity Trust Co., 263 U.S. 413, 
415-16 (1923) and District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman, 
460 U.S. 462, 482-86 (1983) (the “Rocker-Feldman doctrine") that a 
United States District Court has virtually no authority to review 
either final judgments of state courts or issues that are 
"“inextricably intertwined' with [the state court's) decision...." 


Neu v. Abrams, 867 F. Supp. 236, 238 (S.D.N.¥. 1994) (quoting 


Feldman, 460 U.S. at 483-484 n. 16). Since the substance behind 


plaintiff's allegations appears to have been addressed in the state 


court proceedings, and because "the relief sought in (this]} court 


requires a finding that ‘the state court was wrong,...th[is 


attempted} federal proceeding...{would be)...a prohibited appeal of 
the state court judgment,'" Qdom v. Columbia University, 906 F. 
Supp. 188, 196 (S.D.N.¥. 1995) (quoting Pennzoil v. Texaco, 481 
U.S. 1, 25, (1987) (Marshall, J., concurring), review of 
plaintiff's claims would be proper only in state court and, 
ultimately, if appropriate, in the United States Supreme Court. 28 
U.S.C. § 1257(a). Since this Court lacks jurisdiction to review 


plaintiff's claims under the Rocker-Feldman doctrine, they are 


dismissed.
