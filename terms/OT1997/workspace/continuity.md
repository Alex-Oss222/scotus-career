### November 4, 1997 — Bates v. United States

[Public Projection](../records/bates_v_united_states_merits_1997-11-04.md#public-projection). Ginsburg's unanimous Court opinion holds that the version of 20 U.S.C. §1097(a) governing the alleged 1990 conduct requires knowing and willful culpable misapplication, without an additional purpose to injure or defraud. An unpaid refund, accounting mistake, insolvency, or breached funding condition is insufficient by itself. The indictment's deliberate-diversion allegations remain unproved; affirmance allows the reinstated twelve-count prosecution to proceed.

Wells governs fidelity to actual enacted elements; Ratzlaf's contextual willfulness and independently sufficient residual-lenity ground, and Staples, Morissette, and Liparota's protection of offense-specific fault remain intact. Text, context, and the distinct bank-misapplication history leave no residual ambiguity requiring the proposed additional purpose here. The complete meaning of willfulness, including knowledge of illegality, every conversion theory, aiding-and-abetting questions, and independent indictment objections are not decided. No universal rule about statutory silence or mental state is adopted; the later refund-specific amendment is not applied retroactively. Guilt, sufficiency, instructions, and sentence remain outside this pretrial construction ruling.

### November 4, 1997 — State Oil Co. v. Khan

[Public Projection](../records/state_oil_co_v_khan_merits_1997-11-04.md#public-projection). Scalia's unanimous Court opinion replaces Albrecht's per se condemnation of vertical maximum resale-price agreements with rule-of-reason review, effective this date. Albrecht is overruled only to that extent. Per se condemnation requires a sound basis for treating the category as predictably harmful; a ceiling may constrain dealer markups and improve interbrand competition as well as produce harms requiring proof. State Oil's rebate obligation is an actual price agreement, not a unilateral suggestion.

Sylvania and Business Electronics inform the vertical-restraint distinction; Atlantic Richfield and Brunswick preserve independent antitrust injury; Kodak and Brooke Group retain their distinct proof and competitive-harm rules. Minimum resale-price and horizontal price-fixing rules remain unchanged. Harmful service suppression, exclusion, exploitation of dependence, disguised minimum pricing, and proved horizontal arrangements retain their governing rules. Dealer lost margin alone is not antitrust injury. The Court decides no market power, actual harm, damages, expert-evidence outcome, or ultimate liability, and creates no general stare decisis checklist from Hubbard's divided rationales. Lower courts properly followed Albrecht before this overruling. The antitrust component is vacated for rule-of-reason proceedings; the district court's antitrust summary judgment is not automatically restored, all evidence or injury issues are not automatically reopened, and the separate contract dismissal stands.

### November 17, 1997 — City of Monroe v. United States

[Public Projection](../records/city_of_monroe_v_united_states_summary_merits_1997-11-17.md#public-projection). Per curiam Part I, joined by all nine Justices, permits an enforcement court to determine the actual scope and applicability of Attorney General preclearance while forbidding substantive preclearance or reconsideration of approval's wisdom; actual changes must be fairly identified. Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg, holds that the specifically submitted and approved 1968 statewide majority default independently governs Monroe's silent charter. The unprecleared 1966 local amendment does not negate that independent authority. The conflicting plurality injunction is reversed to that limited extent.

City of Rome is distinguished because its charter expressly required plurality; its rejection of implied clearance for local amendments remains binding. McCain and Clark preserve specific submission and the submitting authority's responsibility for ambiguity. Lopez, Young, and Foreman reject clearance by association, long use, or state compulsion. No exemption for state-compelled administration, blanket approval of local implementation, or clearance of an approved delegation's distinct local choices follows. An ambiguous submission would not establish the same approval. The 1966 amendment and other charter revisions remain unapproved; genuinely distinct changes require ordinary coverage and preclearance treatment. Bossier's substantive rules and independent §2 discrimination claims remain separate. No damages, completed-election consequences, replacement-system design, automatic new approval request, or automatic evidentiary hearing is decided.

### November 17, 1997 — Jeffries v. Harleston

[Public Projection](../records/jeffries_v_harleston_merits_1997-11-17.md#public-projection). Souter's Parts I and III command five direct votes (Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer); Part II commands those five plus Stone-Zsela. Six Justices support vacatur; the controlling five-vote mandate does not require a new trial.

Part I rejects final defense judgment on the panel's employment balance. Pickering and Connick require attention to the actual speech, service relationship, and institutional needs. Retained tenure and lack of entitlement to a longer chair term do not categorically defeat retaliation; a reasonable forecast matters but does not alone decide protection. The public-concern address was off campus at an unaffiliated event and was not assigned or controlled as official duty; introduction by the chair title did not establish sponsorship. Real administrative duties and recruitment, fundraising, faculty-interaction, and community interests remain relevant, but no special policymaking, confidential, or additional public-contact role was established. Both jury findings—no actual disruption and reasonable expected disruption—stand. Prevention need not await actual harm. Waters protects a genuinely relied-upon reasonable account only if it describes legally disciplinable speech; no complete investigation code is adopted. Perry, Umbehr, and O'Hare preserve the distinction between benefit entitlement and retaliation. No categorical protection of the entire address, actual-loss requirement, quantified institutional-damages requirement, or wholesale NTEU prospective-ban burden follows; Settle remains confined to its student-curriculum setting.

Part II requires each defendant's actual causal responsibility and a real counterfactual under Mt. Healthy, Umbehr, O'Hare, Kimberlin, and Crawford-El. Once protected speech is a substantial or motivating cause, the same-decision defense requires proof by a preponderance, not a conceivable lawful reason. Nine successful one-year votes and differentiated animus or punitive findings do not automatically establish an independent lawful Board decision. A causally effective proposal may matter, but procurement alone proves no liability; nonvoting proposals, defeated votes, and abstention neither establish nor eliminate other supported causal contributions. Competent direct and circumstantial proof applies without a heightened motive burden. A genuine independent lawful decision or supported personal defense may still dispose of a claim; Bernstein's discontinued claims stay discontinued.

Part III leaves actual verdict harmonization and consequences, sustainable individual defenses, capacity attribution, and relief to ordinary adjudication. Harlow and Anderson v. Creighton require personal-damages immunity at the late-October 1991 conduct date and, for any preserved March 1992 claim, its distinct date; later law supplies no retrospective notice. Punitive answers do not replace protection, causation, or immunity. Prospective relief requires a live remedy; elapsed time alone proves neither mootness nor continuing eligibility. Vacatur reinstates neither the two-year chair injunction nor punitive awards and establishes no final liability, immunity, damages, chair entitlement, new March 1992 claim, or universal retrial requirement. Each controlling part has a direct majority; no combined plurality or Marks rule is necessary.

### November 24, 1997 — United States v. Rybar

[Public Projection](../records/united_states_v_rybar_merits_1997-11-24.md#public-projection). Kennedy's Parts I–II command all nine votes; Part III commands eight, with Stone-Zsela dissenting from remand. Part I applies Hale to uphold §922(o)'s possession component as integral to direct control of transfers and private machinegun-stock expansion. The two completed submachine guns and consecutive gun-show sales make the commercial relation concrete, although the convictions are for possession. There is no transaction-specific interstate-crossing element. Marketability, remote interstate history, generalized gun violence, or presumed foreign or military provenance alone is not the ground; Lopez's remote-effects limit survives. No universal legislative-findings requirement or general police power follows. Government channels and lawful pre-May 19, 1986 possession retain their statutory scope; manufacture date alone proves no exception.

Part II permits personal invocation of the Second Amendment without militia enrollment or service. Military usefulness neither excludes an arm nor establishes immunity; commerce does not categorically erase the right, and necessary acquisition connects to protected keeping. Love, Hale, and Miller require separate attention to the actual weapons, conduct, and restriction. Commerce authority and registration compliance do not resolve that inquiry; Staples supplies statutory culpability, not a constitutional weapon classification.

Part III vacates the affirmance of Counts I and III because the militia prerequisite was material and the lower court's additional discussion was not an adequate independent weapon-and-restriction application. The known automatic characteristics strengthen the regulation argument but do not alone resolve the possession restriction. Hale's registration holding does not validate this prohibition. Existing evidence or genuinely necessary ordinary proceedings may supply the application; no empirical study, automatic hearing, new scrutiny tier, comprehensive catalogue, categorical automatic-weapons exclusion, general danger exception, or universal remand rule is required. Ultimate constitutional validity remains open. The Court orders no acquittal, release, weapon return, trial, or resentencing. Dismissed NFA transfer Counts II and IV stay outside the judgment; receivers, conversion parts, accessories, ordinary semiautomatic arms, and every other §922(o) application are not resolved.

### December 1, 1997 — United States v. Parker

[Public Projection](../records/united_states_v_parker_merits_1997-12-01.md#public-projection). O'Connor's unanimous opinion applies Mussari/Schroeder to the original §228: Congress may punish willful nonpayment of a valid, legally fixed support obligation between a Florida obligor and children in Pennsylvania. Different-state residence is essential; nonpayment longer than one year or greater than $5,000 is an alternative threshold, not a substitute for the interstate relation. No completed payment, travel, flight, wire, voluntary crossing, or evasion motive is added. The required cross-border payment, rather than attenuated poverty or welfare costs, supplies the commerce ground under Lopez.

The law regulates the private obligor and does not commandeer a State. New York and Printz/Mack retain their independent constraints; absence of commandeering alone creates no commerce power. Federal prosecution accepts the valid state order and authorizes no creation or revision of support, custody, parentage, or domestic status, or disregard of a lawful state modification. Proper challenges to the predicate's existence and continuing force remain available. Only willful failures after October 25, 1992 constitute the offense; earlier arrears may establish background or past-due status. Every actual element requires proof beyond reasonable doubt, with lawful ability-related and other defenses preserved. Rybar changes neither the direct-payment rule nor its limits. Affirmance reinstates prosecution only; guilt, sentence, restitution amount, and treatment of older arrears in any later restitution order remain undecided.

### December 2, 1997 — Foster v. Love

[Public Projection](../records/foster_v_love_merits_1997-12-02.md#public-projection). Ginsburg's unanimous opinion holds that Louisiana's conclusive October election of a congressional majority winner conflicts with then-operative 2 U.S.C. §§1 and 7 under Congress's Article I, §4 authority. Legal choice is consummated in October where no selection remains on the prescribed November day; calling the event a primary or one stage of a larger process does not cure that conflict. Section 8's vacancy and failure-to-elect provisions remain distinct; a successful final October majority is not failure to elect.

Voting Rights Coalition supports express federal election-administration authority; Thornton's administration/qualifications distinction and Freightliner's actual-conflict principle remain intact. Relief displaces only premature final congressional selection. Compatible preliminary contests, administration, and the open-ballot form remain available, including an October preliminary contest if operative selection occurs on the federal day. No replacement calendar or deadline, particular primary system, comprehensive rule for every preliminary step or later runoff, state-office regulation, presidential-timing ruling, completed-officeholding disruption, additional legislative-purpose holding, or field-preemption ground is adopted.

### December 2, 1997 — Salinas v. United States

[Public Projection](../records/salinas_v_united_states_merits_1997-12-02.md#public-projection). Thomas's unanimous opinion holds on §666 that a bribe need not demonstrably affect federal funds. It expressly reserves any distinct statutory connection between the bribe and expenditure of federal funds and outer constitutional applications; neither reservation creates a nexus test or burden. Actual agency, corrupt intent, acceptance of value to influence or reward, covered entity business or transactions worth at least $5,000, and qualifying federal benefits exceeding $10,000 in the relevant one-year period remain necessary. Section 666(c)'s bona fide compensation and ordinary-course expense exception remains operative. The $5,000 threshold measures covered business or transactions, not automatically the bribe, watches, or truck. Benefits status is assumed for this review; every federal receipt is not declared a benefit. No precise annual amount, intangible-benefit coverage, or reopened valuation is adjudicated.

On §1962(d), the same opinion requires a knowing agreement to further an undertaking that, if carried out, satisfies the substantive enterprise offense; personal commission or personal agreement to commit two predicates is unnecessary. The unlawful object must encompass substantive elements, including Reves's direction requirement, but each conspirator need not necessarily direct the enterprise personally. Mere association, knowledge, friendship, or employment is insufficient. Wells distinguishes actual statutory predicates from added conditions, Ratzlaf preserves contextual construction and residual lenity, and Shabani informs the agreement inquiry only within its scope; no universal conspiracy code follows. Reves remains the substantive rule and had reserved conspiracy.

The personal-two-act instruction objection fails, but the entire charge, an independent personal-direction instruction, general evidentiary sufficiency, harmlessness, general aiding-and-abetting liability, and any general overt-act rule are not decided. The substantive §1962(c) acquittal remains intact. Affirmance of the two bribery convictions and conspiracy conviction enlarges no conviction and directs no new trial, special remand, or independent sentence review.

### December 8, 1997 — Richenberg v. Cohen

[Public Projection](../records/richenberg_v_cohen_merits_1997-12-08.md#public-projection). O'Connor's five-Justice opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Breyer, applies Thomasson's ordinary-rationality standard, conduct-related likelihood beyond abstract desire, and genuine individualized rebuttal requirement to sustain this discharge. Meaningful rebuttal does not require acceptance of every denial, proof of completed prohibited acts, or individual operational injury. Service and character evidence must be actually considered but does not automatically defeat the inference. Two hearings considered that evidence and Richenberg's denial; equivocal answers about future conduct permit the existing conduct-related assessment. Discounting general character testimony may reflect its probative reach rather than refusal to consider it.

Attraction alone remains insufficient and ordinary attraction is not a statutory act. Sparse reasons or a competing inference alone establish neither a constitutional violation nor a general requirement of additional findings. The supported reading requires no invented demeanor finding or additional agency reason; hypothetical appellate reasons cannot replace actual reasons. Section 654(b)(2) permits rebuttal by showing no engagement, attempt, propensity, or intent concerning homosexual acts. Section 654(e)'s permission to omit processing requires avoidance-or-termination intent and military-best-interest findings, neither established here, and creates no retention command.

Walmer's preliminary admitted-conduct case does not decide this statement challenge. Romer preserves ordinary rationality, reasonable prediction, and conceivable legitimate grounds tied to the actual disadvantage; Weiss gives military judgment substantial weight while retaining independent constitutional review. Jeffries's actual-ground requirement survives, but its civilian balance and retaliation same-decision rule do not replace Thomasson. No heightened scrutiny, actual-purpose burden, attraction-alone rule, or universal administrative checklist is adopted. Independent APA reviewability and relief, speech, due process, directive validity, substantive liberty, and other ungranted claims remain unresolved. The discharge stands; no new hearing, reinstatement, or compensation is ordered.

### December 9, 1997 — Jefferson v. City of Tarrant

[Public Projection](../records/jefferson_v_city_of_tarrant_jurisdictional_dismissal_1997-12-09.md#public-projection). Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg, dismisses for lack of a final state judgment under §1257(a). Cox permits bounded review of a finally resolved federal issue where proceedings are practically concluded; the issue necessarily survives every further outcome; effective later review is unavailable whatever the outcome; or reversal would preclude the relevant litigation and postponement seriously threaten federal policy. State certification and importance alone create no finality.

No route applies here. Unlike Richards, the petitioners' claims and disputed liability and causation remain for substantive adjudication; unlike Sullivan, the damages question need not survive every outcome; unlike Labron/Kilgore, effective later correction is available after an appropriate adverse final judgment; unlike Casarotto, reversal would permit damages litigation rather than protect against litigation itself. Ritchie, including note 7, does not require absolute impossibility of later appeal: a possible route did not make renewed presentation useful in its unusual setting, involving disclosure injury, prosecution outcomes, completed trial and appellate review, and an inadequate contempt alternative. ### January 15, 1998 — Inmates of Suffolk County Jail v. Rouse

Part I applies §3626(b)(2)'s independent termination route when the State establishes that covered prospective prison-conditions relief was approved without substantive need, narrowness, and least-intrusiveness findings. The November 26, 1997 amendment applies to this pending case: §3626(b)(3) preserves relief only on written, record-based findings that it remains necessary to correct a current **and** ongoing federal-right violation, extends no further than necessary to correct it, is narrowly drawn, and is the least intrusive necessary means. Lewis and Plyler addressed the former current **or** ongoing text; neither holding is retroactively rewritten. Consent decrees and pretrial detention fall within covered prospective relief and prison conditions. Negotiated protections are not automatically independent federal rights, but a necessary corrective remedy may exceed the minimum substantive conduct rule; exceeding that minimum alone does not justify termination.

The reported judicial assessment of existing findings and periodic monitoring reports since June 14, 1994, including assaults, rape, disease, and related conditions, supports termination. It identifies neither qualifying preservation findings nor a supported federal violation, including a violation meeting the broader former condition but excluded by the amended conjunction; the narrower text changes no outcome on this record. This is substantive findings review, not rejection for omitted statutory words. Compliance with the decree and absence of a violation remain distinct; no comprehensive legality or permanent safety finding follows. Speculative deterioration supplies neither a current violation nor an established refusal to consider qualifying evidence. Independent review and a proper opportunity to present relevant evidence remain necessary, but possible usefulness of more information or unreproduced originals alone requires no new hearing.

Part II permits Congress to change conditions for continuing prospective federal relief while courts determine actual violations, findings, and scope. Plyler directly supplies conditional prospective termination; Robertson and Wheeling Bridge distinguish changed law from adjudication under unchanged law, and Klein permits genuine legal change preserving the judicial task. The statute here commands neither false facts nor annulment of accrued damages or completed historical effects. Morgan Stanley and Plaut retain their defined securities-limitations-dismissal reinstatement holdings, supplying neither an absolute finality bar nor unlimited reopening power. Rufo keeps its separate equitable role. An application in which the amended conjunction excludes an actually supported violation protected by the original safeguard is expressly undecided.

Part III rejects perpetual entitlement to unchanged federal enforcement of mutable prospective decree terms under valid later remedial law. The reported motion, opposition, and review support rejection of the presented procedural objection without presuming an unreported hearing or approving denial of a demonstrated necessary opportunity. Plyler preserves accrued rights and completed effects. Relabeling continuing judicial duties as contractual cannot preserve coercive supervision contrary to governing conditions. The decree's historical existence and independent accrued claims are not erased.

Part IV assumes a contract and substantial impairment only for deciding the objection. Atchison's federal-nonparty rationality framework governs: a federal court's approval and legislation benefiting States do not make the United States a contracting party. Wolfish retains pretrial protection from punishment, and Lewis preserves meaningful rather than nominal access.
