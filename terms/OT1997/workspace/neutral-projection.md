### November 4, 1997 — Bates v. United States

[Public Projection](../records/bates_v_united_states_merits_1997-11-04.md#public-projection). Ginsburg's unanimous Court opinion holds that the version of 20 U.S.C. §1097(a) governing the alleged 1990 conduct requires knowing and willful culpable misapplication, without an additional purpose to injure or defraud. An unpaid refund, accounting mistake, insolvency, or breached funding condition is insufficient by itself. The indictment's deliberate-diversion allegations remain unproved; affirmance allows the reinstated twelve-count prosecution to proceed.

Wells governs fidelity to actual enacted elements; Ratzlaf's contextual willfulness and independently sufficient residual-lenity ground, and Staples, Morissette, and Liparota's protection of offense-specific fault remain intact. Text, context, and the distinct bank-misapplication history leave no residual ambiguity requiring the proposed additional purpose here. The complete meaning of willfulness, including knowledge of illegality, every conversion theory, aiding-and-abetting questions, and independent indictment objections are not decided. No universal rule about statutory silence or mental state is adopted; the later refund-specific amendment is not applied retroactively. Guilt, sufficiency, instructions, and sentence remain outside this pretrial construction ruling.

### November 4, 1997 — State Oil Co. v. Khan

[Public Projection](../records/state_oil_co_v_khan_merits_1997-11-04.md#public-projection). Scalia's unanimous Court opinion replaces Albrecht's per se condemnation of vertical maximum resale-price agreements with rule-of-reason review, effective this date. Albrecht is overruled only to that extent. Per se condemnation requires a sound basis for treating the category as predictably harmful; a ceiling may constrain dealer markups and improve interbrand competition as well as produce harms requiring proof. State Oil's rebate obligation is an actual price agreement, not a unilateral suggestion.

Sylvania and Business Electronics inform the vertical-restraint distinction; Atlantic Richfield and Brunswick preserve independent antitrust injury; Kodak and Brooke Group retain their distinct proof and competitive-harm rules. Minimum resale-price and horizontal price-fixing rules remain unchanged. Harmful service suppression, exclusion, exploitation of dependence, disguised minimum pricing, and proved horizontal arrangements retain their governing rules. Dealer lost margin alone is not antitrust injury. The Court decides no market power, actual harm, damages, expert-evidence outcome, or ultimate liability, and creates no general stare decisis checklist from Hubbard's divided rationales. Lower courts properly followed Albrecht before this overruling. The antitrust component is vacated for rule-of-reason proceedings; the district court's antitrust summary judgment is not automatically restored, all evidence or injury issues are not automatically reopened, and the separate contract dismissal stands.

### November 17, 1997 — City of Monroe v. United States

[Public Projection](../records/city_of_monroe_v_united_states_summary_merits_1997-11-17.md#public-projection). Per curiam Part I, joined by all nine Justices, permits an enforcement court to determine the actual scope and applicability of Attorney General preclearance while forbidding substantive preclearance or reconsideration of approval's wisdom; actual changes must be fairly identified. Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg, holds that the specifically submitted and approved 1968 statewide majority default independently governs Monroe's silent charter. The unprecleared 1966 local amendment does not negate that independent authority. The conflicting plurality injunction is reversed to that limited extent.

City of Rome is distinguished because its charter expressly required plurality; its rejection of implied clearance for local amendments remains binding. McCain and Clark preserve specific submission and the submitting authority's responsibility for ambiguity. Lopez, Young, and Foreman reject clearance by association, long use, or state compulsion. No exemption for state-compelled administration, blanket approval of local implementation, or clearance of an approved delegation's distinct local choices follows. An ambiguous submission would not establish the same approval. The 1966 amendment and other charter revisions remain unapproved; genuinely distinct changes require ordinary coverage and preclearance treatment. Bossier's substantive rules and independent §2 discrimination claims remain separate. No damages, completed-election consequences, replacement-system design, automatic new approval request, or automatic evidentiary hearing is decided.

### November 17, 1997 — Jeffries v. Harleston

[Public Projection](../records/jeffries_v_harleston_merits_1997-11-17.md#public-projection). Souter's Parts I and III command five direct votes (Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer); Part II commands those five plus Stone-Zsela. Six Justices support vacatur; the controlling five-vote mandate does not require a new trial.

Part I rejects final defense judgment on the panel's employment balance. Pickering and Connick require attention to the actual speech, service relationship, and institutional needs. Retained tenure and lack of entitlement to a longer chair term do not categorically defeat retaliation; a reasonable forecast matters but does not alone decide protection. The public-concern address was off campus at an unaffiliated event and was not assigned or controlled as official duty; introduction by the chair title did not establish sponsorship. Real administrative duties and recruitment, fundraising, faculty-interaction, and community interests remain relevant, but no special policymaking, confidential, or additional public-contact role was established. Both jury findings—no actual disruption and reasonable expected disruption—stand. Prevention need not await actual harm. Waters protects a genuinely relied-upon reasonable account only if it describes legally disciplinable speech; no complete investigation code is adopted. Perry, Umbehr, and O'Hare preserve the distinction between benefit entitlement and retaliation. No categorical protection of the entire address, actual-loss requirement, quantified institutional-damages requirement, or wholesale NTEU prospective-ban burden follows; Settle remains confined to its student-curriculum setting.

Part II requires each defendant's actual causal responsibility and a real counterfactual under Mt. Healthy, Umbehr, O'Hare, Kimberlin, and Crawford-El. Once protected speech is a substantial or motivating cause, the same-decision defense requires proof by a preponderance, not a conceivable lawful reason. Nine successful one-year votes and differentiated animus or punitive findings do not automatically establish an independent lawful Board decision. A causally effective proposal may matter, but procurement alone proves no liability; nonvoting proposals, defeated votes, and abstention neither establish nor eliminate other supported causal contributions. Competent direct and circumstantial proof applies without a heightened motive burden. A genuine independent lawful decision or supported personal defense may still dispose of a claim; Bernstein's discontinued claims stay discontinued.

Part III leaves actual verdict harmonization and consequences, sustainable individual defenses, capacity attribution, and relief to ordinary adjudication. Harlow and Anderson v. Creighton require personal-damages immunity at the late-October 1991 conduct date and, for any preserved March 1992 claim, its distinct date; later law supplies no retrospective notice. Punitive answers do not replace protection, causation, or immunity. Prospective relief requires a live remedy; elapsed time alone proves neither mootness nor continuing eligibility. Vacatur reinstates neither the two-year chair injunction nor punitive awards and establishes no final liability, immunity, damages, chair entitlement, new March 1992 claim, or universal retrial requirement. Each controlling part has a direct majority; no combined plurality or Marks rule is necessary.

### November 24, 1997 — United States v. Rybar

[Public Projection](../records/united_states_v_rybar_merits_1997-11-24.md#public-projection). Kennedy's Parts I–II command all nine votes; Part III commands eight, with Stone-Zsela dissenting from remand. Part I applies Hale to uphold §922(o)'s possession component as integral to direct control of transfers and private machinegun-stock expansion. The two completed submachine guns and consecutive gun-show sales make the commercial relation concrete, although the convictions are for possession. There is no transaction-specific interstate-crossing element. Marketability, remote interstate history, generalized gun violence, or presumed foreign or military provenance alone is not the ground; Lopez's remote-effects limit survives. No universal legislative-findings requirement or general police power follows. Government channels and lawful pre-May 19, 1986 possession retain their statutory scope; manufacture date alone proves no exception.

Part II permits personal invocation of the Second Amendment without militia enrollment or service. Military usefulness neither excludes an arm nor establishes immunity; commerce does not categorically erase the right, and necessary acquisition connects to protected keeping. Love, Hale, and Miller require separate attention to the actual weapons, conduct, and restriction. Commerce authority and registration compliance do not resolve that inquiry; Staples supplies statutory culpability, not a constitutional weapon classification.

Part III vacates the affirmance of Counts I and III because the militia prerequisite was material and the lower court's additional discussion was not an adequate independent weapon-and-restriction application. The known automatic characteristics strengthen the regulation argument but do not alone resolve the possession restriction. Hale's registration holding does not validate this prohibition. Existing evidence or genuinely necessary ordinary proceedings may supply the application; no empirical study, automatic hearing, new scrutiny tier, comprehensive catalogue, categorical automatic-weapons exclusion, general danger exception, or universal remand rule is required. Ultimate constitutional validity remains open. The Court orders no acquittal, release, weapon return, trial, or resentencing. Dismissed NFA transfer Counts II and IV stay outside the judgment; receivers, conversion parts, accessories, ordinary semiautomatic arms, and every other §922(o) application are not resolved.

### December 1, 1997 — United States v. Parker

[Public Projection](../records/united_states_v_parker_merits_1997-12-01.md#public-projection). O'Connor's unanimous opinion applies Mussari/Schroeder to the original §228: Congress may punish willful nonpayment of a valid, legally fixed support obligation between a Florida obligor and children in Pennsylvania. Different-state residence is essential; nonpayment longer than one year or greater than $5,000 is an alternative threshold, not a substitute for the interstate relation. No completed payment, travel, flight, wire, voluntary crossing, or evasion motive is added. The required cross-border payment, rather than attenuated poverty or welfare costs, supplies the commerce ground under Lopez.

The law regulates the private obligor and does not commandeer a State. New York and Printz/Mack retain their independent constraints; absence of commandeering alone creates no commerce power. Federal prosecution accepts the valid state order and authorizes no creation or revision of support, custody, parentage, or domestic status, or disregard of a lawful state modification. ##### Patient exposure and the distinct reduction-of-care theory

**Holding and operative rule:** At summary judgment, a plaintiff seeking prospective relief must support a concrete, actual or imminent personal injury with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, allowing favorable reasonable inferences. **Authority:** Stevens's Part I-A has all nine Justices. Part I-B, addressing the separate care-protection theory, has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in that disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires evidence connecting the particular plaintiff to the threatened harm at the litigation stage, and Lyons distinguishes an existing personal threat from speculation about future exposure. Elsner's illness and depression make her concern personal, but do not establish the requests, medical and witness decisions, continuing impairment and ingestion necessary for the injury alleged. Failure to prove vileness separately required life; death also required unanimous agreement. The latter directions did not convert the preceding discretionary choice into a mandatory death sentence. Background and emotional-disturbance evidence could therefore support life without a false answer about an aggravating fact.

The contrary reading treats the disjunctive wording and later failure-of-aggravation paragraph as confining life to absence of vileness. Read together with the initial choice and paragraph three's express post-aggravator alternative, they do not create the required reasonable likelihood. Two days of testimony, both sides' explanation of mitigation, the prosecutor's acknowledgment that vileness did not require death, and the verdict form's reference to mitigation confirm the judicially authorized route. Counsel and admitted evidence do not cure an otherwise restrictive instruction. Delo permits an evidentiary predicate for a particular-factor instruction; Tuilaepa permits individualized selection without fixed labels or weights. Neither makes admissibility alone sufficient. The Court finds no instruction error, no actual juror mental state, and no evidentiary predicate for every requested factor. It prescribes no universal wording or favorable weight and does not decide an unpresented mitigation-unanimity challenge.

**Holding and operative rule:** Virginia's identified mitigation factors and provision permitting relevant mitigation evidence do not by themselves create a federal due-process entitlement to each requested instructional formulation. No independent state-law entitlement to specified wording or separately preserved arbitrary refusal to honor such an entitlement is established here.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls with eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees in rejection of this distinct claim only.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory allowance of offense circumstances, history, background and other mitigation preserves a broad evidentiary field; its list is not exclusive. That permission does not require the judge to give the requested formulations for prior criminal history, emotional disturbance, impaired capacity and age. The constitutional effective-route inquiry remains separate and is answered in Part I. The Court neither determines every factor's factual support nor decides a different claim that Virginia arbitrarily disregarded an established instruction entitlement.

### January 21, 1998 — LaChance v. Erickson / LaChance v. McManus

#### Holdings

##### Due process protects a meaningful answer, not proved knowing falsity

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fifth Amendment's opportunity to answer an agency misconduct charge does not immunize a knowingly false factual denial from an otherwise lawful employment falsification charge or penalty consideration. An unsuccessful defense is not itself a knowing lie: the agency must establish the actual false statement and requisite culpability under the governing employment rules, while preserving good-faith factual contest, mistakes, uncertain recollection, legal disagreement and procedural nonadmission.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion has all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Loudermill requires notice, an explanation of the employer's evidence and a meaningful opportunity to respond, with adequate later process; it does not presume that an inaccurate statement is knowingly false. Bryson rejects a general privilege to meet an official inquiry with deliberate falsehood, while its criminal statutory elements and proof requirements remain distinct. A denial's grammatical form does not make proved deception necessary to meaningful participation. The concern that an innocent employee will be punished twice merely for losing is answered by proof of the actual falsification charge and lawful process, not immunity after deliberate factual deception is established. A separate supported determination may use evidence also relevant to the underlying misconduct; no different-source or corroboration requirement is created. Insistence that the agency prove its case and disagreement about legal characterization are not automatically false assertions of fact. Nor does absence of an oath create the categorical immunity at issue. These are employment proceedings, with no criminal jury, criminal burden or universal materiality element added. Genuine self-incrimination questions remain separate.

##### The Civil Service Reform Act supplies safeguards without denial immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 7513 and 7701 do not confer categorical immunity for a proved knowingly false factual denial. Their cause, procedure, proof and review safeguards continue to govern every particular charge and sanction; rejection of immunity establishes no employee's falsity, service nexus, regulatory liability or final penalty.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion is joined by all seven other Associate Justices, providing eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejection of the statutory immunity claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 7513(a) permits covered action only for cause promoting service efficiency. Section 7513(b) ordinarily requires at least thirty days' advance written notice of specific reasons; its exception requires reasonable cause to believe the employee committed a crime punishable by imprisonment, not a finding made here. The employee receives a reasonable period of at least seven days to answer orally and in writing and furnish affidavits and documents, representation, and a written decision with specific reasons at the earliest practicable date. The agency may provide a hearing by regulation under subsection (c); that permission is distinct from Board hearing and representation rights under §7701. Subsections (d) and (e) preserve Board appeal and maintenance of the notice, response, decision, action order and supporting materials, available to the employee or Board upon request.

Ordinary misconduct requires preponderance proof under §7701(c); specified performance and senior-executive matters retain their separate substantial-evidence rule. Otherwise supported action still cannot be sustained if the employee shows harmful procedural error, a prohibited personnel practice or legal invalidity. Section 7703(c)'s record-based review for unlawfulness, procedural deficiency, arbitrariness or abuse, and lack of substantial evidence is appellate review, not the agency's proof burden; designated discrimination facts retain their distinct de novo treatment. Section 7703(d)'s OPM substantial-policy-impact and erroneous-interpretation predicates, reconsideration prerequisite when the Director did not intervene, and discretionary appellate review remain intact. None of these protections requires immunity for deliberate falsehood. The employee retains a meaningful answer and review while the agency bears its actual burden. The Court finds no notice exception, review-prerequisite failure or new disciplinary ground in these cases.

##### Penalty consideration and impeachment remain conditional

**Holding and operative rule:** A proved knowing false denial may receive otherwise lawful consideration in selecting an employment penalty or assessing credibility; the categorical bars resting on denial immunity cannot stand. Such use is permitted, not compelled, and an adverse finding on the underlying misconduct alone establishes neither falsification nor an entitlement to enhance a sanction or impeach the employee.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela additionally joins its penalty-consideration proposition and its actual-proof and lawful-procedure qualifications, but agrees only in the disposition removing the distinct impeachment bar.

**Controlling explanation:** The protected chance to contest an allegation does not require an adjudicator to disregard a properly established deliberate deception. Yet relevance, proof of the actual charge, procedural fairness and a lawful penalty basis remain necessary. The statutory decisionmaker must distinguish a knowing factual falsehood from an honestly held denial, ambiguity, memory failure or legal nonadmission. The same safeguards prevent automatic escalation based merely on an employee's unsuccessful defense. The Court therefore removes the categorical penalty and impeachment bars, including the identified McManus impeachment ruling, without selecting an adverse inference or sanction and without displacing unrelated evidence rules.

##### The reporting branch and self-incrimination require their own predicates

**Holding and operative rule:** The Barrett/Roberts reporting disposition is set aside only insofar as it rests on categorical immunity from providing information because it would implicate the employee; further proceedings must address the actual duty and every supported defense. Rejecting a right to lie neither compels waiver of a valid privilege nor decides a genuine compelled-incrimination claim, and it creates no general duty to confess.

**Authority:** Part IV of Souter's opinion has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela joins only the proposition preserving separate compelled-incrimination review and prohibiting compulsory waiver of a valid privilege; he agrees only in the reporting disposition itself.

**Controlling explanation:** Garrity bars criminal use of answers compelled by a threat of public-employment loss. Gardner forbids discharge solely for refusal to waive protection against criminal use of compelled testimony and its fruits, while distinguishing questions specifically, directly and narrowly concerning official duties without a required waiver. The reproduced 5 C.F.R. §5.4 concerns information required by the designated civil-service bodies or authorized representatives and retains its exception for disclosure prohibited by law or regulation. These actual conditions cannot be replaced with either an unrestricted duty to report or automatic immunity whenever information may be self-implicating. No respondent-specific compulsion, criminal-use threat, refusal, immunity or waiver is established here. The Court selects no silence penalty or adverse inference and establishes no universal prohibition on otherwise lawful civil inferences. Barrett and Roberts's unreviewed false-time determinations remain undisturbed; the dependent reporting and investigatory-falsification issues return for lawful consideration.

#### Precedent Treatment

### January 21, 1998 — Lunding v. New York Tax Appeals Tribunal

[Public Projection](../records/lunding_v_new_york_tax_appeals_tribunal_merits_1998-01-21.md#public-projection). The following complete public blocks preserve this event's action, proposition-specific authority, published positions, procedure and source limits.

#### Event

Lunding v. New York Tax Appeals Tribunal, No. 96-1462. Plenary merits decision, January 21, 1998; argued November 5, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the New York Court of Appeals, 89 N.Y.2d 283, 675 N.E.2d 816 (1996), which sustained N.Y. Tax Law § 631(b)(6). The question is whether denying qualifying alimony deductions in calculating nonresidents' New York-source income, within the complete nonresident tax formula, violates Article IV's Privileges and Immunities Clause. Equal Protection and Commerce Clause claims are outside this review.

Christopher and Barbara Lunding were Connecticut residents. Christopher earned income practicing law in New York and paid alimony to a Connecticut former spouse under a Connecticut decree. They claimed a proportionate deduction using approximately 48% of his business income as attributable to New York. Disallowance produced an additional assessment of $3,724 plus interest.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

#### Public Action

The judgment of the New York Court of Appeals is reversed, and the case is remanded for nondiscriminatory treatment of the challenged assessment and appropriate relief under the governing state procedures and constitutional requirements.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Reverse and remand, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Kennedy and Ginsburg.

State proceedings must provide lawful allocation and constitutionally adequate relief. If payment was compelled before a meaningful opportunity to contest the tax, adequate backward-looking correction is required. Independently, if the State clearly held out a postpayment remedy and payment occurred, it may not withdraw that remedy afterward, including for voluntary payments. Actual payment, preservation, amount and applicable procedural conditions remain to be established. No fixed refund, interest award or replacement formula is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer. The same seven Justices adopt both holdings and all their qualifications. Ginsburg dissents, joined in full by Kennedy, on both the income-attribution and independent home-State personal-expense grounds.

#### Holdings

##### Article IV does not permit this inadequately justified categorical exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State taxes another State's citizens on income earned within its borders, a discriminatory deduction rule must rest on a substantial reason beyond noncitizenship, and the discrimination must bear a close relationship to that reason. Section 631(b)(6)'s categorical exclusion of qualifying alimony from deductions against New York-source income fails that inquiry on the source, personal-expense, initial-computation and family-income justifications offered here; genuine source allocation remains permissible, and identical resident and nonresident tax codes are not required.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice Opinion of the Court supplies direct majority authority for this rule, its application and the qualifications below.

**Controlling explanation:** Toomer protects substantial equality in earning a livelihood across state lines while allowing considerable state latitude. Shaffer permits taxation of local income and corresponding source-related deduction limits; Travis nevertheless rejects unjustified unequal personal allowances. Those decisions require examination of the particular deduction and tax, not ordinary rationality alone or mechanical equality.

New York taxes residents' worldwide income and nonresidents' local income. That real difference does not by itself justify excluding all proportionate recognition of alimony. The payment adjusts income available to the payer and lacks an exclusively Connecticut source merely because the former spouses and divorce decree are there. The lower court's Goodwin analogy involved geographically connected home-State property and medical expenses; it does not resolve this alimony provision. The Court adopts no rule requiring apportionment of every personal expense.

The whole calculation also matters. Qualifying alimony reduces hypothetical resident liability and the federal-adjusted-gross-income denominator, but § 631(b)(6) denies the deduction in the New York-source numerator. The initial benefit therefore does not establish substantial equality in the final burden. The ratio was uncapped; no particular actual ratio or total effective rate is found.

The asserted allocation of taxable family income likewise fails to match the enacted line. Payer residence determines the deduction without conditioning it on whether New York taxes the recipient. Examining that mismatch addresses the classification imposing the Lundings' own assessment, without finding how often different family arrangements occur. Neither a hypothetical recipient-based rule nor its constitutionality is decided.

Austin prevents another State's credit, deduction or fiscal choice from curing established discrimination. Connecticut's absence of earned-income taxation in this year supplies no cure. Fulton and Oregon Waste reinforce scrutiny of actual operation and unsupported offsets only by analogy; their Commerce Clause tests do not govern Article IV. States retain justified source limits and permissible cooperative tax arrangements.

##### Relief follows the established tax-remedy predicates

**Holding and operative rule:** The unconstitutional treatment of the challenged assessment requires reversal and remand for lawful state allocation, severability and constitutionally adequate relief. When the State compelled payment before meaningful review, it must provide adequate backward-looking correction; independently, a clearly held-out postpayment remedy cannot be withdrawn after payment, including voluntary payment, subject to ordinary proof and lawful previously announced conditions.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices join the Opinion of the Court's remedial holding in full.

**Controlling explanation:** McKesson requires meaningful correction when payment preceded an adequate opportunity to challenge; Harper distinguishes application of the governing federal rule from the lawful remedy. Fulton preserves state severability and remedial choices, including lawful refunds, constitutionally permissible nondiscriminatory retrospective equalization, or a lawful combination when backward-looking relief is required. Reich separately prevents a State from withdrawing a clear postpayment route after the taxpayer paid and substituting an unpursued prepayment route. Adequate prospective procedures remain permissible with fair notice.

The reported $3,724 additional assessment plus interest does not establish payment or an entitlement to that sum as a refund. The State must apply the constitutional rule through the procedures and payment facts actually established. The Court neither selects the taxpayers' proposed deduction formula as the exclusive remedy nor automatically restores every aspect of the Appellate Division's order. It orders no interest award, new assessment against others, or reopening of unrelated final tax years.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Toomer is applied for substantial equality, a substantial independent reason and the close relationship required between that reason and the discriminatory treatment.
- Shaffer retains nondiscriminatory source taxation and justified corresponding deduction limits; it supplies no categorical exception for every deduction labeled personal.
- Travis is applied to distinguish legitimate source limits from inadequately justified unequal personal allowances. Its rejection of presumed offsets remains operative.
- Austin is applied to reject a cure dependent on another State's tax choices; permissible cooperative arrangements remain available.
- Goodwin's reported home-State property and medical-expense setting is distinguished; that different deduction setting does not resolve this alimony provision.
- Friedsam's earlier statutory construction was superseded by § 631(b)(6); it is not revived as the constitutional rule or imposed as the exclusive replacement formula.
- Fulton and Oregon Waste supply analogical support concerning actual tax operation and unsupported offset explanations. Their Commerce Clause tests and Fulton's limited treatment of Darnell remain confined to their fields.
- McKesson, Harper, Fulton and Reich are applied to preserve conditional backward-looking correction, lawful state remedial choices and the independent protection of a clearly offered postpayment remedy.

#### Law After Decision

A State's narrower nonresident tax base and the personal character of a deduction do not automatically justify denying it. Courts must examine the challenged distinction in the complete tax scheme, require a substantial independent reason closely related to it, and preserve justified source allocation. Section 631(b)(6)'s categorical alimony exclusion cannot be sustained on the reasons offered here.

This decision adds that Article IV application without requiring identical deductions, strict numerical tax equality, the Commerce Clause's compensatory-tax elements or a particular replacement formula. It decides no Equal Protection or Commerce Clause claim, no dispute about federal alimony qualification, and no hypothetical recipient-based alternative. The existing conditional rules governing unconstitutional-tax relief remain in force.

#### Separate Writings

Ginsburg, joined by Kennedy, would affirm on two independent grounds.

First, the payer deduction and recipient inclusion allocate taxable income between former spouses. When both are New York residents, the State taxes the recipient and permits the payer's deduction; when both are Connecticut residents, denying the deduction preserves taxation of New York-source income that would otherwise pass outside the State's resident tax base. In their view, that source-tax and income-attribution relationship supplies a substantial justification in this case. Article IV requires substantial rather than mechanically precise equality. Imperfect treatment of other, mixed-residence couples does not establish these taxpayers' entitlement to remove the income from New York taxation. This position does not depend on a finding of aggregate interstate-payment balance or on Connecticut's imposing a tax.

Second, even treating alimony as an expense, the Connecticut payer, recipient and decree locate this particular marital obligation in the home State; it is not an expense of producing New York professional income. Shaffer and Travis permit the corresponding source limitation, and Goodwin's home-State expense analysis supports leaving that personal expense to Connecticut's law and policy. Travis's general personal exemption is different from a deduction for a particular nonlocal obligation. They also object that correlation with a payer's income does not distinguish alimony from many other personal expenditures; the Court's disclaimer of a universal deduction rule does not, in their view, resolve that distinction.

Their objection to relying on other former couples is a merits objection, not a vote to dismiss for lack of standing. They accept Austin's rule against curing established discrimination through another State's tax choices but find this allocation justified in the first place. They join neither the invalidity holding nor the correction mandate; their grounds are noncontrolling.

The case returns to the New York courts for proceedings consistent with the Article IV ruling, lawful allocation and state-law severability, and appropriate relief under applicable preserved procedures. No particular deduction formula, refund, interest award, compulsory hearing, retained Supreme Court jurisdiction or automatic further review is directed. This Supreme Court review is complete.

#### Holdings

##### The announced reasonable-doubt polling threshold is permissible

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board may require an employer to possess a good-faith reasonable doubt, supported by objective considerations, of an incumbent union's majority support before conducting a safeguarded poll. Using the same prerequisite for polling, withdrawing recognition and seeking a Board election is not, by itself, irrational or inconsistent with the Act; objective doubt is uncertainty about majority support, not proof that the union has lost it.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Act protects employee choice and established bargaining relationships. Auciello permits explained stability policies within statutory boundaries, while Fall River separates their rationality from factual application. Neither decides this polling rule in advance. Even a safeguarded employer poll may unsettle employees and the bargaining relationship. The Board may account for that risk before permitting a poll.

The employer argues that evidence sufficient to withdraw recognition makes polling pointless. But an employer may reasonably wish to determine actual preferences before taking the more consequential step of withdrawing recognition; a lawful option to withdraw is not an obligation to do so. A poll therefore retains a confirmatory use. Differences between a poll and a Board election—including the statutory one-year bar after a valid election—do not compel the same consequences or a judicially selected lower polling threshold. An unsuccessful union in an employer poll may seek a Board election.

Sections 7 and 8(c) protect employee choice and noncoercive expression, but do not themselves establish an unconditional right to poll an incumbent unit. Gissel left undecided whether its recognized allowances for reasonable inquiry are mandatory. Compliance with Struksnes safeguards does not alone answer the separate incumbent-polling prerequisite. The rule is permissible, not compelled for every future Board, and does not authorize a concealed requirement of actual minority proof. Certification and contract protections during their lawful duration, statutory representation routes and protection against coercion remain distinct. No freestanding First Amendment question is decided.

##### The presented challenge reaches the standard actually applied

**Holding and operative rule:** A challenge expressly directed to the amount of evidence demanded before polling fairly includes whether the Board applied its announced reasonable-doubt standard to the order under review. That inquiry reaches the associated whole-record application here; it creates no general permission to add an independent factual question merely because merits briefs discuss it.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, supplies five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The petition challenges a requirement of so much evidence of lost support that a poll becomes meaningless. Allentown's contention that the agency substituted individual repudiations for reasonable uncertainty concerns that asserted evidentiary demand, not a separate judgment. Hubbard preserves the difference between supporting arguments and enlarged questions. Examining the actual standard and the material reports is necessary to resolve this challenge. The Court does not reopen the successor finding, the unit or independently unpreserved objections.

##### An announced doubt standard cannot silently become minority proof

**Holding and operative rule:** Under NLRA §§10(e)–(f) and APA §706, the Board's finding must be supported by substantial evidence on the record as a whole under the substantive standard actually governing the case. When the announced predicate is objective reasonable doubt, the agency cannot sustain its order by effectively demanding proof approaching actual loss of majority support, or silently convert recurring evidentiary outcomes into a different substantive or proof rule.

**Authority:** Scalia's Parts III–IV have Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, providing five direct votes for this framework and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Universal Camera requires relevant evidence a reasonable mind could accept as adequate, considered together with what fairly detracts from the finding. It preserves the Board's choice between fairly conflicting views; it does not permit the governing standard to change during application. Auciello likewise distinguishes circumstantial objective doubt from a numerical showing of actual minority. The agency's §10(c) preponderance burden and the reviewing court's substantial-evidence inquiry remain separate.

An employer's uncertainty may be reasonable without direct repudiations from a counted majority. A course of adverse findings cannot, without a lawful change in rule, redefine doubt as established opposition. Expertise and recurring evidentiary judgments matter, but do not replace the announced legal predicate. Lawful, expressly adopted presumptions or evidentiary rules remain possible, subject to their statutory basis and ordinary review; the Court abolishes no such authority and makes no finding of systematic manipulation. Neither subjective suspicion nor every unverified report necessarily establishes objective doubt. The question remains whether this whole record supports the particular finding under the rule the Board announced.

##### This record requires denial of enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** The finding that Allentown lacked objective reasonable doubt when it announced the poll is unsupported by substantial evidence on the whole record. The enforcement judgment must therefore be reversed with instructions to deny enforcement of the challenged Board order; the record does not call for another agency weighing under the same or a newly demanding threshold.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV has Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, supplying five direct votes for the application and complete mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The ALJ accepted six individual expressions and assumed a seventh supported doubt. Those seven are neither a numerical threshold nor the entire evidentiary picture. Marsh's dissatisfaction with representation, Bloch's report that the night shift did not want the union, and Mohr's assessment as shop steward and bargaining-committee member add material circumstantial force. Group reports are not mechanically counted as additional, nonoverlapping repudiations.

The ALJ identified weaknesses in the reports' sources, scope, interview setting and relation to the rehired workforce, and expressly found Mohr's statement insufficient even in combination with the other evidence. The Court does not erase that cumulative assessment. But the Board separately said the employer could not legitimately rely on Mohr because he described the old workforce. His union responsibilities and the substantial continuity of employees make that dismissal untenable. His failure to specify the size of a majority or personally renounce the union does not answer whether management reasonably doubted continued support.

Taken together, the material reports establish objective uncertainty even though they do not establish actual minority. The source and coverage limitations do not adequately sustain this contrary finding under the announced doubt standard. This is not substitution of a preferred inference between fairly conflicting views: the required quantum was effectively raised toward proof of lost support. The later 19–13 poll supplies no retroactive justification. The holding determines the legality of this enforcement request, not present preferences, an independent right to cease bargaining in every setting, or a permanent representation result.

##### The separate remedy objection remains outside review

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 10(e) bars an objection not urged before the Board unless extraordinary circumstances excuse the omission. The separate objection to the bargaining order's explanation and decertification bar was not preserved, and no such excuse is established; that objection is not reviewed.

**Authority:** Part V has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in leaving the objection unreviewed.

**Controlling explanation:** Auciello preserves §10(e)'s distinction between the particular objection presented to the Board and another objection later asserted in court. The preserved threshold and application challenges do not themselves preserve this separate remedial attack. Correcting the predicate of the existing order resolves enforcement without deciding the barred objection or approving every possible bargaining remedy.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Auciello's accepted-offer cutoff remains intact. Its statutory limits, distinction between circumstantial doubt and actual minority, and separate §10(e) requirement are applied; it had not resolved incumbent polling or the weight of these reports.
- Fall River supplies separate review of rational Board policy and supported factual application. Its continuity and successor principles are not reopened, and its rebuttable presumption does not become proof of employee preference.
- Universal Camera supplies whole-record substantial-evidence review, including contrary evidence and the examiner's actual findings, while retaining deference between fairly conflicting views. The Court applies that standard to this unsupported finding rather than replacing it with de novo factfinding.
- Gissel retains its protection of noncoercive expression and its attention to the employment setting. Its reservation concerning mandatory permission for reasonable inquiry is preserved; the Court derives no unconditional polling entitlement from it.
- Hubbard's distinction between supporting arguments and new questions is applied to the wording and challenged order here. No exception based merely on merits briefing is adopted.
- Struksnes's procedural safeguards and the additional advance-notice requirement remain operative. Their satisfaction does not eliminate the separately permissible incumbent-polling prerequisite.

#### Law After Decision

The reasonable-doubt prerequisite remains a permissible policy for polling an incumbent unit with a rebuttable presumption of majority support. The decision adds a controlling application of that rule: objective uncertainty may rest on cumulative circumstantial evidence, and an agency must apply its announced predicate without silently raising it toward proof of actual minority. No headcount or replacement polling test is adopted. The Court's direct majorities govern their respective questions; the statutory invalidity proposal and the application dissent are not controlling law.

The existing safeguards remain: a purpose of determining the truth of the union's majority claim, communication of that purpose, assurances against reprisal, secret ballot, absence of unfair labor practices or a coercive atmosphere, and advance notice of time and place. Threats, reprisals, promises of benefit and coercive questioning remain regulable. Lawful certification and contract protections, representation procedures and the distinct withdrawal standard retain their own predicates. A future lawful policy may alter the polling rule within statutory bounds, but may not supply a retroactively higher prerequisite to revive this order. No independent First Amendment holding or determination of current employee support is made.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in Parts I and III–IV and the judgment and dissenting from Part II.** The Act's protection of employee choice does not adequately justify demanding essentially the same stringent showing before a safeguarded inquiry as before recognition withdrawal or a binding Board election. Those acts have different consequences: inquiry can resolve uncertainty, while withdrawal immediately changes bargaining and an election invokes distinct statutory rules. Stability is legitimate, but cannot make the rebuttable presumption practically untestable. Compliance with safeguards, independent secret-ballot supervision and the absence of a finding that this poll itself coerced employees support the statutory objection. The writing would invalidate the existing unitary polling threshold while preserving neutral purpose, notice, anti-reprisal assurances, secrecy, advance notice and protection against coercive unfair labor practices. It leaves lawful conclusive periods, the withdrawal standard and a prospectively adopted, source-grounded polling policy intact, and decides no independent First Amendment question. The authors also fully join the Court's independent substantial-evidence ground and denial-of-enforcement mandate even though the facial standard survives.

**Breyer, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in Parts I–II and V and dissenting from Parts III–IV and the judgment.** The granted rule challenge does not require an independent factual-sufficiency decision. If that issue is reached, Universal Camera requires respecting a supported agency choice between competing inferences. Objective doubt operates in the labor setting, where interviews, ambiguity about dissatisfaction versus opposition, incomplete sources for group reports, departmental coverage and the retained workforce affect evidentiary force. The ALJ gave actual reasons and expressly assessed Mohr together with the other reports; the adopted assessment need not be recast as a hidden majority-count requirement. Mohr's union role and continuity give his account weight, but do not require the conclusion management urges. These Justices accept that the agency must obey its governing legal and proof standards and reject a categorical bar to circumstantial evidence; they disagree that the actual application crossed those limits. They would affirm the enforcement judgment and order no further agency proceeding.

#### Procedure After Action

The case returns to the D.C. Circuit with instructions to deny enforcement of the Board order under review. This Supreme Court review is complete. No renewed agency application, evidentiary hearing, new poll, election, permanent decertification, present-preference finding or retained Supreme Court jurisdiction is ordered. Ordinary future representation proceedings remain subject to their own lawful predicates and safeguards.

### January 26, 1998 — Brogan v. United States

[Public Projection](../records/brogan_v_united_states_merits_1998-01-26.md#public-projection). The following complete public blocks preserve this event's action, proposition-specific authority, published positions, procedure and source limits.

#### Event

Brogan v. United States, No. 96-1579. Plenary merits decision, January 26, 1998; argued December 2, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, United States v. Wiener, 96 F.3d 35 (1996). The question is whether the version of 18 U.S.C. §1001 governing Brogan's October 4, 1993 statement excludes a bare false denial of wrongdoing during federal investigative questioning.

Department of Labor and Internal Revenue Service agents made an unannounced visit to Brogan's home. He agreed to answer and denied receiving cash or gifts from JRD Management. The agents already had payment records; only afterward did they reveal those records and warn that lying to investigators was criminal. He did not change his answer, and no further questions were asked. A jury convicted him of the false statement and unlawful employer payments. The latter conviction is outside this review.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

#### Public Action

The judgment on the reviewed §1001 conviction is affirmed. The statute supplies no categorical exculpatory-no exception for an otherwise covered false statement merely because it is a simple denial of wrongdoing.

#### Holdings

##### A false negative answer has no categorical exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** The offense-date §1001 does not categorically exclude an otherwise covered, knowingly and willfully false material denial merely because it is a bare response to investigative questioning about the speaker's wrongdoing. Every actual statutory predicate remains necessary; this decision adopts no universal materiality rule for every §1001 clause and decides no immaterial-statement application.

**Authority:** Thomas's Parts I–II, III-A and IV are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, providing six direct votes for the principal statutory framework. Ginsburg independently agrees with the narrow no-exception proposition in her judgment concurrence. Part III-B(1)'s limited response to the abuse argument has Thomas, Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy; III-B(2)'s further response has only four votes and is not a holding.

**Controlling explanation:** A negative answer communicates a factual proposition. The statute's reference to any false statement is not limited to volunteered narratives, documents, benefit claims or statements under oath. The separate concealment clause's trick, scheme or device requirement does not become an element of every false-statement charge. The Labor and IRS investigation concerns included executive agencies, unlike the court-entity defect in Hubbard.

Gilliland describes protection of authorized governmental functions; it does not add a requirement that a lie successfully deceive investigators or independently pervert their work. Their prior knowledge therefore does not create the claimed categorical exemption, without deciding every distinct materiality question.

Text must be read in context. Ratzlaf preserves contextual fault and residual lenity, and Wells, Bates and Salinas distinguish enacted predicates from unsupported additions. The statute's claims and regulatory-reporting origins, longstanding lower-court doctrine and executive restraint make the proposed limitation substantial, but do not adequately support excluding this category from the enacted false-statements language. Generally applicable interpretive principles differ from a particular exemption lacking a sufficient statutory basis. Circuit disagreement alone supplies neither an amendment nor residual ambiguity requiring this exemption. Internal charging policy and unenacted proposals likewise create no statutory defense, and congressional silence does not ratify the doctrine.

The five Justices joining III-B(1) further conclude that potential investigative or charging abuse does not authorize this categorical exclusion. Actual materiality and culpability requirements, lawful charging discretion and Congress's responsibility remain distinct responses; discretion cannot replace proof or cure ambiguity. This conclusion neither approves every investigative tactic nor decides a claim of actual charging abuse. The Court affirms the reviewed construction without importing later statutory language, supplying unreported trial findings or treating the postanswer warning as proof of earlier knowledge.

##### The privilege does not authorize a deliberately false answer

**Holding and operative rule:** The privilege against compelled self-incrimination provides no general right to answer falsely instead of invoking lawful protection against answering. It therefore supplies no constitutional-avoidance basis for the categorical exculpatory-no exception on this record; actual coercion, a valid privilege claim and unlawful demands for waiver remain distinct questions.

**Authority:** Thomas's principal analysis outside III-B has Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, providing six direct votes. Ginsburg's judgment concurrence is not counted as a join in this entire explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Bryson rejects deliberate falsehood as a privileged substitute for lawful resistance to an official inquiry. Its benefit-affidavit facts do not themselves resolve every oral interview, but the privilege's function does not become a general license to lie. Brogan could remain silent; the difficulty of choosing among admission, silence and denial does not establish the claimed entitlement to false speech.

LaChance preserves the same distinction between a meaningful response and proved knowing falsity in employment proceedings, while expressly leaving this criminal statute to its own construction. Its administrative burden and service requirements do not migrate here. Garrity and Gardner retain their actual compelled-testimony and waiver protections. The Court creates no confession duty, universal warning requirement or rule approving every use of silence, and finds no custodial compulsion or waiver of a valid privilege in this interview.

##### Affirmance leaves unpresented element and trial questions unresolved

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the presented statutory exception leaves the reviewed conviction in force without reopening the separate payment conviction or ordering a proceeding on hypothetical trial errors. The meaning of willfulness concerning knowledge of illegality, actual surprise or lack of reflection, materiality, jury allocation and conduct-time notice remain subject to their own properly presented claims and supported records.

**Authority:** Part IV has the same six direct principal-opinion joins; Ginsburg independently concurs in affirmance and these limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Brogan's reported concession that the answer was false and knowingly and willfully given permits resolution of the categorical exception; it does not define culpability for every future case. The Second Circuit's reservation about knowledge of illegality and its discussion of possible surprise are not findings of error in this prosecution. Gaudin requires a jury determination beyond reasonable doubt when materiality is an actual element, but supplies no finding that this jury lost such an issue. Wells decides materiality under §1014, not every §1001 clause. Lanier protects conduct-time warning; a later decision cannot supply missing notice in 1993, but no new notice violation is adjudicated here. Literal truth, ambiguous questions, immaterial statements, coercion and actual charging abuse are not decided. The original denial preceded the agents' warning; failure to correct is not treated as an additional charged statement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Hubbard retains its offense-date institution requirement, its rejection of Bramblett's incompatible all-branch construction and its question-versus-argument boundary. This included executive-agency investigation presents no excluded-court nexus or new litigation exception.
- Gilliland's agency-function rationale is applied without adding successful deception or an independent perversion-of-functions element. It does not exempt simple investigative denials.
- Bryson supplies the privilege boundary against deliberate falsity as a substitute for lawful resistance. Its affidavit and benefit setting is distinguished from the present interview; it does not decide every coercion or oral-statement question.
- Ratzlaf's contextual interpretation and independent residual-lenity principle remain intact. They do not establish automatic ambiguity from circuit disagreement or a universal knowledge-of-illegality requirement.
- Wells, Bates and Salinas retain the actual statutory conduct, institution, transaction and fault predicates of their respective offenses. They inform the distinction between enacted requirements and unsupported additions without transferring their elements to §1001.
- Gaudin's jury-allocation rule and Lanier's conduct-time warning rule remain operative at their stated scopes. Neither establishes a trial error or new remand here.
- LaChance preserves actual employment proof and process while rejecting categorical knowing-denial immunity; it creates no criminal §1001 rule. Garrity and Gardner retain their separate compelled-testimony and waiver protections.

#### Law After Decision

A bare exculpatory denial is not a categorical exception to the offense-date false-statements provision. This adds a criminal statutory coverage holding distinct from LaChance's employment rule. The actual included-agency matter, conduct, culpability, applicable materiality and criminal proof requirements remain necessary; negative phrasing does not establish immunity or automatically prove those predicates. The Court imposes no universal duty to answer, warning code, enforceable charging-policy defense or complete definition of willfulness.

The narrow statutory proposition has seven supporters, while the principal framework has the more limited joins shown above. The further abuse response in III-B(2), the Ginsburg–Souter concern analysis and the Stevens–Breyer exception are noncontrolling. No combined rationale is inferred from common concerns. The 1996 revision is not applied to the 1993 statement, and the later LaChance decision supplies no retrospective notice. Literal truth, ambiguity, immateriality, genuine compulsion and actual abuse retain their own legal predicates and procedural scope.

#### Separate Writings

**Thomas's Part III-B(2), joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy.** These four Justices add that a simple-denial exception would not reliably prevent the feared escalation: an interrogator could press for a more detailed false account outside the proposed exemption. This policy response has no majority and establishes no rule approving investigative tactics or rejecting an actual abuse claim. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional argument; Souter joins neither part of III-B.

**Ginsburg, joined by Souter, concurring in the judgment.** The unqualified false-statements language forecloses the judicial exemption, but permits a serious problem requiring congressional attention. Officials who already know the answer can elicit a predictable denial and generate a new felony when the underlying conduct cannot be proved, limitations have run, or the conduct is innocent. This is different from merely imposing an additional punishment for an established underlying crime. The reported interview's surprise, absent prior warning and existing records illustrate the concern without proving that this prosecution was unlawful or that Brogan's underlying conduct was innocent.

The statute began with fraudulent claims in 1863, expanded in 1918 under a financial-fraud limitation and broadened in 1934 to reach regulatory falsehoods. Its present reach into informal criminal questioning extends well beyond those original problems. That history supports legislative attention, though it does not overcome the enacted language. In Nunley, the Court vacated a conviction at the Government's request because prosecution lacked normally withheld internal approval; that disposition established no statutory exculpatory-no defense. The charging-era manual said the statute did not apply to exculpatory denials; the 1996 and September 1997 versions stated restrictive charging policies. They evidence restraint, not judicially enforceable immunity. The sentencing policy excluding ordinary nonperjurious denials from an obstruction enhancement likewise does not amend the substantive offense.

Affirmance does not require conviction whenever a suspect denies wrongdoing. Wiener left open whether willfulness requires knowledge that the answer is unlawful and recognized that surprise or lack of reflection can bear on actual intent. Those reservations supply no finding of error or new proceeding here. Materiality language added in 1996 governs its own temporal scope and cannot be substituted for the 1993 statute. Proposed oral-denial protections and warnings were never enacted; they illustrate possible legislative choices, not present defenses. Congressional silence does not ratify the lower-court exception. The concern about creating a criminal offense also differs from adverse employment consequences for a proved knowing false denial under LaChance; accepting that employment rule does not eliminate this concern.

**Souter, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the principal opinion except its discrete response to prosecutorial-abuse concerns in III-B, and joins Ginsburg's concurrence calling for congressional attention. His statement identifies that limited withheld assent; it does not withdraw his support from the remaining statutory, privilege or interpretive analysis.

**Stevens, joined by Breyer, dissenting.** They would reverse only insofar as the judgment sustains the §1001 conviction. A bounded statutory exception should cover a bare reactive oral denial of the questioned person's own suspected wrongdoing during an unsolicited criminal inquiry. Volunteered deception, affirmative misdirection, benefit submissions, regulatory reports and embellished accounts remain outside that exception. The Second Circuit accepted that Brogan's answer fell within the doctrine's core, so resolving its disputed margins is unnecessary here.

The dissent relies on intended statutory coverage, purpose, ordinary criminal-law background and sustained concrete judicial and executive practice. Williams illustrates that a claimed false instrument must actually make the statutory factual assertion; Sorrells and Palmer illustrate generally applicable limits concerning entrapment and statutory territorial reach. Their different holdings do not themselves create this defense, but demonstrate that broad language does not exhaust interpretation. The history of claims and regulatory reporting, together with longstanding treatment of ordinary defensive denials, supports the limited construction here. The dissent does not make executive policy binding law, infer congressional ratification from silence, adopt an unenacted bill, or announce a constitutional right to lie. Its substantial agreement with Ginsburg's concern leads to a different statutory judgment. The proposed reversal would not disturb the separate payment conviction, command release or prescribe an unverified sentence.

#### Procedure After Action

The reviewed §1001 conviction remains in force. This Supreme Court review is complete, with no additional remand instruction, new trial, evidentiary hearing, resentencing or retained jurisdiction. The separate employer-payment conviction and independently unpresented questions receive no new disposition.

### January 26, 1998 — Oubre v. Entergy Operations, Inc.

[Public Projection](../records/oubre_v_entergy_operations_inc_merits_1998-01-26.md#public-projection). The following complete public blocks preserve this event's action, proposition-specific authority, published positions, procedure and source limits.

#### Event

Oubre v. Entergy Operations, Inc., No. 96-1291. Plenary merits decision, January 26, 1998; argued November 12, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 112 F.3d 787, which affirmed summary judgment barring Oubre's ADEA action because she retained consideration paid for a concededly deficient release. Review concerns statutory waiver validity, asserted ratification and prior tender, with separate monetary accounting and discrimination merits reserved.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

#### Public Action

The judgment is reversed and the case remanded. Oubre may pursue her ADEA action without first returning the severance payments; the deficient release and her retention of its consideration do not bar that action.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Reverse and remand, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The mandate removes the challenged ADEA defense. It makes no discrimination or compensation determination and orders no restitution or setoff. Any independently authorized monetary adjustment must be considered separately without making repayment a precondition to suit.

#### Opinion Topology

O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Breyer concurs, joined by O'Connor; both fully join the Court. Scalia and Thomas each dissent separately and join neither the Court nor one another's dissent. Scalia would affirm on lack of tender alone and rejects ratification here; Thomas would affirm on ratification and also preserves tender as a distinct condition.

#### Holdings

##### A deficient ADEA waiver cannot be enforced through retention or prior tender

**Holding and operative rule:** A release failing the applicable minimum requirements of 29 U.S.C. §626(f) cannot bar an ADEA action merely because the employee retains its consideration, whether the asserted bar is called ratification, prior tender or estoppel resting on that same retention. The party asserting waiver validity retains the statutory burden of proving a knowing and voluntary waiver under the applicable paragraph. The ruling neither invalidates a compliant waiver nor decides a distinct fraud, contract or equitable claim.

**Authority:** The seven Justices joining the Opinion of the Court adopt this entire statutory rule, its application and its bounded remedy. The concurrence's additional voidability explanation is not part of that holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress made minimum safeguards conditions of an effective ADEA waiver. This individual agreement omitted a specific reference to ADEA rights, allowed fourteen rather than at least twenty-one days for consideration, and supplied no seven-day postexecution revocation opportunity. Signature, actual attorney consultation and later awareness do not supply those missing protections. No group program triggering the forty-five-day period and disclosures, or settlement of an already filed charge or action under paragraph (2), is established. The latter setting has its own requirements: paragraph (1)(A)–(E) and a reasonable consideration period.

Entergy's strongest argument distinguishes initial execution from later informed affirmation and invokes the ordinary obligation to restore a bargain's benefits before repudiating it. But the asserted consequence would make this deficient waiver extinguish the very claim Congress protected. Congress need not name each contract doctrine that could otherwise circumvent its mandatory conditions. A tender requirement accomplishing the same bar cannot stand. The Court need not accept Entergy's description of common law in every setting to resolve its asserted effect here.

Mezzanatto permits its specified knowing-and-voluntary evidentiary waiver while preserving right-specific statutory restrictions; it supplies no exception to these express safeguards or burden. McKennon distinguishes an ADEA claim from legally supported remedial limitations; it does not establish a severance-repayment right. Likewise, §626(f)(4)'s protection of EEOC enforcement and employee participation remains distinct from private monetary relief.

Oubre's ability to repay is unknown and unnecessary to the holding. Lawful restitution, recoupment, setoff and the release's effect on other rights remain undecided. Preserving those questions creates no recovery entitlement or automatic $6,258 deduction. The employee must still prove her discrimination claim.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Mezzanatto retains its bounded evidentiary-waiver rule and right-specific statutory limits. It does not permit common-law consent principles to replace OWBPA's express requirements or assigned burden.
- McKennon retains its separation of ADEA liability from supported remedial limitations. Its after-acquired-misconduct proof requirement and relief rules do not decide severance accounting.
- Brooklyn Savings' protection against private defeat of a public statutory safeguard remains consistent with the right-specific distinction; no universal prohibition of statutory settlements is adopted.

#### Law After Decision

An employer cannot obtain the effect of an ADEA waiver that fails Congress's minimum conditions by relying on retention of the payment or making its return a prerequisite to the action. This construction adds the statutory ratification-and-tender rule while leaving ordinary discrimination burdens and independently lawful remedies intact. It declares neither the whole contract absolutely void nor every payment immune from recovery. Other claims, genuinely distinct equitable conduct, and a later compliant agreement require their own grounds. No automatic repayment or generalized employer accounting right follows.

#### Separate Writings

Breyer, joined by O'Connor, fully agrees with the Court but explains why the deficient arrangement should be treated as voidable at the worker's election rather than absolutely void. The statute protects the employee; absolute nullity could let the employer use its own omission to cancel reciprocal benefits even when the employee does not sue. Filing the ADEA action rejects the promise not to sue. An employer may then seek whatever restitution, relief from reciprocal obligations or damages credit the law and agreement independently permit. Their concurrence awards none of those remedies and preserves the Court's prohibition on prior tender. This additional explanation is noncontrolling.

Scalia would affirm for failure to tender back. In his view, restoration before repudiating an exchange is a surviving common-law condition distinct from original waiver validity. He nevertheless rejects ratification on this record: discovering that required statutory safeguards were omitted does not remove that impediment, unlike discovering a concealed fraud or mistake that can be knowingly accepted afterward. He therefore does not join Thomas's ratification ground, and makes no finding that Oubre could repay or that a monetary judgment is warranted.

Thomas would affirm on later knowing ratification and separately preserves the tender condition. He distinguishes Congress's minimum conditions for original execution from common-law affirmation of a voidable bargain after discovery of its defect. His background-common-law analysis, reflected in Astoria, Isbrandtsen and Norfolk Redevelopment, requires a sufficient statutory basis for displacing those distinct doctrines; he finds none here. The reported knowing retention therefore supports affirmance in his view. He does not find the original agreement compliant, shift §626(f)(3)'s original-validity burden or award restitution. His ground and Scalia's uncured-impediment objection remain different.

No constructive discharge, age discrimination, willfulness, damages, fees, reinstatement, automatic trial, wholesale revival of state claims, new hearing or payment order is directed. Any accounting must rest on independently applicable law and the actual agreement. This Supreme Court review is complete.

### January 26, 1998 — South Dakota v. Yankton Sioux Tribe

[Public Projection](../records/south_dakota_v_yankton_sioux_tribe_merits_1998-01-26.md#public-projection). The following complete public blocks preserve this event's action, proposition-specific authority, published positions, procedure and source limits.

#### Event

South Dakota v. Yankton Sioux Tribe, No. 96-1581. Plenary merits decision, January 26, 1998; argued December 8, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, 99 F.3d 1439, which affirmed a declaration that the 1894 Act left the reservation's exterior boundary unchanged and that the waste-site tract lies within it. Review concerns the ceded, unallotted lands and the declaration's continuing consequences after EPA permitted a compacted-clay liner.

The Tribe did not appeal the separate denial of its injunction and rejection of its asserted regulation of this non-Indian activity. The Waste District supported the State without appealing after construction could proceed. The existing state permit and operative reservation-status declaration remain the subject of the controversy.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

#### Public Action

The Eighth Circuit's judgment is reversed and the case remanded. The 1894 Act removed the ceded, unallotted settler lands, including the established waste-site tract, from the reservation. The Court does not decide whether the entire reservation was disestablished.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Reverse and remand, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Court of Appeals shall enter judgment recognizing diminishment as to the ceded lands and address properly remaining parcel-specific or statutory jurisdiction questions consistently with that boundary. #### Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt the three holdings and their qualifications. No separate writing issues.

#### Holdings

##### The existing declaration presents a continuing concrete controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** The EPA's waiver of a particular liner requirement does not moot the State's challenge where the operative declaration continues to determine the territorial legal regime governing its issued permit for this identified site. That continuing legal relationship permits review; anticipated similar projects or a wish to erase unfavorable precedent alone would not.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies direct authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Lee requires concrete personal application throughout review and distinguishes limited jurisdictional correction from power to decide the merits. This case concerns an actual permitted facility and an extant declaration classifying its land, rather than a chain of future requests or possible enforcement. Reversing that declaration changes the parties' current legal relationship. The EPA waiver permits clay-lined construction but does not vacate the territorial determination. The Court adopts no rule that an adverse declaration always preserves jurisdiction after every underlying consequence disappears. Nor does jurisdiction to review this declaration reopen the Tribe's separate, unappealed regulatory and injunction ruling.

##### The cession and purchase diminished the reservation as to the ceded lands

**Holding and operative rule:** The Act of August 15, 1894 diminished the Yankton Sioux Reservation by removing the ordinary ceded, unallotted lands disposed of under its settler-opening program. The comprehensive surrender and unconditional federal purchase, read with the entire agreement, establish clear congressional intent. Article XVIII's treaty-saving language protects its annuity and associated entitlement assurances without preserving the 1858 boundary over those ceded lands.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this entire statutory construction, its evidentiary limits and its application to the identified site.

**Controlling explanation:** Solem makes statutory text most probative and gives exceptional, but not conclusive, weight to comprehensive cession coupled with guaranteed compensation. Article I surrenders all tribal claim, right, title and interest in the specified unallotted lands; Article II obligates the United States to pay $600,000. The appropriation and distribution provisions confirm a purchase, not an undertaking merely to sell for the Tribe and remit proceeds. Hagen preserves that clear-cession route even though this Act lacks the operative public-domain restoration command decisive there. No particular formula is indispensable.

Article XVIII is substantial contrary text: it disclaims treaty abrogation, preserves all 1858 provisions as though the agreement had not been made, and separately guarantees continued annuities. Ownership and reservation status can differ, so a sale alone would not answer the Tribe's boundary-preservation argument. But the whole agreement's comprehensive surrender and federal purchase carry territorial significance beyond opening for settlement. Literal preservation of the treaty's possession and exclusion provisions would also contradict the settlement commanded by the transaction. The documented concern over federal annuities, past claims and future entitlements explains the clause's reassurance; its broad words do not undo the specific land surrender. The Court neither deletes those words nor decides every other treaty entitlement.

The state school-section disposition and Article XVII's distinction between ceded and other lands reinforce this reading. A specific school exception and a permanent liquor restriction could also serve purposes within retained boundaries, so neither independently controls. Article VIII's retained agency and school uses caution against total termination. Contemporary common-property and state-law statements corroborate the enacted choice without establishing every signatory's understanding of future boundaries. Conflicting later labels carry little force. State administration, settlement and demographics remain subordinate clues, not events changing boundaries.

Dion's clear-and-plain requirement and the tribal ambiguity canon remain intact. The complete enactment supplies the congressional choice here; genuine unresolved ambiguity would favor retention. No quantified proof burden or demographic threshold is created.

##### The supported parcel category determines the mandate's limits

**Holding and operative rule:** The identified waste-site tract falls outside the reservation because it is established as ceded, unallotted land disposed of under this program, not merely because a non-Indian now owns it in fee. The declaration must be corrected on that ground. Total disestablishment and the status or authority associated with other land categories remain undecided.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court adopts this application and bounded remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The reported 1904 homestead deed matches the complaint's parcel and supplies affirmative evidence of the land category. Seymour's distinction between title and reservation boundaries remains controlling; fee ownership alone cannot remove land from a retained reservation. The Court therefore decides the ceded-land question without classifying every allotment, trust parcel, Article VIII agency or school tract, or later acquisition. Separate dependent-community and unextinguished-allotment categories under §1151 retain their own predicates. Hagen likewise requires the territorial holding to remain distinct from every sovereign's powers. Neither the present holding nor remand restores the unappealed tribal regulatory claim, cancels the EPA waiver, orders a composite liner or adjudicates private title. Properly remaining parcel-specific and statutory jurisdiction questions may be addressed below consistently with this boundary ruling.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Hagen is applied for an operative congressional-act inquiry, unequal evidentiary weights and geographic limits. Its surviving public-domain restoration command is distinguished from this Act's cession-and-purchase ground; neither mere opening nor fee ownership suffices.
- Solem is applied to the complete Act and its strongly weighted cession/guaranteed-compensation inference. Its sales-agency setting is distinguished; its tribal ambiguity canon and subordinate treatment of later evidence remain intact.
- DeCoteau's controlling clear-cession principle supports the reading without making its precise words, purchase structure or negotiations mandatory for every case.
- Dion retains the clear-and-plain requirement concerning the particular treaty right affected; no general cancellation of treaty obligations is adopted.
- Rosebud preserves the absence of a mandatory verbal formula. Seymour and Mattz continue to distinguish sale, settlement and fee ownership from congressional diminishment.
- Bourland's separate inquiry into exclusion-derived regulation and residual authority is not converted into a boundary rule. Sac and Fox's distinct Indian-country categories remain intact; Negonsott's Kansas-specific authorization supplies no general territorial rule here.
- Lee is applied to the actual continuing permit/declaration controversy, without treating corrective jurisdiction alone as power to decide merits.

#### Law After Decision

The original 1858 line does not preserve reservation status over the ordinary ceded, unallotted settler lands governed by the 1894 transaction. The State has primary territorial jurisdiction at the established waste site. This whole-act construction adds that specific diminishment holding while retaining the existing congressional-intent framework, tribal ambiguity canon and distinction between ownership and territory.

The decision supplies no complete regional boundary map, total-disestablishment holding, universal state jurisdiction or cancellation of every treaty promise. Allotments, trust property, retained agency and school parcels, later acquisitions, other §1151 categories and surviving federal or tribal powers require their own legal and factual predicates. Demographic change and long state administration cannot themselves create or reverse diminishment.

#### Procedure After Action

### January 29, 1998 — McCardle v. Haddad

#### Participation

#### Opinion Topology

#### Holdings

##### The trial motion did not preserve search immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 50 requires the trial motion to identify the judgment sought and its supporting legal and factual grounds; postverdict renewal does not ordinarily permit a new ground omitted from that motion. Functional presentation, including adequate explicit incorporation, can suffice without a particular label, but pleading qualified immunity and invoking it for the stop did not preserve the distinct search-immunity ground here; no demonstrated exceptional injustice requires reaching that ground.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies nine direct votes for this rule, its application and all stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 50(a)(2) gives the court and opposing party an opportunity to address the actual ground before submission. Rule 50(b) renews the legal questions raised by the trial motion. The official explanatory note permits explicit reference to earlier materials and argument; mistaken terminology alone is immaterial. The answer pleaded immunity, but a pleading is not itself the required trial motion.

Counsel expressly requested judgment on the stop on the merits or immunity, then separately argued that the search was lawful. The complete reported presentation identified no search-immunity ground or incorporation supplying it. Search legality and immunity for an objectively reasonable legal mistake are distinct, even when they may overlap. The Court adopts no requirement of separate titles or magic words and no prohibition on alternative defenses. Gomez treats immunity as an affirmative defense; Elder requires all relevant law on a preserved issue, without creating the missing motion ground.

The strongest contrary argument combines the answer and search-legality presentation as sufficient notice. The express claim-specific separation defeats that reading. No actual prejudice is independently found. Nor does immunity's importance itself establish exceptional injustice. The search record must be viewed favorably to McCardle; the adverse general stop verdict supplies no contrary universal factual findings. On that record, no probable-cause, consent, arrest, inventory or supported protective-search justification compelled judgment for Haddad. Long requires specific articulable grounds supporting danger and possible access to weapons within a protective scope; a valid stop alone is insufficient. The officer's denial of a search or subjective fear alone is not the constitutional test.

Those circumstances defeat the asserted injustice without deciding ultimate immunity or issuing a new search-merits holding. The Court leaves the outer limits of exceptional review open, adds no categorical instruction or pretrial-motion prerequisite, and infers no further default from unavailable original papers.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Gomez is applied to distinguish an affirmative immunity defense from an obligation courts must supply without its proper presentation.
- Elder is applied to preserve review of all relevant law on a preserved immunity issue; it does not revive an omitted ground or admit a new factual record.
- Harlow and Anderson retain objective, particularized conduct-date immunity. The Court neither grants nor denies that defense on its merits.
- Long informs the rejection of claimed exceptional injustice at its actual protective-search scope; neither a valid stop nor the officer's subjective account alone establishes a lawful search.
- Crawford-El and Fletcher retain ordinary procedure and the distinction between constitutional liability and personal defenses. Their holdings create no immunity-specific Rule 50 exception or motive element for this search claim.
- Johnson, Behrens, Clanton and Smith retain their legal/factual and claim-specific limits. Their interlocutory settings do not restrict this final-judgment channel or enlarge the trial motion. Lanier and later immunity decisions supply no notice retrospectively for the March 1992 conduct.
- Allentown's distinction between a fairly included argument and an independent unpreserved objection remains confined to its actual question and governing statute; Rule 50 controls here.
- Farrar and Hensley remain unchanged. Their explained assessment of actual success permits zero without mechanical proportionality or a categorical nominal-relief presumption. The Court does not approve the lower fee rationale or decide a fee amount.

#### Law After Decision

A pleaded immunity defense and a trial invocation concerning one claim do not automatically preserve immunity as a renewed-judgment ground concerning another. Courts must assess what the actual motion conveyed, including any adequate incorporation, rather than require a verbal formula. This application leaves lawful alternative defenses and genuinely supported exceptional-review questions intact.

Ultimate qualified immunity and an independent Supreme Court ruling on search legality remain undecided. Failure to preserve immunity does not itself establish constitutional liability, causation, compensable loss or punitive culpability. No stop, punitive-instruction or fee holding is added, and no new substantive immunity framework or defense burden replaces existing law.

No remand, trial, hearing, discovery or production order, immunity reconsideration, new award, injunction or scheduled return is directed. ### February 2, 1998 — Alexander v. Whitman

[Public Projection](../records/alexander_v_whitman_merits_1998-02-02.md#public-projection). The following complete public blocks preserve this event's action, proposition-specific authority, published positions, procedure and source limits.

#### Event

Alexander v. Whitman; Third Circuit No. 95-5414. Plenary merits decision, February 2, 1998. On review of 114 F.3d 1392, decided May 23, 1997, affirming a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal. Review concerns Fourteenth Amendment personhood before live birth and the mother's constitutional challenge to New Jersey's live-birth line for wrongful-death and survival remedies. No separate Supreme Court docket or exact argument date is established.

Alexander alleges that her child was stillborn after a near-term pregnancy and that the surrogate refused estate letters needed for identified claims in her state malpractice action. Her personal state claim seeks emotional-distress relief. Factual allegations, including prenatal pain, are accepted at this stage; negligence, causation, the exact death mechanism and damages are not found. The federal money-damages dismissal was not appealed, no class was certified, and the lawyers' distinct personal and representative claims are outside this review.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

#### Public Action

The judgment is affirmed insofar as reviewed. The child's constitutional-personhood claim and the mother's liberty and separate wrongful-death and survival equality challenges remain dismissed. Other components remain undisturbed without adoption of their reasoning.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed insofar as reviewed, 9–0.**

The affirmance covers each of the four reviewed substantive claims. No remand, estate letters, new tort cause, class certification, damages, injunction, hearing or trial is ordered. The mother's independently available state claims remain unaffected. Both lawyers' components and the unappealed federal damages count remain outside the decision. #### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer, in full | Supports every reviewed affirmance and the bounded mandate | Parts I–V |

#### Holdings

##### The actual denied-letter controversy permits bounded review

**Holding and operative rule:** At the pleading stage, Alexander's alleged actual denial of letters needed for her identified state action supplies a concrete personal obstacle traceable to the responsible surrogate and capable of meaningful partial redress. That controversy permits review of her own constitutional challenge; the exact child-personhood question is already controlled by Roe and falls within the confined controlled-merits setting preserved in Steel Co., without a finding that every proposed estate or class capacity exists.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees in reaching the authorized claims without joining that threshold rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires the claimant's own injury and stage-appropriate support. The alleged refusal is a present obstacle, not speculation about another pregnancy; removing it could matter even though malpractice and recovery remain uncertain. Its summary-judgment evidence requirement does not become a pleading-stage affidavit rule. Lee requires claim-specific injury and relief, while Yankton distinguishes a continuing legal relationship from merely unfavorable precedent. No later settlement, expiration or mootness is established.

The responsible surrogate's actual refusal supplies the pleaded connection. Office titles and other surrogates' consent orders do not establish every defendant's role, and no relief is ordered against any defendant. The mother has her own controversy; neither lawyers nor an uncertified class can supply a missing one. Steel Co. preserves Norton and Avrech's confined already-controlled settings, permitting application of Roe's exact personhood holding without a general license to assume constitutional power over unsettled merits. The lawyers' independent and representative claims are unnecessary to that resolution and outside review. The Court enters no new ruling on their standing or entitlement and orders no lawyer-specific remand.

##### Roe's constitutional-personhood rule governs the stillbirth claim

**Holding and operative rule:** A fetus not born alive is not a person within the Fourteenth Amendment under Roe's governing definition. This constitutional category neither determines when biological life begins nor forbids States from providing broader lawful prenatal or stillbirth protections.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Roe expressly addressed the Amendment's use of person, while distinguishing that inquiry from medical, philosophical and theological accounts of life. Its discussion of prenatal tort and property protection did not turn each state-law protection into federal constitutional personhood. Accepted allegations of development, humanity and pain therefore do not resolve the legal category.

The strongest distinction is that this case concerns a wanted near-term child allegedly harmed without conflict with the mother's abortion decision. That circumstance matters to permissible state protection and her separate rights, but does not narrow Roe's express definition. Casey preserved reproductive liberty while leaving fetal personhood undecided in that case; reserving a question did not overrule the existing independent holding. The Court applies that rule without adopting a universal description of prenatal legal status or deciding any new abortion restriction.

##### The remedial line does not trigger the asserted maternal-liberty protection

**Holding and operative rule:** On these allegations, limiting additional wrongful-death and survival remedies does not itself interfere with the mother's protected reproductive decision or relationship in the manner necessary to trigger heightened constitutional protection. The Court leaves the outer scope of prenatal familial liberty open and decides no categorical rule that every tort limitation is incapable of burdening a protected right.

**Authority:** Part III has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in dismissal of this component only.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey protects the decision whether to continue a pregnancy and requires attention to the actual legal duty and attributable interference. Its complete previability standard, as applied in Fargo and Miller, imposes the applicable justification burden only after its trigger is established; a law's connection to pregnancy alone is insufficient. Here the challenged statutes prohibit no reproductive choice, compel no termination or treatment, delay no necessary care, require no disclosure and confer no third-party veto on the alleged facts.

The mother argues that denying legal protection against prenatal injury burdens her choice to continue pregnancy. Generalized loss of possible tort deterrence does not itself identify the required state interference. Her existing parental tort route is relevant context, not proof of complete protection or successful recovery. The Court neither diminishes the importance of a wanted pregnancy nor recognizes a general affirmative entitlement to every requested damages remedy. The absence of such a general entitlement does not answer her independent equality claims, which require the separate analysis that follows.

##### The wrongful-death line has a rational relation to its derivative remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** New Jersey may use live birth as the administrable event identifying the independent decedent whose anticipated pecuniary contributions support survivors' derivative wrongful-death recovery. On this record that relationship satisfies ordinary equal-protection review despite comparable near-boundary losses; the existence of a parental emotional-distress action is not independently sufficient to validate the exclusion.

**Authority:** Part IV-A has all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Beach permits reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds and imperfect lines without formal legislative findings. Heller requires a relationship to the actual disadvantage, and Romer prevents treating remaining general protection as an automatic answer. Neither nonsuspect status nor absence of a fundamental tort right alone proves validity.

Wrongful death compensates survivors' pecuniary loss, including qualifying support, services and advice, with separately authorized medical, hospital and funeral expenses. Live birth identifies an independently existing decedent from whom those derivative interests are measured, distinct from the parents' direct injury from loss of pregnancy. A newborn's future contributions may be as uncertain as a near-term stillborn child's; no contrary factual finding is necessary. That comparison shows an imperfect boundary without eliminating its relationship to the defined cause.

Limiting liability alone would merely repeat the exclusion, and the Court rejects any premise that all prenatal proof is impossible. The legal connection between independent decedent status and the derivative remedy supplies the sufficient ground. Giardina's parental cause has different beneficiaries and damages; Carey v. Lovett requires distress producing physical manifestations or destroying basic emotional security, not ordinary worry or upset. No recovery or equivalent compensation is presumed. A wider state remedy could be lawful, but ordinary review does not require the best or most inclusive line.

##### The assumed survival line separately defines the claim transmitted to an estate

**Holding and operative rule:** On the undisputed appellate construction that excludes stillbirth from survival recovery, New Jersey may condition an estate's continuation of its decedent's own accrued cause on live birth, including where a prenatal injury is carried into that person's life. The condition rationally identifies the independent legal holder whose claim survives; it does not rest on a finding that prenatal pain is impossible or that injury begins only at birth.

**Authority:** Part IV-B has all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Survival continues the deceased's own cause through an executor or administrator, including conscious pain and suffering and other damages and expenses allowed by state law. That function differs from survivors' expected contributions, so the wrongful-death explanation alone would not suffice. Live birth supplies a legal-holder boundary for the cause the estate continues. Recognizing a prenatal injury conditionally upon live birth does not require recognition of an independently transmissible fetal estate when birth never occurs.

The strongest objection compares the same prenatal pain followed by stillbirth with pain followed by minutes of postnatal life. The allegations are accepted, and a parental distress action may compensate neither loss. Heller and Romer require more than relabeling the exclusion. The relevant relationship here is the ownership and transmission of this particular decedent-based cause, rather than medical denial of pain or residual compensation. Beach permits that relevant legal boundary without requiring a gestational threshold, individualized substitute or new legislative study.

Giardina did not decide survival. The parallel exclusion was assumed in the federal appeal without dispute, and the Court decides the federal challenge on that express premise. It announces no authoritative New Jersey construction. Because the challenge fails even on the assumed exclusion, no certification, evidentiary remand or state-law rewrite is necessary. Broader lawful state protection remains possible.

- Roe's express Fourteenth Amendment personhood holding is applied, while its distinction between constitutional status and permissible prenatal protections remains intact.
- Casey's retained choice, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health protections remain unchanged. Its fetal-personhood reservation does not displace Roe's independent holding. Its complete six-step rule, Fargo and Miller retain their actual-duty and attributable-interference triggers; no obstacle-only substitute or new tort-remedy burden is adopted.
- Beach supplies conceivable legitimate grounds and tolerance of imperfect fit, retaining Moreno and Cleburne's limits on bare harm and irrational prejudice.

#### Holdings

##### Each defendant has his own protected relationship to the room

**Holding and operative rule:** Fields's sustained paid access, key, independent entry and permission to bring guests establish his own legitimate privacy interest in the apartment and private room. Crawley's repeated, expressly permitted sharing of the closed room's privacy also establishes his own interest on these facts, although he had no established overnight plan that evening; neither mere presence nor another person's rights would suffice.

**Authority:** Part I-A has all nine Justices. Part I-B has O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. The other four assume Crawley's interest to decide the observation question and do not join that extension.

**Controlling explanation:** Rakas requires the defendant's personal protected interest, rather than borrowed rights. Jones, as preserved in Rakas, and Olson show that residential privacy does not depend on formal ownership. Fields's actual control is substantially more than permission to be present. His lack of residence or stored possessions does not negate that relationship.

Olson specifically protected an overnight guest. Crawley's earlier overnight visit does not make this occasion another overnight stay. His protection instead rests on his recurring relationship, the extended visit with permission and Warner's respect for the jointly closed room. The Court thus extends residential sharing beyond Olson's precise facts on this supported combination, without protecting every commercial visitor. The predominant cocaine activity does not automatically destroy an otherwise legitimate personal interest. Recognizing either relationship remains separate from deciding whether the particular information was exposed to observation. These existing conviction challenges present an actual controversy; the personal Fourth Amendment inquiry does not require assuming Article III jurisdiction.

##### Limited residential sharing is not itself general publication

**Holding and operative rule:** Limited visibility of bedroom activity to cotenants using shared residential space is not, by that fact alone, general publication. An incompletely closed blind cannot establish absence of subjective privacy independently of the actual vantage from which the challenged police observation was obtained.

**Authority:** Part II-A has O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. The Court does not adopt Part II-B's four-Justice application as a majority search determination.

**Controlling explanation:** Katz protects reasonable personal privacy, and Pinson and Myers protect concealed domestic information without making physical entry or a particular device indispensable. Their distinction of ordinary visible openings does not dispense with examining where the observer stood. Ciraolo concerns public navigable airspace; Riley's competing writings address actual public availability; Dunn's open-field observation depended on the actual position outside protected curtilage. None makes every shared apartment yard public.

The substantial gap, common tenant use and absence of extraordinary viewing measures are important exposure facts. But visibility from one class of permitted residential users cannot simply be described as publication to everyone, and imperfect screening alone does not establish that the occupants exhibited no desire for privacy. The street and sidewalk afforded no view here. Conversely, fencing and investigative purpose do not establish every constitutional predicate, and no automatic common-yard immunity follows. The Court states the limited-sharing principle without a majority finding that these officers occupied protected curtilage or conducted a search. The competing applications remain distinct.

##### Ultimate home-entry exigency receives independent appellate review

**Holding and operative rule:** Appellate courts independently determine whether the established circumstances supplied a constitutionally sufficient exigency for warrantless home entry. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences retain clear-error protection, and grounded contextual experience receives genuine due weight without controlling the ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Authority:** Part III-A has O'Connor, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia withholds this extension; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Ornelas allocates ultimate reasonable suspicion for a stop and probable cause for a warrantless search to independent appellate judgment while protecting historical findings. Its functional reasons extend to this distinct home-entry question: consistent constitutional boundaries require comparison of established circumstances, while the trial court retains its superior role in deciding what happened and whom to credit. Grounded local experience matters, but cannot manufacture an observation or make the ultimate conclusion presumptively correct.

This is an extension of Ornelas, not a statement that it had already resolved every mixed question. Richards separately adopted independent review of ultimate announcement sufficiency. The two inquiries remain distinct, and neither allocation resolves the underlying facts or requires suppression, a new hearing or plea withdrawal. Gates's substantial-basis review of a magistrate's warrant decision remains different. The Court adds no numerical weighting, intermediate standard or police-credibility presumption.

##### Entry authority and announcement require separate contemporaneous justification

**Holding and operative rule:** Probable cause alone does not authorize warrantless home entry; an applicable consent or exigency justification is required. Separately, an announcement exception requires particular facts and rational inferences known when force begins supporting reasonable suspicion of danger, futility or threatened evidence loss from announcement or delay; later discoveries cannot supply earlier justification.

**Authority:** Part III-B has all eight Associate Justices. Four reserve the ultimate applications and four would sustain them; neither application has a controlling majority.

**Controlling explanation:** Payton and Hayden preserve the home-entry requirement and actual urgency. Wilson and Richards address the different reasonableness of the manner of entry. Richards requires more than a hunch but less than probable cause for its announcement exceptions, and independently reviews ultimate sufficiency while protecting historical findings. Its governing allocation applies despite an individual Justice's earlier disagreement with that methodology.

The porch warning, Fields's known violent history and the readily disposable cocaine are relevant contemporaneous circumstances. The loaded weapon later discovered and flight after entry are not earlier grounds. A serious drug offense alone supplies no categorical exception. Nor does entering the apartment through an open door erase the force already used at the common-building door; calling police identity after force cannot cure a defect existing when force began.

The warning need not be treated as conclusive proof that every occupant knew the officers' identity, authority and purpose. A danger or destruction inference remains distinct from an independent futility finding. Prior time to obtain a warrant and the front/rear tactical plan belong in the actual sequence, but the Court adopts no general police-created-exigency test and makes no majority finding that this entry was valid or invalid. If an antecedent search was unlawful, its causal consequences and any untainted independent justification must be considered separately.

##### A later warrant or consent does not automatically decide suppression

**Holding and operative rule:** If an antecedent intrusion was unlawful, a later warrant is an independent source only when both the decision to seek it and its issuance are genuinely independent of the illegality. Consent must satisfy its own authority, scope and voluntariness requirements and any applicable taint analysis; neither a warrant's arrival nor a rights explanation and signature automatically validates an earlier intrusion or decides exclusion.

**Authority:** Part IV has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela additionally agrees in returning suppression, consent, independence, attenuation and later-warrant consequences to the lower courts without final suppression, but joins no additional remedial rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Murray requires actual independence, not merely an affidavit that omits mention of an earlier entry. Karo preserves the possibility of sufficient untainted warrant information. Pinson and Myers distinguish correcting a search premise from deciding exclusion or reviving unavailable arguments. Warner's consent came while police secured the apartment; its legal effect cannot be established by the signature alone. The warrant and published recitations do not conclusively establish the necessary independent-source, consent or attenuation findings.

Other remedial grounds remain available only if supported and procedurally open. Leon does not protect knowing or reckless deception, abandonment of judicial neutrality, reliance on an affidavit wholly lacking reasonable probable-cause indicia, or a facially deficient warrant; its inquiry includes obtaining and supplying officers and assumes execution within scope. Evans's qualifying court-clerical error supplies no general exception for deliberate police investigation. Inevitable discovery requires its own actual predicates. Intentional waiver and forfeiture remain distinct under Olano; if Rule 52(b) is properly invoked, error, plainness, substantial rights and the separate fairness, integrity or public-reputation inquiry retain their roles. No such omission or predicate is found merely because original papers are not reproduced.

The Court orders no new hearing, affidavit-excision proceeding or final admission/exclusion. Rule 11(a)(2) permits withdrawal after prevailing on the reserved issue; this limited correction itself neither awards Crawley withdrawal nor vacates Fields's jury verdict.

##### A legal immunity appeal accepts the proper factual assumptions

**Holding and operative rule:** An interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal may resolve whether the properly assumed conduct violated clearly established law, but may not choose among disputed accounts of duties, knowledge, participation or motive. **Authority:** Parts I–II of Stevens's opinion are joined in full by all eight other participating Justices. Each holding and its qualifications have nine direct votes.

##### The particular November 1995 uncertainty supports personal damages immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** On the proper assumptions about these seven employees' actual direct and confidential prosecutorial work, the law in November 1995 left reasonable uncertainty whether political affiliation could be appropriate to their functions. Vacco, Buscaglia and Page therefore receive personal damages immunity on the reviewed First Amendment claims, without a final constitutional classification of any position or an office-wide confidentiality, at-will or civil-service exception.

**Authority:** The unanimous Parts I–II authority stated above applies to this holding, each employee's application and the claim-limited mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** Branti asks whether affiliation is appropriate for effective performance of the particular work; ordinary trust and professional secrecy are insufficient. Its reservation concerned a prosecutor's deputy, not every support employee. Harlow and Anderson nevertheless protect reasonable conduct-time legal mistakes independently of ultimate constitutional permission. Elder requires all relevant pre-existing law; obvious coverage needs no factual twin, but an abstract affiliation right does not resolve every uncertain application.

The affirmative connection here is direct work with prosecutors developing confidential investigations, audits and cases. Mungo's audit-investigator work had the reported direct prosecutor relationship, without invented statutory powers. Matthews and Angiletta shared that relationship without borrowing others' individual admissions. [Public Projection](../records/j_roderick_macarthur_foundation_v_fbi_merits_1998-02-13.md#public-projection). The following complete public blocks preserve this event's action, proposition-specific authority, published positions, procedure and source limits.

#### Event

J. Roderick MacArthur Foundation and Lance E. Lindblom v. Federal Bureau of Investigation; D.C. Circuit No. 95-5386, 102 F.3d 600. Plenary merits decision, February 13, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 3. On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the D.C. Circuit's December 24, 1996 affirmance of summary judgment. No separate Supreme Court docket or exact argument date is stated.

The questions concern continued retention of Lindblom's First Amendment records under the Privacy Act and the plaintiffs' separate constitutional standing. The Foundation has no statutory claim. The FBI retained identified records about Lindblom's associations; the district court found they arose from legitimate investigations of others with whom plaintiffs had incidental, apparently innocent contacts. Initial collection is unchallenged. The September 6, 1995 district order dismissed the complaint with prejudice. Original monetary and equitable relief were sought; neither a final monetary entitlement nor abandonment of Lindblom's statutory money request is established. FOIA and Fifth/Ninth Amendment claims are outside review.

#### Holdings

##### I. A live personal statutory controversy and an express statutory action permit merits review

**Holding and operative rule:** Actual continued custody of identified records describing this claimant's protected associations supplies a concrete personal information interest directly traceable to the retaining agency; an actually requested award within §552a(g)(1)(D)/(g)(4)'s legally available monetary category can meaningfully redress the claimed invasion if its requirements are established. Jurisdiction does not depend on first proving the disputed retention violation or final damages entitlement, and the substantial statutory claim falls within the Act's express agency action and conditional sovereign consent without creating a new constitutional damages cause.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in reaching the merits without joining this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. requires Article III power before these disputed merits and, with Bell v. Hood, separates a substantial federal claim from ultimate success. The acknowledged personal records establish the occurrence of the claimed intrusion. Warth and Havens recognize the difference between invasion of one's own protected interest and a demand for lawful government; neither supplies a Privacy Act remedy or excuses summary-judgment proof under Lujan and Lee. Subjective chill, presumed emotional loss and speculation about employers do not carry this statutory conclusion.

The FBI itself retains the information. Lindblom actually sought damages, the statutory claim continued on appeal, and no abandonment or legal bar to every monetary remedy is established. Arizonans rejects legally unavailable relief; it does not require a renewed formulaic prayer. Alexander supports meaningful partial redress despite uncertainty about recovery, without transferring its pleading-stage standard here. Culpability, adverse effect and monetary entitlement still require their own legal treatment.

Section 1331 and the review statute provide jurisdictional channels, not consent. Section 552a(g)(1)(D) authorizes an individual action against the agency and jurisdiction over it; (g)(4) supplies conditional United States monetary liability. Meyer requires that separate source of cause and obligation; the text supplies it here. Bell is not a waiver, §702 supplies no money, and no agency Bivens action is created. The Court neither classifies every federal waiver condition as nonjurisdictional nor presumes every remaining statutory predicate satisfied.

##### II. The protected personal retention invasion can itself be an adverse effect

**Holding and operative rule:** Actual unwanted maintenance of identified records describing this individual's protected associations, if outside §552a(e)(7)'s lawful alternatives, can itself supply the adverse personal effect required by §552a(g)(1)(D). A merely abstract or technical violation with no adverse personal consequence is insufficient; an adverse agency determination, public disclosure, lost employment or pecuniary loss is not independently required as such.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Breyer, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Scalia and Thomas reserve this construction; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Section (D) requires personal adverse effect, not the adverse determination separately required by (C). Its additional words exclude abstract procedural noncompliance without personal impact. Where the substantive protection concerns keeping the claimant's own associational information, the actual invasion and its adverse personal consequence can coincide. Requiring a different kind of downstream injury would leave that particular interest unprotected merely because the information remained undisclosed.

Havens illustrates a direct personal statutory invasion but does not decide this additional Privacy Act language. Albright distinguishes adverse effect, causation, intentional or willful conduct and monetary recovery; its recognition of emotional trauma does not make such trauma exclusive or settle the damages minimum. Doe v. FBI enforced a no-adverse-effect finding unchallenged in that case, where money was not pursued on appeal. Neither finding nor abandonment is established here. The Court does not find distress, declare these records unlawful, equate every violation with adverse effect or treat adverse effect as actual damages under (g)(4).

##### III. Continued retention must satisfy the complete Continuing Pertinence Test

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 552a(e)(7) regulates continued maintenance as well as original collection. Unless retention is expressly authorized by statute or by the individual, the information must remain pertinent to and within the scope of an authorized law-enforcement activity; lawful original collection is not conclusive permission to retain it after every qualifying connection has ceased. The Court adopts the following complete rule:

1. **Does the challenged record fall within the statutory protection?**

   The plaintiff identifies records describing their First Amendment activities and establishes that the government continues to maintain them. The court first checks whether retention is independently authorized by statute or by the individual.

2. **Can the government identify an authorized law-enforcement activity?**

   The agency must establish the legal authority for that activity. It need not prove the plaintiff committed a crime. Intelligence investigations, criminal investigations, and authorized civil enforcement may qualify.

3. **Does the information remain pertinent to that activity?**

   The court examines the actual relationship, not a hypothetical future possibility. Relevant considerations include:

   - Why the information was collected.
   - What investigative purpose it still serves.
   - Whether it preserves necessary context or evidence.
   - Whether subsequent events have changed its relevance.
   - Whether continued use or indexing exceeds the justified investigative scope.

   No single consideration automatically decides the case.

4. **What disposition and remedy does the law authorize?**

   If a qualifying connection remains, the agency may retain the information under the statutory exception. If no qualifying connection remains, the court determines the violation and available relief under the Privacy Act.

   The court separately accounts for lawful evidence-preservation and federal records-management duties.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Each joins the entire four-step rule, including every listed consideration and the separate preservation/relief requirements. Scalia and Thomas reject the complete rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Act defines maintenance to include keeping, collection, use and dissemination, while (e)(7) attaches its prohibition and alternatives to that continuing conduct. Congress's different acquisition-based terms elsewhere do not make initial lawfulness permanently dispositive. The three alternatives remain distinct: express statutory authorization, individual authorization and authorized-law-enforcement pertinence.

Authorized activity is broader than an active investigation or a current investigation of this plaintiff. Genuine historical intelligence value, connected or later inquiries, necessary evidentiary context and legitimate preservation of a law-enforcement record may supply an actual continuing relationship. Age, closure, innocence and name retrieval alone decide nothing. Conversely, the agency's general mission or the possibility that any datum might someday prove useful cannot conclusively establish every record's pertinence indefinitely. The Government's identified continuing-interest contention remains available for record-specific assessment.

Step 2 requires the agency to establish the activity's legal authority, without proof that the plaintiff committed a crime. Step 3's considerations illuminate actual pertinence and scope; they are nonexclusive, none is automatically decisive, and they create no current-necessity or least-restrictive-recordkeeping standard. The rule imposes no periodic audit, automatic request-response duty, deadline, universal consultation, prescribed explanation format or public disclosure of classified reasons, and no general shift of the independent burdens concerning injury, fault or damages. Ordinary judicial procedure and appropriate confidential review remain available.

The contrary historical-activity construction properly recognizes preservation value but improperly makes that value conclusive solely from acquisition. Department of Defense v. FLRA keeps disclosure protections and their exceptions distinct; confidentiality does not replace Congress's separate retention restriction. No dissemination claim, automatic purge on closure or present finding of nonpertinence is decided.

##### IV-A. The separate constitutional claims lack the required supported connection

**Holding and operative rule:** The Foundation's affidavits do not establish the causal mechanism connecting FBI retention to prospective grantees' or employers' knowledge and reactions; Lindblom's separately presented constitutional claim supplies no independent supported basis beyond that failed derivative theory. Both constitutional-standing rejections therefore stand, without a ruling on the First Amendment merits or a categorical exclusion of reputational or associational injury.

**Authority:** Part IV-A has all nine Justices. Scalia and Thomas's additional injury/imminence ground is separate and noncontrolling.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan and Lee require specific support at summary judgment, with favorable reasonable inferences. Predictions of stigma or employment effects do not themselves establish how these audiences learn of the retained references. Meese involved an actual public governmental label and detailed evidence connecting it to threatened harm. It shows why completed loss or identification of every deterred person is unnecessary; it does not supply the missing exposure link here. Nor does a claimant's own publicity categorically make all resulting injury self-inflicted.

Lindblom supplied no separate constitutional-standing argument or evidentiary showing. His pleaded subjective chill is not summary-judgment proof under Laird, and his employment elsewhere supplies no independent demonstrated constitutional injury. The actual personal statutory information interest has a different direct connection to the FBI's custody; its recognition does not manufacture the missing presentation of the distinct constitutional claim. The Court decides neither that FBI recordkeeping can never violate the First Amendment nor that these statutory records have no adverse personal effect.

##### IV-B. The constitutional dismissals must be without prejudice

**Holding and operative rule:** A dismissal of these constitutional claims for want of Article III standing cannot carry a merits-preclusive effect. The standing rejections are sustained, but the district judgment's with-prejudice effect is vacated and the case is remanded to conform those dismissals to their jurisdictional ground.

**Authority:** Part IV-B has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only. The correction is unanimous.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans and Lee distinguish limited authority to correct a lower court's jurisdictional disposition from authority to decide constitutional merits. The September 6 order dismissed the entire complaint with prejudice. Leaving that effect on claims dismissed for lack of standing would imply a merits adjudication the courts lack power to enter. Section 2106 permits the bounded correction but creates no substantive jurisdiction. No declaration or injunction is restored, and no evidentiary proceeding on the constitutional merits is directed.

##### V-A. The statutory judgment requires record-specific further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** The conclusive original-collection rule cannot sustain summary judgment on Lindblom's retention claim. That component is vacated and remanded for the complete test's application to the actual records, with any independent statutory or individual authorization and lawful preservation obligation considered separately; the Court awards neither expungement nor damages.

**Authority:** Part V-A has Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Scalia and Thomas oppose statutory remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The finding of legitimate original compilation does not answer whether each record retains the relationship now required. The Government contends that some other persons and organizations remain of investigative interest; that contention may matter, but the Court does not make the missing record-specific pertinence determination in either party's favor. Confidential and in camera assessment remains available where appropriate. Any concrete evidence-preservation or federal records-management duty must be accounted for without treating a general reference to preservation as conclusive permission or as an automatic destruction command. The remand corrects the governing legal rule; it does not itself reverse the discovery refusal, require additional evidence or pleadings, order an agency review program, or find intentional wrongdoing. Unreviewed claims remain undisturbed.

##### V-B. Each remedial route retains its independent requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** The retention restriction does not create unlimited relief: monetary liability under §552a(g)(1)(D)/(g)(4), amendment relief under (d)/(g)(1)(A)/(g)(2), access relief, and any independently permissible prospective equity retain their distinct conditions. Neither an automatic monetary award nor a general expungement entitlement follows from the Court's construction.

**Authority:** Part V-B has all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees that no final award issues and joins Part V-A's separate lawful-relief and preservation directions, without adopting V-B's additional grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** Intentional or willful noncompliance, causally resulting compensable actual damages and the statutory minimum remain separate questions; the Court decides no amount or nonpecuniary-damages issue. Costs and fees do not independently supply redress for an otherwise absent controversy. No constitutional damages cause against the FBI is created.

An amendment route must satisfy its applicable request, refusal, review and exemption predicates. Exhausted access requests do not alone establish amendment exhaustion. Nagel recognizes the distinct (e)(7) claim and monetary route; Clarkson links possible expungement to the amendment provisions and their limits; Doe rejects bypassing the Act's remedies. Their qualified accounts supply no unconditional purge warrant. Sections (j) and (k) do not permit exemption from (e)(7), and a regulation cannot defeat that nonexemptible duty merely by exempting civil liability. A valid (d) exemption may nonetheless defeat a derivative amendment claim. A FOIA exemption or general records-system designation does not alone settle those distinct predicates; no waiver is presumed.

Reich and §702 preserve possible nonmonetary restraint subject to an existing lawful action, statutory preclusion and ordinary equitable limits. The Court need not decide that entire route because the monetary action permits this statutory review. No separate APA claim is created. If a mandatory decree is authorized, §702 requires identification of the responsible officer or successor by name or title; absence of a personal name from the caption alone does not answer every equitable question. These reservations govern any further relief, not the independently resolved Article III controversy.

#### Law After Decision

Courts reviewing maintained First Amendment records must apply the complete Continuing Pertinence Test. Original lawful collection remains relevant but cannot conclusively excuse all later retention. Genuine historical law-enforcement value remains permissible; closure, age, innocence and name indexing do not automatically require deletion. The distinct statutory, individual-authorization, remedy and preservation requirements remain operative.

The six-Justice adverse-effect holding recognizes the actual unlawful personal information invasion as potentially sufficient under (g)(1)(D), without establishing actual damages. The eight-Associate threshold/action rulings permit merits review of this existing personal monetary controversy without assuming ultimate recovery. The constitutional dismissals rest on the actual causal and claim-specific defects, with no new categorical restriction on reputational injury. No periodic review program, unrestricted disclosure, automatic damages or unconditional expungement rule is created.

#### Separate Writings

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree that the actual personal statutory controversy permits review, that the constitutional claims fail on the Court's stated grounds, and that their dismissals must be without prejudice. They reserve whether the direct retention invasion alone satisfies statutory adverse effect because statutory nonviolation suffices on their view.

For them, a record pertinent to a lawful investigation remains pertinent to that authorized activity even after it is completed. The continuing maintenance prohibition still forbids keeping records never within a lawful statutory category; it need not require a renewed present-use justification for the conceded lawful historical record. Legitimate intelligence preservation reinforces that reading. They would affirm statutory summary judgment and reject the complete test's continuing-connection requirement, while accepting its severable protected-record, legal-authority and independent-remedy principles. They do not find the information unlawfully collected, endorse unrestricted disclosure or regard closure and innocent contact as automatic violations.

They also conclude that the reported constitutional predictions lack sufficiently concrete or imminent personal consequences on this record, independently of traceability. They impose no named-lost-grantee rule, particular affidavit form or numerical probability requirement. That additional ground has two votes and is not the Court's holding. No final monetary or expungement entitlement follows under their position.

#### Procedure After Action

The statutory summary judgment is vacated and the case returns through the Court of Appeals for lawful further proceedings under the complete test. Present pertinence, independent authorization, remaining monetary liability, lawful amendment or equitable relief, confidentiality and any concrete preservation obligation remain for their proper determination. The constitutional-standing rejections remain in place; the lower judgment must be conformed to dismissal of those claims without prejudice. No constitutional declaration or injunction revives. Original collection, abandoned claims and other unreviewed components remain undisturbed.
