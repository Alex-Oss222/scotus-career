## Standards and Tests selection: Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review. An earlier pleading-stage appeal does not by itself exhaust review of a later summary-judgment order: a separable legal immunity question must independently satisfy the collateral-order conditions. This permits neither repetition of an unchanged ruling nor relitigation of matters resolved by the first mandate. Having to defend a different surviving claim does not itself defeat otherwise authorized claim-specific review, but neither supplies missing authorization nor makes that other claim reviewable.

Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

### Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

**Governing question:** Proof, discovery and causation when unconstitutional motive is an element of a claim and qualified immunity is asserted.

**Current rule:** Qualified immunity imposes no categorical direct-evidence requirement for unconstitutional motive. The plaintiff must establish the actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation through legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence under ordinary procedural burdens. Specific nonconclusory support, lawful discovery limits and the clearly-established-law defense remain available; the rule abolishes neither all heightened specificity requirements nor a burden supplied by the underlying claim. Ordinary preponderance governs unconstitutional motive in the §1983 retaliation claim; immunity and litigation burden cannot justify a special clear-and-convincing quantum. Courts may decide dispositive legal defenses first and require specific nonconclusory support, but cannot categorically withhold discovery needed to obtain proof until the rejected heightened showing is made. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, sequencing, protective orders, relevant proportional discovery and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available. Under Mt. Healthy the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful explanation does not establish that defense or exclude competent motive evidence.

## Standards and Tests selection: Criminal fair warning under §242

### Criminal fair warning under §242

**Governing question:** Whether pre-existing law gave adequate warning that an official's charged conduct was constitutionally unlawful under 18 U.S.C. §242.

**Current rule:** The statute alone or as authoritatively construed must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; novel or unforeseeable criminal applications are forbidden. Relevant pre-existing law may include lower-court authority, without a Supreme-Court-only requirement, automatic sufficiency of a single lower decision or fixed numerical consensus. An obviously covered application needs no factually matching precedent, but abstract invocation of liberty or bodily security does not settle an actually unresolved application. Criminal fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold.

## Standards and Tests selection: Political retaliation in existing government service relationships

### Political retaliation in existing government service relationships

**Governing question:** Termination, nonrenewal or withdrawal of an existing independent-contractor or recurring service-provider relationship for citizen speech or partisan allegiance.

**Current rule:** Government may not terminate or refuse to renew an existing contractor relationship because of private-citizen speech on a matter of public concern when the speaker's and public's interests outweigh legitimate contracting and operational interests. Apply Connick's content, form and context inquiry and Pickering's balance adjusted to the actual service relationship, including genuine performance, efficiency, cost, trust and disruption concerns; independence, other clients, contacts and responsibilities may matter in either direction. Neither contractor status, termination authority, lack of property entitlement nor absence of direct governmental purchase categorically defeats protection. Private delivery alone does not remove qualifying expression.

For withdrawal based on refusing partisan support or supporting an opponent, apply the distinct Elrod–Branti rule: government must demonstrate that political affiliation is appropriate for effective performance of the actual work. Labels, general patronage advantages and dislike of opposition do not suffice; genuinely policymaking or confidential functions may justify the qualification. Public-concern speech and materially mixed speech/affiliation claims require their appropriate service-adjusted inquiry rather than an undifferentiated affiliation or management rule.

The claimant must show protected conduct was a substantial or motivating factor. Government may defeat the claim by proving by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred absent that conduct; a merely possible lawful reason does not establish the defense. Entity attribution, personal defenses, injury and relief retain their separate requirements.

**Authority by component:**

- Existing-contractor expression and the causation sequence: Protected expression in an existing contractual relationship and Causation, the same-decision defense and the limited remand, 1996-06-28; Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

## Standards and Tests selection: Voluntary public employment and substantive workplace safety duties

### Voluntary public employment and substantive workplace safety duties

**Governing question:** Whether a voluntary public-employment relationship creates a general substantive-due-process duty to provide workplace safety.

**Current rule:** Voluntary municipal employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care, and substantive due process imposes no independent general obligation to provide such employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks. A state-law safety duty, even assuming it creates a protected liberty interest, does not convert its breach alone into a substantive constitutional wrong. The underlying federal right and constitutional arbitrariness remain distinct from state-law entitlement and municipal attribution.

## Standards and Tests selection: Modification of institutional consent decrees

### Modification of institutional consent decrees

**Governing question:** When Rule 60(b)(5) permits modification of a continuing institutional consent decree and how the change must be framed.

**Current rule:** The party seeking modification must prove a significant change in fact or law making continued enforcement inequitable. An unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary, but actual anticipation and acceptance of the relevant risk impose a heavy burden to justify relief. Dissatisfaction, a new administration, or the availability of a cheaper constitutional alternative alone does not suffice. Once the threshold is met, the modification must be suitably tailored to the proven change and preserve lawful protection of the decree's remaining purposes; the court may not simply rewrite the decree to the constitutional minimum. Practical administration and cost may inform the selection of lawful relief but cannot substitute for the threshold showing or authorize unconstitutional conditions. A clarification that the Constitution permits an alternative practice does not by itself eliminate a voluntarily accepted stricter obligation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prison-conditions prospective relief

### Prison-conditions prospective relief

**Governing question:** The original §3626 scope, necessity findings and preliminary-relief conditions.

**Current rule:** In a civil proceeding arising under federal law concerning conditions of confinement or officials' effects on confined persons' lives, prospective relief must be narrowly drawn, extend no further than necessary to correct the particular plaintiffs' federal-right violation and be the least intrusive necessary means; the court must make those findings and give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice-system effects. Relief requiring or permitting an official to exceed state or local authority or otherwise violate that law also requires federal permission, necessity to correct the federal violation and absence of any other corrective relief. The statute confers no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves otherwise applicable remedial limits.

Otherwise authorized preliminary injunctive relief must be narrow, no broader than necessary for the identified harm and least intrusive, with substantial safety/system weight and respect for the distinct state-law conditions. It expires ninety days after entry unless the court makes the prospective-relief findings and makes the order final before expiration. An existing permanent decree does not automatically become a preliminary order.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays

### Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays

**Governing question:** When existing prospective relief becomes terminable or stayed under original §3626.

**Current rule:** On a party's or intervener's motion, prospective prison-conditions relief is terminable two years after grant or approval, one year after denial of a termination motion under §3626(b)(1), or, for an order on or before enactment, two years after April 26, 1996. A defendant or intervener is entitled to immediate termination when relief was entered without the required need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness findings. Relief shall not terminate if the court makes written, record-based findings that it remains necessary to correct a current OR ongoing federal-right violation and is narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary and least intrusive. Earlier agreed modification or termination and otherwise lawful earlier judicial modification or termination remain possible.

The court must promptly decide modification or termination motions. Under original §3626(e), prospective relief subject to an actual pending motion is automatically stayed from day thirty after filing a motion under (b)(1) or (b)(2), or day 180 for a motion under other law, until entry of the final order deciding it. Enactment alone creates neither a termination nor an automatic stay.

Section 3626(b)(2) is an independent statutory route: the State must establish its termination predicate, and the court must independently apply the full original (b)(3) preservation condition. Rufo's equitable changed-circumstances threshold is not an additional prerequisite. Existing substantive findings can satisfy the statute without ritual wording. Necessary corrective relief may extend beyond the minimum substantive conduct rule, but negotiated decree terms do not thereby become independent federal rights preserving every obligation. Applied with these safeguards, the rule changes prospective governing law without displacing Article III adjudication or promising beneficiaries perpetual enforcement of unchanged terms. Courts still determine actual violations, findings, necessity and scope; the statute supplies no legislative finding of compliance or permission to cancel accrued damages.

## Standards and Tests selection: Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments

### Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments

**Governing question:** Whether and on what conditions §27A(b) requires reinstatement of a completed, unappealed private securities limitations dismissal.

**Current rule:** Under Morgan Stanley, Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement under §27A(b) of a qualifying private §10(b) action even though its limitations dismissal became final without appeal before the provision's December 19, 1991 enactment: the action must have commenced on or before June 19, 1991, been dismissed as time barred after that date, and been timely under the jurisdiction's law, including retroactivity principles, as it existed June 19, 1991, with a reinstatement motion filed within sixty days after enactment. The court independently determines those cumulative conditions and, when they are satisfied, must reinstate without discretionary balancing, retaining independent responsibility for all claims, defenses, and remedies; this expressly retrospective entitlement does not authorize legislative findings, dictated liability, or unrestricted reopening of completed merits judgments. A narrowly defined, exclusively retrospective class does not by itself turn this procedure into a legislative judgment: the statute covers every action meeting its legal criteria and selects no parties for legislatively determined liability.

**Authority by component:**

- Defined statutory entitlement, cumulative conditions, mandatory reinstatement, independent judicial adjudication and constitutional limits: Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. — Bounded statutory reinstatement of completed securities limitations dismissals, May 23, 1994. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.
- Application to a final unappealed dismissal and the boundary for narrow, exclusively retrospective class coverage: Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. — Section 27A(b) permits mandatory reinstatement of this completed limitations dismissal, April 18, 1995. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994.

**Exceptions and limits:** Congress may not command findings contrary to the record, impose liability on identified defendants by legislative declaration, or disguise an individualized award as a procedural reopening law. The Court decides no general power to set aside completed judgments adjudicating the underlying merits, no constitutionality of every retrospective statute, and no independent due-process question; applicable due-process protections and other constitutional restrictions remain. Section 27A(a), other applications of subsection (b), general severability, federal waiver, continuing prospective relief, and independently authorized judicial reopening are not decided. The decision neither adjudicates other parties' completed cases nor creates criminal or innocence remedies. Fraud, reliance, causation, liability, damages, and ordinary merits defenses remain for the courts; reinstatement establishes none of them.

**Related current rules:** Federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review concerns judicial retroactivity alone, which supplies no reopening entitlement. Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct separates express temporal direction from constitutional authority; the ordinary presumption against retroactivity does not defeat §27A(b)'s express reach. Robertson's pending-litigation distinction between changing governing law and prescribing adjudication remains intact; Morgan Stanley supplies the completed-dismissal rule Robertson left open.

**Holdings navigation:** Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. — Bounded statutory reinstatement of completed securities limitations dismissals; Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. — Section 27A(b) permits mandatory reinstatement of this completed limitations dismissal.

#### Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1279.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether §1983 adds a freestanding governmental-power-abuse element for municipal employees

**Holding:** A municipal employee who establishes deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and municipal responsibility need not also prove an independent, freestanding abuse-of-governmental-power element. The existence of the constitutional wrong and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous statutory holding.

**Material application:** The Fifth Circuit’s additional statutory requirement cannot sustain dismissal merely because the city acted as an employer. Its rejection does not establish the underlying federal right or final municipal attribution; attribution is assumed only to resolve the constitutional question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employment alone does not establish municipal liability, and deficient training does not create a substantive constitutional right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The extra statutory element is rejected, but dismissal of the federal claim is affirmed on the separate constitutional grounds below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658, supplies municipal policy and causation requirements distinct from employment alone. *Canton v. Harris*, 489 U.S. 378, is clarified as an attribution doctrine, not a source of the underlying substantive right. *Oklahoma City v. Tuttle*, 471 U.S. 808, preserves the distinction between constitutional wrong and municipal responsibility.

##### Whether voluntary municipal employment or these pleaded safety omissions establish a substantive-due-process deprivation

**Holding:** The substantive Due Process Clause does not impose an independent general obligation on a municipality to provide its voluntary employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks of harm. Accepting public employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous duty limitation.

**Material application:** The complaint alleges known hazards and deliberate failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers. Those allegations are accepted at dismissal; they do not make voluntary employment a custodial restraint.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not excuse violation of other applicable federal rights. Actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury, a different properly pleaded affirmative-danger claim and procedural due process are not decided. State safety and tort remedies are not adjudicated, and knowing allegations are not reduced to negligence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal substantive-due-process claim. No federal compensation or supervision order is entered; independently available state-law remedies retain their own merits and procedural requirements.

**Holding:** Bounds requires a meaningful practical opportunity for court access through effective libraries, trained assistance or other effective arrangements considered as a whole; adequate books do not discharge that duty for prisoners unable to use them without needed assistance. **Holding:** Article III permits §27A(b)'s expressly retrospective requirement to reinstate a private §10(b) action commenced on or before June 19, 1991, dismissed as time barred after June 19, 1991, and timely under the jurisdiction's law, including retroactivity principles, as that law existed on June 19, 1991, upon a motion filed within sixty days after the provision's December 19, 1991 enactment. - Chase Securities Corp. v. Donaldson, 325 U.S. 304 (1945): preserves its due-process treatment of revived limitations claims without treating that decision as dispositive of Article III.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may not declare a completed judicial factual finding erroneous and compel the contrary finding, dictate a merits judgment, or disguise an individualized award or assessment of liability as a procedural reopening statute. This holding does not decide compelled reopening of merits judgments or individualized legislative commands to adjudicate liability. The Court announces no independent due-process holding and does not decide §27A(a), general severability, fraud, liability, damages or entitlement to judgment on the securities merits. Pending litigation, prospective relief and independently authorized judicial reopening remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's judgment sustaining the reinstatement procedure is affirmed. Proceedings may continue under §27A(b). The federal court must determine the statutory prerequisites and reinstate the action when they are proved; it has no discretion to deny reinstatement through a balancing test. The securities claims, defenses and remedies remain for ordinary judicial adjudication. No fraud finding, liability judgment or damages award is entered.

**Material judgment:** Affirmed, 5–3. Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support affirmance; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent. O'Connor does not participate.

#### Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1121.
**Decided:** April 18, 1995.

##### Section 27A(b) permits mandatory reinstatement of this completed limitations dismissal

**Holding:** Under Morgan Stanley, Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement under §27A(b) of a qualifying private §10(b) action even though its limitations dismissal became final without appeal before the provision's December 19, 1991 enactment: the action must have commenced on or before June 19, 1991, been dismissed as time barred after that date, and been timely under the jurisdiction's law, including retroactivity principles, as it existed June 19, 1991, with a reinstatement motion filed within sixty days after enactment. The court independently determines those cumulative conditions and, when they are satisfied, must reinstate without discretionary balancing, retaining independent responsibility for all claims, defenses, and remedies; this expressly retrospective entitlement does not authorize legislative findings, dictated liability, or unrestricted reopening of completed merits judgments.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Those five direct rationale joins control every proposition, qualification, and remedy stated here. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas dissent; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** A narrowly defined, exclusively retrospective class does not by itself turn this procedure into a legislative judgment: the statute covers every action meeting its legal criteria and selects no parties for legislatively determined liability. The statutory conditions are satisfied, including the timely February 11, 1992 motion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994: applies its five-Justice Article III holding sustaining §27A(b)'s bounded mandatory reinstatement procedure to this qualifying completed private limitations dismissal; preserves independent eligibility and merits adjudication and all constitutional limits.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, March 25, 1992: applies its distinction between genuine legislative change and prescribed judicial adjudication; Morgan Stanley supplies the completed-dismissal rule that Robertson did not decide.
- United States v. Klein, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 128: applies its protection of the independent judicial function; a class-defined entitlement to renewed adjudication does not dictate findings or securities liability.
- McCullough v. Virginia, 172 U.S. 102: preserves protection of completed judicial determinations while distinguishing the statutory entitlement to litigate a limitations-dismissed claim from a legislative declaration of facts or liability contrary to an adjudication.
- James B. Beam Distilling Co. v. Georgia, 501 U.S. 529: preserves finality against reopening by later judicial doctrine alone; the entitlement here comes from an express statute whose Article III validity Morgan Stanley establishes.
- Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 59 U.S. (18 How.) 421: distinguishes the continuing operation of prospective equitable relief; that setting does not itself decide this completed limitations dismissal.
- United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians, 448 U.S. 371: distinguishes the Government's waiver of its own defense; no governmental waiver of a private defendant's defense supplies the holding here.
- Chase Securities Corp. v. Donaldson, 325 U.S. 304: leaves its due-process revival rule intact without treating it as a controlling answer to Article III.
- Lampf, Pleva, Lipkind, Prupis & Petigrow v. Gilbertson, 501 U.S. 350: identifies the limitations rule that produced the dismissal and the statutory response; §27A(b) supplies reinstatement without establishing the securities merits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may not command findings contrary to the record, impose liability on identified defendants by legislative declaration, or disguise an individualized award as a procedural reopening law. The Court decides no general power to set aside completed judgments adjudicating the underlying merits, no constitutionality of every retrospective statute, and no independent due-process question; applicable due-process protections and other constitutional restrictions remain. Section 27A(a), other applications of subsection (b), general severability, federal waiver, continuing prospective relief, and independently authorized judicial reopening are not decided. The decision neither adjudicates other parties' completed cases nor creates criminal or innocence remedies. Fraud, reliance, causation, liability, damages, and ordinary merits defenses remain for the courts; reinstatement establishes none of them.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment of the Sixth Circuit is reversed. The case is remanded for reinstatement under the satisfied conditions of §27A(b) and further judicial proceedings on the underlying securities claims. Reinstatement is required, not a discretionary remedy. The courts retain responsibility for the merits, defenses, and any relief; no merits outcome is prescribed.

**Material judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 5–4. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support reversal; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent.

##### II. Conditional termination changes governing law without displacing Article III adjudication

**Holding:** Section 3626(b)(2), applied with the full original (b)(3) safeguard, does not violate Article III by changing the legal authority for continued prospective enforcement of this decree.
