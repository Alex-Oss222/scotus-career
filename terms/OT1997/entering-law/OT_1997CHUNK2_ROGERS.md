## Standards and Tests selection: Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

## Standards and Tests selection: Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

### Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

**Governing question:** Harmlessness for preserved Doyle error and the preserved personal-intent instructional category properly available on federal habeas.

**Current rule:** Use of post-Miranda silence to impeach a trial explanation violates Doyle and constitutes constitutional trial error rather than a nonconstitutional rule or structural defect. Permitted prewarning impeachment remains distinct. For preserved Doyle error properly before a federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that it did not contribute to the verdict, considering the whole record. Collateral posture alone does not substitute Kotteakos; relief depends on failure of the State's Chapman showing and is not automatic. Other habeas gates and structural-error rules remain independent; this does not prescribe every collateral-review procedure. The same State-borne Chapman inquiry governs a preserved constitutional trial violation in which the combined jury instructions and prosecutor’s statement permit conviction without proof of the defendant’s own intent required by the offense. Kotteakos’s substantial-and-injurious-effect and grave-doubt inquiry does not govern that category. If an available violation is established and the State fails to prove that it did not contribute to the verdict, conditional relief must allow a reasonable retrial period for the affected conviction; identifying the wrong review standard alone does not establish a violation or command a writ. The personal-intent category also covers omission of the defendant’s required aiding purpose without an accompanying prosecutorial statement. Such omission is trial error, not automatically structural error. Necessary jury findings embracing the omitted purpose are sufficient but not the exclusive route to harmlessness: supported whole-record inferences may show no contribution to the actual verdict. The reviewing court must ask whether the instructions led the jury to consider the evidence supporting that inference. If the omission prevented reliable consideration of the personal-purpose question, strong evidence alone is insufficient. Knowledge plus assistance, general mental capacity, or evidence sufficient for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction cannot substitute for the required actual-verdict inquiry.

## Standards and Tests selection: Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

### Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

**Governing question:** What instructions govern forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)?

**Current rule:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of forfeited error only if there is error, it is plain, and it affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, unlike failure timely to assert it. Plain means clear or obvious under current law. The defendant ordinarily must show outcome-affecting prejudice. Even then correction is discretionary and should occur only if the error seriously affects fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings; actual innocence is unnecessary, but substantial-rights impact alone is insufficient. Presence of alternates instructed not to participate supplies neither prejudice nor a presumption without a showing of participation or chilling effect. Initially unsettled trial law, special outcome-independent categories, justified prejudice presumptions and hearing authority remain unresolved. Johnson assumes substantial-rights injury without deciding whether withholding materiality is structural or harmless; its fourth-condition application supplies no general guilt-based exception or innocence prerequisite. When settled law permitted the trial action but intervening controlling law makes it erroneous during direct review, plainness is measured at appellate consideration. A forfeited omission of a genuine jury element proceeds under the distinct Rule 52(b) conditions rather than automatic reversal.

## Standards and Tests selection: A vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error

### A vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error

**Governing question:** What instructions govern a vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error?

**Current rule:** An unconstitutional reasonable-doubt instruction vitiating the governing standard for all factual findings leaves no constitutionally valid jury verdict of guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The defect requires reversal without harmless-error assessment; an appellate hypothetical verdict cannot replace the jury determination. Other instructional mistakes are not thereby classified as structural, and collateral-review gates remain separate.

## Standards and Tests selection: Knowledge of machinegun characteristics in unregistered possession

### Knowledge of machinegun characteristics in unregistered possession

**Governing question:** What must be proved when a conventional-looking rifle falls within the whole-weapon machinegun definition.

**Current rule:** For §5861(d) possession of an unregistered rifle prosecuted as a whole-weapon machinegun under §5845(b), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the characteristics making it a machinegun: it shoots, is designed to shoot, or can readily be restored to shoot automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, by a single function of the trigger. Knowledge that the weapon is unregistered or that possession is illegal is not required. Ordinary possession of a rifle is not enough to presume knowledge of its automatic-fire characteristics.

**Authority by component:**

**Present operation effective:** Staples v. United States, 1994-05-23.

## Verbatim authority selection: Whether the prior-act instruction created a reasonable likelihood that jurors bypassed elemental proof

Source: `state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md`; SHA-256 `1422e45b6c579a978c55af29d7d7ff12ec163b43de82473de6d8a60724e41cde`.

##### Whether the prior-act instruction created a reasonable likelihood that jurors bypassed elemental proof

**Holding:** An ambiguous instruction violates due process only when, read with the charge and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood the jury applied it in a way that relieved the State of proving an element. This instruction did not create that likelihood because it conditioned any inference on the jury's finding that McGuire committed the prior acts and expressly prohibited a bad-character inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion joined at this level by Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; five of eight participants. Stone, Stevens, and O'Connor join only the distinct prior-injury-evidence proposition.

**Material application:** The complete charge neither directed a finding that McGuire caused the earlier injuries nor shifted the prosecution's burden on identity or any other element.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not approve every prior-act instruction or decide a general propensity-evidence rule. Because the controlling majority finds no constitutional error, it does not reach Teague, Rose, or Chapman or require harmless-error review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed. The habeas writ remains denied; no acquittal, retrial, or release is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cupp supplies whole-charge review, and Boyde supplies the reasonable-likelihood standard. Francis and Sandstrom are distinguished and preserved for mandatory presumptions or shifts in elemental persuasion.

##### This personal-intent omission permits harmless-error review under Chapman

**Holding:** The preserved omission of Roy's purpose to facilitate robbery is a constitutional trial error subject to harmlessness review, not an error requiring automatic reversal merely because an element was omitted. For this personal-intent violation properly available on federal habeas, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt, on the whole record, that the error did not contribute to the verdict; neither collateral posture nor absence of accompanying prosecutorial argument substitutes a weaker burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Knowledge and affirmative assistance do not equal purpose to facilitate robbery. The rule does not extend Chapman to every constitutional habeas claim, revive resolved exhaustion issues, or decide AEDPA's temporal reach.
