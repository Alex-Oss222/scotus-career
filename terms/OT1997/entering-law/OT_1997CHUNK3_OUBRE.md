## Standards and Tests selection: After-acquired misconduct and age-discrimination remedies

### After-acquired misconduct and age-discrimination remedies

**Governing question:** The effect of misconduct discovered after an allegedly age-discriminatory discharge.

**Current rule:** Misconduct unknown to the employer at discharge does not erase an ADEA violation or categorically eliminate all relief. The employee retains the liability burden. To limit relief, the employer must prove misconduct so serious that it would in fact have discharged the employee for that reason alone, not merely that discharge would have been permissible. Once proved, reinstatement and front pay are ordinarily inappropriate; backpay’s starting measure runs from unlawful discharge to actual discovery, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party’s legitimate interests.

**Authority by component:**

- Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation: Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation, 1995-01-23. O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt this proposition and explanation; it is a direct majority holding.
- Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief: Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief, 1995-01-23. O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt the proof requirement and bounded remedial rule.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-23; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Actual age motivation, ultimate persuasion and separate willfulness requirements remain unchanged. A litigation assumption of discrimination does not establish liability.

**Holdings navigation:** Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation; Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief.

## Standards and Tests selection: Advance plea-statement waivers for impeachment

### Advance plea-statement waivers for impeachment

**Governing question:** Enforceability of a plea-discussion agreement permitting impeachment of the defendant’s own inconsistent testimony.

**Current rule:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that statements may impeach the defendant’s own inconsistent trial testimony. Absent an affirmative indication of unknowing or involuntary entry, that limited waiver is enforceable. Supported ignorance, fraud, coercion, involuntariness and agreement-scope objections receive individual review. Inconsistency alone creates no nonconsensual impeachment exception.

**Authority by component:**

- A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable: A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable, 1995-01-18. Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Six Justices adopt the limited waiver rule and its presumption; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a different requirement that the Government prove knowing and voluntary acceptance and is not counted as a join in that presumption.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-18; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Then-Rule 410 and parallel Rule 11(e)(6) protect against use against the participating defendant in civil or criminal proceedings of withdrawn guilty pleas, no-contest pleas, related Rule 11 or comparable state-proceeding statements, and prosecutor plea discussions resulting in no guilty plea or a withdrawn plea. The fairness exception requires another statement from the same plea or discussion already introduced and fairness requiring contemporaneous consideration. The criminal perjury-or-false-statement exception requires oath, an on-record statement and counsel’s presence; ordinary impeachment is not that exception. The waiver holding decides no case-in-chief, derivative, sentencing or other-person-testimony use, no independent perjury-use term and no universal waiver of fundamental protections. Independent constitutional and evidentiary limits remain; the concurrence’s additional Government-proof requirement does not control.

**Holdings navigation:** A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable.

## Verbatim selected Holdings case: McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co.

Source: [state/holdings/labor-and-employment.md](../../../state/holdings/labor-and-employment.md), exact case heading below.

#### McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1543.
**Decided:** January 23, 1995.

##### Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation

**Holding:** Misconduct unknown to the employer when it discharged an employee cannot defeat liability for an age-discriminatory discharge or categorically bar every remedy. The employee must still establish an ADEA violation under the governing liability rules; an employer’s assumption of discrimination for summary judgment is not a final liability finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hazen Paper’s actual-motive requirement remains controlling; Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse are distinguished because they concern reasons involved in the challenged decision. St. Mary’s Honor Center retains its existing proof framework and does not make rejection of an employer’s explanation an automatic liability judgment.

##### Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief

**Holding:** To limit relief through after-acquired evidence, the employer must prove misconduct so serious that it would in fact have discharged the employee for that reason alone had it known, rather than merely showing that discharge was permissible. Once that showing is made, reinstatement and front pay are ordinarily inappropriate, and backpay’s starting measure is the period from unlawful discharge to actual discovery, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party’s legitimate interests.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hazen Paper’s knowledge-or-recklessness standard continues to govern any claim of willfulness; this remedial ruling does not lower it. Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse supply no complete forfeiture rule for a reason discovered after the challenged discharge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine protected opposition or participation cannot supply a lawful discharge ground; this case announces no general ban on obtaining evidence of discrimination. No final discrimination, willfulness, compensation, liquidated-damages, fee, or reinstatement finding is made. Section 626(c)(1)’s termination of the individual right to bring an action when the EEOC commences an action to enforce that employee’s right remains distinct; no such agency action is established here. The 1991 Title VII amendments supply no new rule for this ADEA discharge.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is reversed and remanded for liability adjudication without treating unknown misconduct as a discharge reason and for relief under the employer's actual-would-discharge showing. No final discrimination or damages determination is entered.

## Verbatim selected Holdings case: United States v. Mezzanatto

Source: [state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md](../../../state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md), exact case heading below.

#### United States v. Mezzanatto

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1340.
**Decided:** January 18, 1995.

##### A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable

**Holding:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that his statements may be used to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony; absent an affirmative indication that this agreement was entered unknowingly or involuntarily, the limited waiver is enforceable. Supported challenges based on ignorance, fraud, coercion, involuntariness, or the agreement's scope remain subject to individual review, and inconsistency alone creates no nonconsensual impeachment exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only; his different proof requirement is not controlling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ricketts v. Adamson, 483 U.S. 1 (1987): draws on enforcement of an agreement's actual lawful terms, without importing consequences that were not agreed.
- Town of Newton v. Rumery, 480 U.S. 386 (1987): applies its rejection of automatic invalidity while preserving individual voluntariness and overreaching review; no new universal public-interest evidentiary burden is imposed.
- Brady v. United States, 397 U.S. 742 (1970): preserves knowing-and-voluntary assent despite the existence of bargaining pressure; pressure alone does not establish coercion.
- Peretz v. United States, 501 U.S. 923 (1991): uses its bounded recognition of consent as an analogy, not a rule that every institutional protection can be waived.
- Brooklyn Savings Bank v. O'Neil, 324 U.S. 697 (1945): distinguishes its prohibition on private defeat of the particular public statutory protection there; the Court does not deny that some protections may be nonwaivable.
- Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993): preserves the distinction between mental capacity and an actually informed, voluntary choice; competence or a signature alone does not dispose of a supported contrary record.
- United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993): applies the distinction between intentional relinquishment and forfeiture, preserving right-specific requirements and the actual preserved objection here.
- United States v. Salerno, 505 U.S. 317 (1992): distinguishes express relinquishment from dispensing with enacted evidentiary conditions or treating independent contradiction as disclosure; its holdings remain unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Then-Rule 410 excludes, against the defendant who made the plea or participated in the discussions in any civil or criminal proceeding, a later-withdrawn guilty plea, a no-contest plea, statements in Rule 11 or comparable state proceedings regarding either such plea, and statements in discussions with the prosecuting attorney that produce no guilty plea or one later withdrawn. Rule 11(e)(6) is substantively parallel. The fairness exception requires another statement from the same plea or discussion to have been introduced and fairness to require contemporaneous consideration. The criminal perjury-or-false-statement exception requires the statement to have been made under oath, on the record, and in counsel's presence; ordinary impeachment is not that exception. This decision validates no case-in-chief, derivative, sentencing, or other-person-testimony use and does not independently adjudicate the agreement's separate perjury-use term. Ordinary constitutional and evidentiary restrictions and the actual agreement's scope remain independent. The Court decides no universal rule for every advance waiver or every fundamental institutional protection.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit's categorical nonwaiver ruling and resulting new-trial order are reversed, 7–2, for ordinary implementation of the limited waiver. No mandatory validity hearing, factual coercion finding, acquittal, independent retrial direction or sentencing ruling is ordered. If preserved nonconstitutional error is found, the Government must establish whole-record fair assurance that it did not substantially sway the judgment; grave doubt defeats harmlessness. No constitutional error is newly reached, and Olano's forfeited-error burden is not substituted.

## Exact governing text — 29 U.S.C. §626(f)

Official 1994 Code: [govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title29/html/USCODE-1994-title29-chap14-sec626.htm); [local full section and amendment history](../research/chunk3_middle/USCODE-1994-title29-sec626.txt). Section 626(f) was added by Pub. L. 101–433, §201, October 16, 1990, 104 Stat. 983; §202(a) excludes pre-enactment waivers. The 1991 amendment changes subsection (e), not the reproduced waiver text.

(f) Waiver
(1) An individual may not waive any right or claim under this chapter unless the waiver is knowing and voluntary. Except as provided in paragraph (2), a waiver may not be considered knowing and voluntary unless at a minimum—
(A) the waiver is part of an agreement between the individual and the employer that is written in a manner calculated to be understood by such individual, or by the average individual eligible to participate;
(B) the waiver specifically refers to rights or claims arising under this chapter;
(C) the individual does not waive rights or claims that may arise after the date the waiver is executed;
(D) the individual waives rights or claims only in exchange for consideration in addition to anything of value to which the individual already is entitled;
(E) the individual is advised in writing to consult with an attorney prior to executing the agreement;
(F)(i) the individual is given a period of at least 21 days within which to consider the agreement; or
(ii) if a waiver is requested in connection with an exit incentive or other employment termination program offered to a group or class of employees, the individual is given a period of at least 45 days within which to consider the agreement;
(G) the agreement provides that for a period of at least 7 days following the execution of such agreement, the individual may revoke the agreement, and the agreement shall not become effective or enforceable until the revocation period has expired;
(H) if a waiver is requested in connection with an exit incentive or other employment termination program offered to a group or class of employees, the employer (at the commencement of the period specified in subparagraph (F)) informs the individual in writing in a manner calculated to be understood by the average individual eligible to participate, as to—
(i) any class, unit, or group of individuals covered by such program, any eligibility factors for such program, and any time limits applicable to such program; and
(ii) the job titles and ages of all individuals eligible or selected for the program, and the ages of all individuals in the same job classification or organizational unit who are not eligible or selected for the program.
(2) A waiver in settlement of a charge filed with the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, or an action filed in court by the individual or the individual's representative, alleging age discrimination of a kind prohibited under section 623 or 633a of this title may not be considered knowing and voluntary unless at a minimum—
(A) subparagraphs (A) through (E) of paragraph (1) have been met; and
(B) the individual is given a reasonable period of time within which to consider the settlement agreement.
(3) In any dispute that may arise over whether any of the requirements, conditions, and circumstances set forth in subparagraph (A), (B), (C), (D), (E), (F), (G), or (H) of paragraph (1), or subparagraph (A) or (B) of paragraph (2), have been met, the party asserting the validity of a waiver shall have the burden of proving in a court of competent jurisdiction that a waiver was knowing and voluntary pursuant to paragraph (1) or (2).
(4) No waiver agreement may affect the Commission's rights and responsibilities to enforce this chapter. No waiver may be used to justify interfering with the protected right of an employee to file a charge or participate in an investigation or proceeding conducted by the Commission.
