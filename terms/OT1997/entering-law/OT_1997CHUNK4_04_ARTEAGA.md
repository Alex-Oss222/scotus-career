#### Zatko v. California, 502 U.S. 16 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-5052, 91-5111, 91-5166, 91-5167, 91-5244, 91-5246, 91-5307, 91-5331, 91-5332, 91-5401, 91-5416, 91-5476, 91-5583, 91-5594, 91-5692, 91-5730, and 91-5732.  
**Decided:** November 4, 1991.

##### Whether extreme repetitive frivolous filing permits denial of pending Rule 39.8 fee-waiver motions

**Holding:** When the Court is satisfied that pending submissions are frivolous and the applicants' repeated frivolous filings constitute extreme abuse of its process, Rule 39.8 permits denial of in-forma-pauperis status, meaning permission to file without paying fees. Denial may require payment of the docketing fee and compliance with ordinary petition form rules by a stated deadline; it does not decide petition merits or impose a broader prospective filing ban.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; five of the eight participating Justices.

**Material application:** The present submissions were found frivolous; the applicants' extraordinary filing histories supported the finding of continuing abuse.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling is discretionary. It adopts no numerical threshold, does not make earlier denials alone conclusive, and decides no underlying claim or constitutional entitlement to unrestricted free filing.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The seventeen pending fee-waiver motions are denied, with an opportunity to pay docketing fees and submit petitions in the required form by November 25, 1991. Ordinary screening remains available upon compliance.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** In re McDonald and In re Sindram are applied as authority for tailored protection of judicial process; neither is extended to a categorical prospective ban.

#### Demos v. Storrie, 507 U.S. 290 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-6846.

**Decided:** March 8, 1993.

##### The official-interference allegation is not defeated by filing history

**Holding:** Rule 39.8 permits denial of leave to file without prepayment only upon a finding that the particular petition is frivolous or malicious; a documented pattern of prior unsuccessful filings does not substitute for that finding. Demos's allegation that official interference prevented a timely appeal of the underlying prefiling order is a particular, non-conclusory claim that clears the nonfrivolousness threshold on this record, so the Court grants leave without deciding that the allegation warrants certiorari or would succeed on the merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices; all nine participating Justices adopt this filing-specific application and its limits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zatko v. California, 502 U.S. 16 (November 4, 1991): applied as the existing discretionary, particular-submission rule; its extreme-abuse finding does not establish the frivolousness of a different current petition.
- Martin v. District of Columbia Court of Appeals, No. 92-5584 (November 2, 1992): applied; its filing-specific frivolousness standard controls this later fee motion.
- In re Demos, 500 U.S. 16 (1991): distinguished; its narrower, earlier extraordinary-writ restriction addressed different filings and does not itself resolve this civil-certiorari fee question.
- Denton v. Hernandez, 504 U.S. 25 (May 4, 1992): its distinction between an arguable claim and a clearly baseless allegation informs this limited assessment without importing its statutory screening rules wholesale into Rule 39.

##### No prospective filing restriction on this record

**Holding:** The Court declines to impose a prospective fee-and-form condition on Demos's unidentified future civil-certiorari petitions because the established record does not show that individualized treatment of particular filings is inadequate. This case-specific refusal decides neither that a prior warning is invariably required nor that prospective restrictions are categorically unlawful.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices; all nine participate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- In re McDonald, 489 U.S. 180 (1989): preserved as authority for targeted protection against extraordinary-writ abuse; no extension to all future civil-certiorari petitions is made here.
- In re Sindram, 498 U.S. 177 (1991): its extraordinary-writ setting is distinguished; its existing force is unchanged.
- Martin v. District of Columbia Court of Appeals (November 2, 1992): applied; the same unanimous decline to impose a prospective restriction controls here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No finding establishes official interference, excuse of the unappealed default or merit in the underlying §1983 action. Existing extraordinary-writ restrictions are distinct; no universal warning requirement or numerical filing threshold is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Grant the fee motion and decline prospective restrictions. Denial of certiorari in the ordinary course supplies no merits precedent.

### Factual frivolousness and review of fee-screening dismissals

**Governing question:** Dismissal as factually frivolous under then-effective 28 U.S.C. §1915(d), and appellate review of that screening determination.

**Current rule:** Under former §1915(d), where that provision governs, a court may dismiss a claim proceeding without prepayment when its factual allegations are clearly baseless—irrational or wholly incredible—even without judicially noticeable contrary facts. The initial assessment must favor the plaintiff; mere improbability, extraordinary circumstances or ordinary disbelief is insufficient, and screening cannot decide genuine factual disputes. Review is for abuse of discretion, with legal errors independently correctable. The reviewing court must address relevant circumstances, including pro se status, resolution of genuine factual disputes, legal errors, adequacy of reasons, prejudice and whether more specific pleading could cure the defect. Such dismissal is not a merits adjudication barring a paid complaint with the same allegations; its possible effect on later fee-screening decisions makes prejudice and curability material.

**Authority by component:**
- Irrational-or-wholly-incredible threshold and plaintiff-favorable limit: Denton v. Hernandez, May 4, 1992, “Factual frivolousness under section 1915(d).”
- Review, relevant circumstances and nonmerits character: the same record, “Appellate review and the consequences of dismissal.” O'Connor for Stone, White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas controls both propositions; the dissenters' agreement with the factual standard is not a whole-opinion join.

**Present operation effective:** Denton, May 4, 1992, within its former-law scope; PLRA, April 26, 1996, replaces the statutory provision.

**Exceptions and limits:** Corroborating affidavits are not a pleading prerequisite. Rule 12(b)(6) merits pleading remains distinct from factual-frivolity screening.

**Open question:** No absolute notice-and-amendment entitlement or comprehensive preclusion rule for later fee applications is adopted.

**Holdings navigation:** Denton v. Hernandez, 504 U.S. 25 (1992) — Fee-waiver screening and appellate review.

**Effective transition:** PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §804(a)(5), April 26, 1996, replaces the former screening provision with mandatory §1915(e)(2) grounds and separate counsel discretion. See Dismissal and counsel requests under amended §1915. Denton's former-law rule is retained for its surviving temporal and interpretive relevance; no current-term holding settles every new statutory ground, review standard or disputed pending-case application.
