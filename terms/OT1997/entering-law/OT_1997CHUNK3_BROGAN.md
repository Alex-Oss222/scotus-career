## Standards and Tests selection: Knowing unlawfulness in willful currency structuring

### Knowing unlawfulness in willful currency structuring

**Governing question:** What §5322(a)’s willfulness requires for the charged §5324(3) structuring offense.

**Current rule:** The Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowledge of the financial institution’s reporting duty and a purpose to evade it. Reasonable circumstantial inferences may establish knowledge; knowledge of a section number or having read the Code is unnecessary. If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity on that additional knowledge requirement, lenity independently requires this narrower criminal construction; conflicting history or general purpose cannot enlarge the crime beyond sufficiently clear enacted language.

**Authority by component:**

- Knowledge elements and proof: Ratzlaf v. United States — Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful, 1994-01-11. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of nine participants adopt the rule and instructional-error disposition.
- Independent residual-lenity ground: Ratzlaf v. United States — Residual ambiguity independently requires the narrower criminal construction, 1994-01-11. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; the same six Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding.

**Present operation effective:** Ratzlaf v. United States, 1994-01-11.

**Exceptions and limits:** The §5322(a) exception for §5315 and its regulations is separate from §5324(3). This is no universal ignorance-of-law defense, legal-proceeds requirement or civil-forfeiture rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful.

## Standards and Tests selection: Jury determination of criminal materiality

### Jury determination of criminal materiality

**Governing question:** Allocation of materiality when it is an element of the charged offense.

**Current rule:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to decide materiality beyond a reasonable doubt when materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense. The judge states the statutory meaning and legal standard; describing its application as a mixed question cannot remove an element from the jury.

**Authority by component:**

- The jury decides application of the materiality element: The jury decides application of the materiality element, 1995-06-19. Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-19; the component authorities identified above.

**Open question:** The decision does not make materiality an element of every §1001 clause or establish a universal structural-error rule for its withdrawal from the jury.

**Holdings navigation:** The jury decides application of the materiality element.

## Standards and Tests selection: False statements and influencing purpose under §1014

### False statements and influencing purpose under §1014

**Governing question:** Elements of the false-statement or willful-overvaluation offense in the applicable version of 18 U.S.C. §1014.

**Current rule:** Section 1014 adds no objective materiality element requiring a natural tendency or capacity to influence. The Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt knowingly making a false statement or report, or willfully overvaluing land, property or security, together with the purpose of influencing a statutorily enumerated institution in a covered transaction. The transaction must concern an application, advance, discount, purchase, purchase agreement, repurchase agreement, commitment or loan; a change or extension through renewal, deferment or otherwise; or acceptance, release or substitution of its security.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Wells — Section 1014 does not add an objective materiality element, 1997-02-26; Thomas Part II with all except Stevens (eight) supplies the element construction

**Present operation effective:** Wells, 1997-02-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** Institutional coverage, including FDIC-insured status where applicable, is not established merely because the transaction is financial. Jury determination of actual offense elements and independently available trial-error objections retain their force; the materiality construction alone restores neither a conviction nor a sentence.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Wells (1997-02-26) — Section 1014 does not add an objective materiality element.

## Standards and Tests selection: Criminal fair warning under §242

### Criminal fair warning under §242

**Governing question:** Whether pre-existing law gave adequate warning that an official's charged conduct was constitutionally unlawful under 18 U.S.C. §242.

**Current rule:** The statute alone or as authoritatively construed must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; novel or unforeseeable criminal applications are forbidden. Relevant pre-existing law may include lower-court authority, without a Supreme-Court-only requirement, automatic sufficiency of a single lower decision or fixed numerical consensus. An obviously covered application needs no factually matching precedent, but abstract invocation of liberty or bodily security does not settle an actually unresolved application. Criminal fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Lanier — Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted, 1997-03-31; Stone-Zsela for all nine supplies conduct-time notice and the limit on reliance on bad purpose
- United States v. Lanier — Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition supplies the permissible legal landscape
- United States v. Lanier — Obvious applications need no factual twin, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition rejects a factual-twin requirement while preserving unsettled boundaries
- United States v. Lanier — The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition identifies the shared clarity protection without merging criminal liability

**Present operation effective:** Lanier, 1997-03-31.

**Exceptions and limits:** Willful bad purpose cannot cure an unknowable constitutional prohibition. The Government must still establish constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness and every other element under the criminal burden. Due-process rights and direct official assaults outside custody are not categorically excluded, but no particular count's merits or notice is predetermined.

**Related current rules:** Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review retains its civil review, timing and preservation mechanics.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Lanier (1997-03-31) — Criminal Procedure / Criminal civil-rights liability and fair warning.

## Verbatim selected Holdings case: Hubbard v. United States

Source: [state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md](../../../state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md), exact case heading below.

#### Hubbard v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-172.
**Decided:** May 15, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Antecedent questions of governing law (The court-entity question is properly before the Court).

##### A federal court is not an included department or agency under this statute

**Holding:** Under the offense-date §§6 and 1001, a federal court is neither an enumerated executive department nor an agency within the statute's contextual meaning; the department definition's branch-wide contextual alternative does not extend this statute to the court, and the distinct agency definition does not do so either. Counts V–VII therefore lack the asserted court nexus, without deciding whether another Judicial Branch entity could qualify as an agency or whether an independently established nexus to a different included institution would support a different prosecution.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part II, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rowland v. California Men's Colony, decided January 12, 1993: applied for an enacted definition operating subject to a genuine contextual qualification; its association-eligibility holding supplies no categorical result under a different statute.
- United States v. Rodgers, 466 U.S. 475: preserved; jurisdiction includes the authorized functions of an included agency. Footnote 4 reserved the judicial-function question and did not decide these filings or eliminate the entity requirement.
- United States v. Bramblett, 348 U.S. 503: its incompatible all-branch construction is expressly overruled in Part III, not avoided as merely an unnecessary judicial passage.
- Brown v. Gardner, decided December 12, 1994: consistent with enforcing enacted conditions without adding a category the statute does not establish.
- Heintz v. Jenkins, decided April 18, 1995: distinguished in statutory setting; rejection of an added FDCPA litigation exemption does not resolve §1001's entity definition or supply an exception here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The department contextual alternative and the distinct agency qualification remain real; the decision classifies neither every Judicial Branch body nor every trustee. It creates no independent judicial-function or private-litigation exception, right to lie, or additional institutional nexus. Other enacted offenses retain their own scope.

##### Bramblett's incompatible all-branch construction is overruled

**Holding:** Bramblett's construction of department in §1001 to include the executive, legislative, and judicial branches is overruled, including the rationale supporting its legislative-branch result. The decision does not adjudicate the independent House Disbursing Office agency or Treasury-nexus theories Bramblett left open, classify every other judicial entity, or alter §287's separate false-claims coverage.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part III, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices. No extended stare decisis rationale commands a majority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bramblett, 348 U.S. 503: expressly overruled as to its incompatible all-branch department construction, including the rationale supporting its legislative result; no new ruling resolves the independent agency or Treasury theories it declined to decide.
- United States v. Rodgers, 466 U.S. 475: retained at its authorized-function jurisdiction holding and express reservation of the judicial-function issue; its citation of Bramblett does not preserve the displaced entity construction.
- Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission, decided December 16, 1991: its retained FELA construction and specialized reliance setting remain intact; the separate writings explain its significance without creating a new majority stare decisis framework here.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, decided January 18, 1995: its retention of Southland and its actual contractual setting remain intact; disagreement about this precedent does not displace that holding.

##### Reverse only the affirmance of Counts V–VII and remand to vacate them

**Holding:** The Sixth Circuit's affirmance of Counts V, VI, and VII is reversed, and the case is remanded for lawful proceedings to vacate those §1001 convictions on the asserted court-nexus ground. Counts I–IV and VIII–X remain undisturbed by this review; no exact prison, probation, supervised-release, fine, immediate-release, general resentencing, retrial, or new-prosecution command follows, and any ordinary implementation requires lawful proceedings and the actual criminal judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bramblett, 348 U.S. 503: no longer supports these court-based §1001 convictions; the seven mandate votes differ in the broader extent of their precedent treatment as stated in Part III and Stone-Zsela's concurrence.
- United States v. Rodgers, 466 U.S. 475: its jurisdiction holding does not supply a new entity nexus or an independent basis to affirm these convictions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The affirmance of Counts V–VII is reversed and remanded for lawful vacatur of those §1001 convictions. Counts I–IV and VIII–X remain undisturbed. No exact sentence, immediate release, general resentencing, retrial or new prosecution is ordered; implementation requires the actual criminal judgment and lawful proceedings.

## Verbatim selected Holdings case: United States v. Wells

Source: [state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md](../../../state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md), exact case heading below.

#### United States v. Wells

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1228.
**Decided:** February 26, 1997.

For the separate questions in United States v. Wells, No. 95-1228 decided February 26, 1997, see Federal Courts — Preservation and questions passed upon below (The Court may review the statutory question actually decided below).

##### Section 1014 does not add an objective materiality element

**Holding:** Section 1014 does not require an additional finding that a statement had a natural tendency or objective capacity to influence the institution's decision. The Government must still prove beyond a reasonable doubt the applicable conduct—knowingly making a false statement or report, or willfully overvaluing land, property or security—and the purpose of influencing an institution enumerated by the statute in a covered transaction.

The covered transactions are an application, advance, discount, purchase, purchase agreement, repurchase agreement, commitment or loan; a change or extension of one through renewal, deferment or otherwise; or acceptance, release or substitution of its security. The institutional requirement includes the FDIC-insured institutions relevant here; it is not displaced by calling a transaction financial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices. Stevens dissents from this rule and vacatur.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purpose to influence is distinct from objective materiality and remains an element alongside the applicable knowing-false-statement or willful-overvaluation conduct and covered institution and transaction. Prosecutorial discretion, likely consequential lies and sentencing do not replace proof. No general materiality ruling for §1001 or every fraud statute follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The two-conviction appellate judgment is vacated 8–1. Part III's limits command all nine votes: acquittals cannot be reopened; remaining indictment, instruction, evidence, conspiracy and sentence grounds require their own availability and merits determinations. Neither a new trial, restored conviction or sentence nor harmlessness follows automatically.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kay rejects actual-influence defenses within its predecessor setting; Williams preserves its conclusion that the checks were not factual assertions. Gaudin requires juries to decide actual offense elements but creates none. Roy distinguishes permissible error review from an established harmlessness result and does not automatically govern this different direct appeal.

## Verbatim selected Holdings case: Ratzlaf v. United States, 510 U.S. 135

Source: [state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md](../../../state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md), exact case heading below.

#### Ratzlaf v. United States, 510 U.S. 135

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1196.

**Decided:** 1994-01-11.

##### Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful

**Holding:** For the charged offense under 31 U.S.C. §§5322(a) and 5324(3), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowing the financial institution's reporting duty and acting for the purpose of evading it. Knowledge may be established through reasonable circumstantial inferences; the prosecution need not prove knowledge of a section number or that the defendant read the Code.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of nine participants adopt the rule and instructional-error disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Spies v. United States, 317 U.S. 492 (1943): applied for contextual interpretation of willfulness and permissible circumstantial proof.
- Cheek v. United States, 498 U.S. 192 (1991): its recognition of a statutory known-duty requirement informs this distinct scheme; its tax rule is not made universal.
- Liparota v. United States, 471 U.S. 419 (1985): applied for context-sensitive knowledge requirements where apparently lawful conduct may otherwise be swept into a crime.
- Potter v. United States, 155 U.S. 438 (1894): the criminal statute's separate willfulness term receives operative effect rather than disappearing into other elements.

**Independent alternative holding:** If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity about whether §5322(a) requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful, the rule of lenity independently requires that narrower construction of this charged criminal offense. Legislative purpose and conflicting legislative history do not justify dispensing with the additional knowledge requirement when the enacted criminal language does not clearly do so.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; the same six Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hughey v. United States, 495 U.S. 411 (1990): applied to resolve residual criminal statutory ambiguity in favor of the accused.
- Crandon v. United States, 494 U.S. 152 (1990): applied to prevent policy or legislative materials from enlarging a crime beyond sufficiently clear text.
- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336 (1971): applied for fair warning and Congress's responsibility to define criminal liability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The bank's reporting duty and the customer's structuring prohibition are distinct. Section 5322(a)'s exception for §5315 and its regulations remains an exception to the penalty provision, not to §5324(3). No universal ignorance-of-law defense, requirement that proceeds be lawful or civil-forfeiture rule is announced.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed Ninth Circuit judgment for both Waldemar and Loretta Ratzlaf is reversed and remanded. Waldemar's conspiracy, four structuring and interstate-travel counts and Loretta's conspiracy and interstate-travel counts require count-specific implementation of the erroneous instruction; no count is declared automatically invalid. Hunt's convictions are outside review. No acquittal or unconditional discharge is ordered.

## Verbatim selected Holdings case: United States v. Lanier

Source: [state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md](../../../state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md), exact case heading below.

#### United States v. Lanier

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1717.
**Decided:** March 31, 1997.

##### What law must supply fair warning under 18 U.S.C. §242

**Holding:** Section 242, read with authoritative constructions, must have made the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably apparent when the defendant acted. Neither a later decision nor willful bad purpose can supply missing conduct-time notice, and novel or unforeseeable criminal enlargement is forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Moral reprehensibility and state criminality do not replace a definite federal right and notice. Vagueness, lenity, and the bar on unexpected retrospective enlargement retain related but distinct functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* supplies definite-right and separate willfulness requirements; courts construe the criminal statute rather than create common-law offenses.

##### Whether notice requires a Supreme Court decision on fundamentally similar facts

**Holding:** Fair warning may come from relevant pre-existing lower-court authority and from an obviously covered application of an established rule without a factual twin. Neither a Supreme-Court-only requirement nor a fundamentally-similar-facts prerequisite is lawful; conversely, an abstract liberty or bodily-security proposition does not settle an uncertain application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** One lower-court decision or a fixed numerical consensus is not automatically sufficient. Actual conflicts, authority, particular context, and express reservations matter. No count's constitutional basis, notice, personal responsibility, or official nexus is resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Elder* supplies examination of all relevant existing authority. *Anderson* requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not repeated factual identity. *Pinder* and *Uhlrig* preserve unsettled-application and attribution limits; later holdings cannot supply earlier notice.

##### Whether the civil clarity standard substitutes for criminal elements

**Holding:** Criminal fair warning under §242 and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: pre-existing law must make the constitutional unlawfulness apparent. The common threshold does not displace proof of deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the criminal burden of proof, or any other statutory element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Civil immunity analysis cannot itself convict, and judicial office alone does not prove that every act occurred under color of law. No categorically stricter criminal clarity standard is adopted.

##### Whether due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody are categorically excluded

**Holding:** Appellate review may not exclude due-process rights from §242 categorically or treat the victim's lack of custody as automatic immunity for an official's own assault. Where a specific constitutional provision covers the claim, that provision governs; the Fourth and Eighth Amendments do not exhaust every potentially covered abusive official act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the en banc judgment and remand for count-specific consideration under the proper conduct-time notice rule and all remaining properly presented issues. The mandate does not reinstate convictions or sentence, order acquittal or retrial, or require a new evidentiary hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal constitutional-assault formula or factual assault finding is made. Constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation, omitted protection, and supervisory attribution remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* and *Price* preserve §242's due-process reach. *DeShaney*'s private-violence rule does not immunize direct official wrongdoing. *Graham* supplies the specific-provision rule. *Collins*, *Doe v. Taylor*, *Pinder*, *Uhlrig*, and *Nabozny* retain their distinct deprivation and attribution boundaries, without supplying notice retrospectively.

## Exact offense-date statute — 18 U.S.C. §1001

The statement occurred October 4, 1993. The applicable 1988-edition text is reproduced in the [sanitized official source-facts handoff](../freeze/OT_1997CHUNK3_SOURCE_FACTS_BROGAN.md):

> Whoever, in any matter within the jurisdiction of any department or agency of the United States knowingly and willfully falsifies, conceals or covers up by any trick, scheme, or device a material fact, or makes any false, fictitious or fraudulent statements or representations, or makes or uses any false writing or document knowing the same to contain any false, fictitious or fraudulent statement or entry, shall be fined not more than $10,000 or imprisoned not more than five years, or both.

This complete text also appears in Bryson, 396 U.S.64,65 n.1 (1969), independently checked in the official predivergence reporter. No sentencing issue is resolved here.

## Surviving predivergence source — Bryson

**Bryson v. United States, 396 U.S.64,68–73 (1969)** was checked in full, including its footnotes and Douglas/Black dissent. The Court upheld the §1001 conviction for a deliberately false non-Communist affidavit furnished to obtain NLRB services; the asserted invalidity of the affidavit requirement supplied no defense to the distinct deceit offense. It stated that lawful methods of challenging governmental questions do not include knowingly and willfully lying, while retaining the offense's actual elements. Its context was a submitted affidavit under an express statutory scheme, not a holding specifically adjudicating a bare oral exculpatory denial during a criminal investigation. The dissent's institutional-scope position does not override the majority. Bryson's citation to Bramblett cannot restore the all-branch construction later displaced by the public Hubbard holding.

### December 2, 1997 — Salinas v. United States

[Public Projection](../records/salinas_v_united_states_merits_1997-12-02.md#public-projection). Thomas's unanimous opinion holds on §666 that a bribe need not demonstrably affect federal funds. Actual agency, corrupt intent, acceptance of value to influence or reward, covered entity business or transactions worth at least $5,000, and qualifying federal benefits exceeding $10,000 in the relevant one-year period remain necessary. Section 666(c)'s bona fide compensation and ordinary-course expense exception remains operative. The $5,000 threshold measures covered business or transactions, not automatically the bribe, watches, or truck. Benefits status is assumed for this review; every federal receipt is not declared a benefit. No precise annual amount, intangible-benefit coverage, or reopened valuation is adjudicated.

On §1962(d), the same opinion requires a knowing agreement to further an undertaking that, if carried out, satisfies the substantive enterprise offense; personal commission or personal agreement to commit two predicates is unnecessary. The unlawful object must encompass substantive elements, including Reves's direction requirement, but each conspirator need not necessarily direct the enterprise personally. Mere association, knowledge, friendship, or employment is insufficient. Wells distinguishes actual statutory predicates from added conditions, Ratzlaf preserves contextual construction and residual lenity, and Shabani informs the agreement inquiry only within its scope; no universal conspiracy code follows. Reves remains the substantive rule and had reserved conspiracy.

The personal-two-act instruction objection fails, but the entire charge, an independent personal-direction instruction, general evidentiary sufficiency, harmlessness, general aiding-and-abetting liability, and any general overt-act rule are not decided. The substantive §1962(c) acquittal remains intact. Affirmance of the two bribery convictions and conspiracy conviction enlarges no conviction and directs no new trial, special remand, or independent sentence review.
