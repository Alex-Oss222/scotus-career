## Establishment Clause law effective June 23, 1997 — Agostini v. Felton

The [Agostini decision](../../OT1996/records/Agostini_v_Felton_merits_1997-06-23.md#public-projection) governs the present operation of the religion authorities excerpted below. Stone's Opinion of the Court has the full joins of O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent.

Agostini v. Felton, June 23, 1997, retains Lemon as the governing Establishment Clause test and modifies its operation. Purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry, not freestanding prongs or an alternative inquiry. A specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls conduct it resolves; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; an otherwise legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives the attribution-and-function inquiry. Governmental organization of worship is prohibited. Agostini leaves unpresented ceremonial applications to their own facts and preserves distinct legislative-prayer, private-expression and accommodation doctrines; it creates no universal exception for an adult audience.

Aguilar's categorical prohibition of the described onsite public remedial-instruction service is overruled. Ball's corresponding rationale is displaced insofar as religious-school location, presumed indoctrination or incidental institutional benefit supplies the constitutional defect without prohibited governmental function; Ball's completed judgment is not reopened. Ordinary secular supervision alone establishes no religious sponsorship, devotional financing or improper institutional control. The decision supplies a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5) and requires tailored prospective relief, not permission to disregard an operative injunction.

Rufo and Rodriguez de Quijas retain their relief and lower-court precedent rules. Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette and Chabad retain their actual holdings, as do Witters and Mueller; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Earlier excerpts below state the holdings of their own dates. Their references to Lemon's independent prongs or Aguilar and Ball remaining intact describe those earlier decisions, not the test's present operation. Lee and Ingebretsen's official-worship protections survive within the specific-prohibition route; private expression and secular civic acknowledgment remain distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt

### Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt

**Governing question:** When evidentiary overlap, conspiracy or completed criminal contempt bars a successive prosecution.

**Current rule:** Evidentiary use of an uncharged crime does not itself place the defendant in jeopardy for that crime. A conspiracy and its substantive objects remain distinct offenses; prosecuting the substantive crime does not bar prosecution of the agreement merely because acts help prove both. Grady's essential-conduct rule remains operative and does not displace the established agreement/substantive-offense distinction or become a same-evidence or same-transaction rule. Ordinary evidentiary restrictions retain their separate force. Beyond Blockburger, Grady bars a later prosecution when establishing an essential element requires proof of conduct constituting an offense already prosecuted; evidence reuse or a common episode alone does not suffice. Completed nonsummary criminal contempt places the accused in criminal jeopardy despite private initiation or a protective purpose. When contempt necessarily prosecutes a substantive offense forbidden by the order, the Government cannot prosecute that same offense again. Coercive civil sanctions, summary courtroom contempt and separate sovereigns remain outside Dixon. Foster II-V may proceed under its case-specific judgment, but no controlling rationale governs those counts: the Blockburger-only/overruling and special judicial-order rationales are incompatible, not logical subsets. Grady survives without an invented general contempt exception.

- Nonsummary punitive contempt triggers double-jeopardy protection: United States v. Dixon — Nonsummary punitive contempt triggers double-jeopardy protection, June 28, 1993. White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). ## Standards and Tests selection: A punitive drug tax after criminal punishment

### A punitive drug tax after criminal punishment

**Governing question:** When an ostensibly civil drug assessment is a second criminal punishment.

**Current rule:** An extraordinary drug assessment operates as punishment when its combined features make it part of the criminal sanction: liability depends on illegal possession and arrest, collection follows criminal enforcement and is subordinate to fines and forfeitures, and the assessment taxes confiscated contraband no longer possessed at exceptional rates. On the same-offense premise, the State cannot impose that punitive assessment after completed criminal punishment. The combination controls; neither a high rate, unlawful subject matter nor a revenue label alone resolves the issue, and no universal cost ceiling or mathematical ratio is established.

**Authority by component:**

- Combined punitive operation and successive-punishment consequence: Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch — The surviving drug assessment is a second punishment, 1994-06-06. Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the combined-operation rule and affirm disallowance as applied.

**Present operation effective:** Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch, 1994-06-06.

**Open question:** Reverse sequencing, different sovereigns and independently administered confidential taxes are not decided. The rule does not abolish civil taxation of illegal activity.

**Holdings navigation:** The surviving drug assessment is a second punishment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Civil forfeiture and criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy

### Civil forfeiture and criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy

**Governing question:** Classification of forfeiture under §§881(a)(6), 881(a)(7) and 981(a)(1)(A) for successive-punishment purposes.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress intended civil proceedings. Civil intent can be overcome only by the clearest proof that the scheme is so punitive in purpose or effect as to be criminal. Neither a civil label nor proceeding against property supplies an irrebuttable exemption. Deterrence, burden on an owner, connection to crime and owner defenses alone do not establish the demanding contrary showing. Forfeiture’s nonpunitive functions include removing facilitating property, discouraging unlawful use and recovering actual illicit proceeds; it is not universally limited to reimbursing Government costs under Halper’s method for fixed personal civil penalties. Excessive Fines coverage does not itself establish Double Jeopardy criminal punishment; Austin’s distinct protection remains.

## Standards and Tests selection: Punitive purpose or effect in nominally civil commitment

### Punitive purpose or effect in nominally civil commitment

**Governing question:** Whether legislatively civil commitment constitutes punishment for Ex Post Facto purposes.

**Current rule:** First determine the legislature's intended civil or criminal character. Civil intent may be overcome only by the clearest proof that purpose or effect is punitive. ## Standards and Tests selection: Scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702

### Scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702

**Governing question:** What instructions govern scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702?

**Current rule:** Frye's mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite does not govern scientific expert evidence under the federal Rules. Under original Rules 104(a) and 702, the proponent must establish by a preponderance a preliminary foundation of scientifically reliable knowledge fitting the disputed factual question and assisting the factfinder. The judge examines principles, methodology and valid application flexibly. Relevant nonexclusive considerations include testability and testing, peer review and publication, meaningful error information and operational standards, and acceptance; none is an invariant prerequisite. Acceptance alone does not establish admissibility, and its absence alone does not require exclusion. Admissibility does not determine causation or sufficiency. No later Rule text, universal technical-expertise rule or appellate-review standard is adopted.

## Standards and Tests selection: Guidelines departure classification and appellate review

### Guidelines departure classification and appellate review

**Governing question:** Permissibility and appellate review of a departure from the Guidelines range.

**Current rule:** Departure decisions receive abuse-of-discretion review: legal interpretation and permission are independently reviewed; historical findings are protected unless clearly erroneous; supported case-specific judgments of unusualness and degree receive substantial deference. Under §3553(b), the range governs unless a circumstance inadequately considered in kind or degree by the Commission should produce a different sentence, assessed from Guidelines, policy statements and official commentary. Forbidden factors cannot support departure. Encouraged factors may support it if the applicable guideline has not accounted for them. Discouraged factors, and encouraged factors already accounted for, require exceptional degree or another supported distinguishing feature; when the guideline and adjustments account for the factor, §5K2.0 requires a degree substantially exceeding the ordinary case. For an unmentioned factor, examine the relevant guideline’s structure and theory and the Guidelines as a whole; such departures should be highly infrequent. Rarity alone does not suffice, and no compulsory classification sequence is imposed. Sentencing purposes remain relevant without creating extra categorical prohibitions. Section 4A1.3 forbids departure below category I’s lower range for a first offender on exceptionally low recidivism risk. Extraordinary prison vulnerability or an unusually significant burden from successive state/federal proceedings may be considered on supported findings under the unmentioned-factor inquiry; police status, notoriety or lawful successive prosecution alone creates no entitlement or prescribed reduction.

**Authority by component:**

- Permissible supported vulnerability and successive-proceeding burdens: III-C. The supported extraordinary vulnerability may be considered; III-D. The significant burden of these successive proceedings may be considered, same date; Kennedy with Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas (five).

**Present operation effective:** Koon and Powell, 1996-06-13.

**Related current rules:** “Review of Guidelines departures resting on invalid grounds” supplies the unchanged Williams burden to establish the same actual sentence without invalid factors and the independent reasonable-extent requirement. “Authoritative interpretive Guidelines commentary” governs commentary within its statutory and textual limits.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

### Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

**Governing question:** Appellate review of ultimate reasonable suspicion and probable cause after a nonwarrant search.

**Current rule:** Appellate courts independently decide whether established circumstances, objectively considered in their totality, supply reasonable suspicion for the stop and probable cause for the nonwarrant search. Ultimate sufficiency needs no clear-error finding. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review. Grounded contextual inferences of resident judges and experienced local officers receive genuine due weight, without controlling ultimate constitutional sufficiency. Experience does not establish an unproved observation, and no police-credibility presumption, numerical weight or intermediate standard follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Covered removal grounds and the bar on appellate remand review

### Covered removal grounds and the bar on appellate remand review

**Governing question:** When §§1447(c)–(d) bar review, including a removal also invoking bankruptcy jurisdiction.

**Current rule:** A remand to the originating state court for a timely raised removal-procedure defect or lack of subject-matter jurisdiction is not reviewable on appeal or otherwise under §1447(d), even when removal also invokes §1452; a procedural-remand motion must be made within thirty days after removal. The then-applicable exception permits review of a case removed pursuant to §1443. Courts may identify the stated ground but may not review a covered ground's correctness merely because it is alleged erroneous. A Burford abstention remand is outside this bar, but absence of the bar does not itself create an appellate route. Section 1452(b) coexists with the ordinary bar rather than displacing it.

## Standards and Tests selection: Ancillary enforcement and a new judgment debtor

### Ancillary enforcement and a new judgment debtor

**Governing question:** Whether an existing federal judgment supplies jurisdiction over a later independent action imposing its debt on another person.

**Current rule:** The court's power to identify, preserve and execute against its judgment debtor's assets does not confer jurisdiction over a later independent action imposing that debtor's full obligation on another person. Such an action needs an independent jurisdictional basis; factual connection, remedial need, Rule 69 and a supplemental-jurisdiction claim lacking an original federal anchor cannot supply it. The decree-effectuation branch of ancillary jurisdiction remains distinct from factual interdependence and from creation of a new obligation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Official graduation worship and independent religious pressure

### Official graduation worship and independent religious pressure

**Governing question:** Clergy-led prayer organized by public secondary-school officials for the official graduation program.

**Current rule:** Officials violate the Establishment Clause by selecting clergy, requesting invocations and benedictions, guiding their religious content and incorporating the worship into the official ceremony. Nonsectarian wording and optional attendance do not cure that sponsorship; no separate punishment or quantified-pressure showing is required for this ground. Independently, school-directed clergy prayer is forbidden when the school's control and the occasion's importance place objecting adolescents under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the ceremony. Optional attendance and standing or remaining silent do not necessarily remove that pressure; mere offense is insufficient. The official-sponsorship prohibition also applies when a school organizes, adopts, directs or religiously supervises worship as part of its own event but uses student initiation or delivery or a statutory disclaimer. Those features alone do not make the worship private, and the sponsorship ground remains independent of quantified pressure. Separately, actual pressure to participate in school-directed worship in compulsory or comparably pressured settings violates the Establishment Clause; offense at a genuinely private viewpoint alone does not suffice.

**Authority by component:**

- Independently sufficient pressure ground: *Lee*, “The school-directed ceremony also imposes impermissible religious pressure”; Kennedy's concurrence joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. ## Standards and Tests selection: Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

### Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

**Governing question:** Viewpoint-neutral access for private religious expression and the distinct establishment inquiry in the forums and benefits actually opened.

**Current rule:** When a public school admits private family-life and child-rearing discussion to an after-hours forum, it cannot exclude an otherwise eligible presentation solely for its religious viewpoint, even assuming a nonpublic forum. Neutral restrictions outside the forum's actual subjects remain possible. Equal access to voluntary, publicly accessible after-hours films without school sponsorship has a secular forum purpose, does not primarily advance religion as governmental activity and causes no excessive entanglement. That establishment holding concerns private speech, not an official devotional program; it supplies no replacement for the inherited establishment framework or universal ruling on worship restrictions. A State may not categorically exclude an otherwise eligible private unattended religious display from a forum actually opened to comparable expression merely because its message is religious or its location adjoins or lies within a capitol. Pinette’s access holding includes the proposal’s offered private-attribution safeguard; it establishes neither adequacy of a later sign nor a comprehensive establishment test. Chabad separately holds that an interior capitol location does not establish religion when actual context makes the expression clearly private. Actual selection, financing, control, adoption, preference or obscured private sponsorship can alter that conclusion; no forum label or unspecified sign decides attribution. In a generally available, privately edited student-publication program, a religious editorial viewpoint cannot exclude an otherwise eligible publication from printing support. Its secular expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent attribution and controlled vendor payments together permit that benefit; neutrality alone does not authorize devotional financing or governmental worship.

## Standards and Tests selection: Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review. An earlier pleading-stage appeal does not by itself exhaust review of a later summary-judgment order: a separable legal immunity question must independently satisfy the collateral-order conditions. This permits neither repetition of an unchanged ruling nor relitigation of matters resolved by the first mandate. Having to defend a different surviving claim does not itself defeat otherwise authorized claim-specific review, but neither supplies missing authorization nor makes that other claim reviewable.

**Governing question:** Proof, discovery and causation when unconstitutional motive is an element of a claim and qualified immunity is asserted.

## Standards and Tests selection: Criminal fair warning under §242

### Criminal fair warning under §242

**Governing question:** Whether pre-existing law gave adequate warning that an official's charged conduct was constitutionally unlawful under 18 U.S.C. §242.

Relevant pre-existing law may include lower-court authority, without a Supreme-Court-only requirement, automatic sufficiency of a single lower decision or fixed numerical consensus. ## Standards and Tests selection: Prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements

### Prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements

**Governing question:** What instructions govern prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements?

**Current rule:** Ordinary investigative work to identify a perpetrator before probable cause has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for alleged fabrication; later courtroom use does not retrospectively transform it into advocacy. Genuine evaluation and preparation in an advocate's role remain protected; neither all pre-indictment nor all post-indictment acts share one classification. Statements to the press announcing an indictment also lack absolute prosecutorial immunity merely because they concern prosecution or cause injury through subsequent proceedings. Qualified immunity, an actionable constitutional deprivation and other liability predicates remain separate.

Preparing and filing charging papers or a warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions and presenting the State's legal case receive absolute immunity when they are advocacy functions. Bad purpose or alleged false evidence does not remove that protection; the Court adopts no general dishonesty exception. A prosecutor's distinct personal oath supplying facts to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant is instead a complaining-witness evidentiary function without absolute prosecutorial immunity. That classification does not require proof of falsity and leaves ordinary courtroom testimony immunity intact. Denial of this immunity does not establish liability: constitutional deprivation, culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury and available personal defenses remain separate. A judge's warrant does not categorically sever causation where material deception procured it, while a truthful corrected account may still establish probable cause.

#### United States v. Felix, 503 U.S. 378 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1599.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

##### Whether prior evidentiary use places a defendant in jeopardy for distinct uncharged substantive offenses

**Holding:** The use of evidence of an uncharged crime in one trial does not itself place the defendant in jeopardy for that crime. The later Oklahoma substantive charges concern different acts from the Missouri attempt and are not barred merely because evidence of those acts helped prove intent in Missouri.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of White's opinion of the Court, supported by all nine Justices; all nine Justices adopt the distinct-prosecution rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Missouri attempt concerned different acts from the earlier Oklahoma offenses. Ordinary objections to the admissibility of particular evidence remain.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment reversing substantive counts 2–6 and conspiracy count 1 is reversed, restoring the prosecution’s judgments against the double-jeopardy objection, subject to preserved unresolved appellate issues. The separate disposition of travel counts 9–10 is undisturbed; no automatic new trial or evidentiary ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Dowling v. United States, 493 U.S. 342 — applied: evidentiary use does not itself constitute prosecution for the described offense.
- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508 — applied and confined to essential-conduct analysis; its express rejection of a same-evidence test remains operative.

##### Whether overlapping substantive acts bar prosecution of the distinct conspiracy agreement

**Holding:** A conspiracy and its substantive objects are distinct offenses; prosecution of the substantive crime does not bar prosecution of the agreement merely because the same acts help prove both. The overlapping Missouri conduct therefore does not bar this Oklahoma conspiracy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of White's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt this rationale. Stevens and Blackmun concur only in the conspiracy judgment on a narrower ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Grady remains in force; no same-transaction requirement or new evidentiary exemption is adopted. A materially different single-incident successive prosecution remains for its own case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment reversing substantive counts 2–6 and conspiracy count 1 is reversed, restoring the prosecution’s judgments against the double-jeopardy objection, subject to preserved unresolved appellate issues. The separate disposition of travel counts 9–10 is undisturbed; no automatic new trial or evidentiary ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bayer, 331 U.S. 532 — applied: the agreement is distinct from its execution.
- Pinkerton v. United States, 328 U.S. 640 — applied: substantive crime and conspiracy remain separately punishable offenses.
- Iannelli v. United States, 420 U.S. 770 — applied: conspiracy’s independent character survives overlapping proof.
- Garrett v. United States, 471 U.S. 773 — applied by analogy to the treatment of multilayered criminal conduct.

**Holding:** For forfeiture under sections 881(a)(6), 881(a)(7) and 981(a)(1)(A), the court first determines whether Congress intended civil proceedings; that intent can be overcome only by the clearest proof that the scheme is so punitive in purpose or effect as to be criminal. ##### How are departures under the 1992 Guidelines reviewed and classified?

**Holding:** Departure decisions receive abuse-of-discretion review, independently correcting legal error and unsupported grounds. When a guideline and adjustments account for a factor, §5K2.0 requires degree substantially exceeding the ordinary. Unmentioned factors require attention to the relevant guideline's structure and theory and the Guidelines as a whole, with unguided departures highly infrequent. Rarity alone does not justify departure. Just punishment, deterrence, public protection, training, medical care and correctional treatment remain relevant without additional categorical bans beyond the Commission's scheme. **Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela agrees in this component's judgment only. Application and extent are sustained, 9–0.

**Decided:** June 3, 1996

##### Does section 1447(d) bar review of a remand entered on Burford abstention grounds?

**Holding:** Section 1447(d), read with section 1447(c), bars appellate review of remands actually resting on the covered subject-matter-jurisdiction grounds or timely raised removal-procedure defects; a procedural-defect motion must be made within thirty days. A remand on Burford abstention grounds is outside that bar, although escaping the bar does not itself establish an affirmative appellate channel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts I-II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; unanimous controlling rationale for all questions in this entry.

**Controlling explanation:** The reviewing court identifies the remand's actual stated ground; it does not reconsider the correctness of a covered ground as a way around the bar. Burford abstention does not assert defective federal jurisdiction or removal procedure. The statutory section 1443 exception does not govern, and the reserved Cohill question about discretionary remand of state-law claims is not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Thermtron's covered-ground construction remains controlling; Rice and Gravitt retain the bar on review of covered remands even when error is alleged. Cohill's distinct remand-review question remains open.

##### Is this conclusive surrender of the federal forum reviewable under section 1291?

**Holding:** A remand that conclusively surrenders the entire federal action and leaves no further federal adjudicative work is reviewable under section 1291 because it effectively puts the litigant out of federal court. Independently, this order satisfies the collateral-order doctrine: it conclusively determines an important public question of federal jurisdiction, separate from the merits, that would be effectively unreviewable after a later federal judgment that will never occur.

**Controlling explanation:** The first, independently sufficient ground applies Cone's practical treatment of complete federal-forum surrender; it does not pretend that this remand is a conventional Catlin final merits judgment. The second, independently sufficient ground satisfies Cohen and Digital Equipment because it concerns the public allocation of judicial responsibility and forecloses later review. Litigation expense, a private settlement right or a bare wish to avoid trial would not meet that test. Each ground independently supports jurisdiction; the order's particular conclusive effect matters.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Not every stay, remand or interlocutory order is appealable. Section 1292(b) certification is unnecessary on these grounds; that distinct route still requires a written finding of a controlling legal question, substantial ground for disagreement and material advancement of termination, a timely application within ten days, appellate permission, and no automatic stay.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Cone supplies the independently sufficient effectively-out-of-court route for complete surrender of the federal forum.
- Catlin retains its conventional final-decision rule, which is not stretched into a fictional final merits disposition here.
- Cohen supplies the independent collateral-order requirements.
- Digital Equipment requires public importance and effective unreviewability; a private right to avoid litigation is insufficient.
- Thermtron's mandamus-only language is disavowed insofar as it conflicts with the later Cone application. Its construction of section 1447(d)'s covered grounds remains intact.

##### May Burford abstention support outright remand of independent legal damages claims?

**Holding:** Burford abstention permits outright dismissal or remand only when the relief sought is equitable or otherwise discretionary; an independent legal damages claim cannot be surrendered on that basis. A properly justified stay may preserve federal jurisdiction over such a claim, but this decision neither requires nor automatically authorizes one.

**Controlling explanation:** These independent contract claims seek legal damages. State liquidation interests, setoff, participation in liquidation, procedural merger of law and equity, and the presence of a genuine declaratory claim do not make all the requested relief discretionary. Wilton's discretion over an action seeking only declaratory relief does not convert this mixed action into such a case. The actual state asset orders permit proceedings elsewhere; the Court does not assume that every insurance action disposes of assets under exclusive state custody.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether Burford's predicates otherwise exist. They require timely and adequate state review and either difficult state-law questions bearing on public policy transcending the case's result, or disruption of coherent state policy on a matter of substantial public concern; those alternatives are not cumulative, and complexity or potential conflict alone is insufficient. The Court rejects neither all abstention-related stays in damages actions nor the distinct doctrines involving state-tax comity, Thibodaux, Colorado River or foreign forum non conveniens.

The Court decides no arbitration entitlement or sequencing priority. Any Federal Arbitration Act section 3 application still requires a written covered agreement and referable issue, the court's satisfaction, an application by a party, a stay according to the agreement, and absence of applicant default. Ordinary evenhanded formation, fraud, duress, unconscionability, assent and preservation rules remain; coverage, scope, delegation, nonsignatory enforcement and McCarran-Ferguson issues are undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit's judgment vacating the abstention remand is affirmed, 9-0. Federal proceedings resume without an ordered stay, arbitration, damages award, distribution of assets or new evidentiary hearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wilton's discretion over solely declaratory relief does not authorize surrender of an independent legal damages action.
