## Exact relevant rules and qualifications

Selected actual rules and the complete new review-method explanation are copied verbatim below. Their Fourth Amendment and criminal-remedy subjects remain intact.

### Limited-sharing rule

**Holding and operative rule:** Limited visibility of bedroom activity to cotenants using shared residential space is not, by that fact alone, general publication. An incompletely closed blind cannot establish absence of subjective privacy independently of the actual vantage from which the challenged police observation was obtained.

**Authority:** Part II-A has O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. The Court does not adopt Part II-B's four-Justice application as a majority search determination.

### New home-entry review allocation

**Holding and operative rule:** Appellate courts independently determine whether the established circumstances supplied a constitutionally sufficient exigency for warrantless home entry. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences retain clear-error protection, and grounded contextual experience receives genuine due weight without controlling the ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Authority:** Part III-A has O'Connor, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia withholds this extension; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Ornelas allocates ultimate reasonable suspicion for a stop and probable cause for a warrantless search to independent appellate judgment while protecting historical findings. Its functional reasons extend to this distinct home-entry question: consistent constitutional boundaries require comparison of established circumstances, while the trial court retains its superior role in deciding what happened and whom to credit. Grounded local experience matters, but cannot manufacture an observation or make the ultimate conclusion presumptively correct.

This is an extension of Ornelas, not a statement that it had already resolved every mixed question. Richards separately adopted independent review of ultimate announcement sufficiency. The two inquiries remain distinct, and neither allocation resolves the underlying facts or requires suppression, a new hearing or plea withdrawal. Gates's substantial-basis review of a magistrate's warrant decision remains different. The Court adds no numerical weighting, intermediate standard or police-credibility presumption.

### Conditional remedy rule

**Holding and operative rule:** If an antecedent intrusion was unlawful, a later warrant is an independent source only when both the decision to seek it and its issuance are genuinely independent of the illegality. Consent must satisfy its own authority, scope and voluntariness requirements and any applicable taint analysis; neither a warrant's arrival nor a rights explanation and signature automatically validates an earlier intrusion or decides exclusion.

**Authority:** Part IV has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela additionally agrees in returning suppression, consent, independence, attenuation and later-warrant consequences to the lower courts without final suppression, but joins no additional remedial rationale.

### No combined application holding

**Judgment and authority:** Five Justices vacate the reviewed suppression determination and dependent appellate affirmances and remand. O'Connor, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would require a renewed actual-vantage application while reserving search and entry legality. Stone-Zsela finds the observation a search and the planned exigency insufficient, leaving remedies for remand. The four affirming Justices find the actual shared view and specific pre-force exceptions sufficient.

The directly supported propositions above control their subjects. The two judgment-supporting application rationales are not treated as a single Marks holding: the plurality leaves open a lawful observation and entry that the concurrence rejects on this record, while the concurrence adds a police-created-exigency ground the plurality does not adopt. Neither full rationale is a logical subset supplying a common answer. The binding judgment therefore requires lawful further proceedings under the actual majority rules, without imposing either noncontrolling application as the Court's final answer.
