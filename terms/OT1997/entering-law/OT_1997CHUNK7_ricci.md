## Standards and Tests selection: Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop

### Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop

**Governing question:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause despite another investigative purpose.

**Current rule:** Objective probable cause of a traffic violation supports an ordinary initial stop notwithstanding an unrelated investigative aim. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce that violation absent that aim, or on compliance with customary assignment and enforcement policies. Manner, scope and duration remain independently constrained: a materially greater detention or search requires independent grounds. This rule does not remove purpose-specific conditions from inventory and administrative exceptions; inventories retain standardized criteria or established routine and genuine inventory purposes rather than investigatory rummaging. Purposeful racial selection remains independently prohibited by Equal Protection.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective initial-stop rule: Objective traffic probable cause supports this ordinary initial stop; independent execution and discrimination limits: Policy, execution and discrimination limits, 1996-06-10; Thomas’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Whren, 1996-06-10.

**Open question:** No discovery entitlement, immunity, suppression consequence or damages remedy for discriminatory selection is decided. Armstrong’s federal-charging threshold is not transferred to stop claims; traffic probable cause does not authorize every later intrusion.

**Holdings navigation:** Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806.

## Standards and Tests selection: Possessory seizures and the claimant's own Fourth Amendment interest

### Possessory seizures and the claimant's own Fourth Amendment interest

**Governing question:** What instructions govern possessory seizures and the claimant's own Fourth Amendment interest?

**Current rule:** Meaningful government-attributable interference with possession of a home is a Fourth Amendment seizure without a search, privacy or liberty invasion, or criminal investigative purpose. A competing due-process characterization does not displace the specific seizure claim; coverage does not establish unreasonableness or §1983 liability. A suppression claimant must show interference with his or her own protected property interest in a seizure or reasonable expectation of privacy in a search. Conspiracy membership neither creates an exception to that personal-interest requirement nor destroys an actual protected interest. Attribution, immunity and the remaining merits must be separately established.

**Authority by component:**

- A seizure of a home protects possession independently of privacy: Soldal v. Cook County — A seizure of a home protects possession independently of privacy, 1992-12-08. White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; nine Justices adopt the same rule.
- A due-process characterization does not displace the specific seizure claim: Soldal v. Cook County — A due-process characterization does not displace the specific seizure claim, 1992-12-08. White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; unanimous holding.
- Suppression depends on the defendant’s own protected interest: United States v. Padilla — Suppression depends on the defendant’s own protected interest, 1993-05-03. Per curiam Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-05-03; Soldal v. Cook County; United States v. Padilla, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Soldal v. Cook County, 506 U.S. 56 (1992); United States v. Padilla, 508 U.S. 77 (1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Criminal fair warning under §242

### Criminal fair warning under §242

**Governing question:** Whether pre-existing law gave adequate warning that an official's charged conduct was constitutionally unlawful under 18 U.S.C. §242.

**Current rule:** The statute alone or as authoritatively construed must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; novel or unforeseeable criminal applications are forbidden. Relevant pre-existing law may include lower-court authority, without a Supreme-Court-only requirement, automatic sufficiency of a single lower decision or fixed numerical consensus. An obviously covered application needs no factually matching precedent, but abstract invocation of liberty or bodily security does not settle an actually unresolved application. Criminal fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Lanier — Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted, 1997-03-31; Stone-Zsela for all nine supplies conduct-time notice and the limit on reliance on bad purpose
- United States v. Lanier — Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition supplies the permissible legal landscape
- United States v. Lanier — Obvious applications need no factual twin, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition rejects a factual-twin requirement while preserving unsettled boundaries
- United States v. Lanier — The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition identifies the shared clarity protection without merging criminal liability

**Present operation effective:** Lanier, 1997-03-31.

**Exceptions and limits:** Willful bad purpose cannot cure an unknowable constitutional prohibition. The Government must still establish constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness and every other element under the criminal burden. Due-process rights and direct official assaults outside custody are not categorically excluded, but no particular count's merits or notice is predetermined.

**Related current rules:** Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review retains its civil review, timing and preservation mechanics.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Lanier (1997-03-31) — Criminal Procedure / Criminal civil-rights liability and fair warning.


## Verbatim authority from state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md

#### Soldal v. Cook County, 506 U.S. 56 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6516.

**Decided:** December 8, 1992.

##### A seizure of a home protects possession independently of privacy

**Holding:** Meaningful government-attributable interference with possession of a home is a seizure under the Fourth Amendment even without a search, invasion of privacy or liberty, or criminal investigation. The family’s alleged forcible dispossession meets that threshold, but coverage alone does not establish unreasonableness or every element of §1983 liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; nine Justices adopt the same rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Jacobsen, 466 U.S. 109 — applied; possession and privacy remain independently protected interests.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696 — applied; seizure scrutiny does not require an accompanying search.
- Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321 — applied; plain-view seizure remains subject to the Fourth Amendment.
- Adickes v. S. H. Kress & Co., 398 U.S. 144 — applied to the summary-judgment joint-action inference, without a final conspiracy finding.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 — preserved; seizure coverage does not establish municipal liability.
- Wyatt v. Cole, May 18, 1992 — preserved; attribution does not settle immunity or a private merits defense.

##### A due-process characterization does not displace the specific seizure claim

**Holding:** A court may not reject an otherwise applicable Fourth Amendment claim merely because the same conduct also concerns a deprivation of property under the Due Process Clause. Each applicable constitutional protection must be examined on its own terms; this disposition does not adjudicate the family’s separate substantive-due-process claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386 — applied; the specific Fourth Amendment source governs its covered conduct and is not displaced by a dominant-character theory.
- Parratt v. Taylor, 451 U.S. 527 — distinguished; postdeprivation procedural remedies do not answer the distinct seizure claim.
- Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517 — confined to its prison setting for the relevant coverage proposition; no rule excluding possession of a home follows.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Private action alone is insufficient. Ultimate reasonableness, municipal policy, causation, immunity, damages and the separate substantive-due-process claim remain undecided. The holding addresses a home, without deciding coverage of every property category.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse adverse summary judgment and remand the seizure claim; no final finding of unreasonableness or award of damages.


## Verbatim authority from state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md

#### Wilson v. Arkansas

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-5707.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Announcement and reasonable home entry

**Holding:** Common-law announcement of identity and authority forms part of Fourth Amendment reasonableness when officers enter a dwelling. Supported countervailing circumstances, including danger, futility, escape or threatened destruction of evidence, may justify unannounced entry; actual justification and remedy remain for the state court.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The exact quantum of suspicion required for each exception and whether every violation requires suppression remain undecided. The federal entry statutes' every detail is not constitutionalized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miller v. United States, 357 U.S. 301: supplies common-law announcement background; federal statutory particulars are not all constitutionalized.
- Sabbath v. United States, 391 U.S. 585: supplies federal statutory entry analysis without deciding this constitutional application.
- Ker v. California, 374 U.S. 23: applies state-entry reasonableness with circumstance-dependent excuses.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for the state court to evaluate the actual entry, record-supported excuses and remedy. No violation, automatic suppression or final vacation of the conviction is established.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Richards v. Wisconsin, April 28, 1997, resolves the reserved quantum for the identified warrant-execution exceptions as particularized reasonable suspicion and rejects the categorical felony-drug exception; constitutional exclusion after a violation remains open.


## Verbatim authority from state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md

#### Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-5841.
**Decided:** June 10, 1996.

##### Investigative motive and customary enforcement in an ordinary traffic stop

**Holding:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause of a traffic violation does not become unreasonable under the Fourth Amendment because officers also investigate an unrelated offense. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce the violation absent that other aim. Independent constitutional limits on manner, scope, and duration remain; detention or search materially exceeding what the traffic violation reasonably justifies requires independent grounds.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous, including the application and qualifications.

**Material application:** The accepted observations of waiting unusually long at a stop sign, failing to signal, and unreasonable speed supply traffic probable cause. Petitioners' proposed objective “would-have” test receives the same rejection as a subjective-motive prerequisite: variable local priorities, uniforms, assignments, markings, and enforcement customs do not change constitutional authority for the known violation. A separate balance of ordinary traffic-safety gains against plainclothes-intervention anxiety supplies no additional requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Inventory and administrative-inspection authority retain their genuine-purpose and standardized or other source-specific constraints; motive is not irrelevant to every constitutional question. General Order 303.1 compliance, its immediate-safety-threat exception for otherwise unauthorized plainclothes or unmarked enforcement, and its extreme-circumstances oral-warning exception are not adjudicated. Neither speed nor missing execution details establish an exception or constitutional execution finding. The later search is not independently presented.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Scott* applies objective assessment of justified action; *Robinson*, *Gustafson*, and *Villamonte-Marquez* retain their distinct lawful-arrest, search, and customs-inspection predicates without creating new power. *Prouse* distinguishes suspicionless discretionary stops; *Martinez-Fuerte* and *Brignoni-Ponce* do not require a further balance for this ordinary probable-cause stop. *Bertine* and *Wells* preserve inventory constraints and forbid investigatory ruses; *Burger* preserves purpose-limited administrative inspection. *Abel* and *Bannister*'s regular-procedure and no-pretext observations do not establish the proposed customary-enforcement condition. *Wilson* preserves reasonable-execution review with its supported danger, futility, escape, and evidence-destruction qualifications, leaving suspicion quantum and automatic suppression open. *Garner*, *Welsh*, and *Winston* retain their distinct deadly-force, home-entry, and bodily-intrusion limits.

##### Review of the accepted predicate and separation of discriminatory selection

**Holding:** The accepted traffic observations permit resolution under *Ornelas*: historical facts, credibility, and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review, with genuine due weight to grounded contextual inferences; the legal standard and ultimate constitutional sufficiency properly at issue receive independent judgment. No new factual inquiry is required to decide this legal-rule challenge.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** Due weight means substantive consideration of supported trial and local-officer context, not a numerical weight, officer-credibility presumption, or intermediate standard insulating constitutional conclusions. Direct review imports no habeas presumption or clear-and-convincing burden and supplies no supposed antecedent drug probable cause.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purposeful racial selection remains independently forbidden by equal protection; traffic probable cause does not establish nondiscrimination. No discrimination finding, police-stop discovery entitlement, immunity rule, suppression consequence, or damages remedy is decided, and assumed adequacy of another remedy is not a ground for judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of suppression on the initial-stop ground, 9–0. The completed vacatur of lesser-included §841 convictions and §860 resentencing, including nonreimposition of Whren's fine, remain undisturbed. Later sentencing appeals are not resolved. No new trial, hearing, release, sentencing proceeding, or determination of every admissibility ground follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Ornelas*'s review allocation is applied unchanged. *Kimberlin* permits circumstantial proof when motive is an underlying element without adding one here. *Armstrong* remains confined to federal charging and discovery; its requirements and unresolved discrimination remedy do not become police-stop rules.


## Verbatim authority from state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md

#### United States v. Lanier

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1717.
**Decided:** March 31, 1997.

##### What law must supply fair warning under 18 U.S.C. §242

**Holding:** Section 242, read with authoritative constructions, must have made the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably apparent when the defendant acted. Neither a later decision nor willful bad purpose can supply missing conduct-time notice, and novel or unforeseeable criminal enlargement is forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Moral reprehensibility and state criminality do not replace a definite federal right and notice. Vagueness, lenity, and the bar on unexpected retrospective enlargement retain related but distinct functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* supplies definite-right and separate willfulness requirements; courts construe the criminal statute rather than create common-law offenses.

##### Whether notice requires a Supreme Court decision on fundamentally similar facts

**Holding:** Fair warning may come from relevant pre-existing lower-court authority and from an obviously covered application of an established rule without a factual twin. Neither a Supreme-Court-only requirement nor a fundamentally-similar-facts prerequisite is lawful; conversely, an abstract liberty or bodily-security proposition does not settle an uncertain application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** One lower-court decision or a fixed numerical consensus is not automatically sufficient. Actual conflicts, authority, particular context, and express reservations matter. No count's constitutional basis, notice, personal responsibility, or official nexus is resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Elder* supplies examination of all relevant existing authority. *Anderson* requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not repeated factual identity. *Pinder* and *Uhlrig* preserve unsettled-application and attribution limits; later holdings cannot supply earlier notice.

##### Whether the civil clarity standard substitutes for criminal elements

**Holding:** Criminal fair warning under §242 and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: pre-existing law must make the constitutional unlawfulness apparent. The common threshold does not displace proof of deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the criminal burden of proof, or any other statutory element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Civil immunity analysis cannot itself convict, and judicial office alone does not prove that every act occurred under color of law. No categorically stricter criminal clarity standard is adopted.

##### Whether due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody are categorically excluded

**Holding:** Appellate review may not exclude due-process rights from §242 categorically or treat the victim's lack of custody as automatic immunity for an official's own assault. Where a specific constitutional provision covers the claim, that provision governs; the Fourth and Eighth Amendments do not exhaust every potentially covered abusive official act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the en banc judgment and remand for count-specific consideration under the proper conduct-time notice rule and all remaining properly presented issues. The mandate does not reinstate convictions or sentence, order acquittal or retrial, or require a new evidentiary hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal constitutional-assault formula or factual assault finding is made. Constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation, omitted protection, and supervisory attribution remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* and *Price* preserve §242's due-process reach. *DeShaney*'s private-violence rule does not immunize direct official wrongdoing. *Graham* supplies the specific-provision rule. *Collins*, *Doe v. Taylor*, *Pinder*, *Uhlrig*, and *Nabozny* retain their distinct deprivation and attribution boundaries, without supplying notice retrospectively.


## Verbatim authority from state/holdings/civil-rights.md

#### Hafer v. Melo, 502 U.S. 21 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-681.  
**Decided:** November 5, 1991.

##### Whether a state officer sued individually is a section 1983 person for acts undertaken through official authority

**Holding:** A state officer sued in an individual capacity is a person under 42 U.S.C. §1983 even when the challenged act was undertaken through official authority, and state sovereign immunity does not independently bar a judgment payable by that officer personally. The plaintiff must still prove the officer's own violation under color of law, and the officer retains applicable personal immunities.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Material application:** The Court of Appeals construed the damages claims as personal claims against Hafer. Her authority to discharge employees supplies the state-law connection without converting the claims into claims against Pennsylvania.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional violations, qualified immunity, damages, and entitlement to reinstatement remain unresolved; no immunity standard changes.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit is affirmed. The individual-capacity claims may proceed; no damages or reinstatement are awarded by this decision.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kentucky v. Graham supplies the distinction between litigation capacities. Will v. Michigan Department of State Police is clarified and preserved for States and official-capacity damages defendants. Scheuer v. Rhodes confirms possible personal liability of executive officers; Monroe v. Pape supplies the under-color-of-law rule for misuse of official power. Ex parte Young's prospective-relief channel is unchanged.


## Verbatim authority from state/holdings/civil-rights.md

#### Ayeni v. Mottola

**Docket or dockets:** Supreme Court No. 94-1202 (Mottola v. Ayeni); Second Circuit Nos. 94-6041(L), 94-6047.
**Decided:** May 27, 1997.

For the present constitutional rule in Ayeni v. Mottola, May 27, 1997, No. 94-1202, see Criminal Procedure — Warrant execution and private observers.

##### The legal immunity appeal uses the assumed conduct and its actual date

**Holding:** On this legal collateral-order appeal, the Court assesses whether the assumed conduct was apparently unlawful under law existing on March 5, 1992; a present violation alone does not defeat immunity, and a factually identical earlier case is not indispensable. The appeal does not permit findings about Mottola's actual role or resolution of disputed liability facts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), Parts I–II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general mandatory merits-first sequence, agency or media damages extension, or §1983 holding is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Anderson protects reasonable mistakes without demanding identical facts; Elder, Pinder and Lanier guide review but supply no retrospective 1992 notice. Davis prevents statutory breach alone from defeating immunity. Johnson and Behrens preserve the legal/factual appellate boundary; FDIC v. Meyer keeps an agency action distinct.

##### Pre-search law clearly forbade the pleaded nonassisting home intrusion

**Holding:** On the pleaded facts, pre-March 5, 1992 law made the officer's gratuitous admission of nonassisting outsiders to expose private home life for an unrelated purpose apparently unlawful. Mottola is therefore not entitled to dismissal on qualified immunity for this claim, but responsibility, causation and compensable injury remain to be proved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Parts III–IV, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm denial of the pleaded immunity defense, 5–4; responsibility, causation and compensable injury require proof. Other force, detention, destruction, pre-warrant-entry and separately treated photograph issues, substantive due process, CBS, unserved agents and the criminal footage proceeding remain outside the decision. No broadcast injury is presumed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bivens, Payton and Andresen supply concrete home and purpose-bound restraints; Matias, Rivera, Clouston and Wright contribute the pre-search documentary, household and assistance limits. Section 3105 confirms the distinction without substituting for constitutional notice. Soldal, Wilson and Bills postdate the search and supply no notice; Harlow–Anderson is preserved.
