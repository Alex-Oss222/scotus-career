## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA interference with prospective welfare-plan rights

### ERISA interference with prospective welfare-plan rights

**Governing question:** Whether welfare benefits outside statutory vesting protection are covered by ERISA §510.

**Current rule:** Section 510 reaches covered discharge or other prohibited conduct undertaken to interfere with attainment of a right the participant may become entitled to under a welfare plan. Nonvesting status is no categorical defense: lawful amendment or termination of plan terms differs from prohibited conduct directed at participants. The claim still requires the applicable participant or beneficiary status, protected plan right, prohibited action, interference purpose, causation and relief authorized through §510's reference to §502.

**Authority by component:**

- Inter-Modal Rail Employees Association v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co. — I. Section 510 includes prospective welfare-plan rights, 1997-05-12; Ginsburg, all nine
- Inter-Modal Rail Employees Association v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co. — II. The categorical dismissal is vacated for the remaining statutory inquiries, 1997-05-12; eight Associates

**Present operation effective:** Inter-Modal, 1997-05-12.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision creates no permanent-benefits or general unfair-dismissal rule. Attainment where the participant is already eligible remains unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Inter-Modal Rail Employees Association v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co. (1997-05-12) — Section 510 includes prospective welfare-plan rights; Inter-Modal Rail Employees Association v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co. (1997-05-12) — The categorical dismissal is vacated for the remaining statutory inquiries.

## Standards and Tests selection: Fiduciary benefit communications and deliberate disloyal deception

### Fiduciary benefit communications and deliberate disloyal deception

**Governing question:** When an employer’s benefit communication is ERISA administration and breaches loyalty.

**Current rule:** Fiduciary responsibility under §3(21)(A) follows the particular exercise of discretionary plan administration, including benefit information communicated in that capacity. Authority over administration, the occasion and linked benefit content may establish that function; general employer status, forecasting or participant perception alone does not. An administrator acting in that capacity violates §404(a)(1) by knowingly and significantly misleading participants about benefit security to induce surrender of existing plan interests for the employer’s benefit. Lawful amendment or termination power does not authorize that deliberate disloyal deception.

**Authority by component:**

- Varity — Parts I–II, functional administration and loyalty breach, 1996-03-19; Souter, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer (six).

**Present operation effective:** Varity, March 19, 1996.

**Open question:** No general duty to volunteer information, answer every inquiry, disclose every forecast or avoid negligent misstatement is decided; not every corporate prediction is fiduciary administration. The distinct retirees’ assignment determination establishes no new substantive assignment rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Varity Corp. v. Howe, 516 U.S. 489.

## Standards and Tests selection: Territorial-water death remedies and the exclusive high-seas field

### Territorial-water death remedies and the exclusive high-seas field

**Governing question:** Whether state or general maritime damages may supplement federal death remedies.

**Current rule:** Moragne’s federal maritime wrongful-death remedy does not itself displace otherwise available state wrongful-death and survival remedies for a nonseafarer killed in territorial waters when no applicable federal statute or controlling uniform maritime rule supplies an exclusive regime. This category excludes Jones Act seamen and pertinent federal maritime-employment classes. Where United States law governs a death beyond a marine league from a United States shore in DOHSA’s geographical field, its pecuniary-loss limit excludes loss of society; dependency cannot restore the category, and general maritime law cannot supplement it.

**Authority by component:**

- Yamaha — Part II, nonseafarer territorial-water supplementation, 1996-01-09; O’Connor for all nine; Moragne’s extension to nonseafarers and inclusion of survival are assumed, not decided.
- Zicherman — DOHSA’s exclusive pecuniary boundary, 1996-01-16; O’Connor for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Zicherman, January 16, 1996, for the high-seas boundary; Yamaha’s territorial-water construction effective January 9, 1996.

**Open question:** These holdings choose no state law, award no category under it and establish no general state-law permission to alter characteristic maritime rights. Other maritime liability standards, high-seas survival/predeath-suffering categories and disputed international choice of law remain unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun, 516 U.S. 199; Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd.; Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd. v. Zicherman, 516 U.S. 217.

## Standards and Tests selection: Domestic compensable loss under the Warsaw Convention

### Domestic compensable loss under the Warsaw Convention

**Governing question:** The source of damage categories and beneficiary rights in a Warsaw Convention action.

**Current rule:** The Convention’s damage language does not supply an independent comprehensive code of compensable loss or beneficiaries. Applicable domestic law supplies those matters subject to the Convention’s conditions and limits. Uniform carrier-liability conditions do not require a judicially created uniform damages code, and lifting a Convention cap does not create a category domestic law excludes.

**Authority by component:**

- Zicherman — Convention and domestic-loss rules, 1996-01-16; O’Connor for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Zicherman, January 16, 1996.

**Open question:** The holding assumes the parties’ accepted use of United States law and supplies no general international conflicts rule or comprehensive decision on treaty exclusivity. “Territorial-water death remedies and the exclusive high-seas field” states the independently governing DOHSA exclusion.

**Holdings navigation:** Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd.; Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd. v. Zicherman, 516 U.S. 217.

#### Inter-Modal Rail Employees Association v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-491.
**Decided:** 1997-05-12.

##### Section 510 includes prospective welfare-plan rights

**Holding:** ERISA §510 protects against a covered discharge or other prohibited action undertaken for the purpose of interfering with attainment of a right a participant may become entitled to under an employee welfare benefit plan, even though the benefit is not subject to ERISA's statutory vesting requirements. Lawful authority to amend or terminate a welfare plan does not itself authorize interference through conduct forbidden by §510.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Plan terms remain amendable; cost reduction alone establishes no interference purpose or permanent benefit entitlement. Whether already eligible employees have attained the relevant right remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Curtiss-Wright preserves lawful amendment and required procedures; Lockheed separates plan design from independent statutory duties. Ingersoll-Rand's pension setting did not already decide welfare coverage.

##### The categorical dismissal is vacated for the remaining statutory inquiries

**Holding:** The Ninth Circuit's welfare-interference dismissal is vacated because its categorical nonvesting ground is erroneous, and the case is remanded for consideration of the properly remaining grounds. Plaintiffs must establish the applicable participant or beneficiary status, relevant plan right, prohibited action, interference purpose, causation and remedy authorized through §510's reference to §502; this decision establishes no violation or automatic recovery.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela would reverse and direct reinstatement of the welfare claims instead.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Eligibility, purpose, causation, mixed motives, business justification, defendants and authorized relief require adjudication. No new business-necessity test or nonemployer-conspiracy holding is adopted. The distinct pension, Railroad Retirement Act, standing, class, FELA, fee-sanction and waived limitations components are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Varity's fiduciary and individual-equity holdings remain separate from §510. Mertens preserves the equitable category limit when §502(a)(3) supplies relief; coverage does not itself authorize compensatory damages.

#### Varity Corp. v. Howe, 516 U.S. 489

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1471.
**Decided:** March 19, 1996.

##### The integrated benefit-security presentation was fiduciary administration

**Holding:** ERISA §3(21)(A) attaches fiduciary responsibility to the particular exercise of discretionary plan administration, including plan information communicated in that capacity. Varity exercised that function when it used administrative authority, benefit comparisons and linked assurances of benefit security to induce the class's transfer; employer status, corporate forecasting or participant perception alone would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Material application:** Current-benefit descriptions and deliberately linked security assurances formed one administrative presentation. Warnings that future conditions depended on MCC's success and that changes might occur did not displace the unchallenged trial findings that the combined presentation was knowingly misleading.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Every employment statement or corporate-solvency prediction is not thereby fiduciary. Corporate restructuring and plan-amendment powers remain distinct from administration.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** John Hancock's particular-function inquiry is applied while keeping status, breach and remedy separate. Curtiss-Wright preserves sponsor amendment authority but distinguishes retained power from authorized exercise; it creates no immunity for this administrative act.

##### Deliberate disloyal deception violates §404

**Holding:** An administrator acting as a fiduciary violates §404(a)(1)'s loyalty duty by knowingly and significantly misleading participants about benefit security to induce surrender of existing plan interests for the employer's benefit. Varity's found deception and the class's reliance meet that rule despite reserved lawful amendment or termination powers.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by the same six Justices as Part I.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This intentional-deception holding decides no general duty to volunteer information, answer every inquiry, disclose every forecast or avoid negligent misstatement. It supplies no substantive rule for the ten retirees' different assignment claim and no permanent-benefits promise.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Firestone's trust-law guidance explains loyalty within ERISA's statutory function, without replacing that threshold. Curtiss-Wright's amendment rule supplies no permission to deceive participants into surrendering present membership.

##### Section 502(a)(3) permits appropriate individual equitable redress for fiduciary breach

**Holding:** Sections 409 and 502(a)(2) do not impose a categorical plan-only bar on §502(a)(3)'s express authorization of appropriate individual equitable relief for a §404 violation. Relief must be traditionally equitable; where another ERISA provision adequately remedies the injury, further equity normally is unnecessary and inappropriate, without an absolute rule governing every alternative pleading.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela's stricter adequate-remedy condition supports the class's judgment but is not the Court's rule.

**Material application:** The class sought restoration after fiduciary deception procured surrender of membership. Rejection of its lifetime-benefits contract theory established neither an adequate §502(a)(1)(B) remedy for that injury nor an automatic bar to restoration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not classify each monetary item as equitable, including replacement-insurance premiums or all unpaid benefits. It neither revives the rejected $7.6 million and $712,332 compensatory awards nor permits general compensation through trust law. Section 510 and estoppel remain unadopted alternatives.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The clarified restoration decree is affirmed. Reinstatement confers only the rights of the M-F plan as it now exists. Past entitlement may be adjusted for intervening events, with relevant additional evidence and a conforming decree. Future lawful changes remain possible subject to applicable law and preserved retaliation or discrimination objections. No new payment amount or immutable lifetime entitlement is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Russell's plan-centered §409 construction is preserved while its distinct §502(a)(3) question is answered. Mertens's equitable-category limit applies; its reserved antecedent action issue is not treated as an earlier action bar. Peacock's statutory-or-plan-violation requirement is met by the proved §404 breach; its later new-debtor jurisdictional holding does not govern this original action. Firestone supplies guidance within enacted limits.

##### The ten retirees' independent assignment-breach ground was not adequately challenged

**Holding:** The categorical plan-only objection does not defeat the ten retirees' individual equitable recovery, and their distinct lower-court assignment-breach determination remains undisturbed because the petition did not sufficiently present a challenge to it. Affirmance decides no new substantive assignment rule, precise monetary classification or categorical unavailability of §502(a)(1)(B) to those retirees.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela provides a sixth judgment vote on the distinct assignment component without a ground.

**Material application:** The ten had retired before the purported assignment and did not consent. Their unauthorized-administration ground did not depend on the class's presentation or voluntary departure; arguments concerning misrepresentation and lifetime contractual benefits did not identify error in that separate ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the clarified decree for this group on the same existing-plan and adjustment terms stated above, without entering a new amount or reviving terminated-employee, punitive, lifetime-contract, settled-disability or pension claims.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Curtiss-Wright does not transform an amendment reservation into proof of an authorized assignment or permanent vesting; Mertens's monetary-category limits remain without a new classification decision.

#### Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun, 516 U.S. 199

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1387.
**Decided:** January 9, 1996.

##### Continued availability of state death and survival remedies

**Holding:** Moragne's federal maritime wrongful-death remedy does not itself displace otherwise available state wrongful-death and survival remedies for a nonseafarer killed in territorial waters where no applicable federal statute or controlling uniform maritime rule makes recovery exclusive. The rule covers persons outside Jones Act seaman and pertinent federal maritime-employment classes; it does not expand remedies in Congress's occupied seaman or high-seas fields.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II and decree, unanimous.

**Material application:** The child killed in Puerto Rico's territorial waters was neither a seaman nor a covered longshore worker. General uniformity concerns do not extend a federal compensation regime beyond its coverage conditions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than decides Moragne's extension to this nonseafarer and inclusion of survival. It selects neither Pennsylvania nor Puerto Rico law and determines no damages category, amount, liability standard, defect, negligence, causation or punitive liability. State labeling does not make substantive damages procedural or authorize alteration of characteristic maritime rights generally.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Third Circuit's rejection of displacement, leaving its partial-summary-judgment dispositions and remand operative; no award is made. The §1292(b) scope holding is under Federal Courts, Yamaha, January 9, 1996, No. 94-1387.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Moragne adds a federal remedy without automatic exclusivity. Miles remains tied to Congress's seaman-remedy choices; Offshore Logistics protects DOHSA's exclusive high-seas field. American Dredging's state-forum practice holding is distinct and does not itself validate substantive state damages.

#### Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd.; Korean Air Lines Co., Ltd. v. Zicherman, 516 U.S. 217

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-1361 and 94-1477.

**Decided:** January 16, 1996.

##### The Convention leaves compensable loss to applicable domestic law

**Holding:** The Warsaw Convention's reference to damage does not supply an independent, comprehensive code identifying every compensable loss or beneficiary. Domestic law supplies those matters, subject to the Convention's own conditions and limits; the parties' accepted use of United States law permits decision here without choosing a general international conflicts rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general international choice-of-law rule or determination of Convention exclusivity in every setting is announced. Removing a Convention liability cap does not create a category of loss excluded by applicable domestic law.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Saks and Floyd are applied for treaty interpretation bounded by the Convention's actual conditions; neither supplies a general damages code.

##### DOHSA excludes loss-of-society damages for both relatives

**Holding:** When United States law governs damages for a death on the high seas beyond a marine league from a United States shore, within the Death on the High Seas Act's geographical field, the Act's restriction to pecuniary loss excludes loss-of-society recovery. Dependency cannot revive that excluded category, and general maritime law cannot supplement the Act to permit it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Other properly preserved damages remain undisturbed. The Court decides no general dependency rule, survival claim, predeath suffering claim, mental-distress claim, beneficiary-standing issue or jury-trial issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm exclusion of the mother's loss-of-society claim; reverse the sister's dependency-based remand. Remand for a conforming damages judgment, without vacating carrier liability or unrelated award components.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Higginbotham and Offshore Logistics govern the statutory exclusion; Miles and Yamaha preserve its field. Gaudet is distinguished by its territorial setting, and American Dredging by its procedural subject.

## Event

Bay Area Laundry and Dry Cleaning Pension Trust Fund v. Ferbar Corp. of California, No. 96-370. Argued November 10, 1997; decided December 15, 1997. Certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 73 F.3d 971, concerning limitations on withdrawal-liability collection.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision. No nonparticipation is established.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

Reversed and remanded, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: none. The Fund may pursue timely later installments under the rules below, but may not recover the first $345.50 installment. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment excluding that first claim. The remand awards no money, directs no full-debt payment, and grants no automatic prospective injunction.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Breyer delivers the opinion of the Court. All eight other Justices join Parts I–II on accrual, installments, acceleration and the bounded remand. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg join Part III on the first-installment exclusion; Stone-Zsela agrees in that component's judgment only. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

**1. Collection accrual requires a payment violation.** Withdrawal creates liability under 29 U.S.C. §1381, but a collection cause does not accrue until the plan has assessed liability, set a schedule and demanded payment, and the employer fails to pay an installment that is due. Section 1451(f) supplies the longer of six years after accrual or three years after the plaintiff acquired or should have acquired actual knowledge of the cause; in fraud or concealment, an action may be brought within six years after discovery of the cause. No fraud or concealment finding is made here. Parts I–II are the unanimous opinion of the Court.

The statutory sequence distinguishes liability from an enforceable delinquency. A limitation cannot begin on this collection claim before the Fund can sue for the missed payment. Knowledge of withdrawal does not itself supply knowledge of an accrued cause, and the discovery provision need not be decisive in every collection case. Milwaukee distinguishes computational dates from actual demand and payment; its calculation holding does not already decide this accrual question. North Star concerns borrowing when Congress has supplied no period, whereas §1451 supplies its own. The Ninth Circuit's March 1985 withdrawal trigger is therefore erroneous.

The plan still must assess and demand payment as soon as practicable under §1399(b)(1). Proper objections to delay remain available on their actual grounds; concern about creditor delay does not authorize substitution of withdrawal-date accrual or a finding that every delayed demand is valid. Scheduled payments commence no later than sixty days after demand notwithstanding review or appeal, as the statute provides. No exhaustion requirement postpones those payments.

**2. Installments accrue separately unless valid acceleration changes maturity.** Each missed installment gives rise to a separate collection claim and its own limitations period. An expired first-payment claim does not extinguish later timely installments; after a legally effective election to accelerate, the period on the accelerated debt runs from that exercise. A later aggregate demand cannot revive installments already barred.

Congress authorized installment obligations and distinguished ordinary delinquency from default and acceleration. Nonpayment default requires failure to cure within sixty days after written notice; an alternative plan-defined default must indicate a substantial likelihood that the employer will be unable to pay. Even a default does not itself establish an election to accelerate. The December 1986 demand, April 1987 cure warning and continued nonpayment must retain their distinct functions. Neither the warning nor delinquency proves an earlier acceleration. Whether the February 1993 complaint's alternative full-debt request itself accomplished acceleration is left open. The lower courts must identify actual demand, due dates and any effective acceleration, without inventing a payment schedule or awarding every requested installment.

**3. The Fund cannot revive its first-payment claim on this petition posture.** Having obtained review on its representation that the conflicting installment rules determined the case and that the single-period rule barred its suit, the Fund may not now recover the first $345.50 installment while obtaining reversal for the later installments. Part III controls through Breyer, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

Other submissions argued that the first payment was timely under the sixty-day provision and the demand's receipt-based terms. Those arguments do not undo the premise on which the Fund presented the controlling conflict for review. Exclusion of the first claim does not decide the unverified demand-receipt date, impose a universal February 1 maturity rule, or establish that every alternative argument forfeits a claim.

## Precedent Treatment

Milwaukee is applied for the distinction between computational assumptions and actual payment, default and acceleration; its installment-calculation rule remains unchanged. Concrete Pipe's neutral adjudication and lawful factual and actuarial rebuttal burdens remain intact; the assessment is not conclusively validated here. North Star's borrowing rule is distinguished because Congress enacted the relevant limitation. Inter-Modal's distinction between substantive plan rights and enforcement predicates is consistent with this decision but supplies no preexisting withdrawal-accrual rule. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Withdrawal-liability collection accrues upon the actual missed due payment after assessment, scheduling and demand. Independently due installments retain separate periods, subject to any valid acceleration and the particular first-claim exclusion here. The as-soon-as-practicable demand duty, interim payment obligation, neutral adjudication, ordinary defenses and statutory fraud/concealment qualification retain their own predicates. No equitable tolling, automatic assessment validity, arbitration outcome or right to every requested remedy is established.
