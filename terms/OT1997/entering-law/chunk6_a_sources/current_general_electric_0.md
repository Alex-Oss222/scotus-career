### December 15, 1997 — General Electric Co. v. Joiner

[Public Projection](../records/general_electric_co_v_joiner_merits_1997-12-15.md#public-projection). Kennedy's Parts I–II are unanimous. Part III has Kennedy, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; Stone-Zsela joins reversal but not its PCB-only application, and Stevens would vacate for appellate reconsideration. Reversal and bounded remand carry eight votes; the complete defense summary judgment is not restored.

Part I requires uniform abuse-of-discretion review of scientific expert admissions and exclusions: legal mistakes remain independently correctable, historical findings receive their proper protection, and permissible case-specific evidentiary judgments receive deference. Consequence for summary judgment creates no asymmetric or particularly stringent exclusion standard. Koon and the task-sensitive reasoning of Pierce and Cooter & Gell inform that allocation without transferring their other subject-specific rules; Ornelas's particular constitutional task remains distinct. Rule 56 protects disputed merits facts without removing antecedent admissibility from the judge.

Part II applies original Rules 104(a) and 702 and Daubert's preponderance foundation of reliable scientific knowledge fitting the actual question. The judge may examine the supported inferential connection to the offered conclusion; credentials, terminology, or a claimed weight-of-evidence method alone do not establish it. This screening is distinct from ultimate causation. A matching study or independent proof from each study is unnecessary: animal work, nonsignificant associations, competing causes, and individually inconclusive materials may contribute to a supported cumulative inference. The offered theory is promotion or acceleration, not necessarily sole causation. No later Rule 702 text, categorical significance requirement, or technical-specialty rule is adopted.

Part III sustains this PCB-only exclusion as permissible, without holding admission forbidden or PCB promotion of lung cancer impossible. Infant-mouse injections and adenomas differ from the adult's lower alleged occupational exposure and small-cell carcinoma; the human studies' nonsignificant excesses, unconnected mineral-oil findings, and mixed exposures bear on fit without creating automatic disqualifications. The experts' combined method and additional materials remain acknowledged; incomplete source reproduction does not prove the absence of an inferential bridge. The unchallenged furan/dioxin exposure ruling survives, and the District Court had not expressly assessed causation admissibility on that assumed exposure. Those questions remain for further proceedings.

Daubert's flexibility and distinction between admissibility and ultimate proof survive; Frye's compulsory general-acceptance prerequisite remains displaced, and Rules 403, 703, 706, and 56 retain separate functions. No mandatory expert assistance, new hearing, trial, admission, damages, collateral reopening, or alternative-theory outcome follows. Full studies, depositions, and the entire record are not reproduced; the reported observations and cumulative method supply no independent medical finding, invented data, or litigant default.
