### Hudson — December 10, 1997

[Public Projection](../records/hudson_v_united_states_merits_1997-12-10.md#public-projection). Stone-Zsela joins Parts I and III and affirmance on the combined actual-operation analysis under Ursery and Hendricks. The sanctions' accountability and protective functions differ from Kurth Ranch and Hendricks; he neither joins the independent Halper accounting ground nor rejects Halper's force. Different operations, excessiveness, and offense identity remain open. Scalia, joined by Thomas, would require successive criminal prosecutions and reject Halper's freestanding civil multiple-punishment method while permitting inquiry whether nominally civil proceedings are actually criminal. That theory lacks a majority and does not displace Halper.
