### December 10, 1997 — Hudson v. United States

[Public Projection](../records/hudson_v_united_states_merits_1997-12-10.md#public-projection). Breyer's Parts I and III command seven votes: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part II commands those six Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join its independent Halper analysis. All nine support affirmance permitting the prosecution to proceed.

Part I applies Ward's civil-intent inquiry and requires the clearest proof to overcome civil classification through the sanctions' supported purpose and actual combined operation. Civil wording cannot immunize actual punishment; deterrence, severity, occupational burden, or criminal overlap is not individually conclusive. Ursery preserves the demanding override and Halper's distinct personal-penalty field; Hendricks requires meaningful operational examination; Mendoza-Martinez supplies indicators without a new exhaustive checklist. The original monetary authorization governs, not increased tiers enacted after the February 1989 notice; good-faith and gravity considerations affect amount without creating a universal willfulness element.

Part II retains and applies Halper's rational remedial relationship, including reasonable approximation. The actual $44,000 assessments bear the requisite relation to the District Court's $72,000 pursuit-cost finding; no additional accounting is required. The initial $200,000 demand is not the final obligation, alleged banking or FDIC losses are not proved government costs, and the agency's failure initially to calculate costs does not establish punishment. Deterrence may coexist with recovery of legitimate pursuit costs. No automatic ratio, below-cost safe harbor, universal compensation ceiling, or statutory-face replacement of Halper follows.

Part III considers the monetary and occupational burdens together. The banking restriction's written-regulatory-authorization exception and protective function in positions of trust distinguish Kurth Ranch's extraordinary arrest-linked contraband tax and Hendricks's prisonlike application; both precedents remain intact. The supported operation supplies no criminal punishment and hence no presented multiple-punishment bar. Materially different schemes or operations remain open. No Blockburger or retained Grady identity determination, attachment rule, Excessive Fines ruling, due-process ruling, settlement waiver, or guilt finding follows; Dixon, Felix, Austin, and the distinct identity and fines rules retain their force. Original administrative instruments, indictment particulars, and underlying cost-hearing materials are not reproduced. The lower opinion's individual-amount discrepancy does not alter its operative $44,000 total, and no additional statutory predicate or waiver is inferred.
