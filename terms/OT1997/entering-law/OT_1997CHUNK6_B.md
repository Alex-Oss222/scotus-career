## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. As of this date the Court has not adopted a general rule governing remote historical movement in every statute.

**Authority by component:**

- Architecture; connection before magnitude; recognition of necessary inclusion; no-insertion rule: United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.
- Direct protection of an interstate instrumentality and enacted vehicle-specific nexus: United States v. Harris, April 27, 1995; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine direct votes
- Direct interference with an interstate service market: American Life League v. Reno, November 13, 1995; Kennedy Part I, unanimous.
- Application of Harris without new doctrine: United States v. Bishop; United States v. Stokes, February 5, 1996; O’Connor Part II, unanimous.
- Anti-evasion/material-impairment rule for the post-1986 machinegun regime: United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996; O’Connor Part I, unanimous.
- Interstate commercial transmission as commerce itself: Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno, April 28, 1997; unanimous commerce holding.
- Legally fixed cross-border child-support obligation: United States v. Mussari; United States v. Schroeder, June 30, 1997; O’Connor, unanimous.

**Present operation effective:** Mussari/Schroeder, June 30, 1997, for the latest application; the controlling architecture dates to Lopez, April 26, 1995, and the anti-evasion/material-impairment branch dates to Hale, February 12, 1996.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:**

1. Define the enacted regulated class.
2. Identify the asserted interstate-commerce connection.
3. Identify the mechanism connecting the class to interstate commerce.
4. Determine whether the mechanism is constitutionally eligible.
5. Only then use aggregation or substantiality to evaluate class-wide magnitude.
6. Give Congress substantial latitude on prediction and regulatory design within the valid power.
7. Test whether the statutory class reasonably fits the asserted mechanism.
8. Confirm that the theory leaves an enforceable constitutional boundary.

**Exceptions and limits:** Noneconomic intrastate conduct is not categorically immune. Traditional State subject matter is not categorically immune. Formal legislative findings are neither universally required nor sufficient by themselves. Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope. Garcia’s rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains intact. Remote movement and generalized social costs independently establish none of the current Commerce grounds. The Court has not yet adopted a general material-interstate-externality doctrine. Mussari/Schroeder directly regulates the private obligor: it commands no State administration and gives no federal power to issue or modify support, custody, parentage or domestic-status orders. The cross-border payment rule adds no flight, travel, voluntary-crossing, bank-wire or evasion-motive element. Not every protester is an interstate enterprise; the service-market ground reaches conduct directed at provision or receipt of the covered services. Hale preserves § 922(o)'s government-related channels and prior-lawful pre-ban stock. No Second Amendment, amended-statute or independent indictment rule is established by these commerce holdings.

**Material development:**

- April 26, 1995 — Lopez: ADDED / CHANGED. Added the controlling decision sequence of regulated class → interstate mechanism → magnitude/aggregation → fit/boundary. Changed the prior substantial-effects law only to the extent necessary to reject aggregation of generalized downstream consequences as a substitute for the missing Commerce connection. Preserved Wickard and Perez within their genuine economic-class settings. Reserved fuller Necessary-and-Proper inclusion questions.
- April 27, 1995 — Harris: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Congress may protect an interstate transportation instrumentality against a direct intrastate forcible taking when the offense requires proof of the particular vehicle's interstate connection. Stone-Zsela's opinion states the Court's instrumentality ground; the Fifth Circuit's aggregate-effects reasoning is rejected. No general remote-movement rule is added.
- November 13, 1995 — American Life League: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Clarified that intentional obstruction directed at provision or receipt of services can be regulated when it directly interferes with an actual interstate service market. This is not downstream crime-cost aggregation.
- February 5, 1996 — Bishop/Stokes: APPLIED ONLY. Applied Harris. No new Commerce rule.
- February 12, 1996 — Hale: ADDED. Added the first operational anti-evasion/material-impairment doctrine. Intrastate possession may be included where excluding it would materially undermine or permit evasion of a valid federal interstate market regime. This development uses the Necessary and Proper Clause together with Commerce authority. Hale does not create a general firearm possession power.
- April 28, 1997 — Pic-A-State: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Clarified that a knowing interstate computer transmission operating as part of a commercial procurement transaction is interstate commerce itself, making aggregate-effects analysis unnecessary.
- June 30, 1997 — Mussari/Schroeder: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Clarified that Congress may protect a legally fixed payment obligation whose statutory relationship is itself cross-border. The different-State relation performs constitutional work; family-policy importance and social costs do not.

## Standards and Tests selection: Lemon test

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method.

**Authority by component:**

- General inquiry and operative qualifications: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function, 1997-06-23. Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.
- Object of the inquiry, evidence role and Holding I limits: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function (controlling explanation and precedent treatment), 1997-06-23; the same five Justices.
- Safeguarded secular instruction: Agostini v. Felton — II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location, 1997-06-23. Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Present operation effective:** Agostini v. Felton, June 23, 1997.

**Object of the inquiry:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Safeguarded secular instruction on religious-school premises:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. An institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone does not excuse devotional instruction or diversion; independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain.

**Former-rule note:** Lemon's independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs ceased to govern as separate or fallback tests on June 23, 1997 (Agostini v. Felton, Holding I); those considerations now serve as evidence within the updated Lemon test. Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises (Agostini v. Felton, Holding II, June 23, 1997); no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed.

**Open question:** Agostini v. Felton decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid, and does not declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller, whose actual holdings remain in force.

**Preserved specific holdings:** Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

**Related current rules:** Denominational selection for general governmental authority; Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits. Each states existing specific holdings that continue to govern their established relationships under this test.

**Holdings navigation:** Agostini v. Felton (1997-06-23) — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function; II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location.

## Standards and Tests selection: FACE conduct predicates and responsibility for civil relief

### FACE conduct predicates and responsibility for civil relief

**Governing question:** The challenged reproductive-service branches of 18 U.S.C. §248 and the grounds and exclusions governing their civil remedies.

**Current rule:** Section 248(a)(1) requires force, threat of force or physical obstruction; intentional injury, intimidation, interference or attempt; and action because a person is or has been obtaining or providing reproductive health services, or intended to intimidate that person, another person or a class from doing so. Section (a)(3)’s service branch separately covers intentional damage, destruction or attempt against facility property because it provides those services. Interference restricts movement; intimidation is reasonable apprehension of bodily harm to oneself or another. Physical obstruction makes ingress or egress impassable, or passage to or from a facility unreasonably difficult or hazardous. Services include medical, surgical, counseling and referral services concerning reproduction, pregnancy or termination, provided in a hospital, clinic, physician’s office or other facility. The inclusive facility definition reaches a hospital, clinic, physician’s office or other reproductive-service facility and its building or structure; the distinct passage terms create no interior-only limit, geographic buffer or general outdoor immunity.

A parent or legal guardian is exempt from every FACE penalty and civil remedy, including private damages and private or governmental injunctions, insofar as the described activity is directed exclusively at that person’s own minor. For a private §248(a)(1) action, the claimant must be involved in providing or seeking to provide, or obtaining or seeking to obtain, services in a reproductive-health facility. For nonexcepted attributable violations, such an eligible claimant may elect $5,000 per violation before final judgment in place of actual compensatory damages. The First Amendment does not categorically require that violation-based statutory sum to equal proved actual loss. Liability must nevertheless rest on the defendant’s legally attributable violation; shared belief, membership, independent advocacy or another’s wrong alone is insufficient, and protected persuasion reducing demand is not itself the unlawful injury. Protected First Amendment expression remains outside liability under §248(d)(1); §248(d)(2) separately creates no new outside-facility speech/religion remedy and preserves existing ones. Other state and local remedies are not preempted.

**Authority by component:**

- Defined conduct and protected-expression boundaries: The defined access restrictions survive the facial speech challenge and The presented substantial-overbreadth challenge fails, 1995-11-13; Kennedy Parts II and III-A, all nine.
- Permissive elected damages and complete own-minor exclusion: Elected statutory damages are not categorically barred by the First Amendment, 1995-11-13; Kennedy Part IV-A, all eight Associates adopt the rationale, Stone-Zsela agrees in disposition.
- Personal attribution and protected persuasion: A damages defendant must be responsible for the violation, 1995-11-13; Kennedy Part IV-B, all nine.

**Present operation effective:** American Life League, 1995-11-13, for the controlling constructions and constitutional remedy limits; FACE applies to conduct on or after May 26, 1994.

**Exceptions and limits:** The review does not adjudicate worship branches, a particular criminal sentence, violation count, amount-specific reasonableness, entitlement to an award, punitive award or general threat mental state. Civil damages attribution is not replaced by an injunction’s actual-concert rule. The own-minor exception creates no immunity under other law or constitutional parental veto. Constitutional facial validity does not settle discriminatory enforcement or RFRA.

**Related current rules:** “RFRA review of prospective enforcement” requires the distinct person-and-application inquiry; no automatic exemption or categorical third-party-harm exception follows.

**Holdings navigation:** American Life League v. Reno.

## Standards and Tests selection: Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

### Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

**Governing question:** How a court tailors a content-neutral injunction regulating speech-related conduct.

**Current rule:** A content-neutral injunction must burden no more speech than necessary to serve the significant governmental interest supporting the remedy. This inquiry is more demanding than the ordinary content-neutral legislative time, place and manner rule, without becoming a universal least-restrictive-means test. Each restriction needs a remedial connection to the established interest and conduct. Public access, medical quiet and residential privacy may support relief, but neither those interests nor prior misconduct justifies territorial restrictions or advance-permission conditions broader than necessary. Removing an invalid restriction also removes its operative predicate; it cannot be restored by relabeling the same conduct under another clause. Within an otherwise valid fixed access zone, an exception permitting nonthreatening initial counseling by up to two people per approached person or group may end when the recipient objectively communicates, verbally or nonverbally, that counseling is unwanted. The counselor must end that close encounter and leave the fixed zone, while retaining lawful advocacy outside it; this supplies neither universal silence nor a mobile personal radius.

O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Stevens's individualized equitable account and the strict-restraint dissent are not joins in this standard.
- Removal of invalidated predicates and lawful remedial discretion: Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc — Relief removes invalidated predicates; Remand permits lawful relief without requiring a personal zone, 1994-06-30. O'Connor's Part IV-B, joined by all eight other Justices: nine at the invalidated-predicate and no-evasion level. This agreement does not settle the permissible content of a new decree. O'Connor's Part IV-C, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight. Stone-Zsela dissents from the refusal to require his bounded post-refusal replacement.

**Present operation effective:** Schenck, 1997-02-19, for the fixed-zone counseling qualification; Madsen's governing standard and other components retain their 1994-06-30 authority.

**Exceptions and limits:** The particular distances and hours adjudicated are applications, not universal safe harbors. The decision does not require a personal buffer zone, decide the reserved independent conduct-language claims, or transplant Casey’s abortion-regulation framework into the advocacy inquiry. Coverage remains confined to responsible parties and persons with notice acting in actual concert or participation; viewpoint, association or independent advocacy alone does not establish concert.

## Standards and Tests selection: Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims

### Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims

**Governing question:** The showing needed to overcome successive or abusive habeas barriers through innocence of the offense.

**Current rule:** The prisoner must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the whole probative record, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The inquiry includes the new evidence together with the existing record rather than deferring conclusively to the original jury’s credibility choices. Passing this demanding procedural gateway establishes neither innocence as a freestanding claim nor the underlying constitutional violation and shifts no ultimate merits burden. The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every submitted statement as true. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner.

**Authority by component:**

- Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway: Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway, 1995-01-23. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support a stricter crime-innocence quantum and would affirm.
- This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission: This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission, 1995-01-23. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, statutory-refusal and remand rationale, joined by Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this bounded rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth vote for vacatur and the same gateway-assessment remand on his stated ground, without joining the separate §2244(b) analysis. No aggregation of different rationales is needed for the five-Justice holding.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-23; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Sawyer’s clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway is distinct. Section 2244(b) permits refusal of a subsequent habeas application by a person held under a state-court judgment after a federal court, justice or judge denied release or another habeas remedy on an earlier habeas application following either an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue or a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, unless the later application alleges and rests on a factual or other ground not adjudicated in the earlier hearing and the court is satisfied that the applicant did not deliberately withhold the newly asserted ground on the earlier application or otherwise abuse the writ. Both exception conditions are required. New evidence is not automatically a new ground, and absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. When both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent, McCleskey requires consideration. The qualified refusal permission does not itself sustain refusal based on the rejected clear-and-convincing crime standard. Other lawful independent barriers and necessary factual-development rules remain.

**Burdens and proof:** The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner’s burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate.

**Open question:** Schlup does not decide whether every successful innocence showing compels review, a general appellate standard for refusal, or an automatic hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay.

**Related current rules:** Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims supplies the separate eligibility inquiry and proof quantum.

**Holdings navigation:** Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway; This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission.

## Standards and Tests selection: Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility

### Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility

**Governing question:** Assessment and consequence of suppressed favorable evidence.

**Current rule:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses in the whole record, including defense preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. No item must independently meet the standard; the defendant need not show that acquittal is more likely than conviction or that remaining evidence is legally insufficient. The prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence held by police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires. Personal ignorance does not remove it from review. Once a material Brady violation is established, a second duplicative prejudice or harmlessness inquiry cannot defeat relief by reassessing the same nondisclosure.

**Authority by component:**

- Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction: Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction, 1995-04-19. Justice Souter’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes for the rule, application and conviction-level relief. The dissent accepts collective assessment but disputes materiality on this record.
- The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team: The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team, 1995-04-19. Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. The dissent’s consideration of the police-held material does not supply additional votes for an affirmative organizational rule.
- A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing: A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing, 1995-04-19. Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes for this claim-specific ground. The dissent finds no material violation and adopts no independent harmlessness ground.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-04-19; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This case-team responsibility does not impute every government office’s knowledge or require unrestricted investigation or open-file discovery. Lawful corroboration, impeachment and investigation-related uses matter collectively; every nondisclosure is not structural error. Independent habeas gates and defense diligence concerning known evidence remain separate.

**Holdings navigation:** Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction; The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team; A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar

### Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar

**Governing question:** The retained original-writ channel and extraordinary showing following §2244(b)(3)(E).

**Current rule:** The statutory ban on ordinary certiorari review of a circuit’s successive-application authorization decision does not repeal §2241’s distinct Supreme Court original habeas authority. An original petition receives independent consideration rather than appellate review of that authorization judgment. Circuit permission to file in district court is not a prerequisite to filing here. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform original-writ discretion; whether they bind it directly remains unresolved. Rule 20 separately requires an explanation for not seeking district relief, how and where state remedies were exhausted or §2254(b) otherwise satisfied, exceptional circumstances warranting the Supreme Court’s discretionary exercise and lack of adequate relief in another form or court. Retained jurisdiction alone does not warrant the writ, further proceedings or an execution stay. Section 1651 aids independently held jurisdiction rather than supplying a freestanding new source.

**Authority by component:**

- Separate jurisdiction and original-writ discretion: The withdrawn review route and retained original jurisdiction; Successive restrictions and original-writ discretion; The separate constitutional innocence claim and original relief, 1996-06-28; Felker Parts I-A, II and IV-B, all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Felker, 1996-06-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Felker’s as-applied constitutional rejection rests on actual independent consideration and an adequate traditional original-writ ground, not approval of eliminating every meaningful forum. Constitutional innocence’s universal merits threshold, other supervisory routes, and the constitutionality of all AEDPA restrictions remain open.

**Holdings navigation:** Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal-prisoner limitation and successive-motion certification

### Federal-prisoner limitation and successive-motion certification

**Governing question:** The new §2255 limitation period and certification for a second or successive motion.

**Current rule:** A §2255 motion has a one-year limitation running from the latest of conviction finality; removal of an unlawful governmental filing impediment that actually prevented the motion; initial Supreme Court recognition of the asserted newly recognized right made retroactively applicable on collateral review; or when due diligence could discover the supporting facts. A second or successive motion requires certification under §2244 by an appropriate appellate panel of either newly discovered evidence which, if proved and considered with all evidence, would establish clearly and convincingly that no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt, or a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court. The evidence route has its own text and is not silently replaced with §2244(b)(2)(B)’s distinct state-prisoner constitutional-error/diligence formulation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Second or successive state-custody habeas applications

### Second or successive state-custody habeas applications

**Governing question:** Repeated/new claims, appellate authorization and district verification under amended §2244(b).

**Current rule:** A claim in a second or successive §2254 application already presented in a prior application must be dismissed. A new claim must also be dismissed unless it relies on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court, or both its factual predicate was not previously discoverable through due diligence and its facts, if proved and viewed with all evidence, would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found the applicant guilty of the underlying offense. Before district filing, the applicant must seek appropriate appellate authorization; a three-judge panel may authorize only on a prima facie showing of these requirements and must act within thirty days. A grant or denial is not appealable and cannot be the subject of rehearing or certiorari. Authorization is not final entitlement: the district court must dismiss unless the applicant establishes the statutory conditions.

Felker Part I-A and II, all nine; the channel temporal result has all nine, its Part I-B explanation Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (six).

**Present operation effective:** Statutory requirements enacted 1996-04-24; Felker’s specified review-channel construction, 1996-06-28.

##### Direct protection of an interstate instrumentality against forcible taking

**Holding:** Congress may protect an instrumentality of interstate commerce against a direct intrastate forcible taking when the offense requires proof of the particular vehicle's interstate connection. #### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. The interstate-externality reservation is unchanged.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. **Material application:** The southern boundary is the south edge of Dixie Way's pavement, leaving the unpaved south shoulder available subject to lawful restrictions. On the east, the U.S. Highway One shoulder remains usable, but defendants may not ascend the embankment and must remain five feet from the clinic's east line. Pending painted lines, prompt crossing uses an eight-foot strip directly from Dixie Way's south to north edge, with its western line coextensive with Highway One's western right-of-way line; once marked, the crosswalk governs. Within the buffer no other pavement entry is allowed. Crossing permits no stopping, hesitation, picketing, congregation, demonstration or traffic impediment. This is an existing crossing dimension, not a personal-separation order.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Carroll*: applies precision to the access boundary and actual crossing and shoulder permissions.
- *Claiborne Hardware*: confines the expressive burden to supported access relief.
- *City of Ladue v. Gilleo*, June 13, 1994: applies attention to usable alternatives without creating an entitlement to every preferred location.

##### The private-property extension lacks the necessary remedial connection

**Holding:** The north/west extension of the 36-foot exclusion cannot suppress otherwise lawful expression on adjoining private land without a demonstrated connection between that full exclusion and the access or operational harm to be remedied. Florida's approval of that extension is reversed; removing the government's expressive restriction neither authorizes trespass nor adjudicates owners' property rights.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on their stricter grounds; Stevens declines distinct geographic review.

**Material application:** The north/west owners and invitees were excepted from the entry prohibition, while the other expressive restrictions still applied when they acted in concert. Removing the governmental expressive exclusion adjudicates neither permission to enter nor private property boundaries.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Claiborne Hardware*: applies the requirement that relief correspond to the established wrong.
- *Carroll*: applies precision to the unsupported geographic extension.
- *Ladue*: preserves practical channel analysis; it supplies no right to use another person's property.

##### The qualified medical-noise restriction survives

**Holding:** Paragraph (4)'s noise restriction is valid in its medical-operation context from 7:30 a.m. through noon, Monday–Saturday, during surgical procedures and recovery periods, as to the listed and other sounds within earshot of patients inside the clinic. Its affirmance rests on the treatment interference established here, not disagreement with a message, and adds no decibel, intent or newly drafted volume threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-C, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse on strict direct-restraint and adequate-unlawful-predicate grounds, despite accepting fit under the Court's assumed standard; Stevens declines merits.

**Material application:** The covered sounds are singing, chanting, whistling, shouting, yelling, bullhorns, auto horns, amplification equipment and other sounds within the earshot of patients inside the clinic, in the lower court's medical-operation context. Both the 7:30 a.m.–noon Monday–Saturday window and the surgery-or-recovery condition apply; neither is replaced by a newly invented sound threshold.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*: applies the distinction between controlling sound's physical effects and suppressing its content; the heightened injunction rule governs this remedy.
- *Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement*, June 19, 1992: preserves the distinction between a neutral harm and burdens resting on hostility to a message.
- *Carroll*: applies precision through the time, treatment and patient-location limits.

**Holding:** The prohibition on all images observable to patients inside the clinic, even within the stated morning surgery-and-recovery periods, burdens more speech than necessary on this record and must be removed. Patient vulnerability or distress at a message does not establish a general power to prevent visible advocacy; independently lawful threat or intrusion relief remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-D, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on stricter grounds; Stevens declines merits.

- *Erznoznik v. City of Jacksonville*, 422 U.S. 205: applies protection against broad suppression merely because images are visible to unwilling viewers.

**Holding:** Paragraph (5)'s requirement of an indication of interest before any physical approach to a person seeking clinic services within 300 feet burdens more speech than necessary and must be removed. This holding protects peaceful initial advocacy, not a right to accompany, encircle, surround, threaten, obstruct or force communication upon a person who declines it, and approves no universal application of disputed harassment or abuse terms.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-E, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on stricter grounds; Stevens dissents from reversal of this physical-approach condition.

**Material application:** The clause regulates physical approach, not every word or sign within 300 feet. Invited nonthreatening conversation and literature were permitted when a service-seeker approached or inquired, but never inside the 36-foot zone. Protections against accompanying, encircling, surrounding, harassment, threats and physical or verbal abuse of a declining person retain only their independently lawful scope; reversal neither invalidates every conduct protection nor approves every disputed use of those labels.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Claiborne Hardware*: applies protection of persuasion and the connection between misconduct and relief.
- *Boos*: preserves political approach while allowing properly grounded protection against threats and coercion.
- *Carroll*: requires the restriction to distinguish protected initiation from the conduct justifying restraint.

**Holding:** The 300-foot residential prohibition on approaching, congregating, picketing, patrolling, demonstrating and amplification sweeps beyond the supported protection of a targeted home and must be removed. Its advance-invitation qualification does not restore general neighborhood expression; distinct lawful obstruction, trespass, threat and properly focused residential protections are preserved without selecting a replacement distance.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-F, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: six on this bounded ground. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal under their stricter direct-restraint account; the judgment is unanimous.

**Material application:** Paragraph (6)'s separate bans on blocking entrances, exits or driveways and impeding a street providing sole access to the residences' street are distinct from the territorial ban. The separate invited-contact provision allows willing contact outside the clinic buffer, with residential invitation communicated from a distance before contact, not obtained by an uninvited knock or doorbell. Invitation may be revoked, requiring immediate termination and departure. Retained language must conform to reversal and cannot recreate the invalid territorial exclusion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Frisby v. Schultz*, 487 U.S. 474: applies its distinction between focused home picketing and general neighborhood expression; its narrow residential protection remains.
- *City of Ladue v. Gilleo*: applies practical attention to lost channels while preserving the distinction between one's own home and intrusion on another's.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

##### Whether the Government proved predisposition independent of its inducement beyond a reasonable doubt

**Holding:** When the Government induces the charged crime and entrapment is at issue, it must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant’s predisposition existed before and independently of its cultivation; evidence obtained after contact may prove that earlier state of mind. **Proposition-level authority:** White, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, Thomas: six votes control.

- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307 — applied: reasonable prosecution-favorable inferences remain subject to proof beyond reasonable doubt.
- Mathews v. United States, 485 U.S. 58 — preserved: a routine offered opportunity does not automatically support an entrapment instruction.

**Holding:** Because the prosecution’s proof was legally insufficient to overcome entrapment, the proper disposition is acquittal rather than a new trial. Burks bars a second opportunity to supply the missing evidence of independent predisposition.

- Sherman v. United States, 356 U.S. 369 — applied: matter-of-law entrapment supports termination, not a second prosecution opportunity.

#### Kyles v. Whitley

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7927.
**Decided:** April 19, 1995.

##### Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction

**Holding:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses, considered in the whole record and in the defense’s preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. - Schlup v. Delo, January 23, 1995: distinguishes its gateway showing; it supplies no additional innocence prerequisite for this Brady claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The collective inquiry includes legitimate effects on preparation, corroboration, impeachment and examination of the investigation. It does not excuse lack of diligence about evidence already known to the defense, require open-file discovery or make every nondisclosure structural error.

##### The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team

**Holding:** A prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence known to police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires disclosure; personal ignorance does not remove that evidence from the materiality inquiry. This case-specific responsibility does not impute all knowledge of every government office or establish unrestricted investigative or open-file duties.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brady and Bagley: apply their trial-disclosure protection to information held by police acting for this prosecution.
- United States v. Williams, May 4, 1992: distinguishes its confined grand-jury disclosure and dismissal rules; those limits remain effective within their own setting.

##### A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing

**Holding:** Once this suppression is found material under Brady and Bagley, a second prejudice or harmlessness test may not defeat relief merely by reassessing whether the same nondisclosure affected the verdict. This holding rests on the prejudice built into the completed Brady violation and establishes no universal harmlessness rule for other habeas claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bagley: treats materiality as a defining element of the suppression violation, not an initial screen before a second duplicative prejudice test.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993, and O’Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995: preserve their actual category-specific holdings and distinguish them from claim-defining Brady materiality.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is reversed and remanded for a conditional habeas writ requiring release unless Louisiana retries Kyles within a reasonable time fixed by the District Court. No acquittal, immediate unconditional release, fixed number of days, penalty-only relief or adjudication of other claims is ordered.

#### Schlup v. Delo

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7901.
**Decided:** January 23, 1995.

##### Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway

**Holding:** A prisoner seeking to overcome otherwise applicable successive or abusive habeas barriers to independent constitutional claims through innocence of the offense must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the probative record as a whole, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. This procedural gateway does not itself establish innocence, prove any constitutional violation or shift the ultimate burden on the underlying claims; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing rule remains confined to its distinct death-eligibility setting.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: applies and specifies its probability-based crime-innocence exception without changing cause and prejudice.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333: preserves the clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway and resolves the crime-innocence question that decision left open; it is not overruled or given a categorical impeachment exclusion.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467: preserves successive/abusive-claim barriers and the exceptional innocence route, without treating potential constitutional merit as sufficient to pass it.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436: uses the relevant plurality discussion of the whole probative record as persuasive guidance, preserving its actual authority limit.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307: distinguishes trial-record legal sufficiency from the likely effect of an augmented record on reasonable jurors; its sufficiency rule remains intact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every affidavit as true or treat the original verdict as a conclusive answer. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner. A mere possibility of acquittal, a juror who might disagree, ordinary constitutional-merits prejudice or the reviewing judge's personal reasonable doubt is insufficient. The reasonable juror fairly considers the evidence and follows the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt instruction.

##### This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission

**Holding:** The courts' use of the rejected clear-and-convincing crime gateway and reliance on the original jury's credibility function require vacatur and renewed assessment on this record; §2244(b)'s permission to refuse a qualifying subsequent petition does not itself supply an adequate independent ground to sustain that legally mistaken refusal. The Court remands through the Eighth Circuit to the District Court for application of the probability rule and a reasoned determination of necessary factual development, without deciding whether every successful innocence showing compels review, choosing a general appellate standard, or ordering a hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay automatically.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's statutory-refusal and remand portion, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth judgment vote without joining the separate §2244(b) rationale.

**Current-force modification:** AEDPA §106 replaces former §2244(b)'s refusal permission for successive §2254 district-court applications. A claim already presented must be dismissed. A new claim must be dismissed unless it rests on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence and facts that, if proved and considered with the whole evidence, establish by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt. Advance authorization by a three-judge court-of-appeals panel requires a prima facie showing; authorization does not remove the district court's duty to dismiss a claim unless the applicant proves the statutory requirements. This is an independent statutory condition, not a replacement of Schlup's probability gateway or a ruling that the gateway itself satisfies the new text. The original former-law remand stands. Pending-case application is not generally resolved; Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996, No. 95-8836, applies the post-enactment authorization-review bar while reserving whether §§2244(b)(1)–(2) directly bind original habeas consideration in this Court.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McCleskey, 499 U.S. 467: applies the distinction between conditional consideration of new, nonabusive grounds and legally constrained refusal of successive or abusive grounds; neither permission nor exception becomes an unconditional command.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1, and Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293: preserve necessary factual development within their properly presented, available-claim setting and the deliberate-bypass qualification; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a required hearing there, but no successive/abusive gateway is eliminated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2244(b) provides:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The prior-hearing alternatives are a factual evidentiary merits hearing or a legal merits hearing, each followed by a federal denial of release or other habeas remedy to a person held under a state judgment. Both unless conditions must be preserved: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent. The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner's burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate; cause and prejudice is not pressed in this review. Subsection (a)'s separate wording is not inserted into subsection (b), and no new subsection (c) predicate is established.

The videotape was trial evidence. Green's asserted prompt call, Faherty's movement account and other later identification accounts must be assessed with the existing record; the Court finds no witness truthful or false and does not find the gateway satisfied. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and a genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. Remand is not mandatory constitutional-merits review. Any necessary live testimony is for a reasoned determination under the applicable rules, not an automatic consequence of affidavits or vacatur. The merits of the suppression and ineffective-assistance claims, a freestanding innocence entitlement and any ultimate guilt determination remain undecided.

No gateway satisfaction, automatic hearing, constitutional-merits review, writ, retrial, release or stay is ordered.

L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §106, replacement §2244(b)(1)–(4) district-court successive-application mechanism; see also Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996, No. 95-8836, distinct original jurisdiction and reserved direct statutory reach. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

##### General equitable disapproval of delay cannot replace the governing dismissal predicates

**Holding:** A first personal federal habeas petition satisfying jurisdictional and pleading requirements cannot be terminated solely for the reported delay and litigation purpose by an additional equitable sanction dispensing with habeas statutes and Rules. #### Holdings

### I. Direct interference with operating commercial assets supplies the required commerce connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may apply the Hobbs Act to robbery directed at the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce when the taking directly impairs its commercial operations and the Act's enacted commerce element is actually established. The effect of one robbery may be slight: aggregation measures the magnitude of this directly connected economic class, but neither a business label nor generalized economic consequences of crime supplies the antecedent connection.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt the rule, application to the Hobbs Act components and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Lopez requires the enacted class and its actual interstate mechanism before magnitude can establish federal authority. The relevant class here is robbery affecting commerce through direct interference with operating business capital, not all robbery. The targeted businesses purchased interstate inventory and cashed out-of-state, payroll and government-benefit checks. The takings removed money withdrawn for those operations and impaired restocking and check-cashing capacity; the published record also reports one business closing for lack of capital. Taking the working money in a bank drive-through lane or store parking lot does not change its established business function.

American Life League supplies the closest mechanism: conduct directed at an actual interstate market's operations can directly obstruct that market. Once that relationship exists, Wickard and Perez permit evaluation of class-wide effects without demanding a substantial effect from each individual act. The statutory commerce element still requires an actual offense-specific nexus. Its words alone would not validate a constitutionally inadequate connection, and its character as an element does not make it subject-matter jurisdiction.

The contrary argument properly rejects multiplying local losses as a substitute for a constitutional mechanism. But this record identifies that mechanism before aggregation. A theory treating every victim as a potential consumer or every dollar as eventual interstate spending would lack this boundary and is not adopted. Hale, Rybar and Kirk concern the distinct inclusion of possession necessary to a valid machinegun regime; no such anti-evasion rationale is needed here.

The facial commerce objection fails because the Act lawfully reaches this defined class; the Court does not validate every imagined application. ### II. The wholly derivative firearm objection fails with the predicate commerce challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented constitutional objection to the firearm conviction supplies no ground for relief because it depends entirely on the rejected claim that the underlying Hobbs Act business robberies exceed the commerce power. This disposition adjudicates no independent firearm element, instruction, sufficiency, aiding-and-abetting or collateral defect.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices directly adopt this derivative ground. Stone-Zsela agrees with the firearm disposition only and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower opinion identifies the firearm constitutional challenge as dependent on invalidity of the Hobbs Act predicates. Part I rejects that asserted invalidity through the actual business-interference mechanism and proved commerce nexus. The derivative argument therefore fails on its own stated premise. Neither this conclusion nor Salinas's distinct statutory-conspiracy analysis resolves an independent defect that is not presented. The Court does not infer a new admission, revise the indictment or approve the whole jury charge. The separate lower sentencing vacatur remains effective; the commerce affirmance neither reinstates the vulnerable-victim enhancement nor creates a new resentencing or retrial order.
