## Public Projection

## Event

Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County; Nos. 97-1115 and 97-1160. Plenary merits decision on submission, September 21, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 13. The petitions of Metropolitan Dade County and Allied Minority Contractors Association concern the Eleventh Circuit's September 2, 1997 judgment, amended October 8, 1997, 122 F.3d 895, affirming declarations and permanent injunctions against three contracting programs. No exact Supreme Court oral-argument date is established.

The questions concern the evidence and fit required for local Black-owned (BBE), Hispanic-owned (HBE) and women-owned (WBE) construction preferences. The programs cover County-funded construction contracts over $25,000 in general building (SIC 15), heavy construction (SIC 16) and specialty trades (SIC 17), with respective 15, 19 and 11 percent goals. Eligibility requires actual local operation and covered ownership and control, normally at small-business size; a larger firm may retain certification on showing it continues to experience the addressed discrimination.

The measures include set-asides ordinarily requiring three available covered firms; with two, competitive bidding may be waived only if neither obtained a like County contract within three years and price analysis establishes a competitive price. Contract-specific subcontract goals permit waiver for unavailability at a competitive price, but not inability to obtain bonding. Project goals draw on a covered subcontractor pool. Bid preferences lower the comparison price by up to 10 percent without lowering the amount paid; selection factors may raise nonprice evaluation by up to 10 percent. Annual efficacy and five-year continuation reviews apply.

The County offered 1989–91 and 1993 contract studies, broader marketplace, subcontract, business-entry and income studies, and anecdotal accounts. Hurricane Andrew's effect led to omission of 1992. Most firm-size-adjusted award disparities disappeared; residual results differed by group, sector and year. The existing BBE program could affect observed results, but no counterfactual dataset establishes its exact effect. Anecdotal accounts were chiefly BBE, some WBE and none HBE. They are evidence offered, not findings that every incident occurred.

## Participation

All nine participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No recusal or nonparticipation is established.

The Eleventh Circuit judgment is affirmed on the distinct grounds applicable to each program.

## Holdings

### 1. Unequal competitive opportunity supports this review

**Holding and operative rule:** Regular bidders facing a supported likelihood of exclusion from equal competition have an injury even without proving that they would win a contract. The associations may seek common prospective relief where their members possess standing, the interest is germane, and the claims and relief do not require individual participation; facts may be stipulated, but jurisdiction may not.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion has all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela joins the equal-competition injury proposition and the distinction between subsidiary factual review and constitutional legal review; he does not add a distinct associational-standing rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Northeastern Florida Chapter, Associated General Contractors v. Jacksonville identifies the denial of equal bidding opportunity as the injury. United Food & Commercial Workers Union Local 751 v. Brown Group preserves the association requirements and their distinct constitutional and prudential roles. The associations' regularly bidding members and stipulated likely future exclusion establish the necessary injury for all three programs, and the common justification challenge permits common prospective relief. No lost award or injury from every hypothetical contract mechanism is required. This does not confer standing to litigate every distinct operational challenge. Under §1254(1), the Court reviews the final appellate judgment. Subsidiary historical findings receive clear-error review; constitutional sufficiency, the level of scrutiny and remedial fit require independent legal assessment.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Northeastern Florida applies unequal-competition injury; its standing rule does not decide the preferences' validity.
- Brown Group applies the association requirements at their stated scope; its standing rule does not decide the preferences' validity.

### 2. The local racial classifications remain subject to strict scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule:** Local racial and ethnic contracting classifications must rest on a supported compelling justification and narrowly fitted means. General social inequality, numerical imbalance and good intentions alone do not establish that justification; a supported local system of exclusion in which the County participates can permit fitted repair without invariably requiring a prior formal judgment of liability.

**Authority:** Part II has all eight Associates for strict scrutiny and the federal/local distinction. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the affirmative remedial proposition; Scalia reserves the permissible extent of racial remediation beyond the grounds needed to reject these programs. Stone-Zsela does not join this local rule.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co. governs local contracting preferences, and Hopwood v. Texas preserves supported justification, individual equality and narrow fit for state racial classifications. Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña sustained its distinct federal arrangement under Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC's important-objective and substantial-relationship standard. Adarand expressly preserved Croson; its federal rule cannot be transferred to Dade County merely because the County describes its purpose as corrective. The Court declines the proposed replacement of local strict scrutiny. This decision creates neither a right to proportionate contracts nor a prohibition on every properly supported local repair program.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Croson remains controlling for local racial classifications.
- Hopwood remains controlling for state racial classifications and fitted repair.
- Adarand retains its bounded federal application; it is neither overruled nor enlarged.
- Metro Broadcasting retains the different federal standard; it is neither overruled nor enlarged.

### 3. The BBE and HBE programs fail the independent narrow-fit requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** Before using these racial and ethnic contracting preferences, the County must seriously consider reasonably available race-neutral measures responsive to the exclusion it identifies and provide a supported explanation of their inadequacy. This record does not satisfy that requirement for either BBE or HBE; it requires considered alternatives, not trial and exhaustion of every conceivable measure.

**Authority:** Part III has all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela would vacate and remand under a different proposed local rule. Four Associates also find the racial evidence insufficient, but that additional ground does not control.

**Controlling explanation:** Croson's narrow-fit requirement and Hopwood's fitted-repair principle require attention to measures addressing the actual problem. The record identifies procurement information, financing, bonding, payment and discriminatory-treatment obstacles. The County's limited technical and financial assistance and conclusory treatment of alternatives do not show serious assessment of responsive neutral measures; HBE lacks such consideration. Flexibility, waivers, annual review and five-year continuation review cannot supply the missing inquiry. The possibility that an existing BBE program reduced observed disparities matters to evidence, but cannot cure this distinct tailoring defect. The Court therefore need not decide whether the combined racial evidence would otherwise establish a sufficient remedial predicate. It does not constitutionalize a statistical-significance threshold, numerical parity, a requirement of a prior discrimination judgment or a duty to exhaust every possible neutral course.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Croson applies to the unperformed alternatives inquiry without making corrective contracting preferences categorically invalid.
- Hopwood applies its fitted-repair principle to that inquiry without categorical invalidation of corrective preferences.

### 4. The WBE program fails the distinct intermediate-scrutiny inquiry on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** A sex-conscious contracting measure requires an actual important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the classification, with a demanding governmental burden. On independent assessment of the accepted findings and combined evidence, the County has not adequately substantiated the identified gender exclusion needed to justify this three-sector WBE preference; its invalidation is affirmed.

**Authority:** All nine accept the stated intermediate standard. Part IV's application and affirmance have five direct votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate and remand for a different independent assessment of the combined record.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley v. Board of Trustees, Klinger v. Department of Corrections and United States v. Virginia require actual important purposes and a substantial relationship, without importing racial strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. The Court independently examines the constitutional sufficiency of the evidence while retaining subsidiary findings not shown clearly erroneous. Firm-size adjustments largely explain award-dollar differences; the surviving WBE SIC 15 disparity, women's entry disparity and accounts of refusing to deal with female owners matter but do not adequately justify this program's allocation across all three construction sectors. The subcontract denominator and broader marketplace pool do not reliably identify the relevant procurement comparison. The conclusion does not rest merely on calling the trial judge's ultimate constitutional view permissible. Nor does it impose a numerical minimum or rule out sufficient nonstatistical proof. The court of appeals rejected strict racial tailoring for WBE and found its flexibility capable of satisfying substantial relationship if the evidentiary predicate existed. The present failure is that predicate, not the racial alternatives defect, an excessive 11 percent goal, or an obligation to exhaust neutral measures. A later supported and differently designed women's program is not decided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Kelley retains demanding intermediate scrutiny and rejects stereotyped or merely numerical justification; it supplies no strict-scrutiny or statistical-only prerequisite.
- Klinger retains that distinct intermediate standard and actual-justification requirement, without a statistical-only prerequisite.
- Virginia retains demanding intermediate scrutiny and rejection of stereotyped justification, without importing racial strict scrutiny or least restrictive means.

### 5. The existing injunctions remain confined to these programs

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgments and permanent injunctions against the challenged BBE, HBE and WBE programs are affirmed on their respective grounds. The relief addresses the operative programs and established likely future unequal exclusion; it neither prohibits unexamined future corrective measures nor awards a contract, damages or an individual discrimination judgment.

**Authority:** Part V has the eight-Associate coalition for BBE/HBE and the five-Justice WBE coalition identified above. Stone-Zsela would maintain the racial restraints temporarily during his proposed remand, rather than affirm their present permanent disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Concrete future exclusion, not past injury alone, supplies a preventive basis for relief. Hopwood's independent prospective-relief requirements and Federal Election Commission v. Williams's insistence on an actual preventive foundation are satisfied by the still-operative programs and stipulated future exposure. Williams supplies no campaign-finance limitation rule here. Lawful race-neutral outreach and procurement measures remain available. No court is directed to adopt a new preference program, and the separate evidentiary bases are not merged.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Hopwood supplies its independent prospective-relief limits without deciding a future unexamined scheme.
- Williams supplies the actual preventive-basis requirement without deciding a future unexamined scheme.

- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co.: retains local strict scrutiny, supported remedial justification and narrow fit.
- Hopwood v. Texas: retains state strict scrutiny, fitted repair and independent prospective-relief requirements.
- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña: retains its bounded federal application; no local extension follows.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC: retains the separate federal standard.
- Northeastern Florida Chapter, Associated General Contractors v. Jacksonville: applies unequal-competition injury.
- United Food & Commercial Workers Union Local 751 v. Brown Group: applies the association requirements.
- Kelley v. Board of Trustees: preserves distinct demanding intermediate scrutiny without a statistical-only prerequisite.
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections: preserves the intermediate standard and actual-justification requirement without a statistical-only prerequisite.
- United States v. Virginia: preserves demanding intermediate scrutiny without racial strict scrutiny or least restrictive means.

## Law After Decision

Local racial and ethnic contracting preferences remain subject to strict scrutiny, including serious assessment of responsive neutral alternatives without exhaustion of every imaginable measure. Sex classifications retain the separate important-objective/substantial-relationship test. These programs' injunctions stand on different grounds; no numerical constitutional minimum, exact-parity requirement or categorical ban on future supported repair is adopted. The four-Justice additional racial evidence ground and Stone-Zsela's replacement framework are noncontrolling.

## Separate Writings

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the racial judgment on the additional ground that the evidence is insufficient after independent constitutional review. Size controls largely explain contract-dollar disparities; residual differences vary by sector and year, the subcontract and marketplace samples have comparability defects, and broad entry or income disparities do not by themselves establish the required local procurement link. The existing BBE program may mask discrimination, but that possibility supplies no missing adjusted data or finding. They assess the anecdotal proof together with the statistics, without a statistical-only rule or an assumption that every reported incident is false. This four-Justice ground adds no controlling holding; the common narrow-fit ground independently supports the racial judgment. Scalia also reserves the full extent of permissible racial remediation stated in Part II, without conditioning his join in the actual rejection on categorical invalidity of all repair.

Stevens, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, concurs in the BBE/HBE judgment on tailoring alone and dissents from the WBE affirmance. The significant women's entry disparity, surviving general-building disparity and reports of refusing to deal with women require an independent intermediate-scrutiny assessment of their combined force. Firm size may itself reflect barriers to obtaining capacity; controlling for size answers a question different from whether such barriers existed. They preserve supported subsidiary findings and invent neither a corrected regression nor a finding that the program is valid. They would vacate the WBE declaration and injunction for prompt reconsideration, with any necessary interim restraint separately assessed below. They do not direct immediate operation or a right to the entire program and reserve the additional racial evidence question because tailoring suffices.

Stone-Zsela concurs in the WBE judgment and its intermediate-scrutiny ground but dissents from the BBE/HBE affirmances. He would expressly replace Croson's local rule for supported corrective contracting measures with a requirement of lawful local authority, objective evidence of identified exclusion or its continuing effects in the relevant procurement market, a substantial relationship to actual remedy, reasonable eligibility and duration limits, and meaningful opportunity for nonbeneficiaries. The County would bear the burden. Neutral alternatives would inform fit without automatically invalidating a supported program. This is a proposed change, not an extension already accomplished by Adarand.

He would vacate the racial invalidations and remand for program-specific legal assessment of the existing record, preserving ordinary clear-error treatment of subsidiary findings and permitting further findings only as ordinary procedure and the record allow. Quantitative studies, earlier Black-firm receipt and owner-income disparities, and possible effects of an existing program merit reassessment; they do not establish that either BBE or HBE succeeds. He would keep the racial injunctions in force pending adequate findings, permitting narrowing or dissolution only as a supported program is shown to fit. He would leave the WBE injunction in force and preserve lawful neutral measures. His proposed local framework receives no other join and changes no governing law.

## Procedure After Action

The existing permanent injunctions remain effective within their respective programs. No new factual hearing, revised program, contract award, deadline or automatic return is ordered. A later proposed measure must be assessed on its own lawful record.

## Public Projection

## Event

Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Johnson & Higgins, Inc.; No. 96-1743, petition captioned Johnson & Higgins, Inc. v. EEOC; Second Circuit No. 95-6216. Plenary merits decision on submission, September 21, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 13. Review of the Second Circuit's August 8, 1996 decision, 91 F.3d 1529, affirming an interlocutory injunction following partial summary judgment on ADEA liability. Monetary relief was reserved. No exact oral-argument date in this Court is established.

The questions concern independent EEOC enforcement and conciliation, employee status of working owner-directors, the age-based retirement rule and statutory exceptions, and the employment injunction's scope. Johnson & Higgins is a New Jersey corporation conducting an insurance and employee-benefits business. Its board ranges from 20 to 44 directors, with 35 at the investigation's beginning. Nomination by the chairman and election by two thirds of the board select existing senior employees; annual evaluation by six senior directors, compensation review and removal controls continue alongside substantial ownership and governance.

Directors must hold at least 500 shares, and cessation of ownership ends board service. Departure requires share sale; a ten-year agreement provides dividend-equivalent payments in exchange for consulting availability and noncompetition. Stock is predominantly director-owned, with a small fraction issued to other key employees since 1993. The unanimously adopted 1983 policy couples retirement from employment, officer status, directorship and equity at the end of the year age 62 is attained, or earlier when age 60 and fifteen board years coincide. Only the latter branch permits corporate-need extensions approved by the chairman and executive committee, year by year and no later than the end of the age-62 year.

The EEOC learned of the policy during another investigation. No current or former director sought this enforcement action, and affidavits opposed it. Investigation notice, a determination, a July 1992 conciliation meeting and invitation, renewed information requests and refusals preceded suit filed August 5, 1993. The reported releases lacked ADEA-specific and seven-day-revocation terms; no individual's monetary entitlement or release consequence is adjudicated here.

## Participation

All nine participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No recusal or nonparticipation is established.

## Public Action

The Second Circuit judgment affirming ADEA liability and the bounded prospective employment injunction is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Affirm, 6–3. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, who would vacate and remand employee classification and the dependent liability and injunction.

The public enforcement and governing classification rules have nine joins, without converting the dissenters' conditional age-rule agreement into a coverage or liability vote. The maintained employment-retirement policy and incoming employee agreements to it remain restrained; rescission and submission of a compliant policy proceed on a date set below. Separately lawful board and equity arrangements remain possible. Monetary relief is reserved; no damages, reinstatement or board/ownership entitlement is awarded.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope and relationship to judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer in full | Parts I–V; independent enforcement, actual relationship, coverage, age rules and bounded injunction |
| Same opinion, partial join | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela | Parts I–III, IV.A and V; agrees in rejection of the §631(c) executive exception in IV.B without an additional ground |
| Same opinion, partial and conditional joins | Ginsburg | Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas | Parts I–II; Parts IV.A–B only as statutory rules conditioned on employee status; Part V's no-automatic-monetary-or-governance-entitlement limits, not its injunction affirmance |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Scalia | Kennedy and Thomas | Would vacate/remand classification and dependent liability/injunction for complete treatment of proprietor control |

There are nine direct votes for the common classification rule, six for its present employee-status application and injunction, and eight for the executive-exception rationale with the conditional scope stated above. No combined-rationale rule is required.

## Holdings

### 1. Private opposition does not disable the Commission's public enforcement action

**Holding and operative rule:** The EEOC may enforce the ADEA under 29 U.S.C. §626(b) without an affected individual's charge, consent or request for recovery, and §626(f)(4) prevents a private waiver from extinguishing that public authority even if the waiver is valid. The Commission must attempt informal conciliation, conference and persuasion before suit; the actual efforts here satisfy that condition without a duty to repeat an unproductive offer indefinitely.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 626(b) incorporates public enforcement powers and remedies; §626(d)'s charge and waiting requirements govern an individual's action, while §216(b)'s party-plaintiff consent requirement does not govern the Commission. General Telephone Co. of Northwest v. EEOC supports independent public enforcement in its Title VII setting, and Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp. preserves Commission authority despite an individual's separate arrangement. The ADEA's own text decides this case. Notice of investigation, a determination, the July 1992 meeting and invitation, and renewed September and December information requests gave the company the actual violation theory and opportunities to resolve it before the August 5, 1993 suit. Its resistance explains why repetition was unnecessary; categorical denial alone is not a general exception to the statutory attempt requirement. Opposing director affidavits therefore do not bar this suit. Oubre v. Entergy Operations preserves enacted waiver safeguards, but the Court need not determine each release's private effect because §626(f)(4) independently protects enforcement. No automatic private damages, recovery for unwilling persons, repayment or double-recovery ruling follows.

**Precedent treatment:**

- General Telephone supports independent agency enforcement in its statutory setting without replacing the ADEA text.
- Gilmer preserves independent agency enforcement despite an individual's arrangement, without replacing the ADEA text.
- Oubre's private-waiver holding remains distinct; this decision neither enlarges nor retreats from it.

### 2. Employee status depends on the complete relationship

**Holding and operative rule:** The circular employee definition in 29 U.S.C. §630(f) invokes ordinary common-law agency and requires consideration of all relevant incidents of the actual relationship. Corporate form, an employee label, director title, ownership, profit-based compensation or an exclusive three-factor formula cannot alone establish or defeat employment; a genuine proprietor remains outside employee coverage, while actual employment may coexist with ownership and directorship.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden supplies the ordinary-agency presumption and the all-incidents inquiry. Relevant matters include control over work and removal, skill, tools and location, duration, assignment and scheduling authority, compensation, assistants, business integration, benefits and tax treatment; in this setting, governance powers, equity and profit-and-loss arrangements must also receive their actual weight. NLRB v. Town & Country Electric permits compatible roles while preserving actual control and statutory boundaries. Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises likewise distinguishes a genuine relationship from an evidentiary label, and Robinson v. Shell Oil requires attention to the particular statutory setting. Those decisions do not classify every working owner as an employee. Incorporation does not create categorical coverage, and the company's partnership analogy does not erase a distinct employment relationship. ADEA incorporation of FLSA remedies does not import the FLSA's broader definition of employment.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Darden's common-law method applies to the ADEA's circular definition.
- Town & Country preserves compatible roles and actual service within its statutory boundaries; unrelated exclusions are not transplanted.
- Walters supports genuine-relationship inquiry at its statutory scope without importing unrelated Title VII holdings.
- Robinson supports attention to statutory context without importing unrelated Title VII holdings.

### 3. These directors continue covered employment alongside their ownership and governance

**Holding and operative rule:** On the complete undisputed relationship, the employee-directors remain ADEA employees while holding stock and exercising substantial collective governance. Their actual full-time operational service, individual review, compensation determination and removal controls establish employment that election to the board does not extinguish.

**Authority:** Part III has six direct votes: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate and remand classification and the dependent liability and injunction.

**Controlling explanation:** The directors are selected from existing senior officers and continue their ordinary managerial work. Six senior directors conduct annual reviews; compensation depends on performance and an annually determined profit percentage; each director submits a resignation exercisable by two thirds of the board. These controls operate on each individual despite collective ownership. The Court gives substantial weight to the contrary features: required shares, voting and appointment powers, authority over merger or dissolution, profit participation, mutual succession terms, compulsory share sale on departure and the ten-year consulting/noncompetition arrangement. Those features establish genuine ownership and governance, but do not eliminate the continuing operational employment subject to other senior actors' control. Darden requires this complete assessment, rather than acceptance of the lower court's categorical phrasing or exclusive selection of three indicia. The undisputed incidents permit decision now. No inactive director becomes an employee by title alone, and no rule places all partners, shareholders or full-time proprietors within the Act.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Darden applies its complete inquiry to the actual dual relationship without a per se rule for corporate owners.
- Town & Country preserves compatible roles in applying that inquiry without a per se corporate-owner rule.

### 4. A sensible reason for selecting age is not a factor other than age

**Holding and operative rule:** For a covered employee, compulsory retirement at the end of the year age 62 is attained, or earlier at the end of the year both age 60 and fifteen board-service years are attained, is an age-based employment decision. Succession planning does not make those express age conditions reasonable factors other than age under §623(f)(1); the corporate-need extension available only on the age-60/service branch does not remove the age trigger.

**Authority:** Part IV.A's statutory rule has nine direct joins. The six-Justice coverage coalition applies it to liability here; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join only on the express condition that employee status is established.

**Controlling explanation:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins distinguishes years of service actually independent of age from a decision motivated by age or using a criterion as its proxy. These alternatives expressly require age. The chairman's corporate-need finding, approved by the executive committee, permits only year-by-year continuation under the earlier branch and never beyond the end of the year age 62 is attained. The company's reasonable desire for succession therefore cannot satisfy a provision requiring a criterion other than age. It may pursue another legally permissible criterion or separate lawful governance retirement from covered employment. No heightened constitutional age tier is created, and no bona fide occupational qualification, foreign-law conflict or qualifying benefit/seniority-plan defense is established on this record.

**Precedent treatment:** Hazen Paper's actual-age/non-age distinction controls. Its willfulness rule is preserved; facial liability does not itself establish knowledge or reckless disregard or award liquidated damages.

### 5. The executive-retirement provision does not authorize these ages

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 631(c) permits compulsory retirement only for an employee who has attained age 65, following bona fide executive or high-policymaking employment throughout the two immediately preceding years, with an immediate nonforfeitable aggregate annual retirement benefit of at least $44,000 from the employer's pension, profit-sharing, savings or deferred-compensation plans, or their combination. This policy's ages 60 and 62 fail the age condition regardless of the unresolved benefit valuation; executive status alone supplies no exemption.

**Authority:** Part IV.B's statutory construction has the eight Associates. The five Associates affirming coverage apply it here. Stone-Zsela agrees in rejection of this exception without adding a ground; the three classification dissenters retain their coverage condition and do not join the ultimate liability judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The exception contains cumulative conditions, not a general freedom to retire any executive. For benefits paid in a form other than a straight-life annuity without ancillary benefits, or involving employee contributions or rollovers, §631(c)(2) requires the prescribed adjustment to the equivalent of a straight-life annuity without ancillary benefits under a plan without employee or rollover contributions. Neither the ten-year contract nor a general description of generous retirement benefits establishes those requirements. The age condition alone disposes of the asserted applicability to the maintained policy; no benefit amount or annuity valuation is found. The Court neither requires a proprietor to remain in governance nor extends the exception below its enacted age.

**Precedent treatment:** The statutory exception is applied according to its own terms; no earlier decision is displaced and no general executive exclusion is created.

### 6. The prospective order protects employment without guaranteeing ownership or board tenure

**Holding and operative rule:** The Second Circuit's affirmance of liability and the bounded employment injunction stands. The order bars the unlawful employee-retirement policy, agreements by incoming employee-directors to that unlawful retirement, and covered employment termination because of age; it requires rescission and submission of a compliant policy on a date to be set below, while leaving separately lawful ownership and board arrangements available.

**Authority:** Part V's affirmance has the same six-Justice coalition as Part III. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate the dependent commands pending correct classification; all nine accept that coverage alone establishes no monetary or governance entitlement.

**Controlling explanation:** Review under §1254(1) reaches the §1292(a)(1) injunction appeal following partial summary judgment; no final damages award is before the Court. The maintained policy and asserted right to continue it provide a concrete preventive basis under incorporated §217 and §626(b). Federal Election Commission v. Williams requires that independent prospective basis, but supplies no ADEA limitations or tolling rule. The identified policy and described prohibitions satisfy the presented specificity objection; the order is not a general command to obey every employment law. The company may separate board or equity retirement from employment if statutory law permits, but may not use that separation indirectly to end covered employment because of age. Monetary relief, willfulness, lawful effects of individual agreements, setoff, repayment and any proper individual remedy remain for their own adjudication. No automatic damages, reinstatement, lifetime board seat, equity retention or recovery for an unwilling person is awarded.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Williams's prospective-remedy requirement applies at its actual scope.
- Oubre preserves the distinction between statutory protection and independently authorized monetary consequences.

## Holdings

### 1. Past restraints do not alone establish a present controversy under changed law

**Holding and operative rule:** A prospective challenge requires identified intended conduct facing a realistic current enforcement threat; ended injunctions under replaced or materially amended provisions do not alone supply it. Count One must be dismissed on this record, without requiring an actual prosecution or deciding whether any publication or disclosure rule is constitutional.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno and Virginia v. American Booksellers Association permit review before enforcement where the threatened constraint is concrete. Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona requires a continuing personal controversy. Here all injunctions against these plaintiffs have ended, the statutes have materially changed, and no identified intended activity faces a supported current threat. The Party's failure to disavow every future suit does not fill that gap. Caldwell's authoritative exclusion of solely issue advocacy from the current Virginia provisions further weakens a threat resting on issue-only speech, without treating the express vote-against pamphlet as issue-only or deciding any past order. Amendment does not automatically moot every claim, and the importance of timely political speech cannot replace the absent current controversy. No rule limits proper defendants universally to the Commonwealth, bars every suit against a private enforcer, or disposes of accrued damages merely because prospective relief ended.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Pic-A-State applies the concrete-threat requirement and preserves review without prosecution.
- American Booksellers preserves credible pre-enforcement review; no such threat is established here.
- Arizonans applies the continuing-controversy requirement without extending its unavailable-damages holding to these private damages defendants.
- Caldwell supplies current Virginia construction only; it does not adjudicate the past injunctions.

### 2. These pleaded damages counts seek appellate reassessment of particular state judgments

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal district court lacks authority under Rooker and Feldman to adjudicate these counts insofar as their asserted federal wrong is the legal error of the particular state injunctions and their requested damages compensate those orders' direct effects. The rule is confined to that pleaded theory; it does not bar a claim merely because state litigation caused, preceded or bears on an independently actionable injury.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Rooker v. Fidelity Trust Co. and District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman reserve appellate reassessment of state judicial decisions to the proper appellate channel. The orders here applied law after an opportunity to object; prospective terms did not make those adjudications legislative acts. The published descriptions of the four counts demand a determination that the specific restraints were legally erroneous, without identifying a separate procurement wrong. Their later expiration or dissolution and the demand for damages against the procuring party do not change that pleaded wrong. Dennis v. Sparks preserves independent corrupt-procurement claims, and Johnson v. De Grandy preserves original federal enforcement without appellate review of a judgment against the claimant. Neither supplies a missing independent allegation here. The Court rejects a universal rule covering every consequence of a state order and a simple but-for causation test. It makes no finding that the Party's conduct was lawful and decides no preclusion, state action, immunity, limitations, personal responsibility or damages question.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Rooker applies the lower-federal appellate boundary to the actual wrong and relief asserted.
- Feldman applies the original-action versus individual-judgment distinction; prospective relief can remain judicial adjudication.
- Dennis preserves genuinely independent procurement claims; judicial immunity does not immunize private corrupt conspirators.
- De Grandy preserves its independent-enforcement holding and is distinguished from these pleaded attacks on judgments against the claimants.
- Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co. remains the separate state-attribution inquiry; merely winning or filing private litigation does not establish it.

### 3. Heck supplies no universal civil-injunction jurisdiction bar

**Holding and operative rule:** Heck's favorable-invalidation requirement concerns present §1983 cognizability in its bounded custody setting, not lower-federal appellate jurisdiction. It supplies no universal rule requiring favorable termination of every civil injunction before an independent damages action may proceed.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes on this explanation. The separate writings likewise reject a categorical bar but do not add joins to the Court's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Heck v. Humphrey coordinates §1983 with a real lawful opportunity to challenge an outstanding conviction in custody, while preserving independently cognizable injuries and its unresolved populations. Edwards v. Balisok applies that rule to invalidity-dependent disciplinary-credit claims. Spencer v. Kemna keeps Article III injury, statutory access and possible civil relief distinct and preserves the reservations for former prisoners and those without a realistic habeas opportunity. Those decisions neither convert cognizability into subject-matter jurisdiction nor authorize appellate reassessment of these orders. The lower court's assimilation of Heck to Rooker and Feldman is rejected. Affirmance rests on the actual claim-specific jurisdictional analysis, not a new civil-injunction favorable-termination rule.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Heck retains its bounded statutory prerequisite and independent-injury limits; it is not a Rooker-Feldman decision.
- Edwards retains the invalidity-dependent disciplinary-credit extension, without governing these civil orders.
- Spencer preserves the distinct inquiries and unresolved populations; no exemption or universal bar is created here.

### 4. The state claims lack the required supplemental-jurisdiction foundation

**Holding and operative rule:** Where the pleaded federal claims supply no original jurisdiction, §1367(a) supplies no supplemental jurisdiction over their related state claims. Counts Four, Five, Six and Nine therefore remain dismissed without prejudice, without an adjudication of tort merits or a discretionary retention decision under §1367(c)(3).

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part IV, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1367(a) requires a civil action within the district court's original jurisdiction before its supplemental grant operates. Count One lacks a current controversy and the pleaded damages counts demand appellate authority the district court does not possess. The district court's actual no-original-jurisdiction ground therefore supports dismissal. Section 1367(c)(3), which permits declining supplemental jurisdiction after claims within original jurisdiction have been dismissed, is not an interchangeable description of that defect. Nothing here requires a court possessing original jurisdiction to retain every related state claim. State tort elements, timeliness and recovery remain for a competent forum.

**Precedent treatment:** The Court corrects the Fourth Circuit's discretionary description while preserving the district court's §1367(a) ground; no earlier Supreme Court decision is displaced.

### 2. This objective support criterion is constitutional at the demonstrated burden

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may allocate this limited party-expense benefit according to an objective five-percent registered-support criterion where lawfully collected fees, continued candidate access, unrestricted private political spending and the demonstrated record establish no severe associational burden, and the measure reasonably serves stable public participation. Under the Illinois Election Code, including 10 Ill. Comp. Stat. 5/7-10 and 5/10-2, more than five percent in the last gubernatorial election establishes a party generally; more than five percent in specified other statewide races establishes statewide status; more than five percent in a district or political subdivision establishes status there. The Libertarian Party's combined 1994 trustee vote was 5.5 percent, but its gubernatorial vote was 1.68 percent and it had no qualifying district result. Its trustee-based statewide status therefore did not establish congressional-district status.

Established-party primary petitions require 0.5 percent of the relevant party's qualified primary electorate. New-party general-election petitions require, for statewide office, the lesser of 25,000 signatures or one percent of voters in the last statewide general election; for a district, the lesser of five percent of voters in the last regular district election or that statewide cap. The separate postdecennial-redistricting exception uses 5,000 district signatures; a distinct 600-signature established-party exception is not at issue. The complete-slate condition operates within the relevant subdivision, not as a requirement to run congressional candidates with a slate for every statewide office. The primary and general-election routes use different denominators and perform different functions.

The normal district new-party requirement averaged about 7,610 signatures, and about 9,337 in the seven plaintiff districts; the established-party primary averages were about 658 Democratic and 532 Republican signatures. Two third-party congressional candidates qualified in 1994. The 1996 count of fourteen accepted petitions, ten Libertarian, does not establish full compliance: several apparently deficient petitions were accepted without objection. Six 1992 qualifiers used the distinct redistricting exception. None of these counts conclusively establishes that every normal requirement was satisfied or always reasonable.

The rejected December 1995 submissions sought the 1996 primary. Their apparent insufficiency even under established-party requirements supplies no independently adjudicated standing ground. The completed primary is not reopened, but the recurring classification and access controversy remains. The published record establishes no supported genuine dispute of an impossible combined signature, slate and deadline burden requiring trial.

## Holdings

### 1. The recurring access controversy remains reviewable

**Holding and operative rule:** Completion of the 1996 primary does not end this recurring challenge to continuing party classifications and nomination requirements. The Court reaches the access questions without reopening that primary or adopting apparent petition insufficiency as an independent standing ground.

**Authority:** Souter's Part I has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela also joins the recurring-controversy and no-reopened-election conclusions, without an additional petition-sufficiency rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Norman v. Reed preserves review of recurring election conditions that otherwise escape decision within a completed election cycle. These classifications continue to govern the party's congressional route, so the passage of the particular primary does not resolve the dispute. The Court does not independently adjudicate standing on a new assessment of the rejected sheets, or convert acceptance of other deficient petitions into proof of legal compliance. Review permits deciding the continuing rule; it does not require undoing a completed election.

**Precedent treatment:** Norman applies at its election-continuity scope without ordering a new election.

### 2. The actual congressional access system survives this limited challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Anderson and Burdick, Illinois may require meaningful support in the relevant congressional electorate and use different primary and general-election nomination routes where the complete actual system leaves a reasonable available route to the general ballot. On this record, trustee-based statewide status does not compel district-established status, and the combined requirements do not establish the severe or unjustified burden asserted; the holding creates no categorical safe harbor for percentages, slates or cumulative deadlines.

**Authority:** Souter's Part II is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson v. Celebrezze requires assessment of the injury's character and magnitude, the State's precise interests and their need for the burden. Burdick v. Takushi requires narrow fit to a compelling interest for severe burdens and ordinarily permits reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens justified by important regulatory interests. The inquiry considers the whole system, not a nominal alternative alone.

Norman v. Reed permits distinct-electorate support requirements while forbidding party-name barriers and loss of independently qualified components because another electorate failed. Statewide trustee support does not itself show congressional-district support. The different denominators and stages make the petition comparison more than a simple tenfold disparity. Jenness v. Fortson permits meaningful support requirements in an actual workable system; Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party likewise examines the available nomination and advocacy alternatives. Here the capped general-ballot petition route supplies an available alternative without an automatic constitutional right to a public party primary.

The substantial numerical difference and combined conditions deserve examination. The 1994 successes are relevant but not conclusive; disputed 1996 acceptances cannot prove full compliance, and the exceptional 1992 route cannot establish ordinary-year accessibility. No supported genuine dispute of the asserted impossible cumulative burden requires trial. Nor does the record establish Norman's disqualification of an independently qualified component through another electorate's failure. The strongest contrary argument requires a demonstrated heavier cumulative burden or automatic transfer of statewide status that this record does not support. Literal impossibility is not a universal prerequisite to a constitutional claim. Incumbent preservation is no independent justification, and a properly supported future burden challenge remains available.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Anderson applies the actual-burden, precise-interest and necessity inquiry.
- Burdick preserves complete-system review and the severe-burden boundary; its write-in presumption is not generalized.
- Norman preserves electorate-specific support and independently qualified components; it creates no automatic statewide-to-district status.
- Jenness permits a meaningful demonstration of support, not universal approval of five-percent petitions.
- Timmons applies proportionate regulation within actual alternatives, not an incumbent-protection interest or categorical approval of every nomination rule.

### 3. Genuine nomination procedures do not add a personal congressional qualification

**Holding and operative rule:** These genuine support and nomination requirements regulate access procedure rather than adding a personal qualification for service in Congress, so the distinct Qualifications Clause challenge fails. Article I's exclusive qualifications remain binding; a substantive personal exclusion cannot escape that rule merely by being labeled ballot administration.

**Authority:** All eight Associates directly join Part III.B's genuine-procedure conclusion. Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer additionally join Part III.A's application of the exclusive-qualifications premise, providing five direct votes for that explanation. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas do not join III.A. Stone-Zsela supports the claim's rejection without adding a ground; his vote is not counted for either rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton distinguishes substantive personal eligibility from genuine regulation of the time, place and manner of elections. Its rule prevents a State from withholding ordinary ballot access from an otherwise qualified congressional candidate because of a prohibited additional personal qualification. The requirements here concern demonstrated electoral support and nomination, not a candidate's personal eligibility to serve. That procedural classification answers this challenge, while the association analysis separately examines whether the actual burden is constitutionally justified. Calling a restriction procedural does not immunize severe access barriers or disguised personal exclusions. The five Justices joining III.A apply Thornton's controlling exclusivity premise; the three joining III.B alone need not abandon their disagreement with that premise to reject a challenge to genuine procedures.

**Precedent treatment:** Thornton is applied within its substantive-versus-procedural boundary; its existing six-Justice authority is not reconstructed from today's distinct votes.

### 4. The judgment supplies no replacement election system

**Holding and operative rule:** The access judgment is affirmed and the distinct Qualifications Clause claim rejected without reopening the primary, declaring automatic district-established status, substituting a signature number or granting a new statewide qualification. Independently qualified components retain Norman's protection, and supported challenges to different actual cumulative burdens remain open.

**Authority:** Souter's Part IV has all eight Associates on the affirmance and its grounds; Stone-Zsela agrees only with rejection of the distinct Qualifications Clause claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The supported record permits disposition of the actual claims without inventing a genuine factual dispute or designing an alternative nomination scheme. The Court's conclusion does not validate every complete-slate condition, deadline combination or future application. Nor does it decide the separate equal-protection claim that was not pleaded. A later claim must establish its actual injury, applicable interests and remedy rather than treating this bounded affirmance or isolated successful qualification as conclusive.

**Precedent treatment:** Norman retains its independently qualified-component rule and bounded election remedy; neither is displaced by this affirmance.

## Precedent Treatment

- Norman v. Reed: applies recurring-election review and separate-electorate support, preserves party-name expansion and independently qualified components, and distinguishes the cross-component exclusion not established here.
- Anderson v. Celebrezze: applies the character and magnitude of actual burden, precise interest and necessity inquiry.
- Burdick v. Takushi: applies complete-system scrutiny without making a nominal route or numerical label conclusive.
- Jenness v. Fortson: permits meaningful support requirements in their actual setting, not every five-percent rule.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party: applies its examination of meaningful remaining alternatives and proportionate administration; it authorizes no independent protection of established competitors.
- U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton: preserves exclusive substantive congressional qualifications and genuine procedural administration, with its actual prior authority and today's separate rationale joins.

## Law After Decision

Illinois's challenged congressional access route survives on the actual record, with statewide and district support kept distinct and the general-ballot alternative assessed as part of the complete system. Neither party status, a percentage label nor isolated past success alone decides constitutionality. The Qualifications Clause does not invalidate these genuine nomination procedures; it continues to forbid disguised additional personal qualifications. Norman's protection for independently qualified components and supported future cumulative-burden challenges remain available.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Stone-Zsela agrees to reach the recurring controversy and would not reopen the completed election. He would reverse the access judgment because the combination of gubernatorial-vote recognition, district new-party classification, heavier general-election petitions and associated slate requirements unjustifiably favors established organizations. Anderson and Burdick require justification of the combined injury, not just the existence of some route. Jenness permits a workable five-percent requirement in context; Timmons sustained its narrower rule because meaningful participation alternatives remained.

The primary and general-election denominators differ, so a straight ten-to-one calculation does not prove his conclusion. Nor does statewide trustee success automatically establish every district. His objection is that the classification and accumulated requirements impose an inadequately justified comparative burden; literal impossibility is unnecessary. Two 1994 successes show possibility, not equality of opportunity. Norman requires respect for independently qualified candidacies while permitting reasonable district support. His criticism of slate-linked burdens concerns the actual relevant-subdivision condition, not a requirement to nominate a complete statewide slate with every congressional candidate or a finding that an independently qualified component has already been excluded here.

He would require a lawful, realistic congressional general-election route. Illinois could narrow the petition requirement, sever an unjustified slate condition or choose another constitutionally adequate route while retaining valid deadlines, genuine district-support demands and protection against electoral confusion. He would not abolish party primaries, automatically recognize a party in every local office from an unrelated statewide result, or put every candidate on the ballot without qualification. The access claim concerns election administration and political participation. On the separate Qualifications Clause challenge, he agrees only in rejection and adds no ground; he does not join either Article I rationale here. His proposed access relief does not command a majority.
