## Standards and Tests selection: Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

### Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

**Governing question:** Proof, discovery and causation when unconstitutional motive is an element of a claim and qualified immunity is asserted.

**Current rule:** Qualified immunity imposes no categorical direct-evidence requirement for unconstitutional motive. The plaintiff must establish the actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation through legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence under ordinary procedural burdens. Specific nonconclusory support, lawful discovery limits and the clearly-established-law defense remain available; the rule abolishes neither all heightened specificity requirements nor a burden supplied by the underlying claim. Ordinary preponderance governs unconstitutional motive in the §1983 retaliation claim; immunity and litigation burden cannot justify a special clear-and-convincing quantum. Courts may decide dispositive legal defenses first and require specific nonconclusory support, but cannot categorically withhold discovery needed to obtain proof until the rejected heightened showing is made. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, sequencing, protective orders, relevant proportional discovery and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available. Under Mt. Healthy the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful explanation does not establish that defense or exclude competent motive evidence.

**Authority by component:**

- Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims: Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; nine votes support the rule and its operative limits.

- Crawford-El v. Britton — Proof of unconstitutional motive, 1997-06-16; Stone-Zsela, all nine
- Crawford-El v. Britton — Discovery and stage-specific screening, 1997-06-16; all nine
- Crawford-El v. Britton — Actual causation and independent defenses, 1997-06-16; all nine

**Present operation effective:** Crawford-El, 1997-06-16, rejecting a special proof quantum and derivative categorical discovery barrier while preserving the carried direct/circumstantial-evidence rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims. Crawford-El v. Britton (1997-06-16) — Civil Rights / Qualified immunity and motive evidence.

## Standards and Tests selection: Effective prisoner court access and demonstrated obstruction

### Effective prisoner court access and demonstrated obstruction

**Governing question:** The substance and remedial scope of a Bounds access claim.

**Current rule:** A prison must provide a meaningful practical opportunity for court access through effective libraries, trained assistance or other effective arrangements assessed as a whole; books alone do not suffice for prisoners unable to use them without needed help. Access liability requires actual or evidence-supported imminent hindrance of a qualifying nonfrivolous legal claim, subject to restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests. The prisoner need not win the obstructed case or complete an impossible filing; an abstract resource deficiency or preferred staffing plan is insufficient. Direct and collateral sentence challenges and prison-conditions claims qualify, and genuine state-created-liberty claims remain possible under Sandin's actual rule.

Common relief requires a demonstrated common barrier and identified affected group, with supported geographic and functional reach; every member need not already have lost a lawsuit. Relief must fit the proved wrong, respect valid security restrictions and give the State a genuine opportunity to design an effective cure. Necessary implementation may be ordered without treating each program measure as an independent constitutional entitlement.

**Authority by component:**

- Effective arrangements: Lewis v. Casey — Holding I, June 24, 1996; Kennedy Part I for all nine.
- Substantive nonfrivolous-claim hindrance: Holding II, Kennedy for Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and himself (five).
- Supported common relief, remedial fit, security and State design: Holding III, Kennedy for all except Stevens (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Lewis v. Casey, June 24, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** There is no entitlement to a preferred library, physical browsing in every case, counsel for every prisoner or numerical program levels. Ordinary personal injury, causation and stage-appropriate proof remain; the additional preliminary Article III merits screen has only four votes. No categorical exclusion of every other important retained right or cessation of every assistance duty upon filing is adopted; independent communications and petition claims retain their own predicates. A valid security restriction does not become unconstitutional merely because delay causes an access injury, but an unsupported security assertion proves no validity.

**Related current rules:** Prison-conditions prospective relief applies the original §3626 requirements to any lawful remedy.

**Holdings navigation:** Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Political retaliation in existing government service relationships

### Political retaliation in existing government service relationships

**Governing question:** Termination, nonrenewal or withdrawal of an existing independent-contractor or recurring service-provider relationship for citizen speech or partisan allegiance.

**Current rule:** Government may not terminate or refuse to renew an existing contractor relationship because of private-citizen speech on a matter of public concern when the speaker's and public's interests outweigh legitimate contracting and operational interests. Apply Connick's content, form and context inquiry and Pickering's balance adjusted to the actual service relationship, including genuine performance, efficiency, cost, trust and disruption concerns; independence, other clients, contacts and responsibilities may matter in either direction. Neither contractor status, termination authority, lack of property entitlement nor absence of direct governmental purchase categorically defeats protection. Private delivery alone does not remove qualifying expression.

For withdrawal based on refusing partisan support or supporting an opponent, apply the distinct Elrod–Branti rule: government must demonstrate that political affiliation is appropriate for effective performance of the actual work. Labels, general patronage advantages and dislike of opposition do not suffice; genuinely policymaking or confidential functions may justify the qualification. Public-concern speech and materially mixed speech/affiliation claims require their appropriate service-adjusted inquiry rather than an undifferentiated affiliation or management rule.

The claimant must show protected conduct was a substantial or motivating factor. Government may defeat the claim by proving by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred absent that conduct; a merely possible lawful reason does not establish the defense. Entity attribution, personal defenses, injury and relief retain their separate requirements.

**Authority by component:**

- Existing-contractor expression and the causation sequence: Protected expression in an existing contractual relationship and Causation, the same-decision defense and the limited remand, 1996-06-28; Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).
- Recurring-provider affiliation, mixed claims and causation: Political affiliation in an existing recurring service relationship, Distinguishing affiliation demands from speech disputes and Causation, defenses and relief, 1996-06-28; Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Umbehr and O’Hare, 1996-06-28; uncoordinated same-day decisions combined prospectively.

**Open question:** Initial unsuccessful bidders, every future award and general employee status for unrelated purposes are not decided. No comprehensive factual-error or investigation framework is adopted.

**Related current rules:** “Public-employee speech and the employer’s mistaken factual account” retains its limited genuine-ground rules; “Prospective restrictions on employees’ citizen expression” governs its distinct broad advance restraint.

**Holdings navigation:** Board of County Commissioners, Wabaunsee County v. Umbehr, 518 U.S. 668 (1996); O’Hare Truck Service, Inc. v. City of Northlake, 518 U.S. 712 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Confidential psychotherapy communications under Rule 501

### Confidential psychotherapy communications under Rule 501

**Governing question:** Federal common-law protection for confidential treatment communications with licensed psychotherapists.

**Current rule:** Confidential patient communications to a licensed psychotherapist in diagnosis or treatment, including actual confidential psychotherapy by a licensed clinical social worker, are privileged under federal common law. The protection covers treatment conversations and counseling notes, but title alone does not protect administrative exchanges, ordinary workplace counseling or communications outside confidential treatment. Protection does not turn on routine lawsuit-specific balancing of the opponent’s evidentiary need; a valid invocation cannot justify an adverse inference or instruction treating withholding as legally unjustified. Waiver remains possible, but no particular waiver rule is established.

**Authority by component:**

- Protected relationship and social-worker inclusion: I. Confidential psychotherapy communications are privileged; no routine balancing and no adverse inference: II. Protection does not depend on routine balancing in each lawsuit, 1996-06-13. Stevens with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight) on each holding.

**Present operation effective:** Jaffee, 1996-06-13.

**Exceptions and limits:** Rule 501’s Constitution, congressional and authorized-rule boundaries and its separate state-law sentence remain. No general physician-patient privilege follows. Conflict for evidence relevant to federal and state claims, court-ordered examinations, litigation use, constitutional criminal overrides and serious threats remain unadjudicated. The seven-Justice serious-threat illustration is not an operative exception.

**Holdings navigation:** Jaffee v. Redmond, 518 U.S. 1.

## Authority source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md — Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44

#### Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-12.
**Decided:** March 27, 1996.

##### IGRA's binding good-faith adjudication is judicial despite later statutory steps

**Holding:** Adjudication of IGRA's federal good-faith negotiation dispute is a judicial function when its judgment conclusively determines legal relations and activates constrained statutory consequences. Later mediation and executive action, and the absence of a guaranteed compact, do not make that judgment advisory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Stevens's serious doubt and Stone-Zsela's nonjoin supply no additional support.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aetna and Nashville permit conclusive adverse declarations without an immediately executable coercive decree. Brimson supports judicial assistance to administration within its adversarial, conclusive, enforceable-order setting, without creating IGRA contempt power. Ferreira's revisable commissioner process is distinguished. Gordon's jurisdictional disposition supplies no delivered reasoned majority opinion requiring execution in every judicial case; Hayburn produced no Supreme Court merits decision, and its reported circuit communications remain historical materials. Wisconsin's partial-redress rule concerns the judicial capacity to afford meaningful relief. Morgan Stanley and Plaut: Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement of qualifying completed private securities limitations dismissals under §27A(b)'s objectively defined procedure while courts independently determine eligibility and adjudicate the claims. The holdings do not establish an unrestricted power to reopen completed merits judgments and do not determine whether IGRA's conclusive adjudication ceases to be judicial because mediation or executive action follows.

##### A connected officer may be sued prospectively under IGRA within its complete statutory limits

**Holding:** Ex parte Young permits prospective official-capacity relief against Governor Chiles for an ongoing violation of IGRA's good-faith negotiation duty, subject to IGRA's full sequence and remedial limits. The State-directed wording and detailed procedure do not exclude that route in this case; they do not authorize greater remedies through the officer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. No additional general clear-indication theory commands that coalition.

**Material application:** The Governor's representatives negotiated, supplying connection to the alleged violation without establishing that he alone can bind Florida to every term. Mandatory good faith differs from discretion over compact terms, and nonagreement alone does not prove bad faith.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A qualifying request by the Tribe with jurisdiction over the Indian lands triggers §2710(d)(3)(A)'s good-faith duty; suit under (d)(7)(A)(i) may begin only after 180 days. The effective-compact enforcement action under (A)(ii) and Secretary action under (A)(iii) remain separate. Tribal evidence of no compact and failure to respond or respond in good faith precedes the State's good-faith burden under (B)(ii).

An actual adverse finding triggers an order to State and Tribe to conclude a compact within the first sixty days. The court may consider public interest, safety, criminality, financial integrity and adverse economic effects on existing gaming, and shall treat direct-tax demands on the Tribe or Indian lands as evidence of bad faith. Failure to agree during that period requires last-best proposals to a court-appointed mediator, who selects the proposal best comporting with IGRA, other applicable federal law and the findings and order. Submission to both sides begins a second sixty-day period for State consent; consent produces an agreed compact. Nonconsent triggers notice to the Secretary, who shall prescribe procedures in consultation with the Tribe consistently with the selected proposal, IGRA and relevant state law. The findings/order criterion expressly appears in selection under (iv), not verbatim in the Secretary clause (vii).

State nonparticipation does not automatically select the Tribe's proposal. Failure to agree or participate during the ordered period produces statutory progression, not added contempt or an order compelling agreement; every separate obstruction sanction is not decided. Section (d)(4) creates no new taxing authority but preserves agreed regulatory-cost assessments under (d)(3)(C)(iii), separately from direct-tax demands' evidentiary effect. Absence of other taxing authority does not justify refusing negotiation.

Class III gaming still requires approved tribal legislation, a State permitting such gaming for some person, organization or entity, and an effective compact subject to applicable remedial provisions; licensing and regulation remain distinct. Under (d)(8), disapproval may rest on IGRA, other federal law not relating to jurisdiction over gaming on Indian lands, or federal trust obligations. Forty-five-day inaction means approval only insofar as consistent with IGRA, and Federal Register publication remains necessary. Section 2721 severability supplies neither missing predicates nor an alternative program.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical Governor dismissal, 5–4, without finding bad faith or granting a compact, mediator, default proposal, Secretary procedures, damages, game-scope declaration or license. The separate September 22, 1993 merits judgment and stayed appeal No. 93-5256 receive their own lawful treatment and are not vacated. The asserted Secretary substitute remedy, independent administrative authority, complete state-law compacting power, Tenth Amendment defense and mediator-appointments question remain unadjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Young reaches this connected officer and ongoing federal duty without a universal rule for detailed statutes. Edelman's retrospective-compensation boundary remains; prospective costs alone do not establish retrospective relief. Pennhurst is not triggered merely because the federal obligation incorporates state gaming law. Hafer's personal-capacity damages rule does not supply this action. Puerto Rico Aqueduct supplies appealability, not immunity. New York and Voting Rights Coalition do not decide the reserved IGRA constitutional defenses; X-Citement Video preserves appropriate respondent defenses without compelling their resolution here.

##### State amenability lacks a controlling rationale, despite agreement on direct-State statutory intent

**Authority of the judgment:** Florida's immunity-dismissal direction is set aside and remanded by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, 5–4. Stone-Zsela and Stevens would sustain direct-State amenability; Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate without deciding it. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm. No controlling State-amenability rationale exists under Marks: avoidance is not a logical subset of the affirmative constitutional grounds, and three unused conditional alternatives are not actual abrogation joins.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** IGRA unmistakably authorizes an action naming the State through its State-party action, evidentiary burden, State-and-Tribe order and successive State-directed steps. General federal-question jurisdiction, §1362 or scattered State references alone would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Six actual positions across the separate writings: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The other three's conditional acceptance is not counted. This agreement concerns statutory intent alone.

**Current force:** The clear-statement proposition controls; no majority sustains or rejects Florida's constitutional amenability or requires a particular avoidance sequence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State-removal judgment establishes neither jurisdiction nor its absence; lawful jurisdiction is necessary before any State merits adjudication. The State claim is not terminated by immunity, and no abandonment, mootness or indefinite stay is presumed. No express or constructive waiver, reciprocal tribal/state consent or delegated United States immunity exemption establishes this suit. The United States appears only as amicus.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Union Gas is not overruled: its damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion retain their decided force without making every plurality sentence binding. Hans remains effective; separate common-law theories do not become law. Fitzpatrick's distinct Fourteenth Amendment authority is unchanged.

##### Whether a state officer sued individually is a section 1983 person for acts undertaken through official authority

**Holding:** A state officer sued in an individual capacity is a person under 42 U.S.C. §1983 even when the challenged act was undertaken through official authority, and state sovereign immunity does not independently bar a judgment payable by that officer personally. **Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit is affirmed. The individual-capacity claims may proceed; no damages or reinstatement are awarded by this decision.

Michigan Department of State Police is clarified and preserved for States and official-capacity damages defendants. Scheuer v. Rhodes confirms possible personal liability of executive officers; Monroe v. Pape supplies the under-color-of-law rule for misuse of official power. Ex parte Young's prospective-relief channel is unchanged.

## Authority source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md — Crawford-El v. Britton

#### Crawford-El v. Britton

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 94-7203; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-16.

##### Ordinary proof governs unconstitutional motive in this retaliation claim

**Holding:** The ordinary preponderance standard governs proof of unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation claim; neither qualified immunity nor litigation-burden concerns authorize a special clear-and-convincing requirement. Legally competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available, and all actual constitutional elements and independent defenses must still be established or resolved under their applicable burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides the quantum Kimberlin reserved; it neither abolishes every specificity practice nor resolves Leatherman’s individual-officer pleading reservation beyond the barriers decided here. A motive allegation alone defeats no constitutional element, ordinary procedure or conduct-date immunity defense.

##### Necessary discovery cannot be barred by the rejected heightened proof requirement

**Holding:** A court may resolve a genuinely dispositive legal defense first and require specific, nonconclusory support, but it may not categorically demand proof satisfying the rejected heightened quantum before allowing discovery necessary to obtain that proof. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, relevant and proportionate discovery limits, sequencing, protective orders and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available; no unrestricted discovery or particular deposition is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court may clarify allegations, resolve actual dispositive legal defenses and limit discovery to material issues. An adequately supported summary-judgment motion requires responsive evidence; a general credibility attack is insufficient. No fixed discovery plan, particular deposition, unrestricted inquiry, mandatory trial or new interlocutory appeal follows.

##### An actual same-decision showing remains distinct from a conceivable lawful explanation

**Holding:** A merely possible lawful explanation for an official's conduct does not itself establish a same-decision defense or make competent proof of unconstitutional motive inadmissible. Under Mt. Healthy, the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct. The separate clearly-established-law defense remains available on the law governing the conduct when it occurred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Protected communication, personal responsibility, actual motive, causation, injury and conduct-date immunity remain unresolved. Later Kimberlin and Settle doctrine supplies no notice in 1989. Prison-access limits remain and no unrestricted press-conference entitlement is recognized.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the heightened motive burden and dependent directions; preserve the existing remand otherwise. Resolved access-to-courts and due-process claims are not revived. The municipal issue retains its separate panel posture; the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits. No liability, immunity, damages or trial entitlement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kimberlin v. Quinlan: applies the distinction between motive as a constitutional element and objective immunity, competent circumstantial proof and lawful case management; decides here the proof quantum Kimberlin left open.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald: retains the objective clearly-established-law defense and early legal screening; it supplies no independent heightened evidentiary code.
- Anderson v. Creighton: retains particularized conduct-date notice and appropriately tailored discovery; no ultimate immunity determination is made.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County: uses its source-based municipal pleading reasoning by analogy while preserving its express individual-officer reservation outside the specific categorical barriers resolved here.
- Siegert v. Gilley: preserves early examination of a cognizable constitutional deprivation without converting its threshold holding into a special motive burden.
- Anderson v. Liberty Lobby: applies a valid governing substantive burden at summary judgment, without authorizing courts to invent a new one.
- New York Times Co. v. Sullivan: distinguishes the constitutional speech basis of its defamation burden from this retaliation action against an official.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle: applies the claimant's protected-conduct and motivating-factor burden and the defendant's separate preponderance same-decision defense.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board: applies its actual-causation distinction, not school-specific substantive rules; a possible lawful reason is insufficient by itself.
- Pell v. Procunier and Turner v. Safley: preserve prison-access limitations and supply no unrestricted press-conference right.
- Johnson v. Jones and Behrens v. Pelletier: preserve the distinction between legal immunity review and disputed factual sufficiency; no new interlocutory-review right is created.

## Authority source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md — Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343 (1996)

#### Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1511.
**Decided:** June 24, 1996.

##### Whether books alone satisfy the right of meaningful access

**Holding:** Bounds requires a meaningful practical opportunity for court access through effective libraries, trained assistance or other effective arrangements considered as a whole; adequate books do not discharge that duty for prisoners unable to use them without needed assistance. It guarantees neither a preferred library program, physical browsing in every case, professional counsel for every prisoner nor the decree's numerical requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** Bartholic’s dismissal and Harris’s inability to file establish actual obstruction at their supported scope. An inventory-based paging system with prompt delivery may work for prisoners capable of independent use; that conditional possibility proves neither Arizona’s actual adequacy nor adequacy for differing literacy or language needs.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No fixed number of all injured prisoners, right to ultimate success, constitutional library specification or automatic appointed counsel is established.

##### What connection to a legal claim is required for access liability

**Holding:** A Bounds access-liability claim requires actual or evidence-supported imminent hindrance of a qualifying, nonfrivolous legal claim, subject to the validity of restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests. The prisoner need not win the obstructed case or complete an impossible filing, but a deficient inventory, preferred staffing arrangement or abstract wish for better research does not alone establish the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices.

**Material application:** Technical dismissal, inability to file a contemplated qualifying claim or sufficiently concrete threatened obstruction can supply the connection. A procedural defect does not itself make the underlying claim substantively frivolous. Evidence and reasonable inferences may prove the real claim and obstacle before avoidable filing failure.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The additional Article III preliminary-merits screen, subgroup-standing requirement and categorical sentence/conditions and initial-filing restrictions have only four votes. Ordinary Lujan injury, causation and stage-appropriate proof remain. Genuine state-created-liberty claims remain possible under Sandin; no categorical exclusion of every other retained right, universal end of assistance upon filing or general private-litigation subsidy is adopted. Independent communications and petition claims retain their own predicates.

##### What proof and governmental role support classwide prison-access relief

**Holding:** Common relief must correspond to a demonstrated common barrier and an identified affected group; individual obstruction or a resource deficiency alone does not establish an unrestricted statewide violation. Relief must respect restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests and give the State a genuine opportunity to devise an effective cure, while permitting measures actually necessary to repair the proved wrong.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** Common evidence may prove a common barrier without proof that every class member has already lost a suit, but geographical and functional reach require support. The actual findings do not establish a denial at every facility or independent broad entitlements for all non-English-speaking or lockdown prisoners. Opportunity to rebut a presumptive Gluth statewide template did not supply Arizona’s genuine initial remedial design role.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Turner requires examination of the actual reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests with appropriate deference; injury alone does not invalidate a valid restriction, and merely asserting security does not establish validity. Jenkins permits necessary repair measures without making every remedial detail a freestanding constitutional right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Unsupported classwide approval set aside and remanded, 8–1; Stevens accepts a narrower remedial remand. Established obstruction, supported findings and common evidence remain. No unrestricted statewide violation, automatic dismissal or fixed library, telephone, staffing or resource minimum.

##### How original PLRA §802 governs this existing prospective decree and any replacement

**Holding:** Section 802 applies amended section 3626 to prospective relief granted before, on or after April 26, 1996; relief here requires findings that it is narrowly drawn, extends no further than necessary to correct the federal-right violation of the particular plaintiffs, and is the least intrusive means necessary, with substantial weight given to adverse effects on public safety or criminal-justice-system operation. Relief exceeding official state-law authority or otherwise violating state or local law additionally requires federal permission, necessity to correct the federal violation, and absence of any other corrective relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part IV, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only with the statutory remand disposition.

**Material application:** Amended §3626 applies to relief granted before, on or after April 26, 1996. Prospective relief means relief other than compensatory monetary damages. This prison-conditions action is not excluded habeas merely because access assistance facilitates habeas. Existing findings may satisfy actual predicates without ritual wording; reasonable cost or absence of hardship does not substitute for need, narrowness and least intrusiveness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3626(a)(1)(B)’s federal-permission, necessity and no-other-relief conditions govern state/local-law conflicts separately from (A). Subsection (C) supplies no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves existing limits. New preliminary relief must be otherwise authorized and satisfy narrowness, need, least intrusiveness, safety and the separate state-law terms; it expires ninety days after entry unless the required prospective findings are made and the order made final before expiration. The former permanent decree is not automatically a new preliminary order. No constitutional challenge to §802 or prisoner-release remedy is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Prospective-decree approval set aside and remanded, 9–0 in disposition, eight on statutory reasoning. Actual findings, alternatives, improvements and security must be considered; the decree is neither automatically retained nor dissolved.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Plyler v. Moore, Fourth Circuit No. 96-6884, 1997-06-23, resolves the reserved Article III challenge to §3626(b)(2) only with the original (b)(3) current-or-ongoing safeguard; see Constitutional Structure — Congressional amendment of law governing prospective relief.

##### When termination and an automatic stay arise under the original §3626

#### Holdings and Controlling Reasons

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion controls on each rule below, joined by all eight judgment supporters. The future-contours illustration is not necessary to any holding.

##### I. Confidential psychotherapy communications are privileged

**Operative rule:** Federal common law protects confidential communications between a patient and a licensed psychotherapist in the course of diagnosis or treatment, including confidential psychotherapy provided by a licensed clinical social worker. The protection covers the treatment conversations and counseling notes at issue; a professional title alone does not protect administrative conversations, ordinary workplace counseling or communications outside a confidential treatment relationship.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 501 directs courts to interpret common-law privilege through reason and experience, subject to the Constitution, Acts of Congress and rules prescribed under statutory authority. Trammel supplies the responsibility to examine a privilege's present justification; University of Pennsylvania requires a defensible benefit and boundary rather than an assumption that secrecy is always useful. Effective psychotherapy depends on frank disclosure, which predictable confidentiality makes possible. Its benefit extends beyond the patient to the treatment of mental illness.

Widespread state recognition corroborates that judgment, without proving that every State uses identical definitions or exceptions. Proposed Rule 504 reflects considered professional judgment but was not enacted and does not govern. The loss of relevant evidence is serious, especially in a dispute about a killing. Rule 501 nevertheless permits a defined relationship privilege where its public function justifies that cost.

Beyer's license, training and actual psychotherapy bring this treatment within the rule. Clinical social workers perform the same protected function and provide access for patients unable to afford other providers; the record supplies no confidentiality distinction warranting exclusion of this relationship. The holding creates no general physician-patient privilege or privilege for every conversation with a social worker.

Rule 501's separate state-law sentence remains operative for an element of a civil claim or defense governed by state law. Recognition here makes it unnecessary to decide which privilege law would govern evidence relevant to both federal and state claims if the two laws conflict. The Court adopts no general federal-preemption rule for that situation.

##### II. Protection does not depend on routine balancing in each lawsuit

**Operative rule:** A communication within this privilege does not lose protection merely because a judge finds the opposing party's evidentiary need more important in the particular lawsuit. A valid assertion of the privilege cannot support the adverse inference and lack-of-legal-justification instruction given here.

**Controlling explanation:** A patient must know the protection's character when speaking. If disclosure turns on an unpredictable later assessment of usefulness, the uncertainty can deter the very communication that makes treatment effective. The Seventh Circuit's reliance on the availability of other eyewitnesses cannot establish a general balancing test within the privilege.

United States v. Nixon concerns a presumptive executive privilege and demonstrated criminal-trial need; it does not require routine balancing inside every recognized confidential relationship. Daubert and Williamson concern admissibility and reliability under different evidence rules, not this relationship's protection under Rule 501.

Rejecting general balancing does not make the privilege exceptionless. A patient may waive it. The account of partial responses and three redacted pages supplies no adjudicated waiver of all treatment communications; whether any waiver occurred remains undecided. The Court decides no waiver rule, court-ordered-examination rule, litigation-use exception, criminal constitutional override or serious-threat dispute. No adverse inference may rest on a valid assertion of the protected core. Because the actual instruction penalized withholding protected treatment material, the appellate new-trial judgment stands without its balancing rationale.

**Reserved contours in the Court's opinion.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer observe that the privilege must yield in a hypothetical situation where a serious threat to the patient or others can be averted only by therapist disclosure. No such threat is found here; the illustration decides no operative exception, procedure or ordinary evidentiary-need claim. Stone-Zsela does not join that affirmative illustration. All eight leave the actual boundaries for a case presenting them.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Trammel v. United States, 445 U.S. 40:** Applied for reason-and-experience development of privilege with attention to present function and evidentiary cost; its distinct marital rule is unchanged.
- **University of Pennsylvania v. EEOC, 493 U.S. 182:** Distinguished on the proposed relationship and demonstrated justification. Its rejection of an unsupported peer-review privilege does not foreclose this defined treatment privilege.
- **United States v. Nixon, 418 U.S. 683:** Distinguished as executive privilege in criminal process; it supplies no universal case-by-case balancing requirement for confidential treatment.
- **Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals:** Its admissibility and reliability rules remain intact and do not decide Rule 501 privilege.
- **Williamson v. United States:** Its admissibility and reliability rules remain intact and do not decide Rule 501 privilege.

#### Law After Decision

Federal common law now protects the defined confidential psychotherapy relationship, including licensed clinical social-work psychotherapy. Evidentiary usefulness alone does not defeat the privilege, and valid withholding cannot be penalized by the instruction used here. State-law privilege obligations under Rule 501 remain distinct. Waiver and other particular limits require their own supported questions; this decision supplies no comprehensive code of exceptions and overrules no earlier decision.

#### Separate Writings

**Scalia, dissenting.** Rule 501 authorizes common-law development, but the dissent finds the asserted benefit insufficient to justify withholding reliable evidence about disputed conduct. The possibility that confidentiality assists treatment does not by itself establish how much this evidentiary privilege adds to ordinary professional secrecy, while lost evidence directly impairs the adjudicative task. University of Pennsylvania supplies the need for a supported justification, not a categorical prohibition on creating privileges.

The dissent also disputes the professional boundary. Proposed Rule 504 did not include social workers and was never enacted; differences in training, functions and state exceptions caution against treating all such treatment as one established category. Scalia would reverse the federal-privilege basis for the new-trial judgment, leaving unresolved matters in their proper channels. He does not find wholesale waiver or hold that every note must be disclosed, and his objections create no controlling balancing alternative.
