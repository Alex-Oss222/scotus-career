#### Walmer v. Department of Defense

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit No. 93-3377.

**Decided:** January 13, 1997.

For the other questions in Walmer v. Department of Defense, Tenth Circuit No. 93-3377, decided January 13, 1997, see Civil Rights - Military personnel and equal protection (Ordinary rationality sustains this preliminary denial on the stipulated conduct record).

##### The attainder prohibition does not extend to this executive military regulation

**Holding:** The Bill of Attainder Clause restrains legislative punishment, and the challenged executive Army regulation does not satisfy that legislative-action predicate. The attainder ground therefore fails without deciding whether the separation would constitute punishment if the required legislative predicate existed; other constitutional, statutory and administrative restraints on executive action remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Punishment is not reached; honorable separation is not held conclusively nonpunitive. The abandoned APA theory and unpresented due-process or statutory claims are not revived.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Nixon, Selective Service System and Ambassador Books supply and preserve the legislative-action prerequisite. Their distinct punishment inquiries are neither displaced nor answered for this separation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Unanimously reject the attainder ground for want of legislative action; no punishment determination.

#### Romer v. Evans, 517 U.S. 620

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1039.
**Decided:** May 20, 1996.

##### Withdrawal and constitutional foreclosure of ordinary protection for an identified class

**Holding:** Colorado Amendment 2's combined withdrawal of existing orientation-based nondiscrimination protection and foreclosure of comparable protection through ordinary state and local government violates equal protection under ordinary rational-basis review. Its established class-specific disability lacks a rational relationship to the asserted legitimate interests, without reliance on suspect status, political-process strict scrutiny, elimination of generally applicable protection, or a finding about every voter's motive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The authoritative state construction covers legislative, executive, and judicial channels of the State, agencies, schools, and local government, including public and private settings; it is not merely a quota prohibition or local preemption leaving the legislature free to act. Uniformity, regulatory restraint, finite resources, religious and intimate association, parental teaching, and conduct-based morality do not explain this blanket ineligibility across public and impersonal commercial life. Ordinary review permits supported predictions, imperfect categories, and conceivable grounds without formal findings or empirical certainty; a narrower alternative alone is insufficient. The defect concerns the relationship to the actual disadvantage, rather than an independent hostility finding or mandatory least-restrictive-means inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes, without deciding, that generally applicable criminal, tort, contract, and public-service protections remain. It creates no suspect or quasi-suspect tier, general duty to enact nondiscrimination laws, nationwide code, quota entitlement, universal access to every lawmaking level, or presumption invalidating every group-affecting repeal, referendum, or constitutional policy reservation. Particular religious, intimate-association, and expressive defenses retain their own predicates; no municipal exemption is approved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Beach* preserves conceivable legitimate grounds and reasonable generalizations together with the *Moreno/Cleburne* limits on bare harm and irrational prejudice. *Heller*'s controlling proof-burden decision requires a relationship to the actual disadvantage; its separate family-participation holding preserves relevant generalizations and rejects invalidity based on narrower alternatives alone. *Hurley* protects concrete private expression without making ordinary service categorically expressive association; *Swanner* and *American Life League* retain their particular religious-liberty inquiries. *Bowers* remains effective on its specified-conduct question but does not justify this broader disability. *Adarand* adds no orientation tier, and *Klinger*'s treatment-specific comparison does not import *Armstrong*'s charging-discovery threshold into an express classification.

##### Resolution of the preserved alternative and complete injunctive relief

**Holding:** The fully presented rational-basis alternative permits affirmance without adoption of the lower court's strict-scrutiny rationale. The defect permeates Amendment 2's inseparable common operation, warranting the complete existing injunction without a new merits remand or federal rewriting of state law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Material application:** State severability law treats orientation, conduct, practices, and relationships as descriptions of the same class; deletion of a description leaves the same disability. No independently lawful remainder is established. Failure under strict scrutiny is not itself rational-basis failure, and no unconditional State concession supplies the result. The ability to seek another constitutional amendment does not cure the unequal barrier to ordinary protection.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm, 7–2; the permanent injunction remains effective in full. No enactment, damages, factual inquiry, severance proceeding, or new hearing is ordered. Unappealed suspect-status treatment is not reopened, and independently preserved First Amendment claims are neither decided nor presumed abandoned.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hunter* and *Seattle* retain their racial political-restructuring settings without a new fundamental-right extension. *Valtierra*, *Gordon*, and *Crawford* prevent treating every referendum, neutral voting rule, or policy revision as exclusion. *Dalton* requires relief matched to the violation and preservation of independently lawful operation; no general facial formula, *Miller* abortion method, or *44 Liquormart* empirical burden replaces this analysis.

#### Walmer v. Department of Defense

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit No. 93-3377.

**Decided:** January 13, 1997.

For the other questions in Walmer v. Department of Defense, Tenth Circuit No. 93-3377, decided January 13, 1997, see Constitutional Structure - Legislative punishment and executive regulation (The attainder prohibition does not extend to this executive military regulation).

##### Ordinary rationality sustains this preliminary denial on the stipulated conduct record

**Holding:** The challenged conduct-based military personnel rule receives ordinary rational-basis review, which permits reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable predictions and imperfect fit while requiring a relationship to the actual disadvantage and forbidding bare harm or irrational prejudice. On this preliminary record of admitted homosexual acts and stipulated ineligibility for the retention exceptions, discipline, morale and workable military service relationships supply a permissible predictive relationship, so denial of equal-protection preliminary relief is affirmed without a final ruling on another or materially developed record.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is preliminary review of admitted conduct with stipulated exception ineligibility. The full retention-exception criteria, their validity and application to other claimants are not adjudicated. No final validity declaration, orientation-only inference rule, later-statute ruling, Government actual-purpose burden, individualized-impairment requirement or universal scrutiny tier follows. The other equitable factors were assumed below, not found satisfied.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Beach, both relevant Heller propositions and Romer are applied at their actual ordinary-review scope; Goldman and Rostker supply deference without immunity. Bowers, Kelley and Virginia are distinguished at the questions they decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm denial of preliminary equal-protection relief, 5-4; no injunction, final policy-validity declaration or discharge order.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Thomasson v. Perry, Fourth Circuit No. 95-2185, decided June 9, 1997, resolves the reserved statement-based question only for a conduct-related inference with genuine rebuttal under section 654(b)(2); Walmer’s admitted-conduct preliminary holding is unchanged.

#### Thomasson v. Perry

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 95-2185; review of 80 F.3d 915 (en banc).

**Decided:** 1997-06-09.

For the speech question in Thomasson v. Perry, Fourth Circuit No. 95-2185, decided June 9, 1997, see First Amendment — Military statements as relevant evidence.

##### A genuinely rebuttable conduct inference sustains this statement-based separation

**Holding:** Ordinary rational-basis review permits the Navy to apply 10 U.S.C. §654(b)(2) to Thomasson's voluntary declaration of homosexual identity as a rebuttable indication of prohibited conduct, propensity, or intent, with propensity meaning a likelihood of acts beyond abstract preference or desire and a genuine opportunity to rebut that inference. On this record, the unrebutted inference rationally relates to legitimate military personnel interests and sustains the discharge without proof of a completed prohibited act or individualized operational impairment; it is not a conclusive disqualification for private orientation.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Material application:** The Board considered service history, supporting accounts and expert evidence and found the inference unrebutted. No exclusion of evidence or compelled denial of identity was established. Service excellence and absence of completed prohibited conduct do not automatically negate the predictive premise.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 654(b)(2) directs separation after an approved finding that the member stated that he or she is homosexual or bisexual, or words to that effect, unless a further approved finding establishes that the member has demonstrated that he or she does not engage in, attempt to engage in, have a propensity to engage in, or intend to engage in homosexual acts. Section 654(e) removes the processing obligation when the military determines both that the statement or conduct was intended to avoid or terminate service and that separation would not serve the military's best interest; it permits omission of processing, not a compulsory refusal to process. Those predicates are not established here.

Section 654(b)(1) separately addresses actual, attempted or solicited acts. Its retention exception requires all five findings: departure from usual behavior; unlikely recurrence; no force, coercion or intimidation; continued presence consistent with discipline, good order and morale; and no propensity or intent to engage in homosexual acts. These are not the rebuttal requirements of (b)(2). Section 654(b)(3), concerning marriage or attempted marriage to a person known to be of the same biological sex, is not a ground of this discharge. Neither separate route is adjudicated. The Court does not invalidate the directive's narrowing definition or decide an independent APA claim, procedural due-process claim or substantive-liberty claim.

The holding does not approve an irrebuttable status exclusion, every inference from identity, civilian exclusion, denial of rebuttal or retaliation for advocacy. No heightened orientation tier, actual-purpose proof requirement, individualized-impairment prerequisite or least-restrictive-means rule is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the judgment sustaining discharge, 5–4; no reinstatement, backpay, administrative rehearing or independent APA, procedural-due-process or substantive-liberty ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Walmer supplies ordinary review and military prediction; its reserved statement question is resolved only at this application. Beach Communications, the separate Heller holdings and Romer preserve rational relationship, permissible generalization and the prohibition on bare hostility. Weiss supplies deference with independent review. Virginia’s sex-classification rule and Bowers’s conduct holding decide different questions and supply no automatic result here.

#### United States v. National Treasury Employees Union

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1170.
**Decided:** February 22, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see First Amendment — Public-employee honoraria restrictions (A broad prospective honoraria ban requires justification for its actual speech burden).

##### The existing ban is unenforceable throughout the certified class

**Holding:** Relief against the existing §501(b) prohibition extends throughout the certified class of affected Executive Branch employees below GS–16. The Court declines to create a general employment-nexus qualification by moving the series-specific parenthesis in §505(3) into the entire honorarium definition; this class remedy does not establish that every class payment is unrelated or that a law separately and validly restricting connected payments would fail.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pickering remains the merits framework rather than authority to rewrite statutory coverage. Simon & Schuster and Turner retain their stated financial-burden and evidentiary principles; neither compels a judicially created nexus statute. The remedy preserves the independent force of valid ethics laws and decides no new constitutional rule for congressional or judicial employees.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 505(3)'s series-specific nexus qualification and the travel and charitable-payment conditions are set out with this decision's First Amendment holding. The class remedy does not rewrite those conditions or extend to §501(a)'s distinct outside-income ceiling.

##### The injunction does not adjudicate the claims of unrepresented senior employees

**Holding:** The injunction must be confined to the represented class and the individually represented claim retained here; it may not extend this adjudication to unrepresented Executive employees at GS–16 or above. Removing that nonparty protection does not hold their claims meritless or decide materially different applications of the honoraria ban.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Madsen v. Women's Health Center, June 30, 1994, supplies attention to actual persons, claims and the lawful scope of remedial adjustment; it is applied without importing its different clinic facts or an automatic bar on all broader relief in other cases.

##### Crane retains his individual relief without a grade-only remand

**Holding:** Peter G. Crane's existing individual relief is retained; his GS–16 status and resulting exclusion from the certified class do not alone require its removal or a separate remand. This disposition concerns a named respondent under the Government's alternative respondents-plus-class request, not a general constitutional exemption for senior employees or a substantive concession of Crane's entire merits case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III-B, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices; Stone-Zsela adds a judgment-only sixth vote for Crane's relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Madsen's bounded remedial treatment is applied to distinguish the necessary removal of nonparty protection from an unsupported grade-only reopening of an individual judgment. No new universal appellate-waiver rule or general senior-employee First Amendment exemption is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded. Nonenforcement of the existing ban throughout the certified below-GS–16 class is preserved by five votes; Crane retains individual relief by six; relief for unrepresented senior employees is removed. Congressional and judicial coverage, §501(a)'s outside-income ceiling and valid independent ethics restrictions are not invalidated. No penalty or compensation award is entered.

#### Waters v. Churchill, 511 U.S. 661

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1450.

**Decided:** 1994-05-31.

##### A genuine, reasonable account of disciplinable speech supplies a limited safe harbor

**Authority of the judgment:** The Seventh Circuit's categorical actual-words rule is vacated and the case remanded, 7–2; defendants' summary judgment remains set aside. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg support vacatur. No complete controlling rationale exists under Marks; the express majority propositions below have only their stated scope.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** A public employer does not violate the employee's substantive speech right by disciplining her because of a genuinely held, objectively reasonable account of speech that the Pickering–Connick balance permits it to discipline, even if that account is later shown mistaken. The protection requires actual reliance on the permissible ground; it does not protect a reasonable investigation used to conceal retaliation for different protected speech.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, adopts this sufficient condition; Scalia's concurrence in the judgment, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, expressly accepts it under their broader genuine-ground rule. Seven Justices agree at this precise level; only four adopt the full objective-inquiry framework.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563 (1968): applies the public-employee speech balance, adding this limited factual-error permission.
- Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138 (1983): applies contextual public-concern analysis and reasonable prospective assessment of institutional disruption; personal embarrassment alone is insufficient.
- Givhan v. Western Line Consolidated School District, 439 U.S. 410 (1979): preserves private speech protection and the relevance of manner, time and place.
- Mt. Healthy City Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274 (1977): preserves actual causation; a potentially permissible ground is not proof of the ground actually used.

##### An unreasonable false account cannot defeat actually protected speech

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** Discipline because of an unreasonable and incorrect report of speech that was actually protected violates the substantive speech right when the protected speech bears the required causal relation to the action, subject to distinct causation defenses and remedial immunity. This agreement does not establish liability for every investigative imperfection, for an unreasonable report of actually unprotected speech, or without proof of the underlying protected conduct and causal action.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg accept this proposition under objective factual care; Stevens's dissent, joined by Blackmun, expressly accepts it under their broader actual-speech rule. Six Justices agree on this proposition, but the two dissenters do not join the reasonable-error safe harbor or the judgment vacating the appellate rule.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pickering, Connick and Givhan: preserve the substantive protection of the actual public-concern speech within the employment balance.
- Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): distinguishes a property-based hearing entitlement from this substantive speech claim.
- Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274 (1977): retains the distinct causation and same-decision requirements; unreasonableness alone is not an automatic recovery rule.

##### The motive dispute requires remand without restoring defendants' summary judgment

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** The Seventh Circuit's categorical actual-words rule is vacated, but defendants' summary judgment remains set aside because genuine reliance and retaliation for other protected criticism are materially disputed. On remand the employee must show protected conduct was a substantial or motivating factor; the employer may prove by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred without it, and individual qualified immunity remains available and, if necessary, must be assessed as a distinct defense under the law relevant to the 1987 conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's four-Justice plurality and Scalia's three-Justice concurrence in the judgment support this common vacatur and limited remand. Blackmun and Stevens would affirm the appellate reversal under their different actual-speech rule; their votes are not counted as support for vacatur.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), and Givhan, 439 U.S. 410 (1979): apply the employee's causal showing and the employer's preponderance same-decision defense.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800 (1982): preserves individual qualified immunity as distinct from the substantive speech rule.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: requires considering relevant authority on the preserved immunity issue, without expanding the claim or supplying notice from later law.
- Rankin v. McPherson, June 24, 1987, and Anderson v. Creighton, June 25, 1987: may inform later doctrine but cannot themselves establish notice for the January and February 1987 actions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No complete factual-investigation theory commands five votes. The seven-Justice reasonable-and-genuine safe harbor and six-Justice unreasonable-false-actually-protected proposition are express agreements, not a unified Marks framework; the latter includes two judgment dissenters and is not selected as a judgment-supporting narrowest rationale. No universal freestanding investigation duty or rule for unreasonable investigation of actually unprotected speech is established. Personal immunity remains tied to law giving notice at the 1987 conduct, not the 1994 decision itself.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, 7–2, without restoring defendants' summary judgment. Determine genuine reliance, actual motivation and the protection of causally relevant other speech under ordinary summary-judgment and Mt. Healthy rules. The completed investigation's conditional objective adequacy and the reported account's conditional permissibility are not reopened as wholly unresolved issues. Personal immunity is considered if necessary; no damages, reinstatement, municipal-liability finding or revived association claim is ordered.

#### United States v. National Treasury Employees Union

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1170.
**Decided:** February 22, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Scope of public-employee speech relief (The existing ban is unenforceable throughout the certified class; The injunction does not adjudicate the claims of unrepresented senior employees; Crane retains his individual relief without a grade-only remand).

##### A broad prospective honoraria ban requires justification for its actual speech burden

**Holding:** When the Government broadly restricts future compensated expression by its employees acting as citizens, Pickering requires a justification commensurate with the restriction's actual reach and burden on employees and listeners. Section 501(b)'s honoraria ban is unconstitutional as applied to the represented lower-level employees' protected expression unrelated to their employment because the record does not adequately connect that burden to actual or apparent misuse of office; narrower valid ethics restrictions remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563, supplies the employment balance, applied to the breadth of a prospective restriction. Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138, supplies public-concern principles without limiting protected subjects to politics or workplace criticism. Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994, retains its distinct particular-speech and reasonable-account rules. Simon & Schuster v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991, supplies the relevance of financial burdens. Turner Broadcasting System v. FCC, June 27, 1994, preserves substantial but reviewable legislative predictive judgments; it does not decide this employment case.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 501(b) prohibits receipt of an honorarium by a Member, officer or employee; §505(2) excludes special Government employees as defined in 18 U.S.C. §202. Section 505(3) covers money or anything of value for an appearance, speech or article; a series falls within the definition if its subject directly relates to official duties or payment is because of governmental status. That parenthesis does not already restrict every single honorarium. Actual and necessary travel expenses for the individual and one relative are excluded to the extent another person pays or reimburses them, and otherwise deducted to the extent they are not paid or reimbursed. Travel expenses mean transportation, lodging and meals away from residence or principal employment.

Section 501(c) treats a payment on the individual's behalf to a §170(c) charitable organization as not received, subject to a $2,000 ceiling on each payment and no financial benefit to the individual, parent, sibling, spouse, child or dependent relative. Section 504(a) permits an Attorney General civil action and a civil penalty up to the greater of $10,000 or compensation received. Under §504(b), a written opinion from an entity identified by §503 protects its recipient and another covered person whose facts are materially indistinguishable from subsection (a)'s sanction only for conduct after issuance, in good faith and in accordance with the opinion's provisions and findings. These are qualified provisions, not general exemptions. The separate §501(a) high-level outside-income ceiling is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded. Nonenforcement of the existing ban throughout the certified below-GS–16 class is preserved by five votes; Crane retains individual relief by six; relief for unrepresented senior employees is removed. Congressional and judicial coverage, §501(a)'s outside-income ceiling and valid independent ethics restrictions are not invalidated. No penalty or compensation award is entered.

#### Board of County Commissioners, Wabaunsee County v. Umbehr, 518 U.S. 668 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1654.
**Decided:** June 28, 1996.

##### Whether an existing independent contractor can challenge speech-retaliatory termination

**Holding:** Government may not terminate or refuse to renew an existing independent-contractor relationship because the contractor spoke as a private citizen on a matter of public concern when the speech and public interests outweigh the government's legitimate contracting and operational interests. Courts apply Pickering's balance to the actual contractual relationship, with appropriate regard for genuine service needs; contractor status, retained termination power and absence of a property entitlement do not by themselves defeat the claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The County’s existing renewable exclusive arrangement carried valuable benefits despite no direct purchase of hauling. Loss of exclusivity, coordinated negotiation and notice protection can cause injury notwithstanding later direct city contracts. Content, form and context determine public concern. Actual independence, other clients, contacts and responsibilities may affect the balance either way; private communication is not automatically unprotected and offense at criticism is not a service interest.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No initial-bidder, universal future-award, pure affiliation, unrelated employee-status, comprehensive factual-mistake, later-acquired-evidence or mitigation holding. Summary-judgment assumptions establish no actual speech protection, motive, injury or ultimate balance.

##### What causation and same-decision requirements govern the remand

**Holding:** The contractor must establish that protected speech was a substantial or motivating factor in the challenged action. The government may defeat the retaliation claim by proving by a preponderance of the evidence that it would have taken the same action absent the protected speech; identifying a merely possible lawful reason does not establish that defense. Official-capacity liability and relief also require the ordinary entity-attribution and remedial predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The claimant’s motivating-factor burden and government’s preponderance counterfactual defense remain separate. Genuine performance, efficiency, cost, trust and operational grounds remain available; their possible existence is not proof. Personal immunity is distinct from official-capacity entity liability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Waters/Settle do not supply a universal investigation framework. Ordinary Monell attribution and remedy predicates remain necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reviewed official-capacity judgment affirmed and remanded, 7–2. The individual-immunity judgment and McClure’s separate causation judgment are undisturbed; no automatic renewal, reinstatement, damages or trial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Pickering and Connick:** Extended to existing-contractor retaliation through a balance adjusted to the actual service relationship, preserving private-citizen/public-concern and genuine operational-interest inquiries.
- **Perry v. Sindermann:** Applied to distinguish absence of entitlement to continued benefits from an unconstitutional retaliatory condition.
- **Givhan:** Preserves protection for qualifying citizen expression communicated privately; delivery method alone does not resolve the claim.
- **Mt. Healthy:** Applied without changing the motivating-factor burden or the government's preponderance same-decision defense.
- **Waters and Settle:** Their actual genuine-ground and causation distinctions remain operative; no full investigation framework or unrelated curricular rule is adopted here.
- **National Treasury Employees Union:** Its broad prospective-restraint rule remains distinct from this individual termination; its full justification allocation is not automatically imported.
- **Kentucky v. Graham and Monell:** Preserve the distinction between personal immunity and entity claims and the independent requirements for governmental attribution and relief.

#### Thomasson v. Perry

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 95-2185; review of 80 F.3d 915 (en banc).

**Decided:** 1997-06-09.

For the equal-protection question and statutory qualifications in Thomasson v. Perry, Fourth Circuit No. 95-2185, decided June 9, 1997, see Civil Rights — Military personnel and equal protection.

##### Relevant evidentiary use of this voluntary statement does not violate the First Amendment

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not prohibit using Thomasson's voluntary identity statement as relevant evidence of the lawful conduct-related personnel predicate sustained in Part I, with the genuine rebuttal opportunity present here. That evidentiary use does not authorize discharge merely for opposing the policy, punish abstract belief, or make every identity statement an admission of a prohibited act.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The relevant connection rests on the lawful conduct-related inference and meaningful rebuttal sustained in Part I. A bare evidentiary label cannot justify punishment of expression. Advocacy retaliation, an irrelevant inference and abstract belief remain outside the holding. Neither a completed prohibited act nor realized operational disruption is required for this bounded predictive use.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the independent speech challenge by five direct joins. Stevens would independently reverse on speech; Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg reserve that question and supply no additional support for rejecting it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Dawson forbids punishment of irrelevant abstract beliefs; Mitchell permits relevant expression as evidence without importing its criminal predicates into every personnel case. Waters preserves scrutiny of the ground actually relied on without adding a universal investigation checklist or actual-disruption condition.

### November 17, 1997 — Jeffries v. Harleston

[Public Projection](../records/jeffries_v_harleston_merits_1997-11-17.md#public-projection). Souter's Parts I and III command five direct votes (Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer); Part II commands those five plus Stone-Zsela. Six Justices support vacatur; the controlling five-vote mandate does not require a new trial.

Part I rejects final defense judgment on the panel's employment balance. Pickering and Connick require attention to the actual speech, service relationship, and institutional needs. Retained tenure and lack of entitlement to a longer chair term do not categorically defeat retaliation; a reasonable forecast matters but does not alone decide protection. The public-concern address was off campus at an unaffiliated event and was not assigned or controlled as official duty; introduction by the chair title did not establish sponsorship. Real administrative duties and recruitment, fundraising, faculty-interaction, and community interests remain relevant, but no special policymaking, confidential, or additional public-contact role was established. Both jury findings—no actual disruption and reasonable expected disruption—stand. Prevention need not await actual harm. Waters protects a genuinely relied-upon reasonable account only if it describes legally disciplinable speech; no complete investigation code is adopted. Perry, Umbehr, and O'Hare preserve the distinction between benefit entitlement and retaliation. No categorical protection of the entire address, actual-loss requirement, quantified institutional-damages requirement, or wholesale NTEU prospective-ban burden follows; Settle remains confined to its student-curriculum setting.

Part II requires each defendant's actual causal responsibility and a real counterfactual under Mt. Healthy, Umbehr, O'Hare, Kimberlin, and Crawford-El. Once protected speech is a substantial or motivating cause, the same-decision defense requires proof by a preponderance, not a conceivable lawful reason. Nine successful one-year votes and differentiated animus or punitive findings do not automatically establish an independent lawful Board decision. A causally effective proposal may matter, but procurement alone proves no liability; nonvoting proposals, defeated votes, and abstention neither establish nor eliminate other supported causal contributions. Competent direct and circumstantial proof applies without a heightened motive burden. A genuine independent lawful decision or supported personal defense may still dispose of a claim; Bernstein's discontinued claims stay discontinued.

Part III leaves actual verdict harmonization and consequences, sustainable individual defenses, capacity attribution, and relief to ordinary adjudication. Harlow and Anderson v. Creighton require personal-damages immunity at the late-October 1991 conduct date and, for any preserved March 1992 claim, its distinct date; later law supplies no retrospective notice. Punitive answers do not replace protection, causation, or immunity. Prospective relief requires a live remedy; elapsed time alone proves neither mootness nor continuing eligibility. ### February 12, 1998 — Danahy v. Buscaglia

[Public Projection](../records/danahy_v_buscaglia_merits_1998-02-12.md#public-projection). The following complete public blocks preserve this event's action, proposition-specific authority, published positions, procedure and source limits.

#### Event

**Danahy v. Buscaglia**, Second Circuit No. 97-7264, 134 F.3d 1185. Plenary review decided February 12, 1998; October Term 1997. The Court reviews the January 27, 1998 judgment vacating denial of qualified-immunity summary judgment and remanding on seven employees' First Amendment individual-damages claims. The question is whether the individual defendants receive qualified immunity on the facts proper for this interlocutory appeal. No separate oral-argument date is established.

#### Participation

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Public Action

The Court affirms the Court of Appeals' judgment on the reviewed First Amendment personal-damages claims, without deciding whether each dismissal was constitutionally permissible.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The judgment affirms personal damages immunity for Vacco, Buscaglia and Page on the appealed claims of Danahy, Burke, Bissram, Mungo, Matthews, Angiletta and Henesey, preserving the Court of Appeals' vacatur of the summary-judgment denial and its corresponding remand. The judgment does not dismiss the whole action, decide Weiss, Stark or Mahl's claims, or adjudicate other claims outside the appeal. It creates no prospective or official-capacity claim and decides none of their separate requirements. No final constitutional classification, personal-causation or same-decision finding, damages, reinstatement, backpay, new trial, hearing, discovery or description-production order follows.

#### Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, Parts I–II, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Both the legal-review boundary and the particular notice ground have nine direct joins. There are no separate writings.

#### Holdings

##### A legal immunity appeal accepts the proper factual assumptions

**Holding and operative rule:** An interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal may resolve whether the properly assumed conduct violated clearly established law, but may not choose among disputed accounts of duties, knowledge, participation or motive. Section 1254 permits review of the circuit judgment without enlarging the circuit's §1291 jurisdiction; the particular notice question here is separable, and no additional factual-appeal component requiring dismissal has been identified.

**Authority:** Parts I–II of Stevens's opinion are joined in full by all eight other participating Justices. Each holding and its qualifications have nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Johnson separates legal immunity from factual sufficiency, and Behrens permits the legal question only on the proper assumed facts. Clanton and Smith preserve that distinction when reviewing a circuit judgment. The present defense accepts alleged patronage motive and nonpolicymaker status and uses the reported direct prosecutorial work and defendants' knowledge on their assumed-fact footing. It does not depend on finding a different motive or deciding that each employee personally selected targets or set policy. Calling ultimate classification a legal issue would not authorize resolving a genuine historical dispute. Conversely, the existence of unresolved constitutional or factual issues elsewhere in the action does not eliminate this separable legal question. The Court neither resolves those issues nor brings them within the appeal.

##### The particular November 1995 uncertainty supports personal damages immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** On the proper assumptions about these seven employees' actual direct and confidential prosecutorial work, the law in November 1995 left reasonable uncertainty whether political affiliation could be appropriate to their functions. Vacco, Buscaglia and Page therefore receive personal damages immunity on the reviewed First Amendment claims, without a final constitutional classification of any position or an office-wide confidentiality, at-will or civil-service exception.

**Authority:** The unanimous Parts I–II authority stated above applies to this holding, each employee's application and the claim-limited mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** Branti asks whether affiliation is appropriate for effective performance of the particular work; ordinary trust and professional secrecy are insufficient. Its reservation concerned a prosecutor's deputy, not every support employee. Harlow and Anderson nevertheless protect reasonable conduct-time legal mistakes independently of ultimate constitutional permission. Elder requires all relevant pre-existing law; obvious coverage needs no factual twin, but an abstract affiliation right does not resolve every uncertain application.

The affirmative connection here is direct work with prosecutors developing confidential investigations, audits and cases. Danahy supervised investigators working with confidential informants; Burke performed undercover work; Bissram undertook surveillance and undercover work. Mungo's audit-investigator work had the reported direct prosecutor relationship, without invented statutory powers. Matthews and Angiletta shared that relationship without borrowing others' individual admissions. Henesey combined billing analysis, extensive recipient/provider interviews and evidence custody in it; his clerk title does not reduce that account to mere filing.

Those functions, evaluated under Branti and the preconduct functional authorities described in the appellate opinion, permitted reasonable uncertainty about their relationship to prosecutorial discretion. The Court does not find that each employee exercised charging authority or that partisan agreement actually improved performance. The strongest alternative is a partial remand for Henesey, Matthews and Angiletta. Their thinner individual descriptions do not erase the affirmative common relationship, while missing descriptions permit no invented power. The immunity question is reasonable legal error on the supported functions, not final proof of partisan necessity.

Civil-service exemption, general institutional importance and confidentiality alone would not suffice. Patronage motive remains assumed; subjective innocence and a hypothetical same-decision reason supply no alternative defense. Later description refinements and later decisions cannot establish November 1995 notice or require a trial solely to obtain later-demanded descriptions. The result preserves constitutional elements, ordinary proof and other claims while resolving only these personal damages defenses.

### Political retaliation in existing government service relationships

**Governing question:** Termination, nonrenewal or withdrawal of an existing independent-contractor or recurring service-provider relationship for citizen speech or partisan allegiance.

**Current rule:** Government may not terminate or refuse to renew an existing contractor relationship because of private-citizen speech on a matter of public concern when the speaker's and public's interests outweigh legitimate contracting and operational interests. Apply Connick's content, form and context inquiry and Pickering's balance adjusted to the actual service relationship, including genuine performance, efficiency, cost, trust and disruption concerns; independence, other clients, contacts and responsibilities may matter in either direction. Neither contractor status, termination authority, lack of property entitlement nor absence of direct governmental purchase categorically defeats protection. Private delivery alone does not remove qualifying expression.

For withdrawal based on refusing partisan support or supporting an opponent, apply the distinct Elrod–Branti rule: government must demonstrate that political affiliation is appropriate for effective performance of the actual work. Labels, general patronage advantages and dislike of opposition do not suffice; genuinely policymaking or confidential functions may justify the qualification. Public-concern speech and materially mixed speech/affiliation claims require their appropriate service-adjusted inquiry rather than an undifferentiated affiliation or management rule.

The claimant must show protected conduct was a substantial or motivating factor. Government may defeat the claim by proving by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred absent that conduct; a merely possible lawful reason does not establish the defense. Entity attribution, personal defenses, injury and relief retain their separate requirements.

### Public-employee speech and the employer’s mistaken factual account

**Governing question:** Which employer beliefs about employee speech protect a disciplinary decision, and which do not.

**Current rule:** An employer acting on a genuine, reasonable account of speech that would be disciplinable under the governing public-employee-speech rules is protected even if that factual account is mistaken. Conversely, an unreasonable and incorrect account does not defeat a claim when the employee actually spoke protected speech and that speech caused the adverse action. The employee must show protected speech was a substantial or motivating factor; the employer may establish by a preponderance that it would have taken the same action absent the protected speech. These limited propositions do not impose liability for every investigation defect or adopt the plurality’s complete investigative procedure.
