#### Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1424.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### What evidence of Article III injury, causation and redressability is required at summary judgment

**Holding:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury, a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, and a likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. At summary judgment, the plaintiff must supply specific facts by affidavit or other evidence on the challenged elements; favorable reasonable inferences remain available, but allegations cannot substitute for the missing evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the elements and litigation-stage evidentiary requirements. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining this section.

**Material application:** Earlier survival of the pleading stage does not establish the specific facts needed at summary judgment. The Court resolves this action on insufficient threatened personal injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general redressability element controls. Its application to absent funding agencies and uncertain foreign-project responses appears only in the three-Justice Part III-B and is not controlling. Favorable reasonable inferences remain available; the decision does not replace Rule 56 or the separate APA programmatic-review holding of National Wildlife Federation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The merits declaration, mandatory rulemaking relief and dependent §1540(g)(4) fee award are vacated, and the action is remanded for jurisdictional dismissal. Six Justices support dismissal for lack of standing; seven support reversal, with Stevens relying on a noncontrolling statutory ground. No independent fee-entitlement rule or substitute award is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975): applied for the plaintiff's personal stake and burden to establish standing; its statutory-rights discussion remains subject to concrete injury.
- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied for the constitutional elements and the limits on generalized judicial supervision.
- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976): applied for fair traceability and likely redress; no new majority application concerning these absent agencies follows.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied for the evidentiary distinction between pleading and summary judgment; its separate APA programmatic-review holding is not enlarged.
- Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986): applied to require evidence of challenged essential elements while preserving genuine material disputes and reasonable inferences.

##### Whether past visits, indefinite return intentions and general wildlife interests establish imminent personal injury

**Holding:** Aesthetic, recreational and professional interests in observing wildlife can support standing, but these plaintiffs must show threatened interference with their own concrete use. Prior visits and an indefinite intention to return, without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure, do not establish standing for these prospective claims; an ecosystem, animal or professional connection that supplies only general interest in distant species does not cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone's separate concurrence supplies a sixth vote for the record-specific personal-injury conclusion without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** The affidavits describe visits to Egyptian and Sri Lankan project areas but no sufficiently definite return undertaking or continuing activity showing imminent exposure. General ecosystem, animal and professional connections supply no missing evidence of these members' own threatened use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document prerequisite is imposed. Actual use of an affected ecosystem and demonstrated injury transmitted across geographic distance remain undecided. Aesthetic, recreational and professional harm remains cognizable when it affects the claimant's own concrete activity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jurisdictional dismissal leaves the statute's geographic reach unresolved and does not preclude a later action supported by sufficient standing facts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): applied; aesthetic injury is cognizable, but an organization's special interest cannot replace injury to a member.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977): applied to require an injured member for this associational claim; no other associational requirement is changed.
- Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95 (1983): applied for the distinction between past exposure and a present or imminent injury supporting prospective relief.
- Whitmore v. Arkansas, 495 U.S. 149 (1990): applied for actual or imminent rather than conjectural injury.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied to specific affected use; no categorical bar to geographically transmitted injury is established.
- Japan Whaling Association v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): distinguished because its whale-watching and studying members alleged an adverse effect on their own activities; concrete professional or ecological injury remains possible.

##### Whether ESA citizen-suit permission and an asserted procedural right replace threatened concrete injury

**Holding:** ESA §11(g)'s authorization for any person to sue does not give Article III standing to a plaintiff asserting only the public's common interest in lawful administration. A procedural right may protect a plaintiff's threatened concrete interest without requiring proof that the procedure will certainly change the ultimate agency decision, but the procedural label cannot replace that personal interest; these respondents have not shown the required connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the citizen-suit and concrete-interest rule. Stone does not join this section.

**Material application:** Section 11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), permits enforcement but identifies no concrete injury to these members independent of the unsupported future use. The public's common interest in lawful administration cannot supply the missing personal connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may recognize concrete factual injuries previously inadequate in law; the outer reach of that power is not settled. Procedural protections tied to a threatened concrete interest remain cognizable without certainty of a changed ultimate decision. Neither §7(a)(2)'s geographic reach nor the binding effect of a revised rule on absent agencies is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The action is dismissed for want of Article III standing; no new consultation rule or cancellation of any foreign project is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126 (1922): applied to reject a general right to compel lawful administration as a sufficient personal injury.
- Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447 (1923), and Ex parte Levitt, 302 U.S. 633 (1937): applied for the distinction between direct injury and an undifferentiated public grievance.
- United States v. Richardson, 418 U.S. 166 (1974), and Schlesinger v. Reservists Committee to Stop the War, 418 U.S. 208 (1974): applied to generally shared governmental grievances without distinctive concrete harm.
- Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United for Separation of Church and State, Inc., 454 U.S. 464 (1982), and Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied; a claimed right to lawful government conduct alone does not satisfy Article III.
- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), and Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): preserved for Congress's power to recognize injuries and broaden protected interests while retaining the plaintiff's own concrete injury.
- Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Insurance Co., 409 U.S. 205 (1972), and Hardin v. Kentucky Utilities Co., 390 U.S. 1 (1968): distinguished as statutory recognition of concrete personal interests, not authorization of a wholly abstract grievance.

#### Bennett v. Spear

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-813.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether irrigators plead personal injury, traceability and redressability

**Holding:** Irrigators who allege loss of their project water from a Biological Opinion that the operating agency has announced it will follow sufficiently plead concrete injury, causation and likely redress through removal of that constraint. Standing must later be supported by the evidence appropriate to each litigation stage; a citizen-suit authorization does not replace Article III injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Material application:** The Bureau’s announced compliance connects the completed Service action to petitioners’ own alleged loss despite retained operational discretion. The possibility of other eventual choices does not require certainty of a changed allocation or defeat pleading-stage redress.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No biological error or final factual standing determination is made; the statutory review routes and relief remain separate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed ESA/APA dismissal is reversed and remanded for claim-specific proceedings; the unchallenged NEPA dismissal is undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lujan’s personal-interest and stage-of-proof rules control, not its noncontrolling absent-agency application. Humane Society’s missing link to an already completed transfer is distinguished. Tracy permits cognizable economic injury without dispensing with other requirements, and Arizonans requires a continuing controversy without establishing a loss of this alleged injury.

For Bennett v. Spear, No. 95-813, decided March 19, 1997, see Environmental Law, “Endangered Species Act citizen-suit channels,” for the statutory channels and retained conditions, and Administrative Law, “Review of completed biological consultation,” for APA interests and finality.

#### MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-356 and 93-521.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** United States et al. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. et al., No. 93-521, is consolidated with No. 93-356; the same rule invalidation applies to both.

##### The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing

**Holding:** Under 47 U.S.C. §203(b)(2), the Commission may, in its discretion and for good cause shown, modify tariff requirements in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions, but may not replace §203(a)'s mandatory filing obligation throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system or lengthen the statutory notice period beyond 120 days. The challenged permissive-filing rule exceeds that delegation and is invalid; Chevron does not require deference to a construction outside this statutory boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The November 1992 rule makes filing optional, not forbidden, for nondominant long-distance carriers; AT&T alone remains classified as dominant in this record. A modification can have consequential effects. Courts must examine the obligation changed, the scope of the modification clause and whether the agency adjusts the obligation's operation or removes its essential operation for the regulated class. This inquiry does not turn on a numerical market-share threshold or automatically invalidate a class-wide adjustment. Changes to filing form, contents, timing or place remain possible under the statutory conditions; timing changes cannot extend the 120-day notice ceiling. The Court does not categorically foreclose deferral or waiver in limited circumstances, and decides no particular proposed waiver. Good cause alone cannot authorize replacement of mandatory filing throughout this sector.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the challenged order independently fails the special-circumstances-or-conditions requirement; no separate alternative ground commands a majority. It prescribes no tariff rates or replacement program, decides no damages or retrospective nonfiling consequences, resolves no general rule for individual nonenforcement decisions, and adopts no independently sufficient ratification or prohibition theory from §§226 or 332.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applies the inquiry into statutory meaning before deference; no new exception based on economic importance and no replacement of Chevron is adopted.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: preserves contextual interpretation within an actual delegation and the requirement of a discernible agency ground; the agency's competitive-policy rationale is examined, not replaced by a judicial rationale.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves permissible interpretive change within a statutory system; the defect here is the rule's statutory reach, not the fact that the Commission changed its policy.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves the principle that agency expertise operates within binding statutory limits; its distinct nonemployee-access rule is not imported into tariff regulation.
- NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, May 23, 1994: distinguishes a permissible functional construction preserving the statute's operative distinctions from removal of the filing obligation here; its supervisory-status holding remains intact.
- PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, May 31, 1994: distinguishes broad conditioning authority grounded in that Act's operative compliance language; no general rule against consequential agency action follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment setting aside the permissive-detariffing rule is affirmed in Nos. 93-356 and 93-521. No tariff rate, replacement program, damages or retrospective monetary consequence of earlier nonfiling is decided, and no new direction is given in the distinct complaint proceeding.

#### Bennett v. Spear

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-813.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether APA review is available for the §1536 claims and these interests

**Holding:** The consultation claims may proceed under APA §§702 and 704 because the ESA does not preclude that review and its citizen-suit channels supply no other adequate court remedy for the administrative claims. Alleged water restrictions unsupported by the best available scientific and commercial data implicate interests at least arguably protected by the particular §1536(a)(2) data requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous; both APA holdings have nine direct joins.

**Material application:** The interest inquiry concerns the provision sued upon; sound scientific decisionmaking also protects affected persons against unsupported restrictions. The §1540(g)(5) savings clause preserves available rights without itself satisfying every APA condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 702 concerns relief other than money damages and retains other legal or equitable limits and relief forbidden by another consent-to-suit statute. A mandatory or injunctive decree must identify responsible officers and successors by name or title. Section 704 retains final-action and no-other-adequate-remedy conditions; preliminary or intermediate action ordinarily receives review with final action. Unless a statute expressly requires otherwise, final action remains final despite an application for a declaratory order or reconsideration; an administrative appeal postpones finality only when an agency rule requires it and makes the action inoperative meanwhile. No decree or unproved exhaustion prerequisite is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Dalton’s integrated base-closure scheme supported its confined preclusion holding; no comparable scheme is shown here. Its four-Justice recommendation-finality rationale creates no general bar.

##### Whether the Biological Opinion completes agency action with legal consequences

**Holding:** This Biological Opinion is final because it completes the Service’s consultation decision and its incidental-take statement changes legal exposure under §1536(o)(2) when the statement’s conditions are met. The Bureau’s retained operating discretion does not reduce that completed, legally consequential action to tentative advice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Material application:** Practical influence and the Bureau’s announced compliance support causation; the conditional change in taking liability separately establishes legal consequences. Under §1536(a)(2), agencies must use the best available scientific and commercial data to avoid likely jeopardy and destruction or adverse modification of designated critical habitat unless the Committee grants an (h) exemption; none is found here. Under (b)(4), the Secretary must find no (a)(2) violation or offer reasonable and prudent alternatives believed to avoid it, and conclude incidental taking will not violate that subsection. Marine-mammal taking additionally requires §1371(a)(5) authorization. The statement specifies taking impact, reasonable and prudent minimization, necessary marine-mammal measures and implementing conditions including reporting.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section (o)(2) protects only taking compliant with those conditions, not every ESA violation; the separate (o)(1) exemption is not the source of this consequence. No marine-mammal finding is required for these fish. Not every report or prediction is final, and the biological merits, triggered duties, merits application of review standards and final relief remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed dismissal is reversed and remanded, leaving the separate NEPA dismissal undisturbed and ordering no substantive operational relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Franklin permits review of a completed subordinate-agency determination despite a later governmental step; its reserved presidential-status question is not decided.

For Bennett v. Spear, No. 95-813, decided March 19, 1997, see Federal Courts, “Standing to challenge a biological consultation constraint,” and Environmental Law, “Endangered Species Act citizen-suit channels,” for the separate constitutional and citizen-suit holdings.

#### NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co. / Ludwig v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-1612; 93-1613.
**Decided:** January 18, 1995.

##### Reasoned approval of annuity brokerage falls within the banking power

**Holding:** The business of banking under 12 U.S.C. §24 Seventh is not exhausted by its enumerated activities, and the Comptroller's reasonable construction may include financial-intermediary agency sales of the fixed, variable and combined annuity products covered by this approval. The construction must remain within banking and its incidental powers and comply with enacted restrictions; this conditioned brokerage approval authorizes no underwriting, issuance or guarantee of the insurers' obligations and no unrelated commercial enterprise.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), eight Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in both judgments only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Clarke v. Securities Industry Association, 479 U.S. 388: applies its substantial respect for reasonable Comptroller banking-law construction and brokerage analysis to this agency activity.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: applies deference within the ambiguity left after the enacted banking boundaries are determined; clear prohibitions still govern.
- Investment Company Institute v. Camp, 401 U.S. 617: preserves substantive banking restrictions and the need for an actual reasoned agency judgment, which this approval supplies.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218, and Brown v. Gardner, 513 U.S. 115: preserve text-first review and actual statutory limits; neither displaces permissible construction within a genuine delegation.

##### Section 92 does not defeat this annuity classification

**Holding:** The Comptroller reasonably classifies the annuity agency products covered by this approval as investment instruments outside the insurance category of 12 U.S.C. §92 for this statutory purpose, so §92 does not invalidate the approval. The Court does not decide whether §92 exclusively limits genuine insurance-agency activity to banks in qualifying small places, and it neither repeals that provision's conditions nor determines classification under a distinct state insurance law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), eight Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in both judgments only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States National Bank of Oregon v. Independent Insurance Agents of America, Inc., 508 U.S. 439: applies the statutory-survival holding and respects its reservation of scope and agency-construction questions; no repeal issue is reopened.
- Chevron, 467 U.S. 837, and Clarke, 479 U.S. 388: apply permissible, reasoned construction to this statutory classification after examining the text and product features.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Comptroller's approval separately requires advertising, promotional materials and customer descriptions to disclose that the annuities are not bank products and are not FDIC insured, together with a signed purchaser acknowledgment of agency status and lack of federal insurance. Brokerage does not issue, underwrite or guarantee an insurer's undertaking. Applicable banking, securities and insurance requirements otherwise remain.

When §92 applies, it requires the national bank to be located and doing business in a place of no more than 5,000 inhabitants under the last preceding decennial census; the insurer must be authorized by the State where the bank is located; and the agency must operate under Comptroller rules. It permits solicitation and sale of insurance and collection of premiums for agreed fees or commissions, but prohibits the bank from assuming or guaranteeing premium payment and separately prohibits guaranteeing the truth of the insured applicant's statements. Those statutory terms are not the same as this approval's disclosure conditions. Section 24's distinction between without-recourse, customer-order/customer-account securities transactions and own-account activity remains; no own-account investment exception is adjudicated. No individual customer's contract, universal annuity classification or separate state-law preemption dispute is resolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit is reversed in both consolidated dockets, 9–0, and the conditioned approval reinstated. Advertising, promotional materials and customer descriptions must disclose that these are not bank products and are not FDIC insured, with signed acknowledgment of agency status and lack of federal insurance. Otherwise applicable banking, securities and insurance rules remain; no new agency rationale, underwriting or bank guarantee is authorized.

### January 26, 1998 — Allentown Mack Sales & Service, Inc. v. NLRB

[Public Projection](../records/allentown_mack_sales_service_inc_v_nlrb_merits_1998-01-26.md#public-projection). The following complete public blocks preserve this event's action, proposition-specific authority, published positions, procedure and source limits.

#### Event

Allentown Mack Sales & Service, Inc. v. NLRB, No. 96-795. Plenary merits decision, January 26, 1998; argued October 15, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, 83 F.3d 1483 (1996), which enforced the Board's unfair-labor-practice and bargaining order. The question concerns the evidence an employer must possess before polling employees about an incumbent union's majority support, including the asserted substitution of proof of lost support for the announced reasonable-doubt standard.

Allentown rehired 32 of 45 former unit employees after purchasing the branch. The successor finding and appropriate unit are unchallenged. The ALJ found the procedural polling safeguards satisfied but found insufficient objective doubt. The later secret ballot was 19–13 against representation; that result does not establish what management knew when it announced the poll.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

#### Public Action

The judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded to the D.C. Circuit with instructions to deny enforcement of the Board order under review. A different majority sustains the facial validity of the Board's good-faith reasonable-doubt threshold for incumbent-union polling.

#### Holdings

##### The announced reasonable-doubt polling threshold is permissible

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board may require an employer to possess a good-faith reasonable doubt, supported by objective considerations, of an incumbent union's majority support before conducting a safeguarded poll. Using the same prerequisite for polling, withdrawing recognition and seeking a Board election is not, by itself, irrational or inconsistent with the Act; objective doubt is uncertainty about majority support, not proof that the union has lost it.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Act protects employee choice and established bargaining relationships. Auciello permits explained stability policies within statutory boundaries, while Fall River separates their rationality from factual application. Neither decides this polling rule in advance. Even a safeguarded employer poll may unsettle employees and the bargaining relationship. The Board may account for that risk before permitting a poll.

The employer argues that evidence sufficient to withdraw recognition makes polling pointless. But an employer may reasonably wish to determine actual preferences before taking the more consequential step of withdrawing recognition; a lawful option to withdraw is not an obligation to do so. A poll therefore retains a confirmatory use. Differences between a poll and a Board election—including the statutory one-year bar after a valid election—do not compel the same consequences or a judicially selected lower polling threshold. An unsuccessful union in an employer poll may seek a Board election.

Sections 7 and 8(c) protect employee choice and noncoercive expression, but do not themselves establish an unconditional right to poll an incumbent unit. Gissel left undecided whether its recognized allowances for reasonable inquiry are mandatory. Compliance with Struksnes safeguards does not alone answer the separate incumbent-polling prerequisite. The rule is permissible, not compelled for every future Board, and does not authorize a concealed requirement of actual minority proof. Certification and contract protections during their lawful duration, statutory representation routes and protection against coercion remain distinct. No freestanding First Amendment question is decided.

##### The presented challenge reaches the standard actually applied

**Holding and operative rule:** A challenge expressly directed to the amount of evidence demanded before polling fairly includes whether the Board applied its announced reasonable-doubt standard to the order under review. That inquiry reaches the associated whole-record application here; it creates no general permission to add an independent factual question merely because merits briefs discuss it.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, supplies five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The petition challenges a requirement of so much evidence of lost support that a poll becomes meaningless. Allentown's contention that the agency substituted individual repudiations for reasonable uncertainty concerns that asserted evidentiary demand, not a separate judgment. Hubbard preserves the difference between supporting arguments and enlarged questions. Examining the actual standard and the material reports is necessary to resolve this challenge. The Court does not reopen the successor finding, the unit or independently unpreserved objections.

##### An announced doubt standard cannot silently become minority proof

**Holding and operative rule:** Under NLRA §§10(e)–(f) and APA §706, the Board's finding must be supported by substantial evidence on the record as a whole under the substantive standard actually governing the case. When the announced predicate is objective reasonable doubt, the agency cannot sustain its order by effectively demanding proof approaching actual loss of majority support, or silently convert recurring evidentiary outcomes into a different substantive or proof rule.

**Authority:** Scalia's Parts III–IV have Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, providing five direct votes for this framework and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Universal Camera requires relevant evidence a reasonable mind could accept as adequate, considered together with what fairly detracts from the finding. It preserves the Board's choice between fairly conflicting views; it does not permit the governing standard to change during application. Auciello likewise distinguishes circumstantial objective doubt from a numerical showing of actual minority. The agency's §10(c) preponderance burden and the reviewing court's substantial-evidence inquiry remain separate.

An employer's uncertainty may be reasonable without direct repudiations from a counted majority. A course of adverse findings cannot, without a lawful change in rule, redefine doubt as established opposition. Expertise and recurring evidentiary judgments matter, but do not replace the announced legal predicate. Lawful, expressly adopted presumptions or evidentiary rules remain possible, subject to their statutory basis and ordinary review; the Court abolishes no such authority and makes no finding of systematic manipulation. Neither subjective suspicion nor every unverified report necessarily establishes objective doubt. The question remains whether this whole record supports the particular finding under the rule the Board announced.

##### This record requires denial of enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** The finding that Allentown lacked objective reasonable doubt when it announced the poll is unsupported by substantial evidence on the whole record. The enforcement judgment must therefore be reversed with instructions to deny enforcement of the challenged Board order; the record does not call for another agency weighing under the same or a newly demanding threshold.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV has Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, supplying five direct votes for the application and complete mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The ALJ accepted six individual expressions and assumed a seventh supported doubt. Those seven are neither a numerical threshold nor the entire evidentiary picture. Marsh's dissatisfaction with representation, Bloch's report that the night shift did not want the union, and Mohr's assessment as shop steward and bargaining-committee member add material circumstantial force. Group reports are not mechanically counted as additional, nonoverlapping repudiations.

The ALJ identified weaknesses in the reports' sources, scope, interview setting and relation to the rehired workforce, and expressly found Mohr's statement insufficient even in combination with the other evidence. The Court does not erase that cumulative assessment. But the Board separately said the employer could not legitimately rely on Mohr because he described the old workforce. His union responsibilities and the substantial continuity of employees make that dismissal untenable. His failure to specify the size of a majority or personally renounce the union does not answer whether management reasonably doubted continued support.

Taken together, the material reports establish objective uncertainty even though they do not establish actual minority. The source and coverage limitations do not adequately sustain this contrary finding under the announced doubt standard. This is not substitution of a preferred inference between fairly conflicting views: the required quantum was effectively raised toward proof of lost support. The later 19–13 poll supplies no retroactive justification. The holding determines the legality of this enforcement request, not present preferences, an independent right to cease bargaining in every setting, or a permanent representation result.

##### The separate remedy objection remains outside review

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 10(e) bars an objection not urged before the Board unless extraordinary circumstances excuse the omission. The separate objection to the bargaining order's explanation and decertification bar was not preserved, and no such excuse is established; that objection is not reviewed.

**Authority:** Part V has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in leaving the objection unreviewed.

**Controlling explanation:** Auciello preserves §10(e)'s distinction between the particular objection presented to the Board and another objection later asserted in court. The preserved threshold and application challenges do not themselves preserve this separate remedial attack. Correcting the predicate of the existing order resolves enforcement without deciding the barred objection or approving every possible bargaining remedy.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Auciello's accepted-offer cutoff remains intact. Its statutory limits, distinction between circumstantial doubt and actual minority, and separate §10(e) requirement are applied; it had not resolved incumbent polling or the weight of these reports.
- Fall River supplies separate review of rational Board policy and supported factual application. Its continuity and successor principles are not reopened, and its rebuttable presumption does not become proof of employee preference.
- Universal Camera supplies whole-record substantial-evidence review, including contrary evidence and the examiner's actual findings, while retaining deference between fairly conflicting views. The Court applies that standard to this unsupported finding rather than replacing it with de novo factfinding.
- Gissel retains its protection of noncoercive expression and its attention to the employment setting. Its reservation concerning mandatory permission for reasonable inquiry is preserved; the Court derives no unconditional polling entitlement from it.
- Hubbard's distinction between supporting arguments and new questions is applied to the wording and challenged order here. No exception based merely on merits briefing is adopted.

#### Law After Decision

The reasonable-doubt prerequisite remains a permissible policy for polling an incumbent unit with a rebuttable presumption of majority support. The decision adds a controlling application of that rule: objective uncertainty may rest on cumulative circumstantial evidence, and an agency must apply its announced predicate without silently raising it toward proof of actual minority. No headcount or replacement polling test is adopted. The Court's direct majorities govern their respective questions; the statutory invalidity proposal and the application dissent are not controlling law.

The existing safeguards remain: a purpose of determining the truth of the union's majority claim, communication of that purpose, assurances against reprisal, secret ballot, absence of unfair labor practices or a coercive atmosphere, and advance notice of time and place. Threats, reprisals, promises of benefit and coercive questioning remain regulable. Lawful certification and contract protections, representation procedures and the distinct withdrawal standard retain their own predicates. A future lawful policy may alter the polling rule within statutory bounds, but may not supply a retroactively higher prerequisite to revive this order. No independent First Amendment holding or determination of current employee support is made.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in Parts I and III–IV and the judgment and dissenting from Part II.** The Act's protection of employee choice does not adequately justify demanding essentially the same stringent showing before a safeguarded inquiry as before recognition withdrawal or a binding Board election. Those acts have different consequences: inquiry can resolve uncertainty, while withdrawal immediately changes bargaining and an election invokes distinct statutory rules. Stability is legitimate, but cannot make the rebuttable presumption practically untestable. Compliance with safeguards, independent secret-ballot supervision and the absence of a finding that this poll itself coerced employees support the statutory objection. The writing would invalidate the existing unitary polling threshold while preserving neutral purpose, notice, anti-reprisal assurances, secrecy, advance notice and protection against coercive unfair labor practices. It leaves lawful conclusive periods, the withdrawal standard and a prospectively adopted, source-grounded polling policy intact, and decides no independent First Amendment question. The authors also fully join the Court's independent substantial-evidence ground and denial-of-enforcement mandate even though the facial standard survives.

**Breyer, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in Parts I–II and V and dissenting from Parts III–IV and the judgment.** The granted rule challenge does not require an independent factual-sufficiency decision. If that issue is reached, Universal Camera requires respecting a supported agency choice between competing inferences. Objective doubt operates in the labor setting, where interviews, ambiguity about dissatisfaction versus opposition, incomplete sources for group reports, departmental coverage and the retained workforce affect evidentiary force. The ALJ gave actual reasons and expressly assessed Mohr together with the other reports; the adopted assessment need not be recast as a hidden majority-count requirement. #### Holdings

##### I. A live personal statutory controversy and an express statutory action permit merits review

**Holding and operative rule:** Actual continued custody of identified records describing this claimant's protected associations supplies a concrete personal information interest directly traceable to the retaining agency; an actually requested award within §552a(g)(1)(D)/(g)(4)'s legally available monetary category can meaningfully redress the claimed invasion if its requirements are established. Jurisdiction does not depend on first proving the disputed retention violation or final damages entitlement, and the substantial statutory claim falls within the Act's express agency action and conditional sovereign consent without creating a new constitutional damages cause.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in reaching the merits without joining this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. requires Article III power before these disputed merits and, with Bell v. Hood, separates a substantial federal claim from ultimate success. The acknowledged personal records establish the occurrence of the claimed intrusion. Warth and Havens recognize the difference between invasion of one's own protected interest and a demand for lawful government; neither supplies a Privacy Act remedy or excuses summary-judgment proof under Lujan and Lee. Subjective chill, presumed emotional loss and speculation about employers do not carry this statutory conclusion.

The FBI itself retains the information. Lindblom actually sought damages, the statutory claim continued on appeal, and no abandonment or legal bar to every monetary remedy is established. Arizonans rejects legally unavailable relief; it does not require a renewed formulaic prayer. Alexander supports meaningful partial redress despite uncertainty about recovery, without transferring its pleading-stage standard here. Culpability, adverse effect and monetary entitlement still require their own legal treatment.

Section 1331 and the review statute provide jurisdictional channels, not consent. Section 552a(g)(1)(D) authorizes an individual action against the agency and jurisdiction over it; (g)(4) supplies conditional United States monetary liability. Meyer requires that separate source of cause and obligation; the text supplies it here. Bell is not a waiver, §702 supplies no money, and no agency Bivens action is created. The Court neither classifies every federal waiver condition as nonjurisdictional nor presumes every remaining statutory predicate satisfied.

##### II. The protected personal retention invasion can itself be an adverse effect

**Holding and operative rule:** Actual unwanted maintenance of identified records describing this individual's protected associations, if outside §552a(e)(7)'s lawful alternatives, can itself supply the adverse personal effect required by §552a(g)(1)(D). A merely abstract or technical violation with no adverse personal consequence is insufficient; an adverse agency determination, public disclosure, lost employment or pecuniary loss is not independently required as such.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Breyer, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Scalia and Thomas reserve this construction; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Section (D) requires personal adverse effect, not the adverse determination separately required by (C). Its additional words exclude abstract procedural noncompliance without personal impact. Where the substantive protection concerns keeping the claimant's own associational information, the actual invasion and its adverse personal consequence can coincide. Requiring a different kind of downstream injury would leave that particular interest unprotected merely because the information remained undisclosed.

Havens illustrates a direct personal statutory invasion but does not decide this additional Privacy Act language. Albright distinguishes adverse effect, causation, intentional or willful conduct and monetary recovery; its recognition of emotional trauma does not make such trauma exclusive or settle the damages minimum. Doe v. FBI enforced a no-adverse-effect finding unchallenged in that case, where money was not pursued on appeal. Neither finding nor abandonment is established here. The Court does not find distress, declare these records unlawful, equate every violation with adverse effect or treat adverse effect as actual damages under (g)(4).

##### III. Continued retention must satisfy the complete Continuing Pertinence Test

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 552a(e)(7) regulates continued maintenance as well as original collection. Unless retention is expressly authorized by statute or by the individual, the information must remain pertinent to and within the scope of an authorized law-enforcement activity; lawful original collection is not conclusive permission to retain it after every qualifying connection has ceased. The Court adopts the following complete rule:

1. **Does the challenged record fall within the statutory protection?**

   The plaintiff identifies records describing their First Amendment activities and establishes that the government continues to maintain them. The court first checks whether retention is independently authorized by statute or by the individual.

2. **Can the government identify an authorized law-enforcement activity?**

   The agency must establish the legal authority for that activity. It need not prove the plaintiff committed a crime. Intelligence investigations, criminal investigations, and authorized civil enforcement may qualify.

3. **Does the information remain pertinent to that activity?**

   The court examines the actual relationship, not a hypothetical future possibility. Relevant considerations include:

   - Why the information was collected.
   - What investigative purpose it still serves.
   - Whether it preserves necessary context or evidence.
   - Whether subsequent events have changed its relevance.
   - Whether continued use or indexing exceeds the justified investigative scope.

   No single consideration automatically decides the case.

4. **What disposition and remedy does the law authorize?**

   If a qualifying connection remains, the agency may retain the information under the statutory exception. If no qualifying connection remains, the court determines the violation and available relief under the Privacy Act.

   The court separately accounts for lawful evidence-preservation and federal records-management duties.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Each joins the entire four-step rule, including every listed consideration and the separate preservation/relief requirements. Scalia and Thomas reject the complete rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Act defines maintenance to include keeping, collection, use and dissemination, while (e)(7) attaches its prohibition and alternatives to that continuing conduct. Congress's different acquisition-based terms elsewhere do not make initial lawfulness permanently dispositive. The three alternatives remain distinct: express statutory authorization, individual authorization and authorized-law-enforcement pertinence.

Authorized activity is broader than an active investigation or a current investigation of this plaintiff. Genuine historical intelligence value, connected or later inquiries, necessary evidentiary context and legitimate preservation of a law-enforcement record may supply an actual continuing relationship. Age, closure, innocence and name retrieval alone decide nothing. Conversely, the agency's general mission or the possibility that any datum might someday prove useful cannot conclusively establish every record's pertinence indefinitely. The Government's identified continuing-interest contention remains available for record-specific assessment.

Step 2 requires the agency to establish the activity's legal authority, without proof that the plaintiff committed a crime. Step 3's considerations illuminate actual pertinence and scope; they are nonexclusive, none is automatically decisive, and they create no current-necessity or least-restrictive-recordkeeping standard. The rule imposes no periodic audit, automatic request-response duty, deadline, universal consultation, prescribed explanation format or public disclosure of classified reasons, and no general shift of the independent burdens concerning injury, fault or damages. Ordinary judicial procedure and appropriate confidential review remain available.

The contrary historical-activity construction properly recognizes preservation value but improperly makes that value conclusive solely from acquisition. Department of Defense v. FLRA keeps disclosure protections and their exceptions distinct; confidentiality does not replace Congress's separate retention restriction. No dissemination claim, automatic purge on closure or present finding of nonpertinence is decided.

##### IV-A. The separate constitutional claims lack the required supported connection

**Holding and operative rule:** The Foundation's affidavits do not establish the causal mechanism connecting FBI retention to prospective grantees' or employers' knowledge and reactions; Lindblom's separately presented constitutional claim supplies no independent supported basis beyond that failed derivative theory. Both constitutional-standing rejections therefore stand, without a ruling on the First Amendment merits or a categorical exclusion of reputational or associational injury.

**Authority:** Part IV-A has all nine Justices. Scalia and Thomas's additional injury/imminence ground is separate and noncontrolling.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan and Lee require specific support at summary judgment, with favorable reasonable inferences. Predictions of stigma or employment effects do not themselves establish how these audiences learn of the retained references. Meese involved an actual public governmental label and detailed evidence connecting it to threatened harm. It shows why completed loss or identification of every deterred person is unnecessary; it does not supply the missing exposure link here. Nor does a claimant's own publicity categorically make all resulting injury self-inflicted.

Lindblom supplied no separate constitutional-standing argument or evidentiary showing. His pleaded subjective chill is not summary-judgment proof under Laird, and his employment elsewhere supplies no independent demonstrated constitutional injury. The actual personal statutory information interest has a different direct connection to the FBI's custody; its recognition does not manufacture the missing presentation of the distinct constitutional claim. The Court decides neither that FBI recordkeeping can never violate the First Amendment nor that these statutory records have no adverse personal effect.

##### IV-B. The constitutional dismissals must be without prejudice

**Holding and operative rule:** A dismissal of these constitutional claims for want of Article III standing cannot carry a merits-preclusive effect. The standing rejections are sustained, but the district judgment's with-prejudice effect is vacated and the case is remanded to conform those dismissals to their jurisdictional ground.

**Authority:** Part IV-B has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only. The correction is unanimous.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans and Lee distinguish limited authority to correct a lower court's jurisdictional disposition from authority to decide constitutional merits. The September 6 order dismissed the entire complaint with prejudice. Leaving that effect on claims dismissed for lack of standing would imply a merits adjudication the courts lack power to enter. Section 2106 permits the bounded correction but creates no substantive jurisdiction. No declaration or injunction is restored, and no evidentiary proceeding on the constitutional merits is directed.

##### V-A. The statutory judgment requires record-specific further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** The conclusive original-collection rule cannot sustain summary judgment on Lindblom's retention claim. That component is vacated and remanded for the complete test's application to the actual records, with any independent statutory or individual authorization and lawful preservation obligation considered separately; the Court awards neither expungement nor damages.

**Authority:** Part V-A has Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Scalia and Thomas oppose statutory remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The finding of legitimate original compilation does not answer whether each record retains the relationship now required. The Government contends that some other persons and organizations remain of investigative interest; that contention may matter, but the Court does not make the missing record-specific pertinence determination in either party's favor. Confidential and in camera assessment remains available where appropriate. Any concrete evidence-preservation or federal records-management duty must be accounted for without treating a general reference to preservation as conclusive permission or as an automatic destruction command. The remand corrects the governing legal rule; it does not itself reverse the discovery refusal, require additional evidence or pleadings, order an agency review program, or find intentional wrongdoing. Unreviewed claims remain undisturbed.

##### V-B. Each remedial route retains its independent requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** The retention restriction does not create unlimited relief: monetary liability under §552a(g)(1)(D)/(g)(4), amendment relief under (d)/(g)(1)(A)/(g)(2), access relief, and any independently permissible prospective equity retain their distinct conditions. Neither an automatic monetary award nor a general expungement entitlement follows from the Court's construction.

**Authority:** Part V-B has all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees that no final award issues and joins Part V-A's separate lawful-relief and preservation directions, without adopting V-B's additional grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** Intentional or willful noncompliance, causally resulting compensable actual damages and the statutory minimum remain separate questions; the Court decides no amount or nonpecuniary-damages issue. Costs and fees do not independently supply redress for an otherwise absent controversy. No constitutional damages cause against the FBI is created.

An amendment route must satisfy its applicable request, refusal, review and exemption predicates. Exhausted access requests do not alone establish amendment exhaustion. Nagel recognizes the distinct (e)(7) claim and monetary route; Clarkson links possible expungement to the amendment provisions and their limits; Doe rejects bypassing the Act's remedies. Their qualified accounts supply no unconditional purge warrant. Sections (j) and (k) do not permit exemption from (e)(7), and a regulation cannot defeat that nonexemptible duty merely by exempting civil liability. A valid (d) exemption may nonetheless defeat a derivative amendment claim. A FOIA exemption or general records-system designation does not alone settle those distinct predicates; no waiver is presumed.

Reich and §702 preserve possible nonmonetary restraint subject to an existing lawful action, statutory preclusion and ordinary equitable limits. The Court need not decide that entire route because the monetary action permits this statutory review. No separate APA claim is created. If a mandatory decree is authorized, §702 requires identification of the responsible officer or successor by name or title; absence of a personal name from the caption alone does not answer every equitable question. These reservations govern any further relief, not the independently resolved Article III controversy.

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, for the identified citizen-suit and procedural-right clarification; ordinary Article III elements and evidentiary burdens are retained.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

### Endangered Species Act citizen-suit channels

**Governing question:** Scope of 16 U.S.C. §1540(g) citizen suits and their notice and enforcement conditions.

**Current rule:** The authorization of any person removes a separate species-preservation zone-of-interests restriction within the channels Congress actually authorized; Article III injury remains necessary. Section (g)(1)(C) reaches failure to perform a nondiscretionary §1533 duty, including a required consideration of economic effects of an alleged habitat designation. Section (g)(1)(A) does not authorize general supervision of alleged §1536 maladministration. When a habitat designation or revision actually occurs, §1533(b)(2) requires consideration of economic and other relevant impacts; exclusion is permitted if its benefits outweigh inclusion, but forbidden if the best scientific and commercial data show nondesignation will cause extinction.

**Authority by component:**

- Bennett v. Spear — The citizen-suit language is broad within its actual channels, 1997-03-19; unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies the channel-specific reach and preserved §1533(b)(2) conditions

**Present operation effective:** Bennett, 1997-03-19.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** The (A) route generally requires 60 days’ written notice to the Secretary and alleged violator; a commenced civil-penalty action or a commenced, diligently prosecuted United States criminal action addressing the violation bars it. The (B) route separately requires 60 days’ notice explaining the claimed emergency and is barred by the Secretary’s commenced diligent emergency-determination action. The (C) route requires 60 days’ notice to the Secretary, except that suit concerning an emergency posing significant risk to the well-being of a fish, wildlife or plant species may follow notification immediately.

**Exceptions and limits:** A threshold review route establishes neither that a designation occurred nor that a nondiscretionary duty was triggered, violated or must lead to a particular exclusion.

### Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

**Governing question:** Whether the Communications Act’s power to modify filing requirements permits eliminating them for an entire class.

**Current rule:** Section 203(b)(2) permits the Commission, in its discretion and for good cause shown, to modify requirements made by or under §203 in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions; no required notice period may exceed 120 days. That authority permits adjustments to filing form, contents, timing or place, including consequential and class-wide technical changes, but not replacement of mandatory filing throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system. The inquiry examines the obligation and whether its essential operation is adjusted or removed, not a numerical market-share threshold. Good cause alone cannot authorize the replacement. The Act’s exception for connecting carriers preserves their separate inspection duties; §203(c)’s allowance for otherwise lawful authority does not confer unlimited dispensing power.

**Authority by component:**

- Bounded modification and distinct notice authority: MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co — The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing, 1994-06-17. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Present operation effective:** MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co, 1994-06-17.

**Exceptions and limits:** Limited deferral or waiver is not categorically foreclosed, but no particular waiver is approved. Failure of the special-circumstances-or-conditions predicate is a four-Justice alternative, not a separate controlling holding. No universal major-questions doctrine is created; the statute’s permissible flexibility remains available within the actual delegation.

### National-bank annuity brokerage and §92 classification

**Governing question:** The banking power and insurance provision as applied to conditioned annuity agency sales.

**Current rule:** The business of banking under 12 U.S.C. §24 Seventh is not exhausted by its listed activities. The Comptroller may reasonably include agency sales of fixed, variable and combined annuities as financial-intermediary investment activity, within real banking and incidental-power bounds and express statutory restrictions. Reasonable classification of those products as investments outside §92’s insurance category sustains the reviewed brokerage permission; it authorizes no underwriting, issuance or guarantee of an insurer’s obligations or unrelated commercial enterprise.
