## Standards and Tests selection: Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review. An earlier pleading-stage appeal does not by itself exhaust review of a later summary-judgment order: a separable legal immunity question must independently satisfy the collateral-order conditions. This permits neither repetition of an unchanged ruling nor relitigation of matters resolved by the first mandate. Having to defend a different surviving claim does not itself defeat otherwise authorized claim-specific review, but neither supplies missing authorization nor makes that other claim reviewable.

**Authority by component:**

- Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal: Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal, 1995-06-12. Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.
- Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule: Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine support this legal-review proposition.

- Successive-stage review and preserved mandate limits: Behrens — Holding 1, February 21, 1996; Ginsburg Part I for Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and herself (seven).

**Open question:** Genuinely necessary connected review and a categorical rule for every mixed order remain unresolved. Johnson and Kimberlin use their common entering baseline; neither is treated as earlier same-day law for the other.

## Standards and Tests selection: Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

### Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

**Governing question:** Proof, discovery and causation when unconstitutional motive is an element of a claim and qualified immunity is asserted.

**Current rule:** Qualified immunity imposes no categorical direct-evidence requirement for unconstitutional motive. The plaintiff must establish the actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation through legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence under ordinary procedural burdens. Specific nonconclusory support, lawful discovery limits and the clearly-established-law defense remain available; the rule abolishes neither all heightened specificity requirements nor a burden supplied by the underlying claim. Ordinary preponderance governs unconstitutional motive in the §1983 retaliation claim; immunity and litigation burden cannot justify a special clear-and-convincing quantum. Courts may decide dispositive legal defenses first and require specific nonconclusory support, but cannot categorically withhold discovery needed to obtain proof until the rejected heightened showing is made. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, sequencing, protective orders, relevant proportional discovery and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available. Under Mt. Healthy the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful explanation does not establish that defense or exclude competent motive evidence.

**Authority by component:**

- Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims: Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; nine votes support the rule and its operative limits.

- Crawford-El v. Britton — Proof of unconstitutional motive, 1997-06-16; Stone-Zsela, all nine
- Crawford-El v. Britton — Discovery and stage-specific screening, 1997-06-16; all nine
- Crawford-El v. Britton — Actual causation and independent defenses, 1997-06-16; all nine

**Present operation effective:** Crawford-El, 1997-06-16, rejecting a special proof quantum and derivative categorical discovery barrier while preserving the carried direct/circumstantial-evidence rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims. Crawford-El v. Britton (1997-06-16) — Civil Rights / Qualified immunity and motive evidence.

#### Crawford-El v. Britton

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 94-7203; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-16.

##### Ordinary proof governs unconstitutional motive in this retaliation claim

**Holding:** The ordinary preponderance standard governs proof of unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation claim; neither qualified immunity nor litigation-burden concerns authorize a special clear-and-convincing requirement. Legally competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available, and all actual constitutional elements and independent defenses must still be established or resolved under their applicable burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides the quantum Kimberlin reserved; it neither abolishes every specificity practice nor resolves Leatherman’s individual-officer pleading reservation beyond the barriers decided here. A motive allegation alone defeats no constitutional element, ordinary procedure or conduct-date immunity defense.

##### Necessary discovery cannot be barred by the rejected heightened proof requirement

**Holding:** A court may resolve a genuinely dispositive legal defense first and require specific, nonconclusory support, but it may not categorically demand proof satisfying the rejected heightened quantum before allowing discovery necessary to obtain that proof. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, relevant and proportionate discovery limits, sequencing, protective orders and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available; no unrestricted discovery or particular deposition is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court may clarify allegations, resolve actual dispositive legal defenses and limit discovery to material issues. An adequately supported summary-judgment motion requires responsive evidence; a general credibility attack is insufficient. No fixed discovery plan, particular deposition, unrestricted inquiry, mandatory trial or new interlocutory appeal follows.

##### An actual same-decision showing remains distinct from a conceivable lawful explanation

**Holding:** A merely possible lawful explanation for an official's conduct does not itself establish a same-decision defense or make competent proof of unconstitutional motive inadmissible. Under Mt. Healthy, the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct. The separate clearly-established-law defense remains available on the law governing the conduct when it occurred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Protected communication, personal responsibility, actual motive, causation, injury and conduct-date immunity remain unresolved. Later Kimberlin and Settle doctrine supplies no notice in 1989. Prison-access limits remain and no unrestricted press-conference entitlement is recognized.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the heightened motive burden and dependent directions; preserve the existing remand otherwise. Resolved access-to-courts and due-process claims are not revived. The municipal issue retains its separate panel posture; the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits. No liability, immunity, damages or trial entitlement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald: retains the objective clearly-established-law defense and early legal screening; it supplies no independent heightened evidentiary code.
- Anderson v. Creighton: retains particularized conduct-date notice and appropriately tailored discovery; no ultimate immunity determination is made.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County: uses its source-based municipal pleading reasoning by analogy while preserving its express individual-officer reservation outside the specific categorical barriers resolved here.

#### Fletcher v. Kalina

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit No. 95-36129; no Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** June 16, 1997.

##### Protected prosecutorial advocacy remains distinct from personal evidentiary acts

**Holding:** A prosecutor has absolute immunity under §1983 for preparing and filing an information or warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions, and presenting the State's legal case when performing an advocate's function. An allegation of bad purpose or false evidence does not remove that protection from the advocacy function, but the protection does not extend to a distinct personal evidentiary certification merely because the same prosecutor performs both acts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela joins protection of legitimate advocacy and separation of the personal evidentiary act, but not refusal of an additional dishonesty exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The function, rather than a categorical pre-charge or post-charge boundary, determines immunity. No general dishonesty exception to otherwise protected advocacy is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Imbler preserves charging and courtroom advocacy; Burns separates legal presentation from other acts. Buckley preserves genuine preparation while excluding ordinary investigation; Antoine requires historical and functional justification rather than importance to adjudication.

##### A prosecutor personally certifying arrest-warrant facts acts as a complaining witness

**Holding:** A prosecutor who personally swears to factual assertions offered to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant performs the evidentiary function of a complaining witness and has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for that certification. This classification depends on the personal evidentiary undertaking, not on proof of falsity, and does not withdraw immunity from ordinary courtroom witness testimony.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Kalina personally guaranteed the facts offered to obtain Fletcher's arrest warrant; that evidentiary undertaking is separate from preparing and presenting the charging papers.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither actual falsity nor the availability of another truthful affiant determines the functional classification. Ordinary courtroom testimony immunity remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the denial of absolute immunity for the personal certification and remand, while preserving immunity for protected legal preparation, filing and presentation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Malley supplies the warrant-affiant comparison; official prosecutorial title does not immunize the same evidentiary function. Buckley and Antoine extend their functional distinction to the personal certification, without changing their other boundaries.

##### The function-specific immunity denial leaves constitutional liability and personal defenses unresolved

**Holding:** Denial of absolute immunity for the personal oath leaves Fletcher to establish an actionable constitutional deprivation, the required culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury; Kalina retains qualified immunity and other available personal defenses. The existing qualified-immunity framework governs the remand, using the law and information relevant to the conduct at issue, without a new defense-burden rule or a finding that any element or defense has been established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion, adopted in full by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela joins the unresolved-liability and bounded-remand propositions and accepts the existing qualified-immunity framework, without joining the source-specific causation and notice explanations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A judge's warrant does not categorically break causation when material deception procured it, but a corrected truthful account may still establish probable cause. Neither question is resolved here. Disputed historical facts follow ordinary procedural rules; legal immunity is for the judge. No damages judgment or mandatory trial follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hafer preserves personal-capacity liability only on proof of the officer’s own violation and preserves personal defenses. Harlow and Anderson retain objective, particularized clearly-established-law protection. Elder requires consideration of all relevant precedent; Lanier rejects categorical source and factual-twin restrictions, without supplying retrospective notice.

#### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.
- Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183: distinguishes a requirement that the relevant federal right be clearly established from a rule excluding uncited authority; unrelated duties do not defeat immunity.
- Hunter v. Bryant, December 16, 1991: preserves judicial determination under ordinary summary-judgment constraints using the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; its later decision date supplies no retroactive notice.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: preserves the particularized objective inquiry used in current review; because it was decided after April 27, 1987, it is not itself pre-arrest notice authority.
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: retains its warrant protection against a nonconsensual home arrest absent exigent circumstances for the lower court's application; this decision does not resolve that application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for independent immunity review using all relevant law and the proper summary-judgment record. Ultimate immunity, exigency, Fourth Amendment liability and damages remain undecided; no automatic trial or new factfinding is ordered.

#### Johnson v. Jones

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-455.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

##### Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal

**Holding:** A denial of qualified immunity is not immediately appealable under §1291 insofar as it determines only whether the pretrial evidence presents a genuine factual dispute about the official's participation in the alleged conduct. Mitchell's immediate appeal remains available for the separable legal question whether the conduct, on the relevant assumed facts, violated clearly established law; the present excessive-force participation dispute does not meet that condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A proper legal immunity appeal uses the district court's assumed facts. If unstated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bootstrap factual review. Genuine necessary connected review and future mixed-order questions remain at their reserved scope.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541 (1949): applies separateness as a cumulative collateral-order condition.

#### Behrens v. Pelletier, 516 U.S. 299

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1244.

**Decided:** February 21, 1996.

##### A prior appeal does not categorically exhaust legal-immunity review

**Holding:** An earlier pleading-stage qualified-immunity appeal does not, merely because it occurred, bar a later summary-judgment appeal presenting a separable legal immunity question that otherwise satisfies the collateral-order requirements. The later appeal may address legal immunity on properly assumed facts; this rule authorizes neither factual-sufficiency review nor repeated appeals of an unchanged legal ruling or matters resolved by the first mandate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cohen's cumulative conditions and Mitchell's legal-immunity category apply. Digital Equipment's importance and category discipline remains; Johnson supplies no numerical ceiling and retains its factual boundary.

##### An authorized appeal uses assumed facts and excludes factual sufficiency

**Holding:** Within an otherwise authorized qualified-immunity appeal, the appellate court may decide whether conduct on the District Court's properly assumed or sufficiently supported facts violates clearly established law, but may not choose which factual account is true or reweigh whether evidence supports it. If the relevant assumptions are unstated, the court may identify the likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant in the manner Johnson permits, without deciding truth or evidentiary sufficiency.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, unanimous. Stevens and Breyer condition agreement on an otherwise authorized appeal and do not find successive authorization here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Johnson and Kimberlin preserve assumed-fact review without factual selection. Elder requires relevant conduct-date law but creates no jurisdiction or retrospective notice. Swint rejects convenience-based expansion; Doe's factual-sufficiency exclusion remains.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No immunity, constitutional violation, property interest, factual truth, evidentiary sufficiency or damages issue is decided.

##### A different surviving claim does not itself defeat a qualifying immunity appeal

**Holding:** The fact that an official must still defend another claim does not, by itself, eliminate an otherwise authorized immediate appeal of a separable legal immunity denial on a different claim. That claim-specific principle neither supplies missing appellate authorization nor makes the other claim reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join; Stevens and Breyer continue to oppose successive appeal as of right.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mitchell protects against defending the claim to which immunity applies; Swint prevents a proper appeal from carrying unrelated matters and leaves its necessary-connected-review reservation open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse only the categorical Ninth Circuit dismissal and remand to identify proper assumptions favorable to the nonmovant and any actually reviewable legal issue, dismissing factual-sufficiency and otherwise unavailable issues. The summary-judgment denial is not reversed, immunity is not awarded, and neither the separate United States negligence claim nor earlier substitution ruling is reopened. No stay or permission is granted.

#### United States v. Lanier

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1717.
**Decided:** March 31, 1997.

##### What law must supply fair warning under 18 U.S.C. §242

**Holding:** Section 242, read with authoritative constructions, must have made the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably apparent when the defendant acted. Neither a later decision nor willful bad purpose can supply missing conduct-time notice, and novel or unforeseeable criminal enlargement is forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Moral reprehensibility and state criminality do not replace a definite federal right and notice. Vagueness, lenity, and the bar on unexpected retrospective enlargement retain related but distinct functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* supplies definite-right and separate willfulness requirements; courts construe the criminal statute rather than create common-law offenses.

##### Whether notice requires a Supreme Court decision on fundamentally similar facts

**Holding:** Fair warning may come from relevant pre-existing lower-court authority and from an obviously covered application of an established rule without a factual twin. Neither a Supreme-Court-only requirement nor a fundamentally-similar-facts prerequisite is lawful; conversely, an abstract liberty or bodily-security proposition does not settle an uncertain application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** One lower-court decision or a fixed numerical consensus is not automatically sufficient. Actual conflicts, authority, particular context, and express reservations matter. No count's constitutional basis, notice, personal responsibility, or official nexus is resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Elder* supplies examination of all relevant existing authority. *Anderson* requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not repeated factual identity. *Pinder* and *Uhlrig* preserve unsettled-application and attribution limits; later holdings cannot supply earlier notice.

##### Whether the civil clarity standard substitutes for criminal elements

**Holding:** Criminal fair warning under §242 and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: pre-existing law must make the constitutional unlawfulness apparent. The common threshold does not displace proof of deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the criminal burden of proof, or any other statutory element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Civil immunity analysis cannot itself convict, and judicial office alone does not prove that every act occurred under color of law. No categorically stricter criminal clarity standard is adopted.

##### Whether due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody are categorically excluded

**Holding:** Appellate review may not exclude due-process rights from §242 categorically or treat the victim's lack of custody as automatic immunity for an official's own assault. Where a specific constitutional provision covers the claim, that provision governs; the Fourth and Eighth Amendments do not exhaust every potentially covered abusive official act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the en banc judgment and remand for count-specific consideration under the proper conduct-time notice rule and all remaining properly presented issues. The mandate does not reinstate convictions or sentence, order acquittal or retrial, or require a new evidentiary hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal constitutional-assault formula or factual assault finding is made. Constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation, omitted protection, and supervisory attribution remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* and *Price* preserve §242's due-process reach. *DeShaney*'s private-violence rule does not immunize direct official wrongdoing. *Graham* supplies the specific-provision rule. *Collins*, *Doe v. Taylor*, *Pinder*, *Uhlrig*, and *Nabozny* retain their distinct deprivation and attribution boundaries, without supplying notice retrospectively.

## Holdings

**1. Immediate review separates legal immunity from historical facts.** An interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal may resolve a separable legal defense on the proper facts assumed in the nonmovant's favor, but may not decide factual sufficiency, participation, credibility or which disputed account is true. Ultimate constitutional voluntariness is not wholly historical merely because its test considers circumstances. Part I is unanimous.

Johnson and Behrens protect the assumed historical account without foreclosing genuine legal review. Section 1254 permits this Court to review the circuit case but does not enlarge the circuit's §1291 authority. Miller distinguishes the ultimate voluntariness question from historical facts; Ornelas preserves task-specific allocation rather than a universal rule for mixed questions. A court therefore cannot avoid a legal immunity question by labeling the entire coercion inquiry factual, or obtain factual review by attaching a legal label. The factual portions of Cooper's appeal must be dismissed; the distinct reviewable questions remain.

**2. The assumed material warrant deception defeats the asserted legal immunity defense.** On the proper assumed facts, an officer cannot obtain immunity for knowing or reckless factual falsehoods material to probable cause merely because a judge issued the warrant. Franks and Malley supplied the relevant pre-August-1993 rule. Part II-A's complete rule has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela additionally joins its application to the assumed deliberate material falsehoods and its subsidiary-theory limits.

The rule requires actual culpable falsity, personal responsibility and materiality. A truthful corrected account may still support probable cause, and a later recantation does not itself prove that the affiant knowingly fabricated evidence. Judicial issuance does not categorically sever the responsibility of an officer whose material deception procured the warrant. Cooper may contest the facts at the proper stage, but cannot secure interlocutory reversal by substituting his account for Clanton's. The Court does not find what the affidavit actually contained or place Bobby's derivative statement into it as independent probable cause.

The three subsidiary theories remain rejected: sufficiently reliable hearsay may support probable cause; the Fourth Amendment does not universally require filed criminal charges before a warrant; and the federal grand-jury indictment requirement does not govern state prosecutions. These propositions establish neither universal reliability of accomplice statements nor federal liability from a state-law warrant defect.

**3. Deliberate material detention deception does not require a prior case using the same communication system.** The asserted categorical immunity defense fails on the assumed personal communication of deliberate material homicide and no-bond falsehoods to procure otherwise unjustified additional unreasonable physical detention actually caused by that message. The established Franks/Malley prohibition on material official deception has an obvious application to this particular seizure; electronic transmission and a different custodian do not require a factually identical earlier NCIC case. Part II-B is unanimous.

The arrest under the original warrant and the later message's alleged extension of custody are distinct events. The Oklahoma warrant's $5,000 bond provision does not itself establish a federal release entitlement, and Texas's authority to set bond does not prove that the same detention would independently have occurred. The claim requires falsehood, culpability, materiality, attributable causation and an actual unreasonable physical seizure. An inaccurate message, bad motive, state-law breach or passage of one to three days alone is insufficient. If the same lawful detention would have occurred without the message, or Cooper had no material causal role, the pertinent defense remains available. ### The trial motion did not preserve search immunity

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies nine direct votes for this rule, its application and all stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 50(a)(2) gives the court and opposing party an opportunity to address the actual ground before submission. The answer pleaded immunity, but a pleading is not itself the required trial motion.

Counsel expressly requested judgment on the stop on the merits or immunity, then separately argued that the search was lawful. The complete reported presentation identified no search-immunity ground or incorporation supplying it. Search legality and immunity for an objectively reasonable legal mistake are distinct, even when they may overlap. Its affirmance of the municipal judgments and the unappealed state-law dispositions are outside this Court's review. No Fourth or Eighth Amendment claim is presented.

## Holdings

**Holding and operative rule:** In the rapidly evolving high-speed pursuit presented here, where officers must make instant choices amid competing enforcement and safety obligations, reckless or deliberately indifferent risk-taking alone does not establish a substantive-due-process violation. A purpose to cause harm unrelated to a legitimate law-enforcement objective is required; because this plaintiff-favorable record does not establish that purpose, the reviewed federal individual claim fails.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes for the complete rule and application. Neither the two distinct constitutional votes nor Stevens's immunity vote supplies additional support for that rule. No combination of separate rationales is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** Rochin identifies the constitutional boundary against arbitrary executive abuse; its conscience-shocking standard must be applied through precedent, constitutional tradition and the actual setting, not a judge's personal reaction. Daniels excludes negligence but left intermediate culpability unresolved. Whitley explains why competing obligations and an emergency matter to fault, while reserving the civilian due-process question. Farmer's actual-awareness standard concerns prison safety with a materially different opportunity to respond; Doe likewise requires knowledge, authority and a reasonable opportunity for protection against deliberate abuse. Those holdings do not mechanically determine culpability in a rapid pursuit.

The officer chose to pursue, and the favorable account includes dangerous speed, close following distance, danger to others, inadequate communication and alleged policy violations. The lack of a serious predicate offense and the ability to abandon pursuit are substantial criticisms. They do not, in this rapidly developing encounter, establish the improper harmful purpose required for constitutional liability. The collision was unintended, and the supported record supplies no purpose to injure unrelated to enforcement. The Court finds neither wise police judgment nor an adjudicated benevolent motive.

Actual opportunity for deliberation remains important. This rule is not a universal intent requirement, an automatic deliberate-indifference rule whenever an official could desist, or permission to manufacture an emergency. Purposeful collisions, deliberate terrorizing conduct, contrived encounters and materially different opportunities for considered action require their own facts and analysis. The decision creates no seconds-based cutoff, broad noncustodial immunity or mandatory order between constitutional merits and qualified immunity. It resolves neither the excluded municipal and state claims nor a procedural-due-process challenge to state remedies.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Rochin v. California, 342 U.S. 165:** Applied as the executive-abuse boundary, with the required culpability identified through the actual emergency setting.
- **Daniels v. Williams, 474 U.S. 327:** Its negligence exclusion remains; the Court resolves the previously open intermediate-culpability question only for this rapid pursuit.
- **Whitley v. Albers, 475 U.S. 312:** Its competing-obligation analysis informs the rule, but its prison-security holding is not treated as already deciding civilian pursuit liability.
- **Farmer v. Brennan, June 6, 1994:** Distinguished by the custodial duty and actual opportunity for response. Its awareness, reasonable-response and permissible-inference rules remain intact.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District, October 16, 1995:** Distinguished because knowing supervisory acquiescence in deliberate abuse requires authority, opportunity and causal participation not supplied merely by continuing this pursuit.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights, February 26, 1992:** Its bounded workplace-omission holdings do not automatically resolve affirmative police conduct or merge the underlying right with municipal attribution.
- **Pinder v. Johnson, May 21, 1996:** Its distinction between nonprotection and affirmative deprivation remains; it did not decide this culpability question.
- **Uhlrig v. Harder, February 18, 1997:** Its conduct-time immunity disposition is preserved without importing the lower court's unadopted merits test or a mandatory merits-first sequence.
- **United States v. Lanier, March 31, 1997:** Its rejection of categorical immunity for noncustodial official wrongdoing remains. A governing specific constitutional provision must be used where it covers a claim; that principle does not itself dispose of every due-process claim.
- **Albright v. Oliver, January 24, 1994:** Its fractured judgment supplies no controlling general displacement rationale for this case.
- **Washington v. Glucksberg, June 26, 1997:** Its assistance-in-suicide holding remains limited; no exclusive most-specific-history methodology governing all liberties is established or displaced here.

## Law After Decision

The decision establishes a purpose-to-harm requirement for the supported rapid-pursuit setting and rejects the circuit's use of reckless disregard or deliberate indifference alone there. Existing deliberate-indifference rules retain their distinct duties, opportunities for response and proof requirements. Constitutional wrongdoing remains separate from qualified immunity, municipal attribution and state-law liability. A purposeful or materially more deliberative encounter is not resolved merely by calling it a pursuit.

## Separate Writings

Stevens concurs only in the judgment. He would resolve the individual claim on qualified immunity without deciding the difficult constitutional standard. All relevant law existing on May 22, 1990 must be considered under the particularized-notice approach. Garner and Brower concern intentional force or intentionally applied stopping means; Checki concerns intentional misuse of police power to terrorize; Wood II concerns affirmative interference with protective circumstances. The reported pursuit decisions did not establish this claim's constitutional boundary merely by recognizing a possible egregious case, and Daniels reserved intermediate culpability. Neither the absence of an identical case nor a tally of judicial disagreement decides immunity. On the complete favorable account, the existing law did not make this pursuit's federal unlawfulness sufficiently apparent. Later decisions cannot supply retrospective notice. He makes no finding that the conduct was constitutional.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurs in the judgment on a different constitutional ground. The carefully described asserted right against this reckless pursuit and unintended collision lacks sufficient textual, historical or precedential support. A general protection of life or bodily security does not by itself define the constitutional tort. They reject the Court's reliance on an open-ended conscience standard and do not join its affirmative purpose-to-harm definition, although they agree that these facts establish no constitutional violation. Glucksberg does not impose a universally exclusive history method; their objection rests on the absence of adequate support for this particular asserted right and on the limits of judicially defined constitutional tort liability. Their position does not categorically exclude direct intentional assaults outside custody.

Breyer fully joins the Court. He separately preserves flexibility to decide a suitable case on qualified immunity before resolving a difficult or poorly presented constitutional question. That approach is consistent with Uhlrig and the distinction between actual wrongdoing and conduct-time notice. Today's choice to resolve the pursuit standard is not a compulsory merits-first sequence for every later case.

## Holdings

### 1. The two subordinates receive personal immunity on the actual notice and role record

**Holding and operative rule:** Astle and Blank receive qualified immunity because, during the 1988–1989 processing at issue, the law did not make their own conduct's federal unlawfulness sufficiently apparent in the supported administrative circumstances. Consultation with a superior is relevant to those circumstances but is not an automatic defense, and a state regulatory deadline violation does not alone establish a clearly established federal violation.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion for all eight Associate Justices controls the complete ground. Stone-Zsela also joins the no-automatic-defense proposition but agrees with the immunity judgment on a different ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson require objective, particularized conduct-time notice. Elder requires consideration of all relevant authority; Lanier permits an obviously covered application without an identical prior licensing case. Neither those later decisions nor Lewis can supply notice retrospectively. Davis v. Scherer separates a state-law breach from the required federal obligation.

The earlier revocation was judicially sustained, while later administrative proceedings expunged the underlying indications. Expungement did not itself adjudicate automatic reinstatement. Astle sought Blank's advice about that relationship; Blank consulted Turner, and the consultation reached state regional official Klump. Klump's direction to decide went to Turner. The record does not establish that Astle or Blank personally received and defied that instruction or possessed final authority to resolve the conflict.

Prolonged inaction may deny the practical benefit of a hearing available only after denial, and asking a supervisor cannot excuse knowingly refusing a plainly required act within one's control. Those concerns do not establish these two defendants' personal liability on this supported role and notice record. The state rules required investigation within 75 days of a valid application and notification within 30 days after completion, with a hearing after denial; those commands do not themselves decide the federal process claim or entitlement to certification. The Court resolves immunity without deciding the ultimate deprivation or excusing every administrative delay.

### 2. The official-capacity judgment requires an actual Rule 50 and municipal-liability determination

**Holding and operative rule:** Denial of a defendant's summary-judgment motion is not a judgment establishing the plaintiff's liability case and cannot, merely as law of the case, justify a directed liability judgment at trial. DuRose's official-capacity judgment must instead be evaluated on the actual trial evidence under Rule 50 and the requirements of an attributable county policy or authorized final decision causing an established federal deprivation; personal qualified immunity supplies no county defense.

**Authority:** Part II has all eight Associate Justices for the complete Rule 50 correction and bounded remand. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the entity-capacity, separate-deprivation/policy/causation and no-personal-immunity propositions, but not the additional Rule 50 rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Kentucky v. Graham identifies the official-capacity judgment as the entity's judgment. Monell excludes automatic employer liability, and Pembaur requires an actual authorized final policy decision when that is the asserted basis. McMillian directs attention to the entity and the challenged function under applicable law and legally effective usage. A title, operational discretion, employment or absence of immediate supervision alone does not establish final policymaking; its Alabama holding identifies no Oneida policymaker. Collins keeps the underlying federal deprivation distinct from its municipal attribution.

The trial court treated an earlier denial of DuRose's summary-judgment request as though liability already had been established. That premise bypassed the required trial-record determination. The familiar district court must now evaluate that record and explain any supported Rule 50 ruling. Judgment for plaintiffs, judgment for the county or factual issues for a jury remain possible; the Court selects none in advance and orders no automatic new trial. The complete trial record is not reproduced here, and that limitation supplies no adverse factual finding. Neither the $300,000 judgment nor its dependent fee/cost award can remain on the erroneous premise, but the Court fixes no proper amount or independent fee entitlement.

### 3. The distinct later personal claim requires its own identified disposition

**Holding and operative rule:** An order addressing the original pre-expungement pleading cannot dispose of a distinct post-expungement personal claim added later merely by an informal assertion that all personal claims were dismissed. The district court must identify any actual ruling and its basis, or adjudicate the pending request under the standard governing its actual procedural stage.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all eight Associate Justices, controls this procedural ground. Stone-Zsela supports the remand disposition without joining the additional rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The original complaint concerned earlier acts, while the amended pleading added the later processing claim. Treating an earlier order as a disposition of a not-yet-pleaded claim fails to identify what was decided. An informal letter does not fill that gap. If a ruling occurred, its actual basis must be explained; otherwise the pending request requires decision. Pleading review cannot demand trial proof, and summary judgment must use its own record and standard. Crawford-El preserves ordinary procedural burdens and case management rather than a special evidentiary barrier. Nothing in this remand finds DuRose personally aware of the consultation chain, responsible for every subordinate act or liable for a federal violation. The Article 78 adequate-process contention remains undecided for appropriate consideration below.

## Holdings

### 1. Knowing, involuntary transfer into danger states a bounded due-process claim

**Holding and operative rule:** An officer who actually restrains a person's ability to protect herself and personally directs or carries out her involuntary transfer into a serious danger the officer actually appreciates may not deliberately and unreasonably disregard that danger when the officer has authority and a real opportunity to respond. The claim requires injury attributable to that defendant's conduct; negligence, constructive knowledge, another officer's knowledge, assistance in a genuinely voluntary departure or a reasonable protective response that fails does not suffice.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion for all nine Justices controls this direct-restraint application. It adopts no broader free-standing conscience framework or general prison-to-roadside rule.

**Controlling explanation:** DeShaney distinguishes failure to protect from the State's affirmative restraint of a person's ability to protect herself. Youngberg supplies the custody-related protection principle, Daniels excludes negligence, and Collins distinguishes actual compulsion from voluntary exposure. Pinder's noncoercive assurances did not create custody and do not foreclose this alleged compelled transfer. Farmer, Doe and Lewis preserve actual awareness, individual responsibility, reasonable response and genuine opportunity; their distinct settings do not supply a mechanical roadside checklist.

The favorable account alleges specific warnings of Kritis's violence and intoxication, a threat of arrest if Black did not leave with him, and physical removal and placement in his truck. Those successive actions differ from Lewis's rapidly unfolding pursuit. Neither the earlier private chase nor the later crash turns the roadside transfer into a police-pursuit case. No fixed number of seconds establishes opportunity or culpability.

Dusing allegedly received warnings and directed the threat; Dolan and Alsip allegedly removed Black, and Alsip placed her in the truck. Reuthe's alcohol report is not itself culpable placement. Alsip's limited information and the competing voluntary-departure account remain material. Knowledge and responsibility cannot be pooled. Obvious danger may support an inference of actual awareness but does not compel it. The fatal crash permits the causal theory, not a finding against every officer; the renewed fight, uncertain steering cause and legally proper scope of the state causal ruling remain relevant. The Court creates no general duty to investigate every warning, arrest Kritis or prevent private violence, and does not reach noncustodial state-created danger.

### 2. The favorable knowing-coercion account cannot be dismissed categorically on qualified-immunity grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** Qualified immunity does not categorically bar Black's claim on the favorable allegation that an officer knowingly used actual police restraint to compel her transfer into an appreciated serious danger. The relevant clarity is that of February 18–19, 1994, assessed for each officer's own information, authority and acts; the ruling leaves supported factual and legal defenses available at their proper stage.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls this separate conduct-time immunity holding; it neither finds every defendant liable nor substitutes a new defense for Harlow and Anderson.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson ask whether existing law made the actual conduct's federal unlawfulness sufficiently apparent. DeShaney, Youngberg, Daniels and Collins supplied the restraint/direct-deprivation distinction before this encounter. Meador and Heflin applied custodial deliberate indifference. Walton's absence of police custody distinguishes its immunity result. Nishiyama's intentional unreasonable exposure to a known danger supplies relevant warning, but its gross-negligence language and contested noncustodial reach are neither adopted nor indispensable.

An exact roadside factual twin is unnecessary, while a general assertion of bodily security would be insufficient by itself. The alleged knowing use of police control supplies the particular connection. Neither uncertainty about noncustodial protection nor disagreement about every officer's information justifies dismissing that favorable account categorically.

Elder's February 23, 1994 decision, Farmer and later decisions including Pinder, Doe, Lanier, Lewis and DeCarlo may clarify present analysis but cannot supply missing notice retroactively. DeCarlo reinforces personal role and stage-specific adjudication; its different administrative setting does not compel immunity here. There is no mandatory order between difficult constitutional merits and immunity. The Court resolves this properly presented narrow claim without determining disputed knowledge, actual opportunity, causation or the outcome of a later supported motion.

### 3. Probable cause does not defeat the pleaded arbitrary-selection claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Probable cause to arrest does not defeat an equal-protection claim that officers deliberately selected an otherwise chargeable driver solely to impose arbitrary group hostility while deliberately leaving an actually comparable driver unenforced on that basis. The claimant must establish discriminatory purpose and effect, relevant comparison, each defendant's participation and attributable injury; a supported legitimate enforcement distinction defeats the asserted arbitrary comparison.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls this narrow selective-enforcement rule, without an orientation-based scrutiny tier.

**Controlling explanation:** Yick Wo prohibits arbitrary discriminatory enforcement; Oyler preserves enforcement discretion while rejecting selection on an unjustifiable arbitrary basis; Wayte requires purpose and effect. Moreno excludes bare group hostility as a legitimate governmental end. Whren preserves an independent equality claim despite objective Fourth Amendment authority, and Romer applies ordinary review's prohibition on bare hostility without creating a new tier.

Stemler alleges that officers deliberately arrested her while leaving the visibly intoxicated Kritis unenforced because they perceived her as a lesbian and sought to impose arbitrary harm. Her denial of that orientation does not erase the alleged perceived classification. Wince arrested her; Wince, Dusing and Dolan are her individual defendants. Other defendants on Black's claim are not automatically defendants on hers.

Probable cause, a hostile epithet, knowledge of Kritis's characterization, differential arrest or later acquittal alone proves neither purpose nor the necessary comparison. The favorable account permits the claim, but legitimate differences and each defendant's actual responsibility remain for proof. Bowers's particular conduct holding does not authorize hostile use of unrelated DUI laws. The Court imposes no duty to arrest every offender, imports no Armstrong prosecutor-discovery threshold and prescribes no universal new burden sequence from Crawford-El. It resolves neither every kind of personal animosity nor every orientation classification.

### 4. The narrow arbitrary-selection prohibition predates this encounter

**Holding and operative rule:** The existing prohibition against purposeful arbitrary group-based enforcement permits Stemler's narrow claim to proceed despite qualified immunity, subject to proof of the actual comparator, arbitrary purpose, effect and each officer's conduct. The Court relies on law existing before February 18–19, 1994, not later recognition of a new protected classification or retroactive notice from Romer or Whren.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls this distinct immunity application and its proof limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Yick Wo, Oyler, Wayte and Moreno already prohibited the particular arbitrary selection alleged here. Oyler did not make every selective enforcement choice unconstitutional; its rejection of mere selectivity and insufficient statistics remains. The claim rests on deliberate selection for bare group harm, supported by an actual comparator, rather than a demand for heightened scrutiny or an inference from nonarrest alone.

The competing contention that no established orientation tier existed fails to answer that narrower prohibition. Bowers did not exempt unrelated enforcement from equal protection. Romer and Whren clarify present doctrine but supply no February 1994 notice. Harlow and Anderson still require each defendant's particular conduct to fall within the established prohibition. The decision permits ordinary proof and defenses; it does not adjudicate motive, comparative intoxication or responsibility as established facts.

## Holdings

### 1. The internal plan does not establish the asserted categorical bar

**Holding and operative rule:** The existence of the Judicial Conference's nonstatutory internal EEO procedure does not, on the supported remedial record, displace this received individual Fifth Amendment employment-discrimination damages action under Davis v. Passman. The decision establishes no direct action against the court as an agency, no Title VII coverage and no rule foreclosing consideration of a different congressional remedial scheme or another properly supported special factor.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion has all nine Justices for this bounded rejection of the offered bar.

**Controlling explanation:** Davis recognizes the individual constitutional employment action. Meyer rejects a new direct federal-agency action while preserving received individual actions at their own scope; its agency holding cannot simply be transferred to these defendants. Bush requires attention to an actual congressional remedial design. The reported plan originated with the Judicial Conference, and no supported congressional direction makes it the asserted exclusive substitute.

The judicial-employment setting and absence of Title VII coverage warrant attention to the real remedial arrangement, not an inference that any internal procedure necessarily extinguishes the received action. An unquoted plan term or unsupported statutory scheme cannot supply the missing bar. The Court does not hold that only an express exclusivity sentence could ever matter or that internal procedures are always irrelevant.

ALPA likewise requires examination of the institution and the authority assigned to it; it supplies no universal consent exception or blanket rule against preliminary recourse. Proper case management remains, and a materially different exhaustion question requires its own supported record. This holding neither grants automatic equitable relief nor decides the plaintiff's ultimate discrimination case.

### 2. Selecting the chief probation officer is administrative hiring

**Holding and operative rule:** Selection of a chief probation officer is personnel administration, not adjudication, and receives no absolute judicial immunity merely because judges make the choice under statutory appointment authority or embody it in an order. Rejecting that absolute defense leaves qualified immunity, personal responsibility, the constitutional elements and the legal basis for each requested remedy distinct.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine Justices for the functional classification and rejection of absolute immunity.

**Controlling explanation:** Forrester distinguishes personnel administration from judging disputes. Antoine requires historical and functional justification for absolute immunity; importance to judicial work does not suffice. Bogan protects prospective legislative policy while expressly preserving the difference from individualized administrative personnel decisions.

The chief probation officer performs important duties assisting the courts. Neither those duties nor section 3602's placement of appointment authority in judges transforms selecting an employee into deciding a case. Judicial title, operational significance and the formal appointment instrument therefore do not answer the functional question. No legislative abolition or adjudicative act is presented.

The ruling does not declare discrimination or remove every personal defense. Harlow and Anderson govern the actual conduct-time notice and individual conduct questions. Davis and Forrester predate the 1993–1994 events; later decisions such as DeCarlo and Florence guide present adjudication but cannot create earlier notice. The Court neither grants nor conclusively rejects every defendant's qualified-immunity defense on an undeveloped record.

### 3. The supported request requires genuine procedural reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule:** Before sustaining summary judgment on this record, the lower courts must assess Duffy's specific Rule 56(f) request and the material evidence under ordinary standards, without treating the defendants' favorable affidavits or judicial status as conclusive proof of lawful motive. Specific nonconclusory support remains necessary; the court may clarify, tailor or deny an inadequately supported request, resolve an actually dispositive legal issue first, and enter renewed summary judgment when the properly assessed record warrants it.

**Authority:** Part III's complete rule and remedy have the eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela joins the rejection of special evidentiary protection and the prematurity conclusion but would require discovery before further dispositive rulings; that additional mandate is not the Court's holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Crawford-El preserves ordinary motive proof, necessary tailored inquiry, early legal defenses and protective case management; neither unavailable proof demanded in advance nor a general hope of impeachment decides every request. Hicks leaves ultimate persuasion with the plaintiff: rejection of an explanation may support an inference but neither compels liability nor requires a special additional category of evidence. DeCarlo reinforces individual responsibility and decision at the proper procedural stage.

McPhillips had substantial experience, education, favorable references and interview assessments. Those are genuine lawful explanations, not a finding of discriminatory pretext. Inclusive recruitment and a neutral vacancy notice are lawful. The law-clerk comparison concerns different work and inadequately described pools and may remain weak or immaterial.

But Ailts's account refers to interest in appointing a female, while Wolle describes recruiting a diverse qualified pool. Ailts's uncertain source and inferred position limit the inference; they do not conclusively rewrite his account as recruitment alone. No panel policy or adoption by any defendant is found. Counsel's actual request sought to test the stated selection reasons. The district court's reliance on favorable defense and screener affidavits requires a genuine assessment of that request rather than treating production as conclusive proof of motive. An all-male panel carries no special presumption against discrimination.

The Court does not order any particular deposition, a broad personnel inquiry or a trial. More precise identification of sought facts, written inquiry, sequencing and protection of unrelated judicial work remain available. Each defendant's knowledge, participation and purpose must be assessed separately. No clear-and-convincing rule was imposed below, and none is removed by fiction; the correction concerns application of ordinary rules to this record.
