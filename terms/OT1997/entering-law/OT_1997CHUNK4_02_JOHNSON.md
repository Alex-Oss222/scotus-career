#### Sandin v. Conner

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1911.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty

**Holding:** A State creates protected prison liberty when substantive law meaningfully restricts officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation. Mandatory wording alone is insufficient: courts must examine the genuine constraint in the whole disciplinary scheme and the meaningful restraint, without making state codification necessary for directly protected constitutional liberty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is sufficient and nonexclusive. Mandatory procedures, routine transfers and brief inconveniences do not automatically create entitlements; direct constitutional, unexpected-restraint and sentence-duration interests remain. No atypical-and-significant-hardship test controls.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hewitt v. Helms: narrows mechanical reliance on mandatory wording while retaining genuine substantive-predicate liberty protection; no wholesale methodology replacement controls.
- Kentucky Department of Corrections v. Thompson: applies distinction between substantive constraints and procedural directions alone.
- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves significant prison liberty and context-sensitive procedural protection.
- Meachum v. Fano: distinguishes ordinary sentence-authorized management from a genuine protected constraint.
- Vitek v. Jones; Washington v. Harper: preserve distinct directly protected, qualitatively different restraints; this state-entitlement rule is nonexclusive.
- Farmer v. Brennan; Helling v. McKinney: distinguish Eighth Amendment conditions liability from this procedural-liberty inquiry.

##### The reviewed summary judgment and remand

**Holding:** The summary judgment rejecting the reviewed liberty-and-process claim cannot stand on a mechanical reading of Hawaii’s regulatory words or dismissal of genuine punitive constraints. The district court must determine whether the rules genuinely constrain authority, whether the deprivation is meaningful rather than routine, and what process is due; the Court makes no liability or ultimate protected-interest finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Opinion of the Court and remedial disposition; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg join the bounded rule-and-remand ground. Four concurring Justices separately would find the interest already established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantial-evidence language is not rewritten as an express acquittal command; the whole misconduct, impartial-adjudication, evidence and sanction scheme matters. Punitive labels alone do not establish liberty; similarity to separately authorized administrative or protective custody alone does not defeat a punitive constraint. Later expungement supplies no retroactive process and discretionary parole consequences are not inevitable sentence extension. Wolff permits refusal of witnesses for irrelevance, lack of necessity or safety or correctional hazards. Neither the four concurring votes finding an established interest nor the dissent's four-vote replacement framework controls under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wolff: applies witness qualifications and the separate process inquiry once protected liberty is implicated.
- Hewitt; Thompson: require whole-scheme substantive analysis rather than a one-way mandatory-word inference.
- Meachum: preserves ordinary authorized custody without making every punitive restraint legally unconstrained.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit in the reviewed respect, leaving reversal of officials' summary judgment effective, and remand for genuine constraint, meaningful deprivation and due process determinations. The Court finds neither ultimate protected liberty nor liability; unrelated amended-complaint claims and defenses are not resolved.

#### Crawford-El v. Britton

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 94-7203; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-16.

##### Ordinary proof governs unconstitutional motive in this retaliation claim

**Holding:** The ordinary preponderance standard governs proof of unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation claim; neither qualified immunity nor litigation-burden concerns authorize a special clear-and-convincing requirement. Legally competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available, and all actual constitutional elements and independent defenses must still be established or resolved under their applicable burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides the quantum Kimberlin reserved; it neither abolishes every specificity practice nor resolves Leatherman’s individual-officer pleading reservation beyond the barriers decided here. A motive allegation alone defeats no constitutional element, ordinary procedure or conduct-date immunity defense.

##### Necessary discovery cannot be barred by the rejected heightened proof requirement

**Holding:** A court may resolve a genuinely dispositive legal defense first and require specific, nonconclusory support, but it may not categorically demand proof satisfying the rejected heightened quantum before allowing discovery necessary to obtain that proof. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, relevant and proportionate discovery limits, sequencing, protective orders and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available; no unrestricted discovery or particular deposition is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court may clarify allegations, resolve actual dispositive legal defenses and limit discovery to material issues. An adequately supported summary-judgment motion requires responsive evidence; a general credibility attack is insufficient. No fixed discovery plan, particular deposition, unrestricted inquiry, mandatory trial or new interlocutory appeal follows.

##### An actual same-decision showing remains distinct from a conceivable lawful explanation

**Holding:** A merely possible lawful explanation for an official's conduct does not itself establish a same-decision defense or make competent proof of unconstitutional motive inadmissible. Under Mt. Healthy, the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct. The separate clearly-established-law defense remains available on the law governing the conduct when it occurred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Protected communication, personal responsibility, actual motive, causation, injury and conduct-date immunity remain unresolved. Later Kimberlin and Settle doctrine supplies no notice in 1989. Prison-access limits remain and no unrestricted press-conference entitlement is recognized.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the heightened motive burden and dependent directions; preserve the existing remand otherwise. Resolved access-to-courts and due-process claims are not revived. The municipal issue retains its separate panel posture; the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits. No liability, immunity, damages or trial entitlement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kimberlin v. Quinlan: applies the distinction between motive as a constitutional element and objective immunity, competent circumstantial proof and lawful case management; decides here the proof quantum Kimberlin left open.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald: retains the objective clearly-established-law defense and early legal screening; it supplies no independent heightened evidentiary code.
- Anderson v. Creighton: retains particularized conduct-date notice and appropriately tailored discovery; no ultimate immunity determination is made.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County: uses its source-based municipal pleading reasoning by analogy while preserving its express individual-officer reservation outside the specific categorical barriers resolved here.
- Siegert v. Gilley: preserves early examination of a cognizable constitutional deprivation without converting its threshold holding into a special motive burden.
- Anderson v. Liberty Lobby: applies a valid governing substantive burden at summary judgment, without authorizing courts to invent a new one.
- New York Times Co. v. Sullivan: distinguishes the constitutional speech basis of its defamation burden from this retaliation action against an official.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle: applies the claimant's protected-conduct and motivating-factor burden and the defendant's separate preponderance same-decision defense.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board: applies its actual-causation distinction, not school-specific substantive rules; a possible lawful reason is insufficient by itself.
- Pell v. Procunier and Turner v. Safley: preserve prison-access limitations and supply no unrestricted press-conference right.
- Johnson v. Jones and Behrens v. Pelletier: preserve the distinction between legal immunity review and disputed factual sufficiency; no new interlocutory-review right is created.

#### Young v. Harper

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1598.
**Decided:** March 18, 1997.

##### Whether preparole community freedom is protected existing liberty

**Holding:** A prisoner released to live at home, work and participate in ordinary community life under parole-like conditions possesses conditional liberty protected by Morrissey. Legal custody, the preparole label, an overcrowding purpose or denial of formal parole does not dispense with revocation process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Parts II–IV, unanimous.

**Material application:** The August 1991 procedure cannot by itself establish a condition communicated at the October 1990 release or validate the March 1991 telephone return order. Silence in the earlier procedure does not establish all release conditions, prove compliance or make release irrevocable.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Five months is not a constitutional minimum. Formal parole entitlement, short furloughs, work details, ordinary institutional transfers and the later procedure’s prospective validity are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Morrissey protects freedom already conferred; Greenholtz concerns initial release and Meachum ordinary internal transfer. Sandin preserves direct constitutional liberty alongside its sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement route; no exhaustive program-entitlement finding is required.

##### Which preliminary and final safeguards govern withdrawal of that liberty

**Holding:** Revocation of this community liberty requires Morrissey’s preliminary and final protections, with their distinct qualifications. Initial arrest and detention need not await a completed final hearing, but final revocation must rest on constitutionally adequate process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part III, unanimous.

**Material application:** For arrest and detention pending revocation, a reasonably prompt inquiry at or reasonably near the alleged violation or arrest determines probable cause or reasonable ground to detain for a release-condition violation. The decisionmaker must be uninvolved, but may be a different parole officer rather than a judge. Notice must identify the inquiry, its purpose and alleged violations; the participant may appear, speak, present relevant documents and witnesses and, on request, question adverse informants in person. An officer’s finding that identification would expose an informant to harm permits withholding confrontation of that informant. A summary must state the evidence and reasons without formal judicial findings; supported probable cause permits detention and institutional return pending final decision.

The State must offer a final hearing if desired, within a reasonable time after custody and before effective final revocation, to resolve disputed relevant facts and whether circumstances justify revocation, including denial and mitigation. Minimum safeguards are written notice of violations or grounds, disclosure of adverse evidence, a personal opportunity to present witnesses and documents, confrontation and cross-examination unless specifically withheld for good cause, a neutral and detached decisionmaker, and written evidence and reasons. The decisionmaker need not be a judge or lawyer. Informal letters and affidavits may be considered; matters already decided adversely in another forum, including a relevant conviction, need not be retried.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Preliminary informant-danger and final-hearing good-cause exceptions are separate permissions, not commands to withhold confrontation. No exception, waiver, dispositive admission, prior determination or supported probable-cause decision is established here. Morrissey’s two-month discussion establishes no universal deadline or safe harbor; no new evidentiary burden, automatic-release rule or separate counsel holding is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Morrissey’s flexible administrative safeguards govern in full, without converting revocation into a criminal trial or replacing it with a telephone instruction.

##### What relief follows from the failure to provide revocation process

**Holding:** The Tenth Circuit correctly reversed denial of habeas relief because this termination required Morrissey process. Oklahoma may still establish a legally available revocation ground through adequate procedures or rely on an independent lawful basis for custody.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The procedural violation proves neither sentence expiration, formal-parole entitlement nor compliance with every possible condition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmance returns the case for proceedings under the protected-liberty and process holdings; it orders no unconditional release, release date or new state deadline. Permissible interim detention remains subject to the preliminary and final safeguards.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lynce supplies no unproved exhaustion defect or waiver; Gonzales’s concurrency construction supplies no authority for summary revocation.

For the eight-Justice accepted-rehearing holding in Young v. Harper, No. 95-1598, decided March 18, 1997, see Federal Courts, “Accepted rehearing and certiorari finality.”

#### Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1511.
**Decided:** June 24, 1996.

##### Whether books alone satisfy the right of meaningful access

**Holding:** Bounds requires a meaningful practical opportunity for court access through effective libraries, trained assistance or other effective arrangements considered as a whole; adequate books do not discharge that duty for prisoners unable to use them without needed assistance. It guarantees neither a preferred library program, physical browsing in every case, professional counsel for every prisoner nor the decree's numerical requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** Bartholic’s dismissal and Harris’s inability to file establish actual obstruction at their supported scope. An inventory-based paging system with prompt delivery may work for prisoners capable of independent use; that conditional possibility proves neither Arizona’s actual adequacy nor adequacy for differing literacy or language needs.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No fixed number of all injured prisoners, right to ultimate success, constitutional library specification or automatic appointed counsel is established.

##### What connection to a legal claim is required for access liability

**Holding:** A Bounds access-liability claim requires actual or evidence-supported imminent hindrance of a qualifying, nonfrivolous legal claim, subject to the validity of restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests. The prisoner need not win the obstructed case or complete an impossible filing, but a deficient inventory, preferred staffing arrangement or abstract wish for better research does not alone establish the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices.

**Material application:** Technical dismissal, inability to file a contemplated qualifying claim or sufficiently concrete threatened obstruction can supply the connection. A procedural defect does not itself make the underlying claim substantively frivolous. Evidence and reasonable inferences may prove the real claim and obstacle before avoidable filing failure.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The additional Article III preliminary-merits screen, subgroup-standing requirement and categorical sentence/conditions and initial-filing restrictions have only four votes. Ordinary Lujan injury, causation and stage-appropriate proof remain. Genuine state-created-liberty claims remain possible under Sandin; no categorical exclusion of every other retained right, universal end of assistance upon filing or general private-litigation subsidy is adopted. Independent communications and petition claims retain their own predicates.

##### What proof and governmental role support classwide prison-access relief

**Holding:** Common relief must correspond to a demonstrated common barrier and an identified affected group; individual obstruction or a resource deficiency alone does not establish an unrestricted statewide violation. Relief must respect restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests and give the State a genuine opportunity to devise an effective cure, while permitting measures actually necessary to repair the proved wrong.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** Common evidence may prove a common barrier without proof that every class member has already lost a suit, but geographical and functional reach require support. The actual findings do not establish a denial at every facility or independent broad entitlements for all non-English-speaking or lockdown prisoners. Opportunity to rebut a presumptive Gluth statewide template did not supply Arizona’s genuine initial remedial design role.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Turner requires examination of the actual reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests with appropriate deference; injury alone does not invalidate a valid restriction, and merely asserting security does not establish validity. Jenkins permits necessary repair measures without making every remedial detail a freestanding constitutional right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Unsupported classwide approval set aside and remanded, 8–1; Stevens accepts a narrower remedial remand. Established obstruction, supported findings and common evidence remain. No unrestricted statewide violation, automatic dismissal or fixed library, telephone, staffing or resource minimum.

##### How original PLRA §802 governs this existing prospective decree and any replacement

**Holding:** Section 802 applies amended section 3626 to prospective relief granted before, on or after April 26, 1996; relief here requires findings that it is narrowly drawn, extends no further than necessary to correct the federal-right violation of the particular plaintiffs, and is the least intrusive means necessary, with substantial weight given to adverse effects on public safety or criminal-justice-system operation. Relief exceeding official state-law authority or otherwise violating state or local law additionally requires federal permission, necessity to correct the federal violation, and absence of any other corrective relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part IV, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only with the statutory remand disposition.

**Material application:** Amended §3626 applies to relief granted before, on or after April 26, 1996. Prospective relief means relief other than compensatory monetary damages. This prison-conditions action is not excluded habeas merely because access assistance facilitates habeas. Existing findings may satisfy actual predicates without ritual wording; reasonable cost or absence of hardship does not substitute for need, narrowness and least intrusiveness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3626(a)(1)(B)’s federal-permission, necessity and no-other-relief conditions govern state/local-law conflicts separately from (A). Subsection (C) supplies no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves existing limits. New preliminary relief must be otherwise authorized and satisfy narrowness, need, least intrusiveness, safety and the separate state-law terms; it expires ninety days after entry unless the required prospective findings are made and the order made final before expiration. The former permanent decree is not automatically a new preliminary order. No constitutional challenge to §802 or prisoner-release remedy is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Prospective-decree approval set aside and remanded, 9–0 in disposition, eight on statutory reasoning. Actual findings, alternatives, improvements and security must be considered; the decree is neither automatically retained nor dissolved.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Plyler v. Moore, Fourth Circuit No. 96-6884, 1997-06-23, resolves the reserved Article III challenge to §3626(b)(2) only with the original (b)(3) current-or-ongoing safeguard; see Constitutional Structure — Congressional amendment of law governing prospective relief.

##### When termination and an automatic stay arise under the original §3626

**Holding:** The original section 3626 termination provisions do not dissolve relief merely upon enactment: the immediate-termination entitlement for relief entered without the required findings remains subject to written, record-based findings that relief is necessary to correct a current OR ongoing federal-right violation and remains narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary, and least intrusive. The automatic stay requires an actual pending qualifying motion and runs only for the original statutory period described below.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part IV, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only with the statutory remand disposition.

**Material application:** Under (b)(1), a party or intervener may seek termination after the applicable two years from relief, one year after denial under that paragraph, or two years after enactment for preenactment relief; earlier agreement is permitted. Under (b)(2), a defendant or intervener is entitled to immediate termination absent the original required findings, subject to (b)(3)’s written record findings of necessary, narrowly drawn, no-broader-than-necessary and least-intrusive relief for a current OR ongoing violation. Subsection (b)(4) preserves earlier relief available under other law.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Modification/termination motions must be decided promptly. Original (e) stays the challenged relief from day thirty after a pending (b)(1)/(b)(2) motion, or day 180 for a motion under other law, until the final order resolving it. No such filing is established. Consent decrees remain subject to (a); private agreements enforceable federally only by reinstating the settled proceeding are distinct, with available state breach remedies preserved. Relief entered by state courts solely on state claims is outside the statute.

**Operative remedy or transition:** No termination or automatic stay is entered by enactment or this remand.

##### What conditions govern continued special-master assistance

**Holding:** A special master in this prison-conditions remedy must be disinterested and objective, give due regard to public safety, and be warranted by a finding of sufficient remedial complexity; appointments, compensation, work and continued need must comply with section 3626(f)'s separate conditions below. Those requirements apply to the master's statutory role regardless of title and do not themselves establish misconduct or invalidate every prior act or accrued fee.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part IV, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only with the statutory remand disposition.

**Material application:** A necessary appointment under (f) requires each side to submit up to five names, each may strike up to three opposing names, and the court chooses among those remaining; the prescribed partiality interlocutory appeal remains. Compensation is capped at the §3006A appointed-counsel hourly rate plus reasonable costs, paid from Judiciary appropriations. Continuing need must be reviewed every six months. Hearings and proposed findings are on the record; ex parte findings and communications are forbidden. A master may assist remedial plans and be removed at any time, and service ends upon termination of relief. Title alone cannot evade the statutory role.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The prior opportunity to challenge fees survives. Accrued-fee and temporal questions remain for adjudication; no misconduct, retrospective invalidity of every prior act, repayment or automatic new appointment is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bounds v. Smith, 430 U.S. 817, and Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539:** Preserve meaningful access and effective methods rather than a constitutional library specification. Bounds is not overruled; four proposed categorical limits do not become Court law.
- **Murray v. Giarratano, 492 U.S. 1:** Preserves choice among effective assistance arrangements without a general right to appointed counsel for every prisoner.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, and City of Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95:** Preserve personal injury, causation, appropriate prospective exposure and proof at the litigation stage. The Court does not adopt the four-Justice preliminary merits screen or decide standing in gross from a party concession.
- **Turner v. Safley, 482 U.S. 78:** Applies reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests, with actual examination and proper deference rather than automatic acceptance of a security assertion.
- **Missouri v. Jenkins, June 12, 1995:** Applies violation-linked repair while preserving implementation measures necessary to correct a proved wrong. Each means need not independently violate the Constitution.
- **Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, 502 U.S. 367:** Preserves lawful responsive modification; the new statute's distinct mandatory requirements must also be applied.
- **Sandin v. Conner, June 19, 1995:** Preserves its actual rule for meaningful state-created prison liberty under genuine substantive constraints. No categorical hardship replacement is imported to exclude access to those claims.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc., June 30, 1994:** Preserves connection between remedial terms and their predicate; its speech-specific standard is not a universal least-restrictive-means rule or a substitute for section 3626.

No precedent is overruled. Gluth's earlier remedy supplies background and a proposed template, not a finding of statewide injury in this record.

#### Young v. Harper

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1598.
**Decided:** March 18, 1997.

##### Whether an expressly accepted late rehearing filing makes the subsequent certiorari petition timely

**Holding:** When the appellate court grants leave for a late rehearing filing, treats it as timely and keeps the proceeding open until disposition, denial of that accepted rehearing supplies the relevant finality point for certiorari. The petition filed within ninety days of denial on this sequence is timely.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part I, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees with the timely-review resolution without adopting its reasons.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An unauthorized late paper, an en banc suggestion not treated as rehearing, the mandate date by itself, or an order solely extending the Supreme Court filing period does not establish the same result. No statutory period is waived.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The operative Rule 13.3 and FRAP 40 permit this accepted-rehearing sequence. Bowman distinguishes permitted and entertained rehearing from unauthorized submission; Missouri v. Jenkins, 495 U.S. 33 (1990), respects actual rehearing treatment within the statutory limit.

For the conditional-community-liberty, revocation-process and remedy holdings in Young v. Harper, No. 95-1598, decided March 18, 1997, see Criminal Procedure, “Conditional community release and revocation.”

#### Board of County Commissioners, Wabaunsee County v. Umbehr, 518 U.S. 668 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1654.
**Decided:** June 28, 1996.

##### Whether an existing independent contractor can challenge speech-retaliatory termination

**Holding:** Government may not terminate or refuse to renew an existing independent-contractor relationship because the contractor spoke as a private citizen on a matter of public concern when the speech and public interests outweigh the government's legitimate contracting and operational interests. Courts apply Pickering's balance to the actual contractual relationship, with appropriate regard for genuine service needs; contractor status, retained termination power and absence of a property entitlement do not by themselves defeat the claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The County’s existing renewable exclusive arrangement carried valuable benefits despite no direct purchase of hauling. Loss of exclusivity, coordinated negotiation and notice protection can cause injury notwithstanding later direct city contracts. Content, form and context determine public concern. Actual independence, other clients, contacts and responsibilities may affect the balance either way; private communication is not automatically unprotected and offense at criticism is not a service interest.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No initial-bidder, universal future-award, pure affiliation, unrelated employee-status, comprehensive factual-mistake, later-acquired-evidence or mitigation holding. Summary-judgment assumptions establish no actual speech protection, motive, injury or ultimate balance.

##### What causation and same-decision requirements govern the remand

**Holding:** The contractor must establish that protected speech was a substantial or motivating factor in the challenged action. The government may defeat the retaliation claim by proving by a preponderance of the evidence that it would have taken the same action absent the protected speech; identifying a merely possible lawful reason does not establish that defense. Official-capacity liability and relief also require the ordinary entity-attribution and remedial predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The claimant’s motivating-factor burden and government’s preponderance counterfactual defense remain separate. Genuine performance, efficiency, cost, trust and operational grounds remain available; their possible existence is not proof. Personal immunity is distinct from official-capacity entity liability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Waters/Settle do not supply a universal investigation framework. Ordinary Monell attribution and remedy predicates remain necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reviewed official-capacity judgment affirmed and remanded, 7–2. The individual-immunity judgment and McClure’s separate causation judgment are undisturbed; no automatic renewal, reinstatement, damages or trial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Pickering and Connick:** Extended to existing-contractor retaliation through a balance adjusted to the actual service relationship, preserving private-citizen/public-concern and genuine operational-interest inquiries.
- **Perry v. Sindermann:** Applied to distinguish absence of entitlement to continued benefits from an unconstitutional retaliatory condition.
- **Givhan:** Preserves protection for qualifying citizen expression communicated privately; delivery method alone does not resolve the claim.
- **Mt. Healthy:** Applied without changing the motivating-factor burden or the government's preponderance same-decision defense.
- **Waters and Settle:** Their actual genuine-ground and causation distinctions remain operative; no full investigation framework or unrelated curricular rule is adopted here.
- **National Treasury Employees Union:** Its broad prospective-restraint rule remains distinct from this individual termination; its full justification allocation is not automatically imported.
- **Kentucky v. Graham and Monell:** Preserve the distinction between personal immunity and entity claims and the independent requirements for governmental attribution and relief.

### November 17, 1997 — Jeffries v. Harleston

[Public Projection](../records/jeffries_v_harleston_merits_1997-11-17.md#public-projection). Souter's Parts I and III command five direct votes (Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer); Part II commands those five plus Stone-Zsela. Six Justices support vacatur; the controlling five-vote mandate does not require a new trial.

Part I rejects final defense judgment on the panel's employment balance. Pickering and Connick require attention to the actual speech, service relationship, and institutional needs. Retained tenure and lack of entitlement to a longer chair term do not categorically defeat retaliation; a reasonable forecast matters but does not alone decide protection. The public-concern address was off campus at an unaffiliated event and was not assigned or controlled as official duty; introduction by the chair title did not establish sponsorship. Real administrative duties and recruitment, fundraising, faculty-interaction, and community interests remain relevant, but no special policymaking, confidential, or additional public-contact role was established. Both jury findings—no actual disruption and reasonable expected disruption—stand. Prevention need not await actual harm. Waters protects a genuinely relied-upon reasonable account only if it describes legally disciplinable speech; no complete investigation code is adopted. Perry, Umbehr, and O'Hare preserve the distinction between benefit entitlement and retaliation. No categorical protection of the entire address, actual-loss requirement, quantified institutional-damages requirement, or wholesale NTEU prospective-ban burden follows; Settle remains confined to its student-curriculum setting.

Part II requires each defendant's actual causal responsibility and a real counterfactual under Mt. Healthy, Umbehr, O'Hare, Kimberlin, and Crawford-El. Once protected speech is a substantial or motivating cause, the same-decision defense requires proof by a preponderance, not a conceivable lawful reason. Nine successful one-year votes and differentiated animus or punitive findings do not automatically establish an independent lawful Board decision. A causally effective proposal may matter, but procurement alone proves no liability; nonvoting proposals, defeated votes, and abstention neither establish nor eliminate other supported causal contributions. Competent direct and circumstantial proof applies without a heightened motive burden. A genuine independent lawful decision or supported personal defense may still dispose of a claim; Bernstein's discontinued claims stay discontinued.

Part III leaves actual verdict harmonization and consequences, sustainable individual defenses, capacity attribution, and relief to ordinary adjudication. Harlow and Anderson v. Creighton require personal-damages immunity at the late-October 1991 conduct date and, for any preserved March 1992 claim, its distinct date; later law supplies no retrospective notice. Punitive answers do not replace protection, causation, or immunity. Prospective relief requires a live remedy; elapsed time alone proves neither mootness nor continuing eligibility. Vacatur reinstates neither the two-year chair injunction nor punitive awards and establishes no final liability, immunity, damages, chair entitlement, new March 1992 claim, or universal retrial requirement. Each controlling part has a direct majority; no combined plurality or Marks rule is necessary.

### December 29, 1997 — Clanton v. Cooper

[Public Projection](../records/clanton_v_cooper_merits_1997-12-29.md#public-projection). Ginsburg's Part I and Part II-B are unanimous. Part II-A's complete knowing-or-reckless material-warrant-falsity rule has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela joins its deliberate-falsity application and subsidiary-theory limits. Part III's distinct confession remand has the eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela would affirm denial on that branch. Legal immunity rejection for the assumed deliberate warrant and later detention deception, dismissal of factual-appeal portions, and preservation of rejected theories and disposed claims are unanimous; no liability finding follows.

Johnson and Behrens permit interlocutory immunity review of separable legal defenses on properly assumed plaintiff-favorable historical facts, not participation, credibility, or factual sufficiency. Section 1254 does not enlarge the circuit's §1291 authority. Miller makes ultimate constitutional voluntariness distinct from historical facts; Ornelas supplies task-specific allocation rather than a universal mixed-question rule. ### State-created liberty in prison discipline

**Governing question:** When substantive prison law creates a protected liberty interest.

**Current rule:** A State creates protected prison liberty when substantive law meaningfully restricts officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation. Mandatory words alone are insufficient: courts examine the genuine constraint in the whole disciplinary scheme and whether the restraint is meaningful rather than routine. This is a sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule; state codification is unnecessary for directly protected constitutional liberty.

### Revocation of existing parole-like community liberty

**Governing question:** Termination of a prisoner's existing release to ordinary community life under parole-like conditions.

**Current rule:** When a State releases a prisoner to live at home, work and conduct ordinary community life under parole-like supervision, ending that existing conditional liberty requires Morrissey's preliminary and final protections. Retained legal custody, an overcrowding purpose, the preparole label or a separate formal-parole decision does not eliminate the interest. A constitutionally adequate determination must precede final revocation; a completed final hearing need not invariably precede initial arrest or detention.

**Authority by component:**

- Young v. Harper — Part II — Existing parole-like community liberty requires protection, 1997-03-18; Part II joined by all nine supplies the actual-liberty trigger
- Young v. Harper — Part III — Morrissey’s ordinary stages and qualifications govern, 1997-03-18; Part III joined by all nine supplies the process and its distinct exceptions

**Present operation effective:** Young v. Harper, 1997-03-18.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** When the participant is arrested and detained pending revocation, a reasonably prompt inquiry at or reasonably near the place of the alleged violation or arrest determines probable cause or reasonable ground for detention for a release-condition violation. A person not directly involved in the case decides; that person need not be a judge and may be a different parole officer. The participant receives notice of the inquiry, its purpose and the alleged violations, may appear and speak, and may present relevant documents and witnesses. On request he may question an adverse informant in his presence. If the officer determines that identifying the informant would expose that person to harm, confrontation and cross-examination of that informant may be withheld. The officer summarizes the proceedings and states the reasons and evidence supporting the determination; formal judicial findings are unnecessary. Supported probable cause permits continued detention and institutional return pending final decision.

The State must offer a final revocation hearing, if desired, within a reasonable time after custody and before effective final revocation. It must resolve contested relevant facts and whether the circumstances warrant revocation, allowing denial and mitigation. Minimum protections are written notice of the claimed violations or asserted grounds; disclosure of adverse evidence; an opportunity to be heard personally and present witnesses and documents; confrontation and cross-examination unless the officer specifically finds good cause to withhold them; a neutral and detached decisionmaker, whose members need not be judges or lawyers; and a written statement of the evidence relied on and reasons.

Informal evidence, including letters and affidavits, may be considered. Issues already determined adversely in another forum, including a relevant criminal conviction, need not be relitigated. Morrissey’s discussion of two months establishes no universal deadline or safe harbor. Preliminary informant risk and final-stage specific good cause are distinct exceptions, each permitting rather than requiring withholding upon the necessary finding. The separate counsel question remains undecided.

**Exceptions and limits:** Initial parole eligibility, complete state-program entitlement and independently lawful custody grounds remain distinct. The decision supplies no new evidentiary burden or automatic-release consequence.

### Political retaliation in existing government service relationships

**Governing question:** Termination, nonrenewal or withdrawal of an existing independent-contractor or recurring service-provider relationship for citizen speech or partisan allegiance.

**Current rule:** Government may not terminate or refuse to renew an existing contractor relationship because of private-citizen speech on a matter of public concern when the speaker's and public's interests outweigh legitimate contracting and operational interests. Apply Connick's content, form and context inquiry and Pickering's balance adjusted to the actual service relationship, including genuine performance, efficiency, cost, trust and disruption concerns; independence, other clients, contacts and responsibilities may matter in either direction. Neither contractor status, termination authority, lack of property entitlement nor absence of direct governmental purchase categorically defeats protection. Private delivery alone does not remove qualifying expression.

For withdrawal based on refusing partisan support or supporting an opponent, apply the distinct Elrod–Branti rule: government must demonstrate that political affiliation is appropriate for effective performance of the actual work. Labels, general patronage advantages and dislike of opposition do not suffice; genuinely policymaking or confidential functions may justify the qualification. Public-concern speech and materially mixed speech/affiliation claims require their appropriate service-adjusted inquiry rather than an undifferentiated affiliation or management rule.

The claimant must show protected conduct was a substantial or motivating factor. Government may defeat the claim by proving by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred absent that conduct; a merely possible lawful reason does not establish the defense. Entity attribution, personal defenses, injury and relief retain their separate requirements.

- Existing-contractor expression and the causation sequence: Protected expression in an existing contractual relationship and Causation, the same-decision defense and the limited remand, 1996-06-28; Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).
