#### Heck v. Humphrey, 512 U.S. 477

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-6188.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### Necessary invalidity on the present in-custody record

**Holding:** A state prisoner in Heck's position, in custody with a lawful opportunity to challenge the conviction, has no presently cognizable §1983 damages claim when success necessarily establishes the invalidity of that outstanding conviction or sentence, unless the judgment has undergone qualifying invalidation. The rule decides that setting; it does not make present habeas availability a universal element or decide plaintiffs who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity or whose custody has ended.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt this bounded rule; the distinct sufficient rationales are not added together to obtain its support.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez, 411 U.S. 475 (1973): its release-channel distinction informs the new, bounded damages prerequisite; its holding is not misdescribed as already deciding damages-only claims.
- Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539 (1974): preserves civil relief compatible with the operative conviction and custody judgment.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents, 457 U.S. 496 (1982): remains the rule against general §1983 exhaustion; this decision addresses present cognizability instead.

##### Statutory coordination supports this limited prerequisite

**Holding:** For the in-custody, conviction-negating damages claim described in Part I, coordination of §1983 with the available habeas channel supports favorable invalidation as a prerequisite, with the common-law malicious-prosecution analogy implementing that coordination. This is a new, limited restriction on §1983, not conversion of the damages complaint into habeas, a general exhaustion rule, or adoption of every malicious-prosecution element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices adopt this sufficient statutory explanation at this limited scope. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg do not join Part II.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez: extends statutory coordination to the particular damages claim while preserving the distinct relief and conditions of habeas.
- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves its permissible civil-remedy category; factual overlap with prosecution does not remove a claim from that category.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents: distinguishes general exhaustion from the newly adopted prerequisite; exhaustion alone cannot satisfy it.
- Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247 (1978): applies common-law remedial guidance as an aid to construing the federal remedy, not a wholesale importation of a state tort.

##### Qualifying invalidation and deferred accrual

**Holding:** The claim barred under Part I becomes cognizable only when the conviction or sentence has been reversed on direct review, expunged by executive order, declared invalid by a state tribunal authorized to do so, or called into question by a federal court's issuance of a writ of habeas corpus. That claim accrues only upon such invalidation; exhaustion, unsuccessful review, or loss of a review opportunity is not invalidation, and neither immediate accrual with equitable tolling nor a preservation stay is required for this unaccrued claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine directly adopt the four invalidation routes and the accrual consequence for the barred claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carey v. Piphus: applies its common-law-informed approach to the federal remedy without imposing additional constitutional-liability elements.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents: preserves the distinction between exhaustion and existence of a cognizable damages claim.

##### Independent injuries, claim-specific analysis and this dismissal

**Holding:** A claim whose success does not necessarily establish invalidity falls outside this prerequisite even if it concerns the same investigation or trial; courts must compare the civil claim's elements with the basis of the conviction, and compensatory recovery still requires an actual compensable injury. On the accepted characterization of Heck's presented action, dismissal without prejudice stands on lack of present cognizability, without deciding misconduct, immunity, personal responsibility, preclusion, damages, or entitlement to release.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine directly adopt this boundary, application and remedy.

**Material application:** The lower courts' characterization that Heck's action necessarily attacked the conviction was not disputed in the petition or principal merits brief; the late reply established no separately preserved independent-injury count requiring remand. An unlawful-search claim can remain compatible with a conviction through independent source, inevitable discovery or harmless error, but those possibilities establish neither automatic admissibility of every search claim nor compensation for conviction injury without invalidation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves the distinction between compatible civil relief and relief dependent on invalidating custody.
- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: distinguishes its opportunity-only claim; the existing §1983 holding remains intact.
- Carey v. Piphus: applies the requirement of actual compensable injury without adjudicating the amount or merits here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Former prisoners and persons who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity remain outside the question resolved. No universal constitutional malicious-prosecution action, automatic state-law preclusion, automatic habeas conversion or pending-prosecution abstention rule is adopted. Custis's distinct no-meaningful-review reservation in the ACCA channel remains intact. Recognition of a conviction's invalidation by an authorized state tribunal identifies one circumstance in which the present §1983 prerequisite is satisfied. It does not itself create jurisdiction, confer authority to reopen the underlying conviction, or prescribe grounds upon which a state tribunal must grant collateral relief. Those questions remain governed by the independently applicable law authorizing that tribunal's action.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Dismissal without prejudice is affirmed unanimously for lack of present cognizability. The barred claim has not accrued, so its limitations period is not consumed while the prerequisite remains unmet. A later otherwise proper action may follow qualifying invalidation, subject to remaining requirements; no stay, release, automatic conversion to habeas, liability, immunity or damages determination is ordered.

#### Edwards v. Balisok

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1352.
**Decided:** 1997-05-19.

##### Procedural labels do not avoid necessary invalidity

**Holding:** In the in-custody review setting presented, damages and retrospective declaratory claims that a disciplinary adjudicator deliberately concealed exculpatory evidence and acted dishonestly or with disabling bias are not presently cognizable under §1983 when success necessarily establishes the invalidity of the outstanding deprivation of earned good-time credits, absent qualifying invalidation. The inquiry concerns the legal consequence of the actual claim, not its procedural label or omission of a request to restore credits; the reserved position of former prisoners and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity is not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous. This direct agreement does not change Heck's earlier rationale alignments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A later lawful hearing's possibility of the same result does not validate the existing adjudication. Present habeas availability is not made a universal element; former prisoners and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity remain reserved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heck's necessary-implication rule applies at its bounded in-custody scope; Preiser reserves credit restoration to habeas. Wolff and Carey preserve compatible process injuries, not categorical permission for every procedural challenge. Sandin and Young v. Harper preserve meaningful liberty and process without removing remedy-channel limits.

##### Genuinely independent procedural injuries require their own analysis

**Holding:** A claim falls outside Heck's invalidation prerequisite when its success does not necessarily establish that the credit adjudication was invalid, even if it arises from the same disciplinary hearing. A genuinely separable failure to state supporting facts can meet that boundary, and questioning or other procedural allegations must be classified individually; separability establishes neither a due-process violation nor entitlement to damages.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous. This direct agreement does not change Heck's earlier rationale alignments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A nominal-damages label does not save an invalidity-dependent claim. Refusal of questions is neither invariably unconstitutional nor necessarily invalidating; Wolff permits refusal for irrelevance, lack of necessity, safety or correctional hazards. Ordinary pleading, personal responsibility, immunity, liability and damages requirements remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heck and Wolff preserve genuinely independent injuries. Carey allows appropriate nominal relief for an established independent process denial without presuming compensable loss.

##### Qualifying invalidation precedes accrual; an exhaustion stay is not the remedy

**Holding:** For an invalidity-dependent claim within Heck's decided category, qualifying invalidation means reversal on direct review, executive expungement, invalidation by an authorized state tribunal, or a federal writ of habeas corpus calling the custody judgment into question, and the claim accrues only then. Without that event the claim is dismissed without prejudice rather than stayed to impose state-remedy exhaustion; failed review, exhaustion or expiration or loss of a review opportunity is not invalidation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous. This direct agreement does not change Heck's earlier rationale alignments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The barred unaccrued claim consumes no limitations period while the prerequisite is unmet. No conversion to habeas or release follows. Independently required statutory administrative exhaustion and an independently authorized stay remain separate questions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heck supplies all four invalidation routes, accrual and dismissal; Patsy bars general judicial state-remedy exhaustion, which differs from cognizability.

##### Prospective date stamping remains for ordinary equitable adjudication

**Holding:** A genuinely prospective request governing future date stamping of received witness statements ordinarily need not invalidate a completed credit adjudication and is not automatically barred by Heck. Because this request was not adjudicated below, remand requires examination of live standing, ordinary equitable requirements and the actual claim; no prospective label saves relief whose substance necessarily invalidates the past judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous. This direct agreement does not change Heck's earlier rationale alignments.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical procedural exemption and remand unanimously. Dismiss invalidity-dependent damages and retrospective declarations without prejudice absent qualifying invalidation; separately classify genuinely independent reasons, questioning or other theories and adjudicate the unaddressed prospective request. No credit, damages, injunction or misconduct finding follows.

#### Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1424.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### What evidence of Article III injury, causation and redressability is required at summary judgment

**Holding:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury, a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, and a likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. At summary judgment, the plaintiff must supply specific facts by affidavit or other evidence on the challenged elements; favorable reasonable inferences remain available, but allegations cannot substitute for the missing evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the elements and litigation-stage evidentiary requirements. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining this section.

**Material application:** Earlier survival of the pleading stage does not establish the specific facts needed at summary judgment. The Court resolves this action on insufficient threatened personal injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general redressability element controls. Its application to absent funding agencies and uncertain foreign-project responses appears only in the three-Justice Part III-B and is not controlling. Favorable reasonable inferences remain available; the decision does not replace Rule 56 or the separate APA programmatic-review holding of National Wildlife Federation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The merits declaration, mandatory rulemaking relief and dependent §1540(g)(4) fee award are vacated, and the action is remanded for jurisdictional dismissal. Six Justices support dismissal for lack of standing; seven support reversal, with Stevens relying on a noncontrolling statutory ground. No independent fee-entitlement rule or substitute award is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975): applied for the plaintiff's personal stake and burden to establish standing; its statutory-rights discussion remains subject to concrete injury.
- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied for the constitutional elements and the limits on generalized judicial supervision.
- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976): applied for fair traceability and likely redress; no new majority application concerning these absent agencies follows.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied for the evidentiary distinction between pleading and summary judgment; its separate APA programmatic-review holding is not enlarged.
- Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986): applied to require evidence of challenged essential elements while preserving genuine material disputes and reasonable inferences.

##### Whether past visits, indefinite return intentions and general wildlife interests establish imminent personal injury

**Holding:** Aesthetic, recreational and professional interests in observing wildlife can support standing, but these plaintiffs must show threatened interference with their own concrete use. Prior visits and an indefinite intention to return, without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure, do not establish standing for these prospective claims; an ecosystem, animal or professional connection that supplies only general interest in distant species does not cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone's separate concurrence supplies a sixth vote for the record-specific personal-injury conclusion without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** The affidavits describe visits to Egyptian and Sri Lankan project areas but no sufficiently definite return undertaking or continuing activity showing imminent exposure. General ecosystem, animal and professional connections supply no missing evidence of these members' own threatened use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document prerequisite is imposed. Actual use of an affected ecosystem and demonstrated injury transmitted across geographic distance remain undecided. Aesthetic, recreational and professional harm remains cognizable when it affects the claimant's own concrete activity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jurisdictional dismissal leaves the statute's geographic reach unresolved and does not preclude a later action supported by sufficient standing facts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): applied; aesthetic injury is cognizable, but an organization's special interest cannot replace injury to a member.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977): applied to require an injured member for this associational claim; no other associational requirement is changed.
- Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95 (1983): applied for the distinction between past exposure and a present or imminent injury supporting prospective relief.
- Whitmore v. Arkansas, 495 U.S. 149 (1990): applied for actual or imminent rather than conjectural injury.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied to specific affected use; no categorical bar to geographically transmitted injury is established.
- Japan Whaling Association v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): distinguished because its whale-watching and studying members alleged an adverse effect on their own activities; concrete professional or ecological injury remains possible.

##### Whether ESA citizen-suit permission and an asserted procedural right replace threatened concrete injury

**Holding:** ESA §11(g)'s authorization for any person to sue does not give Article III standing to a plaintiff asserting only the public's common interest in lawful administration. A procedural right may protect a plaintiff's threatened concrete interest without requiring proof that the procedure will certainly change the ultimate agency decision, but the procedural label cannot replace that personal interest; these respondents have not shown the required connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the citizen-suit and concrete-interest rule. Stone does not join this section.

**Material application:** Section 11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), permits enforcement but identifies no concrete injury to these members independent of the unsupported future use. The public's common interest in lawful administration cannot supply the missing personal connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may recognize concrete factual injuries previously inadequate in law; the outer reach of that power is not settled. Procedural protections tied to a threatened concrete interest remain cognizable without certainty of a changed ultimate decision. Neither §7(a)(2)'s geographic reach nor the binding effect of a revised rule on absent agencies is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The action is dismissed for want of Article III standing; no new consultation rule or cancellation of any foreign project is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126 (1922): applied to reject a general right to compel lawful administration as a sufficient personal injury.
- Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447 (1923), and Ex parte Levitt, 302 U.S. 633 (1937): applied for the distinction between direct injury and an undifferentiated public grievance.
- United States v. Richardson, 418 U.S. 166 (1974), and Schlesinger v. Reservists Committee to Stop the War, 418 U.S. 208 (1974): applied to generally shared governmental grievances without distinctive concrete harm.
- Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United for Separation of Church and State, Inc., 454 U.S. 464 (1982), and Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied; a claimed right to lawful government conduct alone does not satisfy Article III.
- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), and Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): preserved for Congress's power to recognize injuries and broaden protected interests while retaining the plaintiff's own concrete injury.
- Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Insurance Co., 409 U.S. 205 (1972), and Hardin v. Kentucky Utilities Co., 390 U.S. 1 (1968): distinguished as statutory recognition of concrete personal interests, not authorization of a wholly abstract grievance.

#### Garlotte v. Fordice

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-6790.
**Decided:** May 30, 1995.

##### Custody under a continuous consecutive series

**Holding:** A prisoner serving a continuous unexpired series of consecutive sentences remains in custody under the earlier conviction when invalidating it would advance release from the aggregate term, although its first segment has been served. This establishes the custody threshold, leaving the conviction’s validity and remaining procedural and merits conditions for review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; eight Justices directly support this rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An independently expired sentence's collateral consequences alone remain insufficient under Maleng. The rule concerns a continuous unexpired aggregate whose release date would advance; ordinary habeas procedural and merits requirements remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Peyton v. Rowe, 391 U.S. 54: extends its aggregate treatment from a future segment to a completed first segment of a still-unexpired consecutive series when invalidation advances release.
- Maleng v. Cook, 490 U.S. 488: distinguishes a wholly expired independent sentence and preserves the rule that collateral consequences alone do not supply custody under that conviction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit's jurisdictional dismissal and remand for remaining procedural and merits review, including review of the district court's merits denial. No conviction is invalidated, writ awarded or immediate release ordered.

#### Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-974.
**Decided:** March 3, 1997.

##### Whether this former employee retains a live challenge to the employment rule

**Holding:** An individual prospective employment-rule challenge becomes moot when the employee voluntarily leaves covered employment without an intention to return, realistic recurrence or a surviving claim. A general relief request cannot manufacture nominal damages against a State or official-capacity defendant that is not a damages person under §1983.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous; all nine Justices join every holding.

**Material application:** No class or replacement claimant remained, and the dismissed individual-capacity claims were not preserved on appeal. State nonpersonhood under §1983 is a statutory bar distinct from waivable Eleventh Amendment immunity; a proposed consensual payment cannot supply the missing cause of action. The Attorney General’s constitutional participation under §2403(b) did not overturn the denial of State party status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general requirement to plead nominal damages by name is established. A legally available individual-capacity claim presents a different question. The Court does not fix the precise April 1990 date when mootness attached.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Preiser requires a controversy throughout review. Will and Hafer distinguish the unavailable State or official-capacity damages claim from genuine individual-capacity liability.

##### Whether limited corrective jurisdiction permits vacatur without deciding sponsor standing or constitutional merits

**Holding:** The Court may correct a lower federal court’s jurisdictional error without exercising constitutional merits jurisdiction. On the equities here, that authority requires vacating the appellate judgment and directing vacatur of the District Court declaration and dismissal for mootness, without resolving ultimate initiative-sponsor standing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The prevailing plaintiff ended the covered employment during review efforts; this was not settlement or abandonment by a reviewing loser. The later appellate merits adjudication and absence of a lawful damages residue justify clearing both unreviewable constitutional judgments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2106 does not independently create merits jurisdiction. Intervention, an appeal notice or political commitment does not confer automatic sponsor standing. The original declaration is not declared void when entered, and the Court resolves neither every intervention ruling nor Article XXVIII’s validity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate both merits judgments and dismiss the action as moot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bender and Corrick supply limited correction of jurisdictional error. Lujan and Brown Group retain constitutional injury and lawful representation requirements. Bancorp requires equitable rather than automatic vacatur; Munsingwear and Anderson support judgment clearing within that inquiry.

##### Whether state-law certification can revive a moot federal action

**Holding:** Certification of unsettled state law can assist a live federal controversy but cannot cure the absence of Article III jurisdiction. No certification is ordered in this moot case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The meaning of Article XXVIII and any constitutional challenge in future live litigation remain open. No mandatory certification requirement is adopted.

### February 13, 1998 — J. Roderick MacArthur Foundation and Lance E. Lindblom v. Federal Bureau of Investigation

#### Holdings

##### I. A live personal statutory controversy and an express statutory action permit merits review

**Holding and operative rule:** Actual continued custody of identified records describing this claimant's protected associations supplies a concrete personal information interest directly traceable to the retaining agency; an actually requested award within §552a(g)(1)(D)/(g)(4)'s legally available monetary category can meaningfully redress the claimed invasion if its requirements are established. Jurisdiction does not depend on first proving the disputed retention violation or final damages entitlement, and the substantial statutory claim falls within the Act's express agency action and conditional sovereign consent without creating a new constitutional damages cause.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in reaching the merits without joining this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. requires Article III power before these disputed merits and, with Bell v. Hood, separates a substantial federal claim from ultimate success. The acknowledged personal records establish the occurrence of the claimed intrusion. Warth and Havens recognize the difference between invasion of one's own protected interest and a demand for lawful government; neither supplies a Privacy Act remedy or excuses summary-judgment proof under Lujan and Lee. Subjective chill, presumed emotional loss and speculation about employers do not carry this statutory conclusion.

The FBI itself retains the information. Lindblom actually sought damages, the statutory claim continued on appeal, and no abandonment or legal bar to every monetary remedy is established. Arizonans rejects legally unavailable relief; it does not require a renewed formulaic prayer. Alexander supports meaningful partial redress despite uncertainty about recovery, without transferring its pleading-stage standard here. Culpability, adverse effect and monetary entitlement still require their own legal treatment.

Section 1331 and the review statute provide jurisdictional channels, not consent. Section 552a(g)(1)(D) authorizes an individual action against the agency and jurisdiction over it; (g)(4) supplies conditional United States monetary liability. Meyer requires that separate source of cause and obligation; the text supplies it here. Bell is not a waiver, §702 supplies no money, and no agency Bivens action is created. The Court neither classifies every federal waiver condition as nonjurisdictional nor presumes every remaining statutory predicate satisfied.

##### II. The protected personal retention invasion can itself be an adverse effect

**Holding and operative rule:** Actual unwanted maintenance of identified records describing this individual's protected associations, if outside §552a(e)(7)'s lawful alternatives, can itself supply the adverse personal effect required by §552a(g)(1)(D). A merely abstract or technical violation with no adverse personal consequence is insufficient; an adverse agency determination, public disclosure, lost employment or pecuniary loss is not independently required as such.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Breyer, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Scalia and Thomas reserve this construction; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Section (D) requires personal adverse effect, not the adverse determination separately required by (C). Its additional words exclude abstract procedural noncompliance without personal impact. Where the substantive protection concerns keeping the claimant's own associational information, the actual invasion and its adverse personal consequence can coincide. Requiring a different kind of downstream injury would leave that particular interest unprotected merely because the information remained undisclosed.

Havens illustrates a direct personal statutory invasion but does not decide this additional Privacy Act language. Albright distinguishes adverse effect, causation, intentional or willful conduct and monetary recovery; its recognition of emotional trauma does not make such trauma exclusive or settle the damages minimum. Doe v. FBI enforced a no-adverse-effect finding unchallenged in that case, where money was not pursued on appeal. Neither finding nor abandonment is established here. The Court does not find distress, declare these records unlawful, equate every violation with adverse effect or treat adverse effect as actual damages under (g)(4).

##### III. Continued retention must satisfy the complete Continuing Pertinence Test

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 552a(e)(7) regulates continued maintenance as well as original collection. Unless retention is expressly authorized by statute or by the individual, the information must remain pertinent to and within the scope of an authorized law-enforcement activity; lawful original collection is not conclusive permission to retain it after every qualifying connection has ceased. The Court adopts the following complete rule:

1. **Does the challenged record fall within the statutory protection?**

   The plaintiff identifies records describing their First Amendment activities and establishes that the government continues to maintain them. The court first checks whether retention is independently authorized by statute or by the individual.

2. **Can the government identify an authorized law-enforcement activity?**

   The agency must establish the legal authority for that activity. It need not prove the plaintiff committed a crime. Intelligence investigations, criminal investigations, and authorized civil enforcement may qualify.

3. **Does the information remain pertinent to that activity?**

   The court examines the actual relationship, not a hypothetical future possibility. Relevant considerations include:

   - Why the information was collected.
   - What investigative purpose it still serves.
   - Whether it preserves necessary context or evidence.
   - Whether subsequent events have changed its relevance.
   - Whether continued use or indexing exceeds the justified investigative scope.

   No single consideration automatically decides the case.

4. **What disposition and remedy does the law authorize?**

   If a qualifying connection remains, the agency may retain the information under the statutory exception. If no qualifying connection remains, the court determines the violation and available relief under the Privacy Act.

   The court separately accounts for lawful evidence-preservation and federal records-management duties.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Each joins the entire four-step rule, including every listed consideration and the separate preservation/relief requirements. Scalia and Thomas reject the complete rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Act defines maintenance to include keeping, collection, use and dissemination, while (e)(7) attaches its prohibition and alternatives to that continuing conduct. Congress's different acquisition-based terms elsewhere do not make initial lawfulness permanently dispositive. The three alternatives remain distinct: express statutory authorization, individual authorization and authorized-law-enforcement pertinence.

Authorized activity is broader than an active investigation or a current investigation of this plaintiff. Genuine historical intelligence value, connected or later inquiries, necessary evidentiary context and legitimate preservation of a law-enforcement record may supply an actual continuing relationship. Age, closure, innocence and name retrieval alone decide nothing. Conversely, the agency's general mission or the possibility that any datum might someday prove useful cannot conclusively establish every record's pertinence indefinitely. The Government's identified continuing-interest contention remains available for record-specific assessment.

Step 2 requires the agency to establish the activity's legal authority, without proof that the plaintiff committed a crime. Step 3's considerations illuminate actual pertinence and scope; they are nonexclusive, none is automatically decisive, and they create no current-necessity or least-restrictive-recordkeeping standard. The rule imposes no periodic audit, automatic request-response duty, deadline, universal consultation, prescribed explanation format or public disclosure of classified reasons, and no general shift of the independent burdens concerning injury, fault or damages. Ordinary judicial procedure and appropriate confidential review remain available.

The contrary historical-activity construction properly recognizes preservation value but improperly makes that value conclusive solely from acquisition. Department of Defense v. FLRA keeps disclosure protections and their exceptions distinct; confidentiality does not replace Congress's separate retention restriction. No dissemination claim, automatic purge on closure or present finding of nonpertinence is decided.

##### IV-A. The separate constitutional claims lack the required supported connection

**Holding and operative rule:** The Foundation's affidavits do not establish the causal mechanism connecting FBI retention to prospective grantees' or employers' knowledge and reactions; Lindblom's separately presented constitutional claim supplies no independent supported basis beyond that failed derivative theory. Both constitutional-standing rejections therefore stand, without a ruling on the First Amendment merits or a categorical exclusion of reputational or associational injury.

**Authority:** Part IV-A has all nine Justices. Scalia and Thomas's additional injury/imminence ground is separate and noncontrolling.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan and Lee require specific support at summary judgment, with favorable reasonable inferences. Predictions of stigma or employment effects do not themselves establish how these audiences learn of the retained references. Meese involved an actual public governmental label and detailed evidence connecting it to threatened harm. It shows why completed loss or identification of every deterred person is unnecessary; it does not supply the missing exposure link here. Nor does a claimant's own publicity categorically make all resulting injury self-inflicted.

Lindblom supplied no separate constitutional-standing argument or evidentiary showing. His pleaded subjective chill is not summary-judgment proof under Laird, and his employment elsewhere supplies no independent demonstrated constitutional injury. The actual personal statutory information interest has a different direct connection to the FBI's custody; its recognition does not manufacture the missing presentation of the distinct constitutional claim. The Court decides neither that FBI recordkeeping can never violate the First Amendment nor that these statutory records have no adverse personal effect.

##### IV-B. The constitutional dismissals must be without prejudice

**Holding and operative rule:** A dismissal of these constitutional claims for want of Article III standing cannot carry a merits-preclusive effect. The standing rejections are sustained, but the district judgment's with-prejudice effect is vacated and the case is remanded to conform those dismissals to their jurisdictional ground.

**Authority:** Part IV-B has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only. The correction is unanimous.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans and Lee distinguish limited authority to correct a lower court's jurisdictional disposition from authority to decide constitutional merits. The September 6 order dismissed the entire complaint with prejudice. Leaving that effect on claims dismissed for lack of standing would imply a merits adjudication the courts lack power to enter. Section 2106 permits the bounded correction but creates no substantive jurisdiction. No declaration or injunction is restored, and no evidentiary proceeding on the constitutional merits is directed.

##### V-A. The statutory judgment requires record-specific further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** The conclusive original-collection rule cannot sustain summary judgment on Lindblom's retention claim. That component is vacated and remanded for the complete test's application to the actual records, with any independent statutory or individual authorization and lawful preservation obligation considered separately; the Court awards neither expungement nor damages.

**Authority:** Part V-A has Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Scalia and Thomas oppose statutory remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The finding of legitimate original compilation does not answer whether each record retains the relationship now required. The Government contends that some other persons and organizations remain of investigative interest; that contention may matter, but the Court does not make the missing record-specific pertinence determination in either party's favor. Confidential and in camera assessment remains available where appropriate. Any concrete evidence-preservation or federal records-management duty must be accounted for without treating a general reference to preservation as conclusive permission or as an automatic destruction command. The remand corrects the governing legal rule; it does not itself reverse the discovery refusal, require additional evidence or pleadings, order an agency review program, or find intentional wrongdoing. Unreviewed claims remain undisturbed.

##### V-B. Each remedial route retains its independent requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** The retention restriction does not create unlimited relief: monetary liability under §552a(g)(1)(D)/(g)(4), amendment relief under (d)/(g)(1)(A)/(g)(2), access relief, and any independently permissible prospective equity retain their distinct conditions. Neither an automatic monetary award nor a general expungement entitlement follows from the Court's construction.

**Authority:** Part V-B has all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees that no final award issues and joins Part V-A's separate lawful-relief and preservation directions, without adopting V-B's additional grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** Intentional or willful noncompliance, causally resulting compensable actual damages and the statutory minimum remain separate questions; the Court decides no amount or nonpecuniary-damages issue. Costs and fees do not independently supply redress for an otherwise absent controversy. No constitutional damages cause against the FBI is created.

An amendment route must satisfy its applicable request, refusal, review and exemption predicates. Exhausted access requests do not alone establish amendment exhaustion. Nagel recognizes the distinct (e)(7) claim and monetary route; Clarkson links possible expungement to the amendment provisions and their limits; Doe rejects bypassing the Act's remedies. Their qualified accounts supply no unconditional purge warrant. Sections (j) and (k) do not permit exemption from (e)(7), and a regulation cannot defeat that nonexemptible duty merely by exempting civil liability. A valid (d) exemption may nonetheless defeat a derivative amendment claim. A FOIA exemption or general records-system designation does not alone settle those distinct predicates; no waiver is presumed.

Reich and §702 preserve possible nonmonetary restraint subject to an existing lawful action, statutory preclusion and ordinary equitable limits. The Court need not decide that entire route because the monetary action permits this statutory review. No separate APA claim is created. If a mandatory decree is authorized, §702 requires identification of the responsible officer or successor by name or title; absence of a personal name from the caption alone does not answer every equitable question. These reservations govern any further relief, not the independently resolved Article III controversy.

#### Law After Decision

Courts reviewing maintained First Amendment records must apply the complete Continuing Pertinence Test. Original lawful collection remains relevant but cannot conclusively excuse all later retention. Genuine historical law-enforcement value remains permissible; closure, age, innocence and name indexing do not automatically require deletion. The distinct statutory, individual-authorization, remedy and preservation requirements remain operative.

The six-Justice adverse-effect holding recognizes the actual unlawful personal information invasion as potentially sufficient under (g)(1)(D), without establishing actual damages. The eight-Associate threshold/action rulings permit merits review of this existing personal monetary controversy without assuming ultimate recovery. The constitutional dismissals rest on the actual causal and claim-specific defects, with no new categorical restriction on reputational injury. No periodic review program, unrestricted disclosure, automatic damages or unconditional expungement rule is created.

#### Separate Writings

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree that the actual personal statutory controversy permits review, that the constitutional claims fail on the Court's stated grounds, and that their dismissals must be without prejudice. They reserve whether the direct retention invasion alone satisfies statutory adverse effect because statutory nonviolation suffices on their view.

For them, a record pertinent to a lawful investigation remains pertinent to that authorized activity even after it is completed. The continuing maintenance prohibition still forbids keeping records never within a lawful statutory category; it need not require a renewed present-use justification for the conceded lawful historical record. Legitimate intelligence preservation reinforces that reading. They would affirm statutory summary judgment and reject the complete test's continuing-connection requirement, while accepting its severable protected-record, legal-authority and independent-remedy principles. They do not find the information unlawfully collected, endorse unrestricted disclosure or regard closure and innocent contact as automatic violations.

They also conclude that the reported constitutional predictions lack sufficiently concrete or imminent personal consequences on this record, independently of traceability. They impose no named-lost-grantee rule, particular affidavit form or numerical probability requirement. That additional ground has two votes and is not the Court's holding. No final monetary or expungement entitlement follows under their position.

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, for the identified citizen-suit and procedural-right clarification; ordinary Article III elements and evidentiary burdens are retained.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

**Holdings navigation:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992) — Standing and personal injury.

### Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

**Governing question:** Whether an individual former employee's challenge to an employment rule remains live after departure.

**Current rule:** A prospective individual challenge becomes moot after voluntary departure from covered employment when no intent to return, realistic recurrence or surviving claim is established. A general prayer cannot preserve the controversy by producing nominal damages against a State or an official-capacity defendant whom §1983 does not make a damages person. Consent to payment cannot create the missing cause of action, and certification of an unsettled state-law question cannot supply missing Article III jurisdiction.

**Authority by component:**

### Conviction-negating damages claims and favorable invalidation

**Governing question:** When a prisoner’s §1983 damages claim must await invalidation of the conviction or sentence.

**Current rule:** For an in-custody claimant with a real lawful opportunity to challenge the conviction, a §1983 damages claim whose success necessarily establishes the invalidity of that conviction or sentence cannot proceed before favorable invalidation. Qualifying invalidation is reversal on direct appeal, executive expungement, a declaration of invalidity by an authorized state tribunal, or a federal writ of habeas corpus calling it into question. The court compares the actual civil theory and criminal basis, not labels. Independent injuries whose success is compatible with the criminal judgment remain separately cognizable. The barred claim accrues upon qualifying invalidation; the rule is not a direction to file now and seek a stay or an invented tolling regime. Within the in-custody category, damages or retrospective declarations alleging dishonest concealment of exculpatory evidence or disabling adjudicator bias in an outstanding earned-credit deprivation likewise require invalidation when success necessarily defeats that adjudication. Classify each alleged procedural injury by its legal consequence: a genuinely independent failure to state supporting facts and compatible prospective relief may proceed under ordinary requirements. An invalidity-dependent claim is dismissed without prejudice, not stayed for exhaustion; failed review or loss of a review opportunity is not qualifying invalidation.
