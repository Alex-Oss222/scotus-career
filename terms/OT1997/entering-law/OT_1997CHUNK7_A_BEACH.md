#### Reich v. Collins

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-908.
**Decided:** December 6, 1994.

##### A State may not withdraw its apparent postpayment remedy after payment

**Holding:** When a State holds out a clear postpayment remedy for illegally assessed and collected taxes, including voluntary payments, due process forbids withdrawing that remedy after payment for the constitutional invalidity asserted and then relying on the taxpayer's failure to pursue a different prepayment route. Georgia must afford Reich a clear and certain opportunity for the refund or constitutionally adequate equivalent relief due under federal law, subject to ordinary proof of payment and amount; it may prospectively establish an adequate prepayment system with fair notice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McKesson Corp. v. Division of Alcoholic Beverages and Tobacco, 496 U.S. 18 (1990): applies its clear-and-certain-remedy requirement to withdrawal of an apparently available postpayment route; adequate prepayment systems and lawful remedial choices remain permissible.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): applies the distinction between the federal rule governing open direct review and the lawful remedy; no automatic refund or reopening of final judgments follows.
- Barker v. Kansas, 503 U.S. 594 (1992): preserves its separate reservation of refund questions; substantive pension-tax invalidity does not fix the amount or form of relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The substantive tax invalidity is already accepted. The Court neither declares every Georgia prepayment avenue inadequate nor converts discretionary stays into guaranteed stays. It creates no general government-estoppel doctrine, exemption from clearly announced reasonable requirements, fixed refund, interest award, or reopening of final judgments. Prospective procedural changes remain permissible with adequate review and fair notice.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Georgia Supreme Court's denial of adequate backward-looking relief is reversed and remanded for a clear and certain lawful remedial opportunity. No refund amount, interest award or reopening of final judgments is ordered.

#### United States v. Brockamp and United States v. Scott

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1225.
**Decided:** February 18, 1997.

##### The enacted filing scheme rebuts nonstatutory equitable suspension

**Holding:** Section 6511's integrated filing, allowance and amount scheme does not permit the requested nonstatutory equitable extension for mental incapacity or extraordinary inability to file. Assuming Irwin's rebuttable presumption and the taxpayers' private-collector analogy apply, the scheme rebuts that presumption; incapacity and absence of prejudice supply no additional period.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous.

**Material application:** Return taxes use three years from actual return filing or two years from payment, whichever is later, and two years from payment without a return; stamp-tax claims separately use three years from payment. Section 6511(b)(1) independently prohibits late allowance. Scott's incapacity is accepted, while Brockamp's remains disputed; neither requires a new tolling trial.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming an otherwise timely return-based claim under §6511(a), §6511(b)(2)'s separately prescribed payment window is not enlarged by the requested mental-incapacity or extraordinary-inability tolling. These tax payments fall outside the applicable ordinary windows, including any supported return-filing extension; this amount limit independently defeats the ordinary tax-refund recoveries without deciding whether McGill's disputed remittance was instead a deposit.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, unanimous; the payment-window ground is expressly independently sufficient.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** Section 6511(b)(2)(A) reaches payments within three years plus a supported return-filing extension; its stamp-tax proviso uses three years. Subparagraph (B) uses two years for other claims, and (C) measures allowances without claims from the allowance date. Return-filing extensions differ from assessment agreements under (c). No qualifying agreement, special-period item or mitigation ground is established. The Court announces no universal jurisdictional classification or ruling on fraud, governmental inducement, separate recoupment or constitutional disability claims. Irwin is assumed applicable and rebutted by this scheme; Dalm, Lundy, Williams, Henderson and O'Gilvie retain their distinct consent, route, window, service and payment-trigger holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both appellate judgments are reversed and remanded. Scott's $30,096 award and dependent interest cannot stand on tolling, and earlier interest is not reopened. The lawful consequences of Brockamp's preserved deposit alternative remain for appellate consideration under Part III's eight-Associate rationale; Stone-Zsela agrees with that disposition only. No deposit classification, refund amount or unconditional restoration of the district judgment is ordered.

#### Reiter v. Cooper, 507 U.S. 258 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1496.

**Decided:** March 8, 1993.

##### The statutory claim may be asserted before payment

**Holding:** A shipper may assert its claim for unreasonable-rate damages under 49 U.S.C.§ 11705(b)(3) as a counterclaim in the carrier's action for undercharges on the same shipments; Rule 8(c) allows correction of its designation as a defense when justice requires. Under§ 11706(g), the claim accrues upon delivery or tender of delivery, so payment of the disputed tariff charge is not a prerequisite; a negotiated rate alone supplies no common-law defense to the filed tariff.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this statutory rule and application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc., 497 U.S. 116: applied; its rejection of the negotiated-rate unreasonable-practice policy remains, alongside the statutory unreasonable-rate remedy.
- United States ex rel. Louisville Cement Co. v. ICC, 246 U.S. 638: its payment-based accrual construction was superseded by Congress's delivery-accrual provision; it cannot establish the proposed prerequisite.
- Southern Construction Co. v. Pickard, 371 U.S. 57: applied for assertion of related counterclaims under ordinary federal procedure.
- Texas & Pacific Railway v. Mugg, 202 U.S. 242; Louisville & Nashville Railroad v. Maxwell, 237 U.S. 94; Pittsburgh, Cincinnati, Chicago & St. Louis Railway v. Fink, 250 U.S. 577: their bars on common-law avoidance of filed tariffs remain; statutory reparations are distinct.
- T.I.M.E. Inc. v. United States, 359 U.S. 464: its rejection of a free-standing unreasonable-rate defense does not eliminate the later express statutory cause of action.

##### Same-transaction recoupment survives the affirmative limitation

**Holding:** The two-year period in§ 11706(c)(2) for an affirmative unreasonable-rate damages action does not bar a shipper from reducing liability in a timely carrier action through recoupment arising from the same shipments. This defensive recovery extends only to the asserted liability; the decision creates no unlimited extension for unrelated claims or affirmative recovery beyond it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices support this independent limitation ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bull v. United States, 295 U.S. 247: applied; same-transaction recoupment survives while the principal action is timely.
- United States v. Western Pacific Railroad, 352 U.S. 59: applied for the analogous statutory limitation rationale; the Government's distinct setoff power is not made a condition of private recoupment.

##### Primary jurisdiction does not withdraw judicial jurisdiction

**Holding:** A court hearing the statutory unreasonable-rate claim must allow a reasonable opportunity for the parties to obtain the ICC's determination of rate reasonableness, but that primary-jurisdiction procedure does not deprive the court of jurisdiction. The court may retain the action, ordinarily staying proceedings for that purpose, or dismiss without prejudice only if the parties would not be unfairly disadvantaged; neither prior payment nor a completed ICC ruling is required to file the civil claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this allocation and its qualifications.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Western Pacific Railroad, 352 U.S. 59: applied to allocate the expert rate issue while preserving the judicial claim.
- Mitchell Coal & Coke Co. v. Pennsylvania Railroad, 230 U.S. 247: applied for a stay affording a reasonable opportunity to request an agency ruling.
- Carnation Co. v. Pacific Westbound Conference, 383 U.S. 213: applied to retention or nondisadvantaging dismissal without prejudice.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, 467 U.S. 837: applied to the ICC's longstanding reasonable construction of its reparations authority, without new deference doctrine.
- Myers v. Bethlehem Shipbuilding Corp., 303 U.S. 41, and Heckler v. Ringer, 466 U.S. 602: distinguished; their exhaustion setting does not supply the missing statutory prerequisite here.

##### Separate judgment requires the ordinary discretionary determination

**Holding:** A separate final judgment on the carrier's claim requires Rule 54(b)'s express determination that there is no just reason for delay, considering judicial administration and the actual equities. Prompt suit by a solvent carrier ordinarily favors separate judgment, while actual or threatened insolvency of the party seeking judgment weighs against it without creating an absolute bar; appropriate security and an enforcement stay under Rule 62(h) remain available where other equities justify them.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices support the rule and remand rather than deciding discretion initially.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sears, Roebuck & Co. v. Mackey, 351 U.S. 427, and Cold Metal Process Co. v. United Engineering & Foundry Co., 351 U.S. 445: applied for Rule 54(b)'s separate-judgment authority and safeguards.
- Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. General Electric Co., 446 U.S. 1: applied; actual or threatened insolvency is relevant, not an absolute bar, and protective conditions may address competing risks.
- Crancer v. Lowden, 315 U.S. 631: distinguished; it sustained discretionary collection without an available judicial reparations counterclaim after the shippers chose the separate rail-agency route, rather than requiring collection in every case.
- Keogh v. Chicago & Northwestern Railway, 260 U.S. 156; Arizona Grocery Co. v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway, 284 U.S. 370; Lowden v. Simonds-Shields-Lonsdale Grain Co., 306 U.S. 516: applied for the continuing force of filed rates not disapproved by the ICC.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Referral means allowing a party time to seek an ICC determination, not commanding the agency to answer. Negotiated rates do not supplant filed tariffs. Same-transaction recoupment fixes the estate’s proper claim rather than paying an unrelated preference. No rate, affirmative excess damages or separate-judgment outcome is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for the statutory counterclaims, an appropriate opportunity to obtain the ICC rate ruling and an express Rule 54(b) determination. No automatic carrier priority or insolvency bar applies.

### Unreasonable-rate counterclaims, recoupment and primary jurisdiction

**Governing question:** What instructions govern unreasonable-rate counterclaims, recoupment and primary jurisdiction?

**Current rule:** A shipper may assert §11705(b)(3) unreasonable-rate damages as a counterclaim in an undercharge action on the same shipments; Rule 8(c) permits correcting a defense designation when justice requires. Under §11706(g), accrual occurs upon delivery or tender, without prior payment. A negotiated rate alone supplies no common-law defense to the filed tariff. The two-year affirmative limitation in §11706(c)(2) does not bar same-transaction recoupment up to the carrier's asserted liability, but supplies no extension for unrelated claims or excess affirmative recovery. The court must allow a reasonable opportunity to obtain the ICC's rate determination. It retains jurisdiction and ordinarily stays proceedings; dismissal without prejudice is permitted only if it would not unfairly disadvantage the parties. Neither payment nor a completed ICC ruling is required to file.

Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices support this independent limitation ruling.
- Primary jurisdiction does not withdraw judicial jurisdiction: Reiter v. Cooper — Primary jurisdiction does not withdraw judicial jurisdiction, 1993-03-08. Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this allocation and its qualifications.

### Tax-refund filing and payment limits do not permit incapacity tolling

**Governing question:** Whether mental incapacity or extraordinary inability to file permits a nonstatutory extension of the applicable §6511 tax-refund limits.

**Current rule:** The integrated filing, allowance and recovery-amount scheme in §6511 rebuts the requested equitable extension for mental incapacity or extraordinary inability to file, even assuming Irwin's rebuttable presumption applies. The separate §6511(b)(2) payment lookback is not enlarged by that extension even if the claim is otherwise timely under §6511(a). Actual filing, the return due date and the separately recoverable amount remain distinct; express statutory adjustments retain their own conditions.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Brockamp — The enacted filing scheme rebuts nonstatutory equitable suspension, 1997-02-18; Breyer Part I for all nine supplies the filing-scheme exclusion
- United States v. Brockamp — The payment lookback independently bars these tax-payment recoveries, 1997-02-18; Breyer Part II for all nine supplies the independent amount-limit exclusion

**Present operation effective:** Brockamp, 1997-02-18.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule concerns tax payments; whether a remittance instead is a deposit requires its own determination. It establishes no universal jurisdictional classification and resolves no separate fraud, inducement, recoupment or constitutional theory.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Brockamp and United States v. Scott (1997-02-18) — The enacted filing scheme rebuts nonstatutory equitable suspension; United States v. Brockamp and United States v. Scott (1997-02-18) — Federal Taxation / Refund limitations and equitable tolling.

### December 15, 1997 — Bay Area Laundry v. Ferbar

[Public Projection](../records/bay_area_laundry_v_ferbar_merits_1997-12-15.md#public-projection). Breyer's Parts I–II are unanimous; Part III has the eight Associate Justices, with Stone-Zsela agreeing only in its first-installment-exclusion judgment and filing no separate writing. Withdrawal creates §1381 liability, but collection accrues only after assessment, a schedule and demand, and an actually missed due installment. Section 1451(f) allows the longer of six years after accrual or three years after the plaintiff acquired or should have acquired actual knowledge of the cause; fraud or concealment permits suit within six years after discovery, without a finding of either here. Knowledge of withdrawal is not knowledge of an accrued collection cause.

Each missed installment has its own period unless a legally effective election accelerates the debt; the accelerated-debt period runs from that exercise, and a later aggregate demand revives no barred installment. Nonpayment default requires failure to cure within sixty days after written notice; an alternative plan-defined default must indicate substantial likelihood of inability to pay. Default, delinquency, and a cure warning do not themselves establish acceleration. Whether the February 1993 complaint's alternative full-debt request accelerated the debt remains open. The lower courts must identify actual due dates and effective acceleration without inventing a schedule.

Section 1399(b)(1)'s as-soon-as-practicable assessment-and-demand duty and proper delay objections survive. Scheduled payments commence no later than sixty days after demand notwithstanding review or appeal; no exhaustion rule postpones them. Part III excludes the first $345.50 claim because of the premise on which the Fund presented the controlling installment conflict for review. Receipt-based and sixty-day arguments do not undo that petition posture. No exact receipt date, universal February 1 maturity, new concession, or universal alternative-argument forfeiture rule is found.

Milwaukee distinguishes computational dates from actual demand, payment, default, and acceleration without previously deciding accrual. North Star's borrowing rule does not replace Congress's enacted period. Concrete Pipe's neutral adjudication and lawful factual and actuarial rebuttal burdens and Inter-Modal's rights/enforcement distinction survive. No assessment validity, equitable tolling, arbitration result, or entitlement to every remedy is established. Remand addresses timely later installments and properly remaining assessment, acceleration, and relief issues; it awards no money or full debt and orders no prospective injunction. Original instruments and pleadings are not reproduced, so the demand-receipt date, pre-suit acceleration, and uncompleted arbitration are not supplied.
