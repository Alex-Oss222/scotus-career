## Standards and Tests selection: Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims

### Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims

**Governing question:** The showing needed to overcome successive or abusive habeas barriers through innocence of the offense.

**Current rule:** The prisoner must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the whole probative record, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The inquiry includes the new evidence together with the existing record rather than deferring conclusively to the original jury’s credibility choices. Passing this demanding procedural gateway establishes neither innocence as a freestanding claim nor the underlying constitutional violation and shifts no ultimate merits burden. The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every submitted statement as true. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner.

**Authority by component:**

- Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway: Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway, 1995-01-23. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support a stricter crime-innocence quantum and would affirm.
- This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission: This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission, 1995-01-23. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, statutory-refusal and remand rationale, joined by Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this bounded rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth vote for vacatur and the same gateway-assessment remand on his stated ground, without joining the separate §2244(b) analysis. No aggregation of different rationales is needed for the five-Justice holding.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-23; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Sawyer’s clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway is distinct. Section 2244(b) permits refusal of a subsequent habeas application by a person held under a state-court judgment after a federal court, justice or judge denied release or another habeas remedy on an earlier habeas application following either an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue or a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, unless the later application alleges and rests on a factual or other ground not adjudicated in the earlier hearing and the court is satisfied that the applicant did not deliberately withhold the newly asserted ground on the earlier application or otherwise abuse the writ. Both exception conditions are required. New evidence is not automatically a new ground, and absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. When both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent, McCleskey requires consideration. The qualified refusal permission does not itself sustain refusal based on the rejected clear-and-convincing crime standard. Other lawful independent barriers and necessary factual-development rules remain.

**Burdens and proof:** The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner’s burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate.

**Open question:** Schlup does not decide whether every successful innocence showing compels review, a general appellate standard for refusal, or an automatic hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay.

**Related current rules:** Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims supplies the separate eligibility inquiry and proof quantum.

**Holdings navigation:** Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway; This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission.

## Standards and Tests selection: Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims

### Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims

**Governing question:** Review of successive, abusive or procedurally defaulted capital habeas claims without otherwise required cause and prejudice.

**Current rule:** The prisoner must show by clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the prisoner eligible for death under applicable state law. Examine the offense, required aggravating facts and every other necessary eligibility condition. Additional mitigation affecting only the discretionary selection of death does not satisfy this gateway. Opening the gateway permits consideration of an otherwise barred claim; it does not itself establish the claim or grant relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Eligibility-only scope, no-reasonable-juror inquiry and clear-and-convincing burden: Sawyer v. Whitley, “Capital innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred claims”, June 22, 1992; Kennedy's opinion joined by White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas. Broader judgment concurrences supply no component.

**Present operation effective:** *Sawyer*, June 22, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary cause and prejudice, exhaustion, retroactivity, merits standards and proper factual-development rules remain distinct. Neither ordinary sentencing prejudice nor discretionary mitigation replaces this gateway. Freestanding innocence claims remain unresolved. No categorical exclusion of impeachment evidence follows from its insufficiency on Sawyer’s record.

**Holdings navigation:** Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333 (1992) — Habeas procedural gateways.

**Related current rules:** Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims states Schlup’s distinct January 23, 1995 offense-innocence inquiry; it does not change this death-eligibility quantum.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which law governs a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

**Authority by component:**
- Application-date construction and preserved exceptions: Lindh, Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six), 1997-06-23.

**Present operation effective:** Lindh v. Murphy, 1997-06-23.

## Standards and Tests selection: Second or successive state-custody habeas applications

### Second or successive state-custody habeas applications

**Governing question:** Repeated/new claims, appellate authorization and district verification under amended §2244(b).

**Current rule:** A claim in a second or successive §2254 application already presented in a prior application must be dismissed. A new claim must also be dismissed unless it relies on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court, or both its factual predicate was not previously discoverable through due diligence and its facts, if proved and viewed with all evidence, would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found the applicant guilty of the underlying offense. Before district filing, the applicant must seek appropriate appellate authorization; a three-judge panel may authorize only on a prima facie showing of these requirements and must act within thirty days. A grant or denial is not appealable and cannot be the subject of rehearing or certiorari. Authorization is not final entitlement: the district court must dismiss unless the applicant establishes the statutory conditions.

**Authority by component:**

- Separate repeated/new claim restrictions and two-stage authorization/verification: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §106(b), 28 U.S.C. §2244(b)(1)–(4), enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.
- Post-enactment authorization-review bar and distinction from original filing: The withdrawn review route and retained original jurisdiction; Temporal operation of the channel rule; Successive restrictions and original-writ discretion, 1996-06-28. Felker Part I-A and II, all nine; the channel temporal result has all nine, its Part I-B explanation Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (six).

**Present operation effective:** Statutory requirements enacted 1996-04-24; Felker’s specified review-channel construction, 1996-06-28.

**Effective transition:** Enacted April 24, 1996. “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” states Lindh's noncapital chapter 153 application-date construction, effective 1997-06-23. Separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case clause and Felker's successive-petition treatment remain distinct; Lindh does not settle every other Title I provision's application to pending proceedings. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** A request to announce a new rule is not reliance on an existing Supreme Court rule made retroactive. Schlup’s gateway and §2244(b)(2)(B)’s cumulative conditions are not interchangeable. The district authorization mechanism does not govern Supreme Court original filing; direct mandatory force of paragraphs (1)–(2) there remains reserved.

**Holdings navigation:** Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996). Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application.

#### Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6382.
**Decided:** June 22, 1992.

##### What capital-innocence showing permits review of an otherwise barred habeas claim without cause and prejudice

**Holding:** A prisoner seeking review of an otherwise successive, abusive or procedurally defaulted capital habeas claim without the ordinarily required cause and prejudice must establish by clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the prisoner eligible for death under applicable state law. The inquiry includes the offense, required aggravating facts and every other eligibility condition; additional mitigation affecting only discretionary selection of the punishment does not satisfy this gateway.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt both the eligibility-only scope and clear-and-convincing quantum. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor concur only in the judgment on broader grounds; their votes do not enlarge the controlling rule. No Marks synthesis is necessary.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the capital-eligibility gateway to otherwise barred claims.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The crime-innocence standard is unchanged. Ordinary cause and prejudice, exhaustion, retroactivity, merits requirements and factual development of a properly available claim remain distinct. Passing the gateway does not itself grant relief. No freestanding innocence claim or general bar to consideration of impeachment evidence is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The precise capital formulation previously left open is established effective June 22, 1992. Sawyer's refusal of further review is affirmed on the application stated below; no new hearing, sentencing proceeding or release is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478, preserves cause and prejudice and its miscarriage exception; only the capital-eligibility branch is specified here.
- Smith v. Murray, 477 U.S. 527, has its open sentencing application specified through eligibility rather than discretionary mitigation reweighing.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467, is applied: abuse remains an independent obstacle unless cause and prejudice or the applicable exceptional gateway is established.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436, supplies relevant plurality discussion without becoming a retrospective majority holding.
- Dugger v. Adams, 489 U.S. 401, is applied: possible sentencing inaccuracy does not itself establish exceptional innocence.
- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862, and Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231, supply the distinction between death eligibility and selection of punishment.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104, preserve mitigation protection on the merits without independently reopening a barred claim.
- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668, and United States v. Bagley, 473 U.S. 667, supply different reasonable-probability merits inquiries; they do not replace the exceptional gateway.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, May 4, 1992, retains Townsend for development of properly available claims without removing successive or abusive-claim barriers. Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992, and Sochor v. Florida, June 8, 1992, preserve correction of available weighing errors without eliminating independent barriers; Wright v. West, June 19, 1992, changes no general habeas-review standard.

##### Whether Sawyer's added evidence removes every reasonable basis for death eligibility

**Holding:** Sawyer's psychiatric and family evidence concerns mitigation, while the impeachment affidavits and caregiver account do not clearly and convincingly establish that no reasonable juror would find the surviving bases of death eligibility. His abusive Brady claim and successive sentencing-ineffectiveness claim therefore remain barred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five adopt this gateway application. The remaining four Justices independently support affirmance under broader inquiries but do not join this rationale.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as the stated application to Sawyer's submissions; it is not a categorical exclusion of later impeachment evidence.

**Material application:** Louisiana principal liability permits aggravated-arson eligibility without personal ignition. Sawyer's participation and the lighter-fluid evidence remain material; the preceding beating and scalding independently support the surviving cruelty aggravator. The credibility affidavits do not require every reasonable juror to reject the core account, and the medical and childhood submissions do not negate necessary eligibility facts.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No constitutional merits claim is resolved after the gateway fails; the evidence is not found false in every respect, and death is not held mandatory. The accepted procedural classifications and cause determination are not reopened. The prior Caldwell ruling, a new challenge to the wording of an aggravator, and the distinct lower-court competency claim outside this petition remain unadjudicated here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit's refusal of further habeas review. No new factual hearing, sentencing proceeding, trial or release follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83, supplies the underlying suppressed-evidence claim but no merits disposition here. Strickland, 466 U.S. 668, does not allow ordinary sentencing prejudice to replace the gateway. Sawyer v. Smith, 497 U.S. 227, retains its distinct Caldwell retroactivity disposition.

#### Schlup v. Delo

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7901.
**Decided:** January 23, 1995.

##### Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway

**Holding:** A prisoner seeking to overcome otherwise applicable successive or abusive habeas barriers to independent constitutional claims through innocence of the offense must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the probative record as a whole, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. This procedural gateway does not itself establish innocence, prove any constitutional violation or shift the ultimate burden on the underlying claims; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing rule remains confined to its distinct death-eligibility setting.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: applies and specifies its probability-based crime-innocence exception without changing cause and prejudice.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333: preserves the clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway and resolves the crime-innocence question that decision left open; it is not overruled or given a categorical impeachment exclusion.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467: preserves successive/abusive-claim barriers and the exceptional innocence route, without treating potential constitutional merit as sufficient to pass it.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436: uses the relevant plurality discussion of the whole probative record as persuasive guidance, preserving its actual authority limit.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307: distinguishes trial-record legal sufficiency from the likely effect of an augmented record on reasonable jurors; its sufficiency rule remains intact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every affidavit as true or treat the original verdict as a conclusive answer. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner. A mere possibility of acquittal, a juror who might disagree, ordinary constitutional-merits prejudice or the reviewing judge's personal reasonable doubt is insufficient. The reasonable juror fairly considers the evidence and follows the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt instruction.

##### This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission

**Holding:** The courts' use of the rejected clear-and-convincing crime gateway and reliance on the original jury's credibility function require vacatur and renewed assessment on this record; §2244(b)'s permission to refuse a qualifying subsequent petition does not itself supply an adequate independent ground to sustain that legally mistaken refusal. The Court remands through the Eighth Circuit to the District Court for application of the probability rule and a reasoned determination of necessary factual development, without deciding whether every successful innocence showing compels review, choosing a general appellate standard, or ordering a hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay automatically.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's statutory-refusal and remand portion, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth judgment vote without joining the separate §2244(b) rationale.

**Current-force modification:** AEDPA §106 replaces former §2244(b)'s refusal permission for successive §2254 district-court applications. A claim already presented must be dismissed. A new claim must be dismissed unless it rests on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence and facts that, if proved and considered with the whole evidence, establish by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt. Advance authorization by a three-judge court-of-appeals panel requires a prima facie showing; authorization does not remove the district court's duty to dismiss a claim unless the applicant proves the statutory requirements. This is an independent statutory condition, not a replacement of Schlup's probability gateway or a ruling that the gateway itself satisfies the new text. The original former-law remand stands. Pending-case application is not generally resolved; Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996, No. 95-8836, applies the post-enactment authorization-review bar while reserving whether §§2244(b)(1)–(2) directly bind original habeas consideration in this Court.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McCleskey, 499 U.S. 467: applies the distinction between conditional consideration of new, nonabusive grounds and legally constrained refusal of successive or abusive grounds; neither permission nor exception becomes an unconditional command.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1, and Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293: preserve necessary factual development within their properly presented, available-claim setting and the deliberate-bypass qualification; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a required hearing there, but no successive/abusive gateway is eliminated.
- Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: requires use of the selected crime-innocence rule before the present refusal can be sustained on its innocence assessment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2244(b) provides:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The prior-hearing alternatives are a factual evidentiary merits hearing or a legal merits hearing, each followed by a federal denial of release or other habeas remedy to a person held under a state judgment. Both unless conditions must be preserved: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent. The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner's burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate; cause and prejudice is not pressed in this review. Subsection (a)'s separate wording is not inserted into subsection (b), and no new subsection (c) predicate is established.

The videotape was trial evidence. Green's asserted prompt call, Faherty's movement account and other later identification accounts must be assessed with the existing record; the Court finds no witness truthful or false and does not find the gateway satisfied. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and a genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. Remand is not mandatory constitutional-merits review. Any necessary live testimony is for a reasoned determination under the applicable rules, not an automatic consequence of affidavits or vacatur. The merits of the suppression and ineffective-assistance claims, a freestanding innocence entitlement and any ultimate guilt determination remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit judgment is vacated and remanded through that court to the District Court for the probability gateway and a reasoned decision on necessary factual development. No gateway satisfaction, automatic hearing, constitutional-merits review, writ, retrial, release or stay is ordered.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §106, replacement §2244(b)(1)–(4) district-court successive-application mechanism; see also Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996, No. 95-8836, distinct original jurisdiction and reserved direct statutory reach. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

#### Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-8836; associated application A-890.
**Decided:** June 28, 1996.

##### Whether §2244(b)(3)(E) bars review of circuit authorization while preserving original habeas

**Holding:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars ordinary certiorari review of a court of appeals' grant or denial of authorization to file a second or successive district-court habeas application; the present certiorari request must be dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. The provision does not repeal the distinct original habeas authority retained by §2241, which permits independent consideration of this petition without affirming or reviewing the circuit's authorization judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part I-A, unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 2241 still names the Court and its Justices; §2244(b)(3)(A) concerns advance authorization for district filing, and the amended transfer rule does not name this Court. Direct filing is procedurally original but constitutionally appellate; §1651 aids existing jurisdiction rather than creates it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Unpresented certification, interlocutory and supervisory channels are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Ordinary certiorari dismissed for want of jurisdiction, 9–0, without affirming the authorization judgment.

##### When the new authorization-review bar governs

**Holding:** The authorization and review proceedings begun after AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment are governed by its specified authorization-review bar. That conclusion does not decide the temporal reach of every Title I provision or apply a new substantive restriction backward merely because a petition is pending.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine support the channel result; O’Connor’s Part I-B has Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six. Thomas, Scalia and Kennedy use a distinct relevant-activity approach.

**Material application:** Landgraf’s provision-specific temporal sequence governs the majority reasoning; a procedural/jurisdictional label alone does not answer retroactivity. The May authorization and subsequent review proceedings began after April 24.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No holding determines every Title I provision’s temporal reach or Georgia’s chapter 154 qualification. Chapter 154’s express pending-case direction does not automatically govern other amendments.

##### What successive restrictions and discretionary conditions govern an original petition

**Holding:** The district-court authorization mechanism does not govern filing this original application. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform the Court's consideration of repeated applications, but whether those substantive provisions bind this Court directly is reserved. Retained jurisdiction does not itself entitle an applicant to an original writ.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** For statutory second/successive §2254 applications, (b)(1) dismisses previously presented claims. New claims under (b)(2) require either a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both previously undiscoverable facts despite due diligence and facts which, if proved with the whole evidence, clearly and convincingly establish that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would find guilt. A request to announce a new rule is not the first exception. Before district filing, (b)(3) requires circuit permission by a three-judge panel upon prima facie showing within thirty days; the determination permits no appeal, rehearing or certiorari. Under (b)(4), district dismissal is required unless the applicant actually establishes the conditions, despite authorization.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether (b)(1)–(2) bind this Court directly remains reserved; they inform the Court’s consideration. Rule 20 separately requires reasons for bypassing district court, exhaustion or satisfaction of §2254(b), exceptional circumstances and unavailability of adequate relief in another form or court. Section 2254(b)(1) permits exhaustion, absence of available state corrective process or circumstances rendering it ineffective; (b)(2) permits, but does not require, denial on merits despite nonexhaustion; counsel’s express waiver is necessary under (b)(3). No exhaustion, process adequacy, waiver or affirmative diligence-failure fact is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Original relief denied, 9–0, on the exceptional-original-showing ground; no return, transfer or hearing is ordered.

##### Whether the channel and successive scheme suspend meaningful review or dictate an unlawful outcome here

**Holding:** The specified review restriction and consideration of the operative successive scheme do not suspend constitutionally sufficient habeas review or prescribe a forbidden judicial outcome as applied to these claims. The original petition receives independent examination and fails on an adequate traditional original-writ ground, without deciding that every future statutory exclusion or every nominal surviving remedy is constitutional.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** The retained original route supplies actual independent claim examination and a sufficient traditional denial ground. Klein distinguishes changed law from a command to falsify facts or decide an unchanged rule in a prescribed manner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No approval of every nominal forum, future exclusion of otherwise sufficient innocence claims, all AEDPA, §§2254(d)–(e), or unpresented supervisory restriction.

##### Whether the submitted medical evidence establishes the Schlup gateway

**Holding:** This submission does not establish Schlup's gateway: reliable new evidence, assessed with all probative evidence, making it more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. That conclusion does not adjudicate the separately pleaded constitutional innocence theory or make the gateway and AEDPA's successive conditions interchangeable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part IV-A, all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela withholds this additional application.

**Material application:** Detailed new three-to-five-day estimates and physiological criticisms are considered with Whitaker’s independently favored earlier death estimate, his acknowledgment that the later interval was possible, and the disappearance, car, call and clothing evidence. The submission does not eliminate reasonable adverse assessment at Schlup’s probability threshold. No affidavit is declared false and other-offense evidence retains its motive/intent/state-of-mind limit.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Gateway passage would permit consideration of a trial claim, not prove it, establish statutory (b)(2)(B), or decide a distinct constitutional innocence entitlement.

##### Whether the separately pleaded constitutional innocence claim warrants exceptional original relief

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming a possible constitutional claim based on actual innocence, this considered submission does not establish exceptional circumstances warranting the Court's original habeas intervention. Original relief is denied without establishing a universal freestanding-innocence burden, rejecting every such constitutional claim, or requiring an additional return merely because new expert opinions are offered.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** O’Connor’s Part IV-B, unanimous; independently sufficient original-writ ground.

**Material application:** Herrera supplies a particular-submission insufficiency holding, not one general constitutional innocence burden. The full medical dispute receives consideration; the conviction and circuit refusal alone do not replace examination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal freestanding-innocence threshold, categorical denial of every such right, presumed adequate Georgia remedy or mandatory further return from new expert opinions.

##### Whether A-890 warrants protection after claim-specific consideration is complete

**Holding:** Execution's irreversibility does not alone establish entitlement to a stay. Because the independently considered claims show no sufficiently substantial basis for relief through the available proceedings and no identified unfinished legally required process warrants protection, application A-890 is denied.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part V, unanimous, in the reasoned combined threshold decision.

**Material application:** Bowersox’s substantial-ground assessment remains distinct from Lonchar’s protection of legally required process and McFarland’s counsel/preparation predicates. Neither the petition’s existence nor retained jurisdiction supplies relief by itself.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic lateness penalty, execution date, altered prior stay, exhaustion of all other possible avenues or execution legality determination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The pending stay request is denied, 9–0, after independent examination; no unfinished identified required process or sufficiently substantial relief basis remains.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Ex parte McCardle:** Applied to the specified withdrawn appellate route; no conclusion follows that every separate habeas source is repealed.
- **Ex parte Yerger and Ex parte Bollman:** Applied to the retained statutory habeas channel and its constitutional appellate character. They do not make issuance of an original writ automatic.
- **Landgraf and Rivers:** Their controlling provision-specific temporal inquiry remains in force; the application here concerns post-enactment authorization/review proceedings, not all pending habeas litigation.
- **McCleskey v. Zant:** Its recognition of lawful successive/abuse restrictions supports consideration of the new standards; it does not eliminate their separate textual requirements or settle their mandatory reach here.
- **United States v. Klein:** Its jurisdiction/rule-of-decision distinction is preserved; the present channel and standards do not direct a factual result under unchanged law.
- **Victor and Sandoval:** The whole-charge reasonable-likelihood test is applied by the eight Associates, with its existing limits on isolated terminology and automatic cures intact.
- **Sullivan:** Its structural-error rule is preserved; the Court finds no constitutional vitiation of the proof standard and conducts no harmless-error substitute.
- **Lisenba:** Its fundamental-fairness inquiry governs the presented medical-testimony trial claim. **Ake, Ford and Eddings** do not supply the proposed automatic physician-only testimony requirement.
- **Schlup:** Its gateway remains distinct from a substantive innocence claim and the new statutory conditions; the eight Associates find the present showing insufficient under its governing probability standard.
- **Herrera:** Its particular-submission holding is preserved without converting any separate theory into a general Court rule. The present original denial likewise assumes the possible constitutional claim without deciding its universal merits threshold.
- **Bowersox, Lonchar and McFarland:** Their distinct substantial-ground, required-process and counsel/preparation rules retain their actual triggers. None guarantees additional proceedings or a stay from filing alone.

**Material application:** The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 in §107(c), contrasted with its omission for chapter 153 enacted alongside it, supplies the temporal construction. Lindh filed his noncapital application on July 9, 1992.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary statutory construction may establish temporal direction without a prescribed formula. No universal substantive/procedural classification, permanent constitutional entitlement to filing-date judicial rules, chapter 154 State-qualification ruling, or decision on confrontation, bias, harmlessness, exhaustion or entitlement to a writ is made.

### April 16, 1998 — Carriger v. Stewart

[Record](../records/carriger_v_stewart_merits_1998-04-16.md#public-projection)

#### Event

**Carriger v. Stewart**, Ninth Circuit No. 95-99025, 132 F.3d 463; merits decision, **April 16, 1998**. Review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) concerns the Ninth Circuit's December 17, 1997 en banc judgment reversing the district court and directing habeas relief requiring a new trial. The federal application was filed in August 1995. The district court treated the new claims as abuse of the writ. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is established; the matter is decided on the merits submission.

The questions are whether the lower court used the correct innocence-gateway and state-finding rules, which statutory provisions govern this pending capital application, and whether its Brady new-trial instruction can stand. The suppression claim concerns the corrections file of the State's central witness, Robert Dunbar. His later confessions and repudiation, the state credibility finding, disputed physical corroboration, and evidence of possible joint participation bear on the gateway. The underlying constitutional claim, the gateway permitting its review, and a freestanding innocence claim remain distinct.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at submission and decision. No nonparticipation is established.

#### Public Action

The Court vacates the present instruction to grant a new-trial writ and remands for the specified gateway and former §2254(d)(8) assessment. The Brady claim and both supported attribution theories remain available through a lawful gateway or independent route. The Court does not order a new trial, release or an evidentiary hearing.

#### Holdings

##### 1. Former-law treatment of the pending capital application

**Holding and operative rule.** The ordinary chapter 153 amendments to §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)'s replacement factual presumption do not govern this capital habeas application, already pending when AEDPA took effect. Former §2254(d)(8) governs the state-finding inquiry; this construction preserves separately directed provisions and does not establish or dispense with chapter 154's state-qualification conditions.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five votes for the temporal construction. Stone-Zsela supports the operative former-law direction, but not this distinct rationale; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reserve the temporal question.

**Controlling explanation.** Lindh v. Murphy, decided June 23, 1997, construed the ordinary chapter 153 amendments by comparing them with Congress's express direction applying chapter 154 to qualifying pending capital cases. Although Lindh's holding involved a noncapital application, the statutory comparison also governs this pending capital application outside any established chapter 154 qualification. Capital status alone does not transform the filing into a post-enactment application or satisfy the separate qualification requirements. No Arizona qualification has been established here. Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996), addressed a post-enactment authorization proceeding; its provision-specific result does not make every amended merits or hearing restriction govern an already pending application. The contrary present-authority argument cannot erase the distinctions Congress drew among provisions and regulated proceedings. This holding decides the identified merits and factual-treatment amendments, not every AEDPA provision or future filing. Any genuinely necessary hearing provision retains its own temporal and triggering inquiry; the Court does not decide §2254(e)(2)'s application or order a hearing.

##### 2. The gateway requires a whole-record probability determination and a distinct state-finding assessment

**Holding and operative rule.** To pass the innocence gateway for an otherwise barred constitutional claim, reliable new evidence, assessed with the entire probative record, must show that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The lower court must conduct that inquiry and separately apply former §2254(d)(8); its legally inconsistent analysis cannot sustain the present new-trial instruction, but the Court does not conclusively reject the gateway on this submission.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by five votes.

**Additional operative limits.** The State must plead abuse clearly and particularly, identifying prior history and new claims, before the prisoner's responsive burden arises. Former §2244(b)'s refusal permission concerns a prisoner held under a state judgment after a federal court, justice or judge denied release or another habeas remedy following either an evidentiary merits hearing on a material factual issue or a merits hearing on law. Its exception requires both an unadjudicated factual or other ground and satisfaction that the ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise abusive. When both conditions are met and no other barrier applies, McCleskey requires consideration. New evidence does not necessarily create a new ground. Cause and prejudice, the exceptional innocence route, and independent claim conditions remain distinct. No discretionary refusal may rest on a mistaken gateway standard; gateway passage establishes neither a violation nor automatic relief.

**Controlling explanation.** Schlup v. Delo, decided January 23, 1995, supplies a probability inquiry about reasonable jurors, not the reviewing judge's preferred verdict or confidence in the actual verdict. The en banc opinion recited the correct formula but also used a different confidence inquiry and treated an equally plausible account of Dunbar acting alone as an answer to evidence of joint participation. The required assessment must consider the confession, its later repudiation, the subsequent cellmate account, the corrections information, adverse family accounts, and the disputed tape-fingerprint evidence together. Dunbar's involvement does not itself exclude Carriger's participation. Neither recantation, impeachment, prisoner testimony nor trial inadmissibility categorically removes probative evidence; reliability, timing, motives and corroboration determine its weight.

Former §2254(d) protects a qualifying factual determination made by a competent state court after a merits hearing involving the applicant and the State or its officer or agent, evidenced by adequate written indicia. Paragraph (8) requires consideration of whether the pertinent produced state record as a whole fairly supports the finding. Marshall v. Lonberger, 459 U.S. 422 (1983), supplies the protection for historical and credibility findings; the statute supplies the exception. A plausible different inference, later evidence alone, or the state judge's absence from the original trial does not eliminate that protection. Nor can the prior finding conclusively decide evidence it did not assess. The Court does not find the 1988 credibility determination unsupported.

The argument for immediate gateway rejection gives proper weight to the adverse evidence but does not cure the lower court's failure to perform one consistent legal inquiry on the interdependent record. Meaningful exculpatory evidence remains to be assessed under that inquiry. Remand permits either gateway conclusion and requires no automatic hearing.



##### 3. Conditional Brady materiality does not establish suppression or gateway passage

**Holding and operative rule.** If the materially new corrections-file information described by the en banc court is established to have been suppressed under a legally applicable prosecution duty, its collective lawful impeachment, preparation and investigative uses satisfy Brady materiality on this record. A materially smaller proven suppressed set requires its own collective assessment; neither this conditional conclusion nor a successful gateway establishes the other elements of a Brady violation.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six votes for this conditional instruction. No present Brady violation or entitlement to a writ is adjudicated.

**Controlling explanation.** Kyles v. Whitley, decided April 19, 1995, requires collective consideration of favorable evidence actually suppressed and asks whether its lawful uses create a reasonable probability of a different result. It does not require each item to be independently material, an innocence showing, or a conclusion that acquittal is more likely than conviction. The materially new file information concerns habitual blame shifting, violence and armed conduct contradicting the presentation of the prosecution's central witness. It could affect the framing defense and examination of the investigation, beyond merely adding to the six disclosed burglary convictions and the disclosed gun-form falsehood. The State's reliance on those disclosures, immunity and surviving corroboration does not answer the combined effect of this more specific information.

Suppression remains separate. Knowing that a file exists does not establish knowledge of each favorable item; information actually known to the defense cannot be relabeled suppressed. Carriger's letter directing counsel to obtain the file matters, but does not prove knowledge of every material content. The later confession is not treated as evidence available for trial disclosure years earlier, and speculative investigative fruits are not assumed. Kyles makes knowledge held by police or others acting for this prosecution attributable despite the trial prosecutor's ignorance; it does not impute every government repository's contents. Actual case-team knowledge remains unresolved here. Once a complete Brady violation is established, Kyles requires no second duplicative harmlessness inquiry. Brecht and Roy supply no general alternative prejudice screen overriding that incorporated materiality rule.

##### 4. The mandate preserves the bounded acquisition question without deciding it

**Holding and operative rule.** After the required gateway analysis, the lower courts must decide the Brady claim if the gateway or an independent lawful route permits merits review. Actual case-team attribution and the proposed duty to obtain the State's identified corrections file on its own central witness remain distinct available theories; the mandate neither makes proof of actual team possession the exclusive route nor adopts the proposed acquisition duty.

**Authority.** Stevens's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls the remand by five votes.

**Controlling explanation.** Kyles's affirmative responsibility for information held by the prosecution team can resolve suppression if the requisite facts are established. Its organizational boundary does not itself decide whether the State's choice to rely on this known central witness requires obtaining the identified credibility file from corrections officials. That proposed duty has a specific fair-trial basis and raises a distinct objection concerning acquisition of information from a separate repository. It is neither equivalent to universal government imputation nor established simply because the file would be useful or accessible.

If actual attribution is absent or insufficient and the acquisition theory remains necessary and properly presented through a lawful merits route, its constitutional merits and any actually applicable collateral conditions must be considered. The remand does not require extra fact development solely to avoid an otherwise properly decidable legal question. Unknown presentation, finality or waiver facts establish no Teague defense and impose no duty on Carriger to disprove an unasserted bar. An extension beyond Kyles's precise holding is not automatically a new rule under Teague. If nonretroactivity is properly available, the finality-era dictated-rule inquiry and both contemporary exceptions—substantive restrictions on punishment and watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness—retain their own requirements. No such classification or exception is decided here.

The Court awards no freestanding innocence relief and sets no uniform standard for that claim. Herrera v. Collins, 506 U.S. 390 (1993), supplies only its record-specific insufficiency holding. If the gateway fails and no independent lawful route exists, the Brady claim supplies no writ. If a lawful route and a complete conviction-level Brady violation are established, conditional new-trial relief remains available under the governing habeas rules. No outcome, hearing or retrial period is predetermined.

#### Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the operative vacatur and complete remand, including Parts II–IV, while explaining why he would have affirmed the new-trial relief outright. He separates the Schlup gateway, a freestanding innocence claim, and the constitutional suppression claim. In his assessment, the new evidence permits gateway review, and the combined withheld impeachment undermines confidence in the conviction under Kyles. He does not accept a recanting witness's confession mechanically: incentives, consistency, physical corroboration and later repudiation matter, as does the original prosecution's dependence on the same witness's credibility. His agreement to require correct further analysis does not withdraw that personal assessment or convert it into the Court's finding.

He would extend Kyles's duty to learn to impeachment information about the State's own key witness in the State Department of Corrections' records when the prosecution relies on that witness and the records are readily accessible to the State. On that bounded theory, failure to obtain and disclose material favorable information is suppression even if the trial prosecutor never personally held the file. He would affirm conditional new-trial relief, permitting a lawful retrial, rather than declare innocence or order acquittal. His proposal does not impute every government office's knowledge or impose open-file or unrestricted investigative duties. The Court preserves this theory for lawful consideration but does not adopt it.

**Justice O'Connor, concurring in Parts I and III and dissenting from Parts II and IV; Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join the dissent only.** The four agree that the present writ instruction must be removed but would hold this submission insufficient under the controlling Schlup probability rule. Dunbar's possible guilt does not exclude Carriger's joint participation. The adverse family accounts, disputed but probative tape evidence, and supported state credibility finding retain force even when the confession and new impeachment are considered. In their view, those features make another gateway determination unnecessary: correcting the lower court's formulation still leaves the demanding probability showing unmet. They would preserve independently available unaddressed claims while foreclosing this otherwise-abusive Brady route on the present submission.

Their position neither reinstates a stricter gateway burden nor categorically excludes recantation, prisoner testimony, impeachment or trial-inadmissible evidence. They do not treat the state finding as conclusive on later evidence. O'Connor separately accepts Part I's bounded statutory construction and Part III's conditional full-file materiality; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reserve transition and do not reach materiality. All four reserve the acquisition duty rather than reject its constitutional merits, and find no established absence of actual case-team knowledge. Their reservation leaves any genuinely independent lawful future route to its own conditions.
