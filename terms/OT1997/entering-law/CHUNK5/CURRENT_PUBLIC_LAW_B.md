## Event

United States v. Rybar, Third Circuit No. 95-3185. Merits decision, November 24, 1997; October Term 1997, chunk 1. The Court reviews under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) the December 30, 1996 judgment, 103 F.3d 273, sustaining possession convictions under 18 U.S.C. §922(o). Conditional pleas preserve the Commerce Clause and Second Amendment challenges to Counts I and III.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Commerce Clause challenge is rejected. The appellate judgment affirming possession Counts I and III is vacated, and the case is remanded for the preserved Second Amendment application.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Component | Supporting | Opposing |
|---|---|---|
| Reject the commerce challenge, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | None |
| Recognize personal invocation of the arms guarantee, 9–0; the claim proceeds to application | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | None |
| Vacate the appellate affirmance and remand the arms question, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela would affirm Counts I and III |

The remand orders no acquittal, release, return of weapons, new trial, automatic hearing, or resentencing. Any consequential relief depends on the lawful disposition of the affected possession counts. Dismissed NFA transfer Counts II and IV remain outside the judgment.

## Opinion Topology

Kennedy delivers the opinion of the Court. All other Justices join Parts I–II on commerce power and personal invocation. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer join Part III's vacatur and remand. Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I–II and dissents from Part III and the judgment.

## Holdings

**Congress may regulate these possessions as part of the machinegun-market regime.** Section 922(o)'s possession component constitutionally reaches Rybar's possession of the two completed machineguns because Hale already established that possession may be included as a necessary anti-evasion and anti-impairment component of Congress's direct prohibition on additions to the post-1986 civilian machinegun market. Part I, joined by all nine Justices, rejects Rybar's commerce challenge; the possession component's validity does not depend on adding a transaction-specific interstate-crossing element to §922(o).

Rybar possessed and sold two completed submachine guns in consecutive gun-show transactions. The sales make the market relationship concrete, while the offenses remain possession. The enacted regime restricts both transfer and possession of post-ban machineguns, subject to the statute's government-related channels and prior-lawful-possession exception. A transfer restriction would be materially impaired if newly prohibited stock could be acquired, retained, held, concealed or circulated free of the possession component; Rybar's transactions fall at the center rather than the edge of that anti-evasion mechanism. Lopez requires the Court to identify the statutory class and the interstate mechanism before aggregation or downstream magnitude can perform constitutional work. The mechanism here is not gun violence, fear, insurance cost, productivity, marketability in the abstract, or presumed foreign or military provenance; it is the fitted relationship between possession and enforcement of the valid national machinegun-transfer and stock regime. Hale controls: Rybar creates no broader Commerce category and does not enlarge Hale's anti-evasion rule, but shows that the rule applies when possession occurs in actual commercial transfer circumstances at the core of the regulated market.

The Court does not rewrite §922(o) to require proof that the particular machinegun crossed a State line in Rybar's transaction; the constitutional ground lies in the valid market regime and the necessary inclusion of the defined possession class, not in an omitted case-specific crossing element. Marketability alone, remote interstate history alone and generalized crime costs are each insufficient. The Court does not decide a materially different possession statute lacking a fitted anti-evasion relationship to a valid interstate regime, and it does not establish that every object subject to national regulation may be federally possessed forever because it once moved in commerce. No universally mandatory legislative-findings rule or general police power is adopted. Government-related statutory channels and lawful pre-May 19, 1986 possession retain their statutory scope; manufacture date alone does not establish the latter exception.

**The personal arms claim requires a separate application.** Part II, joined by all nine Justices, holds that Rybar may invoke the personal guarantee without militia enrollment or service. Military usefulness neither excludes an arm nor makes it immune from regulation; a commercial setting does not categorically erase the claim. Necessary acquisition remains connected to protected keeping. Love, Hale and the preserved Miller inquiry require assessment of the actual weapons, conduct and restriction independently of commerce authority and registration duties.

**The displaced affiliation premise requires a bounded return here.** Part III, joined by eight Justices, vacates the appellate affirmance because its material militia requirement is invalid and its additional discussion supplies no adequate independent weapon-and-restriction application. The Chinese Type 54 and U.S. Military M-3 are identified completed automatic weapons, and their transactions are settled inputs. Their known characteristics strengthen the arguments for regulation, but classification alone does not resolve the constitutional restriction. Hale's registration holding does not establish the possession prohibition's validity.

Further consideration may use the existing record or ordinary proceedings genuinely needed. No empirical study, new universal scrutiny tier or comprehensive weapons catalogue is required. The Court does not decide ultimate validity or invalidity, make every lower legal error demand remand, or resolve receivers, conversion parts, accessories, ordinary semiautomatic arms or every §922(o) application.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hale is followed without enlargement: it supplies the anti-evasion/material-impairment rule for the post-1986 civilian machinegun market; its registration rule remains distinct from the possession prohibition.
- Harris and Bishop/Stokes are distinguished as vehicle-taking cases with their own economic/instrumentality mechanism and enacted vehicle nexus.
- American Life League is distinguished as direct interference with an interstate service market.
- Love and Hale's personal-holder and acquisition principles displace a militia-affiliation prerequisite while preserving actual application.
- Miller retains its actual weapon/record significance, without automatic military-use exclusion or immunity.
- Lopez is applied through the Court's controlling sequence: the regulated class and interstate mechanism come first, and downstream consequences cannot create the missing connection. Staples remains a statutory culpability decision and supplies no constitutional weapon classification.

## Law After Decision

The commerce challenge fails under Hale's existing anti-evasion/material-impairment rule; Rybar adds no new Commerce gateway or doctrine. As of this decision, Lopez supplies connection before magnitude and reserves genuine Necessary-and-Proper inclusion; Harris applies a direct vehicle and economic-taking mechanism with an enacted nexus, Stone-Zsela separately emphasizing the instrumentality mechanism; American Life League addresses direct obstruction of an interstate service market; Hale makes the anti-evasion/material-impairment doctrine operational; Bishop/Stokes applies Harris without a new rule; Pic-A-State treats an interstate procurement transmission as interstate commerce itself; Mussari/Schroeder directly protects a legally fixed cross-border payment obligation; and Rybar applies Hale. No interstate-externality branch has been adopted as controlling law. The personal-holder boundaries remain controlling, while the two weapons' ultimate protection against this possession restriction remains open on remand. The Court adopts no categorical automatic-weapons exclusion, general danger exception, universal scrutiny tier or automatic-remand rule.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela would affirm both possession convictions through an independent constitutional application. Rybar holds the personal right without militia affiliation, and a substantial possession restriction cannot be justified merely by calling it commercial regulation. Fully automatic operation—repeated discharge through a single trigger function—is a relevant functional characteristic. Limiting additions to civilian stocks of these completed weapons directly addresses the distinctive risks of sustained automatic fire. Registration can identify and trace arms; licensing and recordkeeping do not themselves prevent additions to that stock. He finds that preventive fit sufficient for this substantial burden in these transactions.

He treats preservation of prior lawful stock as a transition limiting expansion, not evidence that older weapons are safer; age alone establishes no exception. Government channels do not make civilian military usefulness either disqualifying or immune. The continuing availability of ordinary firearms describes the restriction's reach, but substitutes alone would not justify prohibiting a protected arm. His conclusion rests on the identified weapons and mechanism, not invented casualty findings. It establishes no universal rule for dangerous or military arms, no equivalence between machineguns and ordinary semiautomatic firearms, and no result for receivers, conversion components or accessories. The dismissed transfer charges provide no alternative basis for conviction. This independent application is his ground for dissent from remand, not a holding of the Court.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for the preserved arms inquiry under the corrected personal-right rules. The admitted weapon characteristics and transactions need not be relitigated. Ultimate relief follows the lawful resolution of Counts I and III; dismissed Counts II and IV are not revived.

## Event

United States v. Parker. Merits decision, December 1, 1997; October Term 1997, chunk 1. On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Third Circuit's judgment in No. 95-2018, 108 F.3d 28, reversing dismissal of a criminal information. The questions concern Congress's authority to punish willful nonpayment of an existing support obligation where the obligor and children reside in different States, and the statute's relation to state domestic-relations authority.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment reinstating the prosecution is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. **Opposing:** none. The prosecution may proceed on the actual federal elements and properly available defenses. No guilt, conviction, sentence, restitution amount, or treatment of older arrears in a future restitution order is adjudicated.

## Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

**The original §228 validly regulates willful nonpayment of the legally fixed interstate support obligation and does not displace state support adjudication.** O'Connor's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls both grounds and their limits.

Mussari/Schroeder governs the direct payment relation between the Florida obligor and his children in Pennsylvania. Different-state residence is essential; the alternative thresholds of nonpayment longer than one year or an amount greater than $5,000 cannot replace it. No completed payment, travel, flight, wire, voluntary border crossing, or evasion motive is an additional element. Under Lopez, the required cross-border payment supplies the commerce connection itself; the holding does not rest on remote poverty or welfare costs or other generalized economic consequences. Calling the underlying obligation domestic does not remove that interstate relation.

The statute regulates the private obligor, not state government. Absence of commandeering does not independently create commerce power, but the direct-payment ground supplies it here. The federal court accepts the valid state order; it may not create or revise support, custody, parentage, or domestic status, or disregard a lawful state modification. Proper challenges to the predicate order's existence or continuing force remain available.

Only willful failures after October 25, 1992 may constitute the offense. Earlier arrears may supply background or past-due status. Every actual element requires proof beyond reasonable doubt; lawful ability-related and other defenses remain. The decision concerns reinstatement of the information, not proof of those elements or the scope of any eventual restitution order.

## Precedent Treatment

Mussari/Schroeder is applied to the direct interstate-payment obligation. Lopez is applied: the statutory cross-border payment mechanism supplies the connection it requires before magnitude, and attenuated social-cost reasoning cannot supply it. New York and Printz/Mack preserve their distinct constraints on state commandeering; this prosecution directly regulates a private party. Rybar's market holding and separate arms application do not alter this support-payment rule. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

The original §228 reaches willful post-enactment withholding of the specified interstate support payment, subject to its actual thresholds, defenses, and valid state-order predicate. Federal prosecution supplies no authority to create or modify domestic-relations orders.

## Event

Salinas v. United States, No. 96-738. Merits decision, December 2, 1997; argued October 8, 1997; October Term 1997, chunk 1. On certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), the Court reviews affirmance of Salinas's two federal bribery convictions and RICO conspiracy conviction. The questions concern a demonstrable effect on federal funds under §666 and personal commission or agreement to commit two predicates under §1962(d).

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed on both presented statutory questions.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed on each question, 9–0. Supporting both:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. **Opposing either:** none. The substantive §1962(c) acquittal remains intact. No conviction is enlarged, sentence independently reviewed, or new instruction or special remedial remand directed.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court on both questions, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

**Federal bribery — no demonstrable-effects requirement.** Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, holds that §666 does not require proof that the bribe demonstrably affected federal funds.

The text's institutional and transaction predicates do not add traced federal dollars as another element. Wells supports that distinction without construing §666 itself; context and Ratzlaf's residual lenity do not establish the proposed condition. The federally connected jail and corrupt contact-visit scheme permit this answer while expressly reserving whether the statute requires a distinct connection between the bribe and expenditure of federal funds. Outer constitutional applications are separately reserved. Neither reservation adopts a nexus test or burden.

The Government must satisfy agency, corrupt intent, acceptance of a thing of value to influence or reward, covered entity business or transactions worth at least $5,000, and qualifying federal benefits exceeding $10,000 in the relevant one-year period. Section 666(c)'s bona fide compensation and ordinary-course expense exception remains operative. The $5,000 condition concerns the covered business, transaction or series, not automatically the bribe, watches or truck. Qualifying-benefits status is assumed for this review; no rule makes every federal receipt a benefit. No precise annual amount is found, intangible-benefit coverage decided, or valuation reopened. These limits do not excuse proof of an actual element.

**RICO conspiracy — knowing agreement, without two personally promised predicates.** Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, holds that §1962(d) requires a knowing agreement to further an undertaking that, if carried out, would satisfy the substantive enterprise offense; it does not require personal commission or personal agreement to commit two predicate acts.

The agreement offense differs from personal completion of the contemplated crime. Its object must nevertheless be unlawful and encompass the applicable substantive elements, including Reves's governing direction requirement. That requirement does not automatically demand that every conspirator personally direct the enterprise. Mere association, knowledge, friendship or employment is insufficient. Shabani supports the agreement distinction at its own statutory scope, not a universal conspiracy code.

The Court rejects the personal-two-act instruction objection. It does not approve the entire charge, decide an independent personal-direction instruction, general evidentiary sufficiency or harmlessness, announce general aiding-and-abetting liability, or revive the substantive acquittal. No general overt-act holding is adopted.

## Precedent Treatment

Wells is applied by analogy to distinguish actual statutory conditions from the proposed additions; Ratzlaf preserves context and residual lenity. Shabani informs the agreement inquiry without supplying a universal conspiracy rule. Reves remains the governing substantive enterprise rule and had reserved conspiracy; today's construction preserves the substantive object rather than transferring every performance requirement to each conspirator. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Section 666 does not require a demonstrable effect on federal funds; any distinct statutory expenditure nexus and outer constitutional applications remain open. Section 1962(d) requires the specified knowing unlawful agreement without two personally promised predicates. Actual coverage, culpability and substantive-object requirements remain intact.

## Participation

## Public Action

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 5–4. Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. **Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. The discharge remains in place. The decision orders no new hearing, reinstatement or compensation and decides no independent APA claim.

## Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. Justice Stevens dissents, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Souter and Ginsburg. The Chief Justice also files an additional dissenting statement specifying the conditions for further military action under his proposed remedy.

## Holdings

**This record permits the conduct-related inference after genuine consideration of rebuttal.** Ordinary rationality permits the statement inference sustained in Thomasson only with a genuine opportunity to rebut; propensity requires likelihood beyond abstract desire. O'Connor's opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, controls the application sustaining this discharge.

Thomasson requires meaningful rebuttal, not acceptance of every denial. Neither completed prohibited conduct nor individual operational injury is necessary. Richenberg's service and character evidence mattered without automatically defeating the inference. Two hearings received that evidence and his denial; cross-examination also produced equivocal answers about future conduct. The existing finding and evidence permit a conduct-related assessment. Discounting general character testimony can reflect its probative reach rather than categorical refusal to consider rebuttal.

The strongest objection is that the brief explanations repeat attraction without explaining disbelief of the undertaking to obey. The proceedings and reasons need not be read as making orientation conclusive. Their supported reading requires neither an invented demeanor finding nor an additional agency reason. A competing inference or sparse explanation alone does not establish a constitutional violation or a general demand for additional findings. Attraction alone remains insufficient; hypothetical appellate reasons cannot replace actual agency reasons.

Section 654(b)(2) permits rebuttal by demonstrating no engagement in, attempt, propensity toward or intent to engage in homosexual acts. Section 654(e) permits omission of processing only with avoidance-or-termination intent and military-best-interest findings; neither is established, and permission is no retention command. Ordinary attraction is not itself a statutory act.

Walmer's preliminary admitted-conduct record does not decide this statement challenge. Romer preserves ordinary rationality tied to the actual disadvantage; Weiss combines military weight with independent review. Jeffries does not substitute its civilian employment balance or retaliation burden for Thomasson. Independent APA reviewability and relief, speech, due process, directive validity, substantive liberty and other ungranted claims remain unresolved. No heightened scrutiny, actual-purpose burden or universal administrative checklist is adopted.

## Precedent Treatment

Thomasson is applied without altering its ordinary-rationality standard, conduct-related likelihood definition or genuine-rebuttal requirement. Walmer is distinguished as a preliminary admitted-conduct case with stipulated retention-exception ineligibility. Romer retains reasonable prediction and conceivable legitimate grounds without a heightened tier or actual-purpose burden. Weiss preserves constitutional review with substantial military weight and supplies no universal discharge checklist. Jeffries's actual-ground requirement remains operative; its civilian balance and retaliation same-decision rule do not replace the military statement-inference rule.

## Law After Decision

Lower courts must distinguish a genuinely rebuttable prediction of conduct, propensity or intent from conclusive orientation exclusion. Service and character evidence require actual consideration but do not automatically require acceptance of a denial or retention. This application establishes neither an attraction-alone rule nor a general constitutional requirement of additional administrative findings and resolves no independent APA standard or claim.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens's dissent, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg.** The dissenters would reverse and require this discharge to be set aside. Thomasson permits a conduct-related prediction only with genuine rebuttal. The actual explanations do not adequately connect ordinary attraction with likelihood of prohibited conduct or explain rejecting the sworn undertaking and supporting evidence. Receiving evidence is insufficient if the reasons deprive it of meaningful effect. Hypothetical lawful relationships and uncertainty about all future conduct do not supply the missing connection.

The Government's adverse-credibility reading deserves consideration under military deference, but an appellate inference cannot replace the required connection in the actual decision. This is an as-applied equality disagreement: good service and a denial are not automatic defenses; completed acts and individual operational harm are unnecessary; the policy and ordinary scrutiny remain intact. Lawful military proceedings remain available, without deciding every retention qualification or awarding unconditional reinstatement or backpay. No independent APA, speech or due-process ground is adopted.

**Stone-Zsela's additional dissenting statement.** Joining that equality ground and complete set-aside remedy, the Chief Justice specifies that any further military action must rest on a supported individualized finding concerning actual conduct, propensity, intent or credibility under a lawful rule. Inability to predict every future relationship is not itself intent to violate military rules. Additional explanation alone would not replace his proposed removal of this discharge.

## Event

Hudson v. United States, No. 96-976. Argued October 8, 1997; decided December 10, 1997. Certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 92 F.3d 1026, reviewing reinstatement of a criminal prosecution after civil banking sanctions.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision. No Justice was recused or otherwise absent.

## Public Action

## Judgment & Remedy

The Court orders no additional accounting hearing and decides neither guilt nor the identity of the administrative predicates and charged offenses.

## Opinion Topology

## Holdings

**1. Civil intent remains rebuttable through examination of actual punitive operation.** Congress's civil designation of these banking monetary sanctions and the regulatory character of the occupational restriction establish the intended civil framework. That designation may be overcome by the clearest proof that the sanctions' purpose or effect is criminal; their supported design and operation must be assessed together, without treating deterrence, severity, occupational burden or overlap with crime as individually conclusive. Part I controls through Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

Ward supplies the intent-and-effect inquiry. Ursery preserves both its demanding override requirement and the distinct field of personal civil penalties; Hendricks requires meaningful examination of actual operation. The monetary provisions expressly impose civil assessments through administrative procedures. Their good-faith and gravity considerations affect amount and do not establish a universal willfulness element. The applicable original monetary authorization cannot be replaced by the increased tiers enacted after the February 1989 notice. Mendoza-Martinez informs punitive-effect examination without requiring a new exhaustive checklist. Civil wording is relevant, but it cannot make actual punishment immune from constitutional review.

**2. These monetary assessments survive Halper's retained personal-penalty inquiry.** A personal civil penalty remains subject to Halper's requirement of a rational remedial relationship, including reasonable approximation; that rule is not overruled or replaced by a universal statutory-face inquiry. On the supported record, the actual $44,000 in total monetary assessments, compared with the District Court's finding of $72,000 in government costs pursuing these defendants, permits a remedial characterization and requires no further accounting. Part II controls through Breyer, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

The Government may recover legitimate pursuit costs without establishing that deterrence played no part in the regulatory response. The objective relation answers the present claim; the agency's failure initially to calculate its costs does not itself establish punishment. The initial $200,000 demand is not the obligation ultimately imposed. Nor are alleged banking losses substituted for proved government costs. This application creates no automatic monetary ratio, immunity for every penalty below an asserted cost figure, or universal compensation ceiling for other sanctions.

**3. The combined sanctions do not bar this prosecution.** The banking restriction, which permits participation with written regulatory authorization, protects positions of trust in a regulated industry. Considered with the actual financial assessments, its supported operation does not provide the clearest proof of criminal punishment; the subsequent prosecution therefore is not barred on the presented multiple-punishment theory. Part III controls through Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

The restriction's substantial occupational effect does not erase its protective function, and the Court considers that effect together with the monetary burden. Kurth Ranch involved an extraordinary arrest-linked assessment on confiscated contraband whose combined operation made it a second punishment; these sanctions have no comparable defining operation. Hendricks concerned prisonlike custody and the actual failure of a civil treatment structure, facts absent here. Those distinctions preserve both precedents. A different banking scheme or materially different operation could produce a different classification. Because criminal punishment is not established, the Court does not decide Blockburger or retained Grady offense identity, attachment in punitive civil proceedings, the Excessive Fines Clause, or independent due-process objections.

## Precedent Treatment

Halper remains binding in its exceptional personal-civil-penalty field and is applied to the supported monetary obligations and costs. Ursery's rebuttable civil classification and preservation of that field remain intact. Hendricks supplies cumulative actual-operation analysis without transferring its confinement outcome. Ward and Mendoza-Martinez retain their intent/effect and punitive-indicator roles. Kurth Ranch's extraordinary-tax holding is distinguished, not narrowed. Dixon, Grady, Blockburger and Felix retain their offense-identity rules; none is applied to determine the identity of these charges. Austin's distinct Excessive Fines protection remains available in an appropriate case. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Effective December 10, 1997, the identified OCC sanctions supply no multiple-punishment bar to this prosecution. Personal monetary sanctions remain subject to Halper's reasonable-remedial-approximation inquiry, while actual combined operation remains material under the civil-intent/clearest-proof framework. Neither regulatory labeling nor a monetary comparison alone creates a categorical safe harbor. No same-offense rule, fines rule, settlement-waiver rule or confinement rule changes.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I and III and the judgment. He applies Ursery and Hendricks without alteration: the combined design and actual operation of these monetary and occupational measures do not establish the clearest proof of criminal punishment. Their accountability and protective functions differ from Kurth Ranch's extraordinary exaction and Hendricks's prisonlike application. He does not join the separate independent Halper-accounting analysis and does not reject Halper's continuing force. His ground leaves different operational facts, excessiveness and offense identity open.

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurs in the judgment. Their account requires successive criminal prosecutions, and the genuinely civil banking proceedings do not supply the first such prosecution. They would reject Halper's freestanding civil multiple-punishment method, while allowing inquiry into whether a nominally civil proceeding is actually criminal. That theory does not command a majority and does not overrule Halper.

The prior appellate nonparticipation ruling retains its procedural scope; consent supplies no newly found waiver or admission. No accounting, conviction, dismissal of individual counts, or scheduled return to this Court is ordered.

## Holdings

### Possession of prohibited post-1986 machinegun stock

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(o) may reach possession of a completed machinegun within the prohibited post-May 19, 1986 civilian stock without proof of a defendant-specific interstate or commercial transaction, because the possession prohibition is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of Congress's valid national restriction on additions to that stock. The Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause supplies that class-and-regime connection; no additional transaction-specific interstate element is required.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule, its application and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Hale's possession rule does not disappear when a particular transfer cannot be proved. Local conversion creates completed prohibited stock that can be retained, concealed, acquired and kept available for circulation. Excluding that possession would materially impair the stock restriction itself. Rybar applied the same rule where gun-show sales made the market connection concrete; its facts did not convert a proved sale into a universal constitutional prerequisite. Kirk's possession conviction therefore stands without treating the reported sales as admitted elements or reviving dismissed transfer charges.

Lopez requires an identified interstate mechanism before aggregation can measure its magnitude. Here the mechanism is the specific transfer-and-stock regime, its grandfathered lawful stock, and the fitted possession prohibition. It is not dangerousness, national marketability, remote interstate movement, generalized gun crime or downstream productivity. Nor does administrative convenience alone establish necessity. A particular interstate element may supply a connection in another statute, but courts need not insert one where this valid regime supplies the connection. Formal findings are not universally required and cannot create power by recital; the identified enacted structure supplies the necessary relationship here.

**Operative exceptions and limits:** Section 922(o)(2) preserves transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. It separately preserves lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986; earlier manufacture alone is insufficient. Neither exception is established for Count One. The holding establishes no general federal firearms police power, no rule for every locally manufactured object, and no automatic justification for every product-market possession prohibition. It decides neither Second Amendment protection nor every conversion-parts, readily-restorable or wear-induced application of the machinegun definition.

## Precedent Treatment

- Lopez supplies the connection-before-magnitude architecture and the necessary-inclusion route; its limit on generalized downstream effects and judicial insertion of elements remains intact.
- Hale is applied to completed prohibited stock produced by local conversion; its anti-evasion and material-impairment rule and statutory exceptions are preserved.
- Rybar is applied without treating its gun-show sales as a new element or a constitutional condition for every possession prosecution; its separate arms holding is not before the Court.
- Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class aggregation scope; neither supplies an independent disposition here.
- Parker's cross-border payment obligation is a distinct commerce mechanism and supplies no substitute firearms rule.

## Law After Decision

Hale's existing possession authority includes this locally converted completed machinegun even without transaction-specific interstate proof. The decision clarifies that application rather than adding a new Commerce category. It leaves ordinary firearm possession, other statutory applications and independent constitutional challenges subject to their own rules.
