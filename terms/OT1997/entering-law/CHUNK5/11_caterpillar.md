#### Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, 511 U.S. 375

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-263.

**Decided:** 1994-05-16.

##### Postdismissal contract enforcement requires a jurisdictional basis

**Holding:** After a Rule 41(a)(1)(ii) stipulated dismissal that neither incorporates settlement obligations nor retains jurisdiction, a court has no ancillary or inherent jurisdiction to enforce the separate settlement merely because it ended a federal action; an independent jurisdictional basis is required. Neither factual interdependence nor authority to manage proceedings supports jurisdiction when the settlement-breach facts are separate from the former claim and breach violates no obligation in the dismissal order; judicial awareness or approval of the agreement is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule, application, and reversal with remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Willy v. Coastal Corp., 503 U.S. 131 (1992): distinguished; collateral Rule 11 authority does not create jurisdiction over a new settlement contract.
- Julian v. Central Trust Co., 193 U.S. 93 (1904): distinguished by its express reservation concerning judicially conveyed property; broad language is not general settlement-enforcement authority.
- Moore v. New York Cotton Exchange, 270 U.S. 593 (1926): its factual-interdependence basis is inapplicable to these distinct controversies.
- Chambers v. NASCO, Inc., 501 U.S. 32 (1991): its authority over litigation misconduct does not convert every settlement breach into interference with judicial proceedings.
- McNutt v. General Motors Acceptance Corp., 298 U.S. 178 (1936): applied; the party asserting federal jurisdiction bears the burden of establishing it.

##### A lawful dismissal order may make settlement compliance enforceable

**Holding:** A court may enforce settlement obligations through ancillary jurisdiction when, within its lawful dismissal authority, its order incorporates those obligations or expressly retains jurisdiction over the settlement, making breach a violation of the judicial disposition. Under Rule 41(a)(2) the court may impose such terms in its discretion; under Rule 41(a)(1)(ii) it may incorporate or retain jurisdiction if the parties agree, but private consent alone, awareness of the bargain, or an attempted later addition after jurisdiction has ended does not create jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this qualification defining the boundary of the jurisdictional holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Julian's actual reservation-based holding remains effective; Rule 41's distinct mechanisms are applied without creating a mandatory retention rule. Rule 60(b) reopening is reserved and is not treated as contract enforcement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides jurisdiction, not breach, specific performance, damages, Rule 60(b) reopening or diversity for a new claim. Neither private consent nor postdismissal judicial rewriting supplies lost jurisdiction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and remanded for dismissal of the settlement-enforcement motion for lack of the asserted ancillary jurisdiction. No breach adjudication or reopening follows. State contract enforcement and a new federal action with an independent jurisdictional basis remain distinct.

#### U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership, 513 U.S. 18

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-714.
**Decided:** November 8, 1994.

##### Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment

**Holding:** When a judgment has been lawfully brought before the Court for review, subsequent mootness bars adjudication of the underlying merits but does not eliminate the Court’s authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of that judgment and the pending proceeding. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not require vacatur merely because review has become moot.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Walling v. James V. Reuter, Inc.*, 321 U.S. 671 (1944), applies the distinction between inability to complete merits review and authority to make an appropriate disposition of the judgments before the Court.
- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), confirms §2106 dispositional authority when appellate merits review becomes unavailable; it does not make every available disposition mandatory.
- *Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America*, 511 U.S. 375 (1994), is distinguished: jurisdiction over a new settlement-enforcement controversy is different from disposition of a judgment already lawfully under review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither enforces the settlement nor supplies a ruling on bankruptcy priority, plan confirmation or the former automatic-stay dispute. The authority under §2106 to make an equitable disposition of a judgment lawfully before an appellate court differs from authority to reopen a completed judgment after the ordinary review process has ended. This decision does not create a new collateral reopening procedure, displace independently applicable Rule 60 requirements, or make a settlement agreement itself sufficient grounds for vacatur.

##### Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur

**Holding:** A party that voluntarily settles while pursuing review does not ordinarily establish equitable entitlement to vacatur of the judgment it sought to overturn; the movant must show a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances remain possible, but a settlement term calling for vacatur is not itself such a circumstance; Bancorp’s showing establishes none, so vacatur is denied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), preserves equitable relief when review is frustrated by happenstance; its actual refusal to erase an unchallenged judgment does not create automatic vacatur after settlement.
- *Karcher v. May*, 484 U.S. 72 (1987), extends its reason for denying vacatur after voluntary abandonment of review to the voluntary settlement here, while preserving its distinct party-capacity setting.
- *Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.*, 510 U.S. 27 (1993), remains a threshold decision that left settlement vacatur undecided. Its separate vacatur discussion supplies no earlier controlling rule.
- *Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc.*, 511 U.S. 863 (1994), remains a collateral-order decision; it does not establish a private right to erase a judgment following settlement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mootness caused by circumstances beyond the movant’s choice, including unilateral action of the prevailing party, remains distinct. No categorical ban on exceptional settlement relief or universal rule of collateral estoppel is adopted. No Rule 60(b) relief or remand to seek it is ordered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacatur is denied and the Supreme Court proceeding dismissed as moot. The Ninth Circuit judgment remains undisturbed without endorsement of its bankruptcy merits; no other order or underlying Chapter 11 proceeding is vacated or dismissed, and no settlement-enforcement or Rule 60(b) remand is ordered.

#### Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-974.
**Decided:** March 3, 1997.

##### Whether this former employee retains a live challenge to the employment rule

**Holding:** An individual prospective employment-rule challenge becomes moot when the employee voluntarily leaves covered employment without an intention to return, realistic recurrence or a surviving claim. A general relief request cannot manufacture nominal damages against a State or official-capacity defendant that is not a damages person under §1983.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous; all nine Justices join every holding.

**Material application:** No class or replacement claimant remained, and the dismissed individual-capacity claims were not preserved on appeal. State nonpersonhood under §1983 is a statutory bar distinct from waivable Eleventh Amendment immunity; a proposed consensual payment cannot supply the missing cause of action. The Attorney General’s constitutional participation under §2403(b) did not overturn the denial of State party status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general requirement to plead nominal damages by name is established. A legally available individual-capacity claim presents a different question. The Court does not fix the precise April 1990 date when mootness attached.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Preiser requires a controversy throughout review. Will and Hafer distinguish the unavailable State or official-capacity damages claim from genuine individual-capacity liability.

##### Whether limited corrective jurisdiction permits vacatur without deciding sponsor standing or constitutional merits

**Holding:** The Court may correct a lower federal court’s jurisdictional error without exercising constitutional merits jurisdiction. On the equities here, that authority requires vacating the appellate judgment and directing vacatur of the District Court declaration and dismissal for mootness, without resolving ultimate initiative-sponsor standing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The prevailing plaintiff ended the covered employment during review efforts; this was not settlement or abandonment by a reviewing loser. The later appellate merits adjudication and absence of a lawful damages residue justify clearing both unreviewable constitutional judgments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2106 does not independently create merits jurisdiction. Intervention, an appeal notice or political commitment does not confer automatic sponsor standing. The original declaration is not declared void when entered, and the Court resolves neither every intervention ruling nor Article XXVIII’s validity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate both merits judgments and dismiss the action as moot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bender and Corrick supply limited correction of jurisdictional error. Lujan and Brown Group retain constitutional injury and lawful representation requirements. Bancorp requires equitable rather than automatic vacatur; Munsingwear and Anderson support judgment clearing within that inquiry.

##### Whether state-law certification can revive a moot federal action

**Holding:** Certification of unsettled state law can assist a live federal controversy but cannot cure the absence of Article III jurisdiction. No certification is ordered in this moot case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The meaning of Article XXVIII and any constitutional challenge in future live litigation remain open. No mandatory certification requirement is adopted.

### Jurisdiction to enforce a settlement after dismissal

**Governing question:** When a federal court may enforce a settlement after dismissing the original action.

**Current rule:** Enforcement of the settlement is a separate contract controversy requiring an independent jurisdictional basis unless the dismissal order lawfully incorporates the settlement obligation or expressly retains jurisdiction to enforce it. Mere awareness, approval or party consent does not confer jurisdiction. Under Rule 41(a)(2), the court may make compliance or retention a condition of its discretionary dismissal. For a stipulated Rule 41(a)(1)(ii) dismissal, the parties must agree to the retained enforcement authority and the court must timely embody it in its order; a private agreement alone is insufficient.

**Present operation effective:** Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, 1994-05-16.

**Open question:** Possible reopening under Rule 60(b), as distinct from exercising jurisdiction over a separate contract-enforcement action, is not decided.

### Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review

**Governing question:** Whether mootness from voluntary settlement warrants vacating a judgment lawfully brought for review.

**Current rule:** Subsequent mootness prevents a merits decision but leaves authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of the judgment and proceeding. Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not establish entitlement to vacatur: the movant must supply a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances can warrant relief, but a settlement term requiring vacatur is not itself such a circumstance. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not command vacatur whenever review becomes moot.
