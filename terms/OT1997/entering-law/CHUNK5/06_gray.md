#### White v. Illinois, 502 U.S. 346 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6113.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

##### Whether accepted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions require prior production or proof of unavailability

**Holding:** Where the granted review assumes that statements were properly admitted under the firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions, the Confrontation Clause does not require the prosecution first to produce the declarant or establish unavailability. The accepted exception foundations remain necessary; the ruling does not validate a disputed foundation or every police interview.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; nine Justices endorse this rule.

**Material application:** The limited grant accepts the state-law exception foundations for the child's statements as its premise; the Court makes no finding that disputed foundations are satisfied. Their spontaneous and treatment settings supply evidentiary value that later trial testimony cannot recreate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No blanket approval of police questioning, automatic child-necessity rule, harmless-error holding, or ruling on state-law admissibility outside the granted premise is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Illinois Appellate Court judgment is affirmed on the limited Confrontation Clause claim; no new trial or evidentiary hearing is required on the unavailability contention.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Inadi*, 475 U.S. 387 — extended from coconspirator declarations to the two accepted exceptions because later testimony does not replace their contextual value.
- *Ohio v. Roberts*, 448 U.S. 56 — preserved for its received confrontation framework; its former-testimony unavailability analysis is not universalized.
- *Idaho v. Wright*, 497 U.S. 805 — preserved for constitutional reliability outside firmly rooted exceptions; no unsupported judicial assurance substitutes for its requirements.
- *Bourjaily v. United States*, 483 U.S. 171 — firmly rooted exceptions retain their established reliability significance.
- *Coy v. Iowa*, 487 U.S. 1012, and *Maryland v. Craig*, 497 U.S. 836 — distinguished as trial-witness presentation cases; their safeguards remain intact.

##### Whether confrontation is categorically limited to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes

**Holding:** The Confrontation Clause is not categorically confined to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes, so that limiting definition does not supply an alternative basis for admitting these statements. The Court resolves the accepted exceptions under existing confrontation law and leaves any broader reconstruction of that law open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the proposed categorical narrowing. Scalia and Thomas withhold that join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not hold that every hearsay statement violates the Constitution or decide the treatment of a formal investigative accusation on a disputed record. No new categorical testimonial rule replaces the received framework.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Mattox v. United States*, 156 U.S. 237 — retained as evidence of confrontation's substantive protection and its coexistence with established exceptions.
- *California v. Green*, 399 U.S. 149, and *Dutton v. Evans*, 400 U.S. 74 — preserved; the Court's treatment of out-of-court statements is not displaced by a formal-affidavit-only definition.
- *Inadi* and *Roberts* — applied as exception-sensitive confrontation decisions, not replaced with a new categorical testimonial rule.

#### Zafiro v. United States, 506 U.S. 534 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6824.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Concrete prejudice and effective relief under Rule 14

**Holding:** For properly joined defendants, mutually antagonistic defenses alone do not require severance; the district court evaluates whether a joint trial presents a serious risk of compromising a specific trial right or preventing a reliable individualized verdict, and chooses effective relief in its sound discretion. Severance is not required for every showing of prejudice if instructions or other safeguards adequately cure the risk, but instructions are not presumed to cure every risk; Garcia, Soto and Martinez have shown no uncured risk requiring separate trials here.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; eight Justices adopt the rule and its application. Stevens’s narrower concurrence supplies no join in this general formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Richardson v. Marsh, 481 U.S. 200 — applied for joint-trial benefits and the ordinary effectiveness of instructions, not a universal cure.
- Bruton v. United States, 391 U.S. 123 — preserved: a specific confrontation risk may require exclusion, redaction or severance when instructions are inadequate.
- United States v. Lane, 474 U.S. 438 — applied only to Rule 14’s discretion over effective prejudice relief; no Rule 8 misjoinder ruling.
- Schaffer v. United States, 362 U.S. 511 — applied to adequate individualized jury instructions on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Serious risk may arise from another defendant’s evidence, markedly different culpability in complex proof or unavailable exculpatory evidence; the examples are not exhaustive. A better chance of acquittal alone is insufficient. Rule 8 joinder and independent confrontation rights remain distinct; review of effective Rule 14 relief is for abuse of discretion. Gloria’s sufficiency and collateral questions are not reached.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm on petitioner-specific grounds: Gloria’s claim is not reviewed; Garcia, Soto and Martinez receive no new trial because the individualized instructions adequately address the risks shown.

For Zafiro v. United States, January 25, 1993, No. 91-6824, concerning petitioner-specific appellate preservation, see **Federal Courts — Petitioner-specific appellate preservation**.

#### Williamson v. United States, 512 U.S. 594

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5256.

**Decided:** 1994-06-27.

##### Each material assertion must be against the declarant's interest in context

**Holding:** When an unavailable declarant's statement is offered under Rule 804(b)(3) as a statement against penal interest, each offered material assertion must, in the circumstances when made, so tend to expose that declarant to criminal liability that a reasonable person in that position would not have made it unless believing it true; a generally self-inculpatory account does not carry an independently neutral, noninculpatory or self-serving assertion into the exception. This is contextual assessment, not grammatical atomization: an apparently neutral detail or identification of another participant can qualify if its actual effect sufficiently incriminates the declarant, while neither joint inculpation nor custody alone establishes admissibility or exclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg. These seven participating Justices adopt the same rule, contextual qualification and explanation. Justices Kennedy and Thomas favor a different construction; their judgment votes add no support to this rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Salerno, decided June 19, 1992: applies its requirement that the selected hearsay exception's actual conditions be satisfied; its former-testimony and similar-motive holdings remain unchanged and do not themselves resolve Rule 804(b)(3)'s unit.
- Lee v. Illinois, 476 U.S. 530 (1986): draws on its warning about selective unreliability in an accomplice's accusation; does not turn its confrontation analysis into automatic evidentiary exclusion or decide the constitutional claim here.

##### Vacatur preserves the distinction between admissibility error and relief

**Holding:** The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for further proceedings consistent with the assertion-specific Rule construction, without a controlling determination that all of Harris's account qualifies or all of it fails; any properly available harmless-error argument must be considered under the standard applicable to the error actually found. For preserved nonconstitutional evidentiary error, Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos place the applicable harmlessness burden on the Government and require the reviewing court, considering the whole record, to have fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, with grave doubt preventing a harmlessness finding; Chapman instead requires harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt only if constitutional trial error is actually reached and found, a new trial follows if error requiring relief is established, and neither acquittal nor automatic retrial follows from rejecting whole-account admission.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven Justices adopt these shared judgment-consequence limits while retaining the application disagreements described below. All nine Justices vote for vacatur and remand, but Kennedy and Thomas concur in that judgment under their different Rule construction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946): applies its whole-record, substantial-influence inquiry to any established preserved nonconstitutional evidentiary error; no present harmlessness result is reached.
- United States v. Olano, decided April 26, 1993: distinguishes its ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error; neither forfeiture nor intentional waiver is inferred here.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): preserves the Government's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt harmlessness burden for a constitutional trial error if one is reached and found; does not apply that standard merely because the Rule was violated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No controlling application rationale under Marks classifies the whole Harris account; agreement on vacatur does not settle admissibility of particular assertions. The Confrontation Clause claim, firm-rootedness and a nationwide prosecution-side corroboration requirement remain undecided. Rule 804(b)(3)'s express corroboration sentence concerns penal-interest statements offered to exculpate the accused. The separate Harrell/Taggart circuit requirement remains applicable on its own terms, without becoming national law. Neither corroboration nor unavailability substitutes for genuine penal adversity; the abandoned coconspirator theory is not revived and no other exception is held satisfied.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded unanimously, subject to the seven-Justice Rule construction and error-consequence limits. No common classification of every assertion or present harmlessness finding is made. Properly available harmlessness arguments, independently applicable corroboration and the separately preserved constitutional question retain their own requirements. The abandoned coconspirator theory is not revived; a new trial follows only if error requiring relief is established, with no acquittal or unconditional retrial ordered.

### Confrontation and firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions

**Governing question:** Whether production of the declarant or proof of unavailability is a prerequisite to admission under these accepted hearsay exceptions.

**Current rule:** When statements satisfy the firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration or medical-treatment exception, the Confrontation Clause does not require the prosecution first to produce the declarant or establish unavailability. The exception's actual foundations remain necessary; naming an exception cannot establish a disputed foundation or validate every police interview. The Clause is not categorically confined to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes, so that proposed limitation cannot supply an alternative ground for admission. The received exception-sensitive confrontation framework remains in force. Under that framework, a certificate with accepted Rule 803(10) foundation and Rule 902(1) authentication, reporting diligent search and nonentry in regularly maintained public records existing independently of the prosecution, may be admitted without producing the custodian or establishing unavailability. That limited search-and-nonentry function differs from reconstructing possession, classifying weapons or deciding an element for the jury. Authentication alone does not establish constitutional reliability or sufficiency, and competent rebuttal remains available.

**Authority by component:**

- Exception-specific absence of a production or unavailability gate: White v. Illinois — No universal production or unavailability prerequisite, January 15, 1992; Part I of Stone's opinion joined by all nine Justices.
- Rejection of a formal-accusation-only boundary: White — The government's categorical definition of witness is rejected, January 15, 1992; Part II joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter with Stone. Scalia and Thomas do not join that component.

- Limited public-record nonentry certificates: The accepted negative-record certifications require no separate unavailability showing, 1996-02-12; O’Connor Part V, joined by all other Associates (eight). Scalia and Thomas retain their White reservation concerning possible reconsideration of formalized testimonial materials, without changing the governing test.

**Present operation effective:** Hale, 1996-02-12, applying the absence-of-unavailability rule to the bounded public-record certificate class; White’s earlier components remain.

**Exceptions and limits:** Former-testimony unavailability analysis, constitutional reliability outside firmly rooted exceptions, and safeguards for altered presentation of trial witnesses remain distinct. No later testimonial or primary-purpose test is adopted. A disputed search, altered record, missing foundation, contested wording or element-sufficiency challenge requires its own adjudication; no universal formal-affidavit approval follows.

**Holdings navigation:** White v. Illinois, 502 U.S. 346 (1992) — Confrontation and hearsay; United States v. Hale, judgment below 978 F.2d 1016.

### Tailored relief for prejudice in properly joined trials

**Governing question:** What instructions govern tailored relief for prejudice in properly joined trials?

**Current rule:** Mutually antagonistic defenses do not alone require severance under Rule 14. For properly joined defendants, the district court considers serious risk to a specific trial right or a reliable individualized verdict and selects effective relief in its sound discretion, reviewed for abuse of discretion. Instructions or safeguards may cure prejudice without severance, but are not presumed to cure every risk. Joinder, confrontation and proof beyond reasonable doubt retain their independent requirements.

**Authority by component:**

- Concrete prejudice and effective relief under Rule 14: Zafiro v. United States — Concrete prejudice and effective relief under Rule 14, 1993-01-25. O’Connor’s opinion for Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; eight Justices adopt the rule and its application. Stevens’s narrower concurrence supplies no join in this general formulation.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-01-25; Zafiro v. United States, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Zafiro v. United States, 506 U.S. 534 (1993); Zafiro v. United States, 506 U.S. 534 (1993).

### Assertion-specific declarations against penal interest

**Governing question:** Which portions of an unavailable declarant’s narrative qualify under Evidence Rule 804(b)(3).

**Current rule:** Each material assertion offered under Rule 804(b)(3) must, in the circumstances when made, so tend to expose the declarant to criminal liability that a reasonable person in that position would not have made it unless believing it true. A broader generally self-inculpatory narrative does not carry accompanying blame-shifting or neutral assertions into the exception automatically. Context may make an apparently neutral assertion genuinely self-inculpatory; sentence fragments must not be assessed in artificial isolation. The declarant’s unavailability remains a separate prerequisite. The rule’s corroboration requirement for a statement offered to exculpate an accused remains distinct from the unresolved prosecution-side corroboration question.

**Authority by component:**

- Contextual assertion-by-assertion inquiry: Williamson v. United States — Each material assertion must be against the declarant's interest in context, 1994-06-27. Part II of Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg. These seven participating Justices adopt the same rule, contextual qualification and explanation. Justices Kennedy and Thomas favor a different construction; their judgment votes add no support to this rule.

**Present operation effective:** Williamson v. United States, 1994-06-27.

**Open question:** No single controlling application to every assertion in the particular narrative was adopted. Other admissibility grounds and independent constitutional objections require their own analysis.

**Exceptions and limits:** Unavailability, corroboration and harmlessness remain distinct inquiries. Applicable circuit corroboration requirements are not displaced; they do not become a nationwide rule. Exclusion of an assertion does not itself compel retrial.
