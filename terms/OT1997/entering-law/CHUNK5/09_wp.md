#### Kansas v. Hendricks; Hendricks v. Kansas

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-1649 and 95-9075.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.
**Companion or consolidated matters:** Consolidated cross-petitions concern the same commitment and receive one affirmance on the as-applied Ex Post Facto ground.

##### The civil designation of personal commitment remains rebuttable by clearest proof of punishment

**Holding:** In assessing whether Kansas's civil commitment of Hendricks constitutes punishment for Ex Post Facto purposes, the Court first determines the Legislature's intended civil or criminal character; an intended civil scheme is treated as punishment only upon the clearest proof that its purpose or effect is punitive. The Act's civil designation is relevant but not conclusive, and neither confinement, a connection to crime, indeterminate duration, criminal-style procedural protection nor treatment difficulty alone decides that demanding inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory care, review and release obligations are relevant but do not prove actual compliance. Clearest proof does not demand successful cure or make each treatment deficiency punishment; no burden shift, mandatory alternatives inquiry or exhaustive checklist is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ursery’s civil-intent and clearest-proof method is extended from its specified forfeitures to this personal-confinement question, without transferring its forfeiture outcome or Double Jeopardy limits. Ward preserves demanding intent-and-effect analysis; Mendoza-Martinez permits cumulative assessment without a conclusive single indicator.

##### The cumulative original operation makes this retrospective commitment punishment

**Holding:** The cumulative circumstances of Hendricks's original commitment—additional confinement after his sentence, selection tied to earlier sexual offenses, prisonlike restraint, and initially inadequate treatment sustained on the prospect of a future specialized program despite acknowledged possible treatment—provide the clearest proof of punitive operation in this application. Because the Act imposes that additional punishment for conduct predating its enactment, its application to Hendricks violates the Ex Post Facto Clause, and the judgment invalidating this commitment is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Material application:** At the October 1994 hearing, specialist contracts remained unsigned and the dedicated program was not established; commitment nevertheless continued on the prospect of a future program. Procurement efforts, direct-care staff, general hospital professionals, planned evaluation and an earlier prison-treatment offer remain part of the contrary evidence. The cumulative conclusion does not rest on total absence of clinicians or treatment opportunities.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No failed-bid motive, deliberate withholding of effective therapy, complete absence of a clinical plan, rejected adequate alternative, sham release process or later treatment regimen is found. No prospective facial invalidity, automatic rule for later genuinely civil programs, or validity of another commitment route is decided. Double Jeopardy is not reached. The non-dispositive due-process discussion supplies no holding on diagnostic vocabulary, treatment-related process, broader propensity definitions, personality disorder, absolute inability to control conduct or a new instruction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Kansas judgment invalidating this commitment in both dockets, 5–4. Hendricks may not remain confined under this application for pre-enactment conduct. No new factfinding remand, release date, stay, hearing schedule, clinical protocol or alternative custody authorization is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sahhar’s rejection of a hypothetical sentence ceiling presupposes independently lawful civil custody and does not classify this Kansas confinement or import federal procedures. Foucha, Addington and Heller are unchanged; non-dispositive discussion creates no new due-process rule. Morales remains confined to its parole-opportunity subject, Lynce supplies exhaustion only, and Blockburger and retained Grady are not applied.

#### United States v. Ursery; United States v. $405,089.23 in United States Currency, 518 U.S. 267 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-345 and 95-346.
**Decided:** June 24, 1996.

##### Whether the reviewed civil forfeitures impose a second criminal punishment

**Holding:** For forfeiture under sections 881(a)(6), 881(a)(7) and 981(a)(1)(A), the court first determines whether Congress intended civil proceedings; that intent can be overcome only by the clearest proof that the scheme is so punitive in purpose or effect as to be criminal. These schemes and the grounds established here do not meet that demanding contrary-proof standard, so the challenged forfeitures supply no double-jeopardy bar to the respective later proceeding; neither a civil label nor an action against property creates an irrebuttable exemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion of the Court, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes. Scalia and Thomas join only the judgments; additional judgment votes do not enlarge the rationale.

**Material application:** Qualified incorporation of customs procedures and the civil statutory mechanisms establish civil intent. Removing facilitating property, discouraging unlawful use and recovering illicit proceeds supply nonpunitive functions beyond reimbursing enforcement costs. Loss, deterrence, a crime connection and an owner defense alone do not satisfy clearest proof.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither each asset’s nexus, provenance or ownership nor excessiveness, attachment of personal jeopardy, offense identity under Blockburger and retained Grady, coordination of proceedings or settlement waiver. Distinct statutory defenses remain: §§881(a)(6)/(7) protect an owner’s interest upon proof that the relevant act or omission occurred without knowledge or consent; §981(a)(2) protects an owner or lienholder upon proof of lack of knowledge. Section 981(a)(1)(A)’s §5313(a) exception bars seizure/forfeiture for that reporting violation by a federally supervised domestic financial institution, an SEC-regulated institution, or their partner, director or employee. Customs procedures apply only insofar as applicable and consistent with the forfeiture statute.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Ursery reversed and remanded, 7–2 (Stone-Zsela and Stevens would affirm); Currency reversed and remanded, 8–1 (Stone-Zsela favors vacatur and a narrower remand). All nine reject Currency’s blanket bar, but no unanimous rationale or mandate follows. Properly remaining criminal, probable-cause, excessive-fines and property-authority objections remain; no automatic transfer or new sentence.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Kansas v. Hendricks; Hendricks v. Kansas, June 23, 1997, Nos. 95-1649 and 95-9075: extends the civil-intent/clearest-proof method to Ex Post Facto classification of personal confinement; no forfeiture outcome or Double Jeopardy holding is transferred.

##### Whether Excessive Fines coverage itself establishes Double Jeopardy punishment

**Holding:** A forfeiture's coverage by the Excessive Fines Clause does not, without the separate civil-intent and clearest-proof inquiry, establish criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy purposes. Austin's protection and any properly presented excessiveness objection remain effective; today's classification decides neither excessiveness nor an exclusive test for measuring it.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice Court opinion.

**Material application:** Austin’s partly punitive coverage protects against excessive exactions; it does not replace the separate criminal-classification inquiry. Halper’s fixed personal civil-penalty accounting and Kurth Ranch’s combined-operation tax holding retain their distinct fields.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No instrumentality-only fines rule or finding that these forfeitures are nonexcessive. James Daniel Good’s prior-process protection and demonstrated-exigency exception, Bennis’s innocent-owner protection, and Libretti’s statutory and third-party limits remain independently operative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **United States v. Ward, 448 U.S. 242:** Applies legislative-intent and demanding contrary-proof analysis; civil designation is relevant but not conclusive.
- **United States v. One Assortment of 89 Firearms, 465 U.S. 354:** Applies the civil-forfeiture classification test, including the clearest-proof exception, rather than categorical immunity for proceedings against property.
- **Kennedy v. Mendoza-Martinez, 372 U.S. 144:** Preserves consideration of punitive purpose and effect; no individual indicator is conclusive and no new mandatory exhaustive checklist is announced.
- **United States v. Halper, 490 U.S. 435:** Preserves its exceptional fixed-personal-civil-penalty accounting rule, including reasonable remedial approximation, while distinguishing forfeiture's broader nonpunitive functions. It is not overruled or converted into universal asset-value accounting.
- **Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch, June 6, 1994:** Preserves the combined-operation punishment holding for the extraordinary arrest-linked assessment on confiscated contraband; no universal compensation ceiling follows.
- **Austin v. United States, June 28, 1993:** Preserves Excessive Fines coverage and separate excessiveness review. Its partly punitive coverage rule does not automatically answer the Double Jeopardy question.
- **Witte v. United States, June 14, 1995:** Preserves the distinction between consideration of conduct and an independent punishment, and its separate coordination limits; coordination cannot cure an otherwise established successive-prosecution bar.
- **Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S. 299; Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508; United States v. Felix, March 25, 1992; and United States v. Dixon, June 28, 1993:** Preserve necessary-offense and essential-conduct rules and the distinct-agreement limitation. The Court does not reach their application here and does not replace retained Grady with an elements-only rule.
- **United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, December 13, 1993:** Preserves notice and meaningful opportunity before civil seizure of real property; exigency requires showing that less restrictive measures would not prevent sale, destruction or continued unlawful use, with appropriate subsequent process.
- **Bennis v. Michigan, March 4, 1996:** Preserves its stated innocent-owner protection; no qualifying innocent-owner finding is established here.
- **Libretti v. United States, November 7, 1995:** Preserves statutory authority and third-party limits despite agreement; it supplies no settlement waiver in these cases.

No precedent is overruled. The separate writings' broader or contrary theories are not the Court's treatment of precedent.

#### Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch, 511 U.S. 767

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-144.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### The surviving drug assessment is a second punishment

**Holding:** The Double Jeopardy Clause forbids Montana from collecting this surviving drug assessment in a separate proceeding after criminal punishment for the same offense, on the preserved same-offense premise of this litigation. The combined operation of criminally conditioned drug liability, arrest-linked reporting, collection subordinate to fines and forfeitures, an extraordinary charge and taxation of confiscated contraband makes this exaction punishment; no single feature, fixed rate ratio or general tax-cost ceiling establishes the rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the combined-operation rule and affirm disallowance as applied.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision concerns the surviving harvested-marijuana claim, approximately $181,000 in principal. Independent state-law rejection of plants, oil and tar remains undisturbed. Only Richard pleaded to possession; the other five pleaded to conspiracy. The Court declines Montana's unpreserved contrary contention rather than deciding their plea terms or equating the offenses. Calling that contention standing does not establish a jurisdictional defect in this bankruptcy controversy. No general rule resolves the reverse sequence, separate sovereigns, every civil forfeiture, a confidential tax collectible independently of arrest, or all taxation of unlawful conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Benton v. Maryland, 395 U.S. 784: applies the Double Jeopardy Clause to the State through the Fourteenth Amendment.
- North Carolina v. Pearce, 395 U.S. 711: applies the protection against repeated punishment without deciding sanctions within one proceeding.
- United States v. Halper, 490 U.S. 435: extends substance-over-label examination to this unusual tax; its civil-penalty accounting method is not a universal ceiling for taxation.
- A. Magnano Co. v. Hamilton, 292 U.S. 40: applies its recognition that penal operation can deprive a nominal tax of its ordinary revenue character.
- United States v. Sanchez, 340 U.S. 42: distinguishes this combined criminal-process-dependent levy; ordinary high or deterrent taxes remain valid within that decision.
- Helvering v. Mitchell, 303 U.S. 391: preserves genuine remedial tax additions and distinguishes this subsequent punitive exaction.
- United States v. Ward, 448 U.S. 242: civil labeling does not answer whether the particular second exaction constitutes punishment under the retained Halper rule; the general civil/criminal framework is not overruled.
- United States v. Felix, March 25, 1992: preserves the distinction between conspiracy and its substantive objects; no offense identity is inferred from evidentiary overlap.
- United States v. Dixon, June 28, 1993: retains its successive-prosecution holdings and Grady; those rules do not themselves decide the unpreserved plea-specific issue.
- Austin v. United States, June 28, 1993: preserves its Excessive Fines coverage rules without importing a partly-punitive fines test as this Double Jeopardy rule.
- Alexander v. United States, June 28, 1993: preserves its separate Excessive Fines and expressive-burden inquiries; neither supplies the governing test here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The disallowance of the surviving harvested-marijuana assessment is affirmed, including the lower judgment's remittance consequence for sums already recovered. No new exact principal, penalty, interest or refund total is fixed. Independent state-law rejection of the other drug components and the criminal judgments remain undisturbed.

#### United States v. Sahhar

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit Nos. 94-50186 and 94-50356.

**Decided:** December 16, 1996.

##### A hypothetical criminal maximum does not end otherwise lawful civil custody

**Holding:** A separately lawful §4246 commitment does not end solely because the incompetency-related prosecution has been dismissed or custody has exceeded the maximum sentence that a conviction could have carried. Continued psychiatric custody must remain supported by current mental disease or defect causing the statutory danger, an applicable civil-commitment basis and effective review, release and state-placement safeguards; a former charge, incompetence alone, danger without qualifying illness or a stale finding cannot substitute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Jones's acquittal-based initial inference does not apply to a person never adjudicated guilty. The old clinical findings are not a current finding of illness and danger. The unappealed indictment dismissal stands, and no separate enumerated-power challenge is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Jones's duration principle is applied without its acquittal-based initial presumption. Jackson, Foucha, Addington and Baxstrom preserve independent current grounds and adequate procedures; none makes criminal punishment the measure of civil treatment.

##### The sentence-based classification objection does not require release

**Holding:** Equal protection does not require release merely because §4246 custody outlasts the maximum possible sentence for the former federal charge. The lawful federal-custody origin and limited transition toward appropriate state responsibility relate to this civil class, while sentence severity and the duration of present illness-caused danger address different questions; this holding selects no universal scrutiny tier and does not merge equal protection with due process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal scrutiny tier or merger of due process and equality results. Passing the sentence-based classification inquiry does not prove that current confinement is lawful.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heller's actual-disadvantage inquiry is applied without restoring its rejected proof disparity or selecting a new tier. Foucha's noncontrolling equality discussion remains noncontrolling.

##### State placement requires actual and periodically renewed efforts

**Holding:** Under §4246(d), the Attorney General must make all reasonable efforts, continued periodically, to secure the domicile or trial State's assumption of custody, care and treatment, and must release the person to the appropriate state official when an eligible State assumes that responsibility. If neither State assumes responsibility despite those efforts, suitable federal hospitalization may continue only until state assumption or a condition permitting safe unconditional or prescribed-regimen release, whichever occurs first.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Earlier failed conditional placement establishes neither present acceptance nor refusal. Missing current psychiatric predicates cannot be cured by absence of a state placement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Foucha and Jackson support continuing lawful justification; the concrete transfer and periodic-efforts commands come from §4246(d), not a new judicial review schedule.

##### The statutory commitment categories and the unrelated-dismissal route remain distinct

**Holding:** Section 4246(a) applies to a person whose sentence is about to expire, one committed under §4241(d), or one whose charges were dismissed solely for reasons related to mental condition, upon the required director's certification of present illness-caused statutory danger and unavailable suitable state arrangements. A dismissal for reasons not related to mental condition instead triggers §4246(g)'s state referral and, if neither eligible State assumes responsibility, release upon refusal notice and no later than ten days after the director's certification; it cannot be converted into subsection (d)'s continuing-hospitalization branch.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory danger means substantial risk of bodily injury to another or serious damage to another's property, not a free-standing danger-to-self test. Filing the required certification stays release pending the statutory hearing; subsection (d) requires clear and convincing proof of illness-caused danger. No unrelated-dismissal ground is found here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Greenwood's connection to the federal criminal process is not enlarged into a general police power. Addington's ordinary commitment protection is consistent with subsection (d)'s initial clear-and-convincing requirement; no universal discharge burden is inferred.

##### Discharge, conditional release and review retain their separate requirements

**Holding:** Section 4246(e)'s sufficient-recovery determination after the prescribed hearing uses a preponderance of the evidence, distinct from subsection (d)'s commitment burden; safe unconditional release requires immediate discharge, while safe conditional release requires an individually prepared treatment regimen certified appropriate by the director, found appropriate by the court and imposed with an express compliance condition. Counsel, adversarial participation, annual reports, habeas and the independent discharge-motion route remain operative; neither annual reporting nor the 180-day restriction on repeated discharge motions creates an automatic judicial hearing schedule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part V, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No unpresented constitutional challenge to the discharge provision is resolved. Director-certified sufficient recovery requires prompt certification; the court may order discharge or hold the authorized hearing on the Government's or its own motion. A hearing on the same criteria may modify or eliminate the prescribed regimen.

Section 4246(f) permits action on notice of conditional-release noncompliance or other probable cause, including possible arrest and prompt presentation, followed by a hearing on whether noncompliance makes continued release create the statutory danger. It is no general authority to confine everyone once federally charged.

Section 4247(d) supplies counsel, appointed when financially necessary, and opportunities to testify, present evidence, subpoena witnesses, confront and cross-examine. Section 4247(e) requires annual reports with continued-hospitalization recommendations. Section 4247(g) preserves habeas. Section 4247(h) permits counsel or a guardian to seek discharge without the director's certificate, but bars another such motion within 180 days of a determination that hospitalization should continue. A restriction on repeat motions is not a mandatory hearing every 180 days. These actual safeguards, alongside the continuing predicates and placement duty, require lawful current application on remand; they establish no present medical or release finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Addington and Foucha preserve appropriate commitment grounds and proof without replacing §4246(e)'s separate discharge text. Jackson's relationship between custody and lawful purpose remains enforceable through the actual statutory channels.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit unanimously, with a contemporaneous inquiry into continuing lawful predicates, periodically renewed reasonable state-placement efforts and Arizona's actual response. The indictment dismissal stands; no final clinical finding, final release, transfer or permanent confinement is ordered.

#### Sandin v. Conner

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1911.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty

**Holding:** A State creates protected prison liberty when substantive law meaningfully restricts officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation. Mandatory wording alone is insufficient: courts must examine the genuine constraint in the whole disciplinary scheme and the meaningful restraint, without making state codification necessary for directly protected constitutional liberty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is sufficient and nonexclusive. Mandatory procedures, routine transfers and brief inconveniences do not automatically create entitlements; direct constitutional, unexpected-restraint and sentence-duration interests remain. No atypical-and-significant-hardship test controls.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hewitt v. Helms: narrows mechanical reliance on mandatory wording while retaining genuine substantive-predicate liberty protection; no wholesale methodology replacement controls.
- Kentucky Department of Corrections v. Thompson: applies distinction between substantive constraints and procedural directions alone.
- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves significant prison liberty and context-sensitive procedural protection.
- Meachum v. Fano: distinguishes ordinary sentence-authorized management from a genuine protected constraint.
- Vitek v. Jones; Washington v. Harper: preserve distinct directly protected, qualitatively different restraints; this state-entitlement rule is nonexclusive.
- Farmer v. Brennan; Helling v. McKinney: distinguish Eighth Amendment conditions liability from this procedural-liberty inquiry.

##### The reviewed summary judgment and remand

**Holding:** The summary judgment rejecting the reviewed liberty-and-process claim cannot stand on a mechanical reading of Hawaii’s regulatory words or dismissal of genuine punitive constraints. The district court must determine whether the rules genuinely constrain authority, whether the deprivation is meaningful rather than routine, and what process is due; the Court makes no liability or ultimate protected-interest finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Opinion of the Court and remedial disposition; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg join the bounded rule-and-remand ground. Four concurring Justices separately would find the interest already established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantial-evidence language is not rewritten as an express acquittal command; the whole misconduct, impartial-adjudication, evidence and sanction scheme matters. Punitive labels alone do not establish liberty; similarity to separately authorized administrative or protective custody alone does not defeat a punitive constraint. Later expungement supplies no retroactive process and discretionary parole consequences are not inevitable sentence extension. Wolff permits refusal of witnesses for irrelevance, lack of necessity or safety or correctional hazards. Neither the four concurring votes finding an established interest nor the dissent's four-vote replacement framework controls under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wolff: applies witness qualifications and the separate process inquiry once protected liberty is implicated.
- Hewitt; Thompson: require whole-scheme substantive analysis rather than a one-way mandatory-word inference.
- Meachum: preserves ordinary authorized custody without making every punitive restraint legally unconstrained.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit in the reviewed respect, leaving reversal of officials' summary judgment effective, and remand for genuine constraint, meaningful deprivation and due process determinations. The Court finds neither ultimate protected liberty nor liability; unrelated amended-complaint claims and defenses are not resolved.

### Civil forfeiture and criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy

**Governing question:** Classification of forfeiture under §§881(a)(6), 881(a)(7) and 981(a)(1)(A) for successive-punishment purposes.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress intended civil proceedings. Civil intent can be overcome only by the clearest proof that the scheme is so punitive in purpose or effect as to be criminal. Neither a civil label nor proceeding against property supplies an irrebuttable exemption. Deterrence, burden on an owner, connection to crime and owner defenses alone do not establish the demanding contrary showing. Forfeiture’s nonpunitive functions include removing facilitating property, discouraging unlawful use and recovering actual illicit proceeds; it is not universally limited to reimbursing Government costs under Halper’s method for fixed personal civil penalties. Excessive Fines coverage does not itself establish Double Jeopardy criminal punishment; Austin’s distinct protection remains.

**Authority by component:**

- Civil-intent/clearest-proof test and separate clause classification: I. These civil forfeiture proceedings do not constitute a second criminal punishment; II. Excessive Fines coverage does not itself establish Double Jeopardy punishment, 1996-06-24; O’Connor with Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Ursery and United States Currency, 1996-06-24.

**Exceptions and limits:** Halper’s own setting and Kurth Ranch’s extraordinary-tax rule survive. Statutory nexus, provenance, ownership, owner defenses and excessiveness remain independent. Whether punitive civil proceedings place a person in jeopardy, offense identity under Blockburger/retained Grady and coordinated-proceeding treatment remain unadjudicated.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Ursery; United States v. $405,089.23 in United States Currency, 518 U.S. 267 (1996).

### State-created liberty in prison discipline

**Governing question:** When substantive prison law creates a protected liberty interest.

**Current rule:** A State creates protected prison liberty when substantive law meaningfully restricts officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation. Mandatory words alone are insufficient: courts examine the genuine constraint in the whole disciplinary scheme and whether the restraint is meaningful rather than routine. This is a sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule; state codification is unnecessary for directly protected constitutional liberty.

**Authority by component:**

- Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty: Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty, 1995-06-19. Breyer’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-19; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Other direct constitutional, unexpected-restraint and sentence-duration protections are not displaced. A protected interest and required process need their own determinations. No new multifactor test, categorical rejection of punitive constraints or automatic liability follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty.
