#### Field v. Mans, 516 U.S. 59

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-967.
**Decided:** November 28, 1995.

##### Actual and justifiable reliance for the presented §523(a)(2)(A) actual-fraud claim

**Holding:** The creditor must prove actual and justifiable reliance. Justifiability depends on the recipient's knowledge, capacities and circumstances: no general investigation duty applies, but known or personally obvious falsity cannot be relied upon and an actually encountered warning may require inquiry under that recipient-specific standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous; Scalia and Breyer join the rule but dissent from the application and judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Objective unreasonableness may inform actual-reliance credibility but is not an independent universal prudence requirement. Subsection (A) excludes statements about the debtor's or insider's financial condition; (B) separately requires a materially false written statement of that kind, reasonable reliance and debtor-caused making or publication with intent to deceive. Fraud, reliance, justifiability, debt obtained by the conduct and the extent of that debt remain separate. Only actual fraud is decided, not a distinct false-pretense or representation-only theory or the nondischargeable amount.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Darden's established-common-law-term method applies by analogy without its employment factors. Grogan preserves proof by a preponderance. Restatement §§537 and 540–541 distinguish justifiability from universal prudent-person investigation, using general common law rather than any State's tort rules.

**Later-authority backlinks:** In re Cohen, June 30, 1997, Third Circuit No. 96-5155: resolves the reserved extent of liability for a qualifying fraudulent acquisition, including enforceable enhancement and attributable fees and costs; preserves actual and individualized justifiable reliance.

##### Correct-standard review before a final nondischargeability determination

**Holding:** An adverse actual-fraud discharge ruling resting on the broader objective prudent-person investigation duty does not stand merely because its findings include individualized facts relevant to justifiability. The proper standard must be applied to existing findings, supplemented if necessary, with separate determination of properly remaining obtained-by, extent and other statutory issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven direct votes. Scalia and Breyer would affirm on the existing findings.

**Material application:** The bankruptcy court expressly required prudent-person investigation, including possible legal consultation despite accepting lack of sophistication. Findings of actual reliance, detriment and effective extension of credit survive, but do not establish that every dollar of the original note was obtained by later concealment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, 7–2, through the First Circuit for correct-standard application and needed findings. No immediate nondischargeability judgment on the whole debt is ordered; ordinary preservation rules remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The common-law standard and unchanged Grogan burden govern further adjudication without supplying automatic nondischargeability.

#### Ratzlaf v. United States, 510 U.S. 135

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1196.

**Decided:** 1994-01-11.

##### Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful

**Holding:** For the charged offense under 31 U.S.C. §§5322(a) and 5324(3), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowing the financial institution's reporting duty and acting for the purpose of evading it. Knowledge may be established through reasonable circumstantial inferences; the prosecution need not prove knowledge of a section number or that the defendant read the Code.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of nine participants adopt the rule and instructional-error disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Spies v. United States, 317 U.S. 492 (1943): applied for contextual interpretation of willfulness and permissible circumstantial proof.
- Cheek v. United States, 498 U.S. 192 (1991): its recognition of a statutory known-duty requirement informs this distinct scheme; its tax rule is not made universal.
- Liparota v. United States, 471 U.S. 419 (1985): applied for context-sensitive knowledge requirements where apparently lawful conduct may otherwise be swept into a crime.
- Potter v. United States, 155 U.S. 438 (1894): the criminal statute's separate willfulness term receives operative effect rather than disappearing into other elements.

**Independent alternative holding:** If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity about whether §5322(a) requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful, the rule of lenity independently requires that narrower construction of this charged criminal offense. Legislative purpose and conflicting legislative history do not justify dispensing with the additional knowledge requirement when the enacted criminal language does not clearly do so.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; the same six Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hughey v. United States, 495 U.S. 411 (1990): applied to resolve residual criminal statutory ambiguity in favor of the accused.
- Crandon v. United States, 494 U.S. 152 (1990): applied to prevent policy or legislative materials from enlarging a crime beyond sufficiently clear text.
- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336 (1971): applied for fair warning and Congress's responsibility to define criminal liability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The bank's reporting duty and the customer's structuring prohibition are distinct. Section 5322(a)'s exception for §5315 and its regulations remains an exception to the penalty provision, not to §5324(3). No universal ignorance-of-law defense, requirement that proceeds be lawful or civil-forfeiture rule is announced.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed Ninth Circuit judgment for both Waldemar and Loretta Ratzlaf is reversed and remanded. Waldemar's conspiracy, four structuring and interstate-travel counts and Loretta's conspiracy and interstate-travel counts require count-specific implementation of the erroneous instruction; no count is declared automatically invalid. Hunt's convictions are outside review. No acquittal or unconditional discharge is ordered.

#### Staples v. United States, 511 U.S. 600

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1441.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun

**Holding:** To convict a defendant under 26 U.S.C. §5861(d) for possessing an unregistered machinegun in the circumstances presented here, the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the characteristics bringing the rifle within the complete-weapon machinegun definition—a weapon that shoots, is designed to shoot, or can be readily restored to shoot automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, by a single function of the trigger; circumstantial evidence may establish that knowledge. It need not prove knowledge of nonregistration, the registration requirement, or illegality, and the omitted-element instruction requires reversal and remand without a finding of evidentiary insufficiency or an order of acquittal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, supplies five direct joins to this rule and its explanation. Ginsburg, joined by O'Connor, independently concurs in the judgment on the same narrow factual-knowledge requirement; the Court's authority does not depend on combining different rationales under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morissette v. United States, 342 U.S. 246 (1952): applies the presumption of criminal fault despite statutory silence.
- United States v. United States Gypsum Co., 438 U.S. 422 (1978): applies contextual examination before dispensing with fault.
- United States v. Freed, 401 U.S. 601 (1971): distinguishes knowledge of the object's regulated characteristics from knowledge of nonregistration; its registration holding remains intact.
- United States v. Balint, 258 U.S. 250 (1922): preserves the bounded public-welfare category without extending its narcotics construction automatically to this rifle.
- Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994: distinguishes its express-willfulness and knowledge-of-illegality rule; no universal legal-knowledge requirement follows.
- United States v. Thompson/Center Arms Co., June 8, 1992: leaves its making rule and limited parts-package disposition unchanged; neither decides this possession element.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The separate frame/receiver, conversion-part, conversion-parts and one-person-controlled assembly-parts branches of §5845(b) remain undecided. Section 5845(a)'s antique exclusion and its separate exclusion for Secretary-designated collector's items unlikely to be used as weapons remain; the collector exclusion does not encompass machineguns or destructive devices and depends on manufacture date, value, design and other characteristics. No exclusion is established for this rifle. No Second Amendment, facial validity, ultimate knowledge or error-review result is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit is reversed and remanded, 7–2, for the required factual-knowledge instruction. No acquittal, mandatory retrial or new sentence is ordered; properly available sufficiency and error-review questions retain their own requirements.

#### United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-723.
**Decided:** November 29, 1994.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Defending an existing favorable judgment (The two renewed alternatives may defend the existing favorable judgment); First Amendment — Facial challenges to child-pornography definitions (The asserted age-eighteen line does not establish substantial facial overbreadth; The complete lascivious-exhibition category defeats the word-substitution facial attack).

##### The enacted knowledge term reaches minority and sexually explicit character

**Holding:** To convict under 18 U.S.C. §§2252(a)(1) or (2), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the depiction’s sexually explicit character and knew the performer was a minor, as well as the applicable knowing handling act. It need not prove knowledge of the statute, illegality or the precise legal age definition; the actual statutory production, depiction and commerce-or-mail conditions remain distinct, and the Court does not decide whether knowledge of the jurisdictional nexus is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Morissette v. United States*, 342 U.S. 246 (1952), applies the presumption of culpability to the statutory setting rather than treating grammatical distance as conclusive.
- *Staples v. United States*, 511 U.S. 600 (1994), applies its distinction between knowledge of facts making an object regulated and knowledge of legal duties; its weapon-specific holding does not automatically construe §2252.
- *Posters ’N’ Things, Ltd. v. United States*, 511 U.S. 513 (1994), preserves actual factual knowledge as distinct from legal classification and from a desire to cause the forbidden use; its different coverage dispute remains unchanged.
- *Ratzlaf v. United States*, 510 U.S. 135 (1994), is distinguished as a text-specific willfulness requirement concerning unlawful structuring; it creates no general requirement to know that conduct is illegal.
- *Smith v. California*, 361 U.S. 147 (1959), and *New York v. Ferber*, 458 U.S. 747 (1982), inform the constitutional concern about penalizing dissemination without adequate awareness of incriminating content; the Court does not decide an unavoidable constitutional requirement of actual knowledge of minority in every enactment.
- *Osborne v. Ohio*, 495 U.S. 103 (1990), remains distinct: its Ohio default-culpability statute supplied recklessness and supplies no federal default here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a statutory construction of §§2252(a)(1)–(2), not a universal constitutional actual-knowledge minimum, a construction of every other offense or a decision concerning §2251 producers. No new digital-image category, knowledge-of-illegality element, knowledge-of-nexus rule or automatic trial-error remedy is supplied.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded, 7–2. The facial-scienter invalidation and the two renewed facial-definition defenses do not sustain the appellate judgment. The existing minority-knowledge finding is recognized; the indictment-scienter, actual-tape-content and implementation issues remain. No count is unconditionally reinstated or invalidated, and no acquittal, automatic retrial or harmlessness finding is ordered.

### Actual and justifiable reliance for the actual-fraud discharge exception

**Governing question:** Reliance and the extent of nondischargeable liability for an actual-fraud acquisition under §523(a)(2)(A).

**Current rule:** The creditor must prove actual and justifiable reliance by the ordinary preponderance standard. Justifiability depends on that recipient’s knowledge, capacities and circumstances. No universal investigation duty applies, but known or personally obvious falsity defeats justifiability and an actually discovered warning may require inquiry under that individualized standard. Objective imprudence can bear on whether claimed actual reliance occurred, but is not a separate universal prudence condition. Fraud, reliance, justifiability, the obtained-by nexus and the extent of nondischargeable debt remain distinct.

Once money or property is acquired through qualifying §523(a)(2)(A) fraud, the nondischargeable debt includes legally enforceable liability arising from that acquisition, not merely the value received. Connected punitive or treble liability remains included even if wholly punitive, as do valid attributable attorney's fees and costs. State law supplies the underlying liability; federal law supplies discharge treatment. A qualifying federal fraud acquisition and the legal connection remain indispensable: a consumer-fraud label or treble award alone supplies no fraud predicate, and lawful receipts, unrelated obligations and unsupported fee claims remain outside the rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Field — Part I, actual and justifiable reliance, 1995-11-28; Souter for all nine, including the two Justices dissenting only from the application and remand.
- Qualifying federal predicate and debt extent: In re Cohen, In re Cohen — I. Federal fraud remains a necessary predicate, In re Cohen — II. The exception covers valid liability for the fraudulent acquisition, In re Cohen — III. The existing treble judgment is nondischargeable even assuming a wholly punitive enhancement and In re Cohen — IV. Existing attributable fees and costs share the debt's treatment; no new award is made, Thomas for all nine, 1997-06-30.

**Present operation effective:** In re Cohen, 1997-06-30, resolves debt extent; Field's reliance standard remains effective from 1995-11-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Subsection (A) excludes statements respecting the debtor’s or an insider’s financial condition. Subsection (B) separately requires a materially false written statement of that kind, reasonable creditor reliance and the debtor’s causing publication with intent to deceive. This holding does not separately classify a claim of false pretenses or representation without actual fraud.

**Holdings navigation:** Field v. Mans, 516 U.S. 59. In re Cohen (1997-06-30) — Bankruptcy / Fraud discharge exceptions.

### Knowing unlawfulness in willful currency structuring

**Governing question:** What §5322(a)’s willfulness requires for the charged §5324(3) structuring offense.

**Current rule:** The Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowledge of the financial institution’s reporting duty and a purpose to evade it. Reasonable circumstantial inferences may establish knowledge; knowledge of a section number or having read the Code is unnecessary. If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity on that additional knowledge requirement, lenity independently requires this narrower criminal construction; conflicting history or general purpose cannot enlarge the crime beyond sufficiently clear enacted language.

**Authority by component:**

- Knowledge elements and proof: Ratzlaf v. United States — Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful, 1994-01-11. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of nine participants adopt the rule and instructional-error disposition.
- Independent residual-lenity ground: Ratzlaf v. United States — Residual ambiguity independently requires the narrower criminal construction, 1994-01-11. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; the same six Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding.

**Present operation effective:** Ratzlaf v. United States, 1994-01-11.

**Exceptions and limits:** The §5322(a) exception for §5315 and its regulations is separate from §5324(3). This is no universal ignorance-of-law defense, legal-proceeds requirement or civil-forfeiture rule.

### Knowledge of machinegun characteristics in unregistered possession

**Governing question:** What must be proved when a conventional-looking rifle falls within the whole-weapon machinegun definition.

**Current rule:** For §5861(d) possession of an unregistered rifle prosecuted as a whole-weapon machinegun under §5845(b), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the characteristics making it a machinegun: it shoots, is designed to shoot, or can readily be restored to shoot automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, by a single function of the trigger. Knowledge that the weapon is unregistered or that possession is illegal is not required. Ordinary possession of a rifle is not enough to presume knowledge of its automatic-fire characteristics.

**Authority by component:**

- Knowledge of the regulated weapon characteristics: Staples v. United States — Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun, 1994-05-23. Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, supplies five direct joins to this rule and its explanation. Ginsburg, joined by O'Connor, independently concurs in the judgment on the same narrow factual-knowledge requirement; the Court's authority does not depend on combining different rationales under Marks.

**Present operation effective:** Staples v. United States, 1994-05-23.

**Open question:** The holding does not decide every separately defined machinegun part or combination, every other regulated weapon, or the sufficiency of proof in every conversion setting.

### Factual knowledge and depiction boundaries under §§2252 and 2256

**Governing question:** The knowledge and definition requirements governing the reviewed child-pornography handling offenses.

**Current rule:** Under 18 U.S.C. §§2252(a)(1)–(2), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt the applicable knowing handling act, knowledge of the depiction’s sexually explicit character and knowledge that the performer was a minor. Knowledge of the statute, illegality or the precise legal age definition is unnecessary. The under-eighteen definition does not become substantially overbroad merely from difficulty estimating ages near eighteen or different adult-treatment rules for older adolescents. The complete category of lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area retains a sexual-conduct limit: mere bodily visibility or nudity is insufficient, and substituting “lascivious” for “lewd” does not itself create facial vagueness or substantial overbreadth.
