## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. As of this date the Court has not adopted a general rule governing remote historical movement in every statute.

**Authority by component:**

- Architecture; connection before magnitude; recognition of necessary inclusion; no-insertion rule: United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.
- Economic vehicle-taking class and enacted vehicle nexus: United States v. Harris, April 27, 1995; O’Connor’s eight-Justice commerce rationale. Stone concurs in the judgment on the narrower direct-instrumentality/vehicle-specific nexus ground.
- Direct interference with an interstate service market: American Life League v. Reno, November 13, 1995; Kennedy Part I, unanimous.
- Application of Harris without new doctrine: United States v. Bishop; United States v. Stokes, February 5, 1996; O’Connor Part II, unanimous.
- Anti-evasion/material-impairment rule for the post-1986 machinegun regime: United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996; O’Connor Part I, unanimous.
- Interstate commercial transmission as commerce itself: Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno, April 28, 1997; unanimous commerce holding.
- Legally fixed cross-border child-support obligation: United States v. Mussari; United States v. Schroeder, June 30, 1997; O’Connor, unanimous.

**Present operation effective:** Mussari/Schroeder, June 30, 1997, for the latest application; the controlling architecture dates to Lopez, April 26, 1995, and the anti-evasion/material-impairment branch dates to Hale, February 12, 1996.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:**

1. Define the enacted regulated class.
2. Identify the asserted interstate-commerce connection.
3. Identify the mechanism connecting the class to interstate commerce.
4. Determine whether the mechanism is constitutionally eligible.
5. Only then use aggregation or substantiality to evaluate class-wide magnitude.
6. Give Congress substantial latitude on prediction and regulatory design within the valid power.
7. Test whether the statutory class reasonably fits the asserted mechanism.
8. Confirm that the theory leaves an enforceable constitutional boundary.

**Exceptions and limits:** Noneconomic intrastate conduct is not categorically immune. Traditional State subject matter is not categorically immune. Formal legislative findings are neither universally required nor sufficient by themselves. Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope. Garcia’s rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains intact. Remote movement and generalized social costs independently establish none of the current Commerce grounds. The Court has not yet adopted a general material-interstate-externality doctrine. Mussari/Schroeder directly regulates the private obligor: it commands no State administration and gives no federal power to issue or modify support, custody, parentage or domestic-status orders. The cross-border payment rule adds no flight, travel, voluntary-crossing, bank-wire or evasion-motive element. Not every protester is an interstate enterprise; the service-market ground reaches conduct directed at provision or receipt of the covered services. Hale preserves § 922(o)'s government-related channels and prior-lawful pre-ban stock. No Second Amendment, amended-statute or independent indictment rule is established by these commerce holdings.

**Material development:**

- April 26, 1995 — Lopez: ADDED / CHANGED. Added the controlling decision sequence of regulated class → interstate mechanism → magnitude/aggregation → fit/boundary. Changed the prior substantial-effects law only to the extent necessary to reject aggregation of generalized downstream consequences as a substitute for the missing Commerce connection. Preserved Wickard and Perez within their genuine economic-class settings. Reserved fuller Necessary-and-Proper inclusion questions.
- April 27, 1995 — Harris: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Clarified that Lopez does not disable direct regulation of an economic vehicle-taking class connected to an interstate motor-vehicle regime when the statute separately requires a particular-vehicle interstate nexus. Stone’s narrower concurrence emphasizes direct protection of the transportation instrumentality. No general remote-movement rule is added.
- November 13, 1995 — American Life League: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Clarified that intentional obstruction directed at provision or receipt of services can be regulated when it directly interferes with an actual interstate service market. This is not downstream crime-cost aggregation.
- February 5, 1996 — Bishop/Stokes: APPLIED ONLY. Applied Harris. No new Commerce rule.
- February 12, 1996 — Hale: ADDED. Added the first operational anti-evasion/material-impairment doctrine. Intrastate possession may be included where excluding it would materially undermine or permit evasion of a valid federal interstate market regime. This development uses the Necessary and Proper Clause together with Commerce authority. Hale does not create a general firearm possession power.
- April 28, 1997 — Pic-A-State: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Clarified that a knowing interstate computer transmission operating as part of a commercial procurement transaction is interstate commerce itself, making aggregate-effects analysis unnecessary.
- June 30, 1997 — Mussari/Schroeder: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Clarified that Congress may protect a legally fixed payment obligation whose statutory relationship is itself cross-border. The different-State relation performs constitutional work; family-policy importance and social costs do not.

Hale, judgment below 978 F.2d 1016; Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno; United States v. Mussari; United States v. Schroeder.

## Standards and Tests selection: Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition

### Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition

**Governing question:** The permissible scope and causal limits of the regulatory habitat clause.

**Current rule:** The ESA permits “harm” to include significant habitat modification or degradation that actually kills or injures protected wildlife by significantly impairing essential behavioral patterns, including breeding, feeding or sheltering. Actual death or injury must be proximately caused with ordinary foreseeability; bare but-for linkage or speculative future harm is insufficient. An omission may count only where an independently existing legal duty supplies the obligation. This decision creates no general duty to maintain habitat. Independently, where the statutory definition leaves room on indirect injury, the Secretary’s actual-injury construction is reasonable and receives Chevron deference without becoming the only permissible construction.

**Authority by component:**

- 1. Statutory authority for habitat-caused injury: 1. Statutory authority for habitat-caused injury, 1995-06-29. Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer join this statutory proposition. Seven votes make it controlling independently of Part II's deference rationale.
- 2. Reasonable delegated construction: 2. Reasonable delegated construction, 1995-06-29. Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer join. Six votes independently control this rationale; Stone does not join it.
- 3. Actual injury and ordinary causal limits: 3. Actual injury and ordinary causal limits, 1995-06-29. Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part III; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer join the common rule. O'Connor's concrete reproduction example and Palila reservations are her concurrence, not additional majority applications.
- 4. Distinct statutory protections and conditional permissions: 4. Distinct statutory protections and conditional permissions, 1995-06-29. Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part IV; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer join. The statutory qualifications below are integral limits, not new adjudications of applications.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-29; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Section 9(a)(1) governs listed endangered fish or wildlife and persons subject to United States jurisdiction, with taking prohibitions in the United States, territorial sea and high seas, subject to §§6(g)(2) and 10. Threatened-species protections arise separately under §4(d), §9(a)(1)(G) and lawful regulations, including §17.31. For resident fish or wildlife in a State with a §6(c) cooperative agreement, §4(d) rules apply only to the extent also adopted by the State. Section 7’s agency no-jeopardy and critical-habitat obligations do not replace the actual-taking inquiry. A no-jeopardy opinion alone exempts no taking: §7(o)(1) protects activity necessary to a §7(h)-exempted agency action, while §7(o)(2) protects compliance with an actual §7(b)(4) written incidental-take statement’s terms.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Section 10(a)(1)(A) separately permits scientific or propagation-and-survival permissions. Incidental-take permission under §10(a)(1)(B) requires taking incidental to, not the purpose of, otherwise lawful activity. The conservation plan must state likely impacts, minimization/mitigation steps and funding, considered alternatives and rejected reasons, and other necessary appropriate measures. After public comment the Secretary must find incidental taking, maximum-practicable minimization and mitigation, adequate funding, no appreciable reduction in survival-and-recovery likelihood in the wild, fulfillment of other required measures and receipt of assurances. Those findings require issuance with necessary or appropriate conditions and reporting; noncompliance requires revocation. Otherwise lawful land use alone is no exemption.

**Controlling construction:** Section 10(j)’s released experimental populations, including solely their offspring, qualify only while wholly geographically separate from nonexperimental populations of the same species. Outside-range release requires a conservation finding, regulatory identification before release and a best-available-information essentiality determination. Members are treated as threatened; a nonessential population is treated as proposed for listing under §7 except §7(a)(1), with the National Wildlife Refuge and National Park System exception. Critical habitat may not be designated for a nonessential population; earlier authorized separate releases retain the statutory regulatory-determination condition. No population application is decided.

**Open question:** Section 6(g)(2)’s exact exception and its application are not construed: the Record identifies the official text and applicable State-agreement record as necessary to decide that question. The facial rule adjudicates no enforcement scienter or universal knowledge-of-illegality requirement. Separate §11 civil and criminal channels retain applicable notice and hearing requirements and the civil good-faith protection for action believed necessary to protect a person from bodily harm.

**Holdings navigation:** 1. Statutory authority for habitat-caused injury; 2. Reasonable delegated construction; 3. Actual injury and ordinary causal limits; 4. Distinct statutory protections and conditional permissions.

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. The interstate-externality reservation is unchanged.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

#### United States v. Hale, judgment below 978 F.2d 1016

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** February 12, 1996.

##### Congress may include possession in this regulation of the machinegun market

**Holding:** Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market, subject to the subsection's government-related and prior-lawful-possession exceptions, when possession and transfer are sufficiently integrated that excluding possession would permit acquisition, retention, concealment, or continued circulation of the prohibited stock and thereby materially impair enforcement of the valid transfer/stock regime. The relationship is one of anti-evasion and material impairment under the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, and it defeats the presented commerce challenge without a transaction-specific interstate element. General gun violence, fear, insurance cost, marketability alone, or remote interstate history does not supply the ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 922(o)(2) excepts transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions; it separately excepts lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986. Earlier manufacture alone does not satisfy that second exception. No count is newly found within either exception.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lopez is clarified and developed: Lopez reserved intrastate inclusion genuinely necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate regime, and this decision supplies the first operational rule for that route. Wickard and Perez are preserved for economic-class aggregation. Harris, Bishop/Stokes and American Life League rest on distinct mechanisms; none is converted into the source of Hale's anti-evasion rule, and no other statute's interstate element is transferred into §922(o).

#### American Life League v. Reno

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 94-1869; no separate Supreme Court merits docket established; judgment below, 47 F.3d 642.
**Decided:** November 13, 1995.

##### Congressional protection of an interstate medical-services market

**Holding:** Congress may regulate FACE's challenged class of targeted force, threats, physical obstruction and facility destruction intentionally directed at, and directly interfering with, provision or receipt of reproductive health services in an interstate medical-services market. The constitutional connection comes from the conduct's object and operation on that service market itself, not from generalized healthcare costs, violent-crime costs, ideological motive or productivity effects. This direct market-interference ground adds no balancing test and requires no separate interstate-crossing element for each actor; direct regulation of private conduct also compels no state legislation or administration.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** Interstate patients, personnel and supplies, and actual service loss through obstruction and closure establish a direct connection to the medical-services market. Local conduct or ideological purpose does not sever that connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court adopts no exhaustive commerce formula, remote general crime-cost rationale or conclusive effect for legislative statistics. Separate persons-or-things-in-commerce and Fourteenth Amendment §5 grounds are not reached. The parent/legal-guardian exception excludes all §248 penalties and civil remedies for activity directed exclusively at the actor's own minor, without creating immunity under other law or a constitutional parental veto. The challenged reproductive-service branches alone are decided; worship branches remain unchallenged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the commerce/federalism dismissal. FACE's remaining speech, vagueness, civil-remedy, free-exercise and RFRA questions are under First Amendment, American Life League, November 13, 1995, Fourth Circuit No. 94-1869.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** As the first post-Lopez application recognizing a direct market-interference mechanism, the decision applies Lopez's connection-before-magnitude architecture and distinguishes its inadequate downstream-effects chain. Harris's economic vehicle-taking class and enacted vehicle nexus are a distinct mechanism; Robertson's direct-enterprise rule is distinct and does not classify every protester as an enterprise. New York's anti-compulsion limit remains intact.

#### Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-859.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

##### Statutory authority for habitat-caused injury

**Holding:** The ESA permits the Secretary to define harm to include significant habitat modification or degradation that actually kills or injures protected wildlife by significantly impairing essential behavioral patterns, including breeding, feeding, or sheltering. Habitat change without the required animal injury is outside this clause.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer join this statutory proposition. Seven votes make it controlling independently of Part II's deference rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 9's individual-animal protection remains distinct from section 7's jeopardy and critical-habitat duties; environmental purpose alone supplies no authority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T: followed as requiring enacted authority; its rejection of removing a mandatory filing requirement does not foreclose this different statutory definition.
- PUD No. 1 v. Washington Department of Ecology: consistent with authority grounded in the particular statute, rather than environmental purpose alone.

**Independent alternative holding:** Where this ESA definition leaves room concerning indirect injury, the Secretary's actual-injury construction is reasonable and receives Chevron deference. This rationale neither dispenses with statutory authority nor establishes that the regulation is the only permissible construction.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer join. Six votes independently control this rationale; Stone does not join it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The six-Justice deference rationale is independently sufficient and does not make the regulation the only permissible construction or create a criminal knowledge requirement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council: applied to a reasonable construction within statutory ambiguity.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T: followed; deference does not authorize displacement of an unambiguous statutory command.
- Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala: distinguished; own-regulation interpretation does not establish the statutory delegation here.

##### Actual injury and ordinary causal limits

**Holding:** The habitat clause requires actual death or injury proximately caused, with ordinary foreseeability, by significant habitat modification through significant impairment of essential behavior; bare but-for connection or speculative future harm is insufficient. An omission can be considered only where an independently existing legal duty supplies the obligation, and this decision creates no general duty to maintain habitat.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part III; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer join the common rule. O'Connor's concrete reproduction example and Palila reservations are her concurrence, not additional majority applications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A carcass is not indispensable evidence: appropriate proof may establish injury or support prevention of injury that will actually occur. Speculative population effects are insufficient. Specific causal applications and the separate civil/criminal scienter requirements remain open; O'Connor's reproduction example and Palila reservations are not additional majority applications.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Gardner: distinguished; its benefits provision does not decide ESA proximate causation or civil and criminal scienter.

##### Distinct statutory protections and conditional permissions

**Holding:** Facial validity preserves the separate triggers of endangered-species section 9, threatened-species rules under section 4(d), federal-agency section 7, and statutory exceptions and conditional permits under section 10. Neither a no-jeopardy opinion nor otherwise lawful land use alone exempts a taking.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part IV; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer join. The statutory qualifications below are integral limits, not new adjudications of applications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The integral statutory qualifications below remain distinct. The Court does not construe or apply section 6(g)(2); its exact terms and application require the official provision and applicable State-agreement record, absent from the positively reviewed appendix.

Section 3(19) defines take through harass, harm, pursue, hunt, shoot, wound, kill, trap, capture, collect, and attempts at that conduct. Under 50 C.F.R. §17.3, harm means an act actually killing or injuring wildlife; it may include significant habitat modification or degradation where it actually kills or injures by significantly impairing essential behavioral patterns, including breeding, feeding, or sheltering.

Section 9(a)(1) concerns listed endangered fish or wildlife and persons subject to United States jurisdiction, subject to sections 6(g)(2) and 10. Its taking prohibitions cover the United States and territorial sea and the high seas. Section 9(a)(1)(G) separately prohibits violation of regulations lawfully issued for endangered or threatened species. Section 4(d) requires necessary and advisable conservation regulations for threatened species and permits extension of section 9 prohibitions; 50 C.F.R. §17.31 supplies a separate regulatory route. For taking resident fish or wildlife, section 4(d) regulations apply in a State having a section 6(c) cooperative agreement only to the extent the State also adopts them. The Court does not construe the separate section 6(g)(2) exception or decide its application; its exact terms and any application require the official section 6(g)(2) text and applicable State-agreement record, which are not supplied in the positively reviewed appendix.

Section 7's no-jeopardy and critical-habitat obligations govern federal agency action; they do not substitute for section 9's actual-taking inquiry. Section 7(o)(1) protects activity necessary to an agency action exempted under section 7(h). Section 7(o)(2) protects a taking compliant with the terms and conditions of a written section 7(b)(4) incidental-take statement. A no-jeopardy opinion alone is not a blanket exemption.

Section 10(a)(1)(A) permits scientific or propagation-and-survival permissions. Section 10(a)(1)(B) permits taking incidental to, and not the purpose of, otherwise lawful activity. Section 10(a)(2)(A) requires a conservation plan stating likely impacts; minimization and mitigation steps and funding; alternatives considered and reasons rejected; and other necessary and appropriate measures. After public comment, section 10(a)(2)(B) requires findings of incidental taking, minimization and mitigation to the maximum extent practicable, adequate funding, no appreciable reduction in likelihood of survival and recovery in the wild, satisfaction of other required measures, and receipt of required assurances. Upon those findings the Secretary shall issue the permit with necessary or appropriate terms, conditions, and reporting requirements; section 10(a)(2)(C) requires revocation for noncompliance. These requirements do not grant blanket permission for unpermitted land use.

Section 10(j)'s experimental-population treatment is separate: released populations, including solely their offspring, qualify only while wholly geographically separate from nonexperimental populations of the same species. Release outside the current range requires a finding that conservation is furthered, identification by regulation before release, and a best-available-information determination of essentiality. Members are treated as threatened; a nonessential population is treated as proposed for listing for section 7 other than section 7(a)(1), except within the National Wildlife Refuge or National Park System. Critical habitat may not be designated for a nonessential population. Earlier authorized geographically separate releases are subject to the statutory regulatory determination. No experimental-population application is decided here.

Distinct section 11 civil and criminal channels retain their own conditions, including applicable notice and hearing and the civil good-faith protection for action believed necessary to protect a person from bodily harm. This facial decision establishes no new mens rea test or universal knowledge-of-illegality requirement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Tennessee Valley Authority v. Hill: relied on for the Act's operative protection, subject to the distinct permissions and exemptions Congress enacted.
- PUD No. 1 v. Washington Department of Ecology: consistent with giving each environmental provision its own statutory trigger.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the D.C. Circuit's facial invalidation and remand. The regulation remains valid, but particular enforcement requires applicable injury, causation and enforcement predicates. No logging license, damages, fine, particular injunction, conviction or actual permit application is adjudicated.

## Holdings

**Congress may regulate these possessions as part of the machinegun-market regime.** Section 922(o)'s possession component constitutionally reaches Rybar's possession of the two completed machineguns because Hale already established that possession may be included as a necessary anti-evasion and anti-impairment component of Congress's direct prohibition on additions to the post-1986 civilian machinegun market. Part I, joined by all nine Justices, rejects Rybar's commerce challenge; the possession component's validity does not depend on adding a transaction-specific interstate-crossing element to §922(o).

Rybar possessed and sold two completed submachine guns in consecutive gun-show transactions. The sales make the market relationship concrete, while the offenses remain possession. The enacted regime restricts both transfer and possession of post-ban machineguns, subject to the statute's government-related channels and prior-lawful-possession exception. A transfer restriction would be materially impaired if newly prohibited stock could be acquired, retained, held, concealed or circulated free of the possession component; Rybar's transactions fall at the center rather than the edge of that anti-evasion mechanism. Lopez requires the Court to identify the statutory class and the interstate mechanism before aggregation or downstream magnitude can perform constitutional work. The mechanism here is not gun violence, fear, insurance cost, productivity, marketability in the abstract, or presumed foreign or military provenance; it is the fitted relationship between possession and enforcement of the valid national machinegun-transfer and stock regime. Hale controls: Rybar creates no broader Commerce category and does not enlarge Hale's anti-evasion rule, but shows that the rule applies when possession occurs in actual commercial transfer circumstances at the core of the regulated market.

The Court does not rewrite §922(o) to require proof that the particular machinegun crossed a State line in Rybar's transaction; the constitutional ground lies in the valid market regime and the necessary inclusion of the defined possession class, not in an omitted case-specific crossing element. Marketability alone, remote interstate history alone and generalized crime costs are each insufficient. The Court does not decide a materially different possession statute lacking a fitted anti-evasion relationship to a valid interstate regime, and it does not establish that every object subject to national regulation may be federally possessed forever because it once moved in commerce. No universally mandatory legislative-findings rule or general police power is adopted. Government-related statutory channels and lawful pre-May 19, 1986 possession retain their statutory scope; manufacture date alone does not establish the latter exception.

**The personal arms claim requires a separate application.** Part II, joined by all nine Justices, holds that Rybar may invoke the personal guarantee without militia enrollment or service. Military usefulness neither excludes an arm nor makes it immune from regulation; a commercial setting does not categorically erase the claim. Necessary acquisition remains connected to protected keeping. Love, Hale and the preserved Miller inquiry require assessment of the actual weapons, conduct and restriction independently of commerce authority and registration duties.

**The displaced affiliation premise requires a bounded return here.** Part III, joined by eight Justices, vacates the appellate affirmance because its material militia requirement is invalid and its additional discussion supplies no adequate independent weapon-and-restriction application. The Chinese Type 54 and U.S. Military M-3 are identified completed automatic weapons, and their transactions are settled inputs. Their known characteristics strengthen the arguments for regulation, but classification alone does not resolve the constitutional restriction. Hale's registration holding does not establish the possession prohibition's validity.

## Holdings

## Holdings

### Possession of prohibited post-1986 machinegun stock

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(o) may reach possession of a completed machinegun within the prohibited post-May 19, 1986 civilian stock without proof of a defendant-specific interstate or commercial transaction, because the possession prohibition is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of Congress's valid national restriction on additions to that stock. The Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause supplies that class-and-regime connection; no additional transaction-specific interstate element is required.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule, its application and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Hale's possession rule does not disappear when a particular transfer cannot be proved. Local conversion creates completed prohibited stock that can be retained, concealed, acquired and kept available for circulation. Excluding that possession would materially impair the stock restriction itself. Rybar applied the same rule where gun-show sales made the market connection concrete; its facts did not convert a proved sale into a universal constitutional prerequisite. Kirk's possession conviction therefore stands without treating the reported sales as admitted elements or reviving dismissed transfer charges.

Lopez requires an identified interstate mechanism before aggregation can measure its magnitude. Here the mechanism is the specific transfer-and-stock regime, its grandfathered lawful stock, and the fitted possession prohibition. It is not dangerousness, national marketability, remote interstate movement, generalized gun crime or downstream productivity. Nor does administrative convenience alone establish necessity. A particular interstate element may supply a connection in another statute, but courts need not insert one where this valid regime supplies the connection. Formal findings are not universally required and cannot create power by recital; the identified enacted structure supplies the necessary relationship here.

**Operative exceptions and limits:** Section 922(o)(2) preserves transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. It separately preserves lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986; earlier manufacture alone is insufficient. Neither exception is established for Count One. The holding establishes no general federal firearms police power, no rule for every locally manufactured object, and no automatic justification for every product-market possession prohibition. It decides neither Second Amendment protection nor every conversion-parts, readily-restorable or wear-induced application of the machinegun definition.

- Rybar is applied without treating its gun-show sales as a new element or a constitutional condition for every possession prosecution; its separate arms holding is not before the Court.
- Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class aggregation scope; neither supplies an independent disposition here.

## Law After Decision

Hale's existing possession authority includes this locally converted completed machinegun even without transaction-specific interstate proof. The decision clarifies that application rather than adding a new Commerce category. It leaves ordinary firearm possession, other statutory applications and independent constitutional challenges subject to their own rules.

## Holdings

### I. Direct interference with operating commercial assets supplies the required commerce connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may apply the Hobbs Act to robbery directed at the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce when the taking directly impairs its commercial operations and the Act's enacted commerce element is actually established. The effect of one robbery may be slight: aggregation measures the magnitude of this directly connected economic class, but neither a business label nor generalized economic consequences of crime supplies the antecedent connection.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt the rule, application to the Hobbs Act components and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Lopez requires the enacted class and its actual interstate mechanism before magnitude can establish federal authority. The relevant class here is robbery affecting commerce through direct interference with operating business capital, not all robbery. The targeted businesses purchased interstate inventory and cashed out-of-state, payroll and government-benefit checks. The takings removed money withdrawn for those operations and impaired restocking and check-cashing capacity; the published record also reports one business closing for lack of capital. Taking the working money in a bank drive-through lane or store parking lot does not change its established business function.

American Life League supplies the closest mechanism: conduct directed at an actual interstate market's operations can directly obstruct that market. Once that relationship exists, Wickard and Perez permit evaluation of class-wide effects without demanding a substantial effect from each individual act. The statutory commerce element still requires an actual offense-specific nexus. Its words alone would not validate a constitutionally inadequate connection, and its character as an element does not make it subject-matter jurisdiction.

The contrary argument properly rejects multiplying local losses as a substitute for a constitutional mechanism. But this record identifies that mechanism before aggregation. A theory treating every victim as a potential consumer or every dollar as eventual interstate spending would lack this boundary and is not adopted. Hale, Rybar and Kirk concern the distinct inclusion of possession necessary to a valid machinegun regime; no such anti-evasion rationale is needed here.

The facial commerce objection fails because the Act lawfully reaches this defined class; the Court does not validate every imagined application. The actual business-robbery components and conspiracy directed at those robberies likewise require no substantial individual effect. This is no universal conspiracy-elements rule. Purely personal or household robbery, remote historical movement, every asserted depletion theory, a universal dollar floor and constitutional validity without the enacted commerce connection remain undecided.

### II. The wholly derivative firearm objection fails with the predicate commerce challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented constitutional objection to the firearm conviction supplies no ground for relief because it depends entirely on the rejected claim that the underlying Hobbs Act business robberies exceed the commerce power. This disposition adjudicates no independent firearm element, instruction, sufficiency, aiding-and-abetting or collateral defect.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices directly adopt this derivative ground. Stone-Zsela agrees with the firearm disposition only and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower opinion identifies the firearm constitutional challenge as dependent on invalidity of the Hobbs Act predicates. Part I rejects that asserted invalidity through the actual business-interference mechanism and proved commerce nexus. The derivative argument therefore fails on its own stated premise. Neither this conclusion nor Salinas's distinct statutory-conspiracy analysis resolves an independent defect that is not presented. The Court does not infer a new admission, revise the indictment or approve the whole jury charge. The separate lower sentencing vacatur remains effective; the commerce affirmance neither reinstates the vulnerable-victim enhancement nor creates a new resentencing or retrial order.

## Holdings

### I. The damaged property's use must connect it to commerce

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 844(i) requires a connection between the function performed by the damaged property and interstate or foreign commerce, or an activity affecting that commerce. The owner's national affiliation, purchases, an employee's out-of-state insurance and recurring payments do not establish the element merely by existing; their relation to the property's use must be established, without making religious or nonprofit ownership an exclusion or profit-making, rental, sales or an exclusively business purpose a universal prerequisite.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies eight direct joins to the complete construction and its application. Stone-Zsela joins only the propositions that the property-use element has independent work, that the owner's identified economic relationships by themselves do not establish it, and that religious identity creates no exemption. He does not join the complete formulation's rejection of an exclusively commercial-use requirement; his active-commercial-use construction is stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute asks what the damaged property was used to do and how that function connects with commerce. Russell sustains coverage of a rented apartment building because renting the premises placed the property itself in the rental market. Its broad account of statutory coverage does not erase the enacted use limitation, and its rental example does not establish an exclusive class of covered property. The words extending coverage to an activity affecting commerce retain both their breadth and their connection to the property's use.

The Fourth Circuit correctly recited the property-use element. Its application nevertheless treated the congregation's economic relationships as sufficient without establishing their relation to the function of the damaged premises. Annual transfers through a national denomination are actual movements of money, and thus the Government's strongest asserted connection. But the existence of those transfers does not itself explain the building's qualifying function. The same distinction applies to the employees, employee insurance, utilities, religious materials and other purchases. The Court neither doubts those reported relationships nor assumes every purchase came from outside the State.

Bailey/Robinson supplies the statutory distinction between required conduct and another related condition: satisfying one does not dispense with the other. Its firearm-use rule is not transplanted into this statute. Section 844(i)'s own use language does the work here. The Court adds no interstate-travel requirement, no profit-making condition and no requirement that this individual fire substantially affect the national economy. The panel's stated rationale cannot sustain its affirmance; the complete trial proof must be considered under the correct construction. No constitutional ruling or categorical exclusion of religious premises follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Russell remains controlling for actual rental use and is applied without turning that example into exclusive coverage. Bailey/Robinson is applied at the distinction between separately required statutory predicates, not as an independent definition of property use.

### II. The appellate judgment is vacated for review of the existing trial record

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Circuit's affirmance is vacated, and that court must apply the property-use construction to the complete existing trial record and determine the lawful disposition of the §844(i) conviction; this mandate authorizes neither additional evidence nor a new trial. If the existing evidence fails to establish the required use connection, the court must reverse for entry of acquittal, because evidentiary insufficiency cannot be repaired through another trial; if existing proof supports a legally adequate use finding, the court must identify that basis and address any necessary, properly presented constitutional question under current law.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion has the same eight Associate joins. Stone-Zsela joins the independent Burks proposition that actual evidentiary insufficiency requires acquittal and bars a second opportunity to supply missing proof, but rejects this judgment and remand: he finds the proof insufficient now and would direct acquittal immediately. No condition of the Court's further-review mandate is attributed to his position.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court corrects the legal application supporting the appellate affirmance. It does not decide that the complete trial record contains no other evidence relevant to the damaged building's function. Those are different determinations. The lower court must examine existing proof under the rule announced here; it may not treat this remand as permission to build a new prosecution case.

Burks requires acquittal after a determination that the prosecution's evidence is legally insufficient. Jacobson applies that consequence and forbids another opportunity to supply missing proof. Those authorities control if the existing record establishes only institutional relationships without the necessary property-use connection. Conversely, a legally adequate use finding cannot be assumed merely because the conviction once stood. Any such basis must actually be supported, and any necessary preserved constitutional question must then be addressed.

Bailey/Robinson illustrates the distinction between correcting an erroneous statutory premise and deciding every remaining lawful basis or remedial predicate. Its separate carrying branch creates no alternative element here. The remand determines neither acquittal nor retrial in advance, and orders no release, substitute §247 conviction, amended penalty or independent sentencing disposition. The Court reaches neither Congress's outer Commerce Clause authority nor the Establishment Clause objection.

**Precedent treatment:** Burks and Jacobson preserve the mandatory acquittal consequence of actual insufficiency. Bailey/Robinson supplies a bounded remedial analogy, without a new carrying theory, new evidence or an automatic retrial rule.

## Law After Decision

Section 844(i)'s property-use element requires the relationship between the function of the damaged premises and commerce or a commerce-affecting activity. The owner's economic relationships alone do not suffice. Religious and nonprofit premises receive neither categorical exclusion nor automatic coverage. Existing-record review must distinguish a legally adequate use basis from insufficient proof; the latter requires acquittal and cannot support another evidentiary opportunity. The Court changes no Commerce Clause or Establishment Clause standard and decides no §247, penalty-amendment or other-charge question. Stone-Zsela's exclusive active-commercial-use formulation, immediate-acquittal application and separate Establishment explanation do not bind lower courts.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting from the judgment and remand.** The enacted use requirement demands active employment of the building itself in commercial activity. The congregation's purchases, utilities, insurance, staff, donations, mail and national affiliation are economic relationships; they do not transform premises devoted to worship into property actively employed in commerce. The national denomination's annual redistribution of funds to missionaries, seminaries, institutions of higher education and other programs is the Government's strongest argument, but it still describes what the congregation paid and gave in support of worship, rather than commercial use of this building. Russell's rented premises illustrate the distinction because the property itself operated in the rental market. The Fourth Circuit stated the use element but answered it through the congregation's economic connections. Stone-Zsela considers the Government's proof insufficient now. Under Burks and Jacobson he would reverse, direct vacatur of the conviction and entry of acquittal, and deny another trial to repair that proof.

Lopez supplies avoidance context, not a constitutional judgment. Its connection-before-magnitude sequence raises doubt about a reading that reaches every building through an owner's insurance, purchases, payroll or gifts and their downstream economic consequences. Avoidance is available because Congress enacted a use limitation to construe; it permits no inserted element. The existence of jurisdictional wording is not itself a constitutional safe harbor. Lopez preserves genuine economic-class analysis under Wickard and Perez and does not require a substantial effect from each individual act. Stone-Zsela leaves Hinds's particular substantial-effect contention and the outer Commerce question undecided. Hale, Rybar and Kirk's protection of a valid machinegun regime does not dispense with this statute's different property-use element. Robinson's direct interference with operating business assets likewise supplies no all-purchases rule. Robertson's commercial enterprise transactions and Allied-Bruce's broader transaction-involving-commerce language do not replace §844(i)'s property-use text.

This is no religious exemption. Stone-Zsela reserves the reach of the statute over religious property itself used for a commercial enterprise, rental activity, substantial commercial leasing or materially mixed commercial use. He decides no particular application within those categories, no authority under another federal statute, including FACE's unchallenged worship branches, and no reach of differently worded statutes such as the Hobbs Act. Ordinary state arson law and independently valid federal charges retain their own elements and limits.

He separately rejects the Establishment objection as a reason to construe the statute differently for religious property or as an alternative ground for reversal. Under Lemon as Agostini modified it, a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine resolves conduct it covers; an established permissible relationship defeats a covered claim; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved claim proceeds through attribution and function. Purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within that inquiry, not independent or fallback prongs. The governmental act here is prosecution of an alleged arsonist, and its function is ordinary property protection. Everson's established relationship of general public-welfare services, including ordinary police and fire protection, resolves the objection, provided the criminal statute validly applies.

The prosecution organizes no worship, prescribes no religious profession or observance, adopts no doctrine, solicits no religious adherence, governs no theology, confers no religiously allocated civil authority and finances no devotion. Examining a congregation's operations to determine an offense element is not regulation of its religious teaching or governance. Tanford involved the government's own organized commencement worship and therefore a different function. Facial neutrality and the beneficiary's identity are contextual evidence, not independently decisive grounds. Stone-Zsela reaches neither the residual route nor an accommodation, funding, legislative-prayer or ceremonial question; free exercise and religious autonomy retain their distinct sources and burdens. His answer creates no rule permitting every governmental benefit to religion, supplies neither statutory coverage nor an enumerated power, and does not ground his reversal. The second granted question remains unreached; insofar as the Establishment objection falls outside the grant, this discussion states only his reason for declining to rely on it. No other Justice joins this separate explanation.

## Holdings

#### I. Intentional force and threats directed at the provision of reproductive services fall within the established commerce connection

**Holding and operative rule.** Congress may apply §248(a)(1) to intentional force and true threats directed at preventing a person from providing reproductive-health services because that conduct operates directly on access to an actual interstate service market. The connection is the conduct's object and operation, not generalized costs of violence; neither the offender nor each affected clinic must separately cross a state line.

**Authority.** Kennedy's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices, controls the Commerce question and the affirmance on that ground.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that American Life League supplies the governing connection. Its sustained class is direct interference with provision or receipt of reproductive-health services in an interstate market. Lopez requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate effects can justify federal regulation; it does not permit Congress to turn every local crime into a national economic problem. Bird's bottle attack and threat to kill a physician entering the clinic to provide services directly obstruct that service activity. The absence of proof that this physician, clinic staff, patients or supplies crossed a state line does not sever the conduct from the class American Life League sustained. FACE's conduct and purpose elements do the necessary work; the Court inserts no omitted jurisdictional element. Hinds concerns a different statute's property-use requirement and supplies no such element here. The defense's local-violence objection thus fails on the access-directed mechanism, without a new theory based on fear, healthcare expense or lost productivity. This holding does not federalize ordinary assault, trespass, protest or disturbance merely because it occurs near a medical facility, and does not decide materially different mixed-purpose conduct. Alternative §5 authority and reconsideration of protected-choice law are unnecessary.

#### II. The supervision component requires a determination of a continuing controversy before its merits

**Holding and operative rule.** When the actual operation of a challenged supervision condition and any continuing redressable consequence remain unresolved, a live challenge to the conviction does not itself establish jurisdiction to decide that condition's merits. The appellate disposition affirming the condition is vacated, and the matter returns through the Fifth Circuit to determine that threshold on the actual supervision record; the district condition itself is neither vacated nor rewritten by this decision.

**Authority.** Kennedy's Part II-A, joined by all eight other Justices, controls the continuing-controversy requirement and the limited appellate vacatur. Part II-B's precise remand and reservation of the condition's merits is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices including the author. Stone-Zsela does not join Part II-B and would add mandatory replacement if the dispute is live.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that jurisdiction must attach to the relief actually sought. Spencer distinguishes a live custody-related injury from presumed consequences after the particular restraint ends. That requirement informs this distinct sentencing component without making every direct-sentencing appeal equivalent to Spencer's revocation case. The sentencing date and stated terms do not establish commencement, operative delay, tolling, expiration or an actual present burden. Nor does a live conviction challenge establish them. Section 2106 authorizes appropriate further proceedings but creates no merits jurisdiction. A bounded remand allows the lower courts to use judicial materials that establish status, without demanding duplicative production. Only proceedings supported by the actual jurisdictional finding may follow. If the dispute is live, the condition remains subject to §3583(d)'s reasonable relationship to the relevant sentencing purposes, no greater liberty deprivation than reasonably necessary, and pertinent policy-statement requirements. If it is moot, no condition-merits ruling may issue; the proper mootness disposition and any equitable treatment of the lower judgment depend on actual timing and circumstances. The Court presently requires neither retention nor narrowing nor removal of the condition. Past violence supports a serious protective interest but cannot resolve unknown jurisdiction or predetermine the appropriate breadth. No general resentencing or extension of supervision follows.

The separate lower-court familial-classification, overbreadth, vagueness and written-judgment-label disputes are not independently within the questions adjudicated here. Their lower dispositions are not new holdings of this Court.
