# Jeffries — effective public entering law

Effective November 17, 1997. Exact public portion of the completed Jeffries decision. Only adopted controlling propositions are law; separate writings remain noncontrolling.

## Event

Jeffries v. Harleston, Second Circuit No. 93-7876. Merits decision, November 17, 1997; October Term 1997, chunk 1. The Court reviews under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) the April 4, 1995 judgment, 52 F.3d 9, directing judgment for defendants after the shortening of Professor Leonard Jeffries's department-chair term. The questions concern the public-employee speech balance, the independent causal ground, and appropriate further proceedings.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The appellate judgment is vacated and the case remanded under the bounded mandate stated below.

The mandate does not reinstate the district court's two-year chair injunction or punitive awards. Sustainable individual defenses and ordinary verdict treatment remain available; judgment without trial or retrial may follow only if the actual law and procedural record support it. No final liability, immunity, damages, or present entitlement to a chair appointment is determined.

## Holdings

**The actual speech and service relationship govern the employment balance.** The appellate balance cannot sustain final defense judgment on this record and rationale. Neither retained tenure nor lack of entitlement to a longer chair term categorically defeats a retaliation claim; a reasonable forecast is relevant but does not itself decide constitutional protection. Part I, joined by Souter, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls this application.

Pickering and Connick require attention to public expression and actual institutional needs. The address concerned curriculum and racial history, occurred at an unaffiliated off-campus event, and was not assigned or controlled as an official duty. Introduction by the chair title did not establish sponsorship. Course assignments, tenure evaluations, records, meetings and budget work were real responsibilities, but the supported record identified no special policymaking, confidential or additional public-contact obligation. Recruitment, fundraising, faculty interaction and community relationships remain relevant institutional interests. The panel gave its forecast and the retained professorship more legal weight than that combination justified in resolving the whole case for defendants.

The jury's findings of no actual disruption and reasonable expected disruption both stand. Prevention need not await realized harm. Waters protects a genuinely relied-upon reasonable account only when it describes legally disciplinable speech; it creates no complete controlling investigation code. Perry, Umbehr and O'Hare preserve the difference between entitlement to a benefit and retaliation through its withdrawal. The Court adopts neither categorical protection of every part of this address, an actual-loss requirement, quantified damages to the institution, nor NTEU's entire prospective-ban burden for this individual action.

**Nine successful votes do not automatically establish an independent lawful cause.** If protected conduct supplies a substantial or motivating cause, each defendant's actual responsibility and any preponderance same-decision defense must be examined. Different punitive or animus findings do not alone establish that the same action would have occurred without protected speech. Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls that rule and rejection of the blanket causal ground.

Mt. Healthy and the actual-cause holdings of Umbehr, O'Hare and Crawford-El require a real counterfactual, not a conceivable lawful reason. The nine one-year votes are important evidence, but their lack of the six defendants' attributed animus does not itself answer that inquiry. Harleston and Reynolds did not vote; a causally effective proposal may matter, but procurement alone proves no liability. Everett, Badillo and Jacobs cast defeated removal votes; Bloom abstained. Those roles neither establish nor eliminate other supported causal contributions. Claims against Bernstein remain discontinued. A genuine independent lawful Board decision or a supported personal defense may still be dispositive. Ordinary competent direct and circumstantial proof applies without a special heightened motive burden.

**Further proceedings remain bounded.** Part III, joined by Souter, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer, leaves actual verdict harmonization and consequences, sustainable individual defenses, capacity attribution and relief to ordinary adjudication. It orders no compulsory new trial. Harlow and Anderson v. Creighton require particularized personal-damages immunity at the late-October 1991 conduct date, and a distinct date for any preserved March 1992 claim. Later decisions supply no retrospective notice. Punitive answers do not replace protection, cause or immunity; prospective relief has separate live-remedy requirements. Elapsed time alone establishes neither mootness nor continued chair eligibility.

## Precedent Treatment

- Pickering, Connick, and Waters retain the contextual balance, genuine lawful ground and permissible forecast. No complete Waters investigative framework is adopted.
- Perry, Umbehr, and O'Hare are applied to the actual employment benefit, service relationship and causal inquiry.
- Mt. Healthy, Kimberlin, and Crawford-El retain ordinary proof and the actual same-decision defense.
- NTEU's broad prospective-restraint allocation and Settle's student-curriculum holding remain confined to their settings.
- Harlow and Anderson v. Creighton retain conduct-date immunity; no later merits rule establishes earlier notice.

## Law After Decision

The decision applies the role-sensitive employment balance and defendant-specific cause rules to this record, displacing the blanket defense judgment. It establishes no categorical academic-speech exemption, reaction-based exclusion, investigation protocol, universal retrial requirement, or final personal liability. Each controlling part has a direct majority; no combined plurality rule is necessary.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela would classify the address as citizen speech as a matter of law and expressly narrow the disruption rule: offense, controversy, audience hostility, institutional embarrassment, reputational pressure, and disruption consisting of reactions to the viewpoint may not themselves justify punishment of this private public-concern speech. A professor's title and subject matter do not make an unaffiliated address university speech. Assigned teaching, administration and delegated chair speech remain subject to institutional control. Independently proved failure to perform duties, threats, harassment, unlawful discrimination in official work, insubordination, misuse of authority and other regulable misconduct remain lawful grounds.

He joins Part II because the nine votes cannot be declared lawful superseding causes by assuming reaction-generated disruption sufficient. Jeffries must prove each defendant's substantial or motivating causal role, and a defendant may prove the same action would occur for an independently lawful reason. Stone-Zsela considers the conflicting verdicts and differentiated roles to require a limited new trial on those questions, immunity and relief. He would neither reinstate inconsistent verdicts nor decide particular liability, qualified immunity, compensatory or punitive damages, or precise prospective relief. This compulsory-trial remedy and categorical substantive limitation do not command the Court.

O'Connor's dissent, joined by Scalia and Thomas, would affirm on the employment balance. The independent public-concern setting and nonpolicymaking characterization deserve weight, but course assignments, tenure evaluations, internal representation and budget work create a consequential relationship. The identified recruitment, fundraising, faculty-interaction and community interests, supported by the reasonable-expectation finding, justify the chair-term action without waiting for actual harm. The dissent performs that legal balance rather than treating sincere belief, offensive content, retained tenure or the jury's forecast as automatically decisive. It neither invents institutional sponsorship or deficient performance nor adopts the nine-vote rationale or a final immunity determination as an alternative holding.

## Procedure After Action

The Second Circuit must conduct or direct further proceedings consistent with Parts I–III. Actual claim scope, verdict forms, instructions and preservation govern available dispositions. No March 1992 claim is created, no discontinued claim revived, and no current chair appointment or award is automatically restored.
