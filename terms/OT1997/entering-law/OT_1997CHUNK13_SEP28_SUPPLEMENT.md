## Standards and Tests selection: Customs forfeiture promptness duties and dismissal

### Customs forfeiture promptness duties and dismissal

**Governing question:** Whether breach of §§1602–1604 requires dismissal of a timely forfeiture action.

**Current rule:** Breach of §§1602–1604’s separate reporting and commencement duties does not require dismissal of a forfeiture action filed within §1621’s five-year limitations period. Absence of that dismissal sanction does not make the duties optional. An independently supported constitutional-delay claim or other source-authorized relief remains available. This consequence rule does not replace the provisions’ separate reporting and commencement inquiries.

**Authority by component:**

- Mandatory duties, limitations period and absent automatic dismissal: United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property — Mandatory administrative promptness does not supply an unstated dismissal sanction, 1993-12-13. Kennedy's opinion for the Court, Statutory timing portion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this construction and reversal of the contrary ground below.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 1993-12-13.

**Holdings navigation:** Mandatory administrative promptness does not supply an unstated dismissal sanction.

## Standards and Tests selection: Municipal liability defenses and connected interlocutory review

### Municipal liability defenses and connected interlocutory review

**Governing question:** Whether an officers’ immunity appeal permits immediate review of a separate municipal liability defense.

**Current rule:** An ordinary municipal liability defense remains effectively reviewable after final judgment and does not acquire immunity from trial through the officers’ immunity. An expressly reserved, tentative final-policymaker ruling also lacks the conclusiveness required by Cohen. The court of appeals may not decide a separate party’s nonappealable defense merely because it is related to a valid immunity appeal and its resolution would save litigation.

**Authority by component:**

- Ordinary municipal liability remains reviewable after final judgment: Ordinary municipal liability remains reviewable after final judgment, 1995-03-01. Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer; nine Justices adopt this independently sufficient failure of collateral-order jurisdiction.
- The expressly tentative ruling also lacks conclusiveness: The expressly tentative ruling also lacks conclusiveness, 1995-03-01. Part III of Justice Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this independent ground. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela does not join this additional ground.
- A proper immunity appeal does not authorize this separate county appeal: A proper immunity appeal does not authorize this separate county appeal, 1995-03-01. Part II of Justice Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer; nine Justices adopt this bounded rule and its application. Part IV, joined by the eight Associate Justices, leaves the availability and scope of genuinely necessary connected review undecided; the Chief's affirmative view of that possibility is separate and noncontrolling.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-03-01; the component authorities identified above.

**Open question:** Swint finds the county issue neither inextricably intertwined with nor necessary to meaningful immunity review. It leaves genuinely necessary connected review unresolved rather than creating either general permission or a categorical ban.

**Exceptions and limits:** A separate §1292(b) route requires a written district-court statement of a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion and material advancement of termination, application within ten days and discretionary appellate permission. Application alone does not stay proceedings. Rulemaking authority in §§1292(e) and 2072(c) is not itself an operative appeal rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Nominal civil-rights relief and reasonable fees

### Nominal civil-rights relief and reasonable fees

**Governing question:** What instructions govern nominal civil-rights relief and reasonable fees?

**Current rule:** An enforceable nominal-damages judgment directly benefiting the plaintiff by changing the fee defendant's legal obligation establishes prevailing-party eligibility under §1988. Magnitude bears on amount, not that eligibility. Reasonable fees require an explained Hensley assessment of actual success and unrelated unsuccessful work, permitting reduction to zero without mechanical proportionality to damages or a presumption of substantial fees. Correcting an appellate eligibility error does not itself fix the amount or automatically reinstate a prior award.

**Authority by component:**

- Enforceable nominal relief establishes eligibility: Farrar v. Hobby — Enforceable nominal relief establishes eligibility, 1992-12-14. Part I of White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; all nine Justices support this controlling proposition.
- Reasonable amount requires reconsideration on this review: Farrar v. Hobby — Reasonable amount requires reconsideration on this review, 1992-12-14. Part II of White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices control the remedy and stated fee principles.

**Present operation effective:** 1992-12-14; Farrar v. Hobby, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Farrar v. Hobby, 506 U.S. 103 (1992).
