### McCardle — no material change through January 21

### Alexander — apply Lee by claimant and procedural stage; keep Lunding's constitutional field distinct

Read the [clean packet](../freeze/OT_1997CHUNK3_NEUTRAL_ALEXANDER.md) and [initial law](OT_1997CHUNK3_ALEXANDER.md). Lee reinforces the requirement of a claimant's own live controversy, distinct representative predicates and a remedy connected to that controversy. Alexander alleges an actual refusal of letters affecting her identified state action; she need not invent a future pregnancy to identify the alleged existing exclusion. Conversely, the unappealed federal damages count, a proposed uncertified class or the lawyers' representation cannot manufacture an otherwise missing controversy. Proper-defendant and available-relief questions retain their own predicates.

The case is at Rule 12(b)(6). Lee's specific-evidence language expressly concerns summary judgment and must not become a pleading-stage affidavit requirement. Nor does its dismissal of an unsupported reduction-of-care theory establish that an actual denied remedy is abstract injury. A dispute about the child's substantive constitutional personhood remains distinct from Article III power to decide a substantial federal claim. Lee does not decide fetal personhood, displace Roe or Casey, find a maternal liberty burden, or determine the live-birth classification. Its claim-specific correction and equitable judgment-clearing rule permit no automatic restoration of merits relief or mandatory remand merely because another claimant's threshold is unresolved.

Lunding is new, potentially relevant public evidence about examining the relationship between an asserted justification and the actual challenged disadvantage. Its controlling Article IV substantial-reason/close-relationship test is **not** Alexander's ordinary Beach/Heller/Romer review. The seven-Justice majority's rejection of an incomplete tax-base justification, attention to the enacted payer-residence line, and refusal to prescribe a replacement formula do not establish that New Jersey's birth line is irrational or require a particular tort remedy. The different statutory and constitutional predicates must do the work.

Ginsburg and Kennedy's complete two-ground Lunding dissent is noncontrolling but relevant to their supported treatment of a classification tied to the actual plaintiffs' circumstances: source-tax allocation and the independent home-State personal-expense rationale. Its objection to other couples is expressly a merits objection, not a standing dismissal. Their position accepts substantial rather than mechanically precise equality in that Article IV tax setting; it supplies no categorical approval of all bright lines or answer to wrongful-death and survival distinctions. The complete published grounds appear below so each may be assessed without selectively treating only one as their position. None dictates an Alexander vote.

Buchanan's limited statutory-factor holding neither creates a right to a state tort formulation nor authorizes every existing remedial classification; its mitigation application and separate writings concern a different constitutional protection. LaChance's procedural answer and falsehood rules decide no mother/child constitutional status, tax-like justification burden or state damages cause. Neither adds a material operative rule for Alexander beyond the original packet.

### Danahy — no material doctrinal change; LaChance's employment distinctions remain bounded

### MacArthur / Lindblom — material Lee threshold supplement; separate the two injuries and the remedial route

Read the [clean packet](../freeze/OT_1997CHUNK3_NEUTRAL_MACARTHUR.md) and [initial law](OT_1997CHUNK3_MACARTHUR.md). Lee's summary-judgment rule requires specific support with reasonable favorable inferences, not a particular document, numerical risk or certainty. For the Foundation's constitutional claim, a claimed reputational or chilling effect still needs supported personal injury and a causal route connecting FBI retention to third-party knowledge and behavior. Lee creates no categorical requirement to identify each lost applicant and does not displace Meese's supported reputational-injury holding. It supplies no missing public-disclosure mechanism in this record.

Lindblom's own statutory claim must be evaluated separately. The FBI's actual retention of records of his associations may implicate a concrete personal privacy interest, if that interest and invasion are supported under the governing law; it is not automatically equivalent to Lee's unsupported future injury from an assumed reduction in care protection. At the same time, asserting that the FBI violated §552a(e)(7) cannot itself supply a concrete interest, statutory adverse effect or lawful redress. A present personal invasion and a bare wish for lawful administration remain different. Lee adopts no universal rule requiring downstream employment loss before a personal statutory privacy injury can exist, and it does not determine whether this invasion is established.

Lee's provider holding rejects express punishment as an additional injury element while requiring actual application and burden. It is a limited analogy against demanding a penalty where the alleged injury is another concrete present invasion; Lindblom is not subject to the providers' asserted legal duties, and the analogy establishes neither retention illegality nor statutory injury. Article III injury, the actual §552a(g) cause and adverse-effect predicate, sovereign consent, statutory exemptions and requested equitable relief remain distinct. The initial Steel Co./Arizonans/Reich/Meyer rules still govern that analysis. A court cannot assume power to decide contested retention merits; limited corrective jurisdiction may address an actual threshold error but establishes neither a purge entitlement nor an injunction route that Congress withheld.

The complete Lee separate positions matter to any proposed threshold remand. Scalia and Thomas accept the no-penalty rule but would affirm based on the reported absence of a concrete trigger, present burden or supported enforcement threat; their independent hardship ground remains part of the position. The Chief's public ground likewise regards the chains as unformed and preserves later supported claims. These are noncontrolling record applications, not universal rules against threshold remand or all statutory privacy injuries. The six-Justice Lee remand rests on the lower court's failure independently to apply the correct rule, not a direction to invent evidence. A MacArthur remand must identify its own genuinely unresolved threshold or remedial premise and preserve available confidential/in camera treatment; missing research originals alone do not justify discovery.

Lunding does not revise Article III or authorize other-party standing. Its majority analyzes the enacted line while the actual taxpayers suffer an assessment; Ginsburg/Kennedy expressly frame their other-couples objection as merits, not lack of standing. Neither position supplies a causal mechanism for the Foundation or collapses Lindblom's separate statutory personal-interest theory into the Foundation's failed one. Its tax-remedy rules do not authorize expungement. Buchanan creates no Privacy Act rule or general remand mandate, and LaChance's record-maintenance commands under §7513(e) are a different statute, not an authorization to retain every FBI record or an exemption from §552a(e)(7). No new statutory retention, damages or disclosure rule is inferred.

## Verbatim Lee holdings and complete relevant published positions

The following selected complete Holdings sections are copied from [Lee's actual public delta](../freeze/OT_1997CHUNK3_PUBLIC_DELTA_LEE.md#holdings). The source controls; its case-specific facts are not facts in these later cases. The final section reproduces both complete published separate grounds, which remain noncontrolling except for their expressly identified joins in controlling propositions.

### Patient exposure and the distinct reduction-of-care theory

**Holding and operative rule:** At summary judgment, a plaintiff seeking prospective relief must support a concrete, actual or imminent personal injury with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, allowing favorable reasonable inferences. Elsner's progressive illness and prior depression, even with statutory eligibility and the general medical assertions assumed, do not establish sufficiently impending exposure to an unwanted assisted death; likewise, an assumed reduction of medical-care protection does not establish injury without supported personal exposure to changed care or threatened harm from that reduction.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part I-A has all nine Justices. Part I-B, addressing the separate care-protection theory, has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in that disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires evidence connecting the particular plaintiff to the threatened harm at the litigation stage, and Lyons distinguishes an existing personal threat from speculation about future exposure. The seriousness of death does not relax that requirement. Elsner's illness and depression make her concern personal, but do not establish the requests, medical and witness decisions, continuing impairment and ingestion necessary for the injury alleged. This conclusion does not rest on certainty, a numerical probability, a particular document, or a finding that safeguards never fail. The Court assumes rather than finds her six-month qualification and the reported general medical propositions. The separate good-faith-immunity theory fares no better: even assuming a reduction from ordinary reasonable-care protection, no actual changed care or sufficiently impending personal injury from it is established. Lujan permits a procedural protection linked to a threatened concrete interest without certainty that procedure changes the ultimate result; the asserted legal alteration cannot itself supply that missing connection. Section 4.02's retained negligence and misconduct liability is not erased by the assumed construction of §4.01(1). The statutory construction, substantive adequacy of safeguards, and patient-claim traceability and redressability remain undecided. Dismissal is without prejudice.

### Continuing controversy, representation and the proposed class

**Holding and operative rule:** Dutson's personal prospective controversy does not continue after his death on this record, which establishes no surviving personal claim, substitute or certified class. A provider's relationship to patients and a proposed class never certified cannot supply the represented patients' missing injury or an independently continuing controversy.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by all seven other Associate Justices, controls with eight votes; Stone-Zsela agrees in these dispositions only.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans requires a live personal controversy throughout adjudication and keeps corrective jurisdiction distinct from constitutional merits power. Dutson died before the District Court's judgment, and the record establishes no surviving basis for his prospective claim. That conclusion does not dispose of another claimant's independently viable controversy. Powers keeps the representative's own stake, close relationship and hindrance to the third party distinct. The doctors' relationship to vulnerable patients cannot create an injury those patients have not shown; nor can an uncertified proposed class replace a missing injured claimant. The Court adopts no categorical prohibition on provider representation, universal class relation-back rule or damages-pleading requirement.

### Compelled participation: injury and ripeness

**Holding and operative rule:** Absence of an express criminal or statutory penalty does not by itself defeat Article III injury: an actually applicable legal obligation or supported threatened civil enforcement may burden present conduct. Fitness and hardship must both be examined; predominantly legal questions do not alone establish hardship, but the possibility of a later civil-enforcement suit does not categorically require postponing every challenge to a presently burdensome legal duty. The participation claims must be reconsidered under these principles without a present finding of standing or ripeness.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part III-A states the legal boundary with all eight Associate Justices joining. Part III-B's application and remand command six direct votes: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Thomas oppose the remand; no combination of separate rationales is needed for controlling authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires concrete personal application, not punishment as a distinct element. Babbitt rejects forcing a properly situated claimant to undergo prosecution, but its applicable-prohibition setting does not establish injury under these conditional civil provisions. Abbott Laboratories requires both fitness and hardship, and Toilet Goods rests on its actual enforcement and review setting. The Court of Appeals assumed the claimed duties, made absence of penalties decisive, regarded imminence as probably sufficient if injury were cognizable, and carried the same premise into its ripeness ruling. Its acknowledgment of possible civil enforcement did not resolve a present compelled-conduct burden. The strongest basis for affirmance is the absence of a reported current request or enforcement threat. Yet the appellate court's assumed-duty analysis does not establish a sufficient independent determination of concrete application under the correct rule. Further consideration must distinguish §4.01(4)'s express freedom to refuse participation from its transfer-upon-request obligation when care transfers, §3.01(2)'s attending-physician context, and §2.02(4)'s long-term-care witness provision. The lawful record must establish any actual duty and burden, the proper defendants' relationship to it, traceability, redressability, fitness and hardship. The Court finds no duty to prescribe or recommend assistance and no present constitutional or statutory violation. Remand commands neither a new hearing nor additional evidence or filings.

### The District Court's declaration and injunction remain vacated

**Holding and operative rule:** Limited corrective jurisdiction permits clearing a constitutional merits judgment unsupported by a live injured claimant, and the equities here require leaving vacated the District Court's patient-based equal-protection declaration and permanent injunction. Reconsideration of a distinct, previously undecided provider claim does not restore that judgment or establish a new entitlement to relief.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part V has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in leaving the judgment and injunction vacated only.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans distinguishes jurisdictional correction from merits jurisdiction; Bender and Corrick supply that limited corrective authority. Bancorp makes vacatur equitable rather than automatic, with Munsingwear retaining its judgment-clearing role in the proper setting. Here no established live injured claimant supports the patient merits adjudication, and retaining its constitutional declaration and permanent injunction would give effect to a merits determination without the necessary controversy. Section 2106 does not supply substantive jurisdiction. The possibility that a different direct-provider claim may prove justiciable neither cures the patient defect nor resolves that claim's merits. The Court adopts no rule automatically vacating every judgment whenever jurisdiction is disputed and orders no restored or replacement injunction.

### Lee — complete published separate grounds

Scalia, joined by Thomas, agrees that absence of an express penalty is not a categorical injury bar, but would affirm the participation dismissal. Assuming an obligation that arises upon a possible future request does not establish that these plaintiffs presently confront it. The reported absence of a concrete trigger, present burden or supported enforcement threat defeats injury, and absence of supported current hardship independently defeats ripeness despite predominantly legal fitness. The appellate court's conditional observation about imminence does not establish present application. Their position does not foreclose a later action supported by concrete jurisdictional facts.

Stone-Zsela would affirm dismissal without prejudice in full. The patient chain depends on future eligibility, requests, independent medical decisions, safeguard failures and ingestion contrary to true wishes. The providers and facilities identify no present request, concrete duty, compliance cost or credible enforcement threat. In his view that unformed chain prevents identification of injury, causation or a ripe dispute, so remand is unwarranted. He reaches no assisted-death merits and preserves a later appropriate challenge by an eligible patient facing imminent application, a provider confronting a concrete statutory duty and credible enforcement, or a facility with an identified participation conflict.

These are copied from [Lunding's actual public delta](../freeze/OT_1997CHUNK3_PUBLIC_DELTA_LUNDING.md#holdings). ### Article IV does not permit this inadequately justified categorical exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State taxes another State's citizens on income earned within its borders, a discriminatory deduction rule must rest on a substantial reason beyond noncitizenship, and the discrimination must bear a close relationship to that reason. Section 631(b)(6)'s categorical exclusion of qualifying alimony from deductions against New York-source income fails that inquiry on the source, personal-expense, initial-computation and family-income justifications offered here; genuine source allocation remains permissible, and identical resident and nonresident tax codes are not required.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice Opinion of the Court supplies direct majority authority for this rule, its application and the qualifications below.

**Controlling explanation:** Toomer protects substantial equality in earning a livelihood across state lines while allowing considerable state latitude. Shaffer permits taxation of local income and corresponding source-related deduction limits; Travis nevertheless rejects unjustified unequal personal allowances. Those decisions require examination of the particular deduction and tax, not ordinary rationality alone or mechanical equality.

New York taxes residents' worldwide income and nonresidents' local income. That real difference does not by itself justify excluding all proportionate recognition of alimony. The payment adjusts income available to the payer and lacks an exclusively Connecticut source merely because the former spouses and divorce decree are there. The lower court's Goodwin analogy involved geographically connected home-State property and medical expenses; it does not resolve this alimony provision. The Court adopts no rule requiring apportionment of every personal expense.

The whole calculation also matters. Qualifying alimony reduces hypothetical resident liability and the federal-adjusted-gross-income denominator, but § 631(b)(6) denies the deduction in the New York-source numerator. The initial benefit therefore does not establish substantial equality in the final burden. The ratio was uncapped; no particular actual ratio or total effective rate is found.

The asserted allocation of taxable family income likewise fails to match the enacted line. Payer residence determines the deduction without conditioning it on whether New York taxes the recipient. Examining that mismatch addresses the classification imposing the Lundings' own assessment, without finding how often different family arrangements occur. Neither a hypothetical recipient-based rule nor its constitutionality is decided.

Austin prevents another State's credit, deduction or fiscal choice from curing established discrimination. Connecticut's absence of earned-income taxation in this year supplies no cure. Fulton and Oregon Waste reinforce scrutiny of actual operation and unsupported offsets only by analogy; their Commerce Clause tests do not govern Article IV. States retain justified source limits and permissible cooperative tax arrangements.

### Lunding — complete published separate grounds

Ginsburg, joined by Kennedy, would affirm on two independent grounds.

First, the payer deduction and recipient inclusion allocate taxable income between former spouses. When both are New York residents, the State taxes the recipient and permits the payer's deduction; when both are Connecticut residents, denying the deduction preserves taxation of New York-source income that would otherwise pass outside the State's resident tax base. In their view, that source-tax and income-attribution relationship supplies a substantial justification in this case. Article IV requires substantial rather than mechanically precise equality. Imperfect treatment of other, mixed-residence couples does not establish these taxpayers' entitlement to remove the income from New York taxation. This position does not depend on a finding of aggregate interstate-payment balance or on Connecticut's imposing a tax.

Second, even treating alimony as an expense, the Connecticut payer, recipient and decree locate this particular marital obligation in the home State; it is not an expense of producing New York professional income. Shaffer and Travis permit the corresponding source limitation, and Goodwin's home-State expense analysis supports leaving that personal expense to Connecticut's law and policy. Travis's general personal exemption is different from a deduction for a particular nonlocal obligation. They also object that correlation with a payer's income does not distinguish alimony from many other personal expenditures; the Court's disclaimer of a universal deduction rule does not, in their view, resolve that distinction.

Their objection to relying on other former couples is a merits objection, not a vote to dismiss for lack of standing. They accept Austin's rule against curing established discrimination through another State's tax choices but find this allocation justified in the first place. They join neither the invalidity holding nor the correction mandate; their grounds are noncontrolling.
