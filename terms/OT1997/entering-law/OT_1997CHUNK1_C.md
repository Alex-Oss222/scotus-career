## Richenberg v. Cohen — statement inference, genuine rebuttal, and review

- **Controlling application:** [civil-rights.md, Thomasson — “A genuinely rebuttable conduct inference sustains this statement-based separation”](../../../state/holdings/civil-rights.md#a-genuinely-rebuttable-conduct-inference-sustains-this-statement-based-separation), lines 3046–3062; effective June 9, 1997. Ordinary rationality permits a voluntary identity statement as rebuttable evidence of conduct, propensity, or intent. Propensity means likelihood beyond abstract preference or desire. Genuine rebuttal is indispensable; completed acts and individualized operational impairment are not. Service excellence and no completed act do not automatically rebut the inference. The holding does not authorize an irrebuttable orientation exclusion. Read the separate statutory routes and reservations at lines 3054–3058, not only the holding sentence.
- **Governing reusable module:** [STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md, “Military statement-based conduct inference and evidentiary use”](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#military-statement-based-conduct-inference-and-evidentiary-use), lines 7107–7124; present operation June 9, 1997, Thomasson. The current rule and exceptions retain genuine rebuttal, relevant evidentiary use, separate subsection (e) permission, and unresolved independent APA, due-process, directive-validity, and substantive-liberty questions. No present wording/holding conflict found.
- **Speech:** [first-amendment.md, Thomasson — “Relevant evidentiary use of this voluntary statement does not violate the First Amendment”](../../../state/holdings/first-amendment.md#relevant-evidentiary-use-of-this-voluntary-statement-does-not-violate-the-first-amendment), lines 2265–2277; June 9, 1997. Relevant evidence of a lawful conduct predicate is permissible; an evidentiary label cannot justify punishment of irrelevant belief or advocacy. Dawson and Mitchell retain those distinct functions. Waters creates no universal investigation checklist.
- **Earlier military holding:** [civil-rights.md, Walmer — “Ordinary rationality sustains this preliminary denial on the stipulated conduct record”](../../../state/holdings/civil-rights.md#ordinary-rationality-sustains-this-preliminary-denial-on-the-stipulated-conduct-record), lines 3024–3036; January 13, 1997. Preliminary admitted-conduct ruling, with stipulated retention-exception ineligibility. It did not resolve statements, later legislation, a final merits challenge, or another claimant's rebuttal. Its treatment of Goldman and Rostker supplies deference without immunity.
- **Equality:** [civil-rights.md, Romer — “Withdrawal and constitutional foreclosure of ordinary protection for an identified class”](../../../state/holdings/civil-rights.md#withdrawal-and-constitutional-foreclosure-of-ordinary-protection-for-an-identified-class), lines 2710–2722; May 20, 1996. Ordinary rationality permits reasonable prediction and conceivable legitimate grounds but requires a relationship to the actual disadvantage and excludes bare harm or irrational prejudice. Neither a heightened orientation tier nor a mandatory least-restrictive-means/actual-purpose showing follows.
- **Military process:** [STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md, “Germane military judicial assignments and fixed tenure”](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#germane-military-judicial-assignments-and-fixed-tenure), lines 9321–9336; Weiss, January 19, 1994. Independent constitutional review survives substantial military weight. Its contextual tenure analysis is not a universal discharge-review or fact-review standard. Public explanation: [OT1993 chunk 2, Weiss](../../OT1993/output/OT_1993CHUNK2.md#weiss-v-united-states-no-92-1482-including-hernandez-under-rule-122), lines 70–96.
- **Statute and regulation:** Official [1994 10 U.S.C. §654](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title10/html/USCODE-1994-title10-subtitleA-partII-chap37-sec654.htm), subsections (b), (e), (f); local [source](../research/preflight-c/section654.txt), lines 33–64. Section (b)(2) requires an approved statement finding and separation unless a further approved finding establishes the member's demonstration of no acts, attempted acts, propensity, or intent. Section (e) permits omission of processing only on both avoidance/termination intent and military-best-interest findings; it does not command omission. Section (b)(1)'s five cumulative retention findings and (b)(3)'s marriage route are distinct. Section (f)(3) includes both sexual-purpose bodily contact and contact reasonably understood to show propensity or intent; ordinary attraction alone is not that definition. DOD Directive 1332.30, Enclosure 2, ¶C.1.b, quoted in the [published lower opinion](https://static.case.law/f3d/97/html/0256-01.html), requires advice of the presumption and an opportunity to rebut, and narrows propensity to likelihood beyond abstract desire.
- **APA and remedy qualification:** Richenberg below assumed reviewability under 5 U.S.C. §706; its footnote 8 expressly left that question undecided because the Government did not argue it. Thomasson also reserved independent APA relief. Do not turn either into a general military APA holding or assume constitutional issues receive the same factual standard as an ordinary agency-evidence question. The preserved constitutional question can be considered separately from an APA-dependent remedy.

## Jefferson v. City of Tarrant — §1257 finality

- **Statutory channel:** [1994 28 U.S.C. §1257(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title28/html/USCODE-1994-title28-partIV-chap81-sec1257.htm), current certiorari review of final judgments or decrees of the highest state court in which a decision could be had. State certification does not itself establish federal finality. The former §1257 appeal channels are not current routes.
- **Received framework:** [Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn, 420 U.S. 469, 477–487 (1975)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/420/469); local [source](../research/preflight-c/cox.txt), lines 265–308. Each category starts with a finally determined federal issue: (1) the federal ruling is conclusive or remaining outcome preordained, leaving the case practically concluded; (2) the federal issue necessarily survives regardless of further proceedings; (3) later effective review is unavailable whatever the outcome; (4) reversal would preclude further litigation of the relevant cause, rather than merely govern proceedings, and refusal of immediate review might seriously erode federal policy. A chance of prevailing on nonfederal grounds alone does not establish category four.
- **Category one:** [federal-courts.md, Richards — “Whether the dispositive nonparty bar is final for limited federal review”](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md#whether-the-dispositive-nonparty-bar-is-final-for-limited-federal-review), lines 5515–5525; June 10, 1996. Actual disposition of the reviewed parties' remaining claims and preordained implementation supplied finality; claims of a different group did not reopen their controversy. Certification alone was insufficient. Reversal permitting further litigation did not fit category four.
- **Category two:** [federal-courts.md, Sullivan — “The sentencing remand does not prevent review of this completed conviction ruling”](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md#the-sentencing-remand-does-not-prevent-review-of-this-completed-conviction-ruling), lines 2708–2720; June 1, 1993. A resolved conviction question necessarily survived a separate sentencing remand. No general interlocutory exception followed.
- **Category three:** [federal-courts.md, Labron/Kilgore — “Whether the binding state suppression judgments are final enough for §1257 review”](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md#whether-the-binding-state-suppression-judgments-are-final-enough-for-1257-review), lines 5463–5473; July 1, 1996. Later conviction eliminates the State's complaint and acquittal prevents appeal. This is not category four; finality also does not cure an adequate independent state ground.
- **Category four:** [federal-courts.md, Casarotto — “A finally decided federal notice-preemption obstacle is reviewable despite further merits proceedings”](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md#a-finally-decided-federal-notice-preemption-obstacle-is-reviewable-despite-further-merits-proceedings), lines 5498–5506; May 20, 1996. The protected arbitration forum would be lost through the litigation it was designed to avoid. The Southland/Cox rationale does not permit every interlocutory civil appeal.
- **Authority hierarchy:** These four applications supply direct controlling simulated finality law. Kokkonen, federal-courts.md:3630ff, concerns independent jurisdiction for settlement enforcement; De Grandy, federal-courts.md:3914ff, concerns adjudicative scope/preclusion in its voting controversy. Neither replaces §1257 or supplies an independent finality exception. No separate §1257/Cox module was located in Standards; use the full Holdings entries.
- **If merits are lawfully reached:** Distinguish §1988's incorporation of suitable state law from Robertson v. Wegmann, 436 U.S. 584, 588–594 (1978), which reserved materially different death-caused-by-violation settings; Newport v. Fact Concerts, 453 U.S. 247 (1981), bars municipal punitive damages. The supplied neutral preserves the survival/remedy issue. A jurisdiction-only disposition would establish no answer to it. This map does not certify a merits-ready packet on an enlarged question.

## Trest v. Cain — default, appellate initiative, and available relief

- **Direct controlling law:** [criminal-procedure.md, Lambrix — “The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense”](../../../state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md#the-court-may-decide-the-accepted-nonretroactivity-issue-before-the-unresolved-default-defense), lines 281–288; May 12, 1997. Default and Teague are distinct nonjurisdictional limits without inflexible priority. Its preserved-default remand, lines 302–320, required an adequate, independent, preserved, unexcused ground; it did not compel discovery of a defense never asserted.
- **Reusable module:** [STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md, “Sequencing nonretroactivity and procedural default on habeas review”](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#sequencing-nonretroactivity-and-procedural-default-on-habeas-review), lines 1589–1603; Lambrix, present operation May 12, 1997. No jurisdictional defect may be bypassed; preserved adequate independent grounds remain enforceable. No holding/wording conflict found.
- **Exhaustion comparison:** [Granberry v. Greer, 481 U.S. 129, 131–136 (1987)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/481/129), local [source](../research/preflight-c/granberry.txt). The appellate court has discretion respecting omitted exhaustion, considering comity, federalism, judicial efficiency and the actual claim. Neither automatic waiver nor automatic dismissal follows. Exhaustion is not procedural default; Granberry does not itself answer every discretionary-default question.
- **Actual default and exceptions:** Pre-divergence Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722, 729–735 n.1, 750 (1991), preserves adequate independent state defaults, including a supported finding that now-unavailable state remedies would bar an unpresented claim, subject to cause/prejudice or the applicable miscarriage-of-justice exception. The [simulated Coleman stay entry](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md#coleman-v-thompson-504-us-188-1992), lines 288–308, effective May 20, 1992, neither reclassifies default as jurisdiction nor establishes it in Trest. [Schlup](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md#schlup-v-delo), lines 859–899; January 23, 1995, retains its distinct innocence gateway and qualified abuse-of-writ rules. None supplies an innocence finding here.
- **Temporal law:** [federal-courts.md, Lindh — “The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application”](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md#the-chapter-153-amendments-at-issue-do-not-govern-this-pre-enactment-noncapital-application), lines 6841–6853; June 23, 1997; [Standards, “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments”](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#temporal-reach-of-ordinary-aedpa-habeas-amendments), lines 1684–1697. Trest's September 30, 1991 noncapital application uses the covered pre-AEDPA regime. Separately directed provisions and Felker's successive-petition limits remain distinct. Do not treat amended §2254(b)(3)'s express-exhaustion-waiver requirement as a default rule governing this case.
- **Historical lower-court citation caution:** The Fifth Circuit dissent cites the historical Gray v. Netherland for a defense-forfeiture formulation. Governing simulated Gray is instead [criminal-procedure.md](../../../state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md#gray-v-netherland-518-us-152), lines 226–274, June 20, 1996. Its claim-specific preservation and remedy limits do not contain a general rule importing the historical Gray formulation. Use Lambrix and independently surviving law for the present question.
