## Clarifications material to this record

The First Amendment, §1983, *Pickering*, 391 U.S. 563 (1968), *Connick*, 461 U.S. 138 (1983), *Givhan*, 439 U.S. 410 (1979), and *Mt. Healthy*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), supply the employee claim and its separate substantive-protection and causation inquiries. *Perry v. Sindermann*, 408 U.S. 593 (1972), as preserved in *Umbehr*, distinguishes a missing property entitlement from an unconstitutional condition on an employment benefit. Retention of tenure does not itself answer the chair-term claim. The public address's content, form and context and the actual chair functions matter. The current sources establish no categorical rule stripping this address of protection because its speaker held a public academic appointment.

The nine votes for the shortened term and the distinct roles of the six defendants identified by later jury answers require defendant-specific causal analysis. Absence of the same retaliatory animus in the nine voters is not automatically a finding that the same shortened term would have occurred without protected speech, nor automatically proof that another official caused it. An independently sufficient lawful board decision can defeat a claim if established; arithmetic alone does not establish the required counterfactual or each actor's causal contribution.

*Harlow*, 457 U.S. 800 (1982), and *Anderson v. Creighton*, 483 U.S. 635 (1987), remain the independent personal-damages immunity authorities. The separately extracted initial chair-term decision occurred in late October 1991; any preserved March 1992 replacement component has its own conduct date. The 1994–1997 holdings supply no retrospective notice. The publication of *Crawford-El* did not adopt its separate proposed immunity revision. Punitive answers do not settle constitutional protection, personal cause, or immunity. Rule 49/50 preservation and verdict structure are not reconstructed from missing original forms; the Court's authority to tailor appellate relief under 28 U.S.C. §2106 supplies no missing fact.
