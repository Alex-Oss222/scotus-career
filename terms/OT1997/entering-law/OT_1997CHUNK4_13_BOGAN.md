#### Clinton v. Jones, 520 U.S. 681 (1997)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1853.
**Decided:** 1997-05-27.

For connected appellate review and the invalid categorical trial stay, see Federal Courts — Presidential immunity appeals and trial stays, Clinton v. Jones, May 27, 1997, No. 95-1853.

##### No temporary immunity for the presented pre-presidential conduct

**Holding:** A sitting President has no constitutional temporary immunity from a properly brought federal civil action seeking damages for alleged unofficial conduct before taking office. The Presidency alone neither requires dismissal without prejudice nor suspends that action until the term ends; concrete demands upon official duties require their own accommodation analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct rationale votes. Breyer concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns a properly brought federal damages action for alleged unofficial pre-office conduct. It decides no liability, state-court proceedings, criminal proceedings or compulsory attendance at a particular place or time. Litigation's political motive or the claim's abstract importance supplies no constitutional immunity rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fitzgerald preserves official presidential decisionmaking immunity; Forrester distinguishes function from identity and Antoine supplies a limited functional analogy. United States v. Nixon rejects absolute insulation from process but reserves civil-evidence disputes. Nixon v. United States, Morgan Stanley, Plaut and Reich retain their confined structural/remedial holdings and establish no general civil exemption.

##### Official-act immunity turns on function, not presidential status

**Holding:** Fitzgerald's absolute personal civil-damages immunity continues to protect conduct within the outer perimeter of presidential official responsibility; title alone cannot convert pre-presidential conduct into an official presidential act, and alleged illegality or improper motive alone does not strip an otherwise official act of that protection. The possible official-function defense concerning post-inauguration press-secretary statements remains for initial District Court consideration on a developed record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct rationale votes. Breyer concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The post-inauguration press-secretary statements are not classified on this undeveloped record, and not every pleaded allegation is declared unofficial. No new universal classification sequence, reasonable-officeholder test, motive or grave-wrongdoing exception, or qualified-immunity replacement is adopted; state-office defenses and claim elements remain separate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fitzgerald retains its outer-perimeter protection and rejection of improper purpose alone as disqualifying. Forrester distinguishes responsibility performed from present title without diluting that presidential immunity.

##### Concrete presidential duties receive case-specific accommodation

**Holding:** The District Court must give substantial respect to the President's constitutional duties throughout this action; a party seeking protection must identify the particular litigation demand and the conflict or hardship, and any relief must respond to its supported need, duration and alternatives while accounting for the plaintiff's legitimate interests. The court may adjust timing, sequence or manner and may grant a justified, bounded stay, but its discretion cannot authorize significant constitutionally forbidden impairment of another branch's assigned functions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct rationale votes. Breyer concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Landis's clear-showing burden applies when even a fair possibility of harm from the requested stay exists. Relief must remain within justified need, duration and alternatives and be reconsidered as circumstances change; neither a term-long presumption nor a burden requiring the plaintiff to prove exceptional/irreparable prejudice is adopted. There is no absolute date/place/method entitlement, universal hearing, special proof quantum, fixed discovery limit or new interlocutory appeal. Concrete compulsion, privilege, sanctions, irreconcilable scheduling conflict and its precise constitutional remedy are reserved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Proceed under ordinary federal law, determine properly presented official-act and other defenses separately, and assess specifically supported accommodation requests. The denial of immunity and both categorical-stay removals are affirmed unanimously; no liability, damages or immediate fixed-date trial follows. The concurrences supply no additional controlling classification test, scheduling presumption, constitutional exception or attendance standard.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landis governs a supported stay at its stated harm trigger. Fitzgerald explains the institutional importance of presidential duties without immunizing private conduct. United States v. Nixon's civil-process reservation and Reich's presidential-coercion reservation remain intact.

#### Richardson v. McKnight

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-318.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### I. These commercial prison employees are not eligible for Harlow immunity

**Holding:** Guards employed full time by a private, for-profit firm that independently undertakes substantial continuing prison management under limited direct governmental supervision and potential market competition are not entitled to Harlow qualified immunity merely because they exercise delegated governmental custody. Neither the historical showing nor the functional justification establishes that immunity in this institutional setting; brief private assistance, adjunct service, close official supervision and integration into government remain open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the eligibility rule, its limits and the bounded affirmance/remand.

**Material application:** The historical showing did not establish modern immunity from suit for these employees. Potential competition, contractual accountability and private employment arrangements inform functional need without findings of perfect competition, particular insurance coverage or indemnification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** State action is assumed only for the immunity appeal. Neither a constitutional violation, causation, damages, corporate immunity or liability, nor the existence, elements or burdens of an independent private good-faith defense is decided. Eligibility precedes application of Harlow; no replacement notice or conduct test is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Denial of qualified immunity is affirmed, 6–3, and the case is remanded for ordinary proceedings on unresolved predicates and independently available defenses. There is no automatic trial entitlement or damages award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wyatt's separation of attribution, immunity and a potential merits defense is applied in the commercial custodial setting. Harlow remains for eligible officials. Fletcher requires function-specific justification, while Behrens and Johnson v. Jones distinguish legal immunity review from factual-sufficiency appeals. Elder and Crawford-El preserve the actual governing law and constitutional proof requirements without creating eligibility or an additional evidentiary burden.

#### Antoine v. Byers & Anderson, Inc., 508 U.S. 429 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7604.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

##### The ministerial transcript function does not carry absolute judicial immunity

**Holding:** Court reporters performing the statutory task of accurately recording or producing a verbatim transcript do not receive absolute judicial immunity merely because their work is important or integral to a judicial proceeding. A claimant must establish the historically and functionally justified immunity; this ministerial task does not involve the discretionary adjudication that supports judicial immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The burden rests on the person claiming immunity. An authorized federal damages action, actionable deprivation, causation, qualified immunity or other defenses and damages remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the absolute-immunity dismissal and remand for the remaining questions through an authorized cause of action; no automatic liability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Forrester v. White, 484 U.S. 219: functional distinction between adjudication and administration applied to reporting.
- Butz v. Economou, 438 U.S. 478: burden of justifying absolute immunity and functional comparison applied.
- Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S. 478: historical and functional justification required; importance alone is insufficient.
- Imbler v. Pachtman, 424 U.S. 409: immunity for adjudicative functions distinguished from the ministerial transcript task.
- Wyatt v. Cole, May 18, 1992: private-party qualified-immunity holding preserved without extension to the defense decided here.

#### Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, 509 U.S. 259 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7849.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### Investigative fabrication is not protected by absolute prosecutorial immunity

**Holding:** A prosecutor who performs ordinary investigative work to obtain evidence identifying a perpetrator before probable cause has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for alleged fabrication in that function; later courtroom use does not transform the earlier work into advocacy. Genuine evaluation and preparation in an advocate’s role remain protected, and neither all pre-indictment acts nor all post-indictment acts receive a categorical classification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Imbler v. Pachtman, 424 U.S. 409: protected advocacy and genuine preparation preserved; ordinary investigation distinguished.
- Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S. 478: function-specific limits applied to investigation.
- Forrester v. White, 484 U.S. 219, and Antoine, June 7, 1993: function and claimant’s burden control; importance to litigation is insufficient.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Fletcher v. Kalina, June 16, 1997, Ninth Circuit No. 95-36129: the functional distinction extends to a prosecutor’s personal arrest-warrant certification while preserving protected advocacy.

##### A press conference does not carry an advocate’s absolute immunity

**Holding:** A prosecutor’s statements to the press announcing an indictment have no absolute prosecutorial immunity merely because they concern a pending prosecution or cause injury through later judicial proceedings. The ordinary qualified-immunity inquiry and the requirement of an actionable constitutional deprivation remain separate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Allegations are not findings. Genuine advocacy preparation and unreviewed in-court presentation immunity remain; paid or coerced witness allegations require initial treatment below. Qualified immunity, constitutional injury, causation and damages are unresolved; defamation alone is not held actionable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand: no absolute immunity for the pleaded preliminary fabrication (6–3) or press statements (9–0). No liability or witness-immunity change.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Imbler, 424 U.S. 409, and Burns, 500 U.S. 478: press activity lacks the protected advocacy function.
- Tower v. Glover, 467 U.S. 914: policy alone cannot create a new §1983 absolute immunity.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800, and Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: separate qualified-immunity law left unchanged.

#### Fletcher v. Kalina

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit No. 95-36129; no Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** June 16, 1997.

##### Protected prosecutorial advocacy remains distinct from personal evidentiary acts

**Holding:** A prosecutor has absolute immunity under §1983 for preparing and filing an information or warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions, and presenting the State's legal case when performing an advocate's function. An allegation of bad purpose or false evidence does not remove that protection from the advocacy function, but the protection does not extend to a distinct personal evidentiary certification merely because the same prosecutor performs both acts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela joins protection of legitimate advocacy and separation of the personal evidentiary act, but not refusal of an additional dishonesty exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The function, rather than a categorical pre-charge or post-charge boundary, determines immunity. No general dishonesty exception to otherwise protected advocacy is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Imbler preserves charging and courtroom advocacy; Burns separates legal presentation from other acts. Buckley preserves genuine preparation while excluding ordinary investigation; Antoine requires historical and functional justification rather than importance to adjudication.

##### A prosecutor personally certifying arrest-warrant facts acts as a complaining witness

**Holding:** A prosecutor who personally swears to factual assertions offered to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant performs the evidentiary function of a complaining witness and has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for that certification. This classification depends on the personal evidentiary undertaking, not on proof of falsity, and does not withdraw immunity from ordinary courtroom witness testimony.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Kalina personally guaranteed the facts offered to obtain Fletcher's arrest warrant; that evidentiary undertaking is separate from preparing and presenting the charging papers.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither actual falsity nor the availability of another truthful affiant determines the functional classification. Ordinary courtroom testimony immunity remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the denial of absolute immunity for the personal certification and remand, while preserving immunity for protected legal preparation, filing and presentation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Malley supplies the warrant-affiant comparison; official prosecutorial title does not immunize the same evidentiary function. Buckley and Antoine extend their functional distinction to the personal certification, without changing their other boundaries.

##### The function-specific immunity denial leaves constitutional liability and personal defenses unresolved

**Holding:** Denial of absolute immunity for the personal oath leaves Fletcher to establish an actionable constitutional deprivation, the required culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury; Kalina retains qualified immunity and other available personal defenses. The existing qualified-immunity framework governs the remand, using the law and information relevant to the conduct at issue, without a new defense-burden rule or a finding that any element or defense has been established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion, adopted in full by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela joins the unresolved-liability and bounded-remand propositions and accepts the existing qualified-immunity framework, without joining the source-specific causation and notice explanations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A judge's warrant does not categorically break causation when material deception procured it, but a corrected truthful account may still establish probable cause. Neither question is resolved here. Disputed historical facts follow ordinary procedural rules; legal immunity is for the judge. No damages judgment or mandatory trial follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hafer preserves personal-capacity liability only on proof of the officer’s own violation and preserves personal defenses. Harlow and Anderson retain objective, particularized clearly-established-law protection. Elder requires consideration of all relevant precedent; Lanier rejects categorical source and factual-twin restrictions, without supplying retrospective notice.

#### Clinton v. Jones, 520 U.S. 681 (1997)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1853.
**Decided:** 1997-05-27.

For the constitutional immunity and accommodation rules, see Constitutional Structure — Presidential civil immunity and accommodation, Clinton v. Jones, May 27, 1997, No. 95-1853.

##### Immediate review reaches this integrated immunity and trial-protection order

**Holding:** An appeal from the conclusive rejection of the President's asserted immunity from present suit permits review of this same order's categorical trial protection, including its alternative docket-control basis, because those rulings determine the scope and remedy of the same asserted temporal immunity. It does not permit review of independent liability claims or defenses, and creates no automatic appeal from a later scheduling order.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct rationale votes. Breyer concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Common facts, efficiency or presidential importance alone do not authorize connected review. Independent liability, official-defamation merits, general pendent-party jurisdiction and future scheduling/process orders remain outside this appeal; the completed lower-appeal discovery-stay dispute is moot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fitzgerald permits immediate review of asserted immunity from present suit. Swint's necessary/inextricable-review reservation is resolved only for this integrated temporal-protection order; unrelated nonappealable liability questions remain excluded.

##### This categorical trial stay is an abuse of discretion

**Holding:** The District Court abused its discretion by ordering in advance that trial await the end of the Presidency without a developed showing connecting that duration to particular presidential duties and without sufficiently accounting for the plaintiff's timely-adjudication and evidence interests. Ordinary stay authority survives the rejection of constitutional immunity, but generalized burdens and the duration of office do not justify this order.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct rationale votes. Breyer concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Continuing discovery does not remove all trial-delay prejudice. No actual evidentiary loss or disputed survival-law finding is necessary; a later supported request remains possible.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm removal of the Presidency-long stay and Ferguson's exclusively derivative stay, 9–0, without deciding independent Ferguson defenses. No trial date, compelled appearance or particular production is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landis retains ordinary stay power, a clear showing of hardship or inequity when even a fair possibility of harm to the other party exists, and bounded, moderate, nonoppressive duration. A term endpoint is no justification for the entire delay.

### February 12, 1998 — Danahy v. Buscaglia

[Public Projection](../records/danahy_v_buscaglia_merits_1998-02-12.md#public-projection). The following complete public blocks preserve this event's action, proposition-specific authority, published positions, procedure and source limits.

#### Event

**Danahy v. Buscaglia**, Second Circuit No. 97-7264, 134 F.3d 1185. Plenary review decided February 12, 1998; October Term 1997. The Court reviews the January 27, 1998 judgment vacating denial of qualified-immunity summary judgment and remanding on seven employees' First Amendment individual-damages claims. The question is whether the individual defendants receive qualified immunity on the facts proper for this interlocutory appeal. No separate oral-argument date is established.

#### Participation

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Public Action

The Court affirms the Court of Appeals' judgment on the reviewed First Amendment personal-damages claims, without deciding whether each dismissal was constitutionally permissible.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The judgment affirms personal damages immunity for Vacco, Buscaglia and Page on the appealed claims of Danahy, Burke, Bissram, Mungo, Matthews, Angiletta and Henesey, preserving the Court of Appeals' vacatur of the summary-judgment denial and its corresponding remand. The same unanimous coalition supports each employee's disposition.

The judgment does not dismiss the whole action, decide Weiss, Stark or Mahl's claims, or adjudicate other claims outside the appeal. It creates no prospective or official-capacity claim and decides none of their separate requirements. No final constitutional classification, personal-causation or same-decision finding, damages, reinstatement, backpay, new trial, hearing, discovery or description-production order follows.

#### Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, Parts I–II, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Both the legal-review boundary and the particular notice ground have nine direct joins. There are no separate writings.

#### Holdings

##### A legal immunity appeal accepts the proper factual assumptions

**Holding and operative rule:** An interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal may resolve whether the properly assumed conduct violated clearly established law, but may not choose among disputed accounts of duties, knowledge, participation or motive. Section 1254 permits review of the circuit judgment without enlarging the circuit's §1291 jurisdiction; the particular notice question here is separable, and no additional factual-appeal component requiring dismissal has been identified.

**Authority:** Parts I–II of Stevens's opinion are joined in full by all eight other participating Justices. Each holding and its qualifications have nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Johnson separates legal immunity from factual sufficiency, and Behrens permits the legal question only on the proper assumed facts. Clanton and Smith preserve that distinction when reviewing a circuit judgment. The present defense accepts alleged patronage motive and nonpolicymaker status and uses the reported direct prosecutorial work and defendants' knowledge on their assumed-fact footing. It does not depend on finding a different motive or deciding that each employee personally selected targets or set policy. Calling ultimate classification a legal issue would not authorize resolving a genuine historical dispute. Conversely, the existence of unresolved constitutional or factual issues elsewhere in the action does not eliminate this separable legal question. The Court neither resolves those issues nor brings them within the appeal.

##### The particular November 1995 uncertainty supports personal damages immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** On the proper assumptions about these seven employees' actual direct and confidential prosecutorial work, the law in November 1995 left reasonable uncertainty whether political affiliation could be appropriate to their functions. Vacco, Buscaglia and Page therefore receive personal damages immunity on the reviewed First Amendment claims, without a final constitutional classification of any position or an office-wide confidentiality, at-will or civil-service exception.

**Authority:** The unanimous Parts I–II authority stated above applies to this holding, each employee's application and the claim-limited mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** Branti asks whether affiliation is appropriate for effective performance of the particular work; ordinary trust and professional secrecy are insufficient. Its reservation concerned a prosecutor's deputy, not every support employee. Harlow and Anderson nevertheless protect reasonable conduct-time legal mistakes independently of ultimate constitutional permission. Elder requires all relevant pre-existing law; obvious coverage needs no factual twin, but an abstract affiliation right does not resolve every uncertain application.

The affirmative connection here is direct work with prosecutors developing confidential investigations, audits and cases. Danahy supervised investigators working with confidential informants; Burke performed undercover work; Bissram undertook surveillance and undercover work. Mungo's audit-investigator work had the reported direct prosecutor relationship, without invented statutory powers. Matthews and Angiletta shared that relationship without borrowing others' individual admissions. Henesey combined billing analysis, extensive recipient/provider interviews and evidence custody in it; his clerk title does not reduce that account to mere filing.

Those functions, evaluated under Branti and the preconduct functional authorities described in the appellate opinion, permitted reasonable uncertainty about their relationship to prosecutorial discretion. The Court does not find that each employee exercised charging authority or that partisan agreement actually improved performance. The strongest alternative is a partial remand for Henesey, Matthews and Angiletta. Their thinner individual descriptions do not erase the affirmative common relationship, while missing descriptions permit no invented power. The immunity question is reasonable legal error on the supported functions, not final proof of partisan necessity.

Civil-service exemption, general institutional importance and confidentiality alone would not suffice. Patronage motive remains assumed; subjective innocence and a hypothetical same-decision reason supply no alternative defense. Later description refinements and later decisions cannot establish November 1995 notice or require a trial solely to obtain later-demanded descriptions. The result preserves constitutional elements, ordinary proof and other claims while resolving only these personal damages defenses.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Elrod, Branti and Rutan:** Apply protection against impermissible political-affiliation conditions and the actual-function inquiry. Nonreappointment and ordinary confidentiality supply no categorical exception; Branti's reservation concerns a prosecutor's deputy, not all prosecutor-office staff.
- **Harlow and Anderson v. Creighton:** Apply the retained objective, particularized conduct-time defense, without a replacement burden or mandatory merits-first sequence.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Apply all relevant preconduct law on the preserved issue; no new claim, evidence or retrospective notice follows.
- **Johnson, Behrens, Clanton and Smith:** Preserve legal immunity review on proper assumptions and exclude factual selection or unrelated claims. Smith's obvious-force application does not decide this employment boundary.
- **United States v. Lanier:** Preserve obvious coverage without a factual twin; the clarity analogy transfers no criminal element and supplies no retrospective notice.
- **Crawford-El and Mt. Healthy:** Preserve ordinary competent proof, actual personal responsibility and causation, and the independent actual same-decision defense. No such defense is found here, and a dispositive notice ground remains available.
- **Umbehr, O'Hare, Jeffries and Valot:** Preserve their distinct affiliation, speech, causation and remedy requirements; later holdings do not supply November 1995 notice.
- **Fletcher v. Kalina:** Preserve immunity by actual function, without absolute advocacy immunity for personnel decisions by prosecutorial title.
- **McCardle v. Haddad:** Preserve claim-specific procedural requirements; its different Rule 50 omission establishes no omission in this interlocutory appeal.

It creates no general patronage exception for confidential, technical, at-will or exempt employees and does not definitively classify any position. The substantive affiliation rule, ordinary proof requirements, legal/factual appellate boundary and separate personal defenses remain in force. The personal-damages disposition does not establish the legality of the dismissals or decide other claims and remedies.

### Prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements

**Governing question:** What instructions govern prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements?

**Current rule:** Ordinary investigative work to identify a perpetrator before probable cause has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for alleged fabrication; later courtroom use does not retrospectively transform it into advocacy. Genuine evaluation and preparation in an advocate's role remain protected; neither all pre-indictment nor all post-indictment acts share one classification. Statements to the press announcing an indictment also lack absolute prosecutorial immunity merely because they concern prosecution or cause injury through subsequent proceedings. Qualified immunity, an actionable constitutional deprivation and other liability predicates remain separate.

Preparing and filing charging papers or a warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions and presenting the State's legal case receive absolute immunity when they are advocacy functions. Bad purpose or alleged false evidence does not remove that protection; the Court adopts no general dishonesty exception. A prosecutor's distinct personal oath supplying facts to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant is instead a complaining-witness evidentiary function without absolute prosecutorial immunity. That classification does not require proof of falsity and leaves ordinary courtroom testimony immunity intact. Denial of this immunity does not establish liability: constitutional deprivation, culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury and available personal defenses remain separate. A judge's warrant does not categorically sever causation where material deception procured it, while a truthful corrected account may still establish probable cause.

### Ministerial court reporting and absolute judicial immunity

**Governing question:** What instructions govern ministerial court reporting and absolute judicial immunity?

**Current rule:** Accurate recording and production of a verbatim transcript are ministerial functions and do not carry absolute judicial immunity merely because they are important or integral to adjudication. The claimant of immunity must establish its historical and functional justification; discretionary adjudication supplies a different function. Denial of absolute immunity establishes neither a cause of action nor liability, and other defenses remain open.

### Qualified-immunity eligibility of commercial prison employees

**Governing question:** Whether employees of an independently managed commercial prison receive Harlow immunity for delegated custody.

**Current rule:** Full-time guards of a private for-profit firm independently undertaking substantial continuing prison management, under limited direct governmental supervision and potential market competition, do not receive Harlow qualified immunity merely because they perform governmental custody. State attribution and immunity eligibility are separate; eligibility requires its own historical and functional justification before the content of qualified immunity is applied.

**Authority by component:**
- Institutional eligibility exclusion: Richardson v. McKnight, Richardson v. McKnight — I. These commercial prison employees are not eligible for Harlow immunity, Stone-Zsela with Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (six), 1997-06-23.
- Unresolved merits and private defense: Richardson, Richardson v. McKnight — II. Denial of immunity does not establish a constitutional violation or personal liability, same six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Present operation effective:** Richardson v. McKnight, 1997-06-23.

**Open question:** Brief private assistance, adjunct service, close official supervision and governmental integration remain open. The rule establishes neither state action, constitutional injury, causation, damages nor corporate liability, and leaves eligible public officials' Harlow protection intact.

### Presidential civil immunity and case-specific litigation accommodation

**Governing question:** A federal damages action for a sitting President's alleged unofficial conduct before office.

**Current rule:** Presidential status supplies no temporary constitutional immunity requiring dismissal or suspension of the federal action through the term. Fitzgerald's absolute damages immunity still protects acts within the outer perimeter of official presidential responsibility; title cannot make pre-office conduct official, while asserted illegality or improper motive does not alone remove official protection. Courts must give substantial respect to presidential constitutional duties. A party seeking protection must identify the particular litigation demand and conflict or hardship; relief must fit supported need, duration and alternatives while accounting for the plaintiff's timely adjudication and evidence interests. When even a fair possibility exists that a requested stay will harm the other party, the applicant must make a clear showing of hardship or inequity. Timing, sequence, manner or a justified bounded stay may be adjusted, but generalized burdens and the term's duration do not justify a categorical stay, and discretion cannot authorize significant constitutionally forbidden impairment of another branch's assigned functions.
