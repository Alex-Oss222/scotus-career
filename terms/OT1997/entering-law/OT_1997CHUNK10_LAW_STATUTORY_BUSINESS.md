## Standards and Tests selection: Private CERCLA fees and independently necessary response work

### Private CERCLA fees and independently necessary response work

**Governing question:** Which legal-professional expenses qualify as private CERCLA response costs.

**Current rule:** Sections 107(a)(4)(B) and 101(25) do not shift private litigation attorney’s fees; a reference to enforcement activities is not the required authorization for that private award. Work performed by a lawyer can nevertheless qualify when the actual task independently constitutes a necessary response activity, such as identifying other responsible parties in a way that advances cleanup, and the private claimant proves necessity and consistency with the National Contingency Plan. Job title and billing label are not controlling. Work protecting the client’s own allocation or liability position in negotiations with EPA is not thereby a response cost; truly separable qualifying work must be established as such.

**Authority by component:**

- No private litigation fee shifting: Key Tronic Corp. v. United States — CERCLA does not authorize these private litigation fees, 1994-06-06. Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the litigation-fee exclusion; Blackmun, Scalia and Thomas dissent from that component.
- Task-specific response costs and exclusion of liability negotiations: Key Tronic Corp. v. United States — Independent identification work may be a necessary response cost; The claimed EPA-negotiation work protected the client's liability position, 1994-06-06. Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine expressly join this severable task-function portion and its remand. Part III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine expressly join this record-specific functional exclusion and affirmance.

**Present operation effective:** Key Tronic Corp. v. United States, 1994-06-06.

**Exceptions and limits:** The private claimant’s “consistent with” requirement is not the Government’s distinct “not inconsistent with” standard. The holding supplies neither a universal legal-work exclusion nor an award for all useful professional activity.

**Holdings navigation:** CERCLA does not authorize these private litigation fees; Independent identification work may be a necessary response cost; The claimed EPA-negotiation work protected the client's liability position.

## Standards and Tests selection: Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

### Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

**Governing question:** Whether the Communications Act’s power to modify filing requirements permits eliminating them for an entire class.

**Current rule:** Section 203(b)(2) permits the Commission, in its discretion and for good cause shown, to modify requirements made by or under §203 in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions; no required notice period may exceed 120 days. That authority permits adjustments to filing form, contents, timing or place, including consequential and class-wide technical changes, but not replacement of mandatory filing throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system. The inquiry examines the obligation and whether its essential operation is adjusted or removed, not a numerical market-share threshold. Good cause alone cannot authorize the replacement. The Act’s exception for connecting carriers preserves their separate inspection duties; §203(c)’s allowance for otherwise lawful authority does not confer unlimited dispensing power.

**Authority by component:**

- Bounded modification and distinct notice authority: MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co — The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing, 1994-06-17. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Present operation effective:** MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co, 1994-06-17.

**Exceptions and limits:** Limited deferral or waiver is not categorically foreclosed, but no particular waiver is approved. Failure of the special-circumstances-or-conditions predicate is a four-Justice alternative, not a separate controlling holding. No universal major-questions doctrine is created; the statute’s permissible flexibility remains available within the actual delegation.

**Holdings navigation:** The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Express public credit enforcement alongside filed-rate duties

### Express public credit enforcement alongside filed-rate duties

**Governing question:** Availability of an ICC injunction against collection violating credit regulations under the applicable pre-reform provisions.

**Current rule:** Sections 10743(b)(1) and 11702(a)(4) authorize the ICC to seek a judicial injunction against collection violating valid credit regulations; §§10761–10762’s filed-rate duties do not categorically defeat that channel. Section 11702(a)(4) excludes an action under a provision governing rate reasonableness or discrimination, but enforcement of credit and billing requirements is not transformed into such a rate-review action by credit regulation’s antidiscrimination purpose.

**Authority by component:**

- The filed-rate duty does not defeat express public credit-rule enforcement: The filed-rate duty does not defeat express public credit-rule enforcement, 1995-01-10. Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine participating Justices directly adopt this proposition and its qualifications; no aggregation of separate rationales is required.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-10; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Statutory authority alone proves neither a violation nor entitlement to every requested restraint. No general private equitable defense, cancellation of lawful freight principal, universal debt discharge or rule under later transportation reforms is created.

**Holdings navigation:** The filed-rate duty does not defeat express public credit-rule enforcement.

## Standards and Tests selection: Published tariff cancellation and mileage-based undercharges

### Published tariff cancellation and mileage-based undercharges

**Governing question:** Whether a carrier can collect undercharges using a tariff whose essential mileage incorporation was publicly canceled.

**Current rule:** When a carrier’s rate depends on incorporated mileage material and its required participation is canceled through an effective public tariff filing, the incomplete combination supplies no enforceable rate for later transportation unless a legally sufficient replacement applies. This is not a private lapse that leaves a filed rate effective until properly revised: the published cancellation is itself operative. Bankruptcy does not restore the canceled tariff. The ruling does not defeat charges for transportation before cancellation or authorize equitable disregard of an otherwise effective filed rate.

**Authority by component:**

- Effective public cancellation of an essential tariff component: Security Services, Inc. v. Kmart Corp — Published cancellation of essential mileage participation defeats these undercharges, 1994-05-16. Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, and Kennedy; seven Justices adopt the rule and affirmance. Thomas and Ginsburg separately dissent from the statutory-authority and remedy conclusions.

**Present operation effective:** Security Services, Inc. v. Kmart Corp, 1994-05-16.

**Holdings navigation:** Published cancellation of essential mileage participation defeats these undercharges.

#### Key Tronic Corp. v. United States, 511 U.S. 809

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-376.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### CERCLA does not authorize these private litigation fees

**Holding:** CERCLA §§107(a)(4)(B) and 101(25), read in their statutory setting against the American Rule, do not sufficiently authorize shifting the attorney fees incurred in this private cost-recovery litigation. The available private action and inclusion of related enforcement activities within response do not themselves authorize those fees; no universal requirement that Congress use the exact words attorney's fees is imposed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the litigation-fee exclusion; Blackmun, Scalia and Thomas dissent from that component.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. v. Wilderness Society, 421 U.S. 240: applies the statutory-authorization requirement for shifting private litigation fees.
- Runyon v. McCrary, 427 U.S. 160: applies the distinction between an available private remedy and authority to shift its litigation expense.
- West Virginia University Hospitals, Inc. v. Casey, 499 U.S. 83: applies attention to enacted expense categories without requiring one exclusive verbal formula.
- City of Burlington v. Dague, June 24, 1992: distinguishes contingency enhancement under the RCRA and Clean Water Act fee provisions from the absent CERCLA fee authorization.
- Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc., March 1, 1994: preserves statute-specific fee authority; its discretionary copyright-fee permission supplies none under CERCLA.
- Central Bank of Denver v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, April 19, 1994: preserves the received securities action at its bounded scope; it does not authorize every remedial incident under another enactment.

##### Independent identification work may be a necessary response cost

**Holding:** Professional work identifying other responsible polluters may be recovered under §107(a)(4)(B) when it independently performs a necessary response function and is consistent with the national contingency plan, regardless of whether a lawyer or investigator performs it. The claimant must prove the actual tasks, response connection, necessity, plan consistency and recoverable amount; the categorical exclusion of the Air Force identification work is reversed, with allocation and amount left for remand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine expressly join this severable task-function portion and its remand.

**Material application:** The reported Air Force identification advanced an independent cleanup-response function. The claimant must separate mixed tasks and prove necessity, plan consistency and amount without duplicate recovery; ordinary lawsuit preparation does not qualify merely by being called investigation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. v. Wilderness Society, 421 U.S. 240: distinguishes litigation fees from professional work that itself constitutes an authorized response cost.
- Runyon v. McCrary, 427 U.S. 160: leaves the fee-shifting rule intact while requiring identification of the actual statutory cost category.

##### The claimed EPA-negotiation work protected the client's liability position

**Holding:** The claimed legal work preparing and negotiating the agreement fixing Key Tronic's own EPA liability is not recoverable as a necessary response cost because its principal function was protecting the settling client's position, not response activity or enforcement against another liable party. A genuinely separable task that independently satisfies the response-cost requirements is not excluded merely because it occurred during negotiations, but no additional qualifying task or amount is awarded here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine expressly join this record-specific functional exclusion and affirmance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 107(a)(4)(B) requires costs consistent with the national contingency plan; the governmental subsection (A)'s not-inconsistent formulation does not replace it. Section 122(g)(5) protects only matters addressed in the Air Force settlement. The conceded bar to the $4.2 million consent-obligation contribution claim, separately settled response costs and unappealed prejudgment-interest award remain undisturbed. No new cause-of-action restriction, general contribution immunity, federal-facility waiver or government fee entitlement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. v. Wilderness Society, 421 U.S. 240: preserves the need to identify an authorized expense instead of shifting ordinary legal costs because they are useful.
- Runyon v. McCrary, 427 U.S. 160: preserves the distinction between enforcing a substantive obligation and every asserted legal-expense incident.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Denial of the private litigation fees is affirmed by six Justices; denial of the claimed EPA-negotiation work is affirmed unanimously. The categorical exclusion of independent identification work is reversed unanimously and remanded for task-specific statutory eligibility, allocation and amount, without duplicate recovery or restoration of the undifferentiated $155,500 award. The conceded $4.2 million contribution bar, separately settled response costs and unappealed interest award remain undisturbed.

#### MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-356 and 93-521.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** United States et al. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. et al., No. 93-521, is consolidated with No. 93-356; the same rule invalidation applies to both.

##### The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing

**Holding:** Under 47 U.S.C. §203(b)(2), the Commission may, in its discretion and for good cause shown, modify tariff requirements in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions, but may not replace §203(a)'s mandatory filing obligation throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system or lengthen the statutory notice period beyond 120 days. The challenged permissive-filing rule exceeds that delegation and is invalid; Chevron does not require deference to a construction outside this statutory boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The November 1992 rule makes filing optional, not forbidden, for nondominant long-distance carriers; AT&T alone remains classified as dominant in this record. A modification can have consequential effects. Courts must examine the obligation changed, the scope of the modification clause and whether the agency adjusts the obligation's operation or removes its essential operation for the regulated class. This inquiry does not turn on a numerical market-share threshold or automatically invalidate a class-wide adjustment. Changes to filing form, contents, timing or place remain possible under the statutory conditions; timing changes cannot extend the 120-day notice ceiling. The Court does not categorically foreclose deferral or waiver in limited circumstances, and decides no particular proposed waiver. Good cause alone cannot authorize replacement of mandatory filing throughout this sector.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the challenged order independently fails the special-circumstances-or-conditions requirement; no separate alternative ground commands a majority. It prescribes no tariff rates or replacement program, decides no damages or retrospective nonfiling consequences, resolves no general rule for individual nonenforcement decisions, and adopts no independently sufficient ratification or prohibition theory from §§226 or 332.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applies the inquiry into statutory meaning before deference; no new exception based on economic importance and no replacement of Chevron is adopted.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: preserves contextual interpretation within an actual delegation and the requirement of a discernible agency ground; the agency's competitive-policy rationale is examined, not replaced by a judicial rationale.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves permissible interpretive change within a statutory system; the defect here is the rule's statutory reach, not the fact that the Commission changed its policy.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves the principle that agency expertise operates within binding statutory limits; its distinct nonemployee-access rule is not imported into tariff regulation.
- NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, May 23, 1994: distinguishes a permissible functional construction preserving the statute's operative distinctions from removal of the filing obligation here; its supervisory-status holding remains intact.
- PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, May 31, 1994: distinguishes broad conditioning authority grounded in that Act's operative compliance language; no general rule against consequential agency action follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment setting aside the permissive-detariffing rule is affirmed in Nos. 93-356 and 93-521. No tariff rate, replacement program, damages or retrospective monetary consequence of earlier nonfiling is decided, and no new direction is given in the distinct complaint proceeding.

#### Peacock v. Thomas, 516 U.S. 349

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1453.

**Decided:** February 21, 1996.

##### Whether the later collection action has original ERISA jurisdiction

**Holding:** A later action imposing the corporate judgment on Peacock alleges no underlying ERISA or plan violation supporting the asserted federal jurisdiction. Veil piercing is a means of imposing liability, not an independent statutory violation; §502(a)(3) supplies relief for the obligations it identifies, not equitable relief at large.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join this analysis.

**Material application:** Later control and collection obstruction do not erase the original nonfiduciary adjudication or establish fiduciary responsibility during the earlier breaches. An unrestricted compensation demand does not become an authorized remedy merely by being called equitable.

**Limits and questions not reached:** ERISA's possible permission for veil piercing in an appropriately founded action is assumed without decision. No general ban on equitable monetary remedies or rule treating every unsuccessful federal merits theory as jurisdictionally defective follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mertens's bounded equitable-category holding and antecedent-action reservation remain. O'Melveny and Travelers supply no general federal collection rule, and the lower court's categorical federal-preemption rationale is not approved.

##### Whether ancillary enforcement jurisdiction permits a new full-debt action

**Holding:** Authority to identify, preserve and execute against a judgment debtor's assets does not authorize a later independent action imposing its entire debt on another person without an independent jurisdictional basis. Prior party status does not itself make that person a judgment debtor.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** The later conduct differs materially from the original pension violations and requires new facts, duties and defenses. Rule 69 supplies procedure, not original jurisdiction; the original 1987 action predates §1367's December 1, 1990 boundary, and the later case supplies no original claim to which supplemental jurisdiction can attach.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Contempt, asset discovery, ordinary attachment and garnishment, fraudulent-transfer process directed to debtor property and independently founded actions remain available. No particular asset restoration, general alter-ego prohibition, personal-jurisdiction or service holding, governing South Carolina enforcement rule, federal displacement or later-conduct preclusion determination is made. The preserved service objection is neither rejected nor waived; subject-matter jurisdiction alone supplies no nationwide service.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for dismissal of the later action and removal of dependent personal liability, interest, fees and expenses, including the appellate recalculation instruction. The vacated $205,182.01 combined assessment is not reinstated; the original $187,628.93 Tru-Tech judgment remains intact. Otherwise available state-law rights and independently founded actions are not extinguished.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kokkonen's independent-authority requirement and distinct decree-effectuation branch apply; Beecher limits creation of new obligations. Riggs and Labette County are distinguished because they enforced existing official duties rather than shifting the judgment debt.
