#### Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1859.
**Decided:** May 4, 1992.

##### Whether ordinary counsel negligence in developing state-court facts bars a hearing on a properly presented habeas claim

**Holding:** When a federal habeas claim properly presented to the state courts turns on material facts inadequately developed in state proceedings, Townsend’s fifth mandatory-hearing circumstance remains governed by its deliberate-bypass qualification: ordinary counsel negligence, without deliberate bypass, does not bar the necessary federal evidentiary hearing. Tamayo-Reyes is entitled to a hearing confined to whether the manslaughter mental-state element was adequately explained; the supported findings and denial of a new hearing concerning translation of the plea form and plea proceeding remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, and Kennedy. All five adopt this rule, its application, and the limited hearing remedy; five of nine participating Justices constitute a majority. No independently sufficient alternative holding or fractured-opinion analysis is necessary.

**Current-force modification:** The original hearing rule remains controlling under former law. AEDPA §104 adds §2254(e)(2): when an applicant has failed to develop a claim's factual basis in state proceedings, a federal evidentiary hearing is forbidden unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive to collateral review by this Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The enactment supplies an additional statutory constraint where applicable; it does not determine the meaning of failed to develop, equate counsel negligence with that trigger, or adjudicate the original remand's status. The April 24 source admission does not decide this amendment's temporal application to a particular pending proceeding; the original adjudication is unchanged.

**Material application:** Tamayo-Reyes presented the claim to the state courts, and the State identifies no independent and adequate procedural bar. Counsel’s deficient factual development was negligent, not deliberate; the missing facts concern explanation of the manslaughter mental-state element.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Supported findings and the denial of a new hearing about translation of the plea form and plea proceeding remain undisturbed. Coleman’s independent state claim-default rule, other Townsend hearing circumstances and §2254(d)’s distinct factual-presumption rules remain operative. No constitutional error or deliberate bypass on another record is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The limited hearing requirement is affirmed and remanded for a federal evidentiary hearing confined to the manslaughter mental-state explanation, followed by adjudication of that claim. No writ or unconditional release issues now. If the claim is proved and establishes an unconstitutional plea, a conditional writ must allow the State a reasonable opportunity for a lawful plea or trial process.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293 (1963): retained and applied; its fifth circumstance and deliberate-bypass qualification continue to govern inadequate factual development. Its other hearing grounds are not displaced.
- Fay v. Noia, 372 U.S. 391 (1963): its deliberate-bypass discussion remains operative through Townsend for this hearing question; the Court does not revive Fay’s displaced claim-default rule.
- Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722 (1991): distinguished; its cause-and-prejudice rule for independent and adequate state procedural defaults remains in force and is not extended to this hearing entitlement.
- Vasquez v. Hillery, 474 U.S. 254 (1986): relied on for the distinction between supplementing facts and fundamentally altering an exhausted claim; its deliberate-withholding reservation remains open.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §104, new §2254(e)(2), limits on evidentiary hearings. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

Source: `state/holdings/federal-courts.md`, heading `Schlup v. Delo`.

#### Schlup v. Delo

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7901.
**Decided:** January 23, 1995.

##### Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway

**Holding:** A prisoner seeking to overcome otherwise applicable successive or abusive habeas barriers to independent constitutional claims through innocence of the offense must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the probative record as a whole, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. This procedural gateway does not itself establish innocence, prove any constitutional violation or shift the ultimate burden on the underlying claims; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing rule remains confined to its distinct death-eligibility setting.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: applies and specifies its probability-based crime-innocence exception without changing cause and prejudice.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333: preserves the clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway and resolves the crime-innocence question that decision left open; it is not overruled or given a categorical impeachment exclusion.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467: preserves successive/abusive-claim barriers and the exceptional innocence route, without treating potential constitutional merit as sufficient to pass it.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436: uses the relevant plurality discussion of the whole probative record as persuasive guidance, preserving its actual authority limit.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307: distinguishes trial-record legal sufficiency from the likely effect of an augmented record on reasonable jurors; its sufficiency rule remains intact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every affidavit as true or treat the original verdict as a conclusive answer. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner. A mere possibility of acquittal, a juror who might disagree, ordinary constitutional-merits prejudice or the reviewing judge's personal reasonable doubt is insufficient. The reasonable juror fairly considers the evidence and follows the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt instruction.

##### This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission

**Holding:** The courts' use of the rejected clear-and-convincing crime gateway and reliance on the original jury's credibility function require vacatur and renewed assessment on this record; §2244(b)'s permission to refuse a qualifying subsequent petition does not itself supply an adequate independent ground to sustain that legally mistaken refusal. The Court remands through the Eighth Circuit to the District Court for application of the probability rule and a reasoned determination of necessary factual development, without deciding whether every successful innocence showing compels review, choosing a general appellate standard, or ordering a hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay automatically.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's statutory-refusal and remand portion, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth judgment vote without joining the separate §2244(b) rationale.

**Current-force modification:** AEDPA §106 replaces former §2244(b)'s refusal permission for successive §2254 district-court applications. A claim already presented must be dismissed. A new claim must be dismissed unless it rests on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence and facts that, if proved and considered with the whole evidence, establish by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt. Advance authorization by a three-judge court-of-appeals panel requires a prima facie showing; authorization does not remove the district court's duty to dismiss a claim unless the applicant proves the statutory requirements. This is an independent statutory condition, not a replacement of Schlup's probability gateway or a ruling that the gateway itself satisfies the new text. The original former-law remand stands. Pending-case application is not generally resolved; Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996, No. 95-8836, applies the post-enactment authorization-review bar while reserving whether §§2244(b)(1)–(2) directly bind original habeas consideration in this Court.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McCleskey, 499 U.S. 467: applies the distinction between conditional consideration of new, nonabusive grounds and legally constrained refusal of successive or abusive grounds; neither permission nor exception becomes an unconditional command.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1, and Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293: preserve necessary factual development within their properly presented, available-claim setting and the deliberate-bypass qualification; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a required hearing there, but no successive/abusive gateway is eliminated.
- Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: requires use of the selected crime-innocence rule before the present refusal can be sustained on its innocence assessment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2244(b) provides:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The prior-hearing alternatives are a factual evidentiary merits hearing or a legal merits hearing, each followed by a federal denial of release or other habeas remedy to a person held under a state judgment. Both unless conditions must be preserved: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent. The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner's burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate; cause and prejudice is not pressed in this review. Subsection (a)'s separate wording is not inserted into subsection (b), and no new subsection (c) predicate is established.

The videotape was trial evidence. Green's asserted prompt call, Faherty's movement account and other later identification accounts must be assessed with the existing record; the Court finds no witness truthful or false and does not find the gateway satisfied. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and a genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. Remand is not mandatory constitutional-merits review. Any necessary live testimony is for a reasoned determination under the applicable rules, not an automatic consequence of affidavits or vacatur. The merits of the suppression and ineffective-assistance claims, a freestanding innocence entitlement and any ultimate guilt determination remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit judgment is vacated and remanded through that court to the District Court for the probability gateway and a reasoned decision on necessary factual development. No gateway satisfaction, automatic hearing, constitutional-merits review, writ, retrial, release or stay is ordered.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §106, replacement §2244(b)(1)–(4) district-court successive-application mechanism; see also Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996, No. 95-8836, distinct original jurisdiction and reserved direct statutory reach. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

#### Herrera v. Collins, 506 U.S. 390 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7328.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### This record does not establish entitlement to federal innocence relief

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** Herrera's delayed and materially inconsistent affidavits, considered with the identification, physical and documentary evidence supporting his convictions, do not establish entitlement to the requested federal habeas relief or justify the district court's innocence-based stay, even assuming an exceptionally persuasive post-trial showing could constitutionally bar execution. This record-specific rejection establishes no uniform freestanding-innocence burden and does not decide whether such a constitutional claim exists.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, and White's express agreement with its reasons for finding this submission insufficient in his concurrence in the judgment: five Justices adopt this limited record conclusion. White does not join the plurality's broader discussion or an unspecified extraordinarily high threshold. No whole opinion commands five votes; no Marks synthesis establishes a common general test.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307 — trial-record sufficiency remains distinct; White uses its rational-factfinder formulation only as a minimum for an assumed new-evidence claim, without a five-Justice adoption of that general burden.
- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 — applied to require a substantial legal basis for an execution stay.
- Delo v. Stokes, 495 U.S. 320 — applied to the substantial-ground requirement on a second or successive federal petition.
- Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293, and Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, May 4, 1992 — preserved; necessary factual development of a properly available claim remains distinct from legal sufficiency, cognizability, exhaustion and abuse.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, June 22, 1992 — distinguished; its capital-eligibility gateway does not decide freestanding guilt innocence or supply its merits burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No controlling opinion recognizes or rejects a freestanding constitutional innocence claim or fixes its general burden. The undefined extraordinary threshold and White’s rational-factfinder minimum have no established logical-subset relationship under Marks. The hearing, exhaustion, abuse, Teague and distinct Sawyer capital-eligibility gateway rules retain independent operation. The federal habeas disposition does not determine the authority of a State to establish independent postconviction procedures for newly discovered evidence, actual innocence, or scientific developments undermining evidence relied upon at trial. Neither the existence nor the scope of such a state statutory remedy is before the Court. No general federal entitlement to a hearing upon any subsequent scientific disagreement is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm denial of the requested relief, 5–4; the vacation of the innocence-based federal stay remains. No credibility finding, innocence hearing, new writ, stay or execution date is ordered.

#### Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1030.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### A fair state opportunity does not itself bar Miranda habeas review

**Holding:** Powell’s prudential restriction on Fourth Amendment exclusionary claims does not extend to a state prisoner’s claim that the conviction rests on statements obtained in violation of Miranda. A full and fair state opportunity therefore does not itself foreclose that claim; ordinary habeas requirements, merits analysis and the governing harmless-error inquiry remain necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, I–II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Powell, 428 U.S. 465 — distinguished and not extended; its actual prudential scope remains.
- Miranda, 384 U.S. 436 — preserved on habeas for a conviction resting on improperly obtained statements.
- Kimmelman, 477 U.S. 365; Jackson, 443 U.S. 307; Rose v. Mitchell, 443 U.S. 545 — applied as limits on extending Powell to distinct trial protections.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104, and Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: independent voluntariness review remains available on its own predicates.

##### An unpleaded, unconsented coercion theory requires fair litigation

**Holding:** A habeas court may not grant relief on the independent postwarning involuntariness theory presented here when it was neither pleaded nor tried by express or implied consent and the State lacked an opportunity to present relevant evidence. Litigation of Miranda custody and warning issues does not by itself supply consent; any later presentation remains subject to ordinary pleading, exhaustion, retroactivity and other applicable requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: actual police coercion is necessary to due-process involuntariness; absent warnings alone do not establish it.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104 — preserved: voluntariness requires its own legal determination on the proper record.
- Teague, 489 U.S. 288 — reserved here; no new exception or merits resolution of the retroactivity objection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Miranda and due-process involuntariness are independent inquiries. Rule 15(b) consent is absent on this record; custody/warning litigation alone does not provide it. Coercion, exhaustion and Teague questions remain for any lawfully presented claim. No automatic writ, universal prophylactic-safeguard review rule or reopening of Powell-barred search claims follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm availability of Miranda review; reverse the independent postwarning voluntariness ruling for lack of pleading, consent and fair opportunity to litigate; remand for proper merits and remedial analysis.

#### Lindh v. Murphy

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6298.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application

**Holding:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments, including amended §2254(d), do not govern Lindh's noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The application-date construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case rule and the successive-petition provisions governed by Felker; it does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the statutory rule, its limits and reversal with remand.

**Material application:** The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 in §107(c), contrasted with its omission for chapter 153 enacted alongside it, supplies the temporal construction. Lindh filed his noncapital application on July 9, 1992.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary statutory construction may establish temporal direction without a prescribed formula. No universal substantive/procedural classification, permanent constitutional entitlement to filing-date judicial rules, chapter 154 State-qualification ruling, or decision on confrontation, bias, harmlessness, exhaustion or entitlement to a writ is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is reversed and must consider the appeal under pre-AEDPA habeas law; no writ is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf supplies the statutory-direction inquiry and Rivers its provision-specific operation. Felker is distinguished because its authorization-review proceedings began after enactment and its other Title I timing questions were reserved; its successive-petition and retained-original-authority holdings remain. Hughes Aircraft requires attention to the actual statutory change and does not displace the chapter comparison.

Source: `state/holdings/federal-courts.md`, heading `U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership, 513 U.S. 18`.

#### U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership, 513 U.S. 18

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-714.
**Decided:** November 8, 1994.

##### Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment

**Holding:** When a judgment has been lawfully brought before the Court for review, subsequent mootness bars adjudication of the underlying merits but does not eliminate the Court’s authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of that judgment and the pending proceeding. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not require vacatur merely because review has become moot.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Walling v. James V. Reuter, Inc.*, 321 U.S. 671 (1944), applies the distinction between inability to complete merits review and authority to make an appropriate disposition of the judgments before the Court.
- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), confirms §2106 dispositional authority when appellate merits review becomes unavailable; it does not make every available disposition mandatory.
- *Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America*, 511 U.S. 375 (1994), is distinguished: jurisdiction over a new settlement-enforcement controversy is different from disposition of a judgment already lawfully under review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither enforces the settlement nor supplies a ruling on bankruptcy priority, plan confirmation or the former automatic-stay dispute. The authority under §2106 to make an equitable disposition of a judgment lawfully before an appellate court differs from authority to reopen a completed judgment after the ordinary review process has ended. This decision does not create a new collateral reopening procedure, displace independently applicable Rule 60 requirements, or make a settlement agreement itself sufficient grounds for vacatur.

##### Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur

**Holding:** A party that voluntarily settles while pursuing review does not ordinarily establish equitable entitlement to vacatur of the judgment it sought to overturn; the movant must show a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances remain possible, but a settlement term calling for vacatur is not itself such a circumstance; Bancorp’s showing establishes none, so vacatur is denied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), preserves equitable relief when review is frustrated by happenstance; its actual refusal to erase an unchallenged judgment does not create automatic vacatur after settlement.
- *Karcher v. May*, 484 U.S. 72 (1987), extends its reason for denying vacatur after voluntary abandonment of review to the voluntary settlement here, while preserving its distinct party-capacity setting.
- *Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.*, 510 U.S. 27 (1993), remains a threshold decision that left settlement vacatur undecided. Its separate vacatur discussion supplies no earlier controlling rule.
- *Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc.*, 511 U.S. 863 (1994), remains a collateral-order decision; it does not establish a private right to erase a judgment following settlement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mootness caused by circumstances beyond the movant’s choice, including unilateral action of the prevailing party, remains distinct. No categorical ban on exceptional settlement relief or universal rule of collateral estoppel is adopted. No Rule 60(b) relief or remand to seek it is ordered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacatur is denied and the Supreme Court proceeding dismissed as moot. The Ninth Circuit judgment remains undisturbed without endorsement of its bankruptcy merits; no other order or underlying Chapter 11 proceeding is vacated or dismissed, and no settlement-enforcement or Rule 60(b) remand is ordered.

##### The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense

**Holding:** Procedural default and Teague nonretroactivity are distinct, nonjurisdictional limits, with no inflexible rule requiring one always to precede the other. In this case the Court decides the accepted Teague issue while reserving the disputed state-default predicates; that choice neither excuses default nor decides the constitutional merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State did not wholesale waive default. No jurisdictional defect is bypassed and neither universal default-first nor Teague-first sequencing is imposed.

##### This claim invokes an old guidance rule at November 24, 1986 finality

**Holding:** For Lambrix's judgment final on November 24, 1986, the claim that inadequate aggravator guidance in Florida's legally consequential capital jury recommendation can infect the final death sentence invokes an already dictated rule. Teague therefore does not bar this claim as new merely because the judge separately must exercise independent judgment; the ruling establishes no actual instructional defect, harmful influence or failed cure on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins. O'Connor joins once Teague is reached despite disagreeing with sequence; Stone-Zsela takes no position on this question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Judicial independence remains an additional protection, not proof that the influential jury recommendation disappears. No actual defect, harmful transmission or failed cure is found, no new Teague exception is adopted, and current comparison cases are not treated as available at 1986 finality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Godfrey's prefinality meaningful-guidance duty, applied through Teague/Caspari's precise comparison and Stringer's functional analysis, governs. Espinosa's influential-recommendation rule remains distinct from this finality question. Spaziano and Baldwin preserve judicial sentencing without automatic cure; Zant's nonweighing and Barclay's state-law invalidity settings do not authorize uncured unconstitutional influence.

##### Vacatur returns the preserved default defense for resolution

**Holding:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded for resolution of the preserved, unresolved state procedural-default defense and any applicable exception. If the state ground is adequate, independent, preserved and unexcused, relief must be denied on that ground; this common remand direction does not itself decide Teague, a constitutional violation or sentencing relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins at this common-remand scope.

**Material application:** Lambrix requested a limiting instruction at trial; the asserted state bar concerns failure to pursue the issue on direct appeal. State successive treatment of appellate-ineffectiveness claims and alternative merits rejection establish no federal cause finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Coleman and Ford require the actual independent, adequate, preserved and unexcused state ground; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway remains distinct from ordinary cause and prejudice and mere selection error.

##### The old-rule determination binds the subsequent proceedings

**Holding:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded with the new-rule ground conclusively rejected. The lower court must first resolve the preserved, unresolved procedural-default defense; if the claim is barred and unexcused it must deny relief on that ground, while an available claim proceeds to actual merits, effect and lawful correction subject to genuinely applicable unresolved statutory conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III-B, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes for the complete binding mandate. Stone-Zsela joins common vacatur but not this old-rule instruction.

If no lawful cure is available and relief is otherwise warranted, a constitutionally adequate new sentencing proceeding is required, but none is now ordered.

No present writ, hearing, sentence vacatur, guilt retrial, release, stay or execution authorization issues; unrelated claims are untouched.

#### Wright v. West, 505 U.S. 277 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-542.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

##### Whether the actual larceny trial record satisfies Jackson's constitutional sufficiency requirement

**Holding:** On the trial record here, the quantity and recency of West’s possession of distinctive items from the same theft, considered with the jury’s permissible rejection of his incomplete account, allow a rational trier of fact to find the elements of larceny beyond a reasonable doubt; the exhausted Jackson insufficiency claim therefore warrants no habeas relief. The reviewing court must assess the evidence and permissible credibility choices favorably to the prosecution, without retrying guilt, shifting the State’s burden or treating possession as an invariably sufficient substitute for proof of theft.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas and Scalia in the principal opinion, White in his express endorsement of its sufficiency demonstration, O'Connor with Blackmun and Stevens in their concurrence, Kennedy in his concurrence, and Stone in his separate concurrence expressly adopt this same record-specific Jackson proposition: eight Justices. Souter is excluded because his ground is Teague-only. No complete opinion commands a majority, and no broader rule is synthesized under Marks.

**Material application:** More than fifteen distinctive items from the same theft were found in West's residence two to four weeks afterward, and the jury could reject gaps in his incomplete flea-market account. Affirmative possession evidence and permissible credibility choices together sustain the inference; disbelief alone supplies no missing element. The offense threshold was $100 and the prior felony was credibility material, not propensity proof of this theft.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new corroboration requirement, proof quantum, burden shift or categorical possession-to-theft inference is adopted. The facial validity of every possession inference, an independent instruction claim, the Elkins new-evidence claim and a general habeas legal-review standard remain undecided. The broader separate writings do not establish a new Teague rule or replace independent review with general acceptance of a reasonable state decision; Teague's existing operation, Miller's legal/fact distinction and the then-existing §2254(d) remain. Cosby's contextual considerations do not become a constitutional five-factor test. A Teague bar and merits denial are not combined under Marks.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's direction to issue a writ on insufficiency grounds is reversed. Remand preserves other properly remaining claims, including the Elkins claim, under their own requirements. No new trial, acquittal, release, expansion of custody or disposition of all habeas claims is ordered.

**Material judgment:** All nine Justices support reversal and remand; eight expressly support the narrow sufficiency holding and Souter supports the judgment on his separate noncontrolling Teague ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307 — applied unchanged to the actual record with the prescribed weight for rational jury inferences.
- In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358 — retained; the prosecution must prove every element beyond a reasonable doubt and no burden shifts to West.
- Barnes v. United States, 412 U.S. 837 — permissive-inference principle retained as background, not an automatic equation of any possession with theft.

#### Lockhart v. Fretwell, 506 U.S. 364 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1393.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Loss of a displaced substantive rule is not cognizable prejudice here

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to obtain a favorable application of a substantive rule subsequently rejected does not establish Strickland prejudice where the lost benefit is one the governing law does not recognize and the omission otherwise deprived the defendant of no fair or reliable sentencing protection. This limited rule does not replace ordinary reasonable-probability analysis, excuse deficient performance, or require an additional showing of unfairness in the usual ineffective-assistance case.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the rule and its application to the Collins objection.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984): applied and clarified for loss of a subsequently rejected substantive rule; ordinary reasonable-probability analysis remains the usual test.
- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986): applied by analogy for the distinction between a changed outcome and loss of a legally protected benefit; no finding of perjury by Fretwell is made.
- Kimmelman v. Morrison, 477 U.S. 365 (1986): distinguished; a procedural bar on direct habeas consideration does not eliminate a meritorious underlying claim, unlike the substantive premise here.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231 (1988): applied for constitutionally sufficient narrowing at the guilt stage despite duplication at sentencing.
- Collins v. Lockhart, 754 F.2d 258 (8th Cir. 1985), and Perry v. Lockhart, 871 F.2d 1384 (8th Cir. 1989): Collins's displaced double-counting rule supplies no cognizable benefit; Perry's rejection of that rule supplies the accepted litigation premise. Whether Collins could independently be revived is not decided.

##### Teague does not preserve the displaced rule for this prejudice inquiry

**Holding:** Teague's limitation on new rules supporting collateral relief does not require a federal habeas court to disregard a subsequent legal correction favorable to the State when deciding whether an omitted objection caused cognizable Strickland prejudice. Performance remains assessed from counsel's perspective at the time, while the prejudice inquiry here does not preserve an entitlement to the rejected Collins rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this answer to the temporal objection.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 (1989): distinguished from an affirmative entitlement to an obsolete rule; its collateral finality limitation is not made reciprocal.
- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984): preserves the contemporaneous perspective for evaluating performance without mechanically fixing the prejudice inquiry to every favorable rule then available.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice remains the usual rule; no separate unfairness requirement is added in ordinary cases. Deficient performance is conceded. The Court does not revive Collins, decide the Solicitor General’s separate §2254(a) theory or determine the unresolved penalty-preparation claims. Thomas’s state/federal hierarchy observation is not a majority holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit grant of relief on the Collins-only ground. Unresolved independent claims retain their own treatment; no new sentence, stay or execution is ordered.

#### Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7358.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### The protected-silence error remains constitutional trial error

**Holding:** Using Brecht’s silence after Miranda warnings to impeach his trial explanation violates Doyle’s due-process protection; permitted impeachment with prewarning silence is analytically separate. This violation is trial error subject to harmless-error review, not a merely nonconstitutional rule or a structural defect requiring automatic reversal.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Doyle, 426 U.S. 610 — applied: warning-induced silence receives due-process protection.
- Jenkins, 447 U.S. 231, and Fletcher, 455 U.S. 603 — distinguished: prewarning impeachment remains permissible in the circumstances presented.
- Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279 — applied: this evidentiary trial error permits harmlessness analysis; structural defects remain distinct.

##### Chapman governs this preserved Doyle claim on habeas

**Holding:** For this preserved Doyle trial error properly before the federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict. Collateral posture alone does not replace that burden with Kotteakos; the reviewing court must apply Chapman to the whole record, with relief conditional on failure of the State’s showing rather than automatic from the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, II–III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman, 386 U.S. 18 — applied on federal habeas to this preserved Doyle claim with its burden unchanged.
- Kotteakos, 328 U.S. 750 — not extended to replace Chapman here; its existing nonconstitutional-error operation remains.
- Rose, 478 U.S. 570, and Yates, 500 U.S. 391 — continuity supported; prior assumed use is not misdescribed as a square holding on this distinction.
- Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa and Richmond — unchanged: actual correction of capital weighing errors remains distinct.
- Sawyer and Herrera — not extended into a universal innocence requirement for review of preserved trial error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Chapman ruling covers this preserved Doyle claim properly on habeas, not every constitutional collateral claim. Exhaustion, preservation, other habeas gates, structural-error doctrine and capital weighing-error correction remain distinct. The Court does not decide harmlessness or impose an innocence prerequisite.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Seventh Circuit judgment and remand for whole-record Chapman review with the State’s burden. Relief follows only if that showing fails; no automatic writ, release, acquittal or present retrial deadline.

**Later-authority backlinks:** O’Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995: extends Chapman review to the preserved personal-intent claim arising from the combined instructions and prosecutorial argument; the State bears the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden if a violation is found. The Doyle holding remains unchanged, and no general rule for all preserved constitutional trial errors is adopted. See Criminal Procedure — Constitutional trial error on habeas.

#### Davis v. United States, 512 U.S. 452

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1949.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### A reasonable possible present request requires neutral clarification

**Holding:** After a valid waiver, words that a reasonable officer would understand in context as possibly requesting counsel for the present interrogation require a pause in substantive questioning and limited, neutral clarification; a passing unrelated reference to a lawyer or speculation about an unexpressed wish does not. Officers may resume after a clarified voluntary choice to proceed, but must maintain the pause while a reasonable possible present request remains unresolved, and may not demand justification for counsel, promise benefits for continuing, discourage counsel, predict counsel's advice, or repeatedly pressure the suspect under the guise of clarification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt the mandatory clarification rule, its trigger and its limits; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas decline this part.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): develops its protection of an effective custodial counsel choice through the stated postwaiver clarification rule.
- Edwards v. Arizona, 451 U.S. 477 (1981): preserves cessation upon actual invocation; clarification addresses the antecedent genuinely uncertain choice.
- Smith v. Illinois, 469 U.S. 91 (1984): preserves its prohibition on using later answers to defeat an initially clear invocation and resolves the distinct ambiguity question it left open.
- Connecticut v. Barrett, 479 U.S. 523 (1987): applies its attention to the scope of the suspect's communicated choice, without requiring legal terminology.
- McNeil v. Wisconsin, 501 U.S. 171 (1991): preserves the need for an actual expression concerning custodial counsel and does not treat a different judicial-proceeding request as such an expression.

##### The actual clarification and ensuing statements were lawful

**Holding:** Following Davis's valid initial waiver and genuinely uncertain first reference to counsel, the agents lawfully paused offense questioning, neutrally clarified that he was not requesting counsel, and resumed questioning; their later brief reminder did not supply a new full warning or new written waiver. Admission of the challenged November 4 statements therefore supplies no ground to reverse the military judgment on this invocation theory, without using later answers to defeat an initially clear request.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this actual-clarification application and affirmance; the four Justices who reject a duty accept the permissibility of the clarification actually used.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connecticut v. Barrett: applies respect for the scope of the actual communicated choice to the credited clarification and disclaimer.
- Smith v. Illinois: distinguishes genuine clarification of uncertainty from impermissible later-answer erosion of a clear invocation.

##### A clear request requires cessation and cannot be undone by later answers

**Holding:** A clear request for custodial counsel stops police-initiated substantive interrogation unless counsel is present or the suspect initiates further communication and validly waives the protection under the governing rules; no prescribed legal formula is required. An initially clear request cannot be rendered ambiguous by later answers elicited in violation of that protection, and Davis's later clear request was honored by ending the interview.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this preservation and application of the clear-invocation rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Edwards v. Arizona: applies and preserves cessation, counsel-presence and suspect-initiation/valid-waiver requirements.
- Smith v. Illinois: applies and preserves the ban on undoing a clear initial invocation through later answers elicited in violation of counsel protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides postwaiver invocation in the Miranda/Edwards framework accepted by the parties and applied through the military rules. It does not independently resolve direct Fifth Amendment operation in military proceedings, Sixth Amendment attachment, prewaiver conduct, initial-warning adequacy, invalid initial waiver or the separate October 20 interview and evidence dispute. Section 3501 was not the Government's merits ground and supplies no alternative holding on construction, applicability or constitutionality. Withrow's collateral and coercion rules and Stansbury's objective custody inquiry remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court of Military Appeals is affirmed unanimously on the postwaiver invocation issue. No exclusion, new trial or remand follows; the separate October 20 dispute and any §3501 ground remain undecided.

#### O'Neal v. McAninch

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7407.
**Decided:** February 21, 1995.

##### Chapman governs the preserved personal-intent instructional claim

**Holding:** For a preserved constitutional trial violation, properly available on federal habeas, in which the combined jury instructions and prosecutor's statement permit conviction without proof of the defendant's own intent required for the offense, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt, on the whole record, that the error did not contribute to the verdict. Kotteakos's substantial-and-injurious-effect and grave-doubt framework does not govern this claim; the standard for other reserved categories is not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. The broader Part III extension has no controlling coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: extended from preserved Doyle error to the preserved personal-intent instructional/argument category; its original rule survives, while other reserved categories remain unresolved by the Court.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applied with the State's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden and actual-verdict contribution inquiry intact.
- Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946): not extended to replace Chapman for this constitutional claim; its existing operation for genuinely nonconstitutional trial error remains unchanged.
- In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358 (1970): applied as the constitutional requirement that the prosecution prove the elements of the charged offense; no new personal-intent element is imposed on offenses that do not require it.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana, June 1, 1993: preserved as the distinct rule for a defect vitiating the jury's governing reasonable-doubt standard; the assumed error here is not declared structural.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not adopt a general Chapman rule for all preserved constitutional trial errors. Proof of the violation, preservation, exhaustion, independent habeas gates, structural-error rules and a prejudice element defining another constitutional claim remain separate. Actual harmlessness is not decided.

**Later-authority backlinks:** California v. Roy, November 4, 1996, No. 95-2025: extends the State-borne Chapman rule to a preserved personal-intent omission without accompanying prosecutorial argument, while leaving a general extension to all constitutional trial errors unadopted.

##### The proper standard requires renewed review, not an automatic writ

**Holding:** The Sixth Circuit's substantial-influence assessment with a prisoner prejudice burden does not supply the required Chapman determination; its challenged judgment must be vacated for review of the combined claim under the correct rule. If an available constitutional violation is established and the State fails to prove beyond a reasonable doubt on the existing record that it did not contribute to the verdict, the writ shall issue for the affected conviction or convictions unless the State retries O'Neal within a reasonable time; no immediate writ or present retrial deadline is imposed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. The broader Part III extension has no controlling coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: its whole-record remand and conditional-relief distinction are extended to this instructional claim, without automatic reinstatement of a prior writ.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applied to the existing trial record; a weaker substantial-influence finding is not treated as the required finding beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Estelle v. McGuire, December 4, 1991, and Victor v. Nebraska / Sandoval v. California, March 22, 1994: their whole-charge reasonable-likelihood inquiry remains the rule for identifying an ambiguous instruction's constitutional defect; the Court does not decide that antecedent application here.
- Schlup v. Delo, January 23, 1995: its gateway for otherwise barred claims remains independent; neither passage through that gateway nor an innocence showing is invented for this claim.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is vacated and remanded, 7–2, with Chapman directions supported by five Justices. Review concerns the combined personal-intent instructional/argument claim, with District Court proceedings as appropriate. A lawful no-error ruling or a proper assumed-error/Chapman-harmlessness ruling permits denial of the writ. Otherwise the conditional writ issues for the affected convictions unless the State retries within a reasonable time; no present deadline, immediate release or reinstatement of the earlier conditional order is directed.

#### Kyles v. Whitley

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7927.
**Decided:** April 19, 1995.

##### Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction

**Holding:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses, considered in the whole record and in the defense’s preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. No item must independently satisfy that standard, and the defendant need not show acquittal more likely than conviction or that the remaining evidence is legally insufficient; the established suppression here meets the standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83: applies the duty to disclose material favorable evidence to the established suppression.
- United States v. Bagley, 473 U.S. 667: applies reasonable-probability materiality to the combined defense counterfactual, including lawful impeachment and preparation uses.
- Schlup v. Delo, January 23, 1995: distinguishes its gateway showing; it supplies no additional innocence prerequisite for this Brady claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The collective inquiry includes legitimate effects on preparation, corroboration, impeachment and examination of the investigation. It does not excuse lack of diligence about evidence already known to the defense, require open-file discovery or make every nondisclosure structural error.

##### The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team

**Holding:** A prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence known to police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires disclosure; personal ignorance does not remove that evidence from the materiality inquiry. This case-specific responsibility does not impute all knowledge of every government office or establish unrestricted investigative or open-file duties.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brady and Bagley: apply their trial-disclosure protection to information held by police acting for this prosecution.
- United States v. Williams, May 4, 1992: distinguishes its confined grand-jury disclosure and dismissal rules; those limits remain effective within their own setting.

##### A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing

**Holding:** Once this suppression is found material under Brady and Bagley, a second prejudice or harmlessness test may not defeat relief merely by reassessing whether the same nondisclosure affected the verdict. This holding rests on the prejudice built into the completed Brady violation and establishes no universal harmlessness rule for other habeas claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bagley: treats materiality as a defining element of the suppression violation, not an initial screen before a second duplicative prejudice test.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993, and O’Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995: preserve their actual category-specific holdings and distinguish them from claim-defining Brady materiality.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is reversed and remanded for a conditional habeas writ requiring release unless Louisiana retries Kyles within a reasonable time fixed by the District Court. No acquittal, immediate unconditional release, fixed number of days, penalty-only relief or adjudication of other claims is ordered.

#### Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7580.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality

**Holding:** Teague does not bar Graham's claim that Texas's submitted sentencing questions prevented effective use of his youth and unstable background as material mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated that functional requirement before his conviction became final on September 10, 1984; no new procedural-rule exception is created.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288, and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied; the relevant result must be dictated at finality, not merely supported by a later plausible extension.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — preserved as qualified approval; it does not immunize every application of the special issues.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied as the pre-1984 effective-mitigation rule.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied as recognition of that existing rule, not as law inserted retroactively into 1984.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992 — applied at its existing Teague scope; disagreement alone does not establish novelty.
- Franklin v. Lynaugh, 487 U.S. 164, and Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 — distinguished; evidence whose mitigating use fits the questions, and unrestricted sympathy, differ from material culpability mitigation excluded by them.

##### Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force

**Holding:** When the record supports a material mitigating judgment about culpability that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the submitted mechanism prevents that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Graham's youth at seventeen has that independent significance notwithstanding deliberate conduct or predicted future danger, and the three submitted special issues provide no adequate route for it here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied to youth's independent culpability significance; jurors may assign weight but cannot be legally prevented from giving it effect.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — its conditional framework remains; this defective application does not establish facial invalidity.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied beyond a permanent-handicap limitation; a mitigating trait need not have only aggravating relevance to future danger to require an effective vehicle.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 — applied as the reasonable-likelihood method of assessing instructional operation, not as the source of a new substantive rule at 1984 finality.

##### The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration

**Independent alternative holding:** A capital sentencing system may not prevent a jury from acting on the material culpability significance it finds in a defendant's disturbed upbringing merely because that evidence also bears on future dangerousness or lacks a uniquely severe permanent handicap. The evidence of Graham's mother's recurring mental illness and hospitalizations and his shifting family care supports such a mitigating judgment, which the submitted special issues cannot adequately express; this independently requires the same sentencing remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 — applied to relevant background proffered as a basis for less than death.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied; disturbed upbringing cannot be excluded from mitigating effect as a matter of law.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied; neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to the special issues is an exclusive trigger.

##### Positive character as an independent ground

**Authority of the judgment:** The separate contention that Graham's household assistance, religious observance and financial support of his children independently require additional sentencing relief is rejected by all nine Justices, on different grounds. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter regard its asserted independent culpability significance as insubstantial on this record and its future-behavior use as available under the second issue; White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would bar the requested mitigation relief under Teague. Stone-Zsela joins the denial of this independent component in the judgment only. These grounds do not yield a common general rule or a Marks subset; the youth and background holdings independently require sentencing relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule about positive character evidence or its value apart from the asserted culpability and future-danger uses is decided. The jury remains free to hear constitutionally relevant evidence at the new sentencing proceeding. Neither denial of this independent ground nor the sentencing remedy disturbs the murder conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere admission or some future-danger relevance is insufficient when supported culpability mitigation cannot receive effect. The ruling requires neither favorable weight nor a categorical fourth question, finds no psychiatric disability, and imposes no juvenile-execution prohibition or facial invalidity of the scheme. Texas’s later statute is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse sentencing relief denial on youth and background and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital replacement. Preserve the conviction; no automatic acquittal or immediate release.

#### Johnson v. Texas, 509 U.S. 350 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-5653.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### Youth’s independent culpability significance needs an effective sentencing route

**Holding:** Considering the charge, evidence and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood that the special issues prevented Johnson’s jury from giving effect to his youth-related reduced culpability when it truthfully found deliberateness and future danger. Under Graham, Eddings, Penry and Boyde, admission of the evidence and partial relevance to future danger do not supply the missing route; mere possibility of a restrictive reading would be insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, Youth’s independent culpability significance needs an effective sentencing route portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993 — independent youth-culpability holding applied as binding merits law.
- Eddings, 455 U.S. 104 — immaturity’s independent culpability significance applied.
- Lockett, 438 U.S. 586 — material mitigation cannot be legally excluded from effect.
- Penry, 492 U.S. 302 — admission distinguished from meaningful sentencing effect.
- Boyde, 494 U.S. 370 — reasonable likelihood, not mere possibility, applied.
- Jurek, 428 U.S. 262 — conditional facial approval preserved; this application fails.

##### Direct review permits the necessary sentence correction

**Holding:** Because this is direct review of the preserved mitigation claim, Teague does not bar relief. The death sentence must be reversed for an effective sentencing vehicle permitting the material youth mitigation to affect punishment without a false answer to a factual special issue; Texas may provide adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence, while the guilt judgment remains intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, Direct review permits the necessary sentence correction portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The whole-charge reasonable-likelihood standard, not mere possibility, governs. No categorical age bar, facial invalidation of Texas sentencing, right to favorable weight or unrestricted mercy instruction is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse only the death sentence and remand for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence. Guilt remains intact; no exact instruction wording is prescribed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague, 489 U.S. 288 — collateral-review restriction inapplicable to this direct appeal.
- Graham, January 25, 1993 — merits and remedy principles followed; collateral-review limitations do not bar this direct appeal.
- Jurek, 428 U.S. 262 — authority to structure sentencing retained subject to effective mitigation.
