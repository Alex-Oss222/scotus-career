## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which law governs a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims

### Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims

**Governing question:** The showing needed to overcome successive or abusive habeas barriers through innocence of the offense.

**Current rule:** The prisoner must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the whole probative record, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The inquiry includes the new evidence together with the existing record rather than deferring conclusively to the original jury’s credibility choices. Passing this demanding procedural gateway establishes neither innocence as a freestanding claim nor the underlying constitutional violation and shifts no ultimate merits burden. The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every submitted statement as true. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner.

**Authority by component:**

- Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway: Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway, 1995-01-23. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support a stricter crime-innocence quantum and would affirm.
- This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission: This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission, 1995-01-23. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, statutory-refusal and remand rationale, joined by Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this bounded rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth vote for vacatur and the same gateway-assessment remand on his stated ground, without joining the separate §2244(b) analysis. No aggregation of different rationales is needed for the five-Justice holding.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-23; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Sawyer’s clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway is distinct. Section 2244(b) permits refusal of a subsequent habeas application by a person held under a state-court judgment after a federal court, justice or judge denied release or another habeas remedy on an earlier habeas application following either an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue or a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, unless the later application alleges and rests on a factual or other ground not adjudicated in the earlier hearing and the court is satisfied that the applicant did not deliberately withhold the newly asserted ground on the earlier application or otherwise abuse the writ. Both exception conditions are required. New evidence is not automatically a new ground, and absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. When both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent, McCleskey requires consideration. The qualified refusal permission does not itself sustain refusal based on the rejected clear-and-convincing crime standard. Other lawful independent barriers and necessary factual-development rules remain.

**Burdens and proof:** The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner’s burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate.

**Open question:** Schlup does not decide whether every successful innocence showing compels review, a general appellate standard for refusal, or an automatic hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay.

**Related current rules:** Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims supplies the separate eligibility inquiry and proof quantum.

## Standards and Tests selection: Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility

### Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility

**Governing question:** Assessment and consequence of suppressed favorable evidence.

**Current rule:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses in the whole record, including defense preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. No item must independently meet the standard; the defendant need not show that acquittal is more likely than conviction or that remaining evidence is legally insufficient. The prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence held by police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires. Personal ignorance does not remove it from review. Once a material Brady violation is established, a second duplicative prejudice or harmlessness inquiry cannot defeat relief by reassessing the same nondisclosure.

**Exceptions and limits:** This case-team responsibility does not impute every government office’s knowledge or require unrestricted investigation or open-file discovery. ### Miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims

**Governing question:** What instructions govern miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims?

**Current rule:** A full and fair state opportunity does not itself bar federal habeas review of a claim that a conviction rests on statements obtained in violation of Miranda; Powell's Fourth Amendment exclusion restriction does not extend to that claim. ## Standards and Tests selection: Clarifying a possible present request for interrogation counsel

### Clarifying a possible present request for interrogation counsel

**Governing question:** What police must do after a valid waiver when a suspect may be presently asking for counsel.

**Current rule:** After a valid waiver, a statement that a reasonable officer would understand in context as a possible present request for counsel requires a pause in substantive questioning and neutral clarification. Unexpressed wishes and unrelated references to a lawyer do not trigger that duty. While the request remains unresolved, substantive interrogation may not resume; clarification may not discourage counsel through pressure, promises or repeated persuasion. A clear request requires cessation under Edwards, and later answers cannot retroactively erase it. Further interrogation then requires counsel or the suspect’s own initiation followed by a valid waiver.

## Standards and Tests selection: Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

### Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

**Governing question:** Harmlessness for preserved Doyle error and the preserved personal-intent instructional category properly available on federal habeas.

**Current rule:** Use of post-Miranda silence to impeach a trial explanation violates Doyle and constitutes constitutional trial error rather than a nonconstitutional rule or structural defect. Permitted prewarning impeachment remains distinct. For preserved Doyle error properly before a federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that it did not contribute to the verdict, considering the whole record. Collateral posture alone does not substitute Kotteakos; relief depends on failure of the State's Chapman showing and is not automatic. Other habeas gates and structural-error rules remain independent; this does not prescribe every collateral-review procedure. The same State-borne Chapman inquiry governs a preserved constitutional trial violation in which the combined jury instructions and prosecutor’s statement permit conviction without proof of the defendant’s own intent required by the offense. Kotteakos’s substantial-and-injurious-effect and grave-doubt inquiry does not govern that category. If an available violation is established and the State fails to prove that it did not contribute to the verdict, conditional relief must allow a reasonable retrial period for the affected conviction; identifying the wrong review standard alone does not establish a violation or command a writ. The personal-intent category also covers omission of the defendant’s required aiding purpose without an accompanying prosecutorial statement. Such omission is trial error, not automatically structural error. Necessary jury findings embracing the omitted purpose are sufficient but not the exclusive route to harmlessness: supported whole-record inferences may show no contribution to the actual verdict. The reviewing court must ask whether the instructions led the jury to consider the evidence supporting that inference. If the omission prevented reliable consideration of the personal-purpose question, strong evidence alone is insufficient. Knowledge plus assistance, general mental capacity, or evidence sufficient for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction cannot substitute for the required actual-verdict inquiry.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review

### Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review

**Governing question:** Whether mootness from voluntary settlement warrants vacating a judgment lawfully brought for review.

**Current rule:** Subsequent mootness prevents a merits decision but leaves authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of the judgment and proceeding. Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not establish entitlement to vacatur: the movant must supply a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances can warrant relief, but a settlement term requiring vacatur is not itself such a circumstance. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not command vacatur whenever review becomes moot.

Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.
- Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur: Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur, 1994-11-08. **Present operation effective:** 1994-11-08; the component authorities identified above.
