## Standards and Tests selection: Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, for the identified citizen-suit and procedural-right clarification; ordinary Article III elements and evidentiary burdens are retained.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

**Holdings navigation:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992) — Standing and personal injury.

## Standards and Tests selection: Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

### Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

**Governing question:** Whether an individual former employee's challenge to an employment rule remains live after departure.

**Current rule:** A prospective individual challenge becomes moot after voluntary departure from covered employment when no intent to return, realistic recurrence or surviving claim is established. A general prayer cannot preserve the controversy by producing nominal damages against a State or an official-capacity defendant whom §1983 does not make a damages person. Consent to payment cannot create the missing cause of action, and certification of an unsettled state-law question cannot supply missing Article III jurisdiction.

**Authority by component:**

- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona — A continuing personal controversy is absent, 1997-03-03; Stevens for all nine supplies the continuing-controversy and unavailable-damages limits
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona — Certification cannot restore the controversy, 1997-03-03; Stevens for all nine supplies the certification boundary

**Present operation effective:** Arizonans for Official English, 1997-03-03.

**Exceptions and limits:** This is no categorical express-pleading requirement for nominal damages and does not decide a legally available personal-capacity damages claim. Limited authority to correct lower-court jurisdictional error is distinct from merits authority; judgment vacatur remains equitable under the existing rule. No automatic initiative-sponsor standing or mandatory certification rule is established.

**Holdings navigation:** Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona (1997-03-03) — Federal Courts / Mootness, corrective jurisdiction and equitable vacatur.

## Standards and Tests selection: Article III power before disputed statutory merits

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

**Present operation effective:** Steel Co., 1997-06-02.

**Exceptions and limits:** This rule does not prescribe an exhaustive order among all nonmerits thresholds. Norton and Avrech retain their already-controlled-merits and distinct jurisdictional settings. EPCRA citizen-suit coverage of fully corrected reporting defaults remains undecided.

**Holdings navigation:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. (1997-06-02) — Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question.

## Standards and Tests selection: Privacy Act incorporation of FOIA in federal bargaining requests

### Privacy Act incorporation of FOIA in federal bargaining requests

**Governing question:** When a federal labor-information duty can compel personal-record disclosure through the Privacy Act’s FOIA exception.

**Current rule:** Section 7114(b)(4) operates only to the extent disclosure is not prohibited by law. For a record in a system of records, §552a(b) prohibits disclosure absent the subject’s written request or prior written consent or an applicable enumerated exception. Under §552a(b)(2), FOIA itself must require disclosure. Collective-bargaining usefulness does not enlarge Reporters Committee’s ordinary third-party public-interest inquiry. Exemption 6 balances concrete privacy against demonstrated incremental public understanding; it permits withholding rather than commanding secrecy, while the Privacy Act supplies the prohibition through this invoked route when FOIA does not require release.

**Authority by component:**

- Labor duty, Privacy Act trigger and incorporated FOIA inquiry: Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority — The labor duty incorporates the Privacy Act's FOIA exception without alteration, 1994-02-23. Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt this statutory coordination and precedent-based explanation.
- Distinct Exemption 6 permission and resulting Privacy Act prohibition: Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority — The added home addresses do not justify disclosure on this record, 1994-02-23. Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same record-specific balance and reversal of both enforcement components.

**Present operation effective:** Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, 1994-02-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** The labor duty concerns requested, normally maintained, reasonably available data necessary for full and proper bargaining discussion, understanding and negotiation, excluding management/supervisory bargaining guidance, advice, counsel and training. Exemption 6 covers personnel, medical and similar files and a clearly unwarranted invasion; Exemption 7(C)’s different law-enforcement threshold is not substituted. Scattered public availability does not eliminate dissemination privacy. Routine use, subject consent, other exceptions and a different demonstrated transparency use remain separate. Requester neutrality does not erase a protected person’s position when disclosure is opposed on a privilege ground.

**Holdings navigation:** The labor duty incorporates the Privacy Act's FOIA exception without alteration; The added home addresses do not justify disclosure on this record.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equitable restraint of officers acting beyond statutory authority

### Equitable restraint of officers acting beyond statutory authority

**Governing question:** Prospective nonmonetary relief against an implementing federal officer acting outside delegated authority.

**Current rule:** An injured person may seek prospective equitable restraint under federal-question jurisdiction against an officer implementing a restriction beyond delegated statutory authority unless Congress has displaced that remedy. Section 702 waives sovereign immunity for the nonmonetary official-capacity suit even without an APA review cause, while preserving independent legal and equitable limits and relief prohibitions in other consent-to-suit statutes. An injunction must identify the officer or successor responsible for compliance.

**Authority by component:**

- Chamber of Commerce v. Reich — Chamber of Commerce v. Reich — I. Review of the implementing officer, January 14, 1997; Kennedy, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Reich, January 14, 1997.

**Exceptions and limits:** No damages authority, direct injunction against the President or unrestricted review of discretionary presidential policy follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Chamber of Commerce v. Reich (1997-01-14) — Review of the implementing officer.

## Standards and Tests selection: No direct federal-agency Bivens damages action

### No direct federal-agency Bivens damages action

**Governing question:** Whether sovereign consent itself permits constitutional damages against a federal agency.

**Current rule:** A waiver removes sovereign immunity but does not create constitutional damages liability. Bivens’s individual-officer action does not extend directly to a federal agency: that extension would create a new defendant and public obligation. Independently, even assuming compensation and deterrence benefits, the expansion of direct federal fiscal liability and redesign of the remedial structure counsel against judicial creation of that agency action. Existing individual Bivens, Davis and Carlson actions and genuinely authorized statutory remedies retain their own scope; no adequate substitute remedy is presumed.

**Authority by component:**

- Separation of waiver from agency cause: FDIC v. Meyer — Waiver does not create a direct agency Bivens action, 1994-02-23. The cause-of-action portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this independently sufficient ground for rejecting the agency award.
- Independent fiscal-and-remedial reason against extension: FDIC v. Meyer — Independent reason to leave this fiscal and remedial extension to Congress, 1994-02-23. The independent fiscal-and-remedial portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Nine Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative ground.

**Present operation effective:** FDIC v. Meyer, 1994-02-23.

**Holdings navigation:** Waiver does not create a direct agency Bivens action.

#### Chamber of Commerce v. Reich

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 95-5242; no Supreme Court docket assigned.
**Decided:** January 14, 1997.

For the separate questions in Chamber of Commerce v. Reich, D.C. Circuit No. 95-5242; no Supreme Court docket assigned decided January 14, 1997, see Labor and Employment — Proprietary public construction contracts (The procurement delegation does not authorize this labor-policy sanction).

##### Review of the implementing officer

**Holding:** A person injured by an officer's implementation of a restriction outside delegated statutory authority may seek prospective equitable restraint under federal-question jurisdiction unless Congress has displaced that remedy. Franklin v. Massachusetts permits review of completed subordinate acts without resolving presidential APA status. Dalton v. Specter is distinguished by the preclusion structure of its completed base-closure scheme.

#### Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1424.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### What evidence of Article III injury, causation and redressability is required at summary judgment

**Holding:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury, a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, and a likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. At summary judgment, the plaintiff must supply specific facts by affidavit or other evidence on the challenged elements; favorable reasonable inferences remain available, but allegations cannot substitute for the missing evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the elements and litigation-stage evidentiary requirements. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining this section.

**Material application:** Earlier survival of the pleading stage does not establish the specific facts needed at summary judgment. The Court resolves this action on insufficient threatened personal injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general redressability element controls. Its application to absent funding agencies and uncertain foreign-project responses appears only in the three-Justice Part III-B and is not controlling. Favorable reasonable inferences remain available; the decision does not replace Rule 56 or the separate APA programmatic-review holding of National Wildlife Federation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The merits declaration, mandatory rulemaking relief and dependent §1540(g)(4) fee award are vacated, and the action is remanded for jurisdictional dismissal. Six Justices support dismissal for lack of standing; seven support reversal, with Stevens relying on a noncontrolling statutory ground. No independent fee-entitlement rule or substitute award is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975): applied for the plaintiff's personal stake and burden to establish standing; its statutory-rights discussion remains subject to concrete injury.
- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied for the constitutional elements and the limits on generalized judicial supervision.
- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976): applied for fair traceability and likely redress; no new majority application concerning these absent agencies follows.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied for the evidentiary distinction between pleading and summary judgment; its separate APA programmatic-review holding is not enlarged.
- Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986): applied to require evidence of challenged essential elements while preserving genuine material disputes and reasonable inferences.

##### Whether past visits, indefinite return intentions and general wildlife interests establish imminent personal injury

**Holding:** Aesthetic, recreational and professional interests in observing wildlife can support standing, but these plaintiffs must show threatened interference with their own concrete use. Prior visits and an indefinite intention to return, without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure, do not establish standing for these prospective claims; an ecosystem, animal or professional connection that supplies only general interest in distant species does not cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone's separate concurrence supplies a sixth vote for the record-specific personal-injury conclusion without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** The affidavits describe visits to Egyptian and Sri Lankan project areas but no sufficiently definite return undertaking or continuing activity showing imminent exposure. General ecosystem, animal and professional connections supply no missing evidence of these members' own threatened use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document prerequisite is imposed. Actual use of an affected ecosystem and demonstrated injury transmitted across geographic distance remain undecided. Aesthetic, recreational and professional harm remains cognizable when it affects the claimant's own concrete activity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jurisdictional dismissal leaves the statute's geographic reach unresolved and does not preclude a later action supported by sufficient standing facts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): applied; aesthetic injury is cognizable, but an organization's special interest cannot replace injury to a member.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977): applied to require an injured member for this associational claim; no other associational requirement is changed.
- Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95 (1983): applied for the distinction between past exposure and a present or imminent injury supporting prospective relief.
- Whitmore v. Arkansas, 495 U.S. 149 (1990): applied for actual or imminent rather than conjectural injury.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied to specific affected use; no categorical bar to geographically transmitted injury is established.
- Japan Whaling Association v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): distinguished because its whale-watching and studying members alleged an adverse effect on their own activities; concrete professional or ecological injury remains possible.

##### Whether ESA citizen-suit permission and an asserted procedural right replace threatened concrete injury

**Holding:** ESA §11(g)'s authorization for any person to sue does not give Article III standing to a plaintiff asserting only the public's common interest in lawful administration. A procedural right may protect a plaintiff's threatened concrete interest without requiring proof that the procedure will certainly change the ultimate agency decision, but the procedural label cannot replace that personal interest; these respondents have not shown the required connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the citizen-suit and concrete-interest rule. Stone does not join this section.

**Material application:** Section 11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), permits enforcement but identifies no concrete injury to these members independent of the unsupported future use. The public's common interest in lawful administration cannot supply the missing personal connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may recognize concrete factual injuries previously inadequate in law; the outer reach of that power is not settled. Procedural protections tied to a threatened concrete interest remain cognizable without certainty of a changed ultimate decision. Neither §7(a)(2)'s geographic reach nor the binding effect of a revised rule on absent agencies is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The action is dismissed for want of Article III standing; no new consultation rule or cancellation of any foreign project is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126 (1922): applied to reject a general right to compel lawful administration as a sufficient personal injury.
- Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447 (1923), and Ex parte Levitt, 302 U.S. 633 (1937): applied for the distinction between direct injury and an undifferentiated public grievance.
- United States v. Richardson, 418 U.S. 166 (1974), and Schlesinger v. Reservists Committee to Stop the War, 418 U.S. 208 (1974): applied to generally shared governmental grievances without distinctive concrete harm.
- Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United for Separation of Church and State, Inc., 454 U.S. 464 (1982), and Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied; a claimed right to lawful government conduct alone does not satisfy Article III.
- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), and Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): preserved for Congress's power to recognize injuries and broaden protected interests while retaining the plaintiff's own concrete injury.
- Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Insurance Co., 409 U.S. 205 (1972), and Hardin v. Kentucky Utilities Co., 390 U.S. 1 (1968): distinguished as statutory recognition of concrete personal interests, not authorization of a wholly abstract grievance.

#### FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-741.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### FTCA exclusivity does not encompass this federal constitutional claim

**Holding:** Section 2679(a) makes FTCA relief exclusive as against an agency's sue-and-be-sued authority only for claims cognizable under the whole of §1346(b), including its private-person and state-law requirements. Meyer's federal constitutional tort is outside that category, so §2679(a) does not bar this suit in the agency's own name; this statutory conclusion neither supplies a cause of action nor guarantees recovery.

**Proposition-level authority:** The statutory-exclusivity portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this separate threshold holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The §1346(b) category requires a claim against the United States for money damages for property injury or loss, personal injury or death caused by an employee's negligent or wrongful act or omission within employment scope, under circumstances in which a private person would be liable under the law of the place. That last requirement means state substantive law; §2679(b)(1)'s differently worded employee rule is not imported.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Richards v. United States, 369 U.S. 1: applies the FTCA's state-law source and private-person limitation.
- Miree v. DeKalb County, 433 U.S. 25: applies state law as the substantive source under the law-of-the-place provision.
- Loeffler v. Frank, 486 U.S. 549: preserves FTCA coordination while specifying that §2679(a) refers to cognizability under §1346(b), not everything described as tortious.
- United States v. Smith, 499 U.S. 160: distinguishes employee exclusivity under §2679(b)(1) and its treatment of the foreign-country exception from the differently worded agency provision.
- Hubsch v. United States, 338 U.S. 440: preserves cognizability despite failure of proof on an element; distinguishes a claim that never falls within §1346(b)'s category.

##### Former FSLIC's sue-and-be-sued clause waives immunity

**Holding:** Former 12 U.S.C. §1725(c)(4) waives FSLIC's sovereign immunity for this constitutional tort claim, even though a private entity ordinarily would not bear that constitutional duty. An implied restriction on its broad sue-and-be-sued authority requires a clear showing that the suit is inconsistent with the statutory or constitutional scheme, that restriction is necessary to avoid grave interference with governmental functions, or that Congress plainly intended the clause in a narrow sense; FDIC establishes none here.

**Proposition-level authority:** The waiver portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Nine Justices adopt the specific waiver and its stated limits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- FHA v. Burr, 309 U.S. 242: applies liberal construction and all three grounds for a clearly established implied restriction; mere absence of private constitutional liability does not establish one.
- Loeffler v. Frank, 486 U.S. 549: applies the broad waiver rule while keeping separate statutory limits distinct.
- Franchise Tax Board v. United States Postal Service, 467 U.S. 512: preserves private-business liability as support for the incidents of suit recognized there, without turning it into an outer boundary for every waiver.
- United States v. Nordic Village, February 25, 1992: preserves the requirement to identify the particular sovereign consent; the ambiguous bankruptcy provision there does not narrow this express agency clause.

##### Waiver does not create a direct agency Bivens action

**Holding:** A waiver of sovereign immunity removes a defense to suit but does not itself authorize constitutional damages; Bivens does not supply Meyer a direct damages cause of action against FSLIC. Extending an individual-officer action to the federal agency would create a distinct defendant and public obligation, rather than merely provide relief within an existing agency action.

**Proposition-level authority:** The cause-of-action portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this independently sufficient ground for rejecting the agency award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Mitchell, 463 U.S. 206: applies the analytical separation of sovereign consent and a substantive source authorizing relief.
- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: retains its individual-officer action and declines extension directly to a federal agency.
- Davis v. Passman, 442 U.S. 228: preserves the individual employment-discrimination action; it does not supply an agency defendant here.
- Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14: preserves individual-officer liability and its deterrent rationale without making every useful compensatory arrangement an authorized agency action.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992: preserves appropriate relief within an established private action; distinguishes creation of a new action and defendant.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming that a direct agency remedy could advance the compensatory and deterrent purposes of Bivens, the resulting expansion of direct federal financial liability and redesign of the remedial structure counsel against judicial creation of that remedy. Those institutional considerations independently require rejection of this implied agency damages action; the Court does not find that Meyer received an adequate substitute remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The independent fiscal-and-remedial portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Nine Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The $130,000 agency award cannot stand for want of an agency cause. Pattullo's separate immunity judgment is outside review and remains undisturbed. The Court does not decide Meyer's property entitlement, the underlying due-process violation, entitlement under another source, a universal federal waiver, or elimination of existing individual Bivens remedies. Former §1725(c)(4), rather than a silently substituted successor clause, governs this 1982 agency claim. Section 2679(b)(1)'s separate employee rule is not rewritten.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: applies its special-factors limitation to the proposed direct public liability, independently of the individual-defendant distinction.
- United States v. Standard Oil Co. of California, 332 U.S. 301: applies the concern against judicial determination of a new federal fiscal obligation.
- Bush v. Lucas, 462 U.S. 367: applies institutional restraint concerning a new damages remedy; does not find that Bush's civil-service scheme governed or compensated Meyer.
- Schweiker v. Chilicky, 487 U.S. 412: applies caution about remedy creation despite incomplete compensation, without importing its Social Security system as Meyer's actual remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The agency damages judgment is reversed and the $130,000 award cannot stand for want of a direct agency cause. Pattullo's separate, unreviewed immunity judgment remains undisturbed. No constitutional merits finding or new damages trial is ordered.

#### Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-974.
**Decided:** March 3, 1997.

##### Whether this former employee retains a live challenge to the employment rule

**Holding:** An individual prospective employment-rule challenge becomes moot when the employee voluntarily leaves covered employment without an intention to return, realistic recurrence or a surviving claim. A general relief request cannot manufacture nominal damages against a State or official-capacity defendant that is not a damages person under §1983.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous; all nine Justices join every holding.

**Material application:** No class or replacement claimant remained, and the dismissed individual-capacity claims were not preserved on appeal. State nonpersonhood under §1983 is a statutory bar distinct from waivable Eleventh Amendment immunity; a proposed consensual payment cannot supply the missing cause of action. The Attorney General’s constitutional participation under §2403(b) did not overturn the denial of State party status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general requirement to plead nominal damages by name is established. A legally available individual-capacity claim presents a different question. The Court does not fix the precise April 1990 date when mootness attached.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Preiser requires a controversy throughout review. Will and Hafer distinguish the unavailable State or official-capacity damages claim from genuine individual-capacity liability.

##### Whether limited corrective jurisdiction permits vacatur without deciding sponsor standing or constitutional merits

**Holding:** The Court may correct a lower federal court’s jurisdictional error without exercising constitutional merits jurisdiction. On the equities here, that authority requires vacating the appellate judgment and directing vacatur of the District Court declaration and dismissal for mootness, without resolving ultimate initiative-sponsor standing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The prevailing plaintiff ended the covered employment during review efforts; this was not settlement or abandonment by a reviewing loser. The later appellate merits adjudication and absence of a lawful damages residue justify clearing both unreviewable constitutional judgments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2106 does not independently create merits jurisdiction. Intervention, an appeal notice or political commitment does not confer automatic sponsor standing. The original declaration is not declared void when entered, and the Court resolves neither every intervention ruling nor Article XXVIII’s validity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate both merits judgments and dismiss the action as moot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bender and Corrick supply limited correction of jurisdictional error. Lujan and Brown Group retain constitutional injury and lawful representation requirements. Bancorp requires equitable rather than automatic vacatur; Munsingwear and Anderson support judgment clearing within that inquiry.

##### Whether state-law certification can revive a moot federal action

**Holding:** Certification of unsettled state law can assist a live federal controversy but cannot cure the absence of Article III jurisdiction. No certification is ordered in this moot case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The meaning of Article XXVIII and any constitutional challenge in future live litigation remain open. No mandatory certification requirement is adopted.

#### Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136; review of 90 F.3d 1237.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question

**Holding:** A federal court cannot assume disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim. Section 11046(c) describes enforcement and relief without making every statutory liability element jurisdictional; the holding preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences and does not prescribe an exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins, including O'Connor and Kennedy's nonexhaustive sequencing reservation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether EPCRA authorizes suit over reporting violations cured before filing remains undecided. Norton and Avrech retain their distinct settings involving already-controlled merits.

##### Requested relief cannot redress this completed informational deprivation

**Holding:** Assuming past informational injury and traceability, Article III requires requested relief likely to repair that injury or prevent a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. When reports were current before suit and no continuing or imminent violation is alleged, a declaration of past lateness, Treasury penalties, monitoring, future report copies and litigation expenses supply no such redress.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support dismissal on statutory grounds without joining this constitutional rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than rejects informational injury. Standing is absent at commencement, rather than lost by later cessation. Deterrent penalties may redress a supported threatened injury; annual reporting duties alone do not allege one. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party costs on a final order, not independent compensation. A properly presented replacement-information loss claim, monitoring in a live action, recoverable prefiling expenses, associational requirements and statutory temporal reach remain open. Section 11022(f) gives the fire department no transferable automatic inspection entitlement for this plaintiff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand with directions to dismiss, unanimously as to judgment. No merits relief, standing discovery, amendment entitlement or newly inferred recurrence inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to distinguish a substantial federal claim from its ultimate success; a contested statutory element is not automatically jurisdictional.
- **Young v. Northern Illinois Conference:** Applied at that same distinction; its constitutional-defense holding is not expanded into permission to assume Article III.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Its controlling standing elements and stage-appropriate support remain governing. Its three-Justice absent-agency application is neither enlarged nor needed.
- **Church of Scientology v. United States:** Meaningful partial relief remains enough; no effectual relief of that kind is available on these allegations.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** Congress's representative-enforcement choice does not eliminate personal injury and likely redress.
- **Bennett v. Spear:** Supported pleading-stage causal and remedial links remain sufficient; its ongoing constraint differs from the completed reporting deprivation here.
- **Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona:** Applied for the live-controversy requirement and limited corrective authority to vacate without reaching merits.
- **Norton v. Mathews; Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech:** Their already-controlled merits and distinct jurisdictional settings remain intact; neither decides this unresolved EPCRA dispute.
- **Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation:** Its different statutory wording and enforcement scheme do not settle the merits question the Court leaves open.
- **Meghrig v. KFC Western:** Its RCRA cause-and-remedy limits remain statute-specific; it supplies no automatic EPCRA current-violation rule.

#### Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, 510 U.S. 487

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1223.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### The labor duty incorporates the Privacy Act's FOIA exception without alteration

**Holding:** Section 7114(b)(4)'s duty to supply qualifying bargaining data operates only to the extent disclosure is not prohibited by law; when the asserted exception to the Privacy Act is §552a(b)(2), the question is whether FOIA itself requires disclosure. Retained Reporters Committee precedent governs the ordinary third-party public-interest inquiry, so collective-bargaining utility alone does not enlarge the incorporated FOIA balance; the Court does not decide that this precedent's restriction was inevitably compelled by FOIA's original text.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt this statutory coordination and precedent-based explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The labor duty concerns requested data normally maintained by the agency in its regular course of business, reasonably available and necessary for full and proper discussion, understanding and negotiation of subjects within collective bargaining, and not guidance, advice, counsel or training provided for management officials or supervisors relating to collective bargaining. These conditions are not independently disputed or newly adjudicated here. For a record contained in a system of records, §552a(b) bars agency disclosure absent a written request by, or prior written consent of, the individual to whom it pertains, or an applicable enumerated exception. A union request is not the subject individual's request or consent. Routine use under §552a(b)(3) and other exceptions are distinct and undecided; their applicability is not guaranteed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press: retained and applied as statutory precedent for ordinary third-party public-interest analysis and requester neutrality; its original-text justification is not newly endorsed as inevitable, and requester identity can still matter when disclosure is opposed on a privilege ground and the requester is the person protected by that privilege, a situation absent here.
- Department of the Treasury, IRS v. FLRA: applied for respecting enacted limits on labor-agency authority; the uncontested bargaining-necessity determination is not reconsidered.
- Department of State v. Ray: preserved; the incorporated Exemption 6 inquiry remains a concrete balance, not a rule that another statute's useful purpose automatically establishes FOIA-required disclosure.

##### The added home addresses do not justify disclosure on this record

**Holding:** On these requests, official disclosure of employee home addresses would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy under Exemption 6 because the real interest in controlling dissemination of home locations outweighs the absent or negligible demonstrated addition to public understanding after names and work stations were supplied. Exemption 6 permits withholding and FOIA therefore does not require disclosure; the Privacy Act supplies the resulting prohibition against compelling release through the invoked §552a(b)(2) route, without deciding a different demonstrated transparency use, routine use, subject consent or another exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same record-specific balance and reversal of both enforcement components.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exemption 6 applies to personnel, medical and similar files whose disclosure would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy; the requested employee-address records fall within that category. Exemption 7(C) instead concerns information compiled for law-enforcement purposes and disclosures that could reasonably be expected to constitute an unwarranted invasion; that different standard is not used here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of State v. Ray: applied for concrete privacy injury and demonstrated incremental public understanding; its confidentiality assurance is not a prerequisite or automatic exemption, and no general bar to derivative benefits or prior-wrongdoing requirement is added.
- Department of the Air Force v. Rose: applied; disclosure remains the general rule, with the enacted Exemption 6 privacy balance preserved.
- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press: applied to the relevant public interest and continued control over officially disseminated personal information despite scattered public availability; its rap-sheet sensitivity and Exemption 7(C) threshold do not replace the different record or Exemption 6 standard here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Enforcement of both the Gulfport and Lowry disclosure orders is reversed on the asserted §552a(b)(2) ground. No new necessity determination, constitutional privacy remedy or approval of a routine use is ordered; other disclosure routes must meet their own requirements.

§702. Right of review
A person suffering legal wrong because of agency action, or adversely affected or aggrieved by agency action within the meaning of a relevant statute, is entitled to judicial review thereof. An action in a court of the United States seeking relief other than money damages and stating a claim that an agency or an officer or employee thereof acted or failed to act in an official capacity or under color of legal authority shall not be dismissed nor relief therein be denied on the ground that it is against the United States or that the United States is an indispensable party. The United States may be named as a defendant in any such action, and a judgment or decree may be entered against the United States: Provided, That any mandatory or injunctive decree shall specify the Federal officer or officers (by name or by title), and their successors in office, personally responsible for compliance. Nothing herein (1) affects other limitations on judicial review or the power or duty of the court to dismiss any action or deny relief on any other appropriate legal or equitable ground; or (2) confers authority to grant relief if any other statute that grants consent to suit expressly or impliedly forbids the relief which is sought.

## Constitutional chill and concrete reputational injury

Laird v. Tatum, 408 U.S. 1, 13–14 (1972), rejects subjective chill as a substitute for specific present objective harm or a supported threat; it does not adjudicate this later-created statutory privacy right. [Primary reporter](https://static.case.law/us/408/html/0001-01.html); local `research/chunk3_late/laird_cap.txt`.

Meese v. Keene, 481 U.S. 465, 473–76 (1987), recognizes supported reputational/electoral injury from a public governmental label. Stevens wrote for Rehnquist, White, Powell, O’Connor and himself; Scalia did not participate. Its detailed affidavits and public-label mechanism distinguish merely conjectured third-party knowledge. [All primary writings](https://static.case.law/us/481/html/0465-01.html); local `research/chunk3_late/meese_cap.txt`.
