## Standards and Tests selection: Federal lien notice and after-acquired property priority

### Federal lien notice and after-acquired property priority

**Governing question:** What instructions govern federal lien notice and after-acquired property priority?

**Current rule:** A filed federal tax lien has priority over an earlier docketed judgment lien in land acquired only after federal notice, because the judgment lien becomes choate in that land upon acquisition. Both attach on acquisition; priority follows the federal notice rule, not fictional prior attachment. Existing-property choateness and other statutory creditor protections remain distinct.

**Authority by component:**

- Federal notice precedes choateness in after-acquired property: United States v. McDermott — Federal notice precedes choateness in after-acquired property, 1993-03-24. Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-03-24; United States v. McDermott, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. McDermott, 507 U.S. 447 (1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal income-tax duties of corporate assignees and separate liquidating trusts

### Federal income-tax duties of corporate assignees and separate liquidating trusts

**Governing question:** Who files and pays income tax after a bankruptcy plan transfers corporate property or property into a separate liquidating trust, and whether plan silence excuses later duties.

**Current rule:** Under §6012(b)(3), an assignee holding all or substantially all of a corporation's property must file its income-tax returns whether or not the corporation dissolves or the assignee operates the business; §6151(a) requires payment of tax due with the returns. A separate liquidating trust organized to liquidate and distribute transferred property has its own filing obligation under §6012(b)(4), carried out by a fiduciary whose powers satisfy §7701(a)(6) and Regulation §301.7701-6; restrictions on discretion alone do not eliminate fiduciary status. When estate property passes directly into the separate trust without revesting in the debtor followed by a debtor contribution, the asserted grantor-trust theory does not displace that duty. Confirmation under §1141(a) of a plan silent about taxes and government failure to object do not exempt the assignee or fiduciary from statutory return and payment obligations arising after appointment.

**Authority by component:**

- Corporate assignment category and accompanying payment: Holywell Corp. v. Smith — Corporate assignee's filing and payment duty, February 25, 1992.
- Separate liquidating trust, fiduciary status, and direct-transfer boundary: Holywell — Fiduciary of the separate liquidating trust, February 25, 1992.
- No prospective tax exemption from silent confirmation: Holywell — A silent confirmed plan does not excuse prospective tax duties, February 25, 1992. Thomas's unanimous opinion controls all three components.

**Present operation effective:** *Holywell*, February 25, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Grantor-trust provisions retain force where their actual predicates exist. This rule does not prescribe identical duties for every bankruptcy trustee or decide taxes already due before appointment, express adjudication of existing tax claims, amounts, penalties, deductions, or collection details.

**Holdings navigation:** Holywell Corp. v. Smith, 503 U.S. 47 (1992) — Bankruptcy fiduciaries’ filing and payment duties.


## Verbatim authority from state/holdings/federal-taxation.md

#### Holywell Corp. v. Smith, 503 U.S. 47 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1361 and 90-1484.
**Decided:** February 25, 1992.
**Companion or consolidated matters:** *United States v. Smith*, No. 90-1484, consolidated with *Holywell*; both challenge the declaration of no filing or payment duty.

##### Whether the assignee of substantially all corporate property must file corporate returns and pay the associated tax

**Holding:** An assignee holding all or substantially all of a corporation’s property must make its income-tax return under §6012(b)(3), whether or not the corporation dissolves or the assignee continues its business. Smith’s receipt of the corporate estate property satisfies that category, and §6151(a) requires payment of the tax due with those returns.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous statutory construction.

**Material application:** The plan assigned substantially all corporate property to Smith for liquidation and distribution. The filing duty follows the statutory assignee category and does not depend on operating the business or dissolving the corporations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No alternative statutory classification, exact income, deduction, penalty or tax amount is decided. The duties concern returns and accompanying payment after appointment, not taxes already due before appointment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand in both dockets for Smith to file the prescribed corporate returns as assignee and pay the tax due with them under §6151; amounts and implementation are left to ordinary tax and bankruptcy procedures.

##### Whether Smith must file and pay as fiduciary of the separate liquidating trust holding Gould’s former estate property

**Holding:** Smith must file the separate liquidating trust’s return under §6012(b)(4) and pay the associated tax under §6151: the plan vested Gould’s estate property directly in a separate trust organized to liquidate and distribute the transferred assets and authorized Smith to perform those functions, making him its fiduciary despite limits on his discretion. Because the property did not first revest in Gould for contribution to the trust, the asserted grantor-trust theory does not displace Smith’s duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous trust-specific construction and application.

**Material application:** The plan created a separate trust to liquidate and distribute transferred assets within Treasury Regulation §301.7701-4(d); Smith’s powers satisfy §7701(a)(6) and Regulation §301.7701-6 despite limits on his discretion. The estate transferred Gould’s property directly to the trust; Gould did not first recover and contribute it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal tax treatment for all liquidating trusts is adopted. Grantor provisions retain their operation when their predicates exist; the result of a different revesting-and-contribution arrangement is not decided. The Court computes no tax amount.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand in both dockets for Smith to file as trust fiduciary and pay the associated tax, with computation and implementation left to ordinary procedures.

##### Whether confirmation of a plan silent about taxes and the United States’ failure to object excuse later statutory duties

**Holding:** Confirmation under §1141(a) of a plan silent about taxes, coupled with the United States’ failure to object, does not excuse the statutory return and payment obligations imposed on this assignee and trust fiduciary after appointment. The binding treatment of properly encompassed preconfirmation claims does not establish an exemption from these later obligations.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous rejection of the confirmation defense.

**Material application:** The government seeks recognition of Smith’s postappointment duties, not payment from him of taxes already due before appointment. The plan neither stated nor adjudicated an exemption; §6151 connects payment to the required return’s due date.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes, without deciding more broadly, that confirmation can bind creditors concerning properly encompassed preconfirmation claims. Preappointment taxes and express adjudication of existing tax claims remain outside the disposition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The no-duty declaration is reversed in both dockets; confirmation supplies no defense to implementation of the postappointment filing and payment obligations.


## Verbatim authority from state/holdings/federal-taxation.md

#### United States v. Hill, 506 U.S. 546 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1421.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Mineral-interest basis under former §57(a)(8)

**Holding:** For tax years governed by former § 57(a)(8), the adjusted basis offset against percentage depletion is the adjusted basis of the taxpayer’s § 614 interest in the mineral deposit, determined without that year’s depletion deduction; it excludes separately depreciable tangible drilling and development improvements identified in Treasury Regulation § 1.612-4(c)(1). Lawfully capitalized intangible costs recoverable through depletion remain in depletable basis; this classification does not erase distinct statutory provisions governing other costs or later taxable years.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s opinion for the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices. All nine Justices adopt this rule and its explanation; it controls by a direct majority.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for liabilities governed by former §57(a)(8), including the 1981–1982 years adjudicated. The Energy Policy Act of 1992, Pub. L. 102-486 §1915(a), (d), enacted October 24, 1992, removes §613A(c) depletion deductions from amended §57(a)(1) for taxable years beginning after December 31, 1992. Its coordinated §56(g)(4)(F)(ii) exception likewise removes those deductions from clause (i) and subparagraph (C)(i) for those taxable years; clause (i)’s general cost-depletion rule retains its separate condition for property placed in service in a taxable year beginning after December 31, 1989. The holding does not supply a contrary rule for the amended years.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Energy Policy Act of 1992, Pub. L. 102-486 §1915(a), (d), 106 Stat. 3023–3024; enacted October 24, 1992; applicable to taxable years beginning after December 31, 1992; prospective depletion-preference and adjusted-current-earnings exceptions. This is noncase-law scope information, not a judicial holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- INDOPCO, Inc. v. Commissioner, February 26, 1992, left undisturbed and distinguished by issue: capitalization can be required without creating a separate asset; Hill concerns allocation among already distinct depreciation and depletion accounts. No restrictive separate-asset prerequisite is adopted.
The controlling classification follows the coordinated statutory and regulatory text. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled or made into a general rule against multiple tax benefits. Technical Advice Memorandum 8314011 supplies no precedent under then-§ 6110(j)(3); its § 616 deferred-development setting also has an express basis direction different from the costs here. The memorandum is not a judicial holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The former-law classification does not combine depreciation and depletion accounts because assets are commercially integrated. Properly capitalized depletable intangible costs remain in mineral basis; no general ban on multiple tax benefits is adopted. Technical Advice Memorandum 8314011 is nonprecedential and its §616 provision has a distinct express basis direction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Federal Circuit and remand for implementation of the mineral-interest basis calculation for the 1981–1982 liabilities. The prospective 1992 amendment does not govern those years; no new factual hearing is directed.


## Verbatim authority from state/holdings/federal-taxation.md

#### United States v. McDermott, 507 U.S. 447 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1229.

**Decided:** March 24, 1993.

##### Federal notice precedes choateness in after-acquired property

**Holding:** A filed federal tax lien takes priority over a previously docketed judgment lien in land acquired only after federal notice, because the judgment lien becomes choate in that land only upon acquisition. Both liens attach when the debtor acquires the property; priority here follows the federal notice rule, not fictional earlier attachment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- New Britain, 347 U.S. 81 — applied: choateness requires established lienor, amount and property.
- Glass City Bank, 326 U.S. 265 — applied: federal liens reach after-acquired property upon acquisition.
- Vermont, 377 U.S. 351 — distinguished: no separate levy is required for a lien already fixed in existing property; future acquisition remains different.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The priority concerns the September 23 land and substituted proceeds; statutory protections for other creditors and financing interests remain. An asserted earlier contract interest was not preserved within this dispute and is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Tenth Circuit and implement federal-lien priority in the escrowed proceeds; no unrelated lien or property is adjudicated.
