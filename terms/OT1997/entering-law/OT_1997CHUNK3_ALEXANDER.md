## Standards and Tests selection: Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation

### Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation

**Governing question:** Whether a legal obligation regulating previability abortion impermissibly interferes with the patient's protected decision or access to timely, clinically appropriate care.

**Current rule:** Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

**Authority by component:**

- Protected previability choice, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health protection: Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, “The framework retaining protected choice”, June 29, 1992; Stone's opinion joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter.
- All six steps, including the independent obstacle bar, residual severe-interference scrutiny, meaningful lesser-burden substantiation, proof allocation, clinical safeguards and relief: *Casey*, “The standard governing previability regulation after this decision”; Stone's opinion joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. These five direct joins supply the whole rule; no *Marks* synthesis is needed.
- Integrated displacement of the independent trimester timetable: *Casey*, “The trimester timetable and the timing of regulation”; the same five-Justice rationale. The other four votes against continuing the timetable do not join the replacement framework.

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Burdens and proof:** Challengers establish the duty, population, causation and triggering interference. The State then bears the applicable justification and tailoring or fit burden. This does not alter ordinary proof of disputed historical facts or the enacted burden for a separate statutory defense. No State substantiation burden arises merely because a law concerns abortion.

**Exceptions and limits:** Gestational age may support a particular medically justified safety requirement; crossing a trimester boundary does not itself change the standard. The rule creates no general professional immunity, funding entitlement, constitutional fetal-personhood rule, conscience ruling, general right to a particular drug, or universal numerical facial-review formula. It does not decide every postviability restriction.

**Material development:** The independent trimester timing restrictions are displaced only with adoption of this complete substitute. Protected choice, viability and necessary life-and-health care survive. Absence of a substantial obstacle no longer ends the inquiry into demonstrated meaningful interference.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent.

## Standards and Tests selection: Spousal notification that gives practical control over protected choice

### Spousal notification that gives practical control over protected choice

**Governing question:** Whether compulsory spousal notice and certification may condition an adult patient's protected previability decision.

**Current rule:** A notification requirement is invalid when its proved operation gives spouses practical control over protected decisions for a large fraction of the independently defined women compelled to disclose against their wishes, after accounting for usable exceptions. Voluntary consultation by other patients does not cure that restriction. Identify the compelled group before deciding injury; do not count willing disclosure as a constitutionally valid restrictive application or impose a universal numerical threshold. Formal notice rather than consent does not answer practical prevention. Severance and relief against dependent certification, enforcement and notice-compliance reporting must address the unconstitutional duty; a separately lawful confidential demographic field does not fall merely because it shares a subsection.

**Authority by component:**

- Effective control, affected-group review and notice-specific facial qualification: Casey, “Spousal notice and effective control over the protected decision”, June 29, 1992; Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter directly support the rationale.
- Dependent reporting versus independently severable demographic information: *Casey*, “Notice-compliance and nonnotification-reason reports” (the same six) and “The independently severable marital-status field” (all nine).

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** The invalidated §3209 allowed substitute signed statements that the spouse was not the father, could not be located after diligent effort, that pregnancy resulted from spousal sexual assault under §3128 reported to an agency with jurisdiction, or that the woman had reason to believe notification was likely to cause bodily injury to her by her spouse or another person; medical emergency separately excused compliance. The incorporated §3128(c) condition required personal reporting by the victim or her agent within 90 days of the assault. Statements required no notarization but had to warn against punishable false statements. These exceptions did not generally reach psychological coercion, financial retaliation, harm to children or others, exposure of private information, or divorce and custody threats. The holding does not independently adjudicate §3128(c)'s criminal-law reporting requirement. That reporting condition does not limit the distinct rape-omission permission for paternal-support information.

**Related current rules:** “Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation” supplies the five-Justice independently dispositive obstacle rule. Kennedy joins this notice-specific rationale without joining that whole standard.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Spousal control of reproductive choice.

## Standards and Tests selection: Medical emergency and the separate disclosure-health defense

### Medical emergency and the separate disclosure-health defense

**Governing question:** How Pennsylvania's emergency, emergency-explanation and harmful-information provisions operate independently.

**Current rule:** Section 3203 defines emergency through a physician's good-faith clinical judgment that immediate abortion is needed to avert death or that delay creates serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. It is sustained under the prospective construction that compliance cannot create a significant threat to life or health, permitting treatment of preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and prematurely ruptured membranes at onset rather than after catastrophic deterioration. Under §3205(b), the physician must explain the medical indications before emergency abortion only if possible; explanation is not advance permission or a reason to delay necessary care. Separately, §3205(c) protects a physician from liability for omitting §3205(a) information upon proof by a preponderance of a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. Actual occurrence of the feared injury is unnecessary. That defense neither supplies a waiting-period waiver nor replaces emergency treatment protection.

**Authority by component:**

- Emergency construction: Casey, “Medical emergency and the protective construction of §3203”, June 29, 1992.
- Conditional prior explanation: *Casey*, “Emergency explanation under §3205(b).”
- Separate defense and enacted proof burden: *Casey*, “The distinct information-omission defense in §3205(c).” All three are unanimous direct holdings.

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** The significant-threat interpretation is the Court's adoption of the Third Circuit's prospective construction, not extra statutory language or a binding Pennsylvania Supreme Court construction. No separate emergency-vagueness holding or decision about every clinical risk or postviability restriction follows. The statutory defense's burden remains distinct from the constitutional proof allocation.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion emergency care; Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion informed consent.

## Standards and Tests selection: Accurate abortion-consent information, optional materials and receipt certification

### Accurate abortion-consent information, optional materials and receipt certification

**Governing question:** What Pennsylvania may require patients to be told, offered or asked to certify in obtaining informed consent, including independent First Amendment limits.

**Current rule:** Section 3205(a)(1) may require accurate, medically material information about the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks. The three §3205(a)(2) notices may state truthfully: state materials concerning fetal development and alternatives are available and a free copy will be supplied if chosen; medical assistance for prenatal care, childbirth and neonatal care may be available; and the father is liable to assist in support even if he offered to pay for abortion. Assistance and legal liability are not promises of eligibility, payment or collection. Paternal-support information **may** be omitted in a rape pregnancy, including spousal rape, without a police-report condition; omission is permitted, not required, and changes no support obligation. Section 3208 publication and §3205(a)(3) chosen receipt are permissible at their objective, accurate, nonjudgmental scope. Notice of availability is distinct from receipt: patients need not accept, read or endorse materials or listed agencies. Clinicians may correct or criticize them. Section 3205(a)(4) certification may record actual receipt of lawful information, not ideological agreement, unchosen reading or compliance with an invalid prerequisite. The §3205(c) disclosure-health defense remains available on its own terms.

The First Amendment independently permits these bounded medical and ancillary disclosures, voluntary receipt and factual documentation. Payment does not strip professional speech of protection, and truth alone does not justify every compulsory statement. False, irrelevant, coercive or ideological scripts and materially different implementation remain outside the sustained duties. Accurate content does not establish the validity of a timing or exclusive-speaker condition; invalid timing and delivery obligations cannot survive through a certificate.

**Authority by component:**

- Medical content, objective publication/chosen receipt and basic certification: Casey, “Accurate material medical information,” “Statutory publication and chosen receipt of materials,” and “Certification of lawful receipt”, June 29, 1992; unanimous direct holdings at those scopes.
- Each ancillary notice and its qualification: *Casey*, “Notice of available free materials,” “Notice of potentially available medical assistance,” and “Paternal-support notice and the rape omission permission”; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter supply five rationale joins. The additional judgment votes do not broaden that rationale.
- Independent speech rules: *Casey*, the three “Independent speech challenge” holdings; Souter with White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas for medical content, chosen materials and basic certification; the same group without Blackmun for ancillary notices. Stone's judgment-only agreements supply no speech rationale.
- Dependent timing and delivery links: *Casey*, “Certification of lawful receipt,” with the five-Justice invalidation grounds in “Physician delivery of medical information” and “The mandatory 24-hour interval.”

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rape omission permission has no §3209/§3128 reporting or 90-day condition. No finding establishes universal accuracy of actual publications or agencies. No general commercial-speech classification or professional-speech immunity is adopted. Required content remains subject to the separate access framework.

**Material development:** Akron and Thornburgh are qualified only insofar as they categorically prohibit the sustained concise, accurate ancillary notices because they may encourage childbirth; their protection against misleading, coercive and unjustifiably burdensome implementation remains.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion informed consent; Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion information and compelled speech.

## Standards and Tests selection: One-parent informed consent with an independent judicial bypass

### One-parent informed consent with an independent judicial bypass

**Governing question:** The permissible operation of Pennsylvania §3206's parental-consent structure for unemancipated minors under eighteen.

**Current rule:** The minor's informed consent and one parent's informed consent ordinarily suffice, with emergency treatment excused from compliance. Only the mother's consent is required when the father participated in incest. A guardian may consent when both parents are deceased or unavailable within reasonable time and manner; after divorce the custodial parent's consent suffices; if neither parent nor guardian is so available, an adult standing in place of a parent may consent. The parent or guardian considers only the minor's best interests. The minor may elect court authorization without first seeking parental consent. Maturity and capacity with actual informed consent, or alternatively best interests, require authorization through a confidential, expedited process. Counsel is provided absent private counsel or knowing, intelligent waiver; the trial ruling is due within three business days of application and appeal ruling within five business days of filing. Proceedings and records are confidential. Necessary emergency care cannot await those proceedings.

Parental informed consent may use the accurate medical content and qualified materials, assistance and paternal-support notices described in “Accurate abortion-consent information, optional materials and receipt certification,” including optional materials and permission to omit paternal-support information after rape without a police report. This construction creates no parent-specific waiting period, physician-only delivery requirement or compulsory clinic visit. A bypass is not a universal answer to the burdens of a separate obligation.

**Authority by component:**

- Structure, statutory substitutions, emergency exception and alternate bypass routes: Casey, “One-parent informed consent and independent judicial bypass”, June 29, 1992; unanimous rationale.
- Parental information and nonincorporation of additional timing, speaker or attendance obligations: *Casey*, “Accurate information used for informed parental consent”; Souter, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas supply seven rationale joins. Stone agrees only in the judgment on this component.
- No bypass-based approval of an unestablished extra visit: *Casey*, “The parental-attendance objection and the denial of special relief”; Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter supply the controlling ground.

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** These are the sustained statutory safeguards, not a holding that every one is constitutionally indispensable everywhere. The lower court's use of §3205 to explain parental information is a construction, not enacted incorporation of all adult mechanisms. No independent adult-incapacity claim or hypothetical compulsory-visit operation is decided.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Minor consent and independent authorization.

## Standards and Tests selection: Confidential abortion reporting and distinct medical predicates

### Confidential abortion reporting and distinct medical predicates

**Governing question:** The lawful scope of Pennsylvania's confidential medical-basis reporting duties and severable demographic collection.

**Current rule:** Section 3214(a)(8) may require the basis for a physician's judgment that abortion under §3211(b)(1)'s rule for twenty-four or more weeks was necessary to prevent death or substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The report does not create a new substantive necessity test or decide every other later-abortion restriction. Section 3214(a)(10) may require an after-the-fact account of the judgment that a §3203 emergency excused compliance; it creates no advance-permission or pretreatment-report condition and does not narrow the prospective emergency construction. Section 3214(a)(11), incorporating §3210(a), may require the actual inquiries, examinations, tests and diagnostic basis used to determine probable gestational age under the prudent-physician standard. It does not command additional medically unnecessary tests, and §3210(a)'s exception when medical emergency prevents compliance remains. Individual reports omit patient names and retain the confidentiality safeguards accepted in the decision: the reports are not public records; law-enforcement access requires a common pleas court order after an application showing good cause; and the Department's statistical publication must not disclose any physician's, facility's, or patient's identity. Recording clinical judgment neither establishes a new offense from later professional disagreement alone nor provides general immunity from lawful enforcement. Separately, §3214(a)(12)'s confidential marital-status field may survive severance of the invalid spousal-notice and nonnotification-reason fields, without reconstructing those duties.

**Authority by component:**

- Each distinct medical field and its construction: Casey, “Confidential reporting of medical necessity under §3214(a)(8),” “Confidential reporting of an emergency judgment under §3214(a)(10),” and “Confidential reporting of the diagnostic basis under §3214(a)(11)”, June 29, 1992; unanimous direct holdings.
- Independent demographic field: *Casey*, “The independently severable marital-status field”; unanimous direct holding.

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** The separate gestational-age result field and unappealed underlying ascertainment/collection duties are not newly adjudicated. Confidential collection does not itself justify public release, and confidentiality alone does not answer a demonstrated access burden. The public-release relief concerning §§3207(b) and 3214(f) is confined to the plaintiff facilities' covered reports; extension requires its own evidentiary and procedural basis. No general immunity from financial accountability or governmental entitlement to intimate information follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion reporting and public disclosure.

## Standards and Tests selection: North Dakota definitions and separate enforcement conditions

### North Dakota definitions and separate enforcement conditions

**Governing question:** The preserved facial notice challenges to North Dakota’s abortion and medical-emergency definitions and general penalty.

**Current rule:** Section 14-02.1-02(1)’s termination of pregnancy with an intention other than producing a live birth or removing a dead embryo or fetus identifies purposeful termination; diagnostic amniocentesis followed by unintended loss is not abortion merely because its diagnostic purpose is neither exclusion. Section 14-02.1-02(7) supplies a prospective best-clinical-judgment criterion: immediate abortion to avert death, or a twenty-four-hour delay creating grave peril of immediate and irreversible loss of major bodily function. Those terms require professional forecast, not certainty, a numerical prognosis or waiting until harm occurs, and survive the presented facial vagueness claim.

**Authority by component:**

- The specified emergency terms supply a prospective clinical criterion: The specified emergency terms supply a prospective clinical criterion, 1995-02-13. Justice Souter's Part III, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight Justices directly adopt the bounded clarity and enforcement ground; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela would vacate and remand this component.
- A doubtful default-fault rationale does not invalidate the general penalty by itself: A doubtful default-fault rationale does not invalidate the general penalty by itself, 1995-02-13. **Exceptions and limits:** Clarity does not establish that the narrow emergency exception protects all substantively necessary care under Casey. The federal appellate significant-threat construction is neither enacted text nor an authoritative state construction and does not delete immediacy, irreversibility or major function. Section 14-02.1-03(1) excuses its ordinary duties in an emergency but requires explanation of medical indications before abortion if possible and written certification; neither is advance permission to delay necessary care.

**Controlling construction:** Section 12.1-02-02 alone supplies no default willfulness for §14-02.1-11’s silent misdemeanor clause. The latter applies to a chapter violation without another specifically prescribed penalty; its separate willful rule-or-regulation infraction does not amend it or supply every criminal or civil element. Silence alone does not establish facial invalidity, and actual element-specific fault remains open. The separate informed-consent civil remedy specifies $10,000 punitive plus treble actual damages for a completed abortion and $5,000 punitive plus treble actual damages for an attempt; no civil scienter defense or liability is established. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every offense element, while fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remain subject to lawful enforcement.

**Related current rules:** Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation remains controlling without a separate numerical facial threshold. Pennsylvania’s disclosure-health defense and permissive rape-related omission of paternal-support information are not inserted into North Dakota law; their absence requires assessment of properly supported applications, not automatic invalidation. The definition rulings confer no general clinical immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, for the identified citizen-suit and procedural-right clarification; ordinary Article III elements and evidentiary burdens are retained.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

**Holdings navigation:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992) — Standing and personal injury.

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### Medical emergency and the protective construction of §3203

**Holding:** Section 3203 survives this facial challenge under the prospective construction that compliance with the Act cannot create a significant threat to the patient's life or health. Its good-faith clinical standard permits prompt treatment of preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and prematurely ruptured membranes at onset, without waiting for catastrophic deterioration; the ruling does not adjudicate every other postviability restriction or every degree of clinical risk.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3203 defines medical emergency through the physician's good-faith clinical judgment: a condition necessitating immediate abortion to avert death, or one in which delay creates serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. **Holding:** Section 3205(b) requires an explanation of the medical indications supporting emergency abortion before the procedure only if possible. **Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

##### The distinct information-omission defense in §3205(c)

**Holding:** A physician is protected from liability for failing to furnish §3205(a)'s information if the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would have a severely adverse effect on the patient's physical or mental health. **Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

The defense concerns the physician's reasonable belief, not proof that feared injury actually occurred. Its enacted preponderance burden does not displace the constitutional justification allocation. It does not excuse every unfavorable reaction or inconvenience.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The enacted disclosure defense remains available without becoming a general waiting waiver or a substitute for prompt emergency treatment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: ordinary informed-consent administration remains permissible subject to the actual statutory and constitutional limits; no general proof rule is derived from it.

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. ##### The framework retaining protected choice

**Holding:** Before viability, the patient retains the right to decide whether to continue a pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons; the State may not prohibit that protected choice, give another person effective control over it, or prevent timely care necessary for life or health. After viability the State may prohibit abortion only with the required life-and-health protection, while competent practice, genuinely informed consent, safety and lawful administration remain regulable within these boundaries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Viability is individualized and medically supported; no fixed week or conclusive calendar presumption is adopted. Necessary care includes relevant physical and mental health and cannot await imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable treatment, not official approval of the patient's reasons. The holding does not decide constitutional fetal personhood, general public funding, conscience claims, every postviability restriction or admission of a particular drug.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The retained liberty governs the separate provision-specific dispositions. Its six supporters do not all join the complete framework, which has five supporters.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roe v. Wade — Retains the protected previability decision, viability boundary and required postviability life-and-health safeguard; this holding does not itself decide the separate treatment of the trimester timetable.
- Griswold v. Connecticut — Applies protection of reproductive decisionmaking as a foundation of the retained liberty.
- Eisenstadt v. Baird — Applies the individual character of reproductive choice; marital status does not supply official control over the decision.
- Carey v. Population Services International — Applies the principle that substantial restrictions on access can burden the protected decision itself, with no general rule about every law implicating sexual freedom.
- Doe v. Bolton — Retains individualized clinical judgment and meaningful physical-and-mental-health protection; no general professional immunity follows.
- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth — Applies the prohibition on State-conferred spousal control and preserves the distinction between voluntary informed consent and interference with protected choice.
- Bellotti v. Baird, Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft, Hodgson v. Minnesota, and Ohio v. ##### The standard governing previability regulation after this decision

**Holding:** The Court adopts a complete six-step standard governing previability abortion regulation. Substantial obstacles and violations of the protected baseline are independently invalid. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires the State to substantiate its justification and fit and answer identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. The complete standard replaces the independent trimester timetable in the same holding.

The adopted inquiry has the following complete operative scope:

1. Identify the enacted duty, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances, separating actual commands from assumed administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care independently violates the protected baseline and cannot be justified later in the inquiry. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration makes intervention unavoidable. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe, suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons or a legislative definition declaring all noncompliant care unsafe.
2. Identify actual function and purpose. Safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration and financial accountability may supply permissible functions. Truthful persuasion and a preference for childbirth are permissible; moral disapproval, punishment or deterrence through blocked access are not independent justifications. Obstructive purpose established objectively from text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the duty; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish it. Medical and deliberative justifications require support addressing their actual functions.
3. Challengers must establish a concrete, nontrivial additional interference attributable to the duty, including demonstrated expense, appointments, delay, provider-capacity loss, clinical risk, coercion or disclosure consequences. Compare the system with and without that obligation, or the identified challenged package, holding other law and circumstances constant. Consider cumulative operation without double counting. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding injury, retain that group throughout, and use evidence-supported subgroups where appropriate. Do not average burdens over all pregnancies or define the group only as successful claimants. Poverty, distance, work, transportation and abuse matter through their causal connection to the State's added condition; exceptions, bypasses and providers count only if usable. No universal monetary, distance or time cutoff applies. Reliable evidence and reasonable predictions may establish prospective interference without waiting for lost access. Meaningful interference triggers review but does not itself establish invalidity.
4. Apply the protections in sequence. A violation of the baseline, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle ends the inquiry in invalidity. An obstacle seriously obstructs practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care in the affected population; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and successful compliance by some does not establish access for others. No compelling interest rescues an established obstacle. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring; identify the severe intrusion and why the first inquiry did not resolve it. The protections may overlap, but an obstacle cannot be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical decisionmaking, health or protected confidentiality may require exacting justification even where practical access remains usable. For remaining meaningful lesser interference, the State must substantiate its permissible justification and the fit of the additional duty. If challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it, including any inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequence. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice: no particular legislative finding, new study in every case, scientific certainty, numerical benefit calculation or disproof of every hypothetical alternative is required. A court may not substitute its preferred policy once justification and fit are established. Below meaningful interference, the heightened access inquiry ends, but prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary legal requirements remain. Challengers bear duty, population, causation and trigger; the State bears the applicable justification, tailoring or fit burden. Ordinary proof rules for historical facts and separate statutory defenses do not change.
5. Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication. Assess medical reasons against competent evidence, accepted standards, individual circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement has serious evidentiary weight but no association holds a veto; neither a bare clinical assertion nor unsupported official preference is conclusive. Necessary care must remain timely, without an unnecessary committee veto or documentation used as prior permission. Duties must be definite and applicable culpability requirements respected; reasonable later professional disagreement alone does not establish every element of an offense. Fraud, malpractice and misconduct remain enforceable. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material. Concise factual assistance notices need not entail ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accurate content does not itself justify burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Analyze useful confidential records separately from public disclosure and enforcement of invalid conditions.
6. Identify the particular violation and tailor relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under severability law and address dependent certifications, reports and sanctions expressly. Another patient's ability to comply does not defeat an individual claim. Broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation causes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it; voluntary compliance in some cases alone does not justify compulsion. Otherwise limit relief to proved applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not dispense with causation, scope or justification analysis. Cumulative-burden relief addresses the challenged contribution, not all existing regulation. Replacement of the timetable neither dissolves injunctions nor authorizes every challenged duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a direct five-Justice rule; no Marks synthesis or addition of Kennedy's separate approach is required. An independent numerical facial-review threshold is not adopted. The spousal-notice holding supplies its distinct provision-specific affected-group analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The complete framework replaces the independent trimester timetable effective June 29, 1992. Existing injunctions must be conformed to the actual component judgments, not automatically dissolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roe and Doe: preserve the protected decision and clinical safeguards while replacing the independent timetable.
- Carey and Akron: support protection against serious and unjustified interference; the general lesser-burden substantiation rule is expressly adopted here.
- Marks: no aggregation of dissenting rationales is necessary because five Justices directly join the complete rule.
- Ohio v. Akron: its facial-review formulation is qualified at the scope expressly stated in Step 6 and the spousal-notice holding.

Pennsylvania's particular interval result supplies no automatic North Dakota disposition.

##### The abortion definition excludes an unintended diagnostic termination

**Holding:** Section 14-02.1-02(1), defining abortion as termination of human pregnancy with an intention other than producing a live birth or removing a dead embryo or fetus, identifies a purposeful pregnancy termination. Diagnostic amniocentesis followed by an unintended pregnancy loss is not brought within that definition merely because the diagnostic purpose is neither of the two stated exclusions; this reading defeats the preserved facial notice and enforcement objection without a borrowed culpability default.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is not immunity for diagnostic treatment, negligence or other misconduct; it does not decide a completed prosecution, civil award, general knowledge-of-illegality requirement or actual-knowledge minimum for every statutory element. It does not resolve the emergency definition or every sanction attached to consent duties.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64 (1994): applies its contextual definition analysis without transplanting its federal scienter construction or treating fault as a universal cure.
- Kolender v. Lawson, 461 U.S. 352 (1983): applies the separate notice and minimum-enforcement-guidance requirements; distinguishes an undefined official compliance demand from this bounded medical act and purpose.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): preserves its requirement to examine the particular trigger and fault separately; the present ground rests on the category's own words rather than fault addressed to a different element.

##### The specified emergency terms supply a prospective clinical criterion

**Holding:** The preserved facial vagueness challenge to section 14-02.1-02(7) fails because its specified outcomes and timing, assessed through the physician's prospective best clinical judgment, give notice and enforcement guidance: the pregnancy must require immediate abortion to avert death, or a twenty-four-hour delay must create grave peril of immediate and irreversible loss of major bodily function. Gravity, immediacy and major bodily function call for a professional forecast, not certainty, a numerical prognosis or waiting until the threatened harm occurs; this holding does not adjudicate the distinct claim that the exception's substantive breadth fails to protect particular necessary care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Clarity is not a ruling that this restricted emergency category satisfies every Casey life-and-health requirement. Casey's broader necessary-care protection, including individualized physical and mental health and care before catastrophic deterioration, remains law. The emergency undue-burden claim was not pressed below and is not decided here. The federal appellate significant-threat construction is not enacted text or an authoritative North Dakota Supreme Court construction; it does not license deleting immediate, irreversible or major from the statute. The present notice ground stands independently of that broader construction and of any presumed default fault rule.

Section 14-02.1-03(1)'s emergency exception excuses that subsection's ordinary requirements, but requires an explanation of the medical indications before the abortion if possible and written certification of those indications. The conditional explanation is no advance-permission requirement, and certification supplies no authority to delay necessary treatment. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish all elements of a criminal offense or civil damages; disagreement may remain relevant under actual applicable law. Fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct are not immunized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Vuitch, 402 U.S. 62 (1971): applies the intelligibility of professional judgment at the stated scope; its D.C. statute's life-or-health construction and prosecution burden do not become North Dakota law.
- Doe v. Bolton, 410 U.S. 179 (1973): applies its recognition of clinical judgment without importing Georgia's broader statutory construction or creating professional immunity.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): distinguishes its conflicting viability and treatment duties, while retaining independent scrutiny of the actual trigger and culpability for error.
- Kolender v. Lawson, 461 U.S. 352 (1983): applies minimum enforcement guidelines and distinguishes undefined official satisfaction from the stated clinical criteria; no universal all-applications bar is adopted.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992): preserves broad necessary-care safeguards and conditional emergency explanation; its Pennsylvania construction was not a separate emergency-vagueness holding and does not determine this distinct claim.

##### A doubtful default-fault rationale does not invalidate the general penalty by itself

**Holding:** Section 12.1-02-02 alone does not establish default willfulness for the silent misdemeanor clause of section 14-02.1-11, and neither that default nor the latter section's separately expressed willful rule-or-regulation infraction supplies the criminal or civil elements of every chapter violation. Rejection of that additional rationale does not defeat the independently sufficient definition holdings or establish facial invalidity of the general penalty merely because its first sentence lacks an express culpability term; actual element-specific fault and liability remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The misdemeanor clause covers a chapter violation for which no other penalty is specifically prescribed. The distinct infraction clause concerns a willful violation of a promulgated rule or regulation. The latter's express term does not amend the former. The independent civil remedy provides $10,000 punitive damages plus treble actual damages for an abortion without the required informed consent, and $5,000 punitive damages plus treble actual damages for an attempted abortion without compliance. The Court enters no liability determination or award under either provision and creates no civil scienter defense. No separate facial judgment on an unpresented civil-damages theory is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Village of Hoffman Estates v. Flipside, 455 U.S. 489 (1982): applies contextual notice, enforcement and scienter principles, with greater clarity required for criminal sanctions and protected conduct; its commercial all-applications formulation does not govern without its premise.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): preserves the separation of an intelligible clinical standard from fault concerning an erroneous assessment; no particular universal fault level is supplied.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64 (1994): preserves its context-specific distinction between definition and fault; no universal knowledge element or scienter cure is inferred.
- City of Dickinson v. Mueller, 261 N.W.2d 787 (N.D. 1977), and State v. North Dakota Education Association, 262 N.W.2d 731 (N.D. 1978): apply their title limitation when assessing the asserted default; no abortion-specific state construction is attributed to either case.
- State v. Goetz, 312 N.W.2d 1 (N.D. 1981): distinguishes definition of an expressly enacted willfulness term from importing default fault; its securities-law instruction and knowledge-of-illegality discussion do not settle these elements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Access review is vacated and remanded, 6–3; the abortion-definition, emergency-definition and associated limited general-penalty rejections are each affirmed, 8–1. The District Court must promptly consider interim protection under the ordinary standard and current record. No dissolved injunction is automatically restored, no final injunction or hearing is ordered, and no emergency-access claim unpressed below or new parental or speech claim is decided.

#### Romer v. Evans, 517 U.S. 620

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1039.
**Decided:** May 20, 1996.

##### Withdrawal and constitutional foreclosure of ordinary protection for an identified class

**Holding:** Colorado Amendment 2's combined withdrawal of existing orientation-based nondiscrimination protection and foreclosure of comparable protection through ordinary state and local government violates equal protection under ordinary rational-basis review. Its established class-specific disability lacks a rational relationship to the asserted legitimate interests, without reliance on suspect status, political-process strict scrutiny, elimination of generally applicable protection, or a finding about every voter's motive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The authoritative state construction covers legislative, executive, and judicial channels of the State, agencies, schools, and local government, including public and private settings; it is not merely a quota prohibition or local preemption leaving the legislature free to act. Uniformity, regulatory restraint, finite resources, religious and intimate association, parental teaching, and conduct-based morality do not explain this blanket ineligibility across public and impersonal commercial life. Ordinary review permits supported predictions, imperfect categories, and conceivable grounds without formal findings or empirical certainty; a narrower alternative alone is insufficient. The defect concerns the relationship to the actual disadvantage, rather than an independent hostility finding or mandatory least-restrictive-means inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes, without deciding, that generally applicable criminal, tort, contract, and public-service protections remain. It creates no suspect or quasi-suspect tier, general duty to enact nondiscrimination laws, nationwide code, quota entitlement, universal access to every lawmaking level, or presumption invalidating every group-affecting repeal, referendum, or constitutional policy reservation. Particular religious, intimate-association, and expressive defenses retain their own predicates; no municipal exemption is approved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Beach* preserves conceivable legitimate grounds and reasonable generalizations together with the *Moreno/Cleburne* limits on bare harm and irrational prejudice. *Heller*'s controlling proof-burden decision requires a relationship to the actual disadvantage; its separate family-participation holding preserves relevant generalizations and rejects invalidity based on narrower alternatives alone. *Hurley* protects concrete private expression without making ordinary service categorically expressive association; *Swanner* and *American Life League* retain their particular religious-liberty inquiries. *Bowers* remains effective on its specified-conduct question but does not justify this broader disability. *Adarand* adds no orientation tier, and *Klinger*'s treatment-specific comparison does not import *Armstrong*'s charging-discovery threshold into an express classification.

##### Resolution of the preserved alternative and complete injunctive relief

**Holding:** The fully presented rational-basis alternative permits affirmance without adoption of the lower court's strict-scrutiny rationale. The defect permeates Amendment 2's inseparable common operation, warranting the complete existing injunction without a new merits remand or federal rewriting of state law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Material application:** State severability law treats orientation, conduct, practices, and relationships as descriptions of the same class; deletion of a description leaves the same disability. No independently lawful remainder is established. Failure under strict scrutiny is not itself rational-basis failure, and no unconditional State concession supplies the result. The ability to seek another constitutional amendment does not cure the unequal barrier to ordinary protection.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm, 7–2; the permanent injunction remains effective in full. No enactment, damages, factual inquiry, severance proceeding, or new hearing is ordered. Unappealed suspect-status treatment is not reopened, and independently preserved First Amendment claims are neither decided nor presumed abandoned.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hunter* and *Seattle* retain their racial political-restructuring settings without a new fundamental-right extension. *Valtierra*, *Gordon*, and *Crawford* prevent treating every referendum, neutral voting rule, or policy revision as exclusion. *Dalton* requires relief matched to the violation and preservation of independently lawful operation; no general facial formula, *Miller* abortion method, or *44 Liquormart* empirical burden replaces this analysis.

#### Planned Parenthood, Sioux Falls Clinic v. Miller

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit Nos. 94-3326SD and 94-3398SD; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied. 63 F.3d 1452 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** April 15, 1996.

##### Facial review and compulsory parental notice for previability abortions

**Holding:** The complete six-step *Casey* standard, as applied in *Fargo*, governs compulsory minor notice; neither an independent no-valid-applications requirement nor a numerical facial threshold displaces its requirements. Challengers must establish interference caused by the challenged duty before the State must justify it. Compulsory notice violates that standard where its demonstrated operation gives a parent practical control over protected choice or substantially obstructs timely care; a permissible parental-welfare interest cannot justify those established violations.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts I–II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices.

**Material application:** The relevant population is unemancipated minors seeking protected previability care who are subject to the condition, compared with the same system without that condition and interval, holding other relevant circumstances constant. The evidence establishes obstruction, delay, and disclosure consequences attributable to the compulsory duty. Section 34-23A-7 ordinarily requires forty-eight hours after written notice, with personal delivery or certified restricted-delivery mail; mailed notice is deemed delivered at noon on the next regular mail-delivery day, rather than upon mailing. Exceptions cover a physician's good-faith certification that immediate abortion is necessary to avert death or delay risks substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function and insufficient time exists for notice; the recipient's written certification that notice occurred; or the minor's declaration or information indicating statutory abuse or neglect followed by the physician's report. Abuse includes threatened substantial harm, an injurious environment, and substantial emotional or mental impairment. Recipient agencies must keep the abortion from parental disclosure. These real protections nevertheless leave the supported mature and best-interests circumstances without a usable independent route; acknowledgment establishes notice, and an emergency exception conditioned on insufficient time does not cure nonemergency obstruction.

**Independent alternative holding:** The condition's additional delay, forced disclosure, and coercive consequences establish remaining meaningful interference. The State's failure to substantiate fit or adequately answer a concrete, practicable confidential maturity or best-interests alternative independently invalidates this compulsory structure. Meaningful interference triggers justification, rather than automatically establishing invalidity; reliable experience and reasonable predictions may justify a law without scientific certainty or a new study. The State must address the alternative's availability, sufficiency, or material countervailing consequences, not disprove every imagined alternative.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of the same six-Justice Court opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The distinct severe-interference branch retains compelling justification and narrow tailoring for an established serious intrusion on confidentiality, health, or clinical judgment not independently prohibited. The Court need not classify every remaining injury as severe. No finding of obstructive legislative purpose, categorical equivalence of notice and consent, categorical prohibition of forty-eight-hour periods, or independent invalidation of the emergency definition follows. Minors' welfare permits parental involvement; voluntary consultation remains lawful.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Casey* supplies the whole causal, substantive, and remedial framework, including practical-control protection and necessary-care safeguards; *Fargo* applies it without a separate numerical or *Salerno* bar. *Salerno*'s operation elsewhere is reserved. *Matheson*'s immature-dependent-minor holding does not resolve supported mature or best-interests claims. *Bellotti* and the bypass sustained in *Casey* establish a concrete alternative, without requiring transplantation of every consent procedure. *Hodgson* informs assessment of family circumstances, retaining its two-parent setting. *Ohio v. Akron Center* sustained an available bypass without deciding universal necessity.

##### Scope of relief against an indivisible compulsory condition

**Holding:** The compulsory previability minor-notice condition may be enjoined throughout its compulsory operation because the demonstrated defect is indivisible within that condition and the enacted law supplies no usable independent maturity or best-interests route. Voluntary compliance by other families does not cure that defect; relief must match the adjudicated violation and preserve independently lawful requirements and unreviewed rulings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of the same six-Justice Court opinion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm invalidation and remand to conform declaratory and injunctive relief to the reviewed minor condition and enforcement consequences dependent solely on its breach. Preserve the separate information, criminal and civil penalty, and severability rulings. Adult-incapacity and unreviewed postviability applications are neither adjudicated nor automatically authorized for enforcement; excessive decree language resting on this decision must be confined. The Court creates no bypass, tribunal, deadline, new merits trial, or legislative obligation to retain notice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Casey*'s tailored-relief rule and *Dalton*'s preservation of severable nonconflicting operation require an effective bounded injunction, without supplying a new facial threshold or requiring courts to assume a safeguard that does not exist.

#### Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1424.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### What evidence of Article III injury, causation and redressability is required at summary judgment

**Holding:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury, a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, and a likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. At summary judgment, the plaintiff must supply specific facts by affidavit or other evidence on the challenged elements; favorable reasonable inferences remain available, but allegations cannot substitute for the missing evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the elements and litigation-stage evidentiary requirements. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining this section.

**Material application:** Earlier survival of the pleading stage does not establish the specific facts needed at summary judgment. The Court resolves this action on insufficient threatened personal injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general redressability element controls. Its application to absent funding agencies and uncertain foreign-project responses appears only in the three-Justice Part III-B and is not controlling. Favorable reasonable inferences remain available; the decision does not replace Rule 56 or the separate APA programmatic-review holding of National Wildlife Federation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The merits declaration, mandatory rulemaking relief and dependent §1540(g)(4) fee award are vacated, and the action is remanded for jurisdictional dismissal. Six Justices support dismissal for lack of standing; seven support reversal, with Stevens relying on a noncontrolling statutory ground. No independent fee-entitlement rule or substitute award is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975): applied for the plaintiff's personal stake and burden to establish standing; its statutory-rights discussion remains subject to concrete injury.
- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied for the constitutional elements and the limits on generalized judicial supervision.
- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976): applied for fair traceability and likely redress; no new majority application concerning these absent agencies follows.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied for the evidentiary distinction between pleading and summary judgment; its separate APA programmatic-review holding is not enlarged.
- Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986): applied to require evidence of challenged essential elements while preserving genuine material disputes and reasonable inferences.

##### Whether past visits, indefinite return intentions and general wildlife interests establish imminent personal injury

**Holding:** Aesthetic, recreational and professional interests in observing wildlife can support standing, but these plaintiffs must show threatened interference with their own concrete use. Prior visits and an indefinite intention to return, without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure, do not establish standing for these prospective claims; an ecosystem, animal or professional connection that supplies only general interest in distant species does not cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone's separate concurrence supplies a sixth vote for the record-specific personal-injury conclusion without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** The affidavits describe visits to Egyptian and Sri Lankan project areas but no sufficiently definite return undertaking or continuing activity showing imminent exposure. General ecosystem, animal and professional connections supply no missing evidence of these members' own threatened use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document prerequisite is imposed. Actual use of an affected ecosystem and demonstrated injury transmitted across geographic distance remain undecided. Aesthetic, recreational and professional harm remains cognizable when it affects the claimant's own concrete activity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jurisdictional dismissal leaves the statute's geographic reach unresolved and does not preclude a later action supported by sufficient standing facts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): applied; aesthetic injury is cognizable, but an organization's special interest cannot replace injury to a member.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977): applied to require an injured member for this associational claim; no other associational requirement is changed.
- Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95 (1983): applied for the distinction between past exposure and a present or imminent injury supporting prospective relief.
- Whitmore v. Arkansas, 495 U.S. 149 (1990): applied for actual or imminent rather than conjectural injury.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied to specific affected use; no categorical bar to geographically transmitted injury is established.
- Japan Whaling Association v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): distinguished because its whale-watching and studying members alleged an adverse effect on their own activities; concrete professional or ecological injury remains possible.

##### Whether ESA citizen-suit permission and an asserted procedural right replace threatened concrete injury

**Holding:** ESA §11(g)'s authorization for any person to sue does not give Article III standing to a plaintiff asserting only the public's common interest in lawful administration. A procedural right may protect a plaintiff's threatened concrete interest without requiring proof that the procedure will certainly change the ultimate agency decision, but the procedural label cannot replace that personal interest; these respondents have not shown the required connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the citizen-suit and concrete-interest rule. Stone does not join this section.

**Material application:** Section 11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), permits enforcement but identifies no concrete injury to these members independent of the unsupported future use. The public's common interest in lawful administration cannot supply the missing personal connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may recognize concrete factual injuries previously inadequate in law; the outer reach of that power is not settled. Procedural protections tied to a threatened concrete interest remain cognizable without certainty of a changed ultimate decision. Neither §7(a)(2)'s geographic reach nor the binding effect of a revised rule on absent agencies is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The action is dismissed for want of Article III standing; no new consultation rule or cancellation of any foreign project is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126 (1922): applied to reject a general right to compel lawful administration as a sufficient personal injury.
- Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447 (1923), and Ex parte Levitt, 302 U.S. 633 (1937): applied for the distinction between direct injury and an undifferentiated public grievance.
- United States v. Richardson, 418 U.S. 166 (1974), and Schlesinger v. Reservists Committee to Stop the War, 418 U.S. 208 (1974): applied to generally shared governmental grievances without distinctive concrete harm.
- Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United for Separation of Church and State, Inc., 454 U.S. 464 (1982), and Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied; a claimed right to lawful government conduct alone does not satisfy Article III.
- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), and Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): preserved for Congress's power to recognize injuries and broaden protected interests while retaining the plaintiff's own concrete injury.
- Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Insurance Co., 409 U.S. 205 (1972), and Hardin v. Kentucky Utilities Co., 390 U.S. 1 (1968): distinguished as statutory recognition of concrete personal interests, not authorization of a wholly abstract grievance.

#### Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136; review of 90 F.3d 1237.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question

**Holding:** A federal court cannot assume disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim. Section 11046(c) describes enforcement and relief without making every statutory liability element jurisdictional; the holding preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences and does not prescribe an exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins, including O'Connor and Kennedy's nonexhaustive sequencing reservation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether EPCRA authorizes suit over reporting violations cured before filing remains undecided. Norton and Avrech retain their distinct settings involving already-controlled merits.

##### Requested relief cannot redress this completed informational deprivation

**Holding:** Assuming past informational injury and traceability, Article III requires requested relief likely to repair that injury or prevent a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. When reports were current before suit and no continuing or imminent violation is alleged, a declaration of past lateness, Treasury penalties, monitoring, future report copies and litigation expenses supply no such redress.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support dismissal on statutory grounds without joining this constitutional rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than rejects informational injury. Standing is absent at commencement, rather than lost by later cessation. Deterrent penalties may redress a supported threatened injury; annual reporting duties alone do not allege one. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party costs on a final order, not independent compensation. A properly presented replacement-information loss claim, monitoring in a live action, recoverable prefiling expenses, associational requirements and statutory temporal reach remain open. Section 11022(f) gives the fire department no transferable automatic inspection entitlement for this plaintiff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand with directions to dismiss, unanimously as to judgment. No merits relief, standing discovery, amendment entitlement or newly inferred recurrence inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to distinguish a substantial federal claim from its ultimate success; a contested statutory element is not automatically jurisdictional.
- **Young v. Northern Illinois Conference:** Applied at that same distinction; its constitutional-defense holding is not expanded into permission to assume Article III.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Its controlling standing elements and stage-appropriate support remain governing. Its three-Justice absent-agency application is neither enlarged nor needed.

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### Independent speech challenge to medical consent information

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not forbid requiring the accurate, material medical facts in section 3205(a)(1) as part of informed consent to this treatment. This is a bounded consent rule, not a holding that paid medical speech is categorically commercial or that any truthful statement can be compelled.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 8 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Required content is the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks, not an ideological script. Section 3205(c) protects omission of subsection (a) information when the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. It is an information defense, not an emergency-treatment or general delay exception. Stone concurs in the judgment only and adds no rationale vote. The separately invalidated timing and exclusive-speaker conditions do not acquire approval through this speech holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Deny the independent facial medical-content speech claim at this bounded scope; materially false, irrelevant or ideological implementation is not approved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Riley v. National Federation of the Blind, 487 U.S. 781 (1988): payment alone does not remove speech protection.
- Danforth: supplies the legitimate informed-consent connection.
- Wooley v. Maynard, 430 U.S. 705 (1977): distinguished because factual treatment information does not require ideological affirmation.

##### Independent speech challenge to the three ancillary notices

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not facially invalidate the concise truthful notices of available materials, possible assistance and paternal liability at the scopes separately sustained. None requires ideological assent or endorsement of a listed agency; the notice of materials remains distinct from chosen receipt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 7 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Availability means a right to review and obtain free materials if chosen, without acceptance, reading or endorsement. Assistance may be available for prenatal, childbirth and neonatal care, without eligibility or payment guarantees. Paternal liability remains despite an offer to pay for abortion and is not guaranteed collection; rape-related omission is permitted without a police report, including spousal rape. Section 3205(c) protects omission of subsection (a) information when the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. It is an information defense, not an emergency-treatment or general delay exception. Clinicians may correct or criticize information. Stone concurs in the judgments only; Blackmun does not reach these independent speech grounds.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Deny facial relief on each of the three independent speech claims at its bounded factual scope. Concrete falsehood, coercive distribution or materially different enforcement remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Riley: forecloses the compensation-based shortcut; each command is examined independently.
- Wooley: protects against compelled ideological assent, absent from the sustained commands.
- Danforth: supplies the informed-choice connection.

##### Independent speech challenge to chosen materials and lawful receipt certification

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not invalidate state publication and genuinely chosen receipt of objective materials, or a certificate confined to receipt of lawful information rather than ideological assent. This principle does not supply a vote sustaining a contested underlying notice, timing or delivery obligation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 8 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Receipt of a copy occurs only if the patient chooses to review it; no reading or ideological assent is compelled. A written certificate documents actual lawful receipt, not personal agreement. Stone concurs in judgment only. The independent speech ruling does not add votes to any disputed prerequisite or certification dependency.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Deny the independent speech challenge to chosen materials and basic receipt certification. False distribution, forced reading or a certificate demanding ideological agreement remains challengeable.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wooley: distinguished at voluntary receipt and factual certification scope; ideological assent remains protected.
- Danforth: applied to consent documentation.
- Riley: paid status supplies no categorical exception to speech protection.
