## Event

Texas v. United States, No. 97-29. Argued January 14, 1998; decided March 31, 1998. On appeal from the three-judge United States District Court for the District of Columbia. Texas seeks a declaration that §5 of the Voting Rights Act does not apply to school-district interventions authorized by Texas Education Code §§39.131(a)(7) and (8). It argues both that these interventions do not change voting and that they accord with federal education-grant conditions. The District Court dismissed the action as unripe without deciding either merits theory.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the dismissal for lack of a ripe controversy.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The requested declaration remains unavailable in this unripe action. Neither the voting-coverage theory nor the federal-funding theory is adjudicated. No injunction, exemption or merits remand is ordered; review remains available when a sufficiently definite intervention produces a concrete controversy.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### A categorical preclearance exemption is unripe before the challenged intervention is sufficiently defined

**Holding and operative rule:** A request to exempt possible future school-district interventions from §5 is unripe when no sufficiently definite intervention, affected district or allocation of powers establishes a fit legal controversy and no substantial present hardship justifies deciding before those details exist. Texas's challenge therefore must be dismissed without deciding whether any particular intervention changes voting or whether a supported federal-funding conflict affects that question.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Abbott Laboratories requires attention to fitness for decision and the hardship of withholding review. The Commissioner has not selected a currently foreseen or likely district for the challenged master or management-team appointments. Under the recited state policy, lesser sanctions would first be tried; even then the Commissioner selects intervention only to the extent necessary and defines the appointees' actual powers. The legal significance of that implementation cannot be resolved through a universal exemption.

Presley requires a direct relation to voting and leaves extreme displacement of elective authority unresolved. Texas law independently prohibits these appointees from acting concerning a district election, changing the board's size or selection method, setting a tax rate, or adopting a budget with a spending level different from the level set by the board. Those limits do not themselves establish every future appointment's effect. The Attorney General's warning that some implementations could require preclearance is not a determination that an identified voting change occurred.

New York permitted review because the future obligation required substantial present preparations; Pic-A-State involved an existing business stopped by a real criminal constraint. Texas shows no comparable present burden. Its earlier Dallas County request and ninety-day approval period do not establish a current appointment, inevitable delay or present voting change. The Court does not require a party to violate the law before challenging a concrete obligation. It requires the controversy here to become sufficiently definite. Neither federal-grant compatibility nor voting coverage is reached, and Raines's rule for individual legislators is not a prohibition on suits by States.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner:** Applied at the fitness-and-hardship inquiry; uncertainty alone does not require an advisory declaration.
- **New York v. United States:** Distinguished because its future command already required substantial present siting action; timely pre-enforcement review remains available in that setting.
- **Pic-A-State Pennsylvania v. Reno:** Distinguished because an actual criminal constraint had already stopped the plaintiff's existing business; no prosecution prerequisite is added.
- **Presley v. Etowah County Commission / Mack v. Russell County Commission:** Preserved at its direct voting-relation rule and reservation concerning effective replacement of elected authority; no future intervention is classified here.
- **Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment:** Applied to require the Article III inquiry before contested statutory merits.
- **Raines v. Byrd:** Its concrete-injury discipline is respected without extending its individual-legislator rule into a categorical bar on governmental plaintiffs.

## Law After Decision

The dismissal creates no substantive exemption for school-district interventions and no blanket prohibition on pre-enforcement review. Existing §5 law continues to govern an actual covered change. A sufficiently definite later intervention and real dispute may support review through the proper statutory channel; its coverage and any supported federal-funding question remain open.

## Procedure After Action

The District Court's ripeness dismissal stands. Texas receives no advisory coverage ruling or authorization to implement an unidentified change. Any later controversy must satisfy the ordinary jurisdictional and statutory requirements on its own actual facts.
