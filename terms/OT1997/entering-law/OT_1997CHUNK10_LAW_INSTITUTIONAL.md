## Standards and Tests selection: Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments

### Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments

**Governing question:** Whether and on what conditions §27A(b) requires reinstatement of a completed, unappealed private securities limitations dismissal.

**Current rule:** Under Morgan Stanley, Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement under §27A(b) of a qualifying private §10(b) action even though its limitations dismissal became final without appeal before the provision's December 19, 1991 enactment: the action must have commenced on or before June 19, 1991, been dismissed as time barred after that date, and been timely under the jurisdiction's law, including retroactivity principles, as it existed June 19, 1991, with a reinstatement motion filed within sixty days after enactment. The court independently determines those cumulative conditions and, when they are satisfied, must reinstate without discretionary balancing, retaining independent responsibility for all claims, defenses, and remedies; this expressly retrospective entitlement does not authorize legislative findings, dictated liability, or unrestricted reopening of completed merits judgments. A narrowly defined, exclusively retrospective class does not by itself turn this procedure into a legislative judgment: the statute covers every action meeting its legal criteria and selects no parties for legislatively determined liability.

**Authority by component:**

- Defined statutory entitlement, cumulative conditions, mandatory reinstatement, independent judicial adjudication and constitutional limits: Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. — Bounded statutory reinstatement of completed securities limitations dismissals, May 23, 1994. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.
- Application to a final unappealed dismissal and the boundary for narrow, exclusively retrospective class coverage: Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. — Section 27A(b) permits mandatory reinstatement of this completed limitations dismissal, April 18, 1995. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994.

**Exceptions and limits:** Congress may not command findings contrary to the record, impose liability on identified defendants by legislative declaration, or disguise an individualized award as a procedural reopening law. The Court decides no general power to set aside completed judgments adjudicating the underlying merits, no constitutionality of every retrospective statute, and no independent due-process question; applicable due-process protections and other constitutional restrictions remain. Section 27A(a), other applications of subsection (b), general severability, federal waiver, continuing prospective relief, and independently authorized judicial reopening are not decided. The decision neither adjudicates other parties' completed cases nor creates criminal or innocence remedies. Fraud, reliance, causation, liability, damages, and ordinary merits defenses remain for the courts; reinstatement establishes none of them.

**Related current rules:** Federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review concerns judicial retroactivity alone, which supplies no reopening entitlement. Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct separates express temporal direction from constitutional authority; the ordinary presumption against retroactivity does not defeat §27A(b)'s express reach. Robertson's pending-litigation distinction between changing governing law and prescribing adjudication remains intact; Morgan Stanley supplies the completed-dismissal rule Robertson left open.

**Holdings navigation:** Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. — Bounded statutory reinstatement of completed securities limitations dismissals; Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. — Section 27A(b) permits mandatory reinstatement of this completed limitations dismissal.

## Standards and Tests selection: Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

## Event

Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach, No. 96-1482. Plenary merits decision, March 3, 1998; argued November 10, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Ninth Circuit. The question is whether an MDL transferee court may use 28 U.S.C. §1404(a) to assign a transferred action to itself for trial despite §1407(a)’s remand command. Lexecon’s Illinois diversity tort action was transferred to Arizona for coordinated pretrial proceedings. The Arizona court entered pretrial dispositions, denied requests to suggest remand and retained the defamation claim for a defense trial judgment; the Ninth Circuit sustained the self-transfer. Lexecon preserved the objection through remand requests, mandamus and direct appeal.

## Holdings

### I. The MDL transferee cannot defeat mandatory return through self-transfer

**Holding and operative rule:** Unless the transferred action has previously terminated, §1407(a) requires the Panel to remand it to the original district at or before completion of coordinated pretrial proceedings. Section 1404(a) does not authorize the transferee court to retain that continuing action for trial by transferring it from itself to itself.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by all nine Justices and supplies direct statutory authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1407 authorizes coordination for pretrial proceedings and commands return of each continuing action. The Panel may separate and remand particular claims earlier; an action that has actually terminated needs no return. Changing the asserted venue basis does not terminate the action. Addressing the command to the Panel does not authorize the transferee court to prevent its performance. Section 1404(a)’s general permission to transfer to another eligible district or division coexists with, rather than defeats, this specific temporary allocation. Section 1407(f) permits only rules consistent with Acts of Congress, so the cited self-assignment practice cannot alter the return obligation. Subsection (h)’s specific Clayton Act trial-transfer authorization does not confer that power in this tort action. The investment in coordinated pretrial work is substantial, but efficiency cannot delete the enacted sequence. A later lawful transfer by the proper court and consent or waiver questions not presented remain open.

### II. The preserved trial-forum error requires correction of this judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** A party that preserved its §1407 return objection need not prove that a different jury would have reached a different verdict to obtain correction of the resulting unauthorized trial forum. This uncured violation requires setting aside the defamation trial judgment and enforcing statutory remand.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by all nine Justices; its remedy follows the mandatory return right asserted here.

**Controlling explanation:** Lexecon repeatedly requested remand, sought mandamus and then pursued the promised direct appeal. The possibility that Arizona could originally have been a proper venue does not supply the procedure Congress required after an MDL transfer. Caterpillar preserves a completed judgment when the jurisdictional defect actually disappeared before trial; the prohibited retention for this trial never received an equivalent cure. Good Real Property rejects inventing dismissal of a substantive action as a sanction for administrative delay. This case instead directly enforces a particular allocation of the trial forum without extinguishing the cause of action. No constitutional or subject-matter-jurisdiction label is necessary, and the Court adopts no universal rule that every procedural error defeats a completed judgment. The statutory return must occur before any independently lawful later transfer; no permanent right against such a transfer is adjudicated.

### III. Authorized pretrial work and unreviewed tort issues remain separate

**Holding and operative rule:** The improper self-transfer does not automatically invalidate decisions made within authorized pretrial proceedings or decide the merits of the underlying tort claims. Properly remaining appellate challenges must be addressed in their own posture; the unpreserved request for prospective application does not alter this disposition.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees with these dispositions without joining an additional rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The malicious-prosecution and abuse-of-process rulings and the Cotchett Rule 54(b) judgment are pretrial dispositions, not the defamation trial verdict. Lexecon disputed the Rule 54(b) exercise of discretion without denying power to enter an authorized pretrial judgment. Those distinctions prevent wholesale reinstatement of every claim merely because trial retention was unlawful. The argument that the defamation claim should independently have been dismissed before trial remains for proper consideration, not a new factual or tort-law ruling here. The prospectivity request was not presented below or in opposition to review and supplies no basis for a different result in this case. No comprehensive retroactivity doctrine, new waiver finding or adjudication of a later transfer is adopted.

## Precedent Treatment

- Caterpillar’s completed-judgment protection requires an actual pretrial cure; the preserved trial-forum defect here is distinguished as uncured. Its statutory remand duty remains mandatory.
- Good Real Property preserves mandatory administrative duties while rejecting an unstated forfeiture-dismissal sanction; direct enforcement of the specified trial allocation is different.
- City of Chicago retains the distinction between statutory power and convenience or discretionary retention; it supplies no authority to enlarge §1407’s temporary pretrial grant.

## Law After Decision

Section 1407’s return command cannot be displaced by a transferee’s §1404 self-transfer. Authorized pretrial adjudication remains distinct from unauthorized trial retention, and a preserved objection secures the statutory forum remedy without independent proof that the verdict would differ. No general subject-matter-jurisdiction invalidity, bar on consensual arrangements or decision about a later lawful transfer follows.
