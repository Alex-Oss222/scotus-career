### Event

Air Line Pilots Association v. Miller, No. 97-428. Plenary merits decision, May 26, 1998. Argued March 23, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, 108 F.3d 1415. The question is whether nonmember employees who have not agreed to arbitrate must exhaust a union's impartial arbitration procedure before bringing their compulsory-fee objections to federal court. Review concerns exhaustion for the 1992 fee-calculation dispute. Earlier facial challenges to the agency-shop clause were resolved against the pilots and were not appealed; they and other fee years are outside this review. The district court required exhaustion; the court of appeals rejected that requirement and remanded the remaining 1992 fee disputes.

### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

### Public Action

The judgment is affirmed on the exhaustion question.

### Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 7–2.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. **Opposing:** Stevens and Breyer. The nonconsenting objectors may pursue independent judicial adjudication without completing the union's arbitration as a prerequisite. The D.C. Circuit's bounded remand remains; no refund, final chargeable percentage, particular expenditure ruling or discovery plan is ordered.

### Opinion Topology

Souter delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Breyer dissents, joined by Stevens.

### Holdings

**Holding and operative rule:** Nonmember employees who did not agree to arbitrate need not exhaust this union-provided impartial arbitration procedure before bringing their compulsory-fee claims in federal court. Hudson's requirement that a union offer a prompt impartial opportunity to contest fees does not make that private procedure a compulsory preliminary step; the ruling does not displace express statutory exhaustion requirements, appropriate administrative exhaustion or an actual arbitration agreement.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion for the seven Justices identified in Opinion Topology; all seven adopt the complete rule, its explanation and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Hudson protects employees from having to finance activities beyond the lawful scope of compulsory fees. A procedure supplied for that protection does not become a condition of judicial access merely because the union adopts it. AT&T Technologies and First Options require an agreement before contractual arbitration can bind a party; participation solely to protest arbitral authority does not provide that agreement. But nonconsent alone does not answer every exhaustion question. McCarthy requires attention to statutory design and the actual institution involved. Here Congress has not allocated initial decisionmaking to this private arbitrator. The arbitrator does not complete the work of a statutory agency or act as the union's final policymaking authority.

ALPA argues that a nonbinding first step could settle claims, develop facts and reduce expense without displacing later court review. Those possible benefits do not establish a compulsory gate under this arrangement. Objectors must identify the nature of their objections sufficiently for litigation, while the union retains its burden to establish that challenged expenditures are germane to collective representation. Courts retain balanced discretion to defer discovery or other proceedings while prompt arbitration may narrow the actual dispute. Such case management does not make union arbitration a universal prerequisite or give its award contractual finality.

The plaintiffs either did not participate or participated under protest. They remain entitled to independent judicial treatment. The Court does not decide consenting parties' obligations, any particular expenditure's chargeability, audit adequacy or the final amount owed.

### Precedent Treatment

- **Chicago Teachers Union v. Hudson, 475 U.S. 292:** Applied as a requirement to provide an adequate fee explanation, a reasonably prompt impartial opportunity and escrow for reasonably disputed amounts; it is not converted into compulsory exhaustion or preclusive arbitral review.
- **AT&T Technologies v. Communications Workers, 475 U.S. 643:** Applied to preserve agreement as the source of contractual arbitration authority.
- **First Options of Chicago v. Kaplan, May 22, 1995:** Applied to antecedent assent; a protest appearance does not itself establish consent. Its arbitrability rule does not decide every noncontractual exhaustion duty.
- **McCarthy v. Madigan, March 4, 1992:** Applied to the actual statutory scheme and institutional function. Its surviving judicial-exhaustion framework is neither overruled nor expanded into a universal no-consent exception.

### Law After Decision

The union's impartial procedure remains an available protection, but these nonconsenting fee objectors may seek independent federal adjudication directly. The decision adds this private-union application without altering lawful statutory exhaustion, agreed arbitration, ordinary judicial case management or the union's substantive burden to justify chargeable expenditures. The amount and legality of the disputed fees remain to be determined.

### Separate Writings

Breyer, joined by Stevens, would reverse the categorical rejection of exhaustion and remand for a bounded initial-recourse rule. Hudson's balance between prompt protection of objectors and the legitimate collection of representative expenses, informed by Street, Allen and Abood, permits an impartial, expeditious and adequate nonbinding procedure to narrow the dispute before court litigation. Their rule does not depend on contractual assent: escrow and genuinely independent judicial adjudication would protect the employees while preliminary arbitration could avoid duplicate expense. It gives the award neither preclusion nor contractual finality. Pilots who already completed arbitration have taken that step; no automatic loss follows for nonparticipants without considering the lawfulness and actual adequacy of the procedure and their posture. Both agree that a discretionary judicial pause for prompt arbitration can address practical duplication.

### Procedure After Action

The fee dispute returns under the court of appeals' remand for independent findings and appropriate proceedings on the remaining issues. The exhaustion question is resolved; the Court directs neither a final fee award nor a particular course of discovery.

### Source Notes

[Miller v. Air Line Pilots Association, 108 F.3d 1415](https://static.case.law/f3d/108/html/1415-01.html) supplies the lower proceedings, the pilots' nonconsent and the remand's scope. The parties do not dispute Hudson's application to this RLA arrangement; that premise does not decide its application to every private-union arrangement. No finding of consent is drawn from participation under protest.

### Event

Calderon v. Ashmus, No. 97-391. Article III threshold decision, May 26, 1998. Argued March 24, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 123 F.3d 1199. The court of appeals affirmed a preliminary injunction barring California officials from invoking chapter 154's special capital-habeas procedures against the prisoner class, with a clarification tied to current state law. Review was granted on Eleventh Amendment and First Amendment questions. The Court resolves the antecedent Article III question and does not reach those granted issues.

### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

### Public Action

The judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded with instructions to dismiss the complaint for lack of an Article III controversy.

### Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded for jurisdictional dismissal, 9–0.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The declaration that California is unqualified and the injunction preventing officials from asserting chapter 154 cannot remain in force. Dismissal establishes neither California's qualification nor a prisoner's default, and it awards no habeas relief.

### Opinion Topology

Stone-Zsela delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Breyer concurs, joined by Souter; both also join the Court's opinion in full.

### Holdings

**Holding and operative rule:** A prisoner class may not obtain a standalone declaration selecting chapter 154 as inapplicable to future habeas litigation when that declaration would settle only an isolated procedural issue and would resolve neither entitlement to habeas relief nor a complete independent legal claim. This action lacks an Article III controversy and must be dismissed; chapter 154's applicability may be determined when properly presented in a concrete habeas proceeding.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion for all nine Justices controls the rule, its application and jurisdictional remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Declaratory Judgment Act supplies a remedy within an actual controversy, not authority to obtain advance answers to every legal issue that may affect another suit. Aetna permits a conclusive determination of adverse legal relations. Seminole likewise recognizes judicial power to determine an existing statutory duty conclusively even when mediation or executive steps follow. Neither an immediate coercive remedy nor the absence of later proceedings is indispensable.

This complaint does something different. It attempts to settle one procedural feature of the prisoners' eventual custody challenges, leaving the actual habeas controversies to be brought and decided elsewhere. Coffman and Wycoff require attention to that incompleteness rather than merely the importance of the desired advance answer. California's asserted eligibility and the shorter filing regime create serious practical concerns, especially given the reported delays in appointing counsel. But the usefulness of resolving a prospective litigation issue does not make that issue a complete independent legal relation suitable for this declaration. Texas and Ohio Forestry preserve anticipatory adjudication of a sufficiently concrete dispute; they do not dispense with that requirement.

The qualification issue belongs in an actual habeas case with the applicable statutory predicates before the court. The Court decides no individual clock, counsel-system deficiency or claim default. It reaches neither the Eleventh Amendment nor the First Amendment objections, California's actual qualification, underlying habeas merits, §1983 liability or fees. Dismissal does not depend on a categorical objection to declaratory relief or pre-enforcement review.

### Precedent Treatment

- **Aetna Life Insurance Co. v. Haworth, 300 U.S. 227:** Applied for a definite controversy and conclusive declaratory resolution of adverse legal relations.
- **Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, March 27, 1996:** Distinguished because its binding adjudication determines an existing statutory negotiation duty; its rule permitting conclusive declarations despite later statutory steps remains intact.
- **Coffman v. Breeze Corporations, 323 U.S. 316:** Applied to the attempted advance determination of an isolated issue rather than a complete controversy.
- **Public Service Commission v. Wycoff Co., 344 U.S. 237:** Applied to distinguish a conclusive legal relation from a separated issue expected to arise in later litigation.
- **Texas v. United States, March 31, 1998:** Preserved; actual fitness and present hardship govern anticipatory review, without making strategic importance alone sufficient.
- **Ohio Forestry Association v. Sierra Club, May 18, 1998:** Preserved at its concrete-dispute and present-hardship boundary; it does not decide this standalone habeas-procedure action.
- **Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co., June 2, 1997:** Applied in resolving the actual Article III defect before contested merits here, without an exhaustive rule for all threshold sequences.

### Law After Decision

The decision adds a boundary for standalone chapter-154 qualification litigation. It preserves conclusive declaratory judgments and proper anticipatory challenges, while requiring this procedural issue to arise in an actual controversy. Chapter 154's separate qualification, counsel, transcript-deferral, filing, tolling and claim-scope provisions remain unchanged. Neither prospective-officer doctrine nor §1983 supplies jurisdiction for the same isolated declaration merely under another label. No particular independent surviving claim is identified or sustained.

### Separate Writings

Breyer, joined by Souter, emphasizes a possible route to prompt determination in actual habeas litigation. A timely petition may present an amendment question for which chapter 154's applicability matters; the ordinary amendment rules and the more restrictive chapter-154 rules remain distinct. An appropriate ruling may also permit an interlocutory appeal under §1292(b), but only if the district court states in writing that there is a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion and a prospect of materially advancing the litigation's termination, and the court of appeals permits an application made within ten days. An application does not itself stay proceedings. These possibilities promise no amendment, certification, appeal, exhaustion determination or additional filing time for any prisoner. They explain a concrete adjudicative route without treating convenience or aggregate efficiency as Article III jurisdiction.

### Procedure After Action

The case returns for dismissal of the complaint and removal of the declaration and injunction. The Court orders no habeas filing, deadline extension or adjudication of California's eligibility. Any properly presented qualification issue in an actual habeas proceeding must be resolved under that proceeding's independent statutory and jurisdictional requirements.

### Source Notes

[Ashmus v. Calderon, 123 F.3d 1199](https://static.case.law/f3d/123/html/1199-01.html) supplies the lower injunction, its interlocutory review and the reported counsel-system findings. Those findings are not a determination by this Court that California fails chapter 154, and the aggregate appointment delay establishes no particular prisoner's filing deadline. The lower court did not decide the merits of §1983 liability on the interlocutory appeal.

### Event

County of Sacramento v. Lewis, No. 96-1337. Plenary merits decision, May 26, 1998. Argued December 9, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 98 F.3d 434. The case concerns the constitutional culpability required when a rapid police pursuit ends in an unintended fatal collision. After final summary judgment, the court of appeals reversed Deputy Smith's individual qualified-immunity judgment using a deliberate-indifference or reckless-disregard standard. Its affirmance of the municipal judgments and the unappealed state-law dispositions are outside this Court's review. No Fourth or Eighth Amendment claim is presented.

### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

### Public Action

The reviewed judgment against Deputy Smith is reversed.

### Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed, 9–0.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The case returns for proceedings consistent with termination of the reviewed federal individual claim, leaving the district court's favorable individual judgment in effect. Six Justices adopt the Court's contextual constitutional ground; Scalia and Thomas rely on a distinct constitutional ground, and Stevens relies only on qualified immunity. No damages, trial, new evidentiary hearing, municipal retrial or state-law judgment is directed.

### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Supports reversal | Complete rapid-pursuit culpability rule, application, qualifications and remedy |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Stevens | No other Justice | Supports reversal | Individual immunity because May 22, 1990 law did not make this conduct's federal unlawfulness sufficiently apparent; reserves constitutional merits |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Scalia | Thomas | Supports reversal | Distinct constitutional ground; no join in the Court's conscience-based purpose-to-harm standard |
| Concurrence | Breyer | No other Justice | Fully joins the Court and supports reversal | Preserves flexibility to decide an appropriate case on immunity before difficult constitutional merits |

### Holdings

**Holding and operative rule:** In the rapidly evolving high-speed pursuit presented here, where officers must make instant choices amid competing enforcement and safety obligations, reckless or deliberately indifferent risk-taking alone does not establish a substantive-due-process violation. A purpose to cause harm unrelated to a legitimate law-enforcement objective is required; because this plaintiff-favorable record does not establish that purpose, the reviewed federal individual claim fails.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes for the complete rule and application. Neither the two distinct constitutional votes nor Stevens's immunity vote supplies additional support for that rule. No combination of separate rationales is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** Rochin identifies the constitutional boundary against arbitrary executive abuse; its conscience-shocking standard must be applied through precedent, constitutional tradition and the actual setting, not a judge's personal reaction. Daniels excludes negligence but left intermediate culpability unresolved. Whitley explains why competing obligations and an emergency matter to fault, while reserving the civilian due-process question. Farmer's actual-awareness standard concerns prison safety with a materially different opportunity to respond; Doe likewise requires knowledge, authority and a reasonable opportunity for protection against deliberate abuse. Those holdings do not mechanically determine culpability in a rapid pursuit.

The officer chose to pursue, and the favorable account includes dangerous speed, close following distance, danger to others, inadequate communication and alleged policy violations. The lack of a serious predicate offense and the ability to abandon pursuit are substantial criticisms. They do not, in this rapidly developing encounter, establish the improper harmful purpose required for constitutional liability. The collision was unintended, and the supported record supplies no purpose to injure unrelated to enforcement. The Court finds neither wise police judgment nor an adjudicated benevolent motive.

Actual opportunity for deliberation remains important. This rule is not a universal intent requirement, an automatic deliberate-indifference rule whenever an official could desist, or permission to manufacture an emergency. Purposeful collisions, deliberate terrorizing conduct, contrived encounters and materially different opportunities for considered action require their own facts and analysis. The decision creates no seconds-based cutoff, broad noncustodial immunity or mandatory order between constitutional merits and qualified immunity. It resolves neither the excluded municipal and state claims nor a procedural-due-process challenge to state remedies.

### Precedent Treatment

- **Rochin v. California, 342 U.S. 165:** Applied as the executive-abuse boundary, with the required culpability identified through the actual emergency setting.
- **Daniels v. Williams, 474 U.S. 327:** Its negligence exclusion remains; the Court resolves the previously open intermediate-culpability question only for this rapid pursuit.
- **Whitley v. Albers, 475 U.S. 312:** Its competing-obligation analysis informs the rule, but its prison-security holding is not treated as already deciding civilian pursuit liability.
- **Farmer v. Brennan, June 6, 1994:** Distinguished by the custodial duty and actual opportunity for response. Its awareness, reasonable-response and permissible-inference rules remain intact.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District, October 16, 1995:** Distinguished because knowing supervisory acquiescence in deliberate abuse requires authority, opportunity and causal participation not supplied merely by continuing this pursuit.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights, February 26, 1992:** Its bounded workplace-omission holdings do not automatically resolve affirmative police conduct or merge the underlying right with municipal attribution.
- **Pinder v. Johnson, May 21, 1996:** Its distinction between nonprotection and affirmative deprivation remains; it did not decide this culpability question.
- **Uhlrig v. Harder, February 18, 1997:** Its conduct-time immunity disposition is preserved without importing the lower court's unadopted merits test or a mandatory merits-first sequence.
- **United States v. Lanier, March 31, 1997:** Its rejection of categorical immunity for noncustodial official wrongdoing remains. A governing specific constitutional provision must be used where it covers a claim; that principle does not itself dispose of every due-process claim.
- **Albright v. Oliver, January 24, 1994:** Its fractured judgment supplies no controlling general displacement rationale for this case.
- **Washington v. Glucksberg, June 26, 1997:** Its assistance-in-suicide holding remains limited; no exclusive most-specific-history methodology governing all liberties is established or displaced here.

### Law After Decision

The decision establishes a purpose-to-harm requirement for the supported rapid-pursuit setting and rejects the circuit's use of reckless disregard or deliberate indifference alone there. Existing deliberate-indifference rules retain their distinct duties, opportunities for response and proof requirements. Constitutional wrongdoing remains separate from qualified immunity, municipal attribution and state-law liability. A purposeful or materially more deliberative encounter is not resolved merely by calling it a pursuit.

### Separate Writings

Stevens concurs only in the judgment. He would resolve the individual claim on qualified immunity without deciding the difficult constitutional standard. All relevant law existing on May 22, 1990 must be considered under the particularized-notice approach. Garner and Brower concern intentional force or intentionally applied stopping means; Checki concerns intentional misuse of police power to terrorize; Wood II concerns affirmative interference with protective circumstances. The reported pursuit decisions did not establish this claim's constitutional boundary merely by recognizing a possible egregious case, and Daniels reserved intermediate culpability. Neither the absence of an identical case nor a tally of judicial disagreement decides immunity. On the complete favorable account, the existing law did not make this pursuit's federal unlawfulness sufficiently apparent. Later decisions cannot supply retrospective notice. He makes no finding that the conduct was constitutional.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurs in the judgment on a different constitutional ground. The carefully described asserted right against this reckless pursuit and unintended collision lacks sufficient textual, historical or precedential support. A general protection of life or bodily security does not by itself define the constitutional tort. They reject the Court's reliance on an open-ended conscience standard and do not join its affirmative purpose-to-harm definition, although they agree that these facts establish no constitutional violation. Glucksberg does not impose a universally exclusive history method; their objection rests on the absence of adequate support for this particular asserted right and on the limits of judicially defined constitutional tort liability. Their position does not categorically exclude direct intentional assaults outside custody.

Breyer fully joins the Court. He separately preserves flexibility to decide a suitable case on qualified immunity before resolving a difficult or poorly presented constitutional question. That approach is consistent with Uhlrig and the distinction between actual wrongdoing and conduct-time notice. Today's choice to resolve the pursuit standard is not a compulsory merits-first sequence for every later case.

### Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit must give effect to reversal of its ruling against Smith and termination of the reviewed federal individual claim. The district court's favorable individual judgment remains in effect. The municipal judgments and unappealed state-law dispositions are not reopened, and no additional evidentiary proceeding or relief is ordered.

### Source Notes

[Lewis v. Sacramento County, 98 F.3d 434](https://static.case.law/f3d/98/html/0434-01.html) supplies the final-judgment posture, favorable pursuit facts and the undisputed absence of an intended collision. Alleged departmental violations remain distinct from federal constitutional culpability. The lower account does not establish that the riders were dangerous felons or that Smith had a purpose to injure. The immunity notice date is May 22, 1990.
