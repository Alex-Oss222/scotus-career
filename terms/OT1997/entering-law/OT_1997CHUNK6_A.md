## Standards and Tests selection: Actual and justifiable reliance for the actual-fraud discharge exception

### Actual and justifiable reliance for the actual-fraud discharge exception

**Governing question:** Reliance and the extent of nondischargeable liability for an actual-fraud acquisition under §523(a)(2)(A).

**Current rule:** The creditor must prove actual and justifiable reliance by the ordinary preponderance standard. Justifiability depends on that recipient’s knowledge, capacities and circumstances. No universal investigation duty applies, but known or personally obvious falsity defeats justifiability and an actually discovered warning may require inquiry under that individualized standard. Objective imprudence can bear on whether claimed actual reliance occurred, but is not a separate universal prudence condition. Fraud, reliance, justifiability, the obtained-by nexus and the extent of nondischargeable debt remain distinct.

Once money or property is acquired through qualifying §523(a)(2)(A) fraud, the nondischargeable debt includes legally enforceable liability arising from that acquisition, not merely the value received. Connected punitive or treble liability remains included even if wholly punitive, as do valid attributable attorney's fees and costs. State law supplies the underlying liability; federal law supplies discharge treatment. A qualifying federal fraud acquisition and the legal connection remain indispensable: a consumer-fraud label or treble award alone supplies no fraud predicate, and lawful receipts, unrelated obligations and unsupported fee claims remain outside the rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Field — Part I, actual and justifiable reliance, 1995-11-28; Souter for all nine, including the two Justices dissenting only from the application and remand.
- Qualifying federal predicate and debt extent: In re Cohen, In re Cohen — I. Federal fraud remains a necessary predicate, In re Cohen — II. The exception covers valid liability for the fraudulent acquisition, In re Cohen — III. The existing treble judgment is nondischargeable even assuming a wholly punitive enhancement and In re Cohen — IV. Existing attributable fees and costs share the debt's treatment; no new award is made, Thomas for all nine, 1997-06-30.

**Present operation effective:** In re Cohen, 1997-06-30, resolves debt extent; Field's reliance standard remains effective from 1995-11-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Subsection (A) excludes statements respecting the debtor’s or an insider’s financial condition. Subsection (B) separately requires a materially false written statement of that kind, reasonable creditor reliance and the debtor’s causing publication with intent to deceive. This holding does not separately classify a claim of false pretenses or representation without actual fraud.

**Holdings navigation:** Field v. Mans, 516 U.S. 59. In re Cohen (1997-06-30) — Bankruptcy / Fraud discharge exceptions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Meaningful review of punitive-damage amounts

### Meaningful review of punitive-damage amounts

**Governing question:** Due process protection against arbitrary punitive amounts, including meaningful judicial review, substantive excessiveness and territorial limits.

**Current rule:** A State may not remove meaningful judicial review of the amount of a punitive-damage award without an adequate substitute protecting against arbitrary deprivation. Review limited to whether any evidence supports an award, or safeguards directed only to entitlement rather than amount, do not necessarily fill that gap. The inquiry concerns meaningful protection against arbitrary magnitude; it does not establish a fixed constitutional ratio, mandatory remittitur, a prescribed written-opinion format or a required number of reviewing courts. Independently of adequate procedure, due process forbids punitive awards grossly excessive in relation to legitimate punishment and deterrence; fair notice includes possible severity as well as punishable conduct. Review considers reprehensibility, the relationship to actual or supported potential harm to the plaintiff, and authorized or imposed civil or criminal sanctions for comparable misconduct. Reprehensibility is especially important; the guideposts are neither an exhaustive mechanical checklist nor a ratio or dollar ceiling. Small compensation, egregious wrongdoing, difficult detection or difficult valuation can support a larger ratio; speculative potential loss and wealth cannot replace the required relationship to the wrong. A State may not punish conduct lawful where it occurred and without impact on the State or its residents, or use punitive deterrence to impose its policy on other States. Relevant foreign transactions may nevertheless illuminate pattern, intent and reprehensibility.

**Authority by component:**

- Amount review and inadequate substitutes: Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg — Removing amount review without adequate replacement violates due process; Oregon's actual safeguards do not cure the amount-review gap, 1994-06-24. Justice Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this bounded procedural rule, without a substantive numerical rule. Justice Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the application and limited review remedy.
- BMW — Parts I and III-A, substantive excessiveness, notice and qualified guideposts, 1996-05-20; Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer (five).
- BMW — Part II, territorial punishment and evidence distinction, 1996-05-20; the same five plus Stone-Zsela (six).

**Present operation effective:** BMW, May 20, 1996; Oberg’s procedural protection remains operative.

**Open question:** The decision does not itself determine the permissible amount of the award or prescribe the only constitutionally sufficient system of review.

**Holdings navigation:** Removing amount review without adequate replacement violates due process; Oregon's actual safeguards do not cure the amount-review gap; BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore, 517 U.S. 559.

**Open question concerning territorial punishment:** Whether a State may seek to change unlawful conduct elsewhere remains unresolved. The rule imposes no universal exclusion of foreign evidence or rule governing repeated punitive awards for the same conduct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Collateral attacks on ACCA predicates at federal sentencing

### Collateral attacks on ACCA predicates at federal sentencing

**Governing question:** Which attacks on prior state convictions may be heard in the federal ACCA sentencing proceeding.

**Current rule:** The ACCA sentencing proceeding does not supply a general collateral-review forum for attacks on prior state convictions based on ineffective representation or an involuntary counseled plea. A conviction obtained through an unconstitutional complete denial of counsel, without valid waiver, is the constitutional exception. Statutory objections to whether a conviction meets the enhancement’s predicate requirements remain distinct from such collateral attacks. A defendant may pursue authorized review in the proper forum and, after a predicate is invalidated there, apply for relief from the enhanced sentence; the decision promises neither reopening nor its remedy.

**Authority by component:**

- Forum limitation, complete-denial exception and distinct statutory objections: Custis v. United States — The ACCA supplies no general collateral-review procedure at sentencing; The constitutional counsel exception and the proper review channel, 1994-05-23. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct joins to this statutory ground. The same six Justices join O'Connor's separate constitutional ground and its express remedial reservation; the three dissenters would allow the present attacks under their statutory construction.

**Present operation effective:** Custis v. United States, 1994-05-23.

**Open question:** The case does not resolve the position of a defendant who has no meaningful opportunity for authorized review because custody on the predicate has expired. Beecham’s separate conviction-selector rule is not supplied by this entry.

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant-conduct sentencing and a later distinct prosecution

### Relevant-conduct sentencing and a later distinct prosecution

**Governing question:** Whether use of uncharged conduct to select a sentence constitutes prior prosecution or punishment for that conduct.

**Current rule:** Considering relevant uncharged conduct to choose a sentence within the authorized statutory range for the offense of conviction is not itself prosecution or independent punishment for the uncharged offense. That sentencing use therefore does not alone bar a later distinct prosecution under the retained Grady rule. A disguised separate penalty, punishment beyond statutory authority and independent sentencing safeguards remain outside this permission. A general acquittal also does not categorically prohibit reliable relevant conduct proved by a preponderance in ordinary Guidelines sentencing within the punishment legally authorized by the conviction. That use supplies no new conviction, disguised separate acquitted-offense punishment, excess punishment or removal of an actual offense element from the jury. Independent due-process, evidentiary, Guidelines and Rule 32 requirements remain.

**Authority by component:**

- Relevant conduct is not a prior cocaine prosecution or punishment here: Relevant conduct is not a prior cocaine prosecution or punishment here, 1995-06-14. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, constitutional-characterization discussion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six support this rationale. Scalia and Thomas affirm only in the judgment on the narrower distinct-prosecution ground. 
- General acquittal and ordinary conviction-based sentencing: United States v. Watts; United States v. Putra — United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra — I. A general acquittal does not categorically bar these ordinary sentencing findings, January 6, 1997; per curiam opinion joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. This resolves Witte’s acquitted-conduct reservation only at that scope.

**Present operation effective:** Watts and Putra, January 6, 1997, for the ordinary acquitted-conduct rule; Witte’s uncharged-conduct and successive-prosecution boundaries survive.

**Related current rules:** Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt retains the governing successive-prosecution law. Conditional coordination of undischarged sentences addresses a later sentence separately; permission to prosecute orders no conviction or punishment.

**Holdings navigation:** Relevant conduct is not a prior cocaine prosecution or punishment here. United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra (1997-01-06) — A general acquittal does not categorically bar these ordinary sentencing findings.

The Commission’s power to change the acquitted-conduct policy remains unresolved; separate opinions do not settle it.

## Standards and Tests selection: Jury determination of criminal materiality

### Jury determination of criminal materiality

**Governing question:** Allocation of materiality when it is an element of the charged offense.

**Current rule:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to decide materiality beyond a reasonable doubt when materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense. The judge states the statutory meaning and legal standard; describing its application as a mixed question cannot remove an element from the jury.

**Authority by component:**

## Standards and Tests selection: Prior-conviction status admissions in Rule 403 balancing

### Prior-conviction status admissions in Rule 403 balancing

**Governing question:** Whether the name or nature of a prior conviction should be admitted when an adequate status admission is available.

**Current rule:** Rule 403 requires consideration of an actually available, unconditional admission that legally establishes the complete prior-conviction status element. If status is the evidence’s only legitimate purpose and the conviction’s name or nature presents unfair propensity danger substantially outweighing its incremental legitimate probative value, exclude that name or nature. General prosecutorial preference for its own proof cannot replace this comparison. The conviction remains relevant and the status element must still be proved and submitted under adequate instructions.

**Authority by component:**

- Old Chief v. United States — Old Chief v. United States — I. An adequate status admission must enter the Rule 403 comparison, January 7, 1997; Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Old Chief, January 7, 1997.

**Exceptions and limits:** This bounded substitution does not eliminate ordinary narrative evidence or evidence serving a distinct permissible purpose. Preserved error remains subject to the independent Rule 52(a)/Kotteakos whole-record inquiry; a new trial is not automatic.

**Holdings navigation:** Old Chief v. United States (1997-01-07) — An adequate status admission must enter the Rule 403 comparison.

## Standards and Tests selection: State-created liberty in prison discipline

### State-created liberty in prison discipline

**Governing question:** When substantive prison law creates a protected liberty interest.

**Current rule:** A State creates protected prison liberty when substantive law meaningfully restricts officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation. Mandatory words alone are insufficient: courts examine the genuine constraint in the whole disciplinary scheme and whether the restraint is meaningful rather than routine. This is a sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule; state codification is unnecessary for directly protected constitutional liberty.

**Authority by component:**

- Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty: Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty, 1995-06-19. Breyer’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-19; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Other direct constitutional, unexpected-restraint and sentence-duration protections are not displaced. A protected interest and required process need their own determinations. No new multifactor test, categorical rejection of punitive constraints or automatic liability follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty.

## Standards and Tests selection: Scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702

### Scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702

**Governing question:** What instructions govern scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702?

**Current rule:** Frye's mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite does not govern scientific expert evidence under the federal Rules. Under original Rules 104(a) and 702, the proponent must establish by a preponderance a preliminary foundation of scientifically reliable knowledge fitting the disputed factual question and assisting the factfinder. The judge examines principles, methodology and valid application flexibly. Relevant nonexclusive considerations include testability and testing, peer review and publication, meaningful error information and operational standards, and acceptance; none is an invariant prerequisite. Acceptance alone does not establish admissibility, and its absence alone does not require exclusion. Admissibility does not determine causation or sufficiency. No later Rule text, universal technical-expertise rule or appellate-review standard is adopted.

**Authority by component:**

## Standards and Tests selection: Retained mental-state elements and intoxication rebuttal

### Retained mental-state elements and intoxication rebuttal

**Governing question:** Exclusion of otherwise admissible intoxication evidence bearing on a State’s actual purpose-or-knowledge element.

**Current rule:** When the State’s authoritative construction retains purpose or knowledge as an element, due process requires examining that actual element, not an appellate substitute treating the accused as sober. A blanket direction preventing consideration of otherwise admissible intoxication evidence bearing on the retained mental element requires sufficient justification; responsibility for voluntary impairment and deterrence alone do not justify barring relevant rebuttal of the element retained. A general reasonable-doubt charge does not cure the explicit restriction. Ordinary rules of relevance, reliability, privilege and fair trial management remain; no absolute all-evidence right or affirmative intoxication excuse follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Actual-element premise: I. The actual state-law element governs the constitutional inquiry; evidentiary protection and justification: II. This blanket restriction denies a fair opportunity to contest the retained element, 1996-06-13. O’Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Egelhoff, 1996-06-13.

**Open question:** A genuinely different substantive definition, including a law expressly equating intoxication with knowledge, and associated notice or temporal issues remain undecided. No universal due-process method or structural-error classification is adopted. Montana’s unknown-intoxicant exception permits evidence when the defendant proves lack of knowledge of the substance’s intoxicating character when ingested; that exception is not expanded.

**Holdings navigation:** Montana v. Egelhoff, 518 U.S. 37.

## Standards and Tests selection: Voting Rights Act §5 coverage of reallocations of governing authority

### Voting Rights Act §5 coverage of reallocations of governing authority

**Governing question:** Whether a transfer of duties among government officials changes a voting practice subject to preclearance.

**Current rule:** Section 5 requires a direct relation to voting; a redistribution of governing authority does not qualify merely because it diminishes an elected official's influence over a function. Changes in voting procedure, candidacy, electorate, or method of office selection supply familiar but nonexhaustive covered categories. Reallocation of road funds within an elected commission and transfer of road operations to an appointed engineer while the elected commission retains substantial appointment and budget authority do not, on those premises, require preclearance. The Attorney General's interpretation receives substantial consideration within the statutory boundary, but a significant-transfer/different-constituencies formulation cannot substitute for a connection to voting. Postelection timing alone creates no exemption, and coverage remains separate from discriminatory purpose or effect.

**Authority by component:**

- Voting/governing boundary and retained-office scope: Presley v. Etowah County Commission — The two resolutions do not change a voting practice, January 27, 1992.
- Limit on administrative and different-constituencies constructions: Presley — Administrative interpretation does not eliminate the voting boundary, January 27, 1992. Kennedy's opinion joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, and Thomas controls both.

**Present operation effective:** *Presley*, January 27, 1992.

**Open question:** Whether an extreme transfer effectively replacing an elective office with an appointive office requires preclearance is unresolved. The decision does not dispose of independent §2, Title VI, or constitutional claims.

**Holdings navigation:** Presley v. Etowah County Commission; Mack v. Russell County Commission, 502 U.S. 491 (1992) — Voting Rights Act preclearance.

## Standards and Tests selection: Individual legislator standing for institutional dilution

### Individual legislator standing for institutional dilution

**Governing question:** Whether individual legislators may challenge abstract dilution of institutional legislative power after fully effective votes.

**Current rule:** Members whose votes were counted and given full legal effect lack Article III injury on a claim of abstract institutional-power dilution or altered force of future votes. Express statutory permission to sue may remove prudential restrictions but cannot supply personal concrete injury. Losing a fully effective vote differs from complete nullification of votes sufficient to determine an outcome or a personal deprivation of office or pay.

**Authority by component:**
- Personal/institutional boundary and statutory authorization: Raines, Raines v. Byrd — I. These individual legislators lack a concrete personal injury, Scalia with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg (six), 1997-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** Raines, 1997-06-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal ban on official or legislator litigation, legislative-exhaustion requirement or categorical rule against an authorized institutional suit is adopted. Concrete cancellation injury and complete vote nullification retain their distinct Article III questions.

**Holdings navigation:** Raines v. Byrd (1997-06-26) — Federal Courts / Legislator standing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

### Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

**Governing question:** Whether an individual former employee's challenge to an employment rule remains live after departure.

**Current rule:** A prospective individual challenge becomes moot after voluntary departure from covered employment when no intent to return, realistic recurrence or surviving claim is established. A general prayer cannot preserve the controversy by producing nominal damages against a State or an official-capacity defendant whom §1983 does not make a damages person. Consent to payment cannot create the missing cause of action, and certification of an unsettled state-law question cannot supply missing Article III jurisdiction.

## Standards and Tests selection: State compensation limits within federal new-trial review

### State compensation limits within federal new-trial review

**Governing question:** The measure and allocation of compensatory-excessiveness review in a New York diversity action.

**Current rule:** The district court applies CPLR §5501(c)'s material-deviation-from-reasonable-compensation measure through Rule 59, retaining federal new-trial procedure and allocation of functions. Pertinent state decisions guide compensation, without fixed prices or an imported state appellate hierarchy. The court of appeals reviews for abuse of discretion, independently correcting legal error and deferring to the district judge's supported case-specific assessment; the Seventh Amendment permits that bounded review but forbids a first-instance appellate valuation of jury-tried compensation. A remittitur must offer the plaintiff reduced recovery or a new jury trial, not simply impose a substituted amount; federal additur remains forbidden.

## Standards and Tests selection: Judicial claim construction and the retained infringement jury function

### Judicial claim construction and the retained infringement jury function

**Governing question:** Allocation of claim meaning and factual infringement work in a patent damages action.

**Current rule:** A patent damages action carries the Seventh Amendment jury right, while the court determines the public claim’s operative legal meaning using the claim, specification and prosecution history. Judicial construction includes terms of art and genuinely disputed technical usage or expert credibility embedded in that interpretive task, evaluated for consistency with the whole instrument. The jury retains disputed infringement, product operation, external identity and other factual questions outside construction. Technical complexity alone neither removes protected facts from the jury nor makes all patent issues legal. Courts may consult dictionaries, scientific publications and testimony to understand technology and actual technical usage without first finding ambiguity. Those sources may explain but may not displace meaning established by the complete patent, specification and relevant prosecution history properly before the court.

**Authority by component:**

- Markman — Part I, legal meaning and retained factual work, 1996-04-23; Souter for all nine.
- Markman — Part II, technical and credibility disputes within construction, 1996-04-23; Souter and all seven other Associates (eight); Stone-Zsela’s contrary embedded-fact limitation is noncontrolling.

- Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc. — I. Technical evidence may explain the patent but cannot replace its resolved meaning, 1997-05-27; Souter, all nine

**Present operation effective:** Vitronics, 1997-05-27, for permissible consultation and the public instrument's priority; Markman retains allocation of construction and factual infringement.

**Open question:** Trial allocation does not settle appellate review of every subsidiary finding. No universal pretrial construction hearing, exclusion of all extrinsic evidence, allocation for other instruments or comprehensive means-plus-function rule is prescribed.

**Holdings navigation:** Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., 517 U.S. 370. Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc. (1997-05-27) — Business and Commercial Law / Patents and claim construction.

## Standards and Tests selection: Reasonable aircraft-operator airport charges

### Reasonable aircraft-operator airport charges

**Governing question:** What the Anti-Head Tax Act requires for state or municipal airport charges to aircraft operators.

**Current rule:** Sections 1513(a) and (b), read together, permit airport-owner or operator rental, landing and other facility-service charges to aircraft operators only if reasonable. In the setting decided in Northwest Airlines, reasonableness requires fair approximation of facility use, no excess relative to benefits and no discrimination against interstate commerce. Exact equality among unlike users is unnecessary, but each requirement remains independent. A per-passenger charge is not saved merely by a service label or satisfaction of Evansville, and not every expense passed through by an airline is a prohibited passenger charge.

**Authority by component:**

- Statutory categories and three-part reasonableness instruction: Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. County of Kent — The statutory airport-fee condition and its measure, 1994-01-24. Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices adopt both this statutory construction and the stated three-part measure. Thomas rejects this construction.

**Present operation effective:** Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. County of Kent, 1994-01-24.

**Exceptions and limits:** Subsection (a) prohibits charges imposed directly or indirectly on persons traveling in air commerce, their carriage, air-transportation sales or gross receipts from those sales. Subsection (b)’s distinct first clause saves taxes not enumerated in (a), including property, net-income, franchise and sales/use taxes on goods or services; its second saves the reasonable aircraft-operator facility charges. Concession reserves or unequal recovery percentages alone do not establish a defect. No private AHTA action, different DOT standard or displacement of independent constitutional review is decided.

**Holdings navigation:** The statutory airport-fee condition and its measure.

#### Field v. Mans, 516 U.S. 59

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-967.
**Decided:** November 28, 1995.

##### Actual and justifiable reliance for the presented §523(a)(2)(A) actual-fraud claim

**Holding:** The creditor must prove actual and justifiable reliance. Justifiability depends on the recipient's knowledge, capacities and circumstances: no general investigation duty applies, but known or personally obvious falsity cannot be relied upon and an actually encountered warning may require inquiry under that recipient-specific standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous; Scalia and Breyer join the rule but dissent from the application and judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Objective unreasonableness may inform actual-reliance credibility but is not an independent universal prudence requirement. Subsection (A) excludes statements about the debtor's or insider's financial condition; (B) separately requires a materially false written statement of that kind, reasonable reliance and debtor-caused making or publication with intent to deceive. Fraud, reliance, justifiability, debt obtained by the conduct and the extent of that debt remain separate. Only actual fraud is decided, not a distinct false-pretense or representation-only theory or the nondischargeable amount.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Darden's established-common-law-term method applies by analogy without its employment factors. Grogan preserves proof by a preponderance. Restatement §§537 and 540–541 distinguish justifiability from universal prudent-person investigation, using general common law rather than any State's tort rules.

**Later-authority backlinks:** In re Cohen, June 30, 1997, Third Circuit No. 96-5155: resolves the reserved extent of liability for a qualifying fraudulent acquisition, including enforceable enhancement and attributable fees and costs; preserves actual and individualized justifiable reliance.

##### Correct-standard review before a final nondischargeability determination

**Holding:** An adverse actual-fraud discharge ruling resting on the broader objective prudent-person investigation duty does not stand merely because its findings include individualized facts relevant to justifiability. The proper standard must be applied to existing findings, supplemented if necessary, with separate determination of properly remaining obtained-by, extent and other statutory issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven direct votes. Scalia and Breyer would affirm on the existing findings.

**Material application:** The bankruptcy court expressly required prudent-person investigation, including possible legal consultation despite accepting lack of sophistication. Findings of actual reliance, detriment and effective extension of credit survive, but do not establish that every dollar of the original note was obtained by later concealment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, 7–2, through the First Circuit for correct-standard application and needed findings. No immediate nondischargeability judgment on the whole debt is ordered; ordinary preservation rules remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The common-law standard and unchanged Grogan burden govern further adjudication without supplying automatic nondischargeability.

#### In re Cohen

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 96-5155; no separate Supreme Court docket number is supplied.

**Decided:** June 30, 1997.

##### What federal-fraud predicate is necessary before determining debt extent

**Holding:** On the actual-fraud theory presented under 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(2)(A), a qualifying acquisition requires actual and justifiable reliance proved by a preponderance of the evidence. The sustained federal fraud findings permit decision of this debt's extent; a State consumer-fraud label or treble award alone would not establish that predicate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Parts I–IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual-fraud theory does not decide every representation theory. Section (A) excludes statements respecting the debtor's or insider's financial condition; (B) separately requires a materially false writing on that subject, reasonable reliance and debtor-caused making or publication with intent to deceive. Justifiability is recipient-specific: no universal duty to investigate applies, but known or personally obvious falsity and encountered warnings retain their consequences. Objective imprudence may bear on whether reliance actually occurred.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Field supplies actual and individualized justifiable reliance; Grogan supplies proof by a preponderance. The sustained findings establish this predicate without a new fraud hearing.

##### Whether the exception extends beyond the value acquired by fraud

**Holding:** Once money or property is obtained through the fraud covered by §523(a)(2)(A), the nondischargeable debt includes the legally enforceable liability arising from that acquisition, not merely the value the debtor received. A qualifying acquisition and a legal connection between it and the liability remain necessary; lawful receipts and unrelated debts are outside the rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Parts I–IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** State law supplies valid liability and federal law its discharge treatment. No new damages remedy, state cause of action or exception for every debt of a dishonest debtor is created; lawful receipts and unrelated liabilities remain outside the holding. Distinct §523(a)(4) and (6) elements are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Field's reserved debt-extent and obtained-by relationship are resolved for qualifying acquisitions. Noland reinforces adherence to enacted categories without deciding this distinct discharge question through priority or equitable-subordination law.

##### Whether punitive character removes this existing treble liability from the exception

**Holding:** The existing $94,147.50 treble judgment for $31,382.50 in fraudulently acquired rent is nondischargeable in full. Even if the enhancement is assumed wholly punitive, that character does not remove valid liability for this qualifying acquisition from §523(a)(2)(A).

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Parts I–IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The $94,147.50 is the existing total, not an addition to the $31,382.50 fraud acquisition. Wholly punitive character is assumed, not found; unrelated penalties are not covered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Noland supports statutory category discipline without making every penalty nondischargeable. Cox v. Sears's partly compensatory account leaves the exact state-law characterization unnecessary to this result.

##### Whether existing attributable fees and costs are also nondischargeable

**Holding:** Valid State-law attorney's fees and costs imposed because of, and forming part of liability for, this qualifying fraudulent acquisition are also nondischargeable. The Court affirms the existing connected fee-and-cost award with the treble judgment, without adding an amount, awarding new relief or directing further factfinding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Parts I–IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A valid fee entitlement and connection to this fraudulent acquisition are required. No Supreme Court fees, new appellate fees, future costs, interest, enhancement, distribution priority or payment schedule are awarded.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the existing treble judgment and attributable fee-and-cost award as nondischargeable, without recalculation or further factfinding.

#### Custis v. United States, 511 U.S. 485

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5209.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### The ACCA supplies no general collateral-review procedure at sentencing

**Holding:** Under 18 U.S.C. §924(e), a defendant may not use the federal sentencing proceeding to litigate these constitutional attacks on operative state convictions for ineffective assistance and involuntary counseled pleas. The statute does not supply a general collateral-review procedure; the distinct exception for an unconstitutional complete denial of appointed counsel remains, as do objections to whether the enhancement itself satisfies its statutory requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct joins to this statutory ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lewis v. United States, 445 U.S. 55 (1980): applies the distinction between existing conviction status and an unresolved collateral challenge, without extending its civil-disability rationale to every sentencing defect.
- Beecham v. United States, May 16, 1994: preserves the convicting-jurisdiction selector for §921(a)(20), without adding an ACCA collateral-review procedure.

##### The constitutional counsel exception and the proper review channel

**Holding:** The Constitution does not require the ACCA sentencing court to hear Custis's ineffective-assistance and involuntary-counseled-plea attacks, but a conviction obtained through complete denial of appointed counsel in violation of the constitutional entitlement, without valid waiver, may be challenged there. Custis may pursue available review of the state convictions; successful invalidation permits an application concerning the enhancement through an authorized procedure, without a promised reopening or particular result.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six Justices join O'Connor's separate constitutional ground and its express remedial reservation; the three dissenters would allow the present attacks under their statutory construction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gideon v. Wainwright, 372 U.S. 335 (1963): preserves the constitutional appointed-counsel entitlement and valid-waiver boundary.
- Burgett v. Texas, 389 U.S. 109 (1967), and United States v. Tucker, 404 U.S. 443 (1972): preserve the exception for unconstitutional complete counsel deprivation; they do not require the broader sentencing forum sought here.
- Parke v. Raley, December 1, 1992: preserves rebuttable regularity and lawful collateral proof rules without deciding the availability of this forum.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 921(a)(20)'s business-regulation exclusions and state-misdemeanor exclusion for offenses punishable by two years or less remain distinct from collateral review. A conviction expunged, set aside, pardoned or followed by civil-rights restoration is excluded under the convicting jurisdiction's law unless the pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibits shipping, transporting, possessing or receiving firearms. No removal event is established here. Expired-custody and no-meaningful-review cases remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit is affirmed, 6–3, leaving the 235-month sentence intact against these attacks. Original-conviction review and a possible authorized application after successful invalidation remain available under their own rules. No stay, automatic reopening or fixed sentence reduction is ordered.

#### United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1906.

**Decided:** January 6, 1997.

##### A general acquittal does not categorically bar these ordinary sentencing findings

**Holding:** A general acquittal does not prevent a sentencing court from considering reliable relevant conduct proved by a preponderance for ordinary Guidelines sentencing within the punishment legally authorized by the conviction. That permission does not authorize a new conviction, a separate disguised punishment for the acquitted offense, punishment beyond conviction-based authority, removal of an offense element from the jury, or disregard of independent due-process, evidentiary, Guidelines or Rule 32 requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No special factual acquittal, extraordinary magnification ruling, complete sentencing-element classification, or universal below-statutory-maximum safe harbor is adopted. The Commission's authority to change its policy remains undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Dowling and McMillan are applied to the ordinary proof and allocation questions; Witte is extended at its reserved acquitted-conduct boundary only as stated. Gaudin, Roy, Grady and Dixon retain their distinct scopes.

##### Each categorical reversal requires its own lawful sentencing proceedings

**Holding:** Each Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed insofar as it categorically forbids the stated acquitted-conduct consideration, and each case is remanded separately for proceedings under §3661, the applicable Guidelines, Rule 32 and ordinary evidentiary requirements. Permission to consider conduct does not establish the correct sentence, validate an otherwise unsupported finding or require automatic reinstatement of an original term.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The acquittals remain intact. No original sentence, exact range, replacement term, automatic aggregate cap or duplicate-punishment credit is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Koon's distinction between legal permission and lawful sentencing application is preserved; Witte's conditional coordination rule is not enlarged into an automatic remedy.

#### United States v. Gaudin

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-514.

##### The jury decides application of the materiality element

**Holding:** When materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense, the Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to determine it beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge states statutory meaning and the governing legal standard; labeling its application a mixed question does not remove an element from the jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materiality concerns a statement's natural tendency or capacity to influence the relevant governmental decision. The Government conceded that it was an element and did not contest the lower court's error classifications. Clause-specific materiality requirements, universal structural-error treatment and exact unverified preservation history remain undecided. - Kungys v. United States: distinguishes civil denaturalization and its appellate allocation.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana: preserves the actual existing constitutional jury/burden holding; this case creates no universal omitted-element error classification.
- Hubbard v. United States: preserves §1001 entity coverage at its actual scope; supplies no authority to remove HUD offense elements from a jury.

Lawful retrial submitting every element, including materiality, to the jury remains available; no acquittal, disposition of the separate equity-skimming conviction or sentence calculation follows.

#### Montana v. Egelhoff, 518 U.S. 37

**Docket or dockets:** 95-566
**Decided:** June 13, 1996

##### May an appellate court substitute a sober-person culpability rule for the State's retained element?

**Holding:** The constitutional inquiry must address purpose or knowledge actually retained by state law, not a substituted offense treating the defendant as sober. Montana's highest court construed deliberate homicide as retaining that element and §45-2-203 as restricting evidence; that authoritative state-law premise controls.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins on all holdings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not require subjective intent in every offense or decide the validity, notice or retroactivity of a genuinely different culpability definition, including an express intoxication-as-knowledge law.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Patterson preserves genuine offense definition and separate affirmative-defense allocations, not appellate replacement of the retained offense. Gaudin requires jury determination of actual enacted elements. Ratzlaf and Staples remain interpretations of particular federal crimes.

##### May Montana exclude otherwise admissible intoxication evidence from consideration of purpose or knowledge?

**Holding:** Due process forbids applying §45-2-203 to prevent consideration of otherwise admissible intoxication evidence bearing on the retained purpose-or-knowledge element without sufficient justification. Ordinary rules of relevance, reliability, privilege and fair trial management survive; there is no absolute evidence right or affirmative intoxication excuse.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice opinion.

**Material application:** Admission for memory or physical-capacity purposes did not cure the instruction forbidding mental-state use. Historical restrictions, responsibility and deterrence do not sufficiently justify excluding rebuttal of the element retained here. Blackout or high intoxication does not establish absence of purpose, and directed physical conduct does not conclusively establish it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 45-2-203's unknown-intoxicant exception requires the defendant to prove he did not know the substance was intoxicating when ingested by consuming, smoking, sniffing, injecting or otherwise ingesting it. It is not a general exception for involuntary intoxication, unexpected severity or lost memory. No finding that Egelhoff satisfies it or separate ruling on its persuasion burden is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Montana Supreme Court affirmed, 5–4; its new-trial judgment on both homicide counts stands. Otherwise admissible intoxication evidence must be usable on purpose or knowledge. No precise impairment, insufficiency or harmlessness finding, acquittal, release or determination of the remaining lay-opinion, sufficiency and dangerous-offender assignments follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Winship protects proof beyond reasonable doubt of actual elements. Martin distinguishes a separate defense burden from evidence remaining usable against the prosecution's elements. Chambers and Rock protect a meaningful defense subject to justified evidence limits. Victor's whole-charge review does not make general burden language cure an express contrary restriction. Medina's California competency allocation and Cooper's higher-burden prohibition remain distinct. Sullivan's structural-error rule is not extended to every defense-evidence restriction.

#### Old Chief v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-6556.

**Decided:** January 7, 1997.

##### An adequate status admission must enter the Rule 403 comparison

**Holding:** When an actually available, unconditional and legally adequate admission establishes the complete prior-conviction status element, that element is the Government's sole legitimate purpose for the evidence, and the name or nature of the conviction presents unfair propensity danger substantially outweighing its incremental legitimate probative value, Rule 403 requires exclusion of that name or nature. On this record, admitting the similar-assault judgment after rejecting the offered substitute on general prosecutorial-choice grounds was an abuse of discretion; the Ninth Circuit's no-error judgment is reversed.

The admission must establish a conviction qualifying under §922(g)(1) and §921(a)(20). The latter excludes Federal or State antitrust, unfair-trade, restraint-of-trade and similar business-regulation offenses, and State offenses classified as misdemeanors and punishable by two years or less. Conviction status follows the law of the jurisdiction of the proceedings. Expunged or set-aside convictions, pardoned convictions and convictions for which civil rights have been restored do not count, subject to the statute's qualification when the pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibits shipping, transporting, possessing or receiving firearms. No such exclusion is found applicable here; the offered instruction requires appropriate consensual clarification rather than an assumption that its original wording was flawless.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A distinct lawful evidentiary purpose requires its own showing and balance. Partial or conditional admissions need not be accepted; an adequate sanitized judgment may suffice. The preserved judgment-record objection was not forfeited by the separate credibility-instruction history. Legal adequacy of the status admission requires consensual clarification; the status element remains for adequate evidence and jury instructions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Huddleston's safeguards are applied to comparative status proof. Gaudin's requirement that the element be established remains intact; no general compulsory-stipulation doctrine is adopted.

##### Harmlessness remains a separate whole-record inquiry

**Holding:** The established preserved Rule 403 error is subject to Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos: the Government must establish, from the whole record, fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, and grave doubt defeats harmlessness. The case is remanded for that inquiry and lawful consequences for affected convictions; a new trial follows only if error requiring relief is established, without a present finding that every count was affected.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; five Justices. Stone-Zsela joins the error ruling and reversal but opposes leaving harmlessness open.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The error is nonconstitutional, so Chapman and Roy do not replace Kotteakos; Olano's ordinary defendant burden concerns forfeited error. No present harmlessness, acquittal, automatic retrial or count-specific outcome is found.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Williamson's preserved nonconstitutional-error rule is applied unchanged; Kotteakos supplies the substantial-influence inquiry, Olano is distinguished at preservation, and Chapman and Roy remain confined to the constitutional errors they govern.

#### Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., 517 U.S. 370

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-26.
**Decided:** April 23, 1996.

##### Judge and jury responsibilities in a patent damages action

**Holding:** A patent infringement action seeking legal damages carries the Seventh Amendment jury right. The court states the public claim's legally operative meaning; the jury retains disputed infringement facts, external product identity, and other properly factual work under that construction. The action's jury status does not assign every subsidiary task to the jury, and technical complexity alone does not remove otherwise protected facts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, unanimous.

##### Genuine technical and credibility disputes within claim construction

**Holding:** Judicial claim construction includes terms of art informed by extrinsic evidence and resolution of genuine technical-usage or expert-credibility disputes embedded in determining claim meaning. The judge evaluates that evidence against the claim, specification, prosecution history, and the instrument's coherence. This trial allocation neither makes every evidentiary proposition intrinsically legal nor determines appellate review of every subsidiary finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join. His concurrence cannot narrow this direct majority holding; no *Marks* synthesis applies.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Useful technical evidence remains available, and factual infringement work outside construction remains with its proper factfinder. No universal de novo subsidiary review, mandatory separate construction hearing, allocation for every other instrument, or general means-plus-function rule is decided. Uniformity corroborates the historical allocation but cannot alone override a protected jury function.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Tull*, *Terry*, and *Wooddell* distinguish a legal action's jury protection from allocation of particular functions. *Silsby*, *Bischoff*, *Heald*, *Singer*, and *Winans* retain distinctions among instrument construction, technical explanation, comparison of external inventions, and infringement; they do not require a jury for every input into claim meaning. *Gaudin*'s criminal-element holding and *Thompson*'s independent custody review concern different tasks and remain unchanged.

##### Construction of claims 1 and 10 and the independent sufficiency ground

**Holding:** The claim, specification, and prosecution history require clothing as part of the patented inventory. Cash totals or invoices alone cannot satisfy the integrated article-related detection and localization function.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the Court opinion, unanimous.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even crediting petitioners' supported technical evidence favorably and assuming their proposed jury allocation, no reasonable cash-only interpretation performs that function. Ordinary judgment-as-a-matter-of-law principles independently sustain noninfringement because the parties concede noninfringement under a clothing-inclusive construction.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III, unanimous; this independent record-specific ground does not narrow Part II.

**Material application:** General cash/invoice usage, advertising, and technical testimony do not erase this instrument's article requirement. No adverse credibility choice is necessary; subjective intended coverage and legal opinion cannot expand the public grant. At least one scanner and possible batch or transaction processing do not remove the clothing component or require a particular scanning count, permanent item-by-item architecture, or universal tracking technology.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm noninfringement judgment as a matter of law on claims 1 and 10, 9–0. No construction hearing, new trial, restored infringement verdict, or damages award follows. Claim 14's adverse verdict is not reopened; validity, damages, and other unreviewed matters retain their actual status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general rule makes any intrinsic evidence defeat any expert account, permits disbelieving a witness on judgment as a matter of law, or presumes noninfringement. No validity, damages, or general §112 paragraph 6 question is decided.

#### Gasperini v. Center for Humanities, Inc., 518 U.S. 415 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-719.
**Decided:** June 24, 1996.

##### Whether New York’s compensation standard governs federal diversity new-trial review

**Holding:** In this diversity action governed by New York damages law, the federal district court must assess excessiveness under CPLR section 5501(c)'s material-deviation measure through Rule 59. The state compensation constraint applies without importing the State's appellate hierarchy, displacing federal new-trial procedure, creating a fixed damages cap or authorizing federal additur.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** CPLR §5501(c) directs the Appellate Division’s specified itemized-money-verdict review, but authoritative New York cases apply the same material-deviation measure at trial with sparing discretion. Rule 59 preserves the federal new-trial motion and function without prescribing an immutable substantive degree of excessiveness. The state construction, not the statute’s named actor alone, supports trial application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule incorporates every outcome-affecting state procedure, appellate hierarchy, photographic tariff, state conditional increase or punitive-damages standard.

##### Which federal court applies that standard and what appellate review is permissible

**Holding:** The district judge must exercise the federal new-trial function using the state measure, while the court of appeals reviews that determination for abuse of discretion, independently correcting legal error and deferring to the district judge's supported case-specific assessment. The Seventh Amendment permits that review of trial-court discretion but does not permit the appellate court to substitute its own first-instance valuation of the jury's compensatory award; this judgment must be vacated for district reconsideration under the proper allocation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. Stevens joins Part I only.

**Material application:** The unexplained district denial does not demonstrate application of the state measure, but proves no particular wrong formula. The appellate court’s first operative valuation cannot substitute for the assigned trial assessment. Comparable awards guide rather than fix price.

**Limits and questions not reached:** If remittitur is ordered, the plaintiff must choose between the reduced award and a new jury trial; an unconditional reduced judgment and federal additur are not authorized. No remittitur acceptance is established. The separate industry-evidence admissibility ruling remains undisturbed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacated and remanded, 6–3, for district reconsideration of Rule 59 under the state measure and genuine abuse-of-discretion appellate review. Neither $450,000 nor $100,000 is selected; no automatic retrial. Stevens would retain the existing conditional appellate order; Scalia and Thomas would restore the district judgment against this challenge.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64, and Guaranty Trust Co. v. York, 326 U.S. 99:** Apply respect for substantive state recovery limits in diversity without making every outcome-affecting procedural rule binding.
- **Hanna v. Plumer, 380 U.S. 460:** Applies the Federal Rule coverage inquiry before an unguided choice of law. Rule 59's traditional new-trial ground coexists here with the state compensation measure.
- **Byrd v. Blue Ridge Rural Electric Cooperative, Inc., 356 U.S. 525:** Preserves federal institutional allocation rather than requiring reproduction of the state reviewing hierarchy.
- **Browning-Ferris Industries of Vermont, Inc. v. Kelco Disposal, Inc., 492 U.S. 257:** Applies its distinction between state damages constraints and federal new-trial procedure. Its express reservation of the Seventh Amendment appellate question is resolved here only by the bounded abuse-of-discretion rule.
- **Donovan v. Penn Shipping Co., 429 U.S. 648:** Preserves federal procedural control and the bar on appealing an accepted remittitur. The absence of acceptance here distinguishes that bar.
- **Dimick v. Schiedt, 293 U.S. 474:** Preserves traditional remittitur and the prohibition on federal additur. State conditional increases do not alter that distinction.
- **Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg, June 24, 1994, and BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore, May 20, 1996:** Preserve their distinct punitive-damages protections. Neither supplies a compensatory formula, mandatory number of reviewing courts or answer to this federal reexamination question.
- **Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., April 23, 1996:** Preserves function-specific jury allocation and its reservation of subsidiary appellate review; patent construction does not dictate this compensation review.
- **Thompson v. Keohane, November 29, 1995; Ornelas v. United States, May 28, 1996; and Koon v. United States, June 13, 1996:** Retain their particular legal/factual review standards. No universal independent review of mixed issues or separate Seventh Amendment holding is inferred from them.

No precedent is overruled.

#### Raines v. Byrd

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1671.

**Decided:** June 26, 1997.

##### Whether individual Members may challenge abstract institutional dilution after fully effective votes

**Holding:** Individual Members of Congress whose votes were counted and given full effect lack Article III standing to challenge the Line Item Veto Act on the asserted abstract dilution of Congress's institutional power and alteration of future votes. Express statutory permission to sue removes the applicable prudential obstacle but cannot supply the missing concrete personal injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes. Souter supplies the seventh judgment vote on a separate ground.

**Material application:** The Members lost votes that were counted and identify no personal injury from a cancellation; neither House authorized them to represent Congress.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical bar to legislator or official injury is established. Personal exclusion, lost salary, nullified decisive votes, an authorized institutional claim satisfying Article III and concrete cancellation injury remain distinct. Legislative remedies create no exhaustion requirement; no independent traceability holding is added.

#### Sandin v. Conner

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1911.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty

**Holding:** A State creates protected prison liberty when substantive law meaningfully restricts officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation. Mandatory wording alone is insufficient: courts must examine the genuine constraint in the whole disciplinary scheme and the meaningful restraint, without making state codification necessary for directly protected constitutional liberty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is sufficient and nonexclusive. Mandatory procedures, routine transfers and brief inconveniences do not automatically create entitlements; direct constitutional, unexpected-restraint and sentence-duration interests remain. No atypical-and-significant-hardship test controls.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hewitt v. Helms: narrows mechanical reliance on mandatory wording while retaining genuine substantive-predicate liberty protection; no wholesale methodology replacement controls.
- Kentucky Department of Corrections v. Thompson: applies distinction between substantive constraints and procedural directions alone.
- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves significant prison liberty and context-sensitive procedural protection.
- Meachum v. Fano: distinguishes ordinary sentence-authorized management from a genuine protected constraint.
- Vitek v. Jones; Washington v. Harper: preserve distinct directly protected, qualitatively different restraints; this state-entitlement rule is nonexclusive.
- Farmer v. Brennan; Helling v. McKinney: distinguish Eighth Amendment conditions liability from this procedural-liberty inquiry.

##### The reviewed summary judgment and remand

**Holding:** The summary judgment rejecting the reviewed liberty-and-process claim cannot stand on a mechanical reading of Hawaii’s regulatory words or dismissal of genuine punitive constraints. The district court must determine whether the rules genuinely constrain authority, whether the deprivation is meaningful rather than routine, and what process is due; the Court makes no liability or ultimate protected-interest finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Opinion of the Court and remedial disposition; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg join the bounded rule-and-remand ground. Four concurring Justices separately would find the interest already established.

Punitive labels alone do not establish liberty; similarity to separately authorized administrative or protective custody alone does not defeat a punitive constraint. Later expungement supplies no retroactive process and discretionary parole consequences are not inevitable sentence extension. Wolff permits refusal of witnesses for irrelevance, lack of necessity or safety or correctional hazards. Neither the four concurring votes finding an established interest nor the dissent's four-vote replacement framework controls under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wolff: applies witness qualifications and the separate process inquiry once protected liberty is implicated.
- Hewitt; Thompson: require whole-scheme substantive analysis rather than a one-way mandatory-word inference.
- Meachum: preserves ordinary authorized custody without making every punitive restraint legally unconstrained.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit in the reviewed respect, leaving reversal of officials' summary judgment effective, and remand for genuine constraint, meaningful deprivation and due process determinations. #### BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore, 517 U.S. 559

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-896.
**Decided:** May 20, 1996.

##### Substantive excessiveness, fair notice, and qualified guideposts

**Holding:** A punitive award grossly excessive relative to the State's legitimate punishment and deterrence interests violates due process. Fair notice concerns both punishable conduct and possible penalty severity. Constitutional review considers reprehensibility; the relationship to actual or supported potential harm to the plaintiff; and civil or criminal sanctions authorized or imposed for comparable misconduct. Reprehensibility is especially important, but these operative guideposts are neither an exhaustive mechanical checklist nor a fixed ratio or dollar ceiling.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), Parts I and III-A, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins, requiring no *Marks* synthesis. The three-Justice supplemental concurrence supplies no additional Court rule.

**Material application:** Deliberateness, recurrence, vulnerability, and the nature of the injury bear on reprehensibility; substantial punishment may be justified for economic injury without physical harm. Supported threatened harm may exceed compensation, but speculative loss cannot justify a chosen sum. Small compensation, egregious conduct, difficult detection, or hard-to-value injury may support larger ratios. Comparable sanctions inform seriousness and notice without becoming exclusive caps. Wealth may affect deterrence but cannot replace the necessary relationship to the wrong. Adequate procedures and actual review remain essential but do not dispose of a separately presented substantive challenge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No mathematical advance-calculation entitlement, wholesale importation of criminal procedural safeguards, mandatory economic model, general corporate-rights catalogue, or constitutional presumption based merely on differing jury outcomes is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Haslip*'s reasonable constraints and legitimate punitive purposes are developed into the guideposts while its rejection of a universal mathematical boundary survives. *TXO*'s five-Justice procedural-sufficiency holding remains intact and supplies no controlling substantive ratio or potential-harm formula. *Oberg* continues to require meaningful review and lawful correction of punitive amount, or an adequate functional substitute; entitlement or legal-error review alone does not necessarily suffice. This decision resolves substantive excessiveness previously reserved without treating Alabama as having the procedural defect found in *Oberg*.

##### Territorial punishment and relevant foreign evidence

**Holding:** Alabama may not punish conduct lawful where it occurred and having no impact on Alabama or its residents, or use punitive deterrence to impose its policy on other States. Relevant foreign transactions may show pattern, intent, or reprehensibility without themselves becoming objects of punishment. Whether a State may seek to alter unlawful conduct elsewhere remains open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; six. Stone-Zsela's join does not support the substantive framework, excessiveness application, or reversal.

**Material application:** Alabama expressly removed foreign transactions from its remitted amount calculation, and that correction is accepted. A separate state concurrence does not establish continued covert use of the original multiplier. Acceptance of the correction neither validates the remaining amount nor reinstates the original verdict.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding supplies no universal immunity for out-of-state conduct, exclusion of all foreign evidence, or adjudication of every national transaction's legality or another State's authority over unlawful conduct within its jurisdiction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Shutts* and *Allstate v. Hague* support attention to the State's connection with affected transactions without becoming an evidence-exclusion rule or numerical damages ceiling.

##### The remitted award and lawful redetermination

**Holding:** Even after accepting Alabama's territorial correction, the $2 million award is grossly excessive on this record in relation to the established wrong, supported harm, and comparable sanctions. Actual appellate reduction does not conclusively establish constitutionality. Alabama must redetermine the punitive disposition through a lawful new punitive trial or independent appellate determination, selecting the appropriate route in the first instance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts III-B and IV, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; five, against Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg on the judgment.

**Material application:** Deliberate recurring nondisclosure supports punitive entitlement, but the $4,000 economic loss involved no established health or safety danger or supported much larger threatened injury. The estimated 11–14 Alabama transactions illuminate culpability, not an adjudicated aggregate claim. The 500-to-1 disparity is significant here rather than universally forbidden. The $2,000 statutory comparator and other awards do not justify the magnitude; neither that statutory figure nor BMW's proposed $56,000 is a ceiling. Wealth and deterrence do not fill the gap.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse only the $2 million punitive disposition, 5–4, and remand. Fuller explanation of the same amount on the same record is insufficient. No replacement sum, mandatory remittitur, new jury requirement, or constitutional ratio is selected. The $4,000 compensation, suppression liability, lawful punitive entitlement, and BMW AG's separate dismissal remain; the discarded $4 million is not restored. Other purchasers' claims, nationwide loss, repeated punishment for the same conduct, and the permissibility of every judicial alteration remain undecided.

Their second independent objection is historical: appellate correction of legal error differs from reassessing compensation facts or evidentiary weight. Neither an abuse-of-discretion label nor a conditional new-trial option makes prohibited appellate factual reexamination permissible. Browning-Ferris expressly reserved that constitutional issue; Honda's punitive procedure and the other modern allocations do not decide it.

#### Holdings

### Deliberate injury is required; a deliberate act causing negligent or reckless harm is insufficient

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 523(a)(6) requires a deliberate or intentional injury, in addition to the separately stated malicious character; intentionally doing an act that negligently or recklessly causes injury does not satisfy willfulness. This malpractice debt, established on negligence or recklessness rather than injury-directed intent, is therefore dischargeable under that paragraph.

**Authority:** Ginsburg’s opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight other Justices identified in Opinion Topology, directly controls the rule, application and stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory modifier describes injury. Moving it to the treatment decision would make many voluntary acts producing negligent harm sufficient and deprive the injury requirement of independent work. The medical choices here were deliberate, but that does not establish deliberate injury. The exceptionally deficient standard of care found below establishes malpractice; calling it willful and malicious does not answer the federal question.

Tinker involved an intentional invasion of a legally protected right, and McIntyre involved deliberate unauthorized appropriation of property. Neither requires personal hostility, but neither makes a voluntary act alone enough. Tinker itself distinguished negligent driving from intentionally running over a victim. To the extent its broader language is read to include merely reckless injury under the present statute, that reading is rejected. Davis confirms that even conversion liability does not automatically establish willful and malicious injury. Field likewise keeps a federal discharge predicate distinct from a state tort label.

The creditors’ cumulative account of inferior treatment, canceled transfer and discontinued antibiotics establishes the gravity of the alleged wrong, but supplies no additional finding of intended injury. Nor does uninsured practice authorize a judicially created exception. Congress fixes the exceptions. The Court reserves whether subjective knowledge of substantial certainty suffices, the independent meaning of malicious, any other independently pleaded discharge exception and the treatment of proved injury-directed harm. No categorical exemption for medical debts or new criminal-law proof requirement follows.

## Judgment & Remedy

Opposing: Stevens, who would vacate for appellate reconsideration under the proper standard. ## Opinion Topology

|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–II: review and legal inferential-fit rule | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III: PCB-only application and bounded mandate | Kennedy | O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer |
| Concurrence in Parts I–II and the reversing judgment | Stone-Zsela | None |
| Concurrence, joining the Court's opinion in full | Breyer | None |
| Concurrence in Parts I–II and dissent from Part III and the judgment | Stevens | None |

## Holdings

**1. Admission and exclusion receive the same abuse-of-discretion review.** Appellate courts review a trial court's admission or exclusion of scientific expert testimony for abuse of discretion. Legal error remains independently correctable, historical findings receive their proper protection, and permissible case-specific evidentiary judgments receive deference. The importance of an exclusion to summary judgment supplies no different or asymmetric standard. Part I is unanimous.

Daubert left appellate review open. Koon and the task-sensitive reasoning of Pierce and Cooter & Gell support distinguishing correct law from permissible application; their other subject-specific rules do not transfer wholesale to expert testimony. Ornelas's independent review of ultimate constitutional suspicion and probable cause addresses another task. An appellate court may scrutinize a consequential exclusion carefully, but the Federal Rules' allowance of expert evidence does not justify a separate, particularly stringent standard for exclusions. Rule 56 protects the treatment of disputed merits facts; it does not remove the antecedent admissibility decision from the judge.

**2. Reliability includes the inferential connection to the offered proposition.** Original Rules 104(a) and 702 permit the judge to examine whether the expert's reasoning reliably connects the scientific material to the conclusion actually offered. Credentials, accepted terminology, or an assertion of a weight-of-evidence method cannot substitute for an adequate connection; screening that connection does not itself decide ultimate causation. Part II is unanimous at this legal level.

Daubert requires a preliminary foundation, established by a preponderance, of scientifically reliable knowledge fitting the factual question and assisting the factfinder. Principles, methodology and valid application cannot be separated by forbidding any inquiry into the inference drawn. But the rule requires neither a study identical to the plaintiff's circumstances nor independent proof of the conclusion from each study. Animal work, nonsignificant associations, competing causes and individually inconclusive materials may contribute to a supported combined inference. The offered theory here is promotion or acceleration, not necessarily sole causation. No later Rule 702 amendment or categorical statistical-significance requirement is adopted.

**3. The PCB-only exclusion was permissible on this proffer.** The described differences between the experimental materials and Joiner's exposure and disease, considered with the identified limitations of the human studies, permitted the District Court to find an insufficiently supported PCB-only inference. Part III controls through Kennedy, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The holding is that exclusion fell within the permissible range, not that admission was necessarily forbidden or that PCBs cannot promote lung cancer.

The animal work involved infant mice, concentrated injections and alveologenic adenomas; Joiner was an adult with small-cell carcinoma and lower alleged occupational exposure. Two reported human studies showed nonsignificant excesses whose authors did not connect them to PCB exposure. The Norwegian study addressed particular mineral oil and did not mention PCBs; the Yusho group had several potential carcinogenic exposures without an isolated PCB effect in the described findings. These limitations bear on the connection offered, rather than establishing automatic scientific disqualifications. The experts invoked a combined method, exposure information, interviews, medical records and additional research; the Court does not infer that no bridge existed merely because a source extract is incomplete. Considered at the proper discretionary level, the described fit deficiencies support the ruling.

The Eleventh Circuit's separate conclusion that evidence supported furan/dioxin exposure was not challenged in the petition. The District Court had not expressly assessed causation admissibility on that assumed exposure. Those questions require further proceedings and prevent restoration of the full defense judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

Daubert's flexible foundation, valid-application and preponderance rules are applied and supplemented by today's appellate-review rule; its distinction between admissibility and ultimate causation remains. Koon, Pierce and Cooter & Gell inform the allocation of legal correction and discretionary application. Ornelas is distinguished by its particular ultimate constitutional inquiry, not narrowed into a universal rule about mixed questions. Frye's mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite remains displaced. Rules 403, 703 and 706 and Rule 56 keep their separate functions. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Scientific expert admissions and exclusions receive uniform abuse-of-discretion review, including independent correction of legal mistakes. The reliability inquiry may examine the actual inferential connection while remaining distinct from ultimate causal proof and preserving legitimate cumulative methods. The present PCB-only exclusion is sustained within that discretionary range. No universal study-matching condition, categorical animal-study rule, compulsory expert-assistance procedure, technical-specialty rule, collateral reopening entitlement or determination of the alternative-exposure theory follows.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens concurs in Parts I–II but dissents from the application and judgment. The grant concerns review allocation, and the Eleventh Circuit should apply the correct standard to the actual cumulative method. Individually inconclusive studies do not necessarily defeat a combined inference; the described reliance on work by at least thirteen researchers and World Health Organization materials warrants attention to the whole method. He would vacate for appellate reconsideration, without commanding admission or finding causation.

Justice Breyer joins the Court's opinion in full and concurs separately. Difficult scientific questions do not excuse the judge's screening duty. Existing assistance, including a court-appointed expert under Rule 706 and lawful management of the foundation inquiry, may help assess tentative scientific knowledge and its application to an individual case. He requires no expert appointment, new hearing or mandatory panel; those suggestions are not controlling procedures.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I–II and reversal on the uniform-review and inferential-fit grounds. An expert's assertion cannot replace an adequately supported connection. He does not join the particular finding that this PCB-only exclusion was within discretion, and he preserves the unresolved exposure questions and the remainder of the tort action.

The case returns for further proceedings consistent with the review rule and sustained PCB-only exclusion. The unchallenged furan/dioxin exposure ruling remains, and causation admissibility on that theory remains open. No finding of causation, damages award, compulsory admission, automatic new trial or expert appointment is entered.
