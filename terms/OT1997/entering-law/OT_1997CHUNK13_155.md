## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA employee status under general common-law agency

### ERISA employee status under general common-law agency

**Governing question:** Classification as an employee under ERISA's circular employee definition.

**Current rule:** ERISA incorporates general common-law agency, rather than any particular State's law or a substitute based on benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship, including control over the manner and means of work, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, integration into the regular business, the hiring party's business, benefits and tax treatment. No factor, contractual label or expectation of benefits is decisive. Employee classification precedes the resulting vesting or forfeiture inquiry; this construction does not extend to statutes with broader express definitions, including the FLSA's suffer-or-permit definition.

**Authority by component:**
- Statutory construction and all-incidents inquiry: Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, March 24, 1992, “ERISA employee status follows general common-law agency”; Souter's unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Darden, March 24, 1992.

**Holdings navigation:** Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (1992) — ERISA employee classification.

## Standards and Tests selection: Paid organizing and compatible NLRA employee service

### Paid organizing and compatible NLRA employee service

**Governing question:** Whether concurrent union compensation categorically excludes an ordinary worker or applicant from NLRA §2(3).

**Current rule:** Union payment and compatible organizing duties do not categorically exclude a person performing or seeking ordinary compensated employer service. Courts independently examine statutory boundaries and compatible common-law service before sustaining the Board’s permissible inclusion. Service to two principals is possible when service to one does not require abandoning compatible service to the other; union authority to direct departure does not by itself eliminate the employer’s control over work while it lasts. Actual control, qualifications, unlawful discrimination, lawful work-time rules, unprotected misconduct and individual relief remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Town & Country — paid organizing, compatible service and retained exclusions, 1995-11-28; Ginsburg for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Town & Country, November 28, 1995.

**Exceptions and limits:** Section 2(3) retains exclusions for agricultural labor, domestic home service, employment by a parent or spouse, independent contractors, supervisors, Railway Labor Act employers and persons outside the statutory employer definition. Coverage is not confined to a particular employer unless expressly so provided. Workers whose work ceased in a current labor dispute or because of an unfair labor practice remain covered only if they have not obtained regular substantially equivalent employment. Organizing and union allegiance alone are not disqualifying misconduct; otherwise unprotected dishonesty, sabotage or work refusal remains subject to ordinary law.

**Holdings navigation:** NLRB v. Town & Country Electric, Inc., 516 U.S. 85.

## Standards and Tests selection: Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness

### Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness

**Governing question:** What instructions govern age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness?

**Current rule:** ADEA disparate treatment requires age actually to motivate and have a determinative influence on the employment decision. Service-based pension interference alone does not establish it; deliberate use of pension status as an age proxy or mixed age and pension motivation may. Age-triggered vesting and disparate impact remain unresolved. A disparate-treatment violation is willful if the employer knew or recklessly disregarded whether conduct violated the Act, for individual decisions as well as formal policies. Once shown, liquidated damages require no additional outrageousness, direct evidence or predominant-age motive. A good-faith nonreckless mistake about a statutory permission is not willful. Within the assumed McDonnell Douglas framework, the initial showing must support an inference of age discrimination; replacement by someone at least forty does not defeat it. A substantially younger replacement may support that inference, whereas an insignificant difference standing alone does not. No numerical threshold or indispensable replacement element is imposed when other evidence supplies the inference. Employer production of a lawful reason remains distinct from the employee’s ultimate persuasion burden; rejection of the explanation does not compel liability.

**Authority by component:**

- Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation: Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins — Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation, 1993-04-20. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies: Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins — Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies, 1993-04-20. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- O’Connor — Parts I–II, replacement status and relative-age inference, 1996-04-01; all nine support protected-group nonexclusion, the positive relative-age proposition and retained burdens; the eight Associates alone adopt insignificant-gap-alone insufficiency.

**Present operation effective:** O’Connor, April 1, 1996, for the replacement inference; Hazen Paper’s causation and willfulness rules survive.

**Holdings navigation:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, 507 U.S. 604 (1993); O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., 517 U.S. 308.

**Open question:** Wholesale application of McDonnell Douglas to every ADEA claim, alternative reduction-in-force formulations, particular small-gap records and the force of additional evidence remain unresolved.

#### Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1802.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### Which meaning of employee governs ERISA’s circular definition

**Holding:** ERISA’s circular definition of employee incorporates the general common law of agency, not a particular State’s law or a substitute test of benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship—including control over manner and means, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, regular-business integration, the hiring party’s business, benefits and tax treatment—with no single factor decisive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court, supported by all nine Justices; all nine Justices adopt the agency construction and multifactor inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A contractual label or single factor is not decisive. Benefits do not replace classification. The FLSA’s broader express definition remains distinct; Darden’s status, vesting, forfeiture, benefits and independent state-contract remedies are not decided. The denied Extended Earnings Plan cross-petition is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and remanded for application of all relevant common-law agency incidents and further proceedings consistent with the resulting classification; no employee classification or benefit award is made here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Community for Creative Non-Violence v. Reid, 490 U.S. 730 — applied to ERISA: general common-law agency is the default meaning of an unhelpfully defined employee term.
- NLRB v. United Insurance Co., 390 U.S. 254 — applied: all incidents must be assessed with no single decisive factor.
- NLRB v. Hearst Publications, 322 U.S. 111 — limited as a general interpretive model: purpose alone does not displace agency meaning in ERISA.
- United States v. Silk, 331 U.S. 704 — its general purposive emphasis is not followed for undefined employee terms; Reid supplies the governing presumption.
- Rutherford Food Corp. v. McComb, 331 U.S. 722 — distinguished and preserved: FLSA’s broader express definition supports its different reach.
- Kelley v. Southern Pacific, 419 U.S. 318; Baker v. Texas & Pacific, 359 U.S. 227; Robinson v. Baltimore & Ohio, 237 U.S. 84 — retained as supporting applications of ordinary agency meaning.

#### Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, 507 U.S. 604 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1600.

**Decided:** April 20, 1993.

##### Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation

**Holding:** ADEA disparate treatment requires age actually to motivate the employment decision and have a determinative influence; pension interference based only on years of service does not by itself establish that requirement. Liability remains possible when pension status is deliberately used as an age proxy or age and pension status both motivate the decision; age-triggered vesting and disparate impact are not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Aikens, 460 U.S. 711, and Burdine, 450 U.S. 248 — applied: actual motivation may be proved circumstantially; no automatic inference from any unlawful motive.
- Ingersoll-Rand, 498 U.S. 133 — distinguished: ERISA pension interference and age discrimination remain distinct.
- McDonnell Douglas, 411 U.S. 792 — preserved; its legitimate-reason terminology does not make every improper motive an ADEA violation.

##### Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies

**Holding:** An ADEA disparate-treatment violation is willful when the employer knew or recklessly disregarded whether its conduct violated the Act, whether the decision was individual or a formal policy. Once that showing is made, liquidated damages require no additional outrageousness, direct evidence or predominant-age motive; a good-faith, nonreckless mistake about a statutory permission is not willfulness.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thurston, 469 U.S. 111 — reaffirmed and applied to individual disparate-treatment decisions.
- Richland Shoe, 486 U.S. 128 — applied: negligence and mere awareness of statutory relevance are not willfulness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Pension status is no safe harbor when used as an age proxy. Age-based vesting and disparate impact are not decided. A nonreckless good-faith mistake about a statutory defense or exemption can defeat willfulness without defeating ordinary liability. No direct-evidence, outrageousness or predominant-age requirement is added.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for reconsideration of ADEA sufficiency and, if needed, willfulness. The younger replacement, confidentiality demands and comments are not held sufficient or insufficient here. Independent ERISA and state-law judgments retain their own effect; no liquidated-damages award is made.

#### NLRB v. Town & Country Electric, Inc., 516 U.S. 85

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-947.
**Decided:** November 28, 1995.

##### Union compensation and compatible ordinary employment

**Holding:** A worker performing or seeking ordinary compensated service is not excluded from NLRA §2(3) merely because a union also pays the worker to organize. After independent statutory and compatible-common-law-service examination, the Board permissibly treats union compensation and organizing obligations as consistent with employee status; the holding mandates no coverage regardless of actual control or an enacted exclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Material application:** Union organizing duties and authority to direct departure do not themselves eliminate the contractor's control of electrical work while the employment lasts. No actual incompatibility, sabotage or refusal to work is found. Protected organizing, union allegiance and concerted activity cannot themselves be labeled disqualifying misconduct; otherwise unprotected dishonesty, sabotage or work refusal may support lawful action under their applicable rules.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2(3) is not confined to employment by a particular employer unless expressly so provided. Its exclusions remain: agricultural labor, domestic service in a home, work for a parent or spouse, independent contractors, supervisors, Railway Labor Act employers, and persons outside the statutory employer definition. Continued coverage after work ceases in a current labor dispute or because of an unfair labor practice still requires absence of other regular, substantially equivalent employment. Coverage decides no qualification, discrimination, lawful work-time restriction or remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical employee-exclusion ruling and remand, 9–0, for properly remaining grounds; no unconditional enforcement of the entire Board order. Hansen's hiring, referral and discharge and the other applicants' different positions require individual treatment. Compliance must determine who would actually have been hired; no automatic ten-person backpay, amount, reinstatement schedule or bargaining-unit ruling follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chevron applies only after the independent statutory inquiry required by MCI. Darden and Reid supply service/control principles allowing compatible dual service. Health Care's functional construction is analogous within its actual supervisory predicates. Lechmere's nonemployee access rule does not assume these workers to be nonemployees. Phelps Dodge protects covered applicants without establishing discrimination or relief automatically.

#### Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc. / Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-259 and 95-779.

**Decided:** January 14, 1997.

For the other questions in Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc. / Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc., Nos. 95-259 and 95-779, decided January 14, 1997, see Federal Courts - Preservation and question-presented limits (The unpresented promotion-year issue receives no relief).

##### Count genuine employment relationships on each working day

**Holding:** For Title VII's requirement of fifteen or more employees for each working day in each of twenty or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year, count an individual on every working day during an existing employment relationship, whether or not that person worked or was paid that day. Payroll ordinarily evidences that relationship but is not conclusive: a person not yet hired, already separated, an independent contractor or within an express statutory exclusion does not become an employee merely by appearing on a list.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Numerosity's jurisdictional character is not decided. Payroll supplies evidence, not conclusive status. Title VII's own employer and employee exclusions govern.

The industry-affecting-commerce requirement and employer-agent language remain. Employer exclusions remain for the United States, wholly United States-owned corporations, Indian tribes, District of Columbia departments or agencies subject by statute to competitive-service procedures, and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations exempt under §501(c). The first-year-after-March-24-1972 transition for employers with fewer than twenty-five employees does not govern this period. The separate employee exclusions remain for elected state or local public officers, their chosen personal staff, policymaking appointees and immediate advisers concerning the office's constitutional or legal powers; those exclusions do not cover employees subject to applicable state or local civil-service laws. Section 2000e(f)'s inclusion of United States citizens employed abroad does not alter this domestic count. No exclusion is found to cover the two part-time employees here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Darden's genuine-relationship framework is applied by analogy at its common-law scope; Town & Country's attention to compatible employment and enacted exclusions is preserved without transplanting NLRA exclusions into Title VII.

##### The stipulated minimum satisfies the 1990 numerical requirement

**Holding:** Under relationship counting, the stipulated 1990 record establishes at least thirty-eight weeks in which Metropolitan had the required employees on each working day, exceeding the twenty-week requirement. That minimum resolves the 1990 numerical objection to the retaliatory-discharge claim without a remand to recompute an exact total or litigate an unsupported worker-status dispute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Thirty-eight qualifying weeks suffice. The Court does not calculate an exact greater total, classify every payroll entry or reopen stipulated employment relationships; the different nine-week compensation count is not the same set of weeks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Darden and Town & Country preserve the distinction between legal status and an evidentiary list; neither requires relitigation of these stipulated relationships.

##### The remand concerns retaliation, with the numerical objection resolved

**Holding:** The compensation-based judgment is reversed within the retaliatory-discharge dispute in the consolidated cases, and that dispute is remanded for unresolved liability, genuinely remaining defenses and appropriate relief under the correct counting rule. The 1990 numerical objection is resolved, while the promotion dismissal remains undisturbed and this mandate supplies no authority to reopen its calendar years, preservation or unknown 1988 count.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, unanimous as to retaliation reversal and remand; eight Associate joins on the promotion explanation, with Stone-Zsela agreeing only in its disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Coverage establishes no retaliation, discrimination, causation, damages or final liability; the mandate resolves only the numerical objection and preserves ordinary adjudication of genuinely remaining issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Zafiro's distinction between nonreview and merits adjudication governs the promotion limit; Darden and Town & Country provide no automatic liability or remedial award from employee classification.

#### Robinson v. Shell Oil Co.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1376.
**Decided:** February 18, 1997.

##### Former employees fall within this retaliation protection

**Holding:** Title VII §704(a)'s protection of employees includes former employees alleging postemployment retaliation by their former employer because they engaged in Title VII-protected activity. Former-employee status therefore cannot alone support dismissal of Robinson's claim; inclusion within the protected category does not establish retaliatory conduct, motive, causation, actionable harm or a remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No actionable-harm standard, new employment-nexus element, causation rule, universal postemployment tort or deference rule is adopted. The reference, motive and job consequences remain unproved allegations.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The former-employee coverage dismissal is reversed and the retaliation claim returns for unresolved elements, defenses and relief. Discriminatory-discharge merits are not reopened, and no trial, hiring, reinstatement or recovery is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Walters's §701(b) daily-count rule still excludes separated workers on later days; it does not control §704(a)'s different temporal setting. Darden and Town & Country retain their distinct relationship and statutory-context inquiries.

### January 26, 1998 — Oubre v. Entergy Operations, Inc.

[Public Projection](../records/oubre_v_entergy_operations_inc_merits_1998-01-26.md#public-projection). The following complete public blocks preserve this event's action, proposition-specific authority, published positions, procedure and source limits.

#### Event

Oubre v. Entergy Operations, Inc., No. 96-1291. Plenary merits decision, January 26, 1998; argued November 12, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 112 F.3d 787, which affirmed summary judgment barring Oubre's ADEA action because she retained consideration paid for a concededly deficient release. Review concerns statutory waiver validity, asserted ratification and prior tender, with separate monetary accounting and discrimination merits reserved.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

#### Public Action

The judgment is reversed and the case remanded. Oubre may pursue her ADEA action without first returning the severance payments; the deficient release and her retention of its consideration do not bar that action.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Reverse and remand, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The mandate removes the challenged ADEA defense. It makes no discrimination or compensation determination and orders no restitution or setoff. Any independently authorized monetary adjustment must be considered separately without making repayment a precondition to suit.

#### Opinion Topology

O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Breyer concurs, joined by O'Connor; both fully join the Court. Scalia and Thomas each dissent separately and join neither the Court nor one another's dissent. Scalia would affirm on lack of tender alone and rejects ratification here; Thomas would affirm on ratification and also preserves tender as a distinct condition.

#### Holdings

##### A deficient ADEA waiver cannot be enforced through retention or prior tender

**Holding and operative rule:** A release failing the applicable minimum requirements of 29 U.S.C. §626(f) cannot bar an ADEA action merely because the employee retains its consideration, whether the asserted bar is called ratification, prior tender or estoppel resting on that same retention. The party asserting waiver validity retains the statutory burden of proving a knowing and voluntary waiver under the applicable paragraph. The ruling neither invalidates a compliant waiver nor decides a distinct fraud, contract or equitable claim.

**Authority:** The seven Justices joining the Opinion of the Court adopt this entire statutory rule, its application and its bounded remedy. The concurrence's additional voidability explanation is not part of that holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress made minimum safeguards conditions of an effective ADEA waiver. This individual agreement omitted a specific reference to ADEA rights, allowed fourteen rather than at least twenty-one days for consideration, and supplied no seven-day postexecution revocation opportunity. Signature, actual attorney consultation and later awareness do not supply those missing protections. No group program triggering the forty-five-day period and disclosures, or settlement of an already filed charge or action under paragraph (2), is established. The latter setting has its own requirements: paragraph (1)(A)–(E) and a reasonable consideration period.

Entergy's strongest argument distinguishes initial execution from later informed affirmation and invokes the ordinary obligation to restore a bargain's benefits before repudiating it. But the asserted consequence would make this deficient waiver extinguish the very claim Congress protected. Congress need not name each contract doctrine that could otherwise circumvent its mandatory conditions. A tender requirement accomplishing the same bar cannot stand. The Court need not accept Entergy's description of common law in every setting to resolve its asserted effect here.

Mezzanatto permits its specified knowing-and-voluntary evidentiary waiver while preserving right-specific statutory restrictions; it supplies no exception to these express safeguards or burden. McKennon distinguishes an ADEA claim from legally supported remedial limitations; it does not establish a severance-repayment right. Likewise, §626(f)(4)'s protection of EEOC enforcement and employee participation remains distinct from private monetary relief.

Oubre's ability to repay is unknown and unnecessary to the holding. Lawful restitution, recoupment, setoff and the release's effect on other rights remain undecided. Preserving those questions creates no recovery entitlement or automatic $6,258 deduction. The employee must still prove her discrimination claim.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Mezzanatto retains its bounded evidentiary-waiver rule and right-specific statutory limits. It does not permit common-law consent principles to replace OWBPA's express requirements or assigned burden.
- McKennon retains its separation of ADEA liability from supported remedial limitations. Its after-acquired-misconduct proof requirement and relief rules do not decide severance accounting.
- Brooklyn Savings' protection against private defeat of a public statutory safeguard remains consistent with the right-specific distinction; no universal prohibition of statutory settlements is adopted.

#### Law After Decision

An employer cannot obtain the effect of an ADEA waiver that fails Congress's minimum conditions by relying on retention of the payment or making its return a prerequisite to the action. This construction adds the statutory ratification-and-tender rule while leaving ordinary discrimination burdens and independently lawful remedies intact. It declares neither the whole contract absolutely void nor every payment immune from recovery. Other claims, genuinely distinct equitable conduct, and a later compliant agreement require their own grounds. No automatic repayment or generalized employer accounting right follows.

#### Separate Writings

Breyer, joined by O'Connor, fully agrees with the Court but explains why the deficient arrangement should be treated as voidable at the worker's election rather than absolutely void. The statute protects the employee; absolute nullity could let the employer use its own omission to cancel reciprocal benefits even when the employee does not sue. Filing the ADEA action rejects the promise not to sue. An employer may then seek whatever restitution, relief from reciprocal obligations or damages credit the law and agreement independently permit. Their concurrence awards none of those remedies and preserves the Court's prohibition on prior tender. This additional explanation is noncontrolling.

Scalia would affirm for failure to tender back. In his view, restoration before repudiating an exchange is a surviving common-law condition distinct from original waiver validity. He nevertheless rejects ratification on this record: discovering that required statutory safeguards were omitted does not remove that impediment, unlike discovering a concealed fraud or mistake that can be knowingly accepted afterward. He therefore does not join Thomas's ratification ground, and makes no finding that Oubre could repay or that a monetary judgment is warranted.

Thomas would affirm on later knowing ratification and separately preserves the tender condition. He distinguishes Congress's minimum conditions for original execution from common-law affirmation of a voidable bargain after discovery of its defect. His background-common-law analysis, reflected in Astoria, Isbrandtsen and Norfolk Redevelopment, requires a sufficient statutory basis for displacing those distinct doctrines; he finds none here. The reported knowing retention therefore supports affirmance in his view. He does not find the original agreement compliant, shift §626(f)(3)'s original-validity burden or award restitution. His ground and Scalia's uncured-impediment objection remain different.

#### Procedure After Action

The case returns for ordinary proceedings without the rejected ADEA release, retention or prior-tender bar. No constructive discharge, age discrimination, willfulness, damages, fees, reinstatement, automatic trial, wholesale revival of state claims, new hearing or payment order is directed. Any accounting must rest on independently applicable law and the actual agreement. This Supreme Court review is complete.
