## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. As of this date the Court has not adopted a general rule governing remote historical movement in every statute.

**Authority by component:**

- Architecture; connection before magnitude; recognition of necessary inclusion; no-insertion rule: United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.
- Economic vehicle-taking class and enacted vehicle nexus: United States v. Harris, April 27, 1995; O’Connor’s eight-Justice commerce rationale. Stone concurs in the judgment on the narrower direct-instrumentality/vehicle-specific nexus ground.
- Direct interference with an interstate service market: American Life League v. Reno, November 13, 1995; Kennedy Part I, unanimous.
- Application of Harris without new doctrine: United States v. Bishop; United States v. Stokes, February 5, 1996; O’Connor Part II, unanimous.
- Anti-evasion/material-impairment rule for the post-1986 machinegun regime: United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996; O’Connor Part I, unanimous.
- Interstate commercial transmission as commerce itself: Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno, April 28, 1997; unanimous commerce holding.
- Legally fixed cross-border child-support obligation: United States v. Mussari; United States v. Schroeder, June 30, 1997; O’Connor, unanimous.

**Present operation effective:** Mussari/Schroeder, June 30, 1997, for the latest application; the controlling architecture dates to Lopez, April 26, 1995, and the anti-evasion/material-impairment branch dates to Hale, February 12, 1996.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:**

1. Define the enacted regulated class.
2. Identify the asserted interstate-commerce connection.
3. Identify the mechanism connecting the class to interstate commerce.
4. Determine whether the mechanism is constitutionally eligible.
5. Only then use aggregation or substantiality to evaluate class-wide magnitude.
6. Give Congress substantial latitude on prediction and regulatory design within the valid power.
7. Test whether the statutory class reasonably fits the asserted mechanism.
8. Confirm that the theory leaves an enforceable constitutional boundary.

**Exceptions and limits:** Noneconomic intrastate conduct is not categorically immune. Traditional State subject matter is not categorically immune. Formal legislative findings are neither universally required nor sufficient by themselves. Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope. Garcia’s rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains intact. Remote movement and generalized social costs independently establish none of the current Commerce grounds. The Court has not yet adopted a general material-interstate-externality doctrine. Mussari/Schroeder directly regulates the private obligor: it commands no State administration and gives no federal power to issue or modify support, custody, parentage or domestic-status orders. The cross-border payment rule adds no flight, travel, voluntary-crossing, bank-wire or evasion-motive element. Not every protester is an interstate enterprise; the service-market ground reaches conduct directed at provision or receipt of the covered services. Hale preserves § 922(o)'s government-related channels and prior-lawful pre-ban stock. No Second Amendment, amended-statute or independent indictment rule is established by these commerce holdings.

## Standards and Tests selection: FACE conduct predicates and responsibility for civil relief

### FACE conduct predicates and responsibility for civil relief

**Governing question:** The challenged reproductive-service branches of 18 U.S.C. §248 and the grounds and exclusions governing their civil remedies.

**Current rule:** Section 248(a)(1) requires force, threat of force or physical obstruction; intentional injury, intimidation, interference or attempt; and action because a person is or has been obtaining or providing reproductive health services, or intended to intimidate that person, another person or a class from doing so. Section (a)(3)’s service branch separately covers intentional damage, destruction or attempt against facility property because it provides those services. Interference restricts movement; intimidation is reasonable apprehension of bodily harm to oneself or another. Physical obstruction makes ingress or egress impassable, or passage to or from a facility unreasonably difficult or hazardous. Services include medical, surgical, counseling and referral services concerning reproduction, pregnancy or termination, provided in a hospital, clinic, physician’s office or other facility. The inclusive facility definition reaches a hospital, clinic, physician’s office or other reproductive-service facility and its building or structure; the distinct passage terms create no interior-only limit, geographic buffer or general outdoor immunity.

A parent or legal guardian is exempt from every FACE penalty and civil remedy, including private damages and private or governmental injunctions, insofar as the described activity is directed exclusively at that person’s own minor. For a private §248(a)(1) action, the claimant must be involved in providing or seeking to provide, or obtaining or seeking to obtain, services in a reproductive-health facility. For nonexcepted attributable violations, such an eligible claimant may elect $5,000 per violation before final judgment in place of actual compensatory damages. The First Amendment does not categorically require that violation-based statutory sum to equal proved actual loss. Liability must nevertheless rest on the defendant’s legally attributable violation; shared belief, membership, independent advocacy or another’s wrong alone is insufficient, and protected persuasion reducing demand is not itself the unlawful injury. Protected First Amendment expression remains outside liability under §248(d)(1); §248(d)(2) separately creates no new outside-facility speech/religion remedy and preserves existing ones. Other state and local remedies are not preempted.

**Authority by component:**

- Defined conduct and protected-expression boundaries: The defined access restrictions survive the facial speech challenge and The presented substantial-overbreadth challenge fails, 1995-11-13; Kennedy Parts II and III-A, all nine.
- Permissive elected damages and complete own-minor exclusion: Elected statutory damages are not categorically barred by the First Amendment, 1995-11-13; Kennedy Part IV-A, all eight Associates adopt the rationale, Stone-Zsela agrees in disposition.
- Personal attribution and protected persuasion: A damages defendant must be responsible for the violation, 1995-11-13; Kennedy Part IV-B, all nine.

**Present operation effective:** American Life League, 1995-11-13, for the controlling constructions and constitutional remedy limits; FACE applies to conduct on or after May 26, 1994.

**Exceptions and limits:** The review does not adjudicate worship branches, a particular criminal sentence, violation count, amount-specific reasonableness, entitlement to an award, punitive award or general threat mental state. Civil damages attribution is not replaced by an injunction’s actual-concert rule. The own-minor exception creates no immunity under other law or constitutional parental veto. Constitutional facial validity does not settle discriminatory enforcement or RFRA.

**Related current rules:** “RFRA review of prospective enforcement” requires the distinct person-and-application inquiry; no automatic exemption or categorical third-party-harm exception follows.

**Holdings navigation:** American Life League v. Reno.

## Standards and Tests selection: Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

### Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

**Governing question:** How a court tailors a content-neutral injunction regulating speech-related conduct.

**Current rule:** A content-neutral injunction must burden no more speech than necessary to serve the significant governmental interest supporting the remedy. This inquiry is more demanding than the ordinary content-neutral legislative time, place and manner rule, without becoming a universal least-restrictive-means test. Each restriction needs a remedial connection to the established interest and conduct. Public access, medical quiet and residential privacy may support relief, but neither those interests nor prior misconduct justifies territorial restrictions or advance-permission conditions broader than necessary. Removing an invalid restriction also removes its operative predicate; it cannot be restored by relabeling the same conduct under another clause. Within an otherwise valid fixed access zone, an exception permitting nonthreatening initial counseling by up to two people per approached person or group may end when the recipient objectively communicates, verbally or nonverbally, that counseling is unwanted. The counselor must end that close encounter and leave the fixed zone, while retaining lawful advocacy outside it; this supplies neither universal silence nor a mobile personal radius.

## Standards and Tests selection: Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims

### Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims

**Governing question:** The showing needed to overcome successive or abusive habeas barriers through innocence of the offense.

**Current rule:** The prisoner must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the whole probative record, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The inquiry includes the new evidence together with the existing record rather than deferring conclusively to the original jury’s credibility choices. Passing this demanding procedural gateway establishes neither innocence as a freestanding claim nor the underlying constitutional violation and shifts no ultimate merits burden. The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every submitted statement as true. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner.

**Authority by component:**

- Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway: Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway, 1995-01-23. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support a stricter crime-innocence quantum and would affirm.
- This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission: This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission, 1995-01-23. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, statutory-refusal and remand rationale, joined by Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this bounded rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth vote for vacatur and the same gateway-assessment remand on his stated ground, without joining the separate §2244(b) analysis. No aggregation of different rationales is needed for the five-Justice holding.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-23; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Sawyer’s clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway is distinct. Section 2244(b) permits refusal of a subsequent habeas application by a person held under a state-court judgment after a federal court, justice or judge denied release or another habeas remedy on an earlier habeas application following either an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue or a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, unless the later application alleges and rests on a factual or other ground not adjudicated in the earlier hearing and the court is satisfied that the applicant did not deliberately withhold the newly asserted ground on the earlier application or otherwise abuse the writ. Both exception conditions are required. New evidence is not automatically a new ground, and absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. When both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent, McCleskey requires consideration. The qualified refusal permission does not itself sustain refusal based on the rejected clear-and-convincing crime standard. Other lawful independent barriers and necessary factual-development rules remain.

**Burdens and proof:** The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner’s burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate.

**Open question:** Schlup does not decide whether every successful innocence showing compels review, a general appellate standard for refusal, or an automatic hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay.

**Related current rules:** Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims supplies the separate eligibility inquiry and proof quantum.

**Holdings navigation:** Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway; This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission.

## Standards and Tests selection: Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility

### Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility

**Governing question:** Assessment and consequence of suppressed favorable evidence.

**Current rule:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses in the whole record, including defense preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. No item must independently meet the standard; the defendant need not show that acquittal is more likely than conviction or that remaining evidence is legally insufficient. The prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence held by police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires. Personal ignorance does not remove it from review. Once a material Brady violation is established, a second duplicative prejudice or harmlessness inquiry cannot defeat relief by reassessing the same nondisclosure.

**Authority by component:**

- Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction: Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction, 1995-04-19. Justice Souter’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes for the rule, application and conviction-level relief. The dissent accepts collective assessment but disputes materiality on this record.
- The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team: The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team, 1995-04-19. Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. The dissent’s consideration of the police-held material does not supply additional votes for an affirmative organizational rule.
- A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing: A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing, 1995-04-19. Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes for this claim-specific ground. The dissent finds no material violation and adopts no independent harmlessness ground.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-04-19; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This case-team responsibility does not impute every government office’s knowledge or require unrestricted investigation or open-file discovery. Lawful corroboration, impeachment and investigation-related uses matter collectively; every nondisclosure is not structural error. Independent habeas gates and defense diligence concerning known evidence remain separate.

## Standards and Tests selection: Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar

### Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar

**Governing question:** The retained original-writ channel and extraordinary showing following §2244(b)(3)(E).

**Current rule:** The statutory ban on ordinary certiorari review of a circuit’s successive-application authorization decision does not repeal §2241’s distinct Supreme Court original habeas authority. An original petition receives independent consideration rather than appellate review of that authorization judgment. Circuit permission to file in district court is not a prerequisite to filing here. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform original-writ discretion; whether they bind it directly remains unresolved. Rule 20 separately requires an explanation for not seeking district relief, how and where state remedies were exhausted or §2254(b) otherwise satisfied, exceptional circumstances warranting the Supreme Court’s discretionary exercise and lack of adequate relief in another form or court. Retained jurisdiction alone does not warrant the writ, further proceedings or an execution stay. Section 1651 aids independently held jurisdiction rather than supplying a freestanding new source.

**Authority by component:**

**Present operation effective:** Felker, 1996-06-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Felker’s as-applied constitutional rejection rests on actual independent consideration and an adequate traditional original-writ ground, not approval of eliminating every meaningful forum. Constitutional innocence’s universal merits threshold, other supervisory routes, and the constitutionality of all AEDPA restrictions remain open.

**Holdings navigation:** Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal-prisoner limitation and successive-motion certification

### Federal-prisoner limitation and successive-motion certification

**Governing question:** The new §2255 limitation period and certification for a second or successive motion.

**Current rule:** A §2255 motion has a one-year limitation running from the latest of conviction finality; removal of an unlawful governmental filing impediment that actually prevented the motion; initial Supreme Court recognition of the asserted newly recognized right made retroactively applicable on collateral review; or when due diligence could discover the supporting facts. A second or successive motion requires certification under §2244 by an appropriate appellate panel of either newly discovered evidence which, if proved and considered with all evidence, would establish clearly and convincingly that no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt, or a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court. The evidence route has its own text and is not silently replaced with §2244(b)(2)(B)’s distinct state-prisoner constitutional-error/diligence formulation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Second or successive state-custody habeas applications

### Second or successive state-custody habeas applications

**Governing question:** Repeated/new claims, appellate authorization and district verification under amended §2244(b).

**Current rule:** A claim in a second or successive §2254 application already presented in a prior application must be dismissed. A new claim must also be dismissed unless it relies on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court, or both its factual predicate was not previously discoverable through due diligence and its facts, if proved and viewed with all evidence, would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found the applicant guilty of the underlying offense. Before district filing, the applicant must seek appropriate appellate authorization; a three-judge panel may authorize only on a prima facie showing of these requirements and must act within thirty days. A grant or denial is not appealable and cannot be the subject of rehearing or certiorari. Authorization is not final entitlement: the district court must dismiss unless the applicant establishes the statutory conditions.

## Standards and Tests selection: No delay-based substitute for statutory habeas dismissal

### No delay-based substitute for statutory habeas dismissal

**Governing question:** Termination of a first personal federal habeas petition through an additional equitable delay sanction.

**Current rule:** A first personal federal habeas petition meeting jurisdictional and pleading requirements may not be terminated solely for delay and litigation purpose through an additional equitable sanction dispensing with the conditions of habeas statutes and Rules. Section 2243’s law-and-justice direction does not authorize that substitute. Rule 9(a) permits dismissal for delay prejudicing the State’s ability to respond unless reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before prejudice arose; lost execution dates and general finality costs alone do not establish response prejudice, and no five-year presumption was enacted. Other procedural, abuse and claim-insufficiency grounds require their own actual predicates; failed next-friend proceedings cannot be counted as though necessarily prior personal merits petitions. Section 2243 and Rule 4 retain prompt lawful examination, dismissal where nonentitlement plainly appears and otherwise answer or appropriate judicial action, without an automatic writ or evidentiary hearing.

**Authority by component:**

- Source-bound dismissal and Rule 9(a) limits: I. Delay does not supply the asserted additional dismissal sanction, 1996-04-01; Stevens Part I, all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Lonchar, 1996-04-01.

**Effective transition:** Lonchar construed pre-AEDPA law. AEDPA’s enacted filing and successive-application conditions remain distinct and apply when their temporal and substantive predicates are met; the pending-proceeding transition is not decided wholesale.

**Related current rules:** “Equitable delay independently of a habeas procedural bar” governs the separate stay inquiry, which Lonchar preserves even for a first personal petition.

**Holdings navigation:** Lonchar v. Thomas, 517 U.S. 314.

## Standards and Tests selection: Interim protection necessary for required habeas process

### Interim protection necessary for required habeas process

**Governing question:** Execution that would prevent genuinely required habeas process when lawful expedition cannot complete it.

**Current rule:** When execution would prevent completion of process actually required by governing habeas law and lawful prompt disposition cannot finish in time, the court must provide the interim protection necessary to make that identified opportunity meaningful. It must identify the unfinished legal obligation and the timing need. Filing alone, first-petition status and a capital sentence establish neither. Rule 4 screening, lawful procedural dismissal and expedition remain available; if they permit a lawful disposition in time, the rule supplies no further stay entitlement. It preserves McFarland’s actual opportunity and proved obstruction inquiry and Gomez’s independent equitable considerations.

**Authority by component:**

- Conditional necessary-process protection: III. Protection must preserve genuinely required process when lawful expedition cannot, 1996-04-01; Stevens Part III with Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five), Kennedy’s join confined to the necessary opportunity. Stone-Zsela’s judgment support supplies no additional rationale vote.

**Present operation effective:** Lonchar, 1996-04-01.

**Exceptions and limits:** No automatic evidentiary hearing, merits adjudication, stay period or indefinite continuation follows. A successive-petition stay still requires substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted. Barefoot’s initial certified appeal and certiorari-stage standards retain their separate triggers. Genuinely applicable AEDPA requirements, including qualified chapter 154, are not bypassed.

**Holdings navigation:** Lonchar v. Thomas, 517 U.S. 314.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equitable delay independently of a habeas procedural bar

### Equitable delay independently of a habeas procedural bar

**Governing question:** Whether an execution stay may lack equitable support even assuming the challenge is cognizable under §1983 and not barred as abuse of the writ.

**Current rule:** A court considering an execution stay may weigh the State's interest in enforcing its judgment and unjustified delay in presenting a long-available claim independently of a habeas procedural bar. A supported serious constitutional claim and the irreversible consequence of execution require consideration but do not alone establish entitlement to last-minute relief; later evidence strengthening the claim does not necessarily explain withholding the underlying challenge. Equitable vacatur on that ground may proceed while assuming an open §1983 channel and without adjudicating every McCleskey abuse element. The rule supplies a contextual equitable inquiry, not an automatic bar for late claims or an automatic capital stay.

**Authority by component:**

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. #### Schlup v. Delo

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7901.
**Decided:** January 23, 1995.

##### Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway

**Holding:** A prisoner seeking to overcome otherwise applicable successive or abusive habeas barriers to independent constitutional claims through innocence of the offense must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the probative record as a whole, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. This procedural gateway does not itself establish innocence, prove any constitutional violation or shift the ultimate burden on the underlying claims; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing rule remains confined to its distinct death-eligibility setting.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: applies and specifies its probability-based crime-innocence exception without changing cause and prejudice.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333: preserves the clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway and resolves the crime-innocence question that decision left open; it is not overruled or given a categorical impeachment exclusion.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467: preserves successive/abusive-claim barriers and the exceptional innocence route, without treating potential constitutional merit as sufficient to pass it.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436: uses the relevant plurality discussion of the whole probative record as persuasive guidance, preserving its actual authority limit.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307: distinguishes trial-record legal sufficiency from the likely effect of an augmented record on reasonable jurors; its sufficiency rule remains intact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every affidavit as true or treat the original verdict as a conclusive answer. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner. A mere possibility of acquittal, a juror who might disagree, ordinary constitutional-merits prejudice or the reviewing judge's personal reasonable doubt is insufficient. The reasonable juror fairly considers the evidence and follows the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt instruction.

##### This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission

**Holding:** The courts' use of the rejected clear-and-convincing crime gateway and reliance on the original jury's credibility function require vacatur and renewed assessment on this record; §2244(b)'s permission to refuse a qualifying subsequent petition does not itself supply an adequate independent ground to sustain that legally mistaken refusal. The Court remands through the Eighth Circuit to the District Court for application of the probability rule and a reasoned determination of necessary factual development, without deciding whether every successful innocence showing compels review, choosing a general appellate standard, or ordering a hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay automatically.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's statutory-refusal and remand portion, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth judgment vote without joining the separate §2244(b) rationale.

**Current-force modification:** AEDPA §106 replaces former §2244(b)'s refusal permission for successive §2254 district-court applications. A claim already presented must be dismissed. A new claim must be dismissed unless it rests on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence and facts that, if proved and considered with the whole evidence, establish by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt. Advance authorization by a three-judge court-of-appeals panel requires a prima facie showing; authorization does not remove the district court's duty to dismiss a claim unless the applicant proves the statutory requirements. This is an independent statutory condition, not a replacement of Schlup's probability gateway or a ruling that the gateway itself satisfies the new text. The original former-law remand stands. Pending-case application is not generally resolved; Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996, No. 95-8836, applies the post-enactment authorization-review bar while reserving whether §§2244(b)(1)–(2) directly bind original habeas consideration in this Court.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McCleskey, 499 U.S. 467: applies the distinction between conditional consideration of new, nonabusive grounds and legally constrained refusal of successive or abusive grounds; neither permission nor exception becomes an unconditional command.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1, and Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293: preserve necessary factual development within their properly presented, available-claim setting and the deliberate-bypass qualification; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a required hearing there, but no successive/abusive gateway is eliminated.
- Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: requires use of the selected crime-innocence rule before the present refusal can be sustained on its innocence assessment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2244(b) provides:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The prior-hearing alternatives are a factual evidentiary merits hearing or a legal merits hearing, each followed by a federal denial of release or other habeas remedy to a person held under a state judgment. Both unless conditions must be preserved: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent. The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner's burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate; cause and prejudice is not pressed in this review. Subsection (a)'s separate wording is not inserted into subsection (b), and no new subsection (c) predicate is established.

The videotape was trial evidence. Green's asserted prompt call, Faherty's movement account and other later identification accounts must be assessed with the existing record; the Court finds no witness truthful or false and does not find the gateway satisfied. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and a genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. Remand is not mandatory constitutional-merits review. Any necessary live testimony is for a reasoned determination under the applicable rules, not an automatic consequence of affidavits or vacatur. The merits of the suppression and ineffective-assistance claims, a freestanding innocence entitlement and any ultimate guilt determination remain undecided.

No gateway satisfaction, automatic hearing, constitutional-merits review, writ, retrial, release or stay is ordered.

L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §106, replacement §2244(b)(1)–(4) district-court successive-application mechanism; see also Felker v. **Holding:** The authorization and review proceedings begun after AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment are governed by its specified authorization-review bar. **Proposition-level authority:** All nine support the channel result; O’Connor’s Part I-B has Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six. Thomas, Scalia and Kennedy use a distinct relevant-activity approach.

##### What successive restrictions and discretionary conditions govern an original petition

**Holding:** The district-court authorization mechanism does not govern filing this original application. Retained jurisdiction does not itself entitle an applicant to an original writ.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, Part II, unanimous.

A request to announce a new rule is not the first exception. **Limits and questions not reached:** Whether (b)(1)–(2) bind this Court directly remains reserved; they inform the Court’s consideration. ## Holdings

### The authorization refusal is not subject to ordinary Supreme Court review

## Holdings

### I. Direct interference with operating commercial assets supplies the required commerce connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may apply the Hobbs Act to robbery directed at the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce when the taking directly impairs its commercial operations and the Act's enacted commerce element is actually established. The effect of one robbery may be slight: aggregation measures the magnitude of this directly connected economic class, but neither a business label nor generalized economic consequences of crime supplies the antecedent connection.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt the rule, application to the Hobbs Act components and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Lopez requires the enacted class and its actual interstate mechanism before magnitude can establish federal authority. The relevant class here is robbery affecting commerce through direct interference with operating business capital, not all robbery. The targeted businesses purchased interstate inventory and cashed out-of-state, payroll and government-benefit checks. The takings removed money withdrawn for those operations and impaired restocking and check-cashing capacity; the published record also reports one business closing for lack of capital. Taking the working money in a bank drive-through lane or store parking lot does not change its established business function.

American Life League supplies the closest mechanism: conduct directed at an actual interstate market's operations can directly obstruct that market. Once that relationship exists, Wickard and Perez permit evaluation of class-wide effects without demanding a substantial effect from each individual act. The statutory commerce element still requires an actual offense-specific nexus. Its words alone would not validate a constitutionally inadequate connection, and its character as an element does not make it subject-matter jurisdiction.

The contrary argument properly rejects multiplying local losses as a substitute for a constitutional mechanism. But this record identifies that mechanism before aggregation. A theory treating every victim as a potential consumer or every dollar as eventual interstate spending would lack this boundary and is not adopted. Hale, Rybar and Kirk concern the distinct inclusion of possession necessary to a valid machinegun regime; no such anti-evasion rationale is needed here.

The facial commerce objection fails because the Act lawfully reaches this defined class; the Court does not validate every imagined application. The actual business-robbery components and conspiracy directed at those robberies likewise require no substantial individual effect. This is no universal conspiracy-elements rule. Purely personal or household robbery, remote historical movement, every asserted depletion theory, a universal dollar floor and constitutional validity without the enacted commerce connection remain undecided.

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented constitutional objection to the firearm conviction supplies no ground for relief because it depends entirely on the rejected claim that the underlying Hobbs Act business robberies exceed the commerce power. This disposition adjudicates no independent firearm element, instruction, sufficiency, aiding-and-abetting or collateral defect.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices directly adopt this derivative ground. Stone-Zsela agrees with the firearm disposition only and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower opinion identifies the firearm constitutional challenge as dependent on invalidity of the Hobbs Act predicates. Part I rejects that asserted invalidity through the actual business-interference mechanism and proved commerce nexus. The derivative argument therefore fails on its own stated premise. Neither this conclusion nor Salinas's distinct statutory-conspiracy analysis resolves an independent defect that is not presented. The Court does not infer a new admission, revise the indictment or approve the whole jury charge. ## Event

Ohio Adult Parole Authority v. Woodard, No. 96-1769. Argued December 10, 1997; decided March 25, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, reviewing 107 F.3d 1178. The questions concern due process in Ohio's capital-clemency procedure and whether the offered interview compels self-incrimination.

Woodard brought this §1983 action after receiving notice of a voluntary interview and a clemency hearing. He did not request the interview. The District Court granted judgment on the pleadings. The Sixth Circuit rejected an independent entitlement to clemency, but ordered further proceedings on an established-process theory of due process and on a possible unconstitutional condition on silence.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Sixth Circuit's directions for further proceedings on the presented due-process and self-incrimination claims.

## Holdings

### I. Required clemency procedures do not create an entitlement to mercy or triallike review

**Holding and operative rule:** Ohio's required investigation, hearing and advisory recommendation do not create a substantive entitlement to clemency because they do not constrain the Governor to grant it on specified facts. The analogy to a first appeal as of right does not itself require appellate representation, triallike evidence procedure or a new determination of guilt in this discretionary executive process.

**Authority:** Part I-A has all eight Associates, with Stone-Zsela also joining the no-right-to-mercy conclusion. Part I-B has O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela joins only the mercy-versus-criminal-trial distinction. Stevens does not join the rejection of the established-process analogy.

**Controlling explanation:** Dumschat distinguishes seeking clemency from entitlement to its grant. Sandin requires a genuine substantive constraint on a meaningful deprivation; Doe and Johnson likewise distinguish required administration from an entitlement to a favorable discretionary decision. Ohio must investigate, hold the required hearing and recommend action even after an execution stay, but its recommendation is advisory and the Governor retains complete discretion. A later hearing or revised recommendation on new information is permitted, not required. These provisions do not compel clemency.

Evitts protects the first appeal as of right within adjudication of guilt. Clemency performs a different executive function. Its availability after conviction does not itself import appellate or trial procedure. The Court rejects the Sixth Circuit's general integral-stage theory as a basis for the requested remand, while preserving independently retained constitutional rights. Herrera's record-specific innocence ruling establishes no rule that executive mercy is the exclusive constitutionally sufficient substitute for every claim of innocence.

### II. Capital-clemency procedures retain a constitutional minimum, satisfied on these pleadings

**Holding and operative rule:** An established capital-clemency process must afford minimal safeguards against fundamentally arbitrary procedure, including arbitrary denial of any access or substitution of an entirely arbitrary device such as a coin flip for consideration. The notice and offered interview here, considered with the pleaded restrictions, satisfy that minimum; they establish no entitlement to clemency, trial protections, a favorable recommendation or judicial review of mercy's merits.

**Authority:** Part II-A's minimum safeguard has six direct joins: O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part II-B's sufficient-process application has five: O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens disputes the application; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas agree with rejection of the claim on their distinct entitlement/adjudication ground. Both controlling propositions rest on direct majority joins, without combining separate rationales under Marks.

**Controlling explanation:** A valid death sentence reduces the process owed before discretionary mercy is denied, but the prisoner remains alive. That retained interest supplies a minimum protection independent of a state-created entitlement to release. Ford illustrates the distinction between the interest protected and the flexible procedure appropriate to it; this case does not import Ford's separate prohibition on executing an incompetent prisoner or its particular hearing requirements.

Woodard alleges three days' interview notice, ten days' hearing notice, difficulty preparing while postconviction proceedings continued, exclusion of counsel from the interview, chair-controlled counsel participation at the hearing, and inability to testify or submit documents at the hearing. Accepting that pleaded account, the notice and offered personal interview provided access, and those restrictions do not establish fundamentally arbitrary procedure requiring further adjudication. The Court does not find an unmentioned alternative document channel or reject the allegations as untrue. It establishes neither a fixed list of required safeguards nor an accuracy code for every disputed item in a clemency file. Johnson's rejection of a parole-file process entitlement does not extinguish independent constitutional protection.

### III. The optional interview does not establish compelled self-incrimination

**Holding and operative rule:** An optional clemency interview does not compel self-incrimination merely because the prisoner must choose between providing potentially harmful information and remaining silent; absence of counsel, confidentiality or immunity does not itself convert this invitation into compulsion. The conclusion also holds if an ordinary civil adverse inference from silence is assumed, but it does not authorize an automatic privilege penalty, compelled waiver or criminal use of compelled answers.

**Authority:** Part III-A's present voluntary-interview holding has all nine Justices. Part III-B's additional ordinary-inference ground and its limits have all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join that additional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Murphy distinguishes a difficult evidentiary choice from a command backed by a genuine threatened penalty for asserting the privilege. Woodard was invited, not required, to attend or answer. The State's refusal to promise confidentiality or immunity does not establish that it compelled answers. No automatic loss of clemency eligibility for silence, compelled waiver or criminal use of compelled testimony is established.

The eight Associates additionally apply Baxter's distinction between ordinary adverse inferences in civil proceedings and automatic sanctions attached to silence. Even assuming such an inference in this civil clemency evaluation, the voluntary choice does not become compelled testimony. The record does not establish Ohio's actual use of silence or answers; the assumption supplies an alternative legal ground, not a factual finding. LaChance preserves separately supported compulsion and waiver claims, and Johnson confirms that absence of entitlement to a benefit does not excuse an independent unconstitutional penalty. A materially supported coercive condition would therefore require its own analysis.

No general absence of constitutional protection is declared.

## Event

United States v. Scheffer, No. 96-1133. Argued November 3, 1997; decided March 31, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces under 28 U.S.C. §1259, reviewing 44 M.J. 442. The question is whether Military Rule of Evidence 707's categorical exclusion of polygraph evidence violates the accused's constitutional right to present a defense.

An Air Force investigator administered the examination before the urinalysis result was known. The examiner considered Scheffer's answers to show no deception. At his court-martial, Scheffer denied knowing drug ingestion and sought the examiner's testimony to support his credibility after impeachment. The judge excluded it under Rule 707 without an individualized reliability hearing. The military appellate court removed that categorical obstacle on constitutional grounds; it did not hold this proffer necessarily admissible.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the military appellate judgment on the polygraph-exclusion question and returns the case for consistent further proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens.

The constitutional ruling invalidating the categorical exclusion is set aside. The decision neither automatically reinstates all convictions and the sentence nor resolves actual remaining claims. It orders no acquittal, compulsory admission, new trial or individualized reliability hearing.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope and relationship to judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Reliability-based constitutional permission, its application and limits, reversal and bounded remand |
| Concurrence | Scalia | Souter and Thomas | Joins the Court and adds the category-specific credibility-role and collateral-litigation justifications |
| Concurrence | Kennedy | O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer | Joins the Court; distinguishes constitutional permission from evidentiary policy and declines the two additional justifications |
| Dissent | Stevens | No other Justice | Would affirm removal of the categorical obstacle and permit lawful foundation consideration, without commanding admission |

## Holdings

### Supported reliability concerns justify this categorical polygraph rule against the presented constitutional challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** The constitutional right to present a defense permits evidentiary restrictions justified by legitimate trial interests and proportionate to their purposes. Serious, supported concerns about polygraph reliability justify Rule 707's categorical exclusion in this setting, where the accused can present his own factual account and underlying evidence; the Constitution does not require individualized admissibility proceedings for this credibility proffer.

**Authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. The credibility-role and collateral-litigation rationales are separate, three-Justice grounds and do not become holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Chambers and Rock protect a meaningful defense while allowing sufficiently justified evidentiary limits. Egelhoff applies that protection to rebuttal of the mental-state element actually retained by law and expressly preserves reliability restrictions. Here the rulemakers could respond to substantial disagreement about the reliability of polygraph conclusions with a uniform rule. An examiner's scientific certification that the accused is truthful differs from the accused's own account of the events. Scheffer testified to lack of knowing ingestion; Rule 707 did not remove that factual defense or deem the disputed element established.

The Government's administration of this examination and the importance of credibility do not establish that the Constitution requires an individualized foundation hearing. Daubert and Joiner govern the general Federal Rules' scientific-evidence inquiry; they do not constitutionalize that particular screening arrangement for every military evidence rule. The categorical form alone therefore does not make this restriction arbitrary or disproportionate.

The Court does not find that every polygraph is unreliable, determine an accuracy percentage, or resolve this test's truthfulness. Nor does permission to testify alone necessarily establish a complete defense in every case. The holding concerns this justified restriction and this proffer, leaving ordinary evidence rules and actual remaining claims intact. A separate Article 36(a) statutory-validity question is not decided.

**Rule's scope and exception:** Rule 707(a) excludes polygraph results, examiner opinions, and references to offering, failing to take or taking an examination. Rule 707(b) preserves statements made during an examination when they are otherwise admissible; it does not make every examination statement admissible.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Chambers v. Mississippi:** Preserved for meaningful presentation of a defense subject to justified evidentiary limits.
- **Rock v. Arkansas:** Distinguished at the sufficient reliability justification and nature of this credibility proffer; no absolute scientific-evidence entitlement is inferred.
- **Montana v. Egelhoff:** Applied at its protection for rebuttal of retained elements and express reservation for justified reliability rules; the substantive element is not redefined here.
- **Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals:** Preserved as construction of the original general Federal Rules, with flexible reliability-and-fit screening; its architecture is not imposed as a constitutional requirement.
- **General Electric Co. v. Joiner:** Preserved for inferential fit and abuse-of-discretion review under those Rules; it does not invalidate Rule 707's separate categorical scheme.

## Law After Decision

Rule 707 survives this constitutional challenge. A meaningful defense remains protected, but the Constitution permits this supported reliability restriction without a mandatory case-specific polygraph hearing. No constitutional jury-monopoly rule, general bar on credibility-related expertise or general authority to exclude inconvenient defense evidence follows from the three-Justice concurrence. Statutory validity under Article 36(a) remains undecided.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, joined by Souter and Thomas, concurring.** In addition to reliability, two features independently justify this category-specific rule: preserving the court members' responsibility to judge credibility against a scientific certification of the accused's truthfulness, and avoiding recurring collateral litigation over examiner qualifications, question design, countermeasures and interpretation. These grounds permit this polygraph rule; they do not reject every expert opinion bearing on credibility or every defense proffer requiring time. All three fully join the Court's reliability ground. Their additional grounds have three votes and are not controlling.

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, concurring.** Reasonable disagreement about polygraph reliability permits the present rule, but constitutional permission does not establish that a categorical ban is the best evidentiary policy. These Justices decline to make protection of the factfinder from ultimate-issue assistance or collateral-litigation concerns independently sufficient grounds here. Their qualification preserves the possible significance of a stronger future proffer without finding this one admissible, requiring a new hearing or altering the present judgment.

**Stevens, dissenting.** A total prohibition prevents an accused from showing that an important defense proffer has a sufficient foundation. The Government administered this examination, and its results bear on the credibility of the denial of knowing ingestion. Egelhoff's meaningful opportunity to rebut the actual element and Rock's treatment of unsupported categorical exclusion favor lawful foundation review. Allowing Scheffer to speak does not by itself answer the exclusion of corroborative support. Stevens would sustain removal of the per se obstacle without deciding ultimate admission, acquittal or entitlement to a new trial before that inquiry. He preserves a concern under Article 36(a), which directs general federal criminal evidence principles so far as the President considers practicable and bars inconsistency with the military code, but does not rest his disposition on that unbriefed statutory question.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the military appellate system for proceedings consistent with the validity of Rule 707 against the presented constitutional challenge. Actual remaining claims retain their own posture. The Court supplies no unsupported final disposition of every charge or sentence component.

## Holdings

### A categorical preclearance exemption is unripe before the challenged intervention is sufficiently defined

**Holding and operative rule:** A request to exempt possible future school-district interventions from §5 is unripe when no sufficiently definite intervention, affected district or allocation of powers establishes a fit legal controversy and no substantial present hardship justifies deciding before those details exist. Texas's challenge therefore must be dismissed without deciding whether any particular intervention changes voting or whether a supported federal-funding conflict affects that question.

## Event

United States v. Hinds; Fourth Circuit No. 97-4030, district case CR-96-148. Plenary merits decision, April 6, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 6. On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Fourth Circuit's unpublished October 15, 1997 judgment affirming the §844(i) conviction. The questions are whether the Pleasant Ridge United Church of Christ building was used in interstate commerce or an activity affecting commerce under that statute and, if the statute reaches it, whether that application lies within Congress's Commerce Clause authority. Hinds also presented an Establishment Clause objection below. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is established.

Hinds and an accomplice burned the church in Greensboro, North Carolina. Hinds waived a jury and stipulated the elements other than the commerce connection. After hearing evidence on that issue, the District Court denied his motion to dismiss and found him guilty. The Fourth Circuit recited the property-use element, held the evidence sufficient, and rejected the religious objection. The church sent annual funds through its national denomination, employed one full-time and two part-time workers, provided the full-time employee insurance procured from out-of-state companies, and purchased utilities, insurance, religious materials and other goods and services.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at merits submission and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the Fourth Circuit's affirmance and remands for application of §844(i)'s property-use requirement to the existing trial record.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela.

The Fourth Circuit's affirmance is vacated. That court must apply the statutory construction to the existing trial record and determine the lawful disposition of the §844(i) conviction. No additional evidence or new trial is authorized. An actual finding of evidentiary insufficiency requires reversal for entry of acquittal; any supported adequate use basis and necessary, properly presented constitutional question must be addressed under current law. The Court does not itself direct acquittal, release, a substitute conviction or resentencing. Stone-Zsela would instead reverse and direct vacatur of the conviction and immediate entry of acquittal on this count.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Vacates the Fourth Circuit's affirmance and remands | Part I's complete property-function construction and application; Part II's existing-record mandate and constitutional nonreach |
| Same opinion, partial joins | Stevens | Stone-Zsela | Agrees that the affirmance cannot stand; opposes the Court's operative remand | Part I only as to independent work for the property-use element, insufficiency of owner relationships by themselves, and absence of a religious exemption; Part II only as to the independent Burks rule that evidentiary insufficiency requires acquittal and bars retrial to repair proof |
| Concurrence in part and dissent from the judgment and remand | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Would reverse and direct vacatur of the §844(i) conviction and entry of acquittal on that count | Active-commercial-use construction; present insufficiency and immediate acquittal; separate Agostini-based reason for rejecting the Establishment objection as a construction aid or alternative ground |

## Holdings

### I. The damaged property's use must connect it to commerce

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 844(i) requires a connection between the function performed by the damaged property and interstate or foreign commerce, or an activity affecting that commerce. The owner's national affiliation, purchases, an employee's out-of-state insurance and recurring payments do not establish the element merely by existing; their relation to the property's use must be established, without making religious or nonprofit ownership an exclusion or profit-making, rental, sales or an exclusively business purpose a universal prerequisite.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies eight direct joins to the complete construction and its application. Stone-Zsela joins only the propositions that the property-use element has independent work, that the owner's identified economic relationships by themselves do not establish it, and that religious identity creates no exemption. He does not join the complete formulation's rejection of an exclusively commercial-use requirement; his active-commercial-use construction is stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute asks what the damaged property was used to do and how that function connects with commerce. Russell sustains coverage of a rented apartment building because renting the premises placed the property itself in the rental market. Its broad account of statutory coverage does not erase the enacted use limitation, and its rental example does not establish an exclusive class of covered property. The words extending coverage to an activity affecting commerce retain both their breadth and their connection to the property's use.

The Fourth Circuit correctly recited the property-use element. Its application nevertheless treated the congregation's economic relationships as sufficient without establishing their relation to the function of the damaged premises. Annual transfers through a national denomination are actual movements of money, and thus the Government's strongest asserted connection. But the existence of those transfers does not itself explain the building's qualifying function. The same distinction applies to the employees, employee insurance, utilities, religious materials and other purchases. The Court neither doubts those reported relationships nor assumes every purchase came from outside the State.

Bailey/Robinson supplies the statutory distinction between required conduct and another related condition: satisfying one does not dispense with the other. Its firearm-use rule is not transplanted into this statute. Section 844(i)'s own use language does the work here. The Court adds no interstate-travel requirement, no profit-making condition and no requirement that this individual fire substantially affect the national economy. ### II. The appellate judgment is vacated for review of the existing trial record

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Circuit's affirmance is vacated, and that court must apply the property-use construction to the complete existing trial record and determine the lawful disposition of the §844(i) conviction; this mandate authorizes neither additional evidence nor a new trial. **Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion has the same eight Associate joins. **Controlling explanation:** The Court corrects the legal application supporting the appellate affirmance. The lower court must examine existing proof under the rule announced here; it may not treat this remand as permission to build a new prosecution case.

Burks requires acquittal after a determination that the prosecution's evidence is legally insufficient. Jacobson applies that consequence and forbids another opportunity to supply missing proof. Bailey/Robinson illustrates the distinction between correcting an erroneous statutory premise and deciding every remaining lawful basis or remedial predicate. **Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting from the judgment and remand.** The enacted use requirement demands active employment of the building itself in commercial activity. #### Holdings

##### Possession of prohibited post-1986 machinegun stock

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(o) may reach possession of a completed machinegun within the prohibited post-May 19, 1986 civilian stock without proof of a defendant-specific interstate or commercial transaction, because the possession prohibition is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of Congress's valid national restriction on additions to that stock. The Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause supplies that class-and-regime connection; no additional transaction-specific interstate element is required.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule, its application and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Hale's possession rule does not disappear when a particular transfer cannot be proved. Local conversion creates completed prohibited stock that can be retained, concealed, acquired and kept available for circulation. Excluding that possession would materially impair the stock restriction itself. Rybar applied the same rule where gun-show sales made the market connection concrete; its facts did not convert a proved sale into a universal constitutional prerequisite. Kirk's possession conviction therefore stands without treating the reported sales as admitted elements or reviving dismissed transfer charges.

Lopez requires an identified interstate mechanism before aggregation can measure its magnitude. Here the mechanism is the specific transfer-and-stock regime, its grandfathered lawful stock, and the fitted possession prohibition. It is not dangerousness, national marketability, remote interstate movement, generalized gun crime or downstream productivity. Nor does administrative convenience alone establish necessity. A particular interstate element may supply a connection in another statute, but courts need not insert one where this valid regime supplies the connection. Formal findings are not universally required and cannot create power by recital; the identified enacted structure supplies the necessary relationship here.

**Operative exceptions and limits:** Section 922(o)(2) preserves transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. It separately preserves lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986; earlier manufacture alone is insufficient. Neither exception is established for Count One. The holding establishes no general federal firearms police power, no rule for every locally manufactured object, and no automatic justification for every product-market possession prohibition. It decides neither Second Amendment protection nor every conversion-parts, readily-restorable or wear-induced application of the machinegun definition.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lopez supplies the connection-before-magnitude architecture and the necessary-inclusion route; its limit on generalized downstream effects and judicial insertion of elements remains intact.
- Hale is applied to completed prohibited stock produced by local conversion; its anti-evasion and material-impairment rule and statutory exceptions are preserved.
- Rybar is applied without treating its gun-show sales as a new element or a constitutional condition for every possession prosecution; its separate arms holding is not before the Court.
- Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class aggregation scope; neither supplies an independent disposition here.
- Parker's cross-border payment obligation is a distinct commerce mechanism and supplies no substitute firearms rule.

#### Law After Decision

Hale's existing possession authority includes this locally converted completed machinegun even without transaction-specific interstate proof. The decision clarifies that application rather than adding a new Commerce category. It leaves ordinary firearm possession, other statutory applications and independent constitutional challenges subject to their own rules.

**Controlling proposition:** Herrera’s delayed and materially inconsistent affidavits, considered with the identification, physical and documentary evidence supporting his convictions, do not establish entitlement to the requested federal habeas relief or justify the district court’s innocence-based stay, even assuming an exceptionally persuasive post-trial showing could constitutionally bar execution. This record-specific rejection establishes no uniform freestanding-innocence burden and does not decide whether such a constitutional claim exists.

**Authority:** O’Connor’s plurality opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, and White’s express agreement with its reasons for finding this submission insufficient in his concurrence in the judgment: five Justices adopt this limited record conclusion. White does not join the plurality’s broader discussion or an unspecified extraordinarily high threshold. No whole opinion commands five votes; no synthesis under *Marks*, the rule for identifying controlling reasoning in a fractured decision, establishes a common general test.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that these papers do not warrant the federal relief requested. *Jackson* distinguishes constitutional sufficiency of the trial record from a fresh determination of guilt; Herrera does not show that the original conviction failed that requirement. His later affidavits must nevertheless be assessed together with the existing evidence for purposes of the assumed claim. The identifications, connection to the automobile, physical evidence and incriminating letter remain substantial. The delayed accounts attributing both murders to his deceased brother conflict over important circumstances and leave the guilty plea to Rucker’s murder unexplained. Their possible probative value does not make this submission a persuasive affirmative demonstration of innocence. The District Court made no credibility finding or innocence-hearing determination to which this Court could defer. *Barefoot* and *Delo* require substantial grounds for relief before a stay on a successive petition; that requirement is unmet here. The Court does not adjudicate the affidavits false in every respect, define a general innocence standard, or authorize execution of a person whose factual innocence has been established.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Jackson v. Virginia*, 443 U.S. 307: Trial-record sufficiency remains distinct. White uses its rational-factfinder formulation only as a minimum for an assumed new-evidence claim, without five-Justice adoption of that general burden.
- *Barefoot v. Estelle*, 463 U.S. 880: Applied to require a substantial legal basis for an execution stay.
- *Delo v. Stokes*, 495 U.S. 320: Applied to the substantial-ground requirement on a second or successive federal petition.
- *Townsend v. Sain*, 372 U.S. 293, and *Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes*, May 4, 1992: Preserved. Necessary factual development of a properly available claim remains distinct from legal sufficiency, whether the claim is legally available, exhaustion and abuse of the writ.
- *Sawyer v. Whitley*, June 22, 1992: Distinguished. Its gateway concerning eligibility for capital punishment does not decide freestanding innocence of the crime or supply that claim’s merits burden.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

The federal habeas disposition does not determine the authority of a State to establish independent postconviction procedures for newly discovered evidence, actual innocence, or scientific developments undermining evidence relied upon at trial. Neither the existence nor the scope of such a state statutory remedy is before the Court. No general federal entitlement to a hearing upon any subsequent scientific disagreement is established.

#### Current-Law Effect

Effective January 25, 1993, this submission does not justify the requested federal relief even upon the assumption of an exceptional constitutional innocence claim. The existence and precise burden of such a claim remain unresolved by a controlling rationale. No logical subset is established between an undefined extraordinary threshold and White’s rational-factfinder minimum, so *Marks* supplies no general standard. White’s distinct minimum burden is noncontrolling.

The Court does not overrule *Townsend*, recognize a general new claim, replace *Jackson* or import *Sawyer*’s eligibility gateway as a freestanding guilt-innocence standard. The plurality’s broader discussion of whether the claim is legally available, state deadlines and clemency has four votes. The case-specific determination that the showing is insufficient, together with the ordinary requirement of substantial grounds for a stay, supports affirmance. *Townsend* and *Keeney* hearings on properly available, materially undeveloped claims, statutory exhaustion, *McCleskey* abuse-of-the-writ rules, *Teague* and the distinct *Sawyer* gateway retain their own operation.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O’Connor, joined by Justice Kennedy, emphasizes in a separate concurrence that execution of a person established to be factually innocent would be constitutionally intolerable, but that the difficult question of a freestanding federal remedy need not be decided on this submission. The plurality’s account of ordinary habeas practice and Texas’s late-new-trial and clemency procedures does not attract White’s join and supplies no new five-Justice holding that such claims are categorically unavailable or that clemency is adequate.

Justice White concurs in the judgment alone. Assuming that a persuasive showing of innocence could bar execution despite an expired new-evidence deadline, he would require at least a showing that no rational trier could find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt upon the old and new evidence together. He expressly agrees that Herrera’s proof falls short for the reasons in the plurality. This does not adopt an established freestanding claim or a definitive burden.

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, also joins the plurality and separately rejects a constitutional entitlement to reopen a lawfully completed conviction on newly discovered innocence evidence alone. *Townsend*, historical procedural practice and finality support his position; deciding the case on an assumed claim does not cause him to accept a new constitutional right. His categorical position has two votes.

Justice Blackmun dissents, joined by Justices Stevens and Souter in the legal discussion and proposed remand. He would recognize an independent Eighth and Fourteenth Amendment claim because execution of a person shown to be innocent is itself a constitutional injury. *Ford* and *Johnson* show that a valid judgment does not resolve every later objection to execution; *Beck* connects capital punishment to reliable guilt determinations. He would require the prisoner to show probable actual innocence on the whole evidence, exceeding a mere likelihood that a juror would entertain reasonable doubt. He rejects leaving that constitutional claim conclusively to clemency and would remand for district-court consideration of threshold factual and procedural requirements, without declaring Herrera innocent. Blackmun alone adds his broader criticism of restrictions on capital review; Stevens and Souter do not join that concluding commentary.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents separately. He would vacate only the categorical exclusion of a properly presented capital-innocence claim, preserving exhaustion and then-governing abuse-of-the-writ rules. His proposed merits burden is clear and convincing affirmative proof, on the relevant old and new evidence together, that the prisoner did not commit the crime for which execution is sought. *Ford* and *Beck* support extending substantive protection; *Penry*’s exception for substantive limits on capital punishment would extend to that protected class. This is a proposed extension, not a claim that those cases already decided freestanding innocence. Clemency is additional protection, not a conclusive answer to an established constitutional prohibition. Allegations provisionally credited must be capable of satisfying that burden before factual development becomes material. *Townsend* and *Keeney* then retain deliberate bypass, while ordinary counsel negligence alone does not bar a necessary hearing. Any temporary stay requires an express *Barefoot*/*Delo* showing; no indefinite stay follows. If innocence is proved, execution for that crime is barred and another sentencing hearing cannot cure the defect. His proposal neither orders acquittal nor disturbs independent lawful custody, revives the *Brady* claim, changes the trial burden or resolves all noncapital claims.

The [official report, 506 U.S. 390](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep506/usrep506390/usrep506390.pdf), and the [full Fifth Circuit judgment, 954 F.2d 1029](https://static.case.law/f2d/954/html/1029-01.html), support the dates, lower proceedings and old and new evidence. The papers allege innocence; no court finding of innocence or affidavit credibility is established. Source support for the evidentiary details is limited to the judicial materials.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court merits review concludes with affirmance. The Fifth Circuit’s vacation of the district-court stay remains operative. The Court orders no renewed federal stay, new hearing, retrial, sentence reduction or release. The reported state stay pending this review is not converted into an indefinite federal order. No subsequent execution, date or state action is established.

### Schlup v. Delo, No. 93-7901

Merits decision, January 23, 1995

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 3, 1994; merits decision January 23, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, reviewing 11 F.3d 738, which sustained denial of a second federal habeas application without the requested evidentiary hearing. Schlup offers crime-innocence evidence as a gateway to otherwise barred ineffective-assistance and suppression claims arising from his conviction for participation in a prison murder. The questions concern the gateway quantum, the augmented evidentiary inquiry, statutory refusal authority and the proper remand.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court vacates the Eighth Circuit judgment and remands with instructions to return the matter to the District Court for proceedings under the probability crime-innocence gateway. It does not find Schlup innocent or find the gateway satisfied.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rejection of the innocence gateway and denial of renewed review | Vacate and remand, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Eighth Circuit to remand to District Court for the correct whole-record probability assessment and a reasoned determination of needed factual development. |

The three dissenters would affirm under the stricter crime-innocence showing; Scalia and Thomas also support a bounded statutory-discretion ground. The mandate orders no automatic hearing, constitutional-merits adjudication, retrial, writ, release or stay.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer in full; Stone-Zsela in the gateway/all-evidence portion only | Vacatur and remand | Six directly support the gateway rule; five directly support the statutory-refusal/remand rationale. Stone supports the same judgment on his stated ground. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway

**Controlling proposition:** A prisoner seeking to overcome otherwise applicable successive or abusive habeas barriers to independent constitutional claims through innocence of the offense must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the probative record as a whole, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. This procedural gateway does not itself establish innocence, prove any constitutional violation or shift the ultimate burden on the underlying claims; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing rule remains confined to its distinct death-eligibility setting.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support a stricter crime-innocence quantum and would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the probability inquiry concerns innocence of the crime, not whether a concededly guilty person qualified for a death sentence. Carrier supplies the exceptional route where constitutional error probably convicted an innocent person; Sawyer preserves its different eligibility inquiry without deciding this crime question. McCleskey retains the innocence exception while enforcing finality through barriers to repetitive and abusive claims. The prisoner's burden remains demanding because the question concerns reasonable jurors considering the augmented evidentiary record, not the habeas judge's personal doubt. Kuhlmann's plurality discussion supports examination of all probative evidence, but its formulations are not converted into a separate majority holding. Jackson's trial-record sufficiency question cannot replace that task: evidence sufficient when credited at trial may have a different effect when reliable new evidence is considered with it. The State's request for one uniformly higher burden disregards the different question and the gateway's limited function. Passing this threshold permits further consideration within the governing claim rules; it does not award a writ or adjudicate innocence.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: applies and specifies its probability-based crime-innocence exception without changing cause and prejudice.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333: preserves the clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway and resolves the crime-innocence question that decision left open; it is not overruled or given a categorical impeachment exclusion.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467: preserves successive/abusive-claim barriers and the exceptional innocence route, without treating potential constitutional merit as sufficient to pass it.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436: uses the relevant plurality discussion of the whole probative record as persuasive guidance, preserving its actual authority limit.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307: distinguishes trial-record legal sufficiency from the likely effect of an augmented record on reasonable jurors; its sufficiency rule remains intact.

**Operative evidentiary qualifications:** The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every affidavit as true or treat the original verdict as a conclusive answer. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner. A mere possibility of acquittal, a juror who might disagree, ordinary constitutional-merits prejudice or the reviewing judge's personal reasonable doubt is insufficient. The reasonable juror fairly considers the evidence and follows the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt instruction.

##### This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission

**Controlling proposition:** The courts' use of the rejected clear-and-convincing crime gateway and reliance on the original jury's credibility function require vacatur and renewed assessment on this record; §2244(b)'s permission to refuse a qualifying subsequent petition does not itself supply an adequate independent ground to sustain that legally mistaken refusal. The Court remands through the Eighth Circuit to the District Court for application of the probability rule and a reasoned determination of necessary factual development, without deciding whether every successful innocence showing compels review, choosing a general appellate standard, or ordering a hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay automatically.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, statutory-refusal and remand rationale, joined by Justices O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this bounded rationale. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth vote for vacatur and the same gateway-assessment remand on his stated ground, without joining the separate §2244(b) analysis. No aggregation of different rationales is needed for the five-Justice holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that discretion to refuse renewed review must be exercised under the proper governing law. McCleskey explains both the statutory permission and its qualifications; neither the label second petition nor the filing of new affidavits decides the result. The earlier federal consideration of guilt-phase ineffective assistance supplies a real legal-merits proceeding, and the lower court's additional discussion of that claim remains relevant. It does not establish an independently sufficient ground disposing of every claim despite today's legal correction. The new accounts concern the meaning of the existing videotape, identification and the interval between attack and response. The lower analysis used a stricter burden and treated credibility too categorically as the original jury's task. Those features prevent accepting it as the required probability assessment. Even assuming the ultimate refusal is discretionary, reconsideration is required here; the Court need not settle the broader discretion question. Keeney and Townsend preserve necessary factual development of properly available claims, but their predicates are not established merely by this remand. The District Court must decide what further development this gateway assessment needs.

**Precedent treatment:**

- McCleskey, 499 U.S. 467: applies the distinction between conditional consideration of new, nonabusive grounds and legally constrained refusal of successive or abusive grounds; neither permission nor exception becomes an unconditional command.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1, and Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293: preserve necessary factual development within their properly presented, available-claim setting and the deliberate-bypass qualification; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a required hearing there, but no successive/abusive gateway is eliminated.
- Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: requires use of the selected crime-innocence rule before the present refusal can be sustained on its innocence assessment.

**Governing statutory terms:** Section 2244(b) provides:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The prior-hearing alternatives are a factual evidentiary merits hearing or a legal merits hearing, each followed by a federal denial of release or other habeas remedy to a person held under a state judgment. Both unless conditions must be preserved: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent. The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner's burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate; cause and prejudice is not pressed in this review. Subsection (a)'s separate wording is not inserted into subsection (b), and no new subsection (c) predicate is established.

**Application, reservations and remedy:** The videotape was trial evidence. Green's asserted prompt call, Faherty's movement account and other later identification accounts must be assessed with the existing record; the Court finds no witness truthful or false and does not find the gateway satisfied. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and a genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. Remand is not mandatory constitutional-merits review. Any necessary live testimony is for a reasoned determination under the applicable rules, not an automatic consequence of affidavits or vacatur. The merits of the suppression and ineffective-assistance claims, a freestanding innocence entitlement and any ultimate guilt determination remain undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Carrier supplies the crime-innocence route, while Sawyer retains its distinct clear-and-convincing eligibility rule. McCleskey's statutory qualifications, abuse pleading, cause/prejudice framework and innocence exception remain. Kuhlmann's relevant discussion retains plurality force, and Jackson's trial-record sufficiency rule remains separate. Keeney and Townsend govern necessary factual development within their actual available-claim conditions. Herrera retains only its record-specific insufficiency holding, Delo v. Blair supplies no precedent from equal division, Brecht remains a preserved-error merits decision, and McFarland supplies no automatic gateway or stay. No prior controlling holding is overruled.

Effective January 23, 1995, reliable new evidence of crime innocence is assessed with the whole probative record under the more-likely-than-not/no-reasonable-juror gateway. The prisoner retains that demanding burden and must separately establish any underlying constitutional claim. Section 2244(b)'s qualified permission, lawful independent barriers and necessary factual-development rules remain; the Court decides neither a universal mandatory-review rule after a successful showing nor a general appellate standard for refusal. The judgment corrects the legal inquiry and creates no freestanding innocence remedy.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the Court's probability gateway and all-evidence rule and concurs in vacatur and remand. Finality is weighty, but the procedural route remains available where reliable new evidence indicates that constitutional error may have convicted an innocent person. Sawyer's death-eligibility threshold answers a different question. The lower courts must evaluate the videotape, timing and eyewitness accounts together with the trial record; passage through the gateway authorizes consideration of otherwise barred constitutional claims within their governing rules, not an innocence judgment or automatic release.

He adds guidance for the reliability inquiry. The habeas court should explain the new evidence's reliability, any independent corroboration, why the evidence was unavailable at trial, whether delay impairs credibility, whether state misconduct or ineffective counsel explains its absence, and exactly how it changes the evidentiary case the jury heard. These are nonexclusive considerations relevant to reliability and probability, not additional elements, a checklist or a new diligence requirement. He does not decide the witnesses' truthfulness or shift the underlying claims' ultimate burden. He does not join the Court's separate statutory-refusal analysis or announce a general rule governing residual refusal discretion.

Justice Kennedy dissents. Justice Thomas joins his opinion in full; Justice Scalia joins its stricter-gateway rule and application, but not its alternative discussion of the probability formulation. Kennedy would require clear and convincing evidence, on the probative record as a whole, that no reasonable juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. He proposes extending Sawyer's quantum to crime innocence; he does not claim that Sawyer already decided that question. McCleskey's treatment of abusive litigation and the exceptional nature of renewed review support the more rigorous showing in his view. The continuing identifications, uncertain timing and delayed or inconsistent accounts leave the submitted evidence insufficient under that quantum. This is not a finding that every new witness lied or that any inculpatory trial evidence invariably defeats an innocence claim.

Kennedy and Thomas additionally question the Court's predictive formulation if a probability rule is used. They would adapt Jackson's reasonable-juror approach to the enlarged evidentiary record, with limited factual inquiry where the submissions indicate that the necessary innocence showing may be made. They do not substitute the unchanged trial-record-only sufficiency test, order a hearing automatically, or establish an independent failure under a correctly applied Carrier rule. Their proposed stricter rule and application would sustain the judgment; their alternative discussion does not control.

Justice Scalia also dissents in an opinion joined by Justice Thomas. Section 2244(b), in their reading, preserves real authority to decline successive or abusive review, subject to its two qualifications and review for abuse. The earlier federal legal-merits adjudication satisfies a hearing alternative despite the lack of the now-requested factual hearing. They rely on the accepted repeat/abuse posture, the actual consideration and refusal below, and the evidentiary submission to find no abuse; they also regard the stricter gateway as correct. They do not convert permission into mandatory dismissal, classify every present claim as previously adjudicated, or find an unexamined ground deliberately withheld.

Salinger and Sanders support consideration of prior litigation within their respective settings, while McCleskey preserves the statute's conditions and judicial limits on discretion. Wong Doo shows that an adequate independent discretionary ground can sometimes preserve a judgment despite a legal error. Scalia and Thomas do not use that proposition to declare these affidavits necessarily insufficient under a correctly applied probability rule. Their statutory rationale is additional to the stricter-standard objection, but its application here shares their conclusion that the lower court used a legally proper quantum. They recognize that misconception of law or arbitrary reasons can constitute abuse and announce no unconditional affirmance should the proper gateway later be satisfied. These views do not control the remand.

### Kyles v. Whitley, No. 93-7927

Merits decision, April 19, 1995

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 7, 1994; decided April 19, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, reviewing 5 F.3d 806 (October 14, 1993), which affirmed denial of federal habeas relief. The question is whether the established suppressed favorable evidence, viewed collectively, is material to Kyles’s conviction under Brady and Bagley.

Dolores Dye was killed and her automobile taken on September 20, 1984. Kyles’s first trial ended in a deadlock; the second produced a conviction and death sentence. No reason for the deadlock beyond disagreement is established. The prosecution used eyewitness identifications, the murder weapon found behind a stove at the apartment where Kyles stayed, ammunition and a holster, the victim’s purse and papers found in garbage, and pet food of brands she had bought. A print linked Kyles to a receipt in the car, but processing destroyed its readable date. The defense presented a theory that Joseph “Beanie” Wallace was implicated and framed Kyles. Adverse credibility findings and evidence supporting guilt remain part of the record.

The established suppression concerns six contemporaneous eyewitness statements, Beanie’s recorded September 22 and written September 23 accounts, prosecutor Strider’s interview notes, a garbage-retrieval memorandum, and the parking-lot plate list. Smallwood’s earlier sequence differed from his trial observation account; Williams’s early description differed from Kyles’s height and build. Beanie’s accounts bear on corroboration, the police investigation, his possible access, and the provenance of physical evidence. Some lawful uses arise through police examination and preparation without calling Beanie or admitting every statement for its truth. The seventeen-plate list omitted Kyles’s plate, but its completeness is disputed. The Court finds neither Beanie’s guilt nor planting, witness perjury, or a complete list.

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

The Court reverses the denial of relief on the conviction-level Brady claim, 6–3, and remands for a conditional writ. Louisiana must retry Kyles within a reasonable time fixed by the district court or release him under the writ.

The Court does not order acquittal, unconditional immediate release, a fixed number of days, or penalty-only relief. It does not independently adjudicate other claims merely appearing in the briefs.

#### Opinion Topology

|---|---|---|---|---|

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction

**Controlling proposition:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses, considered in the whole record and in the defense’s preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. No item must independently satisfy that standard, and the defendant need not show acquittal more likely than conviction or that the remaining evidence is legally insufficient; the established suppression here meets the standard.

**Authority:** Justice Souter’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes for the rule, application and conviction-level relief. The dissent accepts collective assessment but disputes materiality on this record.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the undisclosed evidence changes the relationship among the prosecution’s apparently reinforcing proofs. Brady requires disclosure of material favorable evidence, and Bagley supplies a probability sufficient to undermine confidence rather than a demand for probable acquittal. Earlier identification accounts permit substantial impeachment; Beanie’s differing accounts support corroboration and examination of the investigation; and the garbage material bears on the provenance of tangible evidence. These uses reinforce one another. They do not all depend on calling Beanie or accepting every statement as true. The remaining identifications, ammunition, holster, pet food and credibility objections remain significant. But counting that evidence as sufficient does not answer how the defense could have challenged its apparent independence after disclosure. The plate list contributes only within its disputed completeness. The Court finds no planting, perjury or factual innocence. It resolves the established cumulative suppression and leaves later evidence and independently unadmitted constitutional claims outside this decision.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83: applies the duty to disclose material favorable evidence to the established suppression.
- United States v. Bagley, 473 U.S. 667: applies reasonable-probability materiality to the combined defense counterfactual, including lawful impeachment and preparation uses.
- Schlup v. Delo, January 23, 1995: distinguishes its gateway showing; it supplies no additional innocence prerequisite for this Brady claim.

##### The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team

**Controlling proposition:** A prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence known to police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires disclosure; personal ignorance does not remove that evidence from the materiality inquiry. This case-specific responsibility does not impute all knowledge of every government office or establish unrestricted investigative or open-file duties.

**Authority:** Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. The dissent’s consideration of the police-held material does not supply additional votes for an affirmative organizational rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the prosecution cannot avoid its disclosure responsibility by separating the trial lawyer from police who worked on the case. Brady protects the fairness of the trial, and Bagley assesses the material effect of nondisclosure; that protection would fail if the same favorable information counted only when personally recalled by the prosecutor. Here the eyewitness statements, recorded accounts, plate list and investigative memorandum arose within the investigation used to prosecute Kyles. Their lawful defense uses therefore enter the same collective inquiry. The rule does not require every agency to search all government files, and it does not convert every nondisclosure into a completed constitutional violation. Materiality and the evidence’s favorable character still matter. United States v. Williams addresses a distinct federal grand-jury duty and its specified remedy conditions; its personal-knowledge limitation does not narrow trial Brady responsibility. The Court creates no open-file entitlement and excuses no lack of diligence concerning evidence already known to the defense.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brady and Bagley: apply their trial-disclosure protection to information held by police acting for this prosecution.
- United States v. Williams, May 4, 1992: distinguishes its confined grand-jury disclosure and dismissal rules; those limits remain effective within their own setting.

##### A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing

**Controlling proposition:** Once this suppression is found material under Brady and Bagley, a second prejudice or harmlessness test may not defeat relief merely by reassessing whether the same nondisclosure affected the verdict. This holding rests on the prejudice built into the completed Brady violation and establishes no universal harmlessness rule for other habeas claims.

**Authority:** Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes for this claim-specific ground. The dissent finds no material violation and adopts no independent harmlessness ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a second duplicative prejudice inquiry cannot rescue a conviction after material suppression has already undermined confidence in its verdict. Bagley makes the requisite effect part of the constitutional violation itself. The Court has found that effect through the combined disclosure counterfactual, not merely identified an error whose effect remains unexamined. Brecht retains Chapman for its specified preserved protected-silence claim, and O’Neal extends that treatment to its specified personal-intent category; neither decision supplies a general habeas rule displacing Brady’s own materiality requirement. Nor does this decision replace Bagley with Chapman. The State’s surviving evidence has already been considered, and it does not restore confidence after the lawful combined uses are taken into account. Ordinary habeas gates and independently presented claims retain their own rules. For this established violation, the appropriate relief is a conditional new-trial opportunity, not an additional weaker review of the same prejudice or a declaration of innocence.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bagley: treats materiality as a defining element of the suppression violation, not an initial screen before a second duplicative prejudice test.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993, and O’Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995: preserve their actual category-specific holdings and distinguish them from claim-defining Brady materiality.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Brady and Bagley govern collective materiality and the trial prosecution’s responsibility. Williams remains confined to its distinct grand-jury setting. Schlup remains a gateway rule. Brecht and O’Neal retain their category-specific holdings; this decision neither adopts a general habeas Kotteakos rule nor extends Chapman universally. No precedent is overruled.

Courts must assess the established favorable suppression collectively, including legitimate effects on preparation, corroboration, impeachment and examination of the investigation. Prosecutors bear case-specific responsibility for investigating-police information. A completed material Brady violation carries no second duplicative harmlessness hurdle. The holdings neither require open-file discovery nor treat every nondisclosure as structural error, and they do not excuse defense lack of diligence about known evidence.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas, dissents. The three accept cumulative reasonable-probability review and consider the police-held material. They find that the supported additional uses do not sufficiently weaken the surviving identifications, physical evidence and adverse credibility findings to undermine confidence in this verdict. They treat the jury’s knowledge of a framing theory as relevant but not conclusive, and Beanie’s absence as a limitation on some uses rather than a bar to every investigative use. Their disagreement concerns the combined application: they reject the majority’s assessment that the new corroboration and provenance challenges materially change the evidentiary picture. They do not demand proof of innocence, impose a materiality threshold on each item, or hold that mere legal sufficiency defeats Brady. Having found no material violation, they would affirm denial of this claim without adopting a second harmlessness ground. Their application is noncontrolling.

Justice Stevens joins the Court’s application of Bagley without withdrawing his earlier request-sensitive criticism of that standard. The Court does not reject that criticism or adopt a new restrictive pretrial-disclosure theory; no additional writing is filed here.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The withdrawn review route and retained original jurisdiction

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars ordinary certiorari review of a court of appeals' grant or denial of authorization to file a second or successive district-court habeas application; the present certiorari request must be dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. The provision does not repeal the distinct original habeas authority retained by §2241, which permits independent consideration of this petition without affirming or reviewing the circuit's authorization judgment.

**Authority:** Part I-A, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The review bar expressly addresses the circuit's authorization determination. Section 2241 separately names this Court and its Justices as habeas decisionmakers, and AEDPA leaves that grant intact. Section 2244(b)(3)(A)'s advance-authorization mechanism expressly precedes filing in a district court; it does not direct applicants to obtain circuit permission before filing an original petition here. The amended circuit-transfer rule likewise does not name this Court.

McCardle requires respect for withdrawal of a specified appellate route. Yerger preserves a separate habeas source against an unwarranted implied repeal, and Bollman explains its statutory source and constitutional appellate character. An application filed directly here is called original as a matter of procedure; it is not an expansion of Article III's enumerated original jurisdiction. Section 1651 authorizes writs in aid of jurisdiction independently possessed, not a new freestanding source. The Court does not decide every possible certified-question, interlocutory or other supervisory route not presented here.

##### Temporal operation of the channel rule

**Controlling proposition:** The authorization and review proceedings begun after AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment are governed by its specified authorization-review bar. That conclusion does not decide the temporal reach of every Title I provision or apply a new substantive restriction backward merely because a petition is pending.

**Authority:** All nine adopt this channel result. Part I-B's explanation is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Thomas, Scalia and Kennedy concur through the distinct relevant-activity method.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf requires attention first to Congress's temporal direction and otherwise to whether applying the particular provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events. A procedural or jurisdictional label alone supplies no answer. This provision governs the new May authorization determination and the ensuing request for its review. Its application to that post-enactment proceeding does not depend on retroactively changing the lawfulness of trial conduct. The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 does not automatically govern every different amendment; Georgia's qualification under that chapter is not established or assumed here.

##### Successive restrictions and original-writ discretion

**Controlling proposition:** The district-court authorization mechanism does not govern filing this original application. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform the Court's consideration of repeated applications, but whether those substantive provisions bind this Court directly is reserved. Retained jurisdiction does not itself entitle an applicant to an original writ.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by all nine Justices.

**Operative distinctions:** For the second or successive §2254 applications described by the statute, paragraph (1) requires dismissal of a claim already presented in a prior application. Paragraph (2) requires dismissal of a new claim unless either it relies on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive to collateral cases by the Supreme Court, or both of these conditions are met: the factual predicate could not previously have been discovered through due diligence; and the facts, if proved and viewed with the evidence as a whole, establish clearly and convincingly that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found the applicant guilty of the underlying offense.

Paragraph (3) requires permission from the appropriate court of appeals before district-court filing; a three-judge panel decides whether there is a prima facie showing and must act within thirty days. Its grant or denial is not appealable and is not subject to a rehearing or certiorari petition. Under paragraph (4), authorization does not finally establish entitlement: the district court must dismiss an authorized claim unless the applicant establishes the statutory requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** McCleskey recognizes lawful restrictions on abusive successive requests; it does not collapse these distinct statutory conditions. A request that this Court announce a new rule is not reliance on an already announced, previously unavailable rule made retroactive by this Court. New medical disagreement, an assertion of innocence or satisfaction of some other gateway does not automatically establish paragraph (2)(B)'s cumulative diligence, constitutional-error and clear-and-convincing conditions. The present submission does not establish those conditions; the Court creates no new default, waiver or affirmative diligence-failure finding to reach that assessment.

Rule 20 separately requires the petition to explain why relief was not sought in district court, state how and where state remedies were exhausted or §2254(b) otherwise satisfied, demonstrate exceptional circumstances warranting this Court's discretionary exercise, and show that adequate relief cannot be obtained in another form or court. Section 2254(b)(1)'s alternatives are exhaustion of available state-court remedies, absence of available state corrective process, or circumstances rendering that process ineffective to protect the applicant's rights. Paragraph (2) separately permits, but does not require, merits denial despite nonexhaustion. Under paragraph (3), a State is not deemed to waive exhaustion or estopped from invoking it unless the State through counsel expressly waives it. No exhaustion, process-availability, effectiveness or waiver question is decided here. The denial below explains the direct request, but does not prove its substantive sufficiency. The Court denies this petition for want of the exceptional original showing; it does not find an otherwise disputed state remedy conclusively available or adequate. Section 2241 permits a lawful transfer, but none is ordered.

##### The constitutional objection in this case

**Controlling proposition:** The specified review restriction and consideration of the operative successive scheme do not suspend constitutionally sufficient habeas review or prescribe a forbidden judicial outcome as applied to these claims. The original petition receives independent examination and fails on an adequate traditional original-writ ground, without deciding that every future statutory exclusion or every nominal surviving remedy is constitutional.

**Authority:** Part II's nine-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Suspension Clause protects the writ's constitutional function; it does not exempt every repeated application from source-bound procedural and substantive regulation. The retained §2241 route is used here, not merely named as an inaccessible alternative. The Court independently considers the actual claims and finds no entitlement under the traditional extraordinary standard. Thus no otherwise sufficient claim is denied solely because all meaningful review has been eliminated.

Yerger supports preserving the separate route, while Klein's distinction between changing governing law and prescribing an outcome prevents treating every jurisdictional amendment as an unlawful command. The provisions applied here establish channels and successive-claim standards rather than direct the Court to falsify a fact or decide under an unchanged rule in a prescribed way. The Court does not approve elimination of every meaningful forum, resolve a case of established innocence excluded from all relief, or decide the constitutionality and temporal operation of §§2254(d)–(e), all of Title I, or every possible supervisory-review restriction.

##### The reasonable-doubt claim

**Authority:** Part I has all nine Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress and the Habeas Rules allocate authority over initial examination, exhaustion, default, repeated applications, prejudicial delay and claims plainly lacking entitlement. Section 2243's direction to dispose of a matter as law and justice require operates within that arrangement; it does not authorize an additional terminal bar whenever a court regards a prisoner's timing or purpose as offensive.

Rule 9(a) specifically addresses delay prejudicing the State's ability to respond. Its reasonable-diligence qualification and Congress's deletion of a proposed five-year presumption would lose their work if generalized delay alone could replace them. The different predicates for repeated or abusive applications likewise cannot be supplied by counting unsuccessful next-friend captions.

The appellate opinion identifies serious conduct, not merely proximity to execution. Those findings retain significance under applicable law. But neither their seriousness nor an appeal to equity establishes a Rule 9 condition, claim insufficiency or another lawful ground to extinguish this petition. The district court's refusal to employ that additional dismissal sanction was not the error the court of appeals supposed.

This conclusion preserves structured judicial judgment and legally authorized control of schedules, stays and sanctions. It excuses no deliberate misconduct, decides no unpresented fraud or inherent-power issue, and eliminates no established exhaustion, default or other claim-specific defense. It does not find that any undisclosed claim warrants relief.

**Statutory qualifications:**

- Pre-AEDPA §2254(a) requires custody violating federal law. Sections (b)–(c) retain exhaustion, including the absence or ineffectiveness of available state corrective process; an available state procedure cannot simply be disregarded. The then-effective factual-presumption provisions remain distinct from any later statutory deference regime.
- Section 2243's initial writ-or-show-cause command has an exception when the application shows nonentitlement. Its law-and-justice direction does not guarantee a writ or evidentiary hearing. Rule 4 requires prompt examination, summary dismissal and notice if lack of entitlement plainly appears from the petition and exhibits, and otherwise an answer or other appropriate judicial action, with service on the respondent and state attorney general. Lawful expedition remains available.
- Rule 9(a) permits dismissal for filing delay that prejudiced the State's ability to respond, unless the petitioner shows that reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before the prejudice arose. Losing an execution date or bearing generalized finality costs does not alone establish that response prejudice. The enacted Rule contains **no five-year presumption**; the deleted proposal in older advisory discussion is not law.
- Rule 9(b) separately concerns second or successive petitions: no new or different grounds after a prior merits determination, or new or different grounds whose earlier omission constituted abuse. Prior proceedings must be examined for their actual disposition, not merely counted.
- Section 2244(b)'s then-effective subsequent-application permission follows a federal denial of habeas relief after an evidentiary hearing on a material factual merits issue or a hearing on a legal merits issue. Its exception requires both an unadjudicated factual or other ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of withholding does not eliminate every other abuse. When both conditions are met and other barriers are absent, McCleskey requires consideration. The State must plead abuse clearly and particularly before the prisoner's burden arises. No such subsequent-petition predicate is established here.

##### II. Independent equitable stay discretion survives the dismissal ruling

**Controlling proposition:** First-petition status does not exclude lawful independent consideration of the State's enforcement interest, unjustified delay or proved obstruction in deciding an execution stay. But the existence of that distinct discretion does not sustain this appellate order, whose stated premise was the supposed authority to dismiss through the sanction rejected in Part I.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** Gomez permits equitable assessment independently of a habeas procedural bar, even on an assumed open procedural route. Its four earlier petitions are facts of that case, not a minimum number needed before equity may operate. The State therefore presents a genuine stay argument, and its reported conduct findings cannot be dismissed as irrelevant merely because this is Lonchar's first personal petition.

McFarland likewise preserves denial for proved inexcusable disregard of a reasonable statutory opportunity and obstruction, while protecting that opportunity. The seriousness of a supported constitutional claim and execution's irreversibility matter without alone compelling last-minute relief. Neither case licenses an unsourced permanent dismissal or establishes what examination this actual petition has received.

The appellate order treats supposed dismissal authority as the reason the district court erred in granting a stay. Once that legal premise is removed, the reported conduct does not itself supply a separate determination that all required process occurred or that a lawful claim-specific disposition is available. Corrective remand allows the courts below to evaluate those questions and genuine independent stay grounds on the proper basis. It does not forbid sustaining a stay refusal on an adequately established independent ground.

**Section 2251 limits:** A federal justice or judge before whom a habeas proceeding is pending **may** stay covered State proceedings before final judgment, after final judgment **of discharge**, or pending appeal. The covered matter must involve the detained person and a matter involved in the habeas proceeding. Proceedings within a granted stay are void; without a stay, they remain as valid as if no habeas proceeding or appeal were pending. Filing alone therefore supplies no suspension, and the statute does not invalidate unrelated State action. Its discretionary text does not by itself answer whether protection is necessary for an otherwise required judicial opportunity.

##### III. Protection must preserve genuinely required process when lawful expedition cannot

**Controlling proposition:** If execution would prevent completion of process actually required by the governing habeas statute and Rules, and lawful prompt disposition cannot finish in time, a court must provide the interim protection necessary to make that identified opportunity meaningful. Mere filing, first-petition status or a capital sentence establishes neither condition.

**Authority:** Part III has five direct supporters: Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy joins only the necessary-opportunity formulation, without resolving the opportunity's extent on this record. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas reserve the general proposition; Stone-Zsela supplies no additional join.

**Controlling explanation:** Barefoot protects meaningful review in its specified initial-appeal setting. Its reasoning supports, by analogy, protection for whatever examination the first-petition statute and Rules actually require. This is an extension of that meaningful-review principle to a different stage, not direct application of a certificate-of-probable-cause rule whose trigger has not been established here. McFarland reinforces protection of a real statutory opportunity while retaining individualized limits for established disregard and obstruction.

The court must identify what process remains due. Rule 4 screening, lawful procedural dismissal, appropriate expedition and the absence of an automatic evidentiary hearing remain effective. If those means permit a lawful disposition in time, this conditional rule supplies no further stay entitlement. Nor does it erase the actual opportunity and obstruction inquiry in McFarland or the independent equitable considerations preserved in Gomez.

Execution cannot replace process that governing law still requires merely because the court lacks time to perform it. Necessary protection makes that legal obligation effective; it does not guarantee every requested merits step. The present decision finds neither that a particular undisclosed claim requires further review nor that a particular period is necessary. Those are matters for the directed examination and reasoned stay redetermination.

**Barefoot's distinct settings remain intact:**

- A first district petition receives no automatic suspension upon filing. The rule above identifies an analogy and requires its own actual-process and timing predicates.
- On an **initial habeas appeal with a certificate of probable cause**, meaningful opportunity to address the merits and an appellate merits decision are required. If execution would moot review and time prevents a considered disposition, a stay must issue. Adequate notice and opportunity to address the merits permit expedited or simultaneous merits/stay decision. Certification requires more than nonfrivolity: a substantial federal showing, including reasonably debatable issues, possible different resolution or questions deserving further consideration; neither ultimate success nor sentence severity alone is the test. No final denial and certified appeal are established here.
- Second or successive petitions retain Rule 9(b)'s actual conditions, lawful expedition and the requirement that a stay reflect substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted. Failed next-friend proceedings do not alone put this petition in that setting.
- Pending certiorari after appellate habeas denial, a stay is not automatic. There must be a reasonable probability that four Members would find the issue sufficiently meritorious for certiorari or notation of probable jurisdiction, a significant possibility of reversal and likely irreparable harm. The application must supply materials for careful assessment; relief should first be sought in the court of appeals, whose action generally receives considerable weight in that setting. These are not the merits criteria for this already-argued challenge.

##### IV. This legal error requires corrective remand and prompt stay redetermination

**Controlling proposition:** The appellate stay-vacatur judgment cannot stand on its erroneous dismissal premise. Remand proceeds through the Eleventh Circuit for prompt district examination of the actual petition and proper State objections and redetermination of the need and terms of §2251 protection. Removing the appellate order restores the displaced district stay pending that decision, subject to lawful modification or dissolution.

**Authority:** All nine support this operative correction. The eight Associates formally vacate and remand; Stone-Zsela would reverse and remand. All support the limited restoration independently of any additional Part III join.

**Controlling explanation:** The order under review displaced an existing district stay on a mistaken legal understanding. Setting it aside restores the prior protection so the directed examination can occur, while requiring the courts below promptly to apply the correct law. That consequence differs from issuing a new Supreme Court stay or declaring every first petitioner entitled to continued protection.

The reported conduct findings remain available for lawful assessment. They do not establish Rule 9 prejudice, procedural default, nonexhaustion, abuse, claim insubstantiality or completion of all required examination. The Court supplies none of those missing determinations. Nor does it direct a hearing or full constitutional-merits adjudication despite lawful barriers. Once the required examination permits lawful disposition or establishes that protection is unnecessary, this mandate supplies no entitlement to further delay. The existing order may be modified or dissolved consistently with the governing law; indefinite continuation is not approved.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Gomez:** Retained as an independent equitable stay rule, not reduced to successive-abuse doctrine. Its assumed open procedural route and record-specific balancing remain material; four earlier petitions are not a minimum trigger. It creates neither the asserted additional first-petition dismissal ground nor an automatic late-claim bar.
- **McFarland:** Applied for a meaningful statutory opportunity and individualized stay discretion, including denial for proved inexcusable disregard and obstruction. Its qualified counsel/preparation proceeding and preapplication pendency holding do not establish Lonchar's preparation facts or an unconditional stay. An actual application is present here.
- **Barefoot:** Part III extends its meaningful-review reasoning by analogy to genuinely required district process. Its initial-appeal/CPC, successive-petition and certiorari formulations remain confined to their respective triggers and qualifications stated above. No certified appeal is established, and lawful expedition is preserved.
- **Schlup:** Its legal-error remand supports correcting the asserted mistaken premise without predetermining further proceedings or relief. Its crime-innocence gateway still requires reliable evidence and the probative record as a whole to show that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. Reliability, likely credibility, delay and inconsistency matter; mere possibility, ordinary merits prejudice or a reviewing judge's doubt does not suffice. No gateway is found satisfied here, and innocence is not an entry requirement for every first petition.
- **Sawyer:** Its separate death-eligibility gateway requires clear and convincing proof that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find eligibility under applicable state law. Additional discretionary mitigation does not satisfy it. It supplies no finding or universal first-petition condition here.
- **McCleskey:** Its abuse rules and clear, particular State pleading requirement remain within their actual habeas setting. The then-effective §2244(b) conditions are preserved rather than replaced by a generalized delay sanction.
- **Delo v. Stokes:** Its abuse-of-discretion review and successive-petition application retain their established scope. They do not by themselves establish an abuse ground or the merits-screening standard for this first personal petition.

No precedent is overruled. The judgment preserves legally authorized equity while rejecting the additional terminal sanction asserted below.

#### Law After Decision

From April 1, 1996, a first personal federal petition cannot be extinguished solely by the described delay and litigation purpose through an additional equitable dismissal rule dispensing with statutory and Habeas Rules conditions. Independent stay equity survives. Five Justices also establish the conditional protection needed for genuinely required process when execution would defeat it and lawful prompt disposition cannot finish in time.

These rules do not guarantee a stay, hearing, full merits proceedings or relief. Exhaustion, default, Rule 4, Rule 9, the then-effective subsequent-application rules, lawful expedition and legally authorized sanctions remain. No five-year delay presumption, later habeas regime, new innocence gateway or numerical timetable is created. The required corrective remand establishes no claim-specific factual predicate.

The appellate judgment vacating the district stay is removed.
