# Current-term neutral projection verbatim selections

### 1998-03-03 — Spencer v. Kemna

[Public Projection](../records/spencer_v_kemna_mootness_1998-03-03.md#public-projection).

#### Event

Spencer v. Kemna, No. 96-7171. Decision after plenary review, March 3, 1998; argued November 12, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Eighth Circuit, which affirmed dismissal of the §2254 petition. Spencer challenges the September 1992 parole revocation, not the original burglary and stealing convictions. He filed on April 1, 1993 while imprisoned under the revocation; the challenged sentence expired October 16, 1993. Review concerns statutory custody, continuing Article III injury, mootness exceptions and the asserted effect of possible civil-remedy limits.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate. No Justice is recused.

#### Public Action

The Eighth Circuit’s judgment affirming dismissal is affirmed on mootness alone.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Affirm dismissal as moot, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens.

The dismissal rests on absence of a continuing Article III controversy, not failure of custody when the petition was filed or a decision on the revocation’s merits. Stevens would reverse and remand the live petition for its remaining lawful adjudication. Stone-Zsela agrees with the ancillary custody-at-filing, capable-of-repetition exception, litigation-delay and civil-remedy dispositions without adding a ground.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg | Affirms mootness dismissal | O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join all parts; Stone-Zsela joins Part II’s actual-continuing-injury ground and mootness result, but agrees only with Part II’s capable-of-repetition and litigation-delay dispositions and Parts I and III’s ancillary dispositions, without those additional rationales |
| Dissent | Stevens | — | Would reverse and remand | Recognizes the particular official adverse adjudication’s continuing reputational consequence; does not decide the revocation merits or an affirmative section 1983 exception |

#### Holdings

##### I. Custody at filing and a continuing controversy are separate requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2254’s custody requirement is satisfied when the petitioner is held under the challenged revocation at filing; later expiration does not retroactively defeat that threshold. Article III nonetheless requires a concrete continuing injury after the challenged sentence expires.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, seven direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with the custody disposition without an additional ground; Stevens also accepts custody at filing.

**Controlling explanation:** Spencer was imprisoned under this revocation on April 1, 1993. The District Court’s alternative suggestion that expiration removed statutory custody is incorrect. The later seven-year imprisonment arises from an unrelated conviction, rather than the unexpired consecutive aggregate addressed in Garlotte. A timely filing therefore satisfies the statutory entry condition without itself supplying an enduring constitutional controversy. Lujan and Arizonans require an actual personal interest throughout adjudication, not simply an interest that existed when suit began.

##### II. The asserted collateral consequences and exceptions do not keep this petition live

**Holding and operative rule:** After this parole-revocation sentence expires, collateral injury is not presumed merely from the revocation; the petitioner must show an actual continuing consequence redressable in this action. Speculative future parole, sentencing or evidentiary uses, the possibility of recurrence and litigation delay do not supply that showing on this record.

**Authority:** Part II’s continuing-injury rule and mootness result have eight direct joins: Stone-Zsela and the seven Associates joining the Court opinion. The seven Associates also directly adopt the specific exception and delay applications.

**Controlling explanation:** An attack on a conviction is distinct from this challenge to a revocation of an otherwise valid sentence. Lane supports examining the actual surviving interest, although Spencer, unlike Lane’s respondents, challenges the adverse revocation finding itself. Future parole use depends on discretionary treatment, sentencing use on later legal developments, impeachment on future testimony and permissible use, and other-crimes evidence on additional contingencies. No present mandatory disability or demonstrated imminent adverse use is established. MacArthur’s actual statutory information interest and existing damages action do not make every retained adverse record a live habeas controversy; the Court adopts no categorical exclusion of reputational injury. The capable-of-repetition exception requires both a duration too short for full review and a reasonable expectation that this claimant will again face the action. Neither the asserted delay nor possible injury to others establishes those conditions. State extensions and delayed judicial action cannot create Article III power through fault or estoppel. Expiration occurred by the sentence’s terms, not a newly established strategic cessation. The Court decides no revocation claim, criminal guilt or exoneration.

##### III. An unresolved future civil remedy does not create this habeas controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** Heck and Edwards do not establish an unavoidable favorable-invalidation bar for every former prisoner: their controlling rules expressly reserve that category and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity. The unresolved scope of a possible later civil action does not create a damages claim in this habeas case or dispense with continuing Article III injury.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, seven direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition of this ancillary contention without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Heck’s decided prerequisite concerns the specified in-custody, conviction-negating damages claim; Edwards preserves that limit while requiring genuinely independent injuries to be classified by their consequences. Neither decision supplies the universal former-prisoner bar assumed by Spencer’s argument. A claim whose success does not necessarily invalidate the revocation would also require its own analysis. The Court need not choose an affirmative former-prisoner exception to reject the asserted inevitable bar. No damages claim is pleaded here, and possible difficulty obtaining another remedy does not empower the Court to decide an otherwise moot habeas petition. The existing reservations remain open rather than becoming either a categorical bar or a newly adjudicated exception.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lane is applied for the continuing-interest inquiry while its unchallenged-revocation posture is distinguished; no automatic consequence is inferred for this revocation.
- Lujan and Arizonans preserve an actual personal controversy throughout review. Arizonans’ vacatur remedy does not require clearing a nonexistent intervening merits judgment here.
- Garlotte’s continuous consecutive-sentence aggregate is distinguished from Spencer’s unrelated later custody; custody at filing remains sufficient for the statutory threshold.
- MacArthur preserves supported actual personal information interests without creating a universal reputation-based habeas rule.
- Heck and Edwards retain their bounded invalidation prerequisite and express former-prisoner/realistic-opportunity reservations; no affirmative section 1983 exception or universal bar is added.

#### Law After Decision

A timely custody-based habeas filing does not survive expiration of the challenged sentence without continuing Article III injury or a valid exception. This record establishes neither. The revocation’s merits remain unadjudicated, and the separate civil-remedy questions expressly reserved by Heck and Edwards remain open.

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens distinguishes Lane because Spencer challenges the official adverse revocation finding itself, not merely completed custody while leaving that finding untouched. He considers the particular government adjudication and the State’s continuing reliance on the incorporated serious-misconduct report a present personal reputational consequence that invalidation could meaningfully remove. He does not presume every revocation carries statutory disabilities or depend on a future crime, audience reaction or invented damages claim. The written order found condition violations and incorporated a report; it was not an express rape conviction, and the State’s characterization remains distinct. MacArthur’s requirement of a supported personal interest does not, in his view, erase this already imposed official determination. He would remand for unresolved lawful adjudication, without granting a writ, finding procedural wrongdoing or deciding the civil-remedy question.

#### Procedure After Action

The habeas action remains dismissed as moot, without constitutional-merits preclusion. No writ, new revocation hearing, expungement, damages or exoneration is ordered. This Supreme Court review is complete, and no continuing return condition is created.

#### Source Notes

The revocation order, incorporated report, custody dates, claimed procedural defects and later separate sentence are reported in [523 U.S. 1](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-1.pdf). The woman’s allegations, Spencer’s admitted cocaine use and denial of force, the written condition-violation order and the State’s description remain distinct. The underlying reports and complete record are available here through published recitations; they establish no criminal conviction for the alleged conduct. No reputation-only submission or damages demand by Spencer is attributed to him. [Lane, 455 U.S. 624](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-455/pdf/USREPORTS-455-624.pdf), supplies the earlier posture and continuing-interest analysis.



### 1998-05-18 — Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes, No. 96-779.

[Authoritative Public Projection](../records/arkansas_educational_television_commission_v_forbes_merits_1998-05-18.md#public-projection).

#### Event

Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes, No. 96-779. Argued October 8, 1997; decided May 18, 1998. On review of the Eighth Circuit's judgment, 93 F.3d 497, reversing judgment for the Commission on Forbes's First Amendment challenge to exclusion from a televised candidate debate.

The state broadcaster planned a selective, moderated debate program. Its spring 1992 selection category covered major-party candidates and other candidates with strong popular support. Forbes later qualified for the ballot, requested inclusion and was denied. Staff cited lack of campaign organization, appreciable voter support and press recognition as a serious candidate. The jury found that political pressure and disagreement with his views did not cause the exclusion. The question is whether this actual program created a forum requiring his admission and whether the exclusion was constitutionally permissible.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

#### Public Action

The Court reverses and directs entry of judgment for the Commission on the reviewed forum claim.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

The case returns for implementation of judgment for the Commission on this claim. No prospective publication requirement, fixed admission formula or compelled future broadcast is imposed.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Breyer | Supports reversal and the specified judgment | Entire opinion. |
| Dissent | Stevens | Souter, Ginsburg | Would sustain the appellate judgment against the exclusion | Insufficiently definite government candidate-selection standards, without universal ballot access. |

#### Holdings

##### 1. This selectively composed candidate debate is a nonpublic forum

**Holding and operative rule:** A public broadcaster's candidate debate is a nonpublic forum when its actual program retains editorial selection and has not been opened generally to every qualified candidate. Exclusions must be reasonable in light of the program's journalistic purpose and viewpoint neutral; neither public ownership nor the label journalism supplies immunity from those requirements.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. That six-Justice coalition controls both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The candidates express their own political views in this debate, so the broadcaster cannot treat their speech simply as the State's message. Yet a selective invitation program is not a general opening of airtime. Cornelius and Perry Education distinguish selective access from a designated public forum and preserve reasonable, viewpoint-neutral management.

The Commission planned a limited, moderated exchange and selected individual participants. Ballot eligibility did not itself open the program to every candidate. Rosenberger protects viewpoints within the opportunity actually created; it does not enlarge every opportunity into general access. Bronx likewise protects comparable private expression in a broad community forum while preserving genuine program boundaries. Its rental setting does not remove the broadcaster's supported selective function here. Denver Area supplies no categorical broadcasting classification or government editorial immunity.

The holding concerns this program, not every public broadcast or every candidate debate. A debate genuinely opened to all qualifying candidates would present a different access question. The State may neither disguise viewpoint exclusion as program definition nor rely on its own classification without examination of actual operation.

**Precedent treatment:** Cornelius and Perry Education supply the selective-access rule; Rosenberger and Bronx preserve viewpoint equality within genuine boundaries; Denver Area is not expanded beyond its distinct components.

##### 2. The supported exclusion is reasonable and viewpoint neutral

**Holding and operative rule:** A genuine assessment of public support and journalistic significance may justify exclusion from this selective debate when it reasonably serves the program's purpose and is not a pretext for viewpoint discrimination. The established criteria and application satisfy that rule here; the Constitution imposes no additional prerequisite that future criteria be published.

**Controlling explanation:** Limited discussion time supports selecting a manageable group that will provide a useful exchange. Reasonableness does not require the best or only conceivable debate design. The advance selection category and staff's supported assessment of organization, voter support and press recognition bear on the program's purpose. The jury's express rejection of political pressure and viewpoint disagreement confirms the absence of those asserted improper causes; it does not by itself substitute for examining the criteria's reasonable relationship to the program.

The contrary standards argument rightly identifies the danger of officials controlling political access through vague judgments of seriousness. It does not establish an additional constitutional requirement of a numerical formula, universal written policy or prospective publication for this otherwise reasonable, neutral selection. Courts retain authority to test genuine viewpoint neutrality and pretext.

Ballot qualification alone neither establishes strong public support nor defeats a legitimate selective format. This holding does not authorize incumbent favoritism, sham criteria, selective administration, exclusion because a viewpoint is unpopular or denial from a program actually opened on terms the candidate satisfies. It decides no damages amount, official immunity, election remedy or future broadcast obligation.

**Precedent treatment:** The nonpublic-forum reasonableness and viewpoint rules are applied to the established record without adding a new publication or formula requirement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Cornelius and Perry Education:** Applied to actual selective access and reasonable, viewpoint-neutral restrictions.
- **Rosenberger:** Preserves private viewpoints within the actual program rather than compelling universal access.
- **Good News/Good Sports and Bronx Household:** Preserve actual comparators and genuine forum boundaries; the planned selective debate differs from broad rental access.
- **Denver Area:** No universal public-broadcast classification or government editorial immunity is drawn from its separate components or incompatible rationales.

#### Law After Decision

This selective debate may use reasonable, genuinely viewpoint-neutral judgments about public support and journalistic significance. Forbes's exclusion satisfies that standard on the established record. Judicial review for pretext and discrimination remains; no universal candidate-access, published-criteria or numerical-threshold rule is created.

#### Separate Writings

Stevens, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, would sustain the appellate reversal on a narrower ground than an automatic right of every ballot-qualified candidate to participate. The State has created a platform for candidates' competing private political speech, and sufficiently definite, reviewable selection standards are necessary to constrain its discretion. Cornelius's access principles, Shuttlesworth's objection to uncontrolled permit discretion and Rosenberger's neutral-allocation requirement inform that conclusion. The spring category existed, but major-party status and strong popular support leave too much room for officials' judgments about seriousness. The jury's rejection of improper motive does not answer this distinct objection to the governing standards. The dissent permits reasonable selection, prescribes no particular polling number, finds no viewpoint bias contrary to the verdict, and would order no automatic damages or future broadcast.

#### Procedure After Action

The lower courts must implement judgment for the Commission on Forbes's reviewed First Amendment forum claim. No additional criterion-publication requirement applies. The abandoned statutory-access claim is not revived, and no separate damages, immunity, costs or future-election determination is made.

#### Source Notes

The reported record at 523 U.S. 666 and the underlying Eighth Circuit judgment identify the advance selection category, invitations, ballot qualification, staff testimony and jury findings. The original trial papers are not reproduced. The verdict rejects improper motive; it is not a finding about every conceivable access standard. No broader past selection practice or later election fact is supplied.



### Diamondstone v. Macaluso — 1998-06-25

[Controlling Record](../records/diamondstone_v_macaluso_merits_1998-06-25.md#public-projection).

#### Event

Diamondstone v. Macaluso, No. 97-7216 below. Plenary merits decision, June 25, 1998. On review of the Second Circuit's June 22, 1998 judgment, 148 F.3d 113, after partial summary judgment and trial. The questions concern the initial insurance-related traffic stops, the significance of judicial notice for each official's immunity and personal supervisory responsibility, and the related state malicious-prosecution remedy. A separate Supreme Court docket and exact argument date are not established.

Macaluso stopped Diamondstone twelve times in 1991–1992. Stops 1, 2 and 11 rested on observed passing/equipment, speeding and turn-signal violations. Stops 3–10 and 12 were initiated to renew demands for insurance proof. The court of appeals reversed the district court's favorable summary judgment on those nine initial stops, left immunity for individual determination, vacated the supervisors' immunity judgments and directed a new state malicious-prosecution trial.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

#### Public Action

The Court affirms the Second Circuit judgment insofar as reviewed, preserving the distinct reversals, vacaturs and remand directions described below.

#### Judgment & Remedy

All nine Justices support the bounded appellate affirmance: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. That common judgment coalition applies to every component below; Stone-Zsela's narrower rationale joins are stated separately.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial stops 3–10 and 12 | Affirm the unlawful-initiation determination, 9–0 | All nine named above | Preserve reversal of Macaluso's Fourth Amendment summary judgment; liability and immunity remain distinct |
| Initial stops 1, 2 and 11 | Preserve independent initial grounds, 9–0 | All nine | No reopening; later restraint and insurance citations retain separate predicates |
| Civil-insurance Fifth Amendment claim | Reject this privilege claim, 9–0 | All nine | No independent seizure authority or new state-citation judgment follows |
| Macaluso's immunity | Affirm individualized remand, 9–0 | All nine | No final grant or denial and no automatic trial |
| Prior, Stanton and Walton | Affirm each vacatur and remand, 9–0 | All nine | Determine each official's own acts, information, causation and immunity |
| State malicious prosecution | Preserve vacatur and new-trial direction, 9–0 | All nine | Retry the state claim under its distinct elements |
| Other dispositions | Leave undisturbed, 9–0 | All nine | Force verdict, zero defamation damages, other surviving verdicts, recusal and Vermont dismissal are not reopened |

The Court awards no damages and commands no federal trial or special factual hearing.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Scalia | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Entire bounded affirmance | Complete stop, privilege, retained-immunity, personal-attribution and consequential-remedy rationale; eight Justices |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Supports each judgment component | Joins the nine identified unlawful initial stops and exact common remand/personal-attribution limits; proposes a different official-burden defense |

Stone-Zsela does not join the complete Harlow/Anderson rationale. He agrees only with the disposition of the civil-insurance privilege and state malicious-prosecution components and adds no ground on them or the unreviewed residual matters. His precise common joins do not extend to every analytical ground of the Court's opinion.

#### Holdings

##### 1. These nine initial insurance stops lacked objective justification

**Holding and operative rule:** An individualized vehicle stop to investigate insurance compliance requires sufficient objective grounds before the seizure begins. On these circumstances, prior nonproduction, intervening time, changed vehicles, valid inspection stickers and absence of fresh evidence did not supply reasonable suspicion for stops 3–10 or 12; anticipated refusal after a renewed stop cannot itself authorize making it.

**Authority:** The eight Associates join the complete Court opinion. Stone-Zsela additionally joins this precise unlawful-initiation conclusion and its preservation of genuinely new violations and distinct later restraints, without adopting every supporting formulation.

**Controlling explanation:** Prouse requires an objective basis for discretionary document-check seizures; knowing the driver does not remove that requirement. Bostick distinguishes permissible questions from detention and rejects refusal alone as automatic seizure authority. Its voluntary-encounter setting does not decide every consequence of a valid regulatory production duty. The State's strongest argument is that repeated nonproduction suggests continued noncompliance. Here that inference lacked a sufficient current connection to actual noncoverage, given the elapsed time, changing inspected cars and lack of new evidence. A sticker is relevant, not conclusive; actual insurance is not substituted for what the officer knew.

Ornelas requires independent review of the ultimate legal sufficiency while protecting historical findings and grounded inferences. The September 10 refusal after stop 11 did not justify the December 22 initiation of stop 12 on this account. License and registration refusal and the disputed alcohol observation followed that stop's initiation and cannot repair its antecedent defect. Whren preserves the separate observed passing/equipment, speeding and turn-signal grounds for stops 1, 2 and 11. Their validity does not decide insurance citations or additional detention. No fixed staleness period, universal silence rule, motive test or probable-cause requirement for every investigative stop is created.

**Precedent treatment:** Prouse and Bostick supply the antecedent-seizure distinction; Ornelas supplies legal review with protected facts; Whren preserves independent objective predicates and separate execution questions.

##### 2. The supported civil-insurance inquiry presents no criminal hazard

**Holding and operative rule:** The self-incrimination privilege does not excuse the challenged insurance production on this record because the actual post-decriminalization scheme supplies no supported criminal exposure or disguised criminal sanction. A civil label alone would not defeat protection against answers exposing the person to criminal responsibility.

**Authority:** The eight Associates join this Court rationale. Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** Lefkowitz preserves protection when civil questioning threatens criminal use. Allen requires attention to substance rather than a label. Vermont decriminalized these minor violations in 1989; the insurance sanction is at most $100, includes no imprisonment and serves financial responsibility. No supported criminal use or punitive substitute is identified. The earlier 1987 rulings concerned a different legal setting. Byers supplies a relevant regulatory distinction but its separate rationales do not create a universal majority exemption from the privilege. A possible DUI accusation does not establish that insurance information would incriminate Diamondstone on that distinct accusation. Absence of this privilege authorizes no seizure and does not establish that silence alone proves an insurance violation under state law. Existing state evidentiary dispositions remain intact.

**Precedent treatment:** Lefkowitz's criminal-use protection and Allen's substance inquiry remain; Byers is used without converting its plurality reasoning into a categorical rule.

##### 3. Immunity requires each stop's contemporaneous law and information

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Harlow and Anderson, each official's immunity depends on whether the particular conduct violated a federal right sufficiently established at the time, assessed objectively on the supported account of that official's acts and information. Relevant judicial communications and superior advice must be assessed individually; neither an adverse state ruling nor superior approval automatically determines federal immunity.

**Authority:** The eight Associates join the complete retained-framework holding. Stone-Zsela joins only the specific remand limits that applicable judicial correction and individual information matter, superior encouragement is no automatic excuse, and a genuinely different later premise remains distinct.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson separates a constitutional violation from an objectively reasonable legal mistake and requires no factually identical precedent. Davis rejects automatic immunity forfeiture for breach of a different state rule. Yet an officer's receipt of a judicial explanation rejecting the same stale stop predicate can bear on the reasonableness of continued reliance under existing federal law. The February citation-evidence ruling and later June stop ruling are different. The latter followed stops 3–6 and preceded stops 7–10; its conflicting June 20/June 30 dates are not resolved here. Later rulings cannot supply earlier knowledge.

Macaluso's claimed further conversations with the hearing officer are neither assumed exonerating advice nor dismissed as nonexistent. Supported timing, content and an objectively reasonable understanding matter; mere sincere disagreement or a bare claim of consultation does not settle immunity. Hunter preserves judicial resolution under ordinary factual constraints; Elder requires relevant conduct-time law. Crawford-El retains ordinary proof and independent defenses. DeCarlo, Ringuette and Smith preserve individual roles and particularized notice without an identical-case demand. Their later dates cannot create 1992 notice. The remand orders neither blanket immunity, blanket denial, automatic discovery, a hearing nor a trial.

**Precedent treatment:** Harlow/Anderson remain governing; Davis preserves the state/federal distinction; Hunter and Elder govern judicial review; Crawford-El, Smith, DeCarlo and Ringuette are applied at their actual procedural and temporal scope.

##### 4. Supervisory responsibility cannot be inferred from title or another person's knowledge

**Holding and operative rule:** The supervisors' immunity judgments cannot stand on the displaced premise that every initial stop was lawful. On remand, personal receipt of information, its content, authority, opportunity, conduct, causation and conduct-time federal clarity must be established separately for Prior, Stanton and Walton; rank, silence or another official's knowledge alone is insufficient.

**Authority:** The eight Associates join the complete Court rationale. Stone-Zsela joins the precise personal-attribution and nonimputation limits and the remand, not every retained-immunity ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Stanton's presence and reported response on July 26 identify a possible personal role without proving his knowledge of every court ruling. The alleged August 11 reports to Prior and Walton can bear on later conduct, particularly stop 12, but cannot retroactively establish responsibility for stops 3–10. Unspecified supervisory encouragement is not attributed to each defendant. Doe distinguishes knowing causal participation from negligence or rank; its school-specific holding does not establish a general police supervisory duty or later notice for earlier conduct. DeCarlo and Ringuette require actual individual roles and federal clarity. Direct authorization and a failure to answer a complaint require their own supported legal and factual analysis. No present finding establishes supervisory liability, defeat of immunity or damages.

**Precedent treatment:** Doe's personal-causation principle is applied without importing its school-specific duty; DeCarlo and Ringuette preserve individualized responsibility and notice.

##### 5. The state retrial remedy preserves distinct tort elements

**Holding and operative rule:** The appellate vacatur and new-trial direction on state malicious prosecution remain because the erroneous instruction that the officer had probable cause to stop could affect the separate probable-cause-to-prosecute element. An unlawful stop does not itself establish the absence of every prosecution ground, institution of proceedings, malice, favorable termination or damages.

**Authority:** The eight Associates join the consequential-remedy rationale. Stone-Zsela supports this component's disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** The rejected instruction could have influenced the jury's assessment of the insurance prosecutions, so the existing retrial remedy remains appropriate. The other Vermont elements retain independent force. This conclusion creates no federal malicious-prosecution rule and reopens neither the force verdict nor the zero-damages defamation determination. Ricci's distinct station-custody holding does not expand this review to adjudicate the later restraint anew. The specific state retrial is not an automatic federal trial or damages award.

**Precedent treatment:** Ricci preserves the distinction between initial authority and additional custody without deciding an unreviewed custody claim here; no new federal tort doctrine is adopted.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Prouse and Bostick:** Applied to antecedent seizure authority, without a categorical evidentiary rule for all refusals or regulatory duties.
- **Ornelas and Whren:** Current review and objective-stop principles remain; independently justified stops and later restraints remain separate.
- **Lefkowitz, Allen and Byers:** Criminal exposure and actual sanction substance govern; no blanket civil-regulatory exception is created.
- **Harlow, Anderson and Davis:** The objective, particularized immunity framework remains, with no automatic forfeiture from a state ruling or defense from superior approval.
- **Hunter, Elder, Crawford-El, Smith, DeCarlo and Ringuette:** Judicial procedure, complete supported facts, conduct-time law and individual roles remain essential. Later decisions do not create retroactive notice.
- **Doe:** Knowing causal personal involvement remains distinct from title; its particular supervisory setting is not generalized into a universal police duty.
- **Johnson and Behrens:** Their interlocutory limits are distinguished from final-judgment review.
- **Ricci:** Its distinct custody inquiry remains; no unreviewed custody or force issue is reopened.

#### Law After Decision

The nine identified initial stops are unlawful under the existing objective seizure rule. Harlow/Anderson remains the governing defense, applied separately by stop and official. Judicial notice can matter without preclusion or automatic liability; superior encouragement supplies no independent authority. The civil-insurance privilege rejection supplies no stop power. The state retrial and unresolved federal responsibility remain distinct. Stone-Zsela’s proposed replacement defense is not adopted.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Applicable judicial rulings rejecting the same premise for materially similar stops can remove any automatic claim of reasonable mistake. An official cannot simply invoke general uncertainty about traffic enforcement after receiving that correction, and supervisory encouragement cannot authorize unconstitutional action. His agreement extends to the identified unlawful initial stops, genuinely new-violation reservation and individual remand limits, without adopting every rationale in the Court's opinion.

He would replace the retained immunity framework with a contextual defense under which the official bears the justificatory burden. Pertinent law, actual judicial correction, each official's information and a genuine reasonable misunderstanding must be examined, rather than supplying a duplicative excuse for deliberate violations. This is a proposed change; the Court retains Harlow/Anderson. He specifies no new quantum of proof, exhaustive factor list or compulsory procedure. A fresh violation or genuinely different factual premise may justify a later stop, and individual knowledge cannot be presumed from rank. Even failure of the defense proves neither every constitutional element nor causation, supervisory liability, damages or a right to trial. His agreement with the privilege disposition and state retrial adds no separate ground on those components.

#### Procedure After Action

The case returns through the court of appeals for the preserved district-court proceedings: individualized immunity and responsibility determinations and the specified state malicious-prosecution retrial. No automatic federal trial, damages award, new hearing requirement or categorical immunity disposition is imposed. Other judgments remain undisturbed.

#### Source Notes

The full reported lower opinion, 148 F.3d 113, supplies the stop chronology and distinct judgment components. Its chronology dates the June ruling June 30, while its immunity discussion says June 20; the supported sequence places it after stops 3–6 and before stops 7–10. The exact date, further hearing-officer conversations and each supervisor’s actual information are not resolved. A date-dependent determination requires the actual order. The scheduled June 25 Supreme Court review has no established separate docket or exact argument date.



### National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley — 1998-06-25

[Controlling Record](../records/national_endowment_for_the_arts_v_finley_merits_1998-06-25.md#public-projection).

#### Event

National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley, No. 97-371. Argued March 31, 1998; decided June 25, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 100 F.3d 671. The question is whether 20 U.S.C. §954(d)(1), requiring the NEA to consider decency and respect for diverse public beliefs and values in evaluating artistic excellence and merit, is facially invalid under the First Amendment or unconstitutionally vague under the Fifth Amendment.

The individual claims concerning earlier grant denials were settled. NAAO's representative challenge concerning eligible member organizations remains live. The Court does not apply the later-enacted provision to the earlier denials or decide all subsequent appropriations exceptions.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

#### Public Action

The Court reverses the facial constitutional judgment and associated injunction and remands for proceedings implementing that disposition.

#### Judgment & Remedy

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing or nonreaching Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First Amendment facial invalidation | Reversed, 7–2 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela and Souter would affirm | Remove the injunction resting on this facial ground; concrete applications remain outside review |
| Distinct facial vagueness ground | Rejected by eight Justices | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela does not reach this ground | Vagueness supplies no independent basis for the facial injunction |

No grant is awarded or withdrawn, settled claim revived or actual denial newly adjudicated. Souter's agreement on vagueness does not alter his vote to retain the injunction on First Amendment grounds.

#### Opinion Topology

#### Holdings

##### 1. The consideration requirement is not facially invalid under the First Amendment

**Holding and operative rule:** In this competitively selective arts-grant program, §954(d)(1)'s requirement to consider general decency and respect for diverse public beliefs and values, alongside artistic excellence and merit, is facially permissible because it prescribes neither a categorical viewpoint exclusion nor fixed disqualifying weight. The consideration is mandatory; an invidious discriminatory application, a penalty on independently financed expression or coercive suppression remains separately reviewable.

**Authority:** The narrow facial-validity part is directly joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas support the judgment on a broader ground; their votes are not added to this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute requires consideration, so the Court cannot treat the words as optional or erase them through panel diversity. But attention to a consideration is not an instruction to deny every work that causes offense. Artistic grants require comparative judgments about excellence, merit, audience and suitability within a program that cannot support every applicant. These considerations can operate contextually without a mandatory viewpoint veto. The facial challenge therefore does not establish that the enacted criterion must operate unconstitutionally in the manner claimed.

Rosenberger protects viewpoints within a generally available private-publication benefit; it does not classify every expenditure as that benefit. Forbes permits genuine selection in its particular candidate debate while preserving review for viewpoint discrimination; it supplies no automatic immunity for arts officials. Denver Area keeps private editorial permission distinct from governmental burdens and yields no universal subsidy rule. Those distinctions permit this narrow conclusion without treating private art as the government's own speech.

The contrary argument correctly insists that consideration must do real work. It does not establish that every meaningful contextual judgment must become a prohibited disqualification. Scarcity alone would not excuse an actual invidious denial. The Court decides neither a particular award nor the legality of a condition suppressing speech financed outside this program. The separate obscenity prohibition in §954(d)(2) is not rewritten.

**Precedent treatment:** Rosenberger's program-specific viewpoint rule and Forbes's genuine-selection and viewpoint-review requirements remain intact; Denver Area is not extended into categorical grant immunity.

##### 2. Comparative artistic standards do not establish facial vagueness here

**Holding and operative rule:** In this selective grant scheme, the decency and respect considerations, read with artistic excellence and merit, are not facially void for vagueness merely because comparative artistic judgment cannot be reduced to precise objective criteria. This conclusion concerns discretionary arts patronage, not a criminal prohibition, a license required to speak or an objective entitlement to payment.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join the narrow vagueness part. Scalia and Thomas independently reject the claim through their broader subsidy rationale. Stone-Zsela does not decide it.

**Controlling explanation:** The scheme necessarily asks decisionmakers to compare works according to artistic judgments. Precision appropriate to a coercive speech prohibition cannot simply be demanded of every such comparison without also disabling ordinary judgments of excellence and merit. The challenged considerations operate within that selective function and impose no criminal liability or permission requirement for creating art. The possibility that uncertainty can discourage applications does not make all aesthetic imprecision unconstitutional. Actual viewpoint discrimination remains distinct and reviewable; permissible imprecision is not permission to conceal an unlawful criterion. Souter's First Amendment objection addresses that separate alleged criterion rather than uncertainty inherent in awarding scarce artistic support.

**Precedent treatment:** The speech-protective concern with uncontrolled coercive discretion is preserved; its demands are applied in light of this program's actual grant function rather than transferred unchanged from criminal or licensing rules.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Rosenberger:** Its viewpoint rule remains tied to the private-publication opportunity actually opened; not every subsidy must be structured as that program.
- **Forbes:** Genuine selective judgment and review of viewpoint discrimination remain compatible. Public ownership and a selective purpose create no blanket constitutional immunity.
- **Denver Area:** Its distinct permission and compulsory-burden holdings remain distinct; none supplies a universal governmental-speech or subsidy exception.
- **Rust and Maher:** The four-Justice spending discussion uses their program-definition and funding-priority distinctions. That discussion establishes no broader holding authorizing viewpoint conformity throughout private arts patronage.

#### Law After Decision

The challenged consideration requirement survives this facial challenge, and permissible imprecision in this comparative arts setting supplies no separate vagueness basis for invalidation. No categorical viewpoint veto, automatic-disqualification rule, required weight, panel-composition safe harbor or universal grant exemption is created. Invidious applications and conditions burdening independent speech retain their own constitutional questions. Section 954(d)(2)'s obscenity prohibition and otherwise lawful artistic and administrative criteria remain separate.

#### Separate Writings

**Supplementary spending discussion by Breyer, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy.** These four Justices add that program-specific appropriations may define what government chooses to support without financing every activity. Rust and Maher illustrate distinctions between funding a defined program and prohibiting independent activity. The observation does not replace the narrower statutory construction or authorize every discriminatory arts award. Ginsburg withholds assent from this further discussion; it has no independent controlling force.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** Government may choose the art it subsidizes, including a preference for decency and respect, without thereby preventing a rejected artist from creating the same work independently. This broader account treats the statutory command as real and does not depend on reducing it to a harmless consideration. Rosenberger's broadly available printing benefit and Forbes's opened candidate opportunity differ from discretionary arts patronage. The concurrence likewise rejects importing speech-related vagueness doctrine from coercive regulation into ordinary grant selection as such. It supplies no approval of penalties on independently funded speech, disguised regulation, every possible unconstitutional condition or departures from the rules of a forum actually opened.

**Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela in the First Amendment part, dissenting.** Requiring officials to consider decency and respect for public beliefs directs attention to whether private art conforms to prevailing views. That criterion disfavors offensive, irreverent and challenging expression even if it is not dispositive in every case. Treating mandatory consideration as legally inconsequential would delete Congress's command, while panel diversity does not remove it. Rosenberger's protection of independent private viewpoints and Forbes's continuing viewpoint constraints cannot be evaded merely because artistic support is selective. The program supports private expression, not a governmental message. Genuine neutral judgments concerning merit, subject, audience, educational suitability, finances and administration remain available; invalidating the proviso would not require funding every applicant. These two Justices would retain facial First Amendment relief without deciding settled individual claims, unrelated remedial questions or public financing of religious exercise.

Souter alone adds that artistic comparative judgment necessarily permits imprecision and joins the Court's narrow rejection of facial vagueness. His objection is to the viewpoint criterion, not unavoidable uncertainty in assessing art. Stone-Zsela does not join this statement and leaves vagueness unresolved.

#### 1998-06-08 — United States v. Beggerly, No. 97-731; Fifth Circuit No. 95-60625, 114 F.3d 484.

[Public Projection](../records/united_states_v_beggerly_merits_1998-06-08.md#public-projection)

##### Event

United States v. Beggerly, No. 97-731. Argued April 27, 1998; decided June 8, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, 114 F.3d 484, decided May 29, 1997 and corrected July 28, 1997. The district court dismissed the independent action, denied the claimants' summary-judgment request and denied amendment asserting Quiet Title Act/Tucker Act jurisdiction. The court of appeals vacated the 1982 consent judgment as to these claimants and directed title for them. The questions concern authority to consider correction of that decree, extraordinary equitable relief and the alternative statutory title route; reopening, title and compensation remain distinct.

##### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

##### Public Action

The Fifth Circuit judgment is reversed and the case remanded to give effect to rejection of the independent action and the untimely alternative Quiet Title Act route.

##### Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The appellate vacatur and title direction cannot stand; the 1982 consent judgment remains operative. No new title trial, extraordinary-relief hearing, valuation, repayment or second payment is ordered. The proposed district-court Tucker Act/inverse-condemnation route exceeding $10,000 supplies no new jurisdiction or amendment relief. Stone-Zsela agrees with that latter disposition without adding a ground.

##### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Supports the complete reversal and remand | I: same-court corrective jurisdiction; II: extraordinary-action threshold; III: QTA notice, period and statutory rejection of additional equitable tolling; IV: title and monetary consequences |
| Same opinion, partial join | Breyer | Stone-Zsela | Supports the complete disposition | Parts I and II and Part IV's reversal/consent-judgment consequences; disposition only on Part III and the separate Tucker Act/inverse-condemnation route, without joining their additional rationales |
| Concurrence | Stevens | Souter | Fully joins the Court and supports reversal | Distinguishes conceivable fraudulent-concealment or equitable-estoppel questions from the additional equitable tolling rejected here; grants no exception or relief |

##### Holdings

###### 1. The original court may consider the narrowly corrective independent action

**Holding and operative rule:** When the United States invoked a court's jurisdiction and obtained the challenged decree there, that same court may entertain a traditional independent action directed to relief from that decree without requiring a second independent jurisdictional grant merely because the request takes a new action's form. That corrective authority does not itself establish a new title action, general subject-matter jurisdiction or sovereign consent to affirmative monetary relief.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion has all nine Justices for bounded corrective jurisdiction. Part IV's additional monetary-route rationale is addressed separately below.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 60(b)'s savings language preserves an existing equitable procedure. Pacific Railroad illustrates its ancillary character: correction of the court's own decree continues the original controversy for jurisdictional purposes. The Government's invocation of that court permits this limited attack on the judgment it obtained; power to entertain the request does not establish entitlement to reopen.

Kokkonen restricts enforcement of a separate settlement contract and expressly reserves reopening. Peacock distinguishes effectuation of a decree from a new action imposing another person's debt. Neither requires the Government's blanket rule that every independent corrective action needs a new jurisdictional grant. The original court's power is actually resolved, not assumed merely to reject the merits.

The warning against new substantive relief remains valid: the authority decided here concerns correction of the same decree, not an independent authorization of every form of relief that might follow. A distinct title or monetary demand must satisfy its own lawful route.

###### 2. The alleged search and disclosure failure does not satisfy the extraordinary threshold

**Holding and operative rule:** An independent action relieving a completed judgment is available only to prevent a grave miscarriage of justice; newly discovered favorable evidence or an allegedly incomplete search and disclosure does not alone establish that exceptional entitlement. On the supported allegations here, the independent action fails, leaving the consent judgment operative without deciding the discovered document's ultimate effect on title.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine Justices for the extraordinary threshold, application and stated reservations.

**Controlling explanation:** Hazel-Atlas supplies the exceptional-injustice principle; Marshall's alleged forged-document case illustrates its force without making forgery indispensable. The traditional conditions reflected in Bankers Mortgage and National Surety—an inequitable judgment, a good defense, fraud, accident or mistake preventing its use, absence of claimant fault or negligence, and absence of an adequate legal remedy—must establish that exceptional need together. They do not provide an ordinary second chance whenever better evidence appears.

Rule 60's independent action must coexist with its ordinary motions and limits, including the one-year limit for the relevant mistake, newly discovered evidence and misconduct motions. Treating the alleged failure to provide relevant information alone as sufficient would bypass those limits. The Court does not decide whether a timely Rule 60(b)(3) motion could have succeeded.

The claimants emphasize the Government's inaccurate representation, its influence on settlement and their sustained efforts to locate records. Those matters are accepted at their supported level. They do not make the alleged incomplete search and disclosure approach the required extraordinary injustice. The Court neither finds the Government's search adequate nor faults the claimants for failing to find the material earlier. A document's presence in federal archives does not establish intentional concealment or obstruction.

Morgan Stanley and Plaut permit expressly bounded legislative reinstatement, with independent judicial eligibility and merits decisions. No comparable legislative command governs this decree; those decisions do not authorize unrestricted judicial reconsideration. The ruling creates no absolute ban on correcting consent judgments, universal intentional-fraud requirement or general archive-search immunity. It makes no separate adverse factual findings on each traditional condition and does not decide whether the new material would have changed the original result.

###### 3. The QTA's notice-based period excludes the additional equitable tolling asserted here

**Holding and operative rule:** For the private title claim presented, §2409a(g)'s twelve-year period runs from when the claimant or predecessor knew or should have known of the United States' adverse claim, not from discovery of convincing evidence supporting the claimant's own title. Additional nonstatutory equitable tolling is incompatible with that notice-triggered, unusually generous period and the Act's protection of certainty in land ownership; this 1994 alternative title action is untimely.

**Authority:** Part III has the eight Associate Justices for the complete statutory rule and application. Stone-Zsela agrees with the QTA disposition without joining that additional statutory rationale. Stevens and Souter fully join while separately addressing distinct doctrines the Court does not decide.

**Controlling explanation:** Irwin supplies a rebuttable equitable presumption, not an additional interval regardless of the enacted scheme. The QTA already accounts for lack of knowledge through its accrual rule. Twelve years to act after actual or constructive notice, together with the special need for predictable land-title repose, rebuts additional nonstatutory tolling of the kind asserted.

Brockamp requires examination of the particular statute's operation; its tax clauses are not imported here. Beach likewise demands provision-specific analysis without converting the QTA period into the separate substantive-right expiration rule governing rescission there. Morgan Stanley and Plaut authorize their defined legislative reinstatement procedure, not a general suspension of this statutory clock.

The claimants knew the adverse claim by 1979 at the latest; the lower court used 1976. Either date places the June 1, 1994 action beyond twelve years. Continued searching and the 1991 discovery neither move accrual nor authorize tolling from the original search until the favorable document emerged. The result needs no adverse diligence or affirmative bad-faith finding.

The Court does not classify every QTA timing provision as jurisdictional, decide unpresented special claimant categories or create a universal rule against every doctrine called equitable. A materially different fraudulent-concealment or equitable-estoppel question is not resolved. No such doctrine is found applicable here, and no new hearing or amendment entitlement follows from leaving it open.

###### 4. Correction, title and monetary relief require separate authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of both asserted routes removes the appellate title award and leaves the 1982 consent judgment operative, so §2409a(b)'s possession-or-compensation option is not activated. Bounded corrective authority supplies no new district-court Tucker Act jurisdiction or amendment entitlement for the inverse-condemnation claim exceeding $10,000, and no repayment, second payment or compensation valuation is ordered.

**Authority:** Part IV's complete remedial rationale has the eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela joins removal of the title award, preservation of the consent judgment and the resulting nonactivation of the possession-or-compensation option; he agrees only in disposition on the separate monetary-jurisdiction route and adds no ground there.

**Controlling explanation:** Nordic Village and Department of Energy v. Ohio require consent to the particular affirmative monetary remedy. Neither an equitable label nor authority over an existing decree creates that consent or enlarges district-court Tucker Act jurisdiction. The proposed amendment cannot obtain it merely by attaching a compensation demand to this unsuccessful corrective action.

The lower court's possession-or-compensation option depended on its title award, which no longer stands. Even permission to reopen would not itself establish the disputed grant's validity or a complete title chain; after rejection of the actual routes, no alternative title trial or valuation is required. The 1975 canceled agreement and the payment under the 1982 settlement remain distinct. The Court creates no obligation to repay that settlement, pay it again or recognize a new recovery forum. A genuinely distinct permitted claim remains unadjudicated, not established.

##### Precedent Treatment

- **Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co., May 16, 1994:** Its separate contract-enforcement limit is distinguished and its reopening reservation respected.
- **Peacock v. Thomas, February 21, 1996:** Distinguished at the boundary between decree effectuation and a new substantive obligation; same-decree correction is not foreclosed.

##### Law After Decision

A court may consider a traditional independent attack on the very decree it entered, but relief requires a grave miscarriage of justice. Power, extraordinary entitlement, statutory title authority and monetary consent remain separate. The QTA period for this private claim begins with notice of the Government's adverse claim, and the asserted additional equitable tolling cannot extend it until favorable title evidence is found. The 1982 settlement remains operative. No general archive-search immunity, definitive concealment or estoppel exception, new title chain or alternative recovery right is established.

##### Separate Writings

Stevens, joined by Souter, fully joins the Court. He distinguishes additional equitable tolling from fraudulent concealment or equitable estoppel. Outrageous governmental misconduct that prevents a person already aware of the Government's adverse claim from discovering her own claim might conceivably present one of those distinct doctrines. That possibility does not replace or qualify their assent to the statutory tolling rule adopted today.

The concurrence neither recognizes a definitive exception nor finds this hypothetical established. It makes no fraud finding, grants no estoppel, postpones no accrual and orders no hearing or amendment. The other Justices' adoption of the Court's bounded statutory ground likewise does not decide that distinct hypothetical adversely. The present reversal and preservation of the settlement remain unanimous.

The appellate order vacating the 1982 judgment and awarding title is removed; that consent judgment remains operative. The district court's blanket denial of power to entertain a same-court independent action is not the adopted ground. No alternative title-merits trial or extraordinary-relief hearing is required, and the grant's validity and complete title chain remain unadjudicated.

Stevens, joined by Souter, fully joins the Court. He distinguishes additional equitable tolling from fraudulent concealment or equitable estoppel. Outrageous governmental misconduct that prevents a person already aware of the Government's adverse claim from discovering her own claim might conceivably present one of those distinct doctrines. That possibility does not replace or qualify their assent to the statutory tolling rule adopted today.

The concurrence neither recognizes a definitive exception nor finds this hypothetical established. It makes no fraud finding, grants no estoppel, postpones no accrual and orders no hearing or amendment. The other Justices' adoption of the Court's bounded statutory ground likewise does not decide that distinct hypothetical adversely. The present reversal and preservation of the settlement remain unanimous.
