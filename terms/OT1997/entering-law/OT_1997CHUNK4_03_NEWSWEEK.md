#### Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner, 516 U.S. 325

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1239.

**Decided:** February 21, 1996.

##### The issuer-based deduction discriminates against interstate investment

**Holding:** A State discriminates against interstate commerce when the tax on a resident's otherwise comparable corporate shares increases as the issuer's local income-tax exposure decreases. Neither the shareholder's local residence nor drafting the geographic advantage as a deduction removes that discrimination, and proof that a particular issuer already lost an investment is unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** The tax is twenty-five cents per $100 of share value, reduced according to the issuer's North Carolina-apportioned income. For corporate shareholders the linked dividend-deduction proportion applies without the ordinary $15,000 dividend-deduction cap.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Oregon Waste's express-geographic-discrimination rule and West Lynn's examination of linked operation apply; a deduction label or shareholder residence does not remove discrimination.

##### Corporate income and share ownership are not shown to be equivalent taxable events

**Holding:** A compensatory-tax defense requires the State to identify the actual intrastate burden being offset, show a roughly equivalent and nonexcessive interstate charge, and establish substantially equivalent taxable events reasonably treated as mutually exclusive proxies. North Carolina's corporate-income and shareholder-property taxes do not satisfy those requirements merely because corporate profits may influence share value; the failure to establish equivalent events independently defeats the defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Different nominal taxpayers do not invariably prevent compensation, and income and property taxes may coexist. This State failed to establish equivalent events.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Oregon Waste supplies the cumulative compensatory conditions, Maryland v. Louisiana the actual-local-burden requirement and Armco the independently fatal equivalent-events defect. Henneford preserves genuinely complementary taxation.

**Independent alternative holding:** A State does not establish the compensatory defense's actual-burden and approximate-equivalence requirements merely by invoking typical price/earnings ratios or aggregate corporate tax contributions. North Carolina's comparison fails to demonstrate the required matching burden on this record, an additional ground for rejecting its defense.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The 1990 claim is not governed by a new universal calculation using the later 7.75% rate; the state discussion also identifies the earlier 7% rate. Its general price/earnings reasoning is neither a statutory command nor an adopted equality finding for every taxpayer. Associated Industries requires the relevant actual matching burden, rather than aggregate or other-transaction offsets.

##### Darnell's contrary Commerce Clause permission is overruled

**Holding:** Darnell v. Indiana is overruled to the extent it permits discriminatory taxation of residents' shares in out-of-state corporations under the Commerce Clause merely by reference to a different corporate-level local tax and an asserted substantial equality. Its distinct equal-protection and standing determinations are not erased.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Darnell v. Indiana, 226 U.S. 390, is expressly overruled only on its incompatible Commerce Clause permission. Oregon Waste and Associated Industries require the cumulative inquiry; Darnell's separate standing and equal-protection determinations remain.

##### Invalidity requires lawful remedial proceedings, not a predetermined refund

**Holding:** Reversal of the tax's constitutional justification requires remand for state-law severability and constitutionally adequate relief. When a State compelled payment before a meaningful opportunity to contest the tax, McKesson requires adequate backward-looking correction; independently, Reich forbids withdrawing a clearly held-out postpayment remedy after payment and substituting an unpursued prepayment route.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** McKesson permits lawful refunds, constitutionally permissible equalizing retrospective treatment or a lawful combination where backward-looking relief is required. Reich's protection applies to a clearly held-out postpayment remedy even after voluntary payment; reasonable previously announced conditions and prospective changes with fair notice remain permissible.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for severability and constitutionally adequate relief according to actual payments, preservation, procedures and lawful state choices. No fixed refund, fee award, interest, assessment against others, blanket stock exemption, deduction change or automatic reinstatement of the intermediate appellate remedy follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** McKesson's conditional backward-looking duty and Reich's independent no-withdrawal protection govern relief without selecting a universal refund formula.

#### Camps Newfound/Owatonna, Inc. v. Town of Harrison

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1988.
**Decided:** 1997-05-19.

##### Paid interstate camp services remain protected against discriminatory taxation

**Holding:** A charitable camp that recruits nationally and sells services to visitors crossing state lines participates in interstate commerce, although its property and performance are local and it is nonprofit. Where the same otherwise qualifying private camp offering receives worse tax treatment because its principal beneficiaries are nonresidents, that express geographic condition discriminates; a claimed resident-service distinction must rest on the actual relationship or offering, not beneficiary residence alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts I–II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; six direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The nonresident subparagraph grants at most $50,000 in exempt current just property value only if the statutory average weekly charge is no more than $30; higher charges receive no exemption under that subparagraph. The average divides tax-year charges by all recipients and by the weeks operated principally for nonresidents; the advertised weekly price is not that calculation. Nonprofit corporations incorporated solely for medical research are excepted from this subparagraph, not from neutral general qualifications. Neither hypothetical exception eligibility, Maine-incorporation validity, all charitable acts, religious liberty, equal protection nor travel rights is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heart of Atlanta treats interstate travel for local services as commerce; Fulton scrutinizes geographic tax conditions despite local property or an exemption form. Tracy requires actual offering/service obligations; its regulated rate, reliability and service duties are absent here, but no utility-style regulation is made universally indispensable. Carbone preserves comparison of the actual private service and distinct genuine public systems.

##### Resident benefit, subsidy and public-capacity labels do not justify this tax condition

**Holding:** This discriminatory private-clientele tax condition can survive only upon a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve; support for resident charity does not establish that showing here. Forgone taxes do not make Maine a purchaser of the camp's services or remove the discriminatory tax condition from review, and a compensatory-tax defense requires an identified intrastate burden, a roughly equivalent nonexcessive charge and substantially equivalent taxable events, none established by general resident contributions or asserted charitable savings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts I–II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; six direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision leaves direct resident benefits, ordinary general-revenue assistance, neutral charitable criteria and actual purchased services available. It requires no worldwide charitable support, presumes no numerical disproportion or default, and imposes no duty to disprove every imagined alternative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Oregon Waste supplies demanding local-purpose/nondiscriminatory-alternative scrutiny. Fulton and Oregon Waste require the identified intrastate burden, rough equivalent nonexcessive charge and substantially equivalent taxable events for compensation. SSC protects actual purchased performance; forgone revenue establishes none. West Lynn examines practical fiscal operation without requiring its dedicated-fund facts.

##### Reversal requires lawful state relief, not an automatic exemption or fixed refund

**Holding:** The judgment sustaining this discriminatory condition must be reversed and the case remanded for state-law severability, remaining neutral exemption conditions and constitutionally adequate relief. If payment was compelled before meaningful review, adequate backward-looking correction is required; independently, a clearly held-out postpayment remedy may not be withdrawn after payment and replaced by an unpursued prepayment route, even where payment was voluntary, subject to reasonable preannounced conditions and fair prospective changes.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; five direct votes. Stone-Zsela requires the exemption and does not join this open remedial choice; Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg would affirm.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the residence-condition validation, 6–3, and remand under the five-Justice state-law remedy instruction. Required correction may take a lawful refund, constitutionally permissible retrospective equalization or combination; no fixed refund, interest, new assessment, substitute subsidy or unconditional exemption is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fulton separates invalidity from lawful state remedial choices. McKesson requires backward correction when payment preceded meaningful review; Reich independently protects a clearly held-out postpayment route even after voluntary payment, subject to reasonable preannounced conditions and fair prospective change.

#### Reich v. Collins

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-908.
**Decided:** December 6, 1994.

##### A State may not withdraw its apparent postpayment remedy after payment

**Holding:** When a State holds out a clear postpayment remedy for illegally assessed and collected taxes, including voluntary payments, due process forbids withdrawing that remedy after payment for the constitutional invalidity asserted and then relying on the taxpayer's failure to pursue a different prepayment route. Georgia must afford Reich a clear and certain opportunity for the refund or constitutionally adequate equivalent relief due under federal law, subject to ordinary proof of payment and amount; it may prospectively establish an adequate prepayment system with fair notice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McKesson Corp. v. Division of Alcoholic Beverages and Tobacco, 496 U.S. 18 (1990): applies its clear-and-certain-remedy requirement to withdrawal of an apparently available postpayment route; adequate prepayment systems and lawful remedial choices remain permissible.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): applies the distinction between the federal rule governing open direct review and the lawful remedy; no automatic refund or reopening of final judgments follows.
- Barker v. Kansas, 503 U.S. 594 (1992): preserves its separate reservation of refund questions; substantive pension-tax invalidity does not fix the amount or form of relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The substantive tax invalidity is already accepted. The Court neither declares every Georgia prepayment avenue inadequate nor converts discretionary stays into guaranteed stays. It creates no general government-estoppel doctrine, exemption from clearly announced reasonable requirements, fixed refund, interest award, or reopening of final judgments. Prospective procedural changes remain permissible with adequate review and fair notice.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Georgia Supreme Court's denial of adequate backward-looking relief is reversed and remanded for a clear and certain lawful remedial opportunity. No refund amount, interest award or reopening of final judgments is ordered.

### January 21, 1998 — Lunding v. New York Tax Appeals Tribunal

[Public Projection](../records/lunding_v_new_york_tax_appeals_tribunal_merits_1998-01-21.md#public-projection). The following complete public blocks preserve this event's action, proposition-specific authority, published positions, procedure and source limits.

#### Event

Lunding v. New York Tax Appeals Tribunal, No. 96-1462. Plenary merits decision, January 21, 1998; argued November 5, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the New York Court of Appeals, 89 N.Y.2d 283, 675 N.E.2d 816 (1996), which sustained N.Y. Tax Law § 631(b)(6). The question is whether denying qualifying alimony deductions in calculating nonresidents' New York-source income, within the complete nonresident tax formula, violates Article IV's Privileges and Immunities Clause. Equal Protection and Commerce Clause claims are outside this review.

Christopher and Barbara Lunding were Connecticut residents. Christopher earned income practicing law in New York and paid alimony to a Connecticut former spouse under a Connecticut decree. They claimed a proportionate deduction using approximately 48% of his business income as attributable to New York. Disallowance produced an additional assessment of $3,724 plus interest.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

#### Public Action

The judgment of the New York Court of Appeals is reversed, and the case is remanded for nondiscriminatory treatment of the challenged assessment and appropriate relief under the governing state procedures and constitutional requirements.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Reverse and remand, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Kennedy and Ginsburg.

State proceedings must provide lawful allocation and constitutionally adequate relief. If payment was compelled before a meaningful opportunity to contest the tax, adequate backward-looking correction is required. Independently, if the State clearly held out a postpayment remedy and payment occurred, it may not withdraw that remedy afterward, including for voluntary payments. Actual payment, preservation, amount and applicable procedural conditions remain to be established. No fixed refund, interest award or replacement formula is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer. The same seven Justices adopt both holdings and all their qualifications. Ginsburg dissents, joined in full by Kennedy, on both the income-attribution and independent home-State personal-expense grounds.

#### Holdings

##### Article IV does not permit this inadequately justified categorical exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State taxes another State's citizens on income earned within its borders, a discriminatory deduction rule must rest on a substantial reason beyond noncitizenship, and the discrimination must bear a close relationship to that reason. Section 631(b)(6)'s categorical exclusion of qualifying alimony from deductions against New York-source income fails that inquiry on the source, personal-expense, initial-computation and family-income justifications offered here; genuine source allocation remains permissible, and identical resident and nonresident tax codes are not required.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice Opinion of the Court supplies direct majority authority for this rule, its application and the qualifications below.

**Controlling explanation:** Toomer protects substantial equality in earning a livelihood across state lines while allowing considerable state latitude. Shaffer permits taxation of local income and corresponding source-related deduction limits; Travis nevertheless rejects unjustified unequal personal allowances. Those decisions require examination of the particular deduction and tax, not ordinary rationality alone or mechanical equality.

New York taxes residents' worldwide income and nonresidents' local income. That real difference does not by itself justify excluding all proportionate recognition of alimony. The payment adjusts income available to the payer and lacks an exclusively Connecticut source merely because the former spouses and divorce decree are there. The lower court's Goodwin analogy involved geographically connected home-State property and medical expenses; it does not resolve this alimony provision. The Court adopts no rule requiring apportionment of every personal expense.

The whole calculation also matters. Qualifying alimony reduces hypothetical resident liability and the federal-adjusted-gross-income denominator, but § 631(b)(6) denies the deduction in the New York-source numerator. The initial benefit therefore does not establish substantial equality in the final burden. The ratio was uncapped; no particular actual ratio or total effective rate is found.

The asserted allocation of taxable family income likewise fails to match the enacted line. Payer residence determines the deduction without conditioning it on whether New York taxes the recipient. Examining that mismatch addresses the classification imposing the Lundings' own assessment, without finding how often different family arrangements occur. Neither a hypothetical recipient-based rule nor its constitutionality is decided.

Austin prevents another State's credit, deduction or fiscal choice from curing established discrimination. Connecticut's absence of earned-income taxation in this year supplies no cure. Fulton and Oregon Waste reinforce scrutiny of actual operation and unsupported offsets only by analogy; their Commerce Clause tests do not govern Article IV. States retain justified source limits and permissible cooperative tax arrangements.

##### Relief follows the established tax-remedy predicates

**Holding and operative rule:** The unconstitutional treatment of the challenged assessment requires reversal and remand for lawful state allocation, severability and constitutionally adequate relief. When the State compelled payment before meaningful review, it must provide adequate backward-looking correction; independently, a clearly held-out postpayment remedy cannot be withdrawn after payment, including voluntary payment, subject to ordinary proof and lawful previously announced conditions.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices join the Opinion of the Court's remedial holding in full.

**Controlling explanation:** McKesson requires meaningful correction when payment preceded an adequate opportunity to challenge; Harper distinguishes application of the governing federal rule from the lawful remedy. Fulton preserves state severability and remedial choices, including lawful refunds, constitutionally permissible nondiscriminatory retrospective equalization, or a lawful combination when backward-looking relief is required. Reich separately prevents a State from withdrawing a clear postpayment route after the taxpayer paid and substituting an unpursued prepayment route. Adequate prospective procedures remain permissible with fair notice.

The reported $3,724 additional assessment plus interest does not establish payment or an entitlement to that sum as a refund. The State must apply the constitutional rule through the procedures and payment facts actually established. The Court neither selects the taxpayers' proposed deduction formula as the exclusive remedy nor automatically restores every aspect of the Appellate Division's order. It orders no interest award, new assessment against others, or reopening of unrelated final tax years.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Toomer is applied for substantial equality, a substantial independent reason and the close relationship required between that reason and the discriminatory treatment.
- Shaffer retains nondiscriminatory source taxation and justified corresponding deduction limits; it supplies no categorical exception for every deduction labeled personal.
- Travis is applied to distinguish legitimate source limits from inadequately justified unequal personal allowances. Its rejection of presumed offsets remains operative.
- Austin is applied to reject a cure dependent on another State's tax choices; permissible cooperative arrangements remain available.
- Goodwin's reported home-State property and medical-expense setting is distinguished; that different deduction setting does not resolve this alimony provision.
- Friedsam's earlier statutory construction was superseded by § 631(b)(6); it is not revived as the constitutional rule or imposed as the exclusive replacement formula.
- Fulton and Oregon Waste supply analogical support concerning actual tax operation and unsupported offset explanations. Their Commerce Clause tests and Fulton's limited treatment of Darnell remain confined to their fields.
- McKesson, Harper, Fulton and Reich are applied to preserve conditional backward-looking correction, lawful state remedial choices and the independent protection of a clearly offered postpayment remedy.

#### Law After Decision

A State's narrower nonresident tax base and the personal character of a deduction do not automatically justify denying it. Courts must examine the challenged distinction in the complete tax scheme, require a substantial independent reason closely related to it, and preserve justified source allocation. Section 631(b)(6)'s categorical alimony exclusion cannot be sustained on the reasons offered here.

This decision adds that Article IV application without requiring identical deductions, strict numerical tax equality, the Commerce Clause's compensatory-tax elements or a particular replacement formula. It decides no Equal Protection or Commerce Clause claim, no dispute about federal alimony qualification, and no hypothetical recipient-based alternative. The existing conditional rules governing unconstitutional-tax relief remain in force.

#### Separate Writings

Ginsburg, joined by Kennedy, would affirm on two independent grounds.

First, the payer deduction and recipient inclusion allocate taxable income between former spouses. When both are New York residents, the State taxes the recipient and permits the payer's deduction; when both are Connecticut residents, denying the deduction preserves taxation of New York-source income that would otherwise pass outside the State's resident tax base. In their view, that source-tax and income-attribution relationship supplies a substantial justification in this case. Article IV requires substantial rather than mechanically precise equality. Imperfect treatment of other, mixed-residence couples does not establish these taxpayers' entitlement to remove the income from New York taxation. This position does not depend on a finding of aggregate interstate-payment balance or on Connecticut's imposing a tax.

Second, even treating alimony as an expense, the Connecticut payer, recipient and decree locate this particular marital obligation in the home State; it is not an expense of producing New York professional income. Shaffer and Travis permit the corresponding source limitation, and Goodwin's home-State expense analysis supports leaving that personal expense to Connecticut's law and policy. Travis's general personal exemption is different from a deduction for a particular nonlocal obligation. They also object that correlation with a payer's income does not distinguish alimony from many other personal expenditures; the Court's disclaimer of a universal deduction rule does not, in their view, resolve that distinction.

Their objection to relying on other former couples is a merits objection, not a vote to dismiss for lack of standing. They accept Austin's rule against curing established discrimination through another State's tax choices but find this allocation justified in the first place. They join neither the invalidity holding nor the correction mandate; their grounds are noncontrolling.

#### Procedure After Action

The case returns to the New York courts for proceedings consistent with the Article IV ruling, lawful allocation and state-law severability, and appropriate relief under applicable preserved procedures. Payment, amount, preservation and the actual remedial predicates must determine any backward-looking correction or monetary relief. No particular deduction formula, refund, interest award, compulsory hearing, retained Supreme Court jurisdiction or automatic further review is directed. This Supreme Court review is complete.

### Backward-looking relief for unconstitutional taxes

**Governing question:** What instructions govern backward-looking relief for unconstitutional taxes?

**Current rule:** Applying a federal tax rule retroactively does not alone determine remedy. When the State required payment before adequate opportunity to contest, due process requires meaningful backward-looking relief; lawful refunds, retroactive nondiscriminatory assessments or a combination may suffice. Meaningful prepayment review presents a different remedial setting. State law and due process govern the correction; no automatic refund of every payment follows. Even if an adequate prepayment route existed, a State that held out a clear postpayment remedy for illegally assessed and collected taxes, including voluntary payments, may not withdraw that remedy after payment and rely on failure to use the other route. It must afford a clear and certain opportunity for a refund or constitutionally adequate equivalent relief, subject to ordinary proof of payment and amount. A prospective adequate prepayment system remains possible with fair notice.

**Authority by component:**

- Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate: Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation — Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate, 1993-06-18. Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

- A State may not withdraw its apparent postpayment remedy after payment: A State may not withdraw its apparent postpayment remedy after payment, 1994-12-06. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: nine direct votes for the bounded procedural rule and remedy.

**Present operation effective:** Reich v. Collins, December 6, 1994, for the after-payment withdrawal limit; Harper’s distinct remedial-choice rules remain.
