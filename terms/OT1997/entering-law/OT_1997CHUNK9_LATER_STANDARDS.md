# Mechanically copied standards

### Judicial exhaustion and damages-only federal prison medical-care claims

**Governing question:** Whether exhaustion of an administrative process must precede an existing federal constitutional damages action when Congress has imposed no exhaustion command.

**Current rule:** An express congressional exhaustion requirement governs according to its terms. In the non-APA judicial-exhaustion setting addressed here, absent such a command the court exercises sound discretion consistent with the statutory scheme, weighing prompt judicial access against the actual interests in agency authority, expertise, correction of error, useful fact development, and judicial efficiency. Under the law governing McCarthy, neither the general BOP delegation nor the then-effective §1997e imposed an exhaustion requirement on the existing *Carlson* federal medical-care damages channel. For claims governed by that former statutory baseline, a federal prisoner seeking only damages for a completed medical-care wrong need not exhaust the general BOP procedure considered in *McCarthy*: it cannot award compensation; its initial 15-day filing period and successive 20- and 30-day appeal periods, even with valid-reason extensions, risk forfeiture; and investigation and written responses without a hearing or formal evidentiary findings provide insufficient adjudicative benefit to outweigh access to court. Speculative FTCA conversion or settlement does not supply an available monetary remedy in that process.

**Authority by component:**

- Statutory primacy, judicial discretion, and actual congressional scope: McCarthy v. Madigan — Statutory design governs the exhaustion inquiry, March 4, 1992.
- Damages-only BOP exception and its operative procedural premises: McCarthy — This damages-only claim need not pass through the general BOP process, March 4, 1992. Blackmun's opinion joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter controls both components; Scalia and Thomas join only the result on their no-compensation ground.

**Present operation effective:** McCarthy, March 4, 1992, within its surviving scope; PLRA, April 26, 1996, changes the covered statutory premise.

**Open question:** Prospective relief, a procedure able to award the requested compensation, and materially different administrative adjudication are not decided. The rule supplies no exception to a congressional command and no extension of *Bivens* to a new setting.

**Holdings navigation:** McCarthy v. Madigan, 503 U.S. 140 (1992) — Administrative exhaustion.

**Related current rules:** APA limits on exhaustion of superior-agency appeals states Darby's separate §704 restriction; Prior administrative exhaustion of an FTCA action states McNeil's express statutory prerequisite. Those decisions do not change McCarthy's former-law damages-only BOP holding; the later express PLRA command operates at its own scope.

**Effective transition:** PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §803(d), April 26, 1996, replaces §1997e with a command to exhaust available remedies before a covered confined-prisoner conditions action under §1983 or other federal law. See Statutory exhaustion and screening of prisoner conditions actions. McCarthy's absence-of-command premise no longer supplies a blanket exemption from that text; disputed pending-case operation is not resolved by admission. The general judicial-exhaustion rule survives outside a controlling statutory command.


### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review. An earlier pleading-stage appeal does not by itself exhaust review of a later summary-judgment order: a separable legal immunity question must independently satisfy the collateral-order conditions. This permits neither repetition of an unchanged ruling nor relitigation of matters resolved by the first mandate. Having to defend a different surviving claim does not itself defeat otherwise authorized claim-specific review, but neither supplies missing authorization nor makes that other claim reviewable.

- Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule: Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine support this legal-review proposition.

**Governing question:** Whether an officers’ immunity appeal permits immediate review of a separate municipal liability defense.

**Current rule:** An ordinary municipal liability defense remains effectively reviewable after final judgment and does not acquire immunity from trial through the officers’ immunity. ### Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation

**Governing question:** The distinction between an underlying federal deprivation and municipal responsibility, including final policymaking for the challenged function.

**Current rule:** A municipal employee who proves deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and the required municipal responsibility need not prove an additional, independent abuse-of-governmental-power element. The federal deprivation and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries. Employment alone establishes neither municipal liability nor a substantive right; *Canton*'s training analysis supplies attribution and causation, not the underlying constitutional guarantee. For a final-policymaker theory, the court identifies through state and local law, including custom or usage with legal force, who speaks finally for the particular entity on the challenged subject. Final operational discretion does not alone answer that question; labels, election, territory, financing or lack of commission control is individually insufficient. An Alabama sheriff's independent local office supplies County final law-enforcement authority in the criminal-investigation function addressed in McMillian, even without commission delegation or review. An attributable policy or final decision must still cause the proved federal injury.

**Authority by component:**

- Rejection of the additional element and separation of deprivation from attribution: Collins v. City of Harker Heights — No extra governmental-abuse element under §1983, February 26, 1992; Part A of Stevens's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

- McMillian v. Monroe County — I. Final authority and the government represented are distinct inquiries, 1997-06-02; Ginsburg, all nine
- McMillian v. Monroe County — II. The sheriff's independent local office supplies County law-enforcement authority, 1997-06-02; Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five
- McMillian v. Monroe County — III. The County claim returns for the remaining liability elements, 1997-06-02; the same five

**Present operation effective:** McMillian, 1997-06-02, for final-policymaker identity and the specified Alabama function; Collins retains the separate underlying-deprivation rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992) — Municipal liability and public employment. McMillian v. Monroe County (1997-06-02) — Civil Rights / Municipal liability and final policymaking.



**Exceptions and limits:** Other sheriff functions, other States' arrangements, individual liability and State liability or immunity require separate analysis. Institutional identity does not prove the truth of misconduct or make every official act municipal policy. Bryan County's pending reargument supplies no hiring, fault or causation change.


### Ministerial court reporting and absolute judicial immunity

**Governing question:** What instructions govern ministerial court reporting and absolute judicial immunity?

**Current rule:** Accurate recording and production of a verbatim transcript are ministerial functions and do not carry absolute judicial immunity merely because they are important or integral to adjudication. The claimant of immunity must establish its historical and functional justification; discretionary adjudication supplies a different function. Denial of absolute immunity establishes neither a cause of action nor liability, and other defenses remain open.

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

### No direct federal-agency Bivens damages action

**Governing question:** Whether sovereign consent itself permits constitutional damages against a federal agency.

**Current rule:** A waiver removes sovereign immunity but does not create constitutional damages liability. Bivens’s individual-officer action does not extend directly to a federal agency: that extension would create a new defendant and public obligation. Independently, even assuming compensation and deterrence benefits, the expansion of direct federal fiscal liability and redesign of the remedial structure counsel against judicial creation of that agency action. Existing individual Bivens, Davis and Carlson actions and genuinely authorized statutory remedies retain their own scope; no adequate substitute remedy is presumed.

### Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

**Governing question:** Proof, discovery and causation when unconstitutional motive is an element of a claim and qualified immunity is asserted.

**Current rule:** Qualified immunity imposes no categorical direct-evidence requirement for unconstitutional motive. The plaintiff must establish the actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation through legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence under ordinary procedural burdens. Specific nonconclusory support, lawful discovery limits and the clearly-established-law defense remain available; the rule abolishes neither all heightened specificity requirements nor a burden supplied by the underlying claim. Ordinary preponderance governs unconstitutional motive in the §1983 retaliation claim; immunity and litigation burden cannot justify a special clear-and-convincing quantum. Courts may decide dispositive legal defenses first and require specific nonconclusory support, but cannot categorically withhold discovery needed to obtain proof until the rejected heightened showing is made. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, sequencing, protective orders, relevant proportional discovery and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available. Under Mt. Healthy the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful explanation does not establish that defense or exclude competent motive evidence.

**Authority by component:**

- Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims: Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; nine votes support the rule and its operative limits.

- Crawford-El v. Britton — Proof of unconstitutional motive, 1997-06-16; Stone-Zsela, all nine

- Crawford-El v. Britton — Actual causation and independent defenses, 1997-06-16; all nine

**Present operation effective:** Crawford-El, 1997-06-16, rejecting a special proof quantum and derivative categorical discovery barrier while preserving the carried direct/circumstantial-evidence rule.

### Knowing supervisory acquiescence in school-authority sexual abuse

**Governing question:** When a school supervisor's own inaction violates a pupil's bodily integrity.

**Current rule:** A supervisor is personally responsible when the supervisor actually knows of sexual abuse through school authority or a substantial risk of it, deliberately and unreasonably disregards that known danger despite authority and a reasonable opportunity to protect the pupil, and that culpable inaction causes the injury or its continuation. A reasonable response is not actionable merely because injury continues. Circumstantial evidence may establish awareness, but warning signs do not compel that finding and what the official merely should have known is insufficient. Supervisory status, negligence, failure to discover misconduct, or breach of a state reporting duty alone does not establish the federal wrong.

**Authority by component:**

- Personal culpability and causation: Knowing, causally effective acquiescence in a teacher’s sexual abuse is personal wrongdoing, 1995-10-16; Ginsburg Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Doe v. Taylor ISD, 1995-10-16.

**Exceptions and limits:** Individual qualified immunity requires its separate conduct-time notice inquiry; recognition of the current rule supplies no retroactive notice. No general monitoring duty, vicarious liability, or finding of ultimate liability follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Doe v. Taylor Independent School District.


### Court-record warrant errors and objectively reasonable reliance

**Governing question:** Federal exclusion for an erroneous warrant entry attributable to a nonpolice court employee.

**Current rule:** Federal exclusion is not required for a clerical warrant-record error attributable to a non-law-enforcement court employee when the arresting officer reasonably relies on apparently current warrant information. Subjective innocence is insufficient; known or sufficiently evident system defects can make reliance objectively unreasonable. The rule does not validate the arrest, determine the source of an unresolved error or automatically require admission.

### Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

**Governing question:** Appellate review of ultimate reasonable suspicion and probable cause after a nonwarrant search.

**Current rule:** Appellate courts independently decide whether established circumstances, objectively considered in their totality, supply reasonable suspicion for the stop and probable cause for the nonwarrant search. Ultimate sufficiency needs no clear-error finding. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review. Grounded contextual inferences of resident judges and experienced local officers receive genuine due weight, without controlling ultimate constitutional sufficiency. Experience does not establish an unproved observation, and no police-credibility presumption, numerical weight or intermediate standard follows.

**Authority by component:**

**Present operation effective:** Ornelas, 1996-05-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Gates’s substantial-basis review of an issuing magistrate remains distinct. Not every mixed question is independently reviewed. The rule creates no new hearing, remedy, preservation exception or immediate conditional-plea withdrawal right.

**Holdings navigation:** Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690.

### Discriminatory routing, charges and linked subsidies in interstate commerce

**Governing question:** When a state or local economic measure must satisfy the discrimination rule rather than ordinary incidental-burden balancing.

**Current rule:** A measure discriminates when it favors local economic interests by burdening interstate competitors, including a waste charge expressly keyed to out-of-state origin, compulsory routing that reserves a processing service to a designated local private operator, or an integrated assessment-and-distribution scheme that collects from the whole market but returns the proceeds only to local producers. The entire linked operation matters; formally equal collection does not cleanse its discriminatory distribution. The government must show a legitimate local purpose that cannot adequately be served by reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives. Ordinary incidental-burden balancing does not validate discrimination. Conservation, sanitation, cost recovery and financing a favored private facility do not themselves meet that burden; contracting with a private operator does not make compulsory market regulation governmental market participation. A municipality that actually purchases collection and disposal may specify the destination as part of that purchased performance, including through a paid hauler and privately owned facility. Separate suppliers or prices do not themselves turn purchased disposal into unrelated downstream business. This bounded purchasing authority does not reach independent transactions merely because government has a contract or financial stake. In comparing regulated distribution businesses with independent commodity sellers, actual compulsory rate, reliability and public-service obligations may distinguish the offerings. Commodity overlap alone does not establish equivalence where a separate sales category free of those obligations has not been shown. Neither a utility label nor equal taxation of local outsiders suffices by itself; no utility immunity or new public-service exception to established discrimination follows.

### Effective correction of sex-based educational exclusion

**Governing question:** The adequacy of a proposed alternative remedy for an unconstitutional exclusion from a public educational opportunity.

**Current rule:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, considering material tangible and intangible benefits. A shared general purpose or equal annual public subsidy alone does not establish adequacy. Government must promptly eliminate the denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, retaining initial responsibility for lawful design under judicial supervision. Continued provision of the same public opportunity without an adequate lawful alternative requires consideration of qualified applicants without the unconstitutional categorical sex bar; promises of improvement do not permit indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Authority by component:**

- Substantive remedial adequacy and lawful government choice: Part II — Adequacy of the proposed alternative and Part III — Effective relief and lawful State choice, 1996-06-26; O’Connor, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven); Thomas did not participate.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Virginia / Virginia v. United States, 1996-06-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** Legitimate qualifications, privacy, physical training and operational accommodations remain available. No identical campus, numerical resource ratio, expenditure minimum, guaranteed career outcome, quota or categorical prohibition of all separate education is prescribed. Admission scrutiny remains demanding intermediate review: a genuine important objective and substantial relationship, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means; neither litigation timing alone nor lack of an original written rationale establishes pretext.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, 518 U.S. 515 (1996).


### Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability

**Governing question:** Who decides agreement to arbitrate arbitrability and how appellate courts review that determination.

**Current rule:** Courts independently decide agreement to arbitrate arbitrability unless ordinary applicable contract law establishes clear and unmistakable delegation. Appearing to object to a panel’s jurisdiction does not itself delegate that question, and a company’s submission does not establish an owner’s personal assent. Actual delegation brings the arbitrator’s arbitrability decision within limited award review. Appellate courts independently review district-court legal conclusions and review findings for clear error; arbitration adds no deference to the district court’s independent determination.
