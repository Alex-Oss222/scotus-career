## Relevant exact new rules and published disagreement

The following complete holdings are copied verbatim from the actual public deltas. They are reading copies with the original legal field, facts, authority and exceptions retained.

### Allentown — question scope and separate preservation

### The presented challenge reaches the standard actually applied

**Holding and operative rule:** A challenge expressly directed to the amount of evidence demanded before polling fairly includes whether the Board applied its announced reasonable-doubt standard to the order under review. That inquiry reaches the associated whole-record application here; it creates no general permission to add an independent factual question merely because merits briefs discuss it.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, supplies five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The petition challenges a requirement of so much evidence of lost support that a poll becomes meaningless. Allentown's contention that the agency substituted individual repudiations for reasonable uncertainty concerns that asserted evidentiary demand, not a separate judgment. Hubbard preserves the difference between supporting arguments and enlarged questions. Examining the actual standard and the material reports is necessary to resolve this challenge. The Court does not reopen the successor finding, the unit or independently unpreserved objections.

### The separate remedy objection remains outside review

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 10(e) bars an objection not urged before the Board unless extraordinary circumstances excuse the omission. The separate objection to the bargaining order's explanation and decertification bar was not preserved, and no such excuse is established; that objection is not reviewed.

**Authority:** Part V has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in leaving the objection unreviewed.

**Controlling explanation:** Auciello preserves §10(e)'s distinction between the particular objection presented to the Board and another objection later asserted in court. The preserved threshold and application challenges do not themselves preserve this separate remedial attack. Correcting the predicate of the existing order resolves enforcement without deciding the barred objection or approving every possible bargaining remedy.

### Allentown — complete relevant Breyer separate ground

The following public disagreement is noncontrolling. Its question-scope objection is independent of its alternative substantial-evidence defense; neither is a present position in any later case.

**Breyer, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in Parts I–II and V and dissenting from Parts III–IV and the judgment.** The granted rule challenge does not require an independent factual-sufficiency decision. If that issue is reached, Universal Camera requires respecting a supported agency choice between competing inferences. Objective doubt operates in the labor setting, where interviews, ambiguity about dissatisfaction versus opposition, incomplete sources for group reports, departmental coverage and the retained workforce affect evidentiary force. The ALJ gave actual reasons and expressly assessed Mohr together with the other reports; the adopted assessment need not be recast as a hidden majority-count requirement. Mohr's union role and continuity give his account weight, but do not require the conclusion management urges. These Justices accept that the agency must obey its governing legal and proof standards and reject a categorical bar to circumstantial evidence; they disagree that the actual application crossed those limits. They would affirm the enforcement judgment and order no further agency proceeding.

### Yankton — continuing controversy

### The existing declaration presents a continuing concrete controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** The EPA's waiver of a particular liner requirement does not moot the State's challenge where the operative declaration continues to determine the territorial legal regime governing its issued permit for this identified site. That continuing legal relationship permits review; anticipated similar projects or a wish to erase unfavorable precedent alone would not.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies direct authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Lee requires concrete personal application throughout review and distinguishes limited jurisdictional correction from power to decide the merits. This case concerns an actual permitted facility and an extant declaration classifying its land, rather than a chain of future requests or possible enforcement. Reversing that declaration changes the parties' current legal relationship. The EPA waiver permits clay-lined construction but does not vacate the territorial determination. The Court adopts no rule that an adverse declaration always preserves jurisdiction after every underlying consequence disappears. Nor does jurisdiction to review this declaration reopen the Tribe's separate, unappealed regulatory and injunction ruling.

## Other public positions and limits reviewed

Allentown's separate Stone-Zsela opinion, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, would invalidate the unitary polling prerequisite while retaining safeguards, conclusive periods, the withdrawal standard and lawful prospective policy; it also joins the controlling application and mandate. That statutory polling objection supplies no different Rule 50, Fourth Amendment, affiliation or Privacy Act rule. Breyer/Stevens/Souter/Ginsburg's full relevant scope/evidence ground is reproduced above; all accept fidelity to actual legal and proof standards, without treating every agency application as concealed rulemaking.

Brogan's narrow no-exception proposition has seven supporters; six join its principal statutory/privilege framework, five its III-B(1) limited abuse response, and only four III-B(2)'s further elaboration argument. Ginsburg, joined by Souter, agrees in coverage while identifying crime-generation risks, distinct fault reservations and congressional responsibility; Souter withholds all of III-B but joins the rest. Stevens, joined by Breyer, proposes a bounded statutory exception for a reactive oral denial during unsolicited criminal questioning, without a constitutional right to lie or a binding charging-policy defense. Those full grounds were reviewed in the linked source. They create no finding of abuse, falsehood, new preserved issue or analogous exception in these later cases, and the four-vote policy response is not binding law.

Oubre's seven-Justice rule preserves express §626(f) conditions and burden while reserving independent monetary relief. Breyer, joined by O'Connor, adds a noncontrolling employee-election/voidability explanation. Scalia would require tender but rejects ratification because learning of a missing statutory safeguard does not cure it; Thomas separately would uphold knowing ratification and preserve tender. Their different common-law grounds were reviewed and are not merged into a common dissent or a universal statutory-waiver doctrine. No later case here presents this ADEA waiver. Yankton is unanimous in all three bounded holdings and has no separate writing; neither its treaty canon nor its ceded-parcel rule migrates to another statute or constitutional field.
