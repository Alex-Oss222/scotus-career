## Standards and Tests selection: Targeted thermal examination of concealed home activity

### Targeted thermal examination of concealed home activity

**Governing question:** When technological reception of a closed home’s heat patterns is a search.

**Current rule:** A targeted technological examination is a Fourth Amendment search when its reported heat-pattern information reveals otherwise unobservable information about concealed domestic operation. External reception and lack of an image identifying a person or object do not remove that protection. Such a search requires a warrant absent a recognized exception; a later physical-entry warrant does not retroactively authorize it. Investigative purpose or equipment use alone is insufficient.

**Authority by component:**

- I. The targeted examination of this closed home's heat patterns is a search: I. The targeted examination of this closed home's heat patterns is a search, 1995-05-08. Part I of Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg: seven Justices. This directly controlling application concerns the covered dwelling and demonstrated differential-heat output. Stone-Zsela also states a broader formulation in his separate writing; the other six do not join that formulation merely by joining this application.
- II. No warrant exception justifies this scan on the established record: II. No warrant exception justifies this scan on the established record, 1995-05-08. Part II of Scalia's opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg: six Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join Part II and expresses no view on its additional proposition. Kennedy and Breyer do not reach justification because they find no search.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-05-08; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding adopts no general rule for every sensor or exterior emission and depends on no finding about public availability, flight altitude or frequency. Search and warrant justification remain distinct from exclusion, subsequent warrant validity and preservation; correction of a no-search ruling revives no forfeited affidavit challenge or automatic suppression remedy.

**Holdings navigation:** I. The targeted examination of this closed home's heat patterns is a search; II. No warrant exception justifies this scan on the established record.

## Standards and Tests selection: Announcement before dwelling entry

### Announcement before dwelling entry

**Governing question:** The role of knock-and-announce in Fourth Amendment reasonableness.

**Current rule:** Common-law announcement of identity and authority forms part of the reasonableness inquiry when officers enter a dwelling. Supported countervailing circumstances, including danger, futility, escape or threatened evidence destruction, may justify entry without announcement. Justification depends on the actual circumstances; the rule is not an inflexible requirement and does not itself decide exclusion or another remedy. A felony-drug category or valid warrant alone cannot justify unannounced force. Particular facts and rational inferences known when the challenged force begins must support reasonable suspicion that announcement would be dangerous or futile, or impede investigation, including evidence destruction. This requires more than a hunch but less than probable cause. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection; grounded experience receives weight, while ultimate constitutional sufficiency is reviewed independently.

**Authority by component:**

- Announcement and reasonable home entry: Announcement and reasonable home entry, 1995-05-22. Thomas's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

- Richards v. Wisconsin — III. Historical findings and constitutional application have different review roles, 1997-04-28; Breyer with Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven

**Present operation effective:** Richards, 1997-04-28, adding the particularized reasonable-suspicion and review rules.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

### Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

**Governing question:** Appellate review of ultimate reasonable suspicion and probable cause after a nonwarrant search.

**Current rule:** Appellate courts independently decide whether established circumstances, objectively considered in their totality, supply reasonable suspicion for the stop and probable cause for the nonwarrant search. Ultimate sufficiency needs no clear-error finding. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review. Grounded contextual inferences of resident judges and experienced local officers receive genuine due weight, without controlling ultimate constitutional sufficiency. Experience does not establish an unproved observation, and no police-credibility presumption, numerical weight or intermediate standard follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Review allocation, objective totality and due weight: II. Ultimate constitutional sufficiency is independently reviewed, with factual protection and genuine due weight, 1996-05-28; O’Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Ornelas, 1996-05-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Gates’s substantial-basis review of an issuing magistrate remains distinct. Not every mixed question is independently reviewed. The rule creates no new hearing, remedy, preservation exception or immediate conditional-plea withdrawal right.

**Holdings navigation:** Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690.

#### United States v. Pinson

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-164.
**Decided:** May 8, 1995.

##### The targeted examination of this closed home's heat patterns is a search

**Holding:** A targeted technological examination of a closed home constitutes a Fourth Amendment search when, as here, its reported heat-pattern information reveals otherwise unobservable information concerning concealed domestic operation. External reception and the absence of an image identifying a person or object do not defeat protection; investigative purpose or use of equipment alone is insufficient, and the Court adopts no general rule governing every sensor or exterior emission.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Katz v. United States, 389 U.S. 347: applied; privacy protection does not require physical penetration of the home.
- United States v. Karo, 468 U.S. 705, Part III: applied to the reported concealed domestic information; its protection does not depend on the information arriving as an ordinary visual image.
- Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321: applied by analogy to the additional examination of concealed information and its rejection of an unimportant-information exemption; no physical manipulation is found here.
- Florida v. Riley, 488 U.S. 445: distinguished as naked-eye observation through actual openings; its opinions supply none of this record's missing altitude or public-flight facts.
- California v. Greenwood, 486 U.S. 35: distinguished; purposeful placement of garbage for collection does not establish surrender of domestic information conveyed by heat.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696: distinguished; the narrow contraband-information character of the sniff is absent from this heat examination.
- Dow Chemical Co. v. United States, 476 U.S. 227: distinguished; the outdoor industrial photographs do not authorize this examination of a closed dwelling.
- Minnesota v. Dickerson, decided June 7, 1993: consistent with the distinction between information obtained through authorized observation and additional unauthorized probing; no tactile search is presented.

##### No warrant exception justifies this scan on the established record

**Holding:** This search of protected home information required a warrant unless a recognized exception applied. The July 25 scan had no warrant, and no consent, exigency, or other recognized exception is established; the subsequent warrant for physical entry does not retroactively authorize the scan.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: six Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Katz v. United States, 389 U.S. 347: applied; protected surveillance is subject to the warrant requirement and its established exceptions.
- United States v. Karo, 468 U.S. 705, Part III: applied; the warrant presumption for concealed home information retains recognized exceptions, including true exigency, none established here.

##### Correct the preserved search ruling without reopening unpresented warrant questions

**Holding:** The Eighth Circuit's judgment is vacated insofar as its rejection of the preserved suppression claim and affirmance of the conviction rest on the categorical no-search ground, and the case is remanded for lawful disposition of that claim under the corrected search rule. Pinson's failure to challenge affidavit sufficiency on appeal remains operative: the mandate directs no new excision-and-probable-cause hearing, revives no waived or forfeited argument automatically, and permits further warrant, suppression, or reliance questions only to the extent properly available under the existing record and ordinary preservation and remedial law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Karo, 468 U.S. 705, Part IV: retained as the distinct possibility of sufficient untainted warrant information; no determination of Pinson's affidavit or entitlement to a new hearing follows.
- United States v. Olano, decided April 26, 1993: retained; intentional waiver differs from forfeiture. If Rule 52(b) is actually invoked, error, plainness, ordinarily outcome-affecting prejudice, and the separate discretionary inquiry concerning fairness, integrity, or public reputation of judicial proceedings must be satisfied as applicable. This mandate does not establish any predicate or revive an argument automatically.
- United States v. Leon, 468 U.S. 897: left available only if procedurally open and factually supported. Objective reliance affords no protection for knowing or reckless deception of the magistrate, abandonment of judicial neutrality, an affidavit so lacking probable-cause indicia that reliance is wholly unreasonable, or facial warrant deficiency precluding reasonable reliance. The inquiry includes information-obtaining and supplying officers and assumes execution within the warrant's scope; no final reliance finding is made.
- Arizona v. Evans, decided March 1, 1995: confined to qualifying non-law-enforcement court-employee clerical error plus objectively reasonable reliance. Reserved police or mixed institutional errors supply no technology exception for this deliberate scan, and no final exclusion ruling follows here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the challenged suppression ruling and conviction affirmance only insofar as they rest on the no-search ground, and remand for lawful disposition of the preserved claim. The appellate failure to challenge affidavit sufficiency remains operative. No new excision hearing, automatic suppression, acquittal, release, trial or replacement sentence is ordered; the separately affirmed lesser-harms sentencing ruling receives no vacatur.

#### United States v. Myers

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit 94-1912; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied
**Decided:** June 11, 1996

##### Is targeted thermal acquisition of concealed domestic operation a search?

**Holding:** A targeted technological examination of a closed home's heat patterns is a search when it reveals otherwise unobservable information about concealed domestic operation. Passive external reception and absence of a recognizable interior image do not defeat protection; equipment use or investigative purpose alone does not establish a search.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven.

**Material application:** The Agema 210 pattern revealed operation of indoor cultivation lights not revealed by ordinary outside observation. Roof vents, heat emissions and lack of curbside garbage did not establish surrender of the underlying domestic information.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No rule governing every instrument, emission, visible smoke observation, market availability or future technological capability is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pinson, May 8, 1995, is applied without change. Katz protects concealed domestic information, and Karo confirms technological acquisition can occur without physical entry. Greenwood's discarded garbage and Place's contraband-confined detection are distinguished.

##### Can a later entry warrant authorize the earlier thermal search?

**Holding:** The scan required a warrant unless a recognized exception applied; none was established, and the later entry warrant could not retroactively authorize it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; six. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pinson's warrant requirement controls. Whren requires authority for the particular intrusion; its ordinary traffic-stop rule supplies none for this home scan.

##### What follows for the preserved derivative-warrant suppression claim?

**Holding:** The judgment rejecting a preserved suppression claim on the erroneous no-search ground must be vacated so the actual claim can be decided under the proper search rule and independently supported, procedurally available remedial grounds. Remand neither presumes exclusion nor creates a new defense or revives a waived claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III, the same seven as Part I.

**Material application:** Myers preserved the contention that the later warrant depended on the scan; Pinson's different affidavit nonchallenge does not defeat that reservation. The record establishes no final untainted sufficiency, objective reliance, independent source or inevitable discovery finding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Leon remains available only on supported facts and proper presentation; it does not protect knowing or reckless deception, an abandoned neutral judicial role, an affidavit wholly lacking reasonable probable-cause indicia, or a facially deficient warrant precluding reasonable reliance. Its inquiry includes obtaining and information-supplying officers and assumes execution within scope. Evans's qualifying non-law-enforcement court-clerical error does not excuse a deliberate police scan. New genuinely forfeited issues remain subject to Olano's error, plainness, substantial-rights and separate discretionary fairness/integrity/public-reputation requirements; intentional waiver extinguishes the error. Those requirements do not attach to the preserved claim. Rule 11(a)(2) permits plea withdrawal upon prevailing on appeal; this limited correction does not itself order it.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Partial vacatur and remand, 7–2, of the suppression ruling and dependent conviction affirmance. No final admission or exclusion, acquittal, release, retrial, new sentence, mandatory hearing, discovery or automatic affidavit-excision proceeding is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pinson's separate remedy inquiry and Karo's possible untainted-warrant inquiry remain. Leon, Evans and Olano retain their actual predicates; Ornelas's legal/factual allocation neither insulates categorical legal error nor changes review of an issuing magistrate's warrant determination.

#### Wilson v. Arkansas

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-5707.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Announcement and reasonable home entry

**Holding:** Common-law announcement of identity and authority forms part of Fourth Amendment reasonableness when officers enter a dwelling. Supported countervailing circumstances, including danger, futility, escape or threatened destruction of evidence, may justify unannounced entry; actual justification and remedy remain for the state court.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The exact quantum of suspicion required for each exception and whether every violation requires suppression remain undecided. The federal entry statutes' every detail is not constitutionalized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miller v. United States, 357 U.S. 301: supplies common-law announcement background; federal statutory particulars are not all constitutionalized.
- Sabbath v. United States, 391 U.S. 585: supplies federal statutory entry analysis without deciding this constitutional application.
- Ker v. California, 374 U.S. 23: applies state-entry reasonableness with circumstance-dependent excuses.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for the state court to evaluate the actual entry, record-supported excuses and remedy. No violation, automatic suppression or final vacation of the conviction is established.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Richards v. Wisconsin, April 28, 1997, resolves the reserved quantum for the identified warrant-execution exceptions as particularized reasonable suspicion and rejects the categorical felony-drug exception; constitutional exclusion after a violation remains open.

#### Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690

**Docket or dockets:** 95-5257; Seventh Circuit Nos. 94-3349 and 94-3350
**Decided:** May 28, 1996

##### Which suppression questions remain preserved for appellate review through these conditional pleas?

**Holding:** Review encompasses both ultimate reasonable suspicion for the initial stop and ultimate probable cause for the warrantless search, as preserved in these conditional pleas. Agreement between the parties about a proposed review standard neither determines that legal standard nor expands review to unpreserved discovery or suppression-merits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 11(a)(2) permits a written reservation of specified adverse pretrial determinations with the court's approval and the Government's consent. The selected orders establish preservation of both thresholds here; incompleteness of other documents does not establish litigant default. The Court reviews the allocation of appellate responsibility, not a newly presented database-discovery question or the ultimate suppression outcome.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Success in obtaining the proper review standard does not itself establish a right to withdraw the pleas. The ordinary requirement of prevailing on the reserved issue remains.

##### How must appellate courts review reasonable suspicion and probable cause for a warrantless search?

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently determine the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of reasonable suspicion for a stop and probable cause for a warrantless search under the objective totality of the circumstances. Historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, while grounded contextual inferences drawn by resident judges and experienced officers receive genuine due weight without deference to the ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts II-III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice majority. Scalia dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Independent review develops workable constitutional standards and permits comparison of recurring circumstances. The reviewing court must nevertheless respect what occurred, the trial court's credibility role, local context and grounded police experience. Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane support allocating mixed questions by function rather than a single label; First Options preserves separate legal and factual review. Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White supply the substantive totality inquiry but do not decide who finally applies it on appeal. Gates's substantial-basis review of a magistrate's warrant decision concerns a different institutional task. Mere acknowledgment of an officer's inference is insufficient due weight, but experience cannot manufacture an unproved fact or entitle the officer's ultimate conclusion to a presumption of correctness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** There is no numerical weighting formula, intermediate standard, new evidentiary fact or police-correctness presumption. Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain deference for their task-specific discretionary determinations; Salve Regina's treatment of legal review remains. The Court imports neither habeas burdens from Thompson nor a general solution to every mixed question; Markman's reserved appellate issue remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane supply functional allocation of legal and factual review; Thompson's habeas setting does not transfer its burdens here.
- First Options preserves independent legal review and protection of historical findings.
- Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White retain objective totality-of-circumstances substantive standards.
- Gates's warrant-issuance substantial-basis rule is distinguished from independent review of a warrantless search.
- Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain their task-specific deference; Salve Regina supports independent legal review.
- Markman does not decide the reserved appellate-review question by analogy alone.

##### What follows for these two appeals and the district court's suppression findings?

**Holding:** Both appellate judgments must be vacated and reconsidered under this allocation, without automatically ordering suppression or reopening the evidentiary record. Under section 636(b)(1)(B), the district judge independently determines specifically objected-to portions of the magistrate's recommendation and may receive evidence or recommit; that duty does not invariably require a new hearing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part III, joined by the same eight-Justice majority.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court accepted the credited loose-panel finding; rust was rejected, scratches were inconclusive, and actual NADDIS identification was unproved. Independent legal review cannot improve those facts by invoking experience. Raddatz permits the district judge's specified independent determination without an invariably repeated hearing; its concern about rejecting credibility findings is not triggered by the acceptance here. Section 636(b)(1)(A) does not set the ultimate suppression standard or the appellate allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes the circuit's limited-consent premise: consent did not authorize dismantling the panel. It adopts no general consent rule and does not revive waived inevitable discovery. The allocation also governs authorized Government suppression appeals, but section 3731's actual conditions remain: the statutory restriction after jeopardy attaches and before verdict, required certification of no delay and substantial material proof, the thirty-day and diligence requirements, and double-jeopardy limits. No independent Government appeal is authorized here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Seventh Circuit judgments, reported at 16 F.3d 714 and 52 F.3d 328, are vacated and remanded, 8-1. The Court orders reconsideration of both constitutional thresholds, not suppression, plea withdrawal, release, a new evidentiary hearing or a particular ultimate result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Raddatz preserves the distinction between a required independent judicial determination and an invariably new hearing; its reserved credibility-rejection question remains untriggered.

#### Richards v. Wisconsin

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-5955.
**Decided:** 1997-04-28.

##### The felony-drug category cannot replace the required execution inquiry

**Holding:** The Fourth Amendment does not permit officers to dispense with announcement solely because a warrant seeks evidence of felonious drug delivery or possession with intent to deliver. A valid search warrant authorizes the search but does not itself establish that an unannounced forcible entry is reasonable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts I–II and IV, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wilson constitutionalizes announcement subject to supported exceptions. Buie's adjoining-space sweep and Maryland v. Wilson's passenger-exit rule retain their specific justifications; the decision does not invalidate every categorical Fourth Amendment rule.

##### Reasonable suspicion governs a supported execution exception

**Holding:** Officers must identify particular circumstances and rational inferences supporting reasonable suspicion that knocking and announcing would be dangerous or futile, or would inhibit effective investigation, including by allowing destruction of evidence. The threshold is below probable cause but requires more than the offense label or an unsupported hunch, and the relevant facts must be known when the challenged force begins.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A destruction-risk exception must connect announcement delay with threatened evidence loss; later discoveries cannot supply earlier justification. No warrantless-search authority, universal waiting time or general police-created-exigency rule follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wilson's reserved suspicion quantum is resolved for these execution exceptions through a practical Buie analogy, without transplanting every protective-sweep rule.

##### Historical findings and constitutional application have different review roles

**Holding:** In reviewing this execution under the reasonable-suspicion standard, the historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, with grounded experience given real weight; the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of those facts is independently assessed. That allocation permits review of the supported record without manufacturing new facts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela and Scalia do not join this review-method extension.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ornelas's functional fact/legal allocation is extended to this warrant-execution question; Robinette separates correcting a general rule from application and remedy. Long permits review of the interwoven federal/state ruling without displacing genuinely independent Wisconsin law.

##### The credited contemporaneous facts justified this entry

**Holding:** The credited look toward a nearby uniformed officer and surprised reaction, combined with the specifically sought disposable cocaine, supplied particularized reasonable suspicion of destruction risk before the first attempt to enter; the subsequent rapid closure and locking strengthened that justification for the later force. The entry was therefore reasonable despite the invalid categorical rule and the judge's advance refusal of no-knock authorization.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruse was not consent to forced entry; late announcement cannot cure earlier unlawful force. The Court decides no independent futility ground, exclusionary remedy after a violation, cross-petition objection or automatic independent-source/inevitable-discovery effect of a valid warrant.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the conviction and denial of suppression on particularized destruction risk. The original hearing suffices; no suppression, new hearing, plea vacatur or sentence change is directed.

## Surviving pre-divergence sources and precise functions

- Katz v. United States, 389 U.S. 347, 351, 361 (1967), and Rakas v. Illinois, 439 U.S. 128, 133–44 & n.12 (1978): personal reasonable privacy, not borrowed rights or a general property-tort rule. A legitimate relationship to premises must be distinguished from exposure of the particular information observed.
- Minnesota v. Olson, 495 U.S. 91, 96–100 & n.5 (1990), protects the overnight guest and separates a protected relationship from a technical property estate. The case does not expressly decide every shorter commercial visit. White’s majority included Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia and Kennedy; Kennedy separately qualified the exigency application as deference to the state court. [Primary opinion](https://static.case.law/us/495/html/0091-01.html).
- California v. Ciraolo, 476 U.S. 207, 213–15 (1986), permits naked-eye observation from public navigable airspace; Stevens and O’Connor joined. It does not establish that every police trespass is a public vantage. [Primary opinion](https://static.case.law/us/476/html/0207-01.html).
- Florida v. Riley, 488 U.S. 445 (1989): White’s plurality joined by Rehnquist, Scalia and Kennedy; O’Connor concurred in judgment, rejecting legality of flight alone as conclusive and examining regular public use. Stevens joined Brennan’s dissent; Blackmun separately addressed proof allocation. The competing writings bear on whether shared access is equivalent to ordinary public observation. [All primary writings](https://static.case.law/us/488/html/0445-01.html).
- United States v. Dunn, 480 U.S. 294, 301–05 (1987), evaluates curtilage by proximity, enclosure, use, and protection against observation in its actual setting; it does not settle every multiunit common yard. Scalia declined the “Third” paragraph of Part II concerning the officer’s inference about the barn’s uses, while joining the remainder. [Primary opinion](https://static.case.law/us/480/html/0294-01.html).
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573, 586 (1980), and Warden v. Hayden, 387 U.S. 294, 298–99 (1967): warrantless home entry requires consent or an applicable exception; actual urgency and contemporaneous information matter. No later-discovered weapon can retroactively supply earlier grounds.
- Murray v. United States, 487 U.S. 533, 542–44 (1988), Scalia’s majority: a later warrant is genuinely independent only if its pursuit was not prompted by the initial illegal entry and tainted information did not affect issuance. Stevens and O’Connor joined Marshall’s dissent; Stevens separately emphasized the factfinding/remand issue. Kennedy did not participate. [All primary writings](https://static.case.law/us/487/html/0533-01.html). Leon, 468 U.S. 897 (1984), and Karo, 468 U.S. 705, Parts III–IV (1984), retain the limitations stated in exact Pinson/Myers sections; the existence of a later warrant is not itself a suppression decision.

Neither O’Melveny & Myers, Ankenbrandt v. Richards, Richards v. Jefferson County nor Richardson v. McKnight performs legal work in the reviewed search/entry dispute. Their appearance in the supplied runtime was name-matching noise and is not preserved as governing authority. No later Supreme Court visitor, property-trespass, police-created-exigency or suppression doctrine is imported.
