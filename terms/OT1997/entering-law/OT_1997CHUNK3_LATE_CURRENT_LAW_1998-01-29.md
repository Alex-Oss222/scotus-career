### The trial motion did not preserve search immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 50 requires the trial motion to identify the judgment sought and its supporting legal and factual grounds; postverdict renewal does not ordinarily permit a new ground omitted from that motion. Functional presentation, including adequate explicit incorporation, can suffice without a particular label, but pleading qualified immunity and invoking it for the stop did not preserve the distinct search-immunity ground here; no demonstrated exceptional injustice requires reaching that ground.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies nine direct votes for this rule, its application and all stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 50(a)(2) gives the court and opposing party an opportunity to address the actual ground before submission. Rule 50(b) renews the legal questions raised by the trial motion. The official explanatory note permits explicit reference to earlier materials and argument; mistaken terminology alone is immaterial. The answer pleaded immunity, but a pleading is not itself the required trial motion.

Counsel expressly requested judgment on the stop on the merits or immunity, then separately argued that the search was lawful. The complete reported presentation identified no search-immunity ground or incorporation supplying it. Search legality and immunity for an objectively reasonable legal mistake are distinct, even when they may overlap. The Court adopts no requirement of separate titles or magic words and no prohibition on alternative defenses. Gomez treats immunity as an affirmative defense; Elder requires all relevant law on a preserved issue, without creating the missing motion ground.

The strongest contrary argument combines the answer and search-legality presentation as sufficient notice. The express claim-specific separation defeats that reading. No actual prejudice is independently found. Nor does immunity's importance itself establish exceptional injustice. The search record must be viewed favorably to McCardle; the adverse general stop verdict supplies no contrary universal factual findings. On that record, no probable-cause, consent, arrest, inventory or supported protective-search justification compelled judgment for Haddad. Long requires specific articulable grounds supporting danger and possible access to weapons within a protective scope; a valid stop alone is insufficient. The officer's denial of a search or subjective fear alone is not the constitutional test.

Those circumstances defeat the asserted injustice without deciding ultimate immunity or issuing a new search-merits holding. The Court leaves the outer limits of exceptional review open, adds no categorical instruction or pretrial-motion prerequisite, and infers no further default from unavailable original papers.
