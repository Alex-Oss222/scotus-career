### December 29, 1997 — Clanton v. Cooper

[Public Projection](../records/clanton_v_cooper_merits_1997-12-29.md#public-projection). Ginsburg's Part I and Part II-B are unanimous. Part II-A's complete knowing-or-reckless material-warrant-falsity rule has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela joins its deliberate-falsity application and subsidiary-theory limits. Part III's distinct confession remand has the eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela would affirm denial on that branch. Legal immunity rejection for the assumed deliberate warrant and later detention deception, dismissal of factual-appeal portions, and preservation of rejected theories and disposed claims are unanimous; no liability finding follows.

Johnson and Behrens permit interlocutory immunity review of separable legal defenses on properly assumed plaintiff-favorable historical facts, not participation, credibility, or factual sufficiency. Section 1254 does not enlarge the circuit's §1291 authority. Miller makes ultimate constitutional voluntariness distinct from historical facts; Ornelas supplies task-specific allocation rather than a universal mixed-question rule. Calling an entire coercion inquiry factual cannot exclude a real legal defense, and legal labels cannot secure factual review. The unavailable factual portions must be dismissed.

Franks and Malley supplied the pre-August-1993 rule against personally responsible knowing or reckless falsehoods material to probable cause; judicial issuance does not categorically insulate the officer. Culpable falsity, materiality, and responsibility remain necessary: a corrected truthful account may establish probable cause, and later recantation alone does not prove fabrication. The Court does not find affidavit contents or treat Bobby's derivative statement as independent probable cause. Sufficiently reliable hearsay remains usable; filed charges are not universally necessary before a warrant; and the federal grand-jury requirement does not govern state prosecutions. These subsidiary limits supply neither universal accomplice reliability nor federal liability for every state-law defect.

The same material-deception law obviously applies to the assumed personal communication of deliberate homicide and no-bond falsehoods actually causing otherwise unjustified additional unreasonable physical detention. A different custodian and electronic transmission require no identical NCIC precedent. The original arrest and later message's asserted detention extension remain distinct. The $5,000 Oklahoma bond establishes no federal release entitlement; Texas bond-setting authority establishes no inevitable independent detention. Falsehood, culpability, materiality, attributable cause, and an actual unreasonable seizure must be proved. Inaccuracy, bad motive, a state-law breach, or one-to-three days alone is insufficient. The same-lawful-detention and no-causal-role defenses survive, and no universal duration of Fourth Amendment coverage is decided.

Part III requires legal review of the distinct involuntary-third-party-statement theory on assumed historical facts, identifying Clanton's own protected interest, the particular use, legal voluntariness, and clear establishment at the relevant 1993 conduct date. The lower court discussed her own interest and notice; its error was the overbroad factual characterization, not complete omission. A statement involuntary but not knowingly false presents a distinct theory. Michael's personal self-incrimination privilege is not borrowed, but that does not categorically exclude Clanton's independent liberty injury. Albright supplies no controlling synthesis, universal Fourth Amendment displacement, or general state-remedy bar.

Jackson, Blackburn, Spano, Haynes, and Fulminante supply voluntariness background; Frazier makes an evidence lie nonconclusive, and Hutto does not make every promised benefit coercive. Threats, leniency, suggested narrative, and asserted physical evidence require the applicable totality inquiry. LaFrance, Merkt, Chiavola, Bradford, and relevant circuit law must be evaluated for their actual reach; third-party trial-use holdings do not automatically establish a particular August 1993 pretrial-detention damages right. Obvious coverage needs no identical case, but an abstract liberty principle or bad intent cannot establish an unsettled extension. Ultimate coercion, the independent due-process right, notice, and immunity on that branch remain open.

Harlow/Anderson remains the objective, particularized conduct-date defense. Elder requires all relevant pre-existing law; Lanier supplies a current clarity analogy without retroactive notice or merger of civil and criminal elements. No mandatory merits-first sequence precludes a dispositive notice ground. Crawford-El and Jeffries preserve ordinary competent proof, actual elements, personal responsibility, and causation without a heightened motive burden; their retaliation burden allocation is not universal fabrication law. Fletcher's function distinction supplies neither prosecutorial immunity to Cooper nor 1993 notice. The eight-Associate opinion adopts these limits; Stone-Zsela joins their deliberate-deception application and proposes a noncontrolling replacement defense below.

The existing district immunity denial remains subject to lawful claim-specific review. No new contextual defense, burden shift, general fabrication or pretrial due-process right, derivative Fifth Amendment right, automatic discovery, trial, hearing, or damages follows. The lower opinion supplies assumed facts; original affidavit, full transcript, pleadings, depositions, messages, and later state voluntariness ruling are not reproduced. Its release-year discrepancy and one-versus-three-day ambiguity supply no exact date or amount. Michael's plea and later ruling do not bind Clanton absent established preclusion predicates, and missing materials prove no litigant default.


### January 5, 1998 — Valot v. Southeast Local School District Board of Education

[Public Projection](../records/valot_v_southeast_local_school_district_board_of_education_merits_1998-01-05.md#public-projection). Souter's Parts I and IV have Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer; Part II has all eight Associate Justices, with Stone-Zsela agreeing in its judgments; Part III has Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The count-1 categorical rejection is reversed and corresponding defense summary judgment vacated, 6–3. Count 2's protected administrative invocation, count 3's protected-right penalty branch, and count 5's state-claim nonretention return, 5–4. Rejection of count 3's independent economic equality theory and count 4's substantive due process is affirmed, 9–0. No procedural-due-process or renewal-property claim is revived.

A public employer may not deny continued employment solely because an employee lawfully and non-shamly uses this established governmental unemployment-benefit mechanism and imposes its prescribed benefit cost; public-concern content is unnecessary for this particular invocation. Perry separates employment entitlement from retaliation protection; California Motor Transport protects governmental redress, while Smith v. Arkansas State Highway Employees distinguishes protection from retaliation from an obligation to accept demands. Connick, Pickering, and Rankin retain employee-speech rules, but this external formal proceeding is not merely an informal workplace complaint. Neither every personal request or internal grievance nor a universal success condition is decided.

The Board admitted that applications and receipts caused nonrenewal. Its dollar-for-dollar cost was real but was the lawful expense of the very invocation punished; no separately established disruption or fraud justifies that action. Later reasonable-assurance practices prove neither summer 1993 assurance nor ineligibility. Eligibility contests, rejection of fraudulent or ineligible claims, neutral filing conditions, lawful prospective changes, and independent job-related decisions remain available. The program is not frozen and operational costs are not universally irrelevant.

Part II holds that known benefit applications rationally indicate possible recurring fiscal expense when considered independently of a protected-right penalty. Beach, Heller, and Romer retain their actual ordinary-equality limits; that conceivable fiscal basis establishes neither a valid rights penalty nor a Mt. Healthy same-decision defense. Collins does not convert voluntary employment or state-law expectations into a distinct fundamental renewal right or substantive deprivation. Annual contracts and an anticipated recommendation guaranteed no renewal; plaintiffs' express substantive-only limitation leaves procedural due process outside the case. Albright remains fractured, without a universal specific-source or state-remedy bar.

Part III returns count 2 because successful receipt does not foreclose retaliation for using the process; it creates no freestanding general access entitlement. The rights-dependent equality branch returns on the corrected protection premise without automatic strict scrutiny or independent liability. The Ohio claim's nonretention must be reconsidered because federal claims survive. Section 1367(c)(3) permits rather than compels declining after all original claims are dismissed and does not eliminate supplemental power; Chicago's power/discretion distinction governs. The state merits, compulsory retention, and other supported grounds for nonretention remain open.

Parts I and IV require ordinary proof of actual causation, personal responsibility, municipal attribution, injury, immunity, and relief. The Board's reason establishes neither each individual's participation nor all remedial predicates; the superintendent's favorable recommendation is not an adverse vote. Under Mt. Healthy, Umbehr, O'Hare, and Crawford-El, protected conduct must play a substantial or motivating role, and an actual same-decision defense requires a preponderance, not a conceivable lawful reason. Harlow/Anderson and Elder preserve particularized conduct-date notice and a dispositive defense without mandatory merits-first sequencing. Later San Filippo, Umbehr, O'Hare, Crawford-El, Jeffries, Clanton, and this decision cannot establish notice for the August 16, 1993 refusal; San Filippo is persuasive 1994 circuit authority, not Supreme Court law.

No immediate partial liability judgment, ultimate immunity disposition, reinstatement, backpay, damages, repayment, mandatory hearing, trial, or discovery follows. A denial resting solely on the corrected categorical ground remains subject to remand. Published lower-court recitations support the Board admission and claim limits; original pleadings, agency files, minutes, and briefs are not reproduced and establish no additional participation, policymaking predicate, concession, or default.


### January 12, 1998 — Smith v. Mattox

[Public Projection](../records/smith_v_mattox_merits_1998-01-12.md#public-projection). Scalia's Parts I–II are unanimous. Part III's complete retained-immunity framework has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela joins its disposition and ordinary-proceedings limits. All nine affirm the denial of qualified-immunity summary judgment on the presented legal question and proper assumed facts, without finding final constitutional liability.

Johnson, Behrens, and Clanton permit a separable legal immunity inquiry on plaintiff-favorable historical assumptions while barring selection among accounts, credibility decisions, or factual-sufficiency review. Section 1254 enlarges no circuit §1291 authority. Unlike Clanton's separately identified factual challenges, no factual-sufficiency component requiring dismissal is established here. The assumed account is accepted solely for this appeal.

Graham requires objectively reasonable arrest force from the officer's contemporaneous perspective, considering crime severity, immediate threat, and active resistance or flight. The earlier bat threat, refusal, flight, and feigned renewed flight justified caution and some restraint; submission neither established completed cuffing nor prohibited every further use of force. On the complete assumed account, however, Smith was wholly unresisting during the maneuver, complained of discomfort from an arm pull, and experienced a grunt and blow followed by multiple fractures requiring surgery. That considerable additional force was obviously outside the remaining latitude for reasonable error under pre-June 11, 1993 law. The whole account, rather than injury alone, supplies obvious unlawfulness; no factually identical handcuffing-injury case is needed. Continued resistance, gun-reaching, or an unassumed safety mistake cannot be inserted into it.

Harlow and Anderson require particularized objective notice, not merely an abstract bodily-security right. Graham supplies the preconduct rule; Elder and Lanier explain relevant-law review and obvious coverage without retrospective notice or transfer of criminal elements. No exhaustive absence-of-authority finding or exclusive factually-similar-case method is adopted. Subjective anger is neither assumed nor necessary; bad purpose, hindsight, or a fracture alone is not decisive. Later Kelly and Post formulations supply no earlier notice.

Harlow/Anderson remains governing immunity law. Mattox may prove a materially different supported account, including continued resistance or necessary force, and obtain lawful fact-dependent reconsideration. Elder imports no new claims or evidence. Crawford-El preserves ordinary competent proof and independent defenses without adding a motive element or retaliation's burden sequence to Graham. Unpleaded entry and probable-cause theories, disposed city and chief claims, unlawful-arrest damages, and a separate Fourteenth Amendment claim remain outside the decision. No final violation, causal finding, supervisory liability, damages, compulsory trial, hearing, discovery plan, special interrogatory, or scheduled return follows. Original pleadings and depositions are not reproduced; no anger, purpose to break the arm, completed cuffing, missing evidence, waiver, or litigant default is inferred.

#### Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights; No. 97-501. — 1998-05-04

[Record](../records/ricci_v_village_of_arlington_heights_merits_1998-05-04.md#public-projection)

##### Event

Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights, No. 97-501. Argued April 21, 1998; decided May 4, 1998. On review of the Seventh Circuit’s affirmance of summary judgment, 116 F.3d 288. The question concerns the reasonableness of automatic station-house custody for operating a business without the required license, accepting probable cause and state and local arrest authority.

Officers at Ricci’s telemarketing business asked about his license, and he acknowledged having none. Under Village policy they took him to the station, locked him in an interview room for approximately an hour while preparing enforcement documents, and released him on recognizance. His wife obtained the license, and the charge was dismissed. The civil licensing ordinance carried a $5–$500 fine for each offense, with each day a separate offense; no accumulated fine is adjudicated here.

The separate premises-search claim was settled, and dismissal of the no-probable-cause claim was not appealed. A distinct warrant-clause argument first raised at appellate oral argument was held waived; Ricci conceded an arrest under a warrant would have been reasonable. The preserved question concerns the objective extent of this custody, not a revived warrant, search or probable-cause claim.

##### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

##### Public Action

The Court reverses insofar as the judgment sustains summary judgment on the categorical probable-cause and local-authority premise, sets aside the adverse judgment on the preserved custody-reasonableness claim, and remands.

##### Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed in the stated respect and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

The adverse summary judgment on the preserved custody-reasonableness claim is set aside. The case returns for further proceedings under the additional-restraint rule. Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer leave the actual custody application for the lower courts; Stone-Zsela would decide that custody unreasonable and limit remand to remaining policy causation, defenses and relief. No application conclusion commands five votes, and no present damages or immunity judgment issues. The settled search claim and unappealed probable-cause dismissal remain undisturbed.

##### Holdings

###### Probable cause does not automatically justify additional station-house custody

**Holding and operative rule:** Probable cause and state or local authority to enforce a fine-only civil licensing ordinance do not, by themselves, establish that transporting an identified operator to a station and confining him is reasonable. The added restraint must reasonably serve the actual enforcement function; nonexclusive possible justifications include securing identification or appearance, ending an actual continuing violation, protecting persons or evidence, or necessary processing in the circumstances. The rule imposes neither least restrictive means nor a duty to issue a citation whenever one is conceivable.

**Authority:** Ginsburg’s Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer. Those five, including the author, directly adopt this rule and the targeted reversal; Stone-Zsela does not join Part II’s affirmative reservation of actual reasonableness.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an established violation supplies authority to intervene, but the permissible extent of the seizure remains a constitutional question. Whren validates an ordinary initial probable-cause traffic stop while preserving independent manner, scope and duration limits. Robinette separates initial authority from justification for later restraint without requiring an indispensable release script. Ayeni likewise confines an authorized intrusion to its lawful function; its home-search setting is not automatically transferred to this business case. Wilson makes relevant history part of reasonableness without constitutionalizing every state rule, and Soldal prevents a civil label from excluding Fourth Amendment protection.

The lower court’s categorical two-predicate premise cannot settle this added custody merely because municipal policy requires it. A policy cannot supply its own constitutional justification. The lower opinion did discuss the continuing operation, potential daily fines, processing documents, one-hour duration and recognizance release; those supported circumstances must not be ignored or described as missing. The governing inquiry concerns their actual relationship to the added restraint.

Fine-only status matters to the reasonable extent of custody but creates no categorical immunity from arrest. The identified examples are possibilities, not findings that a particular justification occurred here. Robinson and Gustafson address consequences of a lawful arrest and cannot establish the disputed antecedent lawfulness by assumption. Hidden dislike of the business is not the ground; Whren’s objective approach remains. The separate warrant theory and the settled or abandoned claims stay outside review. The reversal rejects the automatic formula and sets aside its adverse custody judgment without a five-Justice holding that this custody was unconstitutional.

**Precedent treatment:** Whren, Robinette and Ayeni’s execution limits are applied to this additional licensing restraint; Wilson and Soldal retain their separate historical and coverage functions; Robinson and Gustafson retain the lawful-arrest predicate.

###### No controlling conclusion resolves the actual custody’s reasonableness

**Judgment without controlling application rationale:** Five Justices support targeted reversal and remand, but only Stone-Zsela concludes the actual custody was unreasonable. Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer leave that application open; Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas find the supported short enforcement processing reasonable and would affirm.

**Authority:** Part I’s rule controls by direct majority agreement. Part II’s application reservation has four joins, Stone-Zsela’s contrary application has one, and the dissent’s favorable application has four. These positions cannot be combined into a majority holding that this custody was unlawful or lawful, and no Marks synthesis is adopted. The lower courts must proceed under the controlling rule without a Supreme Court finding of ultimate violation, liability or immunity.

##### Precedent Treatment

- **Whren v. United States:** Preserved for ordinary initial probable-cause stops and applied at its independent execution limit; it does not establish unlimited custody for every civil violation.
- **Ohio v. Robinette:** Its separate later-restraint inquiry informs the new application without a mandatory citation or warning script.
- **Ayeni v. Mottola:** Its purpose-bound execution principle informs the added-restraint inquiry; the home setting and separate immunity decision retain their limits.
- **Wilson v. Arkansas:** History remains relevant to reasonableness, and reversal for a deficient framework need not establish a completed violation. No state arrest rule is automatically constitutionalized.
- **Soldal v. Cook County:** Civil enforcement remains within Fourth Amendment coverage, while reasonableness and liability stay distinct; its summary-judgment remedy does not establish those predicates automatically.
- **United States v. Robinson; Gustafson v. Florida:** Their lawful-arrest premise is preserved; search consequences cannot establish antecedent arrest lawfulness by assumption.
- **County of Riverside v. McLaughlin:** Its timing rule for post-arrest probable-cause review does not itself authorize this custody.
- **United States v. Lanier:** Conduct-time legal clarity remains separate from a present constitutional ruling; no newly stated rule retroactively defeats personal immunity.

##### Law After Decision

Automatic station-house custody for this fine-only civil licensing offense requires an objective enforcement connection beyond probable cause and local permission alone. The rule permits reasonable practical enforcement judgments and supplies no universal citation requirement or categorical fine-only arrest prohibition. The adverse custody judgment is set aside, but no controlling holding determines whether this actual processing satisfies the rule. Municipal policy causation, individual responsibility, conduct-time immunity and relief retain their independent predicates.

##### Separate Writings

**Ginsburg, joined by the other members of the four-Justice Part II coalition, on the unresolved application.** Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer conclude that the categorical premise foreclosed the required assessment of added restraint even though the lower court discussed continuing operation, possible daily fines, processing and duration. They would evaluate those existing facts under the actual-enforcement rule rather than decide now that custody was unlawful. A favorable judgment for defendants remains legally possible after that inquiry. No new hearing is required simply because further facts might exist, and practical processing may suffice when reasonably connected to enforcement. Wilson and Soldal permit correction of a legal framework and adverse summary judgment without a present violation finding.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and in the judgment.** Probable cause identifies the licensing violation but does not answer whether taking this identified operator from his business served a sufficient enforcement purpose. The record identifies an immediately obtainable license and a fine-only regulatory obligation, without failure to identify himself, flight, danger or another reason for removal. Stone-Zsela finds the automatic policy’s custody objectively disconnected from securing compliance or appearance and would decide it unreasonable on this record. His ground is not hidden official hostility toward the fundraising business, which Whren does not make decisive. He would remand only for municipal policy causation, preserved defenses and relief. Individual immunity must use the legal notice available in 1994; this present clarification does not retroactively defeat it. He neither creates a rule for every minor offense nor revives the settled search or abandoned probable-cause claim, and he does not join leaving the constitutional application open.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, dissenting.** These Justices reject unlimited authority from local permission and probable cause but find the necessary enforcement connection established here. Ricci admitted an actual continuing violation; custody lasted about an hour while officers prepared the arrest sheet, local-ordinance complaint and bond receipt and arranged recognizance release. Those tasks concerned enforcement of the same offense, unlike an unrelated additional intrusion under Ayeni. Neither the civil label nor the availability of a possible on-site citation requires proof that citation was impossible before ordinary processing. Whren, Wilson and Robinette preserve review of atypical force, unjustified duration, unrelated later detention or a method lacking a reasonable enforcement relationship. None of those broader permissions is asserted as a categorical rule here. They would affirm this actual application without deciding individual immunity.

##### Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Seventh Circuit with adverse summary judgment on the preserved custody claim set aside. The controlling additional-restraint rule governs further proceedings; the Court supplies no majority determination of actual unreasonableness, damages, municipal causation, officer participation or immunity. No particular evidentiary hearing, new licensing policy or revival of other claims is ordered.
