## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

### Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

**Governing question:** How courts decide whether new legislation governs events preceding enactment.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress prescribed the statute’s temporal reach. If it did, that direction governs subject to independent constitutional limits. Otherwise ask whether applying the provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events by impairing existing rights, increasing liability for past conduct or imposing new duties concerning completed transactions. Such operation requires clear congressional intent. Pending litigation alone does not answer the question. Procedural, jurisdictional and prospective-relief labels are not substitutes for examining the provision’s actual operation. Legislative correction of a judicial construction, or a purpose to restore an earlier understanding, does not by itself establish retrospective reach; this legislative inquiry differs from the temporal operation of judicial decisions.

**Authority by component:**

- Sequential temporal inquiry: Landgraf v. USI Film Products — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.
- Corrective purpose and distinction from judicial interpretation: Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc — Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Present operation effective:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences; Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach.

**Exceptions and limits:** An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. Landgraf concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Related current rules:** Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments supplies Morgan Stanley's and Plaut's separate Article III rule for §27A(b).

## Standards and Tests selection: Convicting-jurisdiction law for the firearms conviction exclusion

### Convicting-jurisdiction law for the firearms conviction exclusion

**Governing question:** Which jurisdiction's law governs whether expungement, set-aside, pardon or restoration of civil rights excludes a conviction under 18 U.S.C. §921(a)(20) for the federal firearms prohibition.

**Current rule:** Apply the law of the jurisdiction where the proceedings occurred to the related conviction exclusion for expungement, set-aside, pardon or restoration of civil rights. State restoration alone does not remove a federal conviction as a §922(g) predicate. The enacted exclusion retains its qualification: a conviction is not excluded if the pardon, expungement or restoration of civil rights expressly provides that the person may not ship, transport, possess, or receive firearms. State administration of ordinary civil rights and difficulty obtaining federal relief do not supply a different sovereign selector or create a judicial restoration procedure.

**Authority by component:**

- Convicting-sovereign selector and retained-firearms qualification: Beecham and Jones — The law of the convicting jurisdiction governs the conviction exclusion, 1994-05-16. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; unanimous direct support.

**Present operation effective:** Beecham and Jones, May 16, 1994 (1994-05-16).

**Open question:** The decision does not establish the availability or satisfaction of a particular federal pardon or restoration mechanism, §925(c) relief, the required civil-rights bundle, automatic versus affirmative restoration, or federal habeas as a set-aside.

**Related current rules:** Collateral attacks on ACCA predicates at federal sentencing governs the distinct sentencing-forum question; it does not replace this conviction-selector rule.

**Holdings navigation:** The law of the convicting jurisdiction governs the conviction exclusion.

## Standards and Tests selection: Personal arms protection, incorporation and necessary acquisition

### Personal arms protection, incorporation and necessary acquisition

**Governing question:** Who may invoke the Second Amendment against federal and state action and what acquisition interest it protects.

**Current rule:** The Second Amendment protects an individual’s keeping and bearing of arms within its constitutional scope; formal militia enrollment, current service or preparation for organized military activity is not a prerequisite. Through the Fourteenth Amendment’s Due Process Clause, the same constitutional standards apply against the States. Cruikshank and Presser cease to control only insofar as they bar that application; Presser’s unauthorized armed-organization disposition remains. Acquisition necessary to constitutionally protected keeping falls within that protection, so a State cannot categorically exclude a claim because it obstructed purchase rather than seized an arm. Coverage still depends on the actual weapon and conduct and is distinct from the validity of a restriction. Military usefulness alone neither excludes an arm nor supplies immunity from manufacture, transfer, possession, taxation or registration controls. In particular, personal protection and military utility alone supply no exemption from NFA identification and registration requirements administering lawful making and transfer.

**Authority by component:**

- Personal holder, state incorporation and necessary acquisition: The arms guarantee is personally held and does not require formal militia enrollment; The Fourteenth Amendment applies the personal arms guarantee to the States; Necessary acquisition cannot be excluded merely because the State obstructed purchase, 1995-12-11. O’Connor Parts III-A–C with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (six).
- Military-utility boundary: The right is personally held, and military usefulness supplies no automatic immunity, 1996-02-12; O’Connor Part III-A, all nine.
- Separate NFA registration obligation: Military usefulness supplies no exemption from the separate registration duty, 1996-02-12; O’Connor Part IV, all Associates (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Hale, 1996-02-12, clarifying military utility and the separate registration obligation; Love’s incorporation and acquisition rules date from 1995-12-11.

**Open question:** No comprehensive weapons classification, scrutiny tier, closed regulatory list, universal defensive-purpose rule or final §922(o) invalidity is established. Purchase from every seller is not guaranteed; licensing and investigation are not categorically prohibited. State-law illegality alone does not establish infringement. Registration that cannot lawfully be approved remains a different unresolved application.

**Holdings navigation:** Love v. Pepersack; United States v. Hale, judgment below 978 F.2d 1016.

## Standards and Tests selection: Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

### Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

**Governing question:** Whether remedial silence or Spending Clause notice bars compensatory damages in the established Title IX private action.

**Current rule:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing relief does not itself create a new cause of action. Title IX's established private action permits compensatory damages for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation, and compensable loss. The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar that compensation for intentional breach of Title IX's express nondiscrimination condition.

**Authority by component:**

- Appropriate relief in an existing action and availability of Title IX compensation: Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools — Compensatory relief in the established Title IX action, February 26, 1992.
- Rejection of the categorical funding-notice bar: Franklin — Notice does not bar damages for the pleaded intentional violation, February 26, 1992. Both holdings rest on White's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; Scalia and Thomas supply judgment votes only on a narrower statutory ground.

- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District — III. Recipient compensation requires its own culpable response, 1997-05-27; five direct joins supply the teacher-harassment recipient rule

**Present operation effective:** Franklin, 1992-02-26, for the remedy-availability rule; Kinman, 1997-05-27, resolves the separate teacher-harassment recipient standard.

**Open question:** Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX supplies Kinman's controlling recipient standard. Recipient attribution outside that setting, punitive relief, unintended discriminatory effects and genuinely ambiguous funding conditions remain unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992) — Statutory discrimination remedies. Kinman v. Omaha Public School District (1997-05-27) — Civil Rights / Teacher harassment and institutional responsibility.

## Standards and Tests selection: Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX

### Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX

**Governing question:** Sex-based teacher harassment and compensatory responsibility of the funding recipient.

**Current rule:** Sexual subject matter or offense alone does not show discrimination because of sex. Same-sex harassment can constitute sex discrimination; the claim requires sex-based causation, unwelcome treatment, sufficient educational harm, recipient responsibility and causal injury. Participation or later continuation does not conclusively establish earlier welcome. The environment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to be objectively abusive and actually experienced as such, assessed in the educational context without mandatory grade-loss, psychiatric-injury or factor requirements. Recipient damages require actual notice to an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial risk and deliberate, unreasonable disregard of that known danger causing injury or continuation.

**Authority by component:**

- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District — I. The sex of the harasser does not exclude a Title IX claim, 1997-05-27; Stone-Zsela Part I, all nine
- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District — II. Participation does not establish welcome, and educational harm is contextual, 1997-05-27; the same nine
- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District — III. Recipient compensation requires its own culpable response, 1997-05-27; Stone-Zsela Part IIA with O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins independent of the remand coalition
- Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc. — The Title IX claim lacks discrimination because of sex, 1997-02-24; Ginsburg for all nine supplies this independently sufficient statutory element

**Present operation effective:** Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, February 24, 1997, states the sex-causation boundary; Kinman, May 27, 1997, adds same-sex coverage and the teacher-harassment recipient standard.

**Exceptions and limits:** Sexual behavior alone does not prove the elements; an opposite-sex comparator is not invariably necessary. Constructive notice, teacher status, ordinary authority-assisted access, a policy breach or the wrongdoer's own knowledge does not satisfy recipient responsibility. A reasonable response does not become unlawful simply because harm continues. No majority adopts Stone-Zsela's additional Part IIB awareness qualifications or the four-Justice agency/negligent-correction alternatives.

**Holdings navigation:** Kinman v. Omaha Public School District (1997-05-27) — Civil Rights / Teacher harassment and institutional responsibility; Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc. (1997-02-24) — The Title IX claim lacks discrimination because of sex.

## Standards and Tests selection: Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment

### Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment

**Governing question:** When Title VII attributes a supervisor-created hostile environment to the employer without higher-management notice.

**Current rule:** The plaintiff must prove by a preponderance that actual delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment. Hiring or firing authority, alter-ego rank, higher-management notice, an express threat, completed personnel action or tangible economic loss is not invariably required; rank, employment or proximity alone is insufficient. Personal purpose or breach of a harassment prohibition does not by itself negate actual aid. Ordinary scope of employment, employer negligence and apparent authority remain separate routes. Apparent authority requires a reasonable belief traceable to the employer's manifestations and reasonable reliance on that appearance, not merely the supervisor's asserted power; it is not an additional element of actual-authority attribution. Prohibitions and correction after notice do not themselves erase earlier authority-aided conduct.

**Authority by component:**

- Andrade v. Mayfair Management, Inc. — Employer responsibility through delegated authority, 1997-02-26; Breyer for all nine supplies the agency-attribution instruction; Meritor's no-automatic-imputation and no-universal-notice-defense limits remain
- Actual-power rule and absence of automatic management-notice requirement: Faragher, Faragher v. City of Boca Raton — I. Actual delegated authority can establish responsibility without higher-management notice, Breyer Part I, all nine, 1997-09-08; Harrison, Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc. — I. Material aid from actual delegated power does not require alter-ego rank, Stone-Zsela Part I, all nine, 1997-09-15.
- Independent apparent-authority mechanics and policy boundary: Harrison, Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc. — II. The charge prejudicially withheld the supported actual-authority route and Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc. — III. Policy and response do not cure the omitted route; a general complete defense remains open, all nine, 1997-09-15; Faragher, Faragher v. City of Boca Raton — III. This ineffective-policy contention does not defeat liability; a general defense remains open, eight Associates, 1997-09-08.

**Present operation effective:** Andrade, February 26, 1997, for actual-authority aid; Harrison, September 15, 1997, for the express proof burden and distinct apparent-authority formulation.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general responsibility for every supervisor's private act or new affirmative defense is established. The hostile-environment elements, attribution, evidentiary sufficiency and proper remedy remain distinct.

**Related current rules:** Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite states the substantive environment threshold, which does not itself establish attribution.

**Open question:** A complete defense based on genuinely effective protection and unreasonable nonuse, its elements and burden, relation to tangible employment action and any distinct remedial limitation remain undecided. No general reporting duty, policy immunity, statutory publication duty, negligence per se for failed dissemination or new burden shift follows. Discriminatory causation and the objective/subjective hostile-environment threshold remain separate from agency.

**Current wording authority:** Faragher, September 8, 1997, applies and clarifies the retained actual-aid rule without adopting a prevention/nonuse defense.

**Holdings navigation:** Andrade v. Mayfair Management, Inc. (1997-02-26) — Labor and Employment / Supervisory harassment and employer attribution; Faragher v. City of Boca Raton (1997-09-08) — Whether actual delegated authority establishes attribution without higher-management notice; Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc. (1997-09-15) — Labor and Employment / Supervisory harassment and employer attribution; Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc. ## Standards and Tests selection: Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review

### Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review?

**Current rule:** Forfeitures to the sovereign under §881(a)(4) and (a)(7), and punitive criminal RICO forfeiture, are subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Civil labeling, in rem form and concurrent remedial aims do not remove the covered statutory forfeitures' partly punitive character. Coverage and lawful conviction or statutory connection do not decide excessiveness. The court must separately assess the exaction in relation to its offenses; the property-offense connection may matter but review is not limited to instrumentality alone. No comprehensive formula, ratio, mandatory list of factors, incorporation holding or automatic return follows. The distinct due-process defense for a separately held automobile interest is stated in ‘Reasonable prevention and a separately held automobile interest’; other constitutional innocent-owner questions remain unresolved. Excessive Fines coverage does not itself establish criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy, which uses the independent civil-intent/clearest-proof inquiry. Alexander’s independent expressive-burden inquiry remains distinct.

**Authority by component:**

- These statutory forfeitures are covered fines: Austin v. United States — These statutory forfeitures are covered fines, 1993-06-28. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- Historical in rem form does not negate punishment: Austin v. United States — Historical in rem form does not negate punishment, 1993-06-28. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- Remand leaves excessiveness distinct from statutory connection: Austin v. United States — Remand leaves excessiveness distinct from statutory connection, 1993-06-28. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- 3. Punitive criminal forfeiture requires Excessive Fines review: Alexander v. United States — 3. Punitive criminal forfeiture requires Excessive Fines review, 1993-06-28. Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas; six Justices adopt coverage and remand. Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy accept the alternative excessiveness remand without joining Part III or adopting a proportionality formula.

- Limited resolution of innocent-owner protection: A qualified owner defense applies to this nuisance forfeiture, 1996-03-04; Stone-Zsela with Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer (five).
- Distinct constitutional classification: II. Excessive Fines coverage does not itself establish Double Jeopardy punishment, 1996-06-24; O’Connor with Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Coverage and excessiveness remain effective under Austin and Alexander, 1993-06-28. Bennis’s separate owner protection became effective 1996-03-04; Ursery’s separate-clause classification became effective 1996-06-24 and does not narrow this coverage.

**Holdings navigation:** Austin v. United States, 509 U.S. 602 (1993); Alexander v. United States; Alexander v. United States; Bennis v. Michigan, 516 U.S. 442; United States v. Ursery; United States v. $405,089.23 in United States Currency, 518 U.S. 267 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: A punitive drug tax after criminal punishment

### A punitive drug tax after criminal punishment

**Governing question:** When an ostensibly civil drug assessment is a second criminal punishment.

**Current rule:** An extraordinary drug assessment operates as punishment when its combined features make it part of the criminal sanction: liability depends on illegal possession and arrest, collection follows criminal enforcement and is subordinate to fines and forfeitures, and the assessment taxes confiscated contraband no longer possessed at exceptional rates. On the same-offense premise, the State cannot impose that punitive assessment after completed criminal punishment. The combination controls; neither a high rate, unlawful subject matter nor a revenue label alone resolves the issue, and no universal cost ceiling or mathematical ratio is established.

**Authority by component:**

- Combined punitive operation and successive-punishment consequence: Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch — The surviving drug assessment is a second punishment, 1994-06-06. Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the combined-operation rule and affirm disallowance as applied.

**Present operation effective:** Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch, 1994-06-06.

**Open question:** Reverse sequencing, different sovereigns and independently administered confidential taxes are not decided. The rule does not abolish civil taxation of illegal activity.

**Holdings navigation:** The surviving drug assessment is a second punishment.

## Authority source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md — Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Federal Courts — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences.

##### Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct

**Holding:** Section 102’s new compensatory and punitive liability does not attach to discriminatory conduct completed before November 21, 1991, because it adds legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. Its Title VII compensation supplements, rather than replaces, §706(g) relief and excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by that provision; punitive damages have the distinct statutory predicates of malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and exclude government, government-agency and political-subdivision respondents. No such damages award or predicate finding is made here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s §102 application, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas independently reach the same unavailability of new damages through their concurrence. Blackmun disagrees about compensation and reserves punitive damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bennett and Bowen: applied to the new monetary burden attached to completed conduct.
- Bradley: distinguished; the collateral fee award did not create this new compensation or punishment for the underlying private conduct.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992): distinguished; appropriate relief in an implied Title IX action does not enlarge Title VII’s earlier express remedial scheme.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished; its express substitution of law governing identified pending disputes does not supply the temporal direction missing here.

##### The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim

**Holding:** Under §102(c), if the complaining party seeks compensatory or punitive damages available under that section, any party may demand a jury trial, and the court must not inform the jury of the statutory monetary limits. Because §102 damages are unavailable for this preenactment conduct, its conditional jury provision supplies no independent right to a new trial; this conclusion does not prohibit applying a genuinely procedural change to an appropriate future procedural act in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s jury portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies the controlling five-Justice explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same conditional conclusion in their concurrence; Blackmun would permit a jury election because he would make compensation available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser: applied to the enacted condition linking the jury option to §102 damages.
- Bradley: distinguished; current-law treatment of collateral matters does not erase an express condition on a new remedy or automatically reopen a completed trial.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

##### Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences

**Holding:** When Congress has prescribed a civil statute’s temporal reach, courts apply that direction subject to constitutional limits; absent sufficient direction, they ask whether applying the provision would impair rights possessed when a party acted, increase liability for past conduct, or impose new duties concerning completed transactions. If it would, the provision does not govern those events without clear congressional intent; a pending case or reference to earlier facts alone does not establish such an effect, and prospective relief, jurisdiction, procedure and collateral matters require examination of what the particular provision actually changes rather than an automatic reopening of completed acts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser Aluminum & Chemical Corp. v. Bonjorno, 494 U.S. 827 (1990): applied to make statutory temporal direction the starting inquiry.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988): applied to the presumption against retrospective legal burdens; no automatic civil constitutional prohibition is inferred.
- Bennett v. New Jersey, 470 U.S. 632 (1985): applied to protect substantive obligations fixed under earlier law.
- Bradley v. School Board of Richmond, 416 U.S. 696 (1974): retained in its collateral litigation-fee setting and reconciled with the new-consequence inquiry, not overruled.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): distinguished as judicial retroactivity; its equal-application rule remains unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

## Authority source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md — Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc., 511 U.S. 298

#### Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc., 511 U.S. 298

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-938.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

##### Section 101’s new contractual coverage does not govern the earlier conduct

**Holding:** Section 101’s expansion of §1981’s making-and-enforcing-contracts coverage to include making, performance, modification, termination, and enjoyment of all benefits, privileges, terms and conditions of the contractual relationship does not govern conduct completed before November 21, 1991: it adds substantive legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. This provision-specific rule does not eliminate a claim already covered by old §1981 or decide a separate dispute over subsection (c)’s protection against nongovernmental discrimination and impairment under color of state law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; these five adopt the complete explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same temporal result through their separate relevant-activity and textual-command method.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, decided earlier April 26, 1994: applied to §101 independently of §102, preserving the provision-specific temporal inquiry and its five-Justice controlling explanation.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988), and Bennett v. New Jersey, 470 U.S. 632 (1985): applied to new substantive consequences for completed events; no constitutional bar against sufficiently directed civil retrospective legislation is adopted.
- Bradley v. School Board of Richmond, 416 U.S. 696 (1974): distinguished in its collateral litigation-fee setting, not overruled.
- Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 491 U.S. 164 (1989): applied as the governing construction of the earlier text within its scope; an old-law enforcement claim is not extinguished by refusing the later expansion.

##### Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach

**Holding:** When Congress expands statutory liability in response to a judicial interpretation, its corrective purpose or use of a new definition does not alone make the amendment govern earlier conduct; sufficient temporal direction remains necessary under Landgraf, and Congress may supply that direction within constitutional limits. Judicial construction of the earlier statute remains distinct: Harper governs application of that construction to open cases, and a defendant need not prove actual reliance on a later-issued judicial decision before invoking the enacted amendment’s limited temporal reach.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Patterson: retained as the old-text construction relevant to the temporal question; Congress’s prospective expansion supersedes its narrower coverage for conduct governed by the amendment, without overruling it as the construction of earlier law.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): applied to distinguish judicial retroactivity from later legislation; the lower court’s selective Chevron Oil balancing is not adopted.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished because its express substitution of law for identified pending disputes supplied direction absent here.
- Frisbie v. Whitney, 9 Wall. 187 (1870), and Freeborn v. Smith, 2 Wall. 160 (1865): distinguished as instances of directed curative legislation; neither establishes automatic retrospective reach for a corrective amendment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress's prospective expansion supersedes Patterson's narrow coverage for conduct governed by the amendment while leaving it the construction of earlier text. ##### Whether the reviewed civil forfeitures impose a second criminal punishment

**Holding:** For forfeiture under sections 881(a)(6), 881(a)(7) and 981(a)(1)(A), the court first determines whether Congress intended civil proceedings; that intent can be overcome only by the clearest proof that the scheme is so punitive in purpose or effect as to be criminal. **Material application:** Qualified incorporation of customs procedures and the civil statutory mechanisms establish civil intent. Removing facilitating property, discouraging unlawful use and recovering illicit proceeds supply nonpunitive functions beyond reimbursing enforcement costs. Loss, deterrence, a crime connection and an owner defense alone do not satisfy clearest proof.

#### Bryan v. United States, No. 96-8422 — 1998-06-15

[Controlling Record](../records/bryan_v_united_states_merits_1998-06-15.md#public-projection).

##### 1. Willfulness requires knowing unlawful dealing, not knowledge of the licensing rule

**Holding and operative rule:** For the charged §922(a)(1)(A) dealing offense, §924(a)(1)(D) requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the firearms dealing itself was unlawful; it does not require awareness of the particular federal licensing duty or the statutory citation. Deliberate dealing alone, unrelated illegality or generalized bad character is insufficient, and the Government must still prove the actual dealing conduct, absence of the required license and all other offense elements.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, seven Justices. Scalia and Ginsburg dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute distinguishes knowing factual conduct from a willful violation. Requiring actual awareness that the dealing is unlawful gives that distinction force without making knowledge of this particular regulatory source an additional element. Ratzlaf requires knowledge that structuring itself is unlawful; it does not establish a universal right to insist on awareness of a particular legal source after the charged conduct's illegality is known. Staples requires knowledge of a weapon's regulated characteristics under a different provision, not a universal legal-knowledge rule. Cheek's particular tax-duty rule likewise responds to that statutory context. Lanier separately forbids unforeseeable criminal enlargement.

The contrary construction properly insists that the charged dealing, rather than an unrelated offense, must be knowingly unlawful. The Court adopts that limit but rejects the further demand for licensing awareness. A purchaser's lie, an independent tax offense or disfavored firearm activity alone cannot replace the element. The reported straw-purchase, serial-number and street-sale evidence can support an inference about knowing unlawful dealing; it is neither irrebuttable proof nor a new appellate factual finding. Context resolves the disputed licensing requirement, leaving no residual ambiguity requiring its addition under Ratzlaf's lenity rule. The Court does not classify every unlicensed owner as a dealer, decide every business-definition or license exception, or enlarge another offense's penalty or mental state.

##### 2. The granted licensing question does not approve or independently adjudicate the later erroneous sentence

**Holding and operative rule:** A charge saying that knowledge of lawbreaking is unnecessary misstates the unlawful-dealing rule, but the Court's rejection of the preserved licensing-knowledge contention does not decide that distinct, unpresented instruction challenge. The reviewed judgment remains effective against the question actually presented, without approval of the erroneous sentence, a harmlessness finding or a new plain-error determination.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, six Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the disposition of this distinct component and adds no ground. Scalia and Ginsburg would reverse on their different answer to the presented statutory question.

**Controlling explanation:** Bryan requested knowledge of the federal licensing duty. The initial charge instead required intentional conduct for a purpose forbidden by law without awareness of the particular law. Its later statement that knowledge of breaking the law was unnecessary raises a different objection, one not specifically made at trial beyond the licensing theory or advanced separately in the court of appeals. Resolving the question presented does not silently endorse the whole charge. The presented-question principles reflected in Izumi preserve the difference between the stated question and an independently disputed ground. Gaudin preserves the jury's task to decide every actual element but does not place a distinct unpresented error before the Court automatically. The reported procedural account supplies this limited posture; the Court determines neither harmlessness nor unpresented plain error. No unrelated count or sentence is reopened.

- **Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994:** Preserved as requiring knowing unlawful structuring and residual lenity; distinguished from a universal requirement to know every specific legal source.
- **Staples v. United States, May 23, 1994:** Distinguished as factual knowledge of regulated weapon characteristics, without eliminating this statute's illegality requirement.
- **Cheek v. United States, 498 U.S. 192:** Distinguished in its particular tax-duty setting; no universal known-source rule is drawn from it.
- **United States v. Lanier, March 31, 1997:** Preserved as an independent fair-warning constraint, not a substitute for statutory willfulness.
- **Brogan v. United States, January 26, 1998:** Its reservation of distinct knowledge-of-illegality questions is honored.
- **Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp., November 30, 1993:** Applied to keep the distinct unpresented instruction question separate.
- **United States v. Gaudin, June 19, 1995:** Preserved as requiring jury determination of actual elements; no new unpresented-error disposition follows.

The Government must prove knowing unlawful firearms dealing, while awareness of the federal licensing command is unnecessary. Context-specific known-duty and factual-characteristics precedents retain their force. The later no-lawbreaking instruction remains erroneous; affirmance on the preserved licensing question does not approve it or decide a new harmlessness or plain-error claim.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The sex of the harasser does not exclude a Title IX claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Same-sex teacher harassment may constitute discrimination because of sex under Title IX; the claimant must still prove sex-based causation, unwelcome treatment, the required educational harm, recipient responsibility and causal injury. Sexual conduct alone does not establish those separate elements, and no opposite-sex comparator is invariably required.

**Authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is unanimous. Part IIA's recipient rule has the direct assent of Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, although four of those Justices oppose remand. Parts IIB–III have only Stone-Zsela's rationale and a different five-Justice remand judgment. Justice Ginsburg's Part IV is supported by all eight Associate Justices, and Part V is unanimous. The rule coalitions rest on direct assent to those portions, not a combination of opposing rationales under Marks.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1681 protects persons from discrimination on the basis of sex in covered education programs and contains no opposite-sex condition. Brown preserves sex-based causation; Oncale's analogous statutory analysis confirms that a same-sex relationship is no categorical bar without importing Title VII's attribution rule. The reported sex-directed selection of Kinman and lack of comparable attentions to male students support the causation issue at this stage, but supply neither a universal comparator requirement nor a finding of every defendant's intent. No sexual-orientation coverage rule is decided, and no supported fact triggers a statutory exception.

##### II. Participation does not establish welcome, and educational harm is contextual

**Holding and operative rule:** Voluntary participation and postgraduation continuation do not conclusively establish that earlier teacher advances were welcome. Actionable harassment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive; the full educational context controls without a psychiatric-injury, measurable-grade-loss or mandatory-factor requirement.

**Controlling explanation:** Meritor separates participation from welcome. Kinman's reported initial objection, the teacher's authority and the surrounding evidence leave a factual dispute that cannot be resolved by choosing credibility at summary judgment. Harris supplies the contextual severity analogy: frequency, gravity, coercion, humiliation, educational authority and interference matter, but no one circumstance is indispensable. Neither graduation nor later participation eliminates an otherwise proved earlier violation; neither makes every later private act actionable. The school's policy extending to welcome conduct, off-duty or off-campus acts and two years after departure is not itself the federal liability test.

##### III. Recipient compensation requires its own culpable response

**Holding and operative rule:** Damages against a funding recipient for teacher harassment require actual notice to an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial risk, followed by deliberate and unreasonable disregard of the known danger causing injury or continuation. Constructive notice, the teacher's status or ordinary authority-assisted access, policy breach, and the wrongdoer's knowledge of her own misconduct alone do not satisfy that recipient standard; a reasonable response does not become unlawful merely because harm continues.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin permits compensation for proved intentional discrimination but expressly leaves recipient attribution open. The recipient's responsibility must therefore be established separately from the teacher's intentional wrong. Title IX's recipient obligation differs from Title VII's express employer-agent relationship; Andrade does not automatically supply the same attribution route here. Section 1682 addresses the recipient's own compliance and gives notice and an opportunity for voluntary correction in federal administrative enforcement. That structure supports attention to the recipient's actual culpable response, but its agency notice, hearing, congressional-report and thirty-day provisions are not literal prerequisites to this private damages action. A delegated teacher relationship may connect conduct to the program without establishing the separate corrective official's awareness and disregard. Administrative and injunctive standards remain governed by their own sources and are not decided. The recipient receives no personal qualified immunity.

**Application and limit:** Five Justices retain the Title IX remand, but no majority adopts a common application rationale. Stone-Zsela would obtain further recipient-specific review under this threshold; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would remand under their distinct agency and negligent-correction grounds; the four other threshold joiners would restore summary judgment. The directly supported threshold governs further proceedings. Stone-Zsela's additional circumstantial-awareness formulation is separately stated and has no other joiners; no inference or presumption beyond the common rule is attributed to the Court.

##### IV. This supervisory record does not establish the constitutional wrong

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Doe, personal supervisory liability requires the particular official's actual knowledge of abuse or a substantial risk, deliberate and unreasonable disregard despite protective authority and opportunity, and causal contribution. Even assuming sufficient awareness for Whitehouse and Mackiel separately, the supported response evidence does not establish deliberate disregard; their reviewed §1983 summary judgments are affirmed on the merits without a new immunity ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** Doe distinguishes knowing acquiescence from negligent supervision and keeps Caplinger's conduct-date immunity ruling separate from constitutional merits. Whitehouse's student, staff and mother reports are not pooled with Mackiel's knowledge from Grosse's October 16 report. Assuming the relevant awareness, the interviews, attempted trace, testing and confrontation, followed by authentication and termination efforts after journal, cards and photographs arrived, show an imperfect response rather than the necessary deliberate refusal to protect. The transfer refusal, delay, nonsuspension and asymmetry remain adverse evidence; they do not on this record establish the constitutional fault. An alleged wrong-line trace is not itself evidence of a deliberately selected error. Denials and polygraphs are not conclusive truth, conclusive corroboration was not required before all action, and the mere existence of an investigation does not invariably defeat liability. The Court resolves this supported record without demanding a confession or treating the later 1992 evidence as proof of earlier awareness. Four Justices' statutory negligence theory remains distinct from the more demanding constitutional rule.

##### V. The District's constitutional attribution is separately absent

**Holding and operative rule:** A school district is responsible under §1983 only for an unconstitutional policy or custom, or a decision attributable to an official with final policymaking authority, that causes the injury. This record establishes neither that policy/custom nor an attributable final decision; ordinary supervisory rank and the teacher's conduct do not supply the missing institutional link.

**Controlling explanation:** Monell bars ordinary vicarious municipal liability while requiring the actual institutional source and causal relationship. The evidence establishes no persistent District practice of permitting abuse, no policy directing it and no final policymaking authority in either supervisor for the asserted decision. Absence of a broader pattern is not declared fatal to every claim based on one final policymaker's decision; that distinct route fails here because its attribution predicate is unestablished. The District does not receive Harlow immunity. Nabozny preserves the distinction between individual wrongs and institutional attribution, and supplies no new selective-enforcement claim. Bryan County's pending reargument supplies no additional merits rule.

The Court determines no damages amount, teacher default, direct-teacher merits disposition, unappealed Nebraska tort claim, universal postgraduation coverage or administrative or injunctive enforcement rule. The received Harlow–Anderson immunity framework remains in force; later Doe, Farmer, Lanier and Nabozny supply no earlier conduct-date notice.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Franklin is applied to compensation for intentional discrimination, and its reserved recipient-attribution question is resolved here at the stated actual-notice and culpable-response threshold.
- Brown is applied to the independent because-of-sex element; its attribution reservation does not supply the new threshold.
- Oncale supplies the same-sex textual analogy without importing Title VII liability rules.
- Meritor supplies the distinction between participation and welcome.
- Harris supplies contextual severity and pervasiveness without a psychological-injury prerequisite, adapted to educational harm.
- Andrade remains an employment-agency and negligence decision; its rules are not automatically transferred to funding-recipient compensation.
- Doe controls individual supervisory culpability and is applied to this record without conflating its separate immunity ruling with constitutional permission.
- Monell controls the District's distinct policy, custom or attributable final-decision requirement.
- Nabozny preserves personal/institutional attribution and supplies no new claim here.
- Harlow remains the received personal-immunity framework; no majority adopts a different defense.
- Anderson retains particularized apparent-unlawfulness analysis and is not retroactive notice before its own date.
- Elder preserves all-relevant-law conduct-date review and imposes no mandatory merits-first sequence.
- Lanier preserves fair warning without a factual twin but supplies no notice for the earlier conduct.
- Farmer's actual-awareness reasoning supports Stone-Zsela's separate analogy; it is not treated as an earlier warning or a newly controlling recipient evidentiary formula.
- Bryan County remains pending for reargument and supplies no merits authority.

#### Law After Decision

Title IX has no categorical same-sex bar, and welcome and educational harm remain distinct factual and legal requirements. Recipient damages require the actual corrective-official notice, culpable response and causal injury adopted by five Justices; the remand rests on a different five-Justice coalition without a shared application rationale. The individual supervisory and District constitutional judgments stand on their separate merits and attribution grounds. Personal immunity law remains unchanged, and statutory remand neither revives every defendant nor establishes liability.

#### Separate Writings

Breyer, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, agrees with the Title IX remand but rejects the higher-management-notice prerequisite as the exclusive route. A recipient may be responsible when actual delegated educational authority materially aids intentional harassment, or when responsible officials knew or reasonably should have known and negligently failed to correct it, causing injury or continuation. The teacher's use of study-hall authority and educational access, repeated reports, transfer request and disputed quality of correction warrant further review under those distinct routes. Franklin leaves attribution open; Meritor and Andrade supply a limited agency analogy rather than strict liability from title. Section 1682's administrative mechanism does not itself make its federal notice and hearing sequence a private-suit prerequisite. This statutory negligence position does not lower Doe's constitutional culpability standard, and these four join the constitutional affirmances.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, joins the recipient threshold but would restore recipient summary judgment. Franklin's remedy does not answer attribution, and the recipient structure, section 1682 and Pennhurst's notice concern favor responsibility for the recipient's actual culpable nonresponse. Even assuming corrective-official awareness, the reported interviews, trace, testing, confrontation and eventual termination efforts do not establish deliberate refusal to act. Delay, asymmetry and failure to separate the teacher from the student support criticism but not the required fault here; an alleged tracing error is not proof it was deliberately selected. They require neither confession nor conclusive proof before protective action, treat no investigation as automatically sufficient and give the District no qualified immunity. Their disagreement is the application and remand, not same-sex coverage or the controlling threshold.

Stone-Zsela's Parts IIB–III explain that actual recipient awareness may be proved circumstantially. Obviousness permits but does not compel an inference; willful avoidance or deliberate diversion of complaints may establish awareness. A deficient complaint system alone does not, no written complaint is indispensable, and separate officials' information cannot simply be pooled. Corrective authority is functional rather than titular; delegated teacher authority and missing complaint channels remain evidence, not automatic liability. He would examine the reported evidence further under these requirements and leaves administrative and injunctive enforcement under their separate sources. These additional proof formulations and his application have no other joiners.

Stone-Zsela separately dissents from the personal supervisory affirmances. The asserted misdirected trace, asymmetric testing and delayed confrontation do not conclusively establish reasonable protection if supported evidence shows the particular official's knowledge, opportunity and culpable nonresponse. He would return Whitehouse's and Mackiel's claims for individualized knowledge, response, causation and defense review, while joining the District's Monell result. He proposes deciding the constitutional merits first and replacing the broader notice defense with an official-burden defense of genuine reasonable reliance on identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the conduct, including reasonable reliance affected by an unexpected controlling-law change. In his view, after a knowing personal deprivation is proved, uncertainty or legal silence alone should not defeat §1983's remedy. Reasonable protection already defeats the deliberate-disregard merits claim; ordinary investigative policy or supervisory discretion is no separate legal permission, and a factual allowance rejected on the merits cannot be repeated as a second shield. The proposal preserves underlying proof burdens, causation, personal and institutional distinctions and other immunities; it finds neither reliance nor its absence conclusively here. Independently, under unchanged Harlow–Anderson, he supports the same remand to fix each omission's date and each official's supported assumed knowledge and response before particularized notice review. Ingraham, Rizzo and Monell supply relevant earlier principles without automatically resolving the specific supervisory omission; Anderson and the May 1987 Jefferson and Lopez authorities can inform only later conduct at their actual scope. Farmer, Doe and later cases cannot supply earlier notice, and 1992 corroboration cannot prove earlier awareness retrospectively. No automatic trial, immunity outcome or liability judgment follows from either proposed remand.

The Eighth Circuit's Title IX reversal/remand is affirmed with its instructions modified to the recipient threshold. The claim returns for further proceedings under the Court's majority-supported recipient threshold, with ordinary factual, causal and remedial questions remaining. The unappealed Nebraska tort dismissal remains undisturbed. No damages, mandatory trial, protective injunction or administrative-enforcement order issues.

SEC. 509. ONLINE FAMILY EMPOWERMENT.

    Title II of the Communications Act of 1934 (47 U.S.C. 201 et seq.) 
is amended by adding at the end the following new section:

``SEC. 230. <<NOTE: 47 USC 230.>>  PROTECTION FOR PRIVATE BLOCKING AND 
            SCREENING OF OFFENSIVE MATERIAL.

    ``(a) Findings.--The Congress finds the following:

[[Page 110 STAT. 138]]

            ``(1) The rapidly developing array of Internet and other 
        interactive computer services available to individual Americans 
        represent an extraordinary advance in the availability of 
        educational and informational resources to our citizens.
            ``(2) These services offer users a great degree of control 
        over the information that they receive, as well as the potential 
        for even greater control in the future as technology develops.
            ``(3) The Internet and other interactive computer services 
        offer a forum for a true diversity of political discourse, 
        unique opportunities for cultural development, and myriad 
        avenues for intellectual activity.
            ``(4) The Internet and other interactive computer services 
        have flourished, to the benefit of all Americans, with a minimum 
        of government regulation.
            ``(5) Increasingly Americans are relying on interactive 
        media for a variety of political, educational, cultural, and 
        entertainment services.

    ``(b) Policy.--It is the policy of the United States--
            ``(1) to promote the continued development of the Internet 
        and other interactive computer services and other interactive 
        media;
            ``(2) to preserve the vibrant and competitive free market 
        that presently exists for the Internet and other interactive 
        computer services, unfettered by Federal or State regulation;
            ``(3) to encourage the development of technologies which 
        maximize user control over what information is received by 
        individuals, families, and schools who use the Internet and 
        other interactive computer services;
            ``(4) to remove disincentives for the development and 
        utilization of blocking and filtering technologies that empower 
        parents to restrict their children's access to objectionable or 
        inappropriate online material; and
            ``(5) to ensure vigorous enforcement of Federal criminal 
        laws to deter and punish trafficking in obscenity, stalking, and 
        harassment by means of computer.

    ``(c) Protection for `Good Samaritan' Blocking and Screening of 
Offensive Material.--
            ``(1) Treatment of publisher or speaker.--No provider or 
        user of an interactive computer service shall be treated as the 
        publisher or speaker of any information provided by another 
        information content provider.
            ``(2) Civil liability.--No provider or user of an 
        interactive computer service shall be held liable on account 
        of--
                    ``(A) any action voluntarily taken in good faith to 
                restrict access to or availability of material that the 
                provider or user considers to be obscene, lewd, 
                lascivious, filthy, excessively violent, harassing, or 
                otherwise objectionable, whether or not such material is 
                constitutionally protected; or
                    ``(B) any action taken to enable or make available 
                to information content providers or others the technical 
                means to restrict access to material described in 
                paragraph (1).

    ``(d) Effect on Other Laws.--
            ``(1) No effect on criminal law.--Nothing in this section 
        shall be construed to impair the enforcement of section 223 of 
        this Act, chapter 71 (relating to obscenity) or 110 (relating

[[Page 110 STAT. 139]]

        to sexual exploitation of children) of title 18, United States 
        Code, or any other Federal criminal statute.
            ``(2) No effect on intellectual property law.--Nothing in 
        this section shall be construed to limit or expand any law 
        pertaining to intellectual property.
            ``(3) State law.--Nothing in this section shall be construed 
        to prevent any State from enforcing any State law that is 
        consistent with this section. No cause of action may be brought 
        and no liability may be imposed under any State or local law 
        that is inconsistent with this section.
            ``(4) No effect on communications privacy law.--Nothing in 
        this section shall be construed to limit the application of the 
        Electronic Communications Privacy Act of 1986 or any of the 
        amendments made by such Act, or any similar State law.

    ``(e) Definitions.--As used in this section:
            ``(1) Internet.--The term `Internet' means the international 
        computer network of both Federal and non-Federal interoperable 
        packet switched data networks.
            ``(2) Interactive computer service.--The term `interactive 
        computer service' means any information service, system, or 
        access software provider that provides or enables computer 
        access by multiple users to a computer server, including 
        specifically a service or system that provides access to the 
        Internet and such systems operated or services offered by 
        libraries or educational institutions.
            ``(3) Information content provider.--The term `information 
        content provider' means any person or entity that is 
        responsible, in whole or in part, for the creation or 
        development of information provided through the Internet or any 
        other interactive computer service.
            ``(4) Access software provider.--The term `access software 
        provider' means a provider of software (including client or 
        server software), or enabling tools that do any one or more of 
        the following:
                    ``(A) filter, screen, allow, or disallow content;
                    ``(B) pick, choose, analyze, or digest content; or
                    ``(C) transmit, receive, display, forward, cache, 
                search, subset, organize, reorganize, or translate 
                content.''.
