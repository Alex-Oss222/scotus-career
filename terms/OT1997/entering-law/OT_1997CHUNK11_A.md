## Standards and Tests selection: Social Security remand judgments and EAJA fees

### Social Security remand judgments and EAJA fees

**Governing question:** What instructions govern social Security remand judgments and EAJA fees?

**Current rule:** A sentence-four §405(g) judgment reversing a benefits denial with remand terminates the civil action and confers prevailing-party status; judgment must be entered rather than retaining the merits action pending an administrative award. Sentence-six remands are available on the Secretary's pre-answer motion for good cause, or for new material evidence with good cause for its earlier omission; these proceedings retain jurisdiction for the further administrative proceedings and return to court. Under the then-effective Rules, EAJA's thirty-day application period follows proper judgment entry and expiration of the applicable sixty-day appeal period; absence of the Rule 58 separate judgment prevents expiration. Hudson's integrated-administration fee rationale does not govern a properly entered sentence-four remand without retained jurisdiction, or retention challenged as improper; it remains available for qualifying sentence-six proceedings subject to EAJA's other conditions. No later deemed-entry rule applies.

## Standards and Tests selection: Administrative interest and LHWCA appellate aggrievement

### Administrative interest and LHWCA appellate aggrievement

**Governing question:** Independent §921(c) appellate aggrievement and the Director’s distinct respondent role in an existing private-party appeal.

**Current rule:** A general interest in correct administration of the LHWCA does not alone make the Director adversely affected or aggrieved under §921(c) by an employee’s unappealed award. Congress may confer an express review right, and genuine financial or independently statutory injury requires its own analysis; this construction is not categorical governmental incapacity to seek review. The Director, as the Secretary’s representative, may respond under then-effective FRAP 15(a) in an employer’s appeal from a Benefits Review Board order while the private parties preserve a live controversy. That respondent role does not authorize an independent Director appeal based solely on administrative interest.

**Authority by component:**

- The Director's general administrative interest does not confer independent review: The Director's general administrative interest does not confer independent review, 1995-03-21. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer: eight direct rationale votes. Justice Ginsburg concurs in the judgment and agrees with the case-specific statutory result, but does not join this explanation. No fractured-opinion synthesis is needed. 
- Respondent status in a live private-party appeal: Ingalls Shipbuilding v. Director, OWCP — Ingalls Shipbuilding, Inc. v. Director, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs — II. The Director may respond in this private-party review, February 18, 1997; O’Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Ingalls, February 18, 1997, resolves the bounded respondent-status reservation; Newport News’s March 21, 1995 independent-review limit survives.

**Open question:** Whether the Director may alone continue after private adversity ends, claims of genuine Special Fund injury, and compensation merits remain unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** The Director's general administrative interest does not confer independent review. Ingalls Shipbuilding, Inc. v. Director, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs (1997-02-18) — The Director may respond in this private-party review.

## Standards and Tests selection: Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use

### Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use

**Governing question:** Compensation for a land-use restriction eliminating all economically beneficial use of the relevant property.

**Current rule:** Complete deprivation effects a taking requiring just compensation unless background state property or nuisance law already excluded the prohibited use from the owner's title. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate a background limitation that independently prohibits the use in the property's circumstances. A new legislative declaration that the use is harmful is insufficient. Apply ordinary adjudicative proof requirements, without a special heightened quantum. Changed knowledge may affect application of genuine nuisance law; legislative labeling cannot substitute for an antecedent title limit. Compensation concerns any actual taking period, not an assumed permanent loss.

**Authority by component:**

- Categorical compensation, background-law exception and allocation: Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, “Complete economic deprivation requires compensation unless background ownership law already foreclosed the use”, June 29, 1992; Scalia, Stone, White, O'Connor and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Lucas*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Penn Central's contextual analysis remains for partial deprivation. No percentage shortcut, parcel-definition rule, automatic valuation, right to anticipated profits or finding that every environmental restriction is invalid follows. Valid authority to regulate remains distinct from responsibility to compensate.

**Holdings navigation:** Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992) — Complete economic deprivation of land.

## Standards and Tests selection: Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

### Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

**Governing question:** The scope of Congress's preventive and remedial power under Fourteenth Amendment §5.

**Current rule:** Congress may prevent or remedy constitutional injuries through legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently hold unconstitutional, but its means must be congruent and proportional to the injury addressed. Section 5 does not authorize replacement of the substantive constitutional right with a different standard. Examine the guarantee, threatened injury and practical statutory design for a supported remedial relationship; formal findings, a prescribed quantity of evidence, proof of a violation in every jurisdiction or individualized violation findings for every prohibition are not mandatory. Geographic limits and expiration provisions may illuminate fit but are not indispensable.

**Authority by component:**
- Enforcement boundary and method: City of Boerne, City of Boerne v. Flores — I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right, Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg (six), 1997-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** City of Boerne, 1997-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Independent Fifteenth Amendment power and other sources of congressional authority retain their own law. The rule creates no general hearing requirement or evidentiary checklist.

**Holdings navigation:** City of Boerne v. Flores (1997-06-25) — I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right.

## Standards and Tests selection: Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

### Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

**Governing question:** Appeal from district habeas and §2255 orders and the certificate procedures in amended §2253 and Rule 22.

**Current rule:** District final habeas and §2255 orders are reviewable in the appropriate circuit, except that §2253(b) supplies no right of appeal from a final order testing a federal criminal removal warrant or detention pending removal. An applicant’s appeal from a final state-process habeas order or §2255 order requires a certificate of appealability from a circuit justice or judge, issued only upon a substantial showing of denial of a constitutional right and identifying the qualifying issues. Rule 22(a) directs original applications to the appropriate district court; a circuit judge must transfer an application there, and after district denial the applicant may pursue an authorized appeal rather than renew before a circuit judge. For state-process habeas appeals, Rule 22(b) permits a district or circuit judge’s certificate; the district judge must issue it or explain refusal and send the certificate/reasons with the notice and file to the appellate court. After district refusal, the applicant may request a circuit judge’s certificate; a request addressed to the court is addressed to its judges, and a notice of appeal without an express request serves as that request. No certificate is required for an appeal by a State or its representative.

**Authority by component:**

- Appeal, certificate, district/circuit application and transfer procedures: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §§102–103, 28 U.S.C. §2253 and Fed. R. App. P. 22, enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

**Present operation effective:** AEDPA enactment, 1996-04-24.

**Effective transition:** Enacted April 24, 1996. “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” states Lindh's noncapital chapter 153 application-date construction, effective 1997-06-23. Separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case clause and Felker's successive-petition treatment remain distinct; Lindh does not settle every other Title I provision's application to pending proceedings. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** Rule 22’s circuit-judge transfer language does not repeal the Supreme Court’s distinct original habeas authority; see “Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar.”

**Holdings navigation:** Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which law governs a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

**Authority by component:**
- Application-date construction and preserved exceptions: Lindh, Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six), 1997-06-23.

**Present operation effective:** Lindh v. Murphy, 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule creates no constitutional entitlement to preserve every filing-date judicial doctrine and decides no capital State qualification, exhaustion, merits or ultimate entitlement to relief.

**Holdings navigation:** Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application.

## Standards and Tests selection: Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar

### Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar

**Governing question:** The retained original-writ channel and extraordinary showing following §2244(b)(3)(E).

**Current rule:** The statutory ban on ordinary certiorari review of a circuit’s successive-application authorization decision does not repeal §2241’s distinct Supreme Court original habeas authority. An original petition receives independent consideration rather than appellate review of that authorization judgment. Circuit permission to file in district court is not a prerequisite to filing here. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform original-writ discretion; whether they bind it directly remains unresolved. Rule 20 separately requires an explanation for not seeking district relief, how and where state remedies were exhausted or §2254(b) otherwise satisfied, exceptional circumstances warranting the Supreme Court’s discretionary exercise and lack of adequate relief in another form or court. Retained jurisdiction alone does not warrant the writ, further proceedings or an execution stay. Section 1651 aids independently held jurisdiction rather than supplying a freestanding new source.

**Authority by component:**

- Separate jurisdiction and original-writ discretion: The withdrawn review route and retained original jurisdiction; Successive restrictions and original-writ discretion; The separate constitutional innocence claim and original relief, 1996-06-28; Felker Parts I-A, II and IV-B, all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Felker, 1996-06-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Felker’s as-applied constitutional rejection rests on actual independent consideration and an adequate traditional original-writ ground, not approval of eliminating every meaningful forum. Constitutional innocence’s universal merits threshold, other supervisory routes, and the constitutionality of all AEDPA restrictions remain open.

**Holdings navigation:** Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996).

## Authority source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md — Shalala v. Schaefer, 509 U.S. 292 (1993)

#### Shalala v. Schaefer, 509 U.S. 292 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-311.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### Sentence-four judgment ends the action and confers prevailing status

**Holding:** A sentence-four judgment reversing the Secretary’s denial of benefits with a remand ends the §405(g) civil action and makes the successful claimant a prevailing party; the court must enter judgment rather than retain the merits action pending an administrative award. Sentence-six remands instead retain jurisdiction for the statutorily specified proceedings and later return to court.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sullivan v. Finkelstein, 496 U.S. 617, and Melkonyan v. Sullivan, 501 U.S. 89: the two statutory remand routes applied.
- Texas State Teachers Assn. v. Garland Independent School District, 489 U.S. 782, and Farrar, December 14, 1992: enforceable judicial relief establishes prevailing status, not automatic fee amount.

##### The missing separate judgment prevents the fee deadline from expiring

**Holding:** Under the then-effective rules, EAJA’s thirty-day application period follows a properly entered judgment and expiration of the applicable sixty-day appeal period; the absence of the required Rule 58 separate judgment prevents that deadline from expiring. Schaefer’s application is timely although the sentence-four remand was itself a final merits disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Indrelunas, 411 U.S. 216: separate-document requirement applied as then in force.
- Bankers Trust Co. v. Mallis, 435 U.S. 381: ability to take an appeal without the document distinguished from expiration of appellate time.
- Melkonyan, 501 U.S. 89: final court judgment, not agency decision, governs the EAJA clock.

##### Hudson does not preserve administrative-fee coverage after an ordinary terminated sentence-four action

**Holding:** Hudson’s integrated administrative-proceeding fee rationale does not apply to a sentence-four remand properly entered without retained jurisdiction, or to retention of jurisdiction that is challenged. It remains applicable to qualifying sentence-six proceedings in which the court retains the action, subject to EAJA’s other requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sentence six permits a pre-answer remand on the Secretary’s good-cause motion, or new material evidence with good cause for earlier omission, retaining jurisdiction for return and later judgment. Prevailing status does not establish every EAJA condition or amount. No later deemed-entry deadline is imported.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the timeliness determination and reviewed award; enter the required separate judgment where still absent. No new administrative-services award or reopening of unreviewed fee issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sullivan v. Hudson, 490 U.S. 877: retained-jurisdiction rationale limited in light of the statutory distinction; sentence-six coverage preserved.
- Finkelstein, 496 U.S. 617, and Melkonyan, 501 U.S. 89: sentence-four termination controls; statutory retention is reserved for sentence six.

## Authority source: state/holdings/property-and-economic-rights.md — Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992)

#### Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-453.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For reviewability of the original categorical-prohibition period, see Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, June 29, 1992, No. 91-453, under Federal Courts — Finality and earlier-period takings claims.

##### Complete economic deprivation requires compensation unless background ownership law already foreclosed the use

**Holding:** A land-use regulation that removes all economically beneficial use of the relevant property effects a taking requiring just compensation unless the prohibited use was already outside the owner's title under background state property or nuisance law. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate an applicable background limitation that would independently prohibit the claimed use in the property's circumstances; a new legislative harm declaration alone does not suffice, and ordinary adjudication supplies the proof requirements without a special heightened quantum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor and Thomas, supplies five votes for the complete rule and its explanation. Kennedy supports reversal through an expectations-sensitive analysis, without joining this categorical formulation. No Marks combination is required or used.

**Material application:** Total loss is the trial finding assumed for the legal question, not a Council concession or appellate affirmance of value. A new legislative declaration of harm cannot replace substantiated background law; changed knowledge may affect application of genuine nuisance principles.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes the trial court's total-loss finding to decide the presented legal issue; it makes no new appraisal and does not resolve the Council's preserved challenge to that finding. No unconditional entitlement to the trial award, final application of state nuisance law, right to anticipated profits, new parcel rule, or disposition of post-amendment permission is adopted. Legitimate environmental purposes remain sufficient to justify regulation; compensation is a distinct consequence. The Court overrules no earlier decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The no-compensation judgment is reversed and remanded for background property and nuisance law and the remaining defenses, including the sustained extent and duration of loss. Any compensation covers the actual taking period; the $1,232,387.50 trial award is not reinstated automatically.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pennsylvania Coal Co. v. Mahon, 260 U.S. 393 (1922): applies its recognition that regulation can go too far despite valid public purposes; does not attribute today's exact categorical rule to that decision.
- Agins v. City of Tiburon, 447 U.S. 255 (1980): specifies and extends its complete-economic-use formulation into the rule and necessary exception stated here; does not adjudicate its distinct legitimate-interest formulation.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104 (1978): preserves contextual analysis for partial deprivation, including economic impact, expectations and the character of governmental action; no percentage automatically defeats or establishes compensation.
- Mugler v. Kansas, 123 U.S. 623 (1887), Hadacheck v. Sebastian, 239 U.S. 394 (1915), Miller v. Schoene, 276 U.S. 272 (1928), and Goldblatt v. Hempstead, 369 U.S. 590 (1962): limits a broad noxious-use reading that would make any legislative harm characterization conclusive despite complete beneficial-use deprivation; preserves their actual outcomes and lawful protective regulation.
- Keystone Bituminous Coal Association v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470 (1987): distinguishes its productive-enterprise and partial-interest setting; no reversal of its actual holding or adoption of a new denominator follows.
- Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): applies existing rules and understandings to identify the protected property interest; does not decide a procedural due-process claim.
- Scranton v. Wheeler, 179 U.S. 141 (1900): applies its antecedent-title-limit principle; a previously existing servitude is distinct from a newly imposed public burden.
- Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp., 458 U.S. 419 (1982), and Kaiser Aetna v. United States, 444 U.S. 164 (1979): preserve physical-occupation and exclusion protections and illuminate the difference between existing limitations and a new imposed servitude; this decision concerns regulatory loss.
- Webb's Fabulous Pharmacies, Inc. v. Beckwith, 449 U.S. 155 (1980): applies the prohibition on converting private property into public property by governmental assertion alone.

## Authority source: state/holdings/constitutional-structure.md — City of Boerne v. Flores

#### City of Boerne v. Flores

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2074.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

For RFRA's State and local application and the prospective effect on earlier statutory applications, see City of Boerne v. Flores, No. 95-2074, decided 1997-06-25, First Amendment — Religious freedom and congressional enforcement.

##### I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right

**Holding:** Congress may enforce the Fourteenth Amendment through preventive and remedial legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently find unconstitutional. **Treatment of earlier authority:** South Carolina v. Katzenbach and Katzenbach v. Morgan retain broad preventive enforcement tied to the constitutional injury, not authority to redefine the right. City of Rome remains intact in its Fifteenth Amendment setting.

## Authority source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md — Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44

#### Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-12.
**Decided:** March 27, 1996.

##### IGRA's binding good-faith adjudication is judicial despite later statutory steps

**Holding:** Adjudication of IGRA's federal good-faith negotiation dispute is a judicial function when its judgment conclusively determines legal relations and activates constrained statutory consequences. Later mediation and executive action, and the absence of a guaranteed compact, do not make that judgment advisory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Stevens's serious doubt and Stone-Zsela's nonjoin supply no additional support.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aetna and Nashville permit conclusive adverse declarations without an immediately executable coercive decree. Brimson supports judicial assistance to administration within its adversarial, conclusive, enforceable-order setting, without creating IGRA contempt power. Ferreira's revisable commissioner process is distinguished. Gordon's jurisdictional disposition supplies no delivered reasoned majority opinion requiring execution in every judicial case; Hayburn produced no Supreme Court merits decision, and its reported circuit communications remain historical materials. Wisconsin's partial-redress rule concerns the judicial capacity to afford meaningful relief. Morgan Stanley and Plaut: Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement of qualifying completed private securities limitations dismissals under §27A(b)'s objectively defined procedure while courts independently determine eligibility and adjudicate the claims. The holdings do not establish an unrestricted power to reopen completed merits judgments and do not determine whether IGRA's conclusive adjudication ceases to be judicial because mediation or executive action follows.

##### A connected officer may be sued prospectively under IGRA within its complete statutory limits

**Holding:** Ex parte Young permits prospective official-capacity relief against Governor Chiles for an ongoing violation of IGRA's good-faith negotiation duty, subject to IGRA's full sequence and remedial limits. The State-directed wording and detailed procedure do not exclude that route in this case; they do not authorize greater remedies through the officer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. No additional general clear-indication theory commands that coalition.

**Material application:** The Governor's representatives negotiated, supplying connection to the alleged violation without establishing that he alone can bind Florida to every term. Mandatory good faith differs from discretion over compact terms, and nonagreement alone does not prove bad faith.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A qualifying request by the Tribe with jurisdiction over the Indian lands triggers §2710(d)(3)(A)'s good-faith duty; suit under (d)(7)(A)(i) may begin only after 180 days. The effective-compact enforcement action under (A)(ii) and Secretary action under (A)(iii) remain separate. Tribal evidence of no compact and failure to respond or respond in good faith precedes the State's good-faith burden under (B)(ii).

An actual adverse finding triggers an order to State and Tribe to conclude a compact within the first sixty days. The court may consider public interest, safety, criminality, financial integrity and adverse economic effects on existing gaming, and shall treat direct-tax demands on the Tribe or Indian lands as evidence of bad faith. Failure to agree during that period requires last-best proposals to a court-appointed mediator, who selects the proposal best comporting with IGRA, other applicable federal law and the findings and order. Submission to both sides begins a second sixty-day period for State consent; consent produces an agreed compact. Nonconsent triggers notice to the Secretary, who shall prescribe procedures in consultation with the Tribe consistently with the selected proposal, IGRA and relevant state law. The findings/order criterion expressly appears in selection under (iv), not verbatim in the Secretary clause (vii).

State nonparticipation does not automatically select the Tribe's proposal. Failure to agree or participate during the ordered period produces statutory progression, not added contempt or an order compelling agreement; every separate obstruction sanction is not decided. Section (d)(4) creates no new taxing authority but preserves agreed regulatory-cost assessments under (d)(3)(C)(iii), separately from direct-tax demands' evidentiary effect. Absence of other taxing authority does not justify refusing negotiation.

Class III gaming still requires approved tribal legislation, a State permitting such gaming for some person, organization or entity, and an effective compact subject to applicable remedial provisions; licensing and regulation remain distinct. Under (d)(8), disapproval may rest on IGRA, other federal law not relating to jurisdiction over gaming on Indian lands, or federal trust obligations. Forty-five-day inaction means approval only insofar as consistent with IGRA, and Federal Register publication remains necessary. Section 2721 severability supplies neither missing predicates nor an alternative program.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical Governor dismissal, 5–4, without finding bad faith or granting a compact, mediator, default proposal, Secretary procedures, damages, game-scope declaration or license. The separate September 22, 1993 merits judgment and stayed appeal No. 93-5256 receive their own lawful treatment and are not vacated. The asserted Secretary substitute remedy, independent administrative authority, complete state-law compacting power, Tenth Amendment defense and mediator-appointments question remain unadjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Young reaches this connected officer and ongoing federal duty without a universal rule for detailed statutes. Edelman's retrospective-compensation boundary remains; prospective costs alone do not establish retrospective relief. Pennhurst is not triggered merely because the federal obligation incorporates state gaming law. Hafer's personal-capacity damages rule does not supply this action. Puerto Rico Aqueduct supplies appealability, not immunity. New York and Voting Rights Coalition do not decide the reserved IGRA constitutional defenses; X-Citement Video preserves appropriate respondent defenses without compelling their resolution here.

##### State amenability lacks a controlling rationale, despite agreement on direct-State statutory intent

**Authority of the judgment:** Florida's immunity-dismissal direction is set aside and remanded by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, 5–4. Stone-Zsela and Stevens would sustain direct-State amenability; Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate without deciding it. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm. No controlling State-amenability rationale exists under Marks: avoidance is not a logical subset of the affirmative constitutional grounds, and three unused conditional alternatives are not actual abrogation joins.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** IGRA unmistakably authorizes an action naming the State through its State-party action, evidentiary burden, State-and-Tribe order and successive State-directed steps. General federal-question jurisdiction, §1362 or scattered State references alone would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Six actual positions across the separate writings: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The other three's conditional acceptance is not counted. This agreement concerns statutory intent alone.

**Current force:** The clear-statement proposition controls; no majority sustains or rejects Florida's constitutional amenability or requires a particular avoidance sequence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State-removal judgment establishes neither jurisdiction nor its absence; lawful jurisdiction is necessary before any State merits adjudication. The State claim is not terminated by immunity, and no abandonment, mootness or indefinite stay is presumed. No express or constructive waiver, reciprocal tribal/state consent or delegated United States immunity exemption establishes this suit. The United States appears only as amicus.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Union Gas is not overruled: its damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion retain their decided force without making every plurality sentence binding. Hans remains effective; separate common-law theories do not become law. Fitzpatrick's distinct Fourteenth Amendment authority is unchanged.

## Authority source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md — Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996)

#### Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-8836; associated application A-890.
**Decided:** June 28, 1996.

##### Whether §2244(b)(3)(E) bars review of circuit authorization while preserving original habeas

**Holding:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars ordinary certiorari review of a court of appeals' grant or denial of authorization to file a second or successive district-court habeas application; the present certiorari request must be dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. The provision does not repeal the distinct original habeas authority retained by §2241, which permits independent consideration of this petition without affirming or reviewing the circuit's authorization judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part I-A, unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 2241 still names the Court and its Justices; §2244(b)(3)(A) concerns advance authorization for district filing, and the amended transfer rule does not name this Court. Direct filing is procedurally original but constitutionally appellate; §1651 aids existing jurisdiction rather than creates it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Unpresented certification, interlocutory and supervisory channels are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Ordinary certiorari dismissed for want of jurisdiction, 9–0, without affirming the authorization judgment.

##### When the new authorization-review bar governs

**Holding:** The authorization and review proceedings begun after AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment are governed by its specified authorization-review bar. That conclusion does not decide the temporal reach of every Title I provision or apply a new substantive restriction backward merely because a petition is pending.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine support the channel result; O’Connor’s Part I-B has Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six. Thomas, Scalia and Kennedy use a distinct relevant-activity approach.

**Material application:** Landgraf’s provision-specific temporal sequence governs the majority reasoning; a procedural/jurisdictional label alone does not answer retroactivity. The May authorization and subsequent review proceedings began after April 24.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No holding determines every Title I provision’s temporal reach or Georgia’s chapter 154 qualification. Chapter 154’s express pending-case direction does not automatically govern other amendments.

##### What successive restrictions and discretionary conditions govern an original petition

**Holding:** The district-court authorization mechanism does not govern filing this original application. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform the Court's consideration of repeated applications, but whether those substantive provisions bind this Court directly is reserved. Retained jurisdiction does not itself entitle an applicant to an original writ.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** For statutory second/successive §2254 applications, (b)(1) dismisses previously presented claims. New claims under (b)(2) require either a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both previously undiscoverable facts despite due diligence and facts which, if proved with the whole evidence, clearly and convincingly establish that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would find guilt. A request to announce a new rule is not the first exception. Before district filing, (b)(3) requires circuit permission by a three-judge panel upon prima facie showing within thirty days; the determination permits no appeal, rehearing or certiorari. Under (b)(4), district dismissal is required unless the applicant actually establishes the conditions, despite authorization.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether (b)(1)–(2) bind this Court directly remains reserved; they inform the Court’s consideration. Rule 20 separately requires reasons for bypassing district court, exhaustion or satisfaction of §2254(b), exceptional circumstances and unavailability of adequate relief in another form or court. Section 2254(b)(1) permits exhaustion, absence of available state corrective process or circumstances rendering it ineffective; (b)(2) permits, but does not require, denial on merits despite nonexhaustion; counsel’s express waiver is necessary under (b)(3). No exhaustion, process adequacy, waiver or affirmative diligence-failure fact is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Original relief denied, 9–0, on the exceptional-original-showing ground; no return, transfer or hearing is ordered.

##### Whether the channel and successive scheme suspend meaningful review or dictate an unlawful outcome here

**Holding:** The specified review restriction and consideration of the operative successive scheme do not suspend constitutionally sufficient habeas review or prescribe a forbidden judicial outcome as applied to these claims. The original petition receives independent examination and fails on an adequate traditional original-writ ground, without deciding that every future statutory exclusion or every nominal surviving remedy is constitutional.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** The retained original route supplies actual independent claim examination and a sufficient traditional denial ground. Klein distinguishes changed law from a command to falsify facts or decide an unchanged rule in a prescribed manner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No approval of every nominal forum, future exclusion of otherwise sufficient innocence claims, all AEDPA, §§2254(d)–(e), or unpresented supervisory restriction.

##### Whether the submitted medical evidence establishes the Schlup gateway

**Holding:** This submission does not establish Schlup's gateway: reliable new evidence, assessed with all probative evidence, making it more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. That conclusion does not adjudicate the separately pleaded constitutional innocence theory or make the gateway and AEDPA's successive conditions interchangeable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part IV-A, all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela withholds this additional application.

**Material application:** Detailed new three-to-five-day estimates and physiological criticisms are considered with Whitaker’s independently favored earlier death estimate, his acknowledgment that the later interval was possible, and the disappearance, car, call and clothing evidence. The submission does not eliminate reasonable adverse assessment at Schlup’s probability threshold. No affidavit is declared false and other-offense evidence retains its motive/intent/state-of-mind limit.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Gateway passage would permit consideration of a trial claim, not prove it, establish statutory (b)(2)(B), or decide a distinct constitutional innocence entitlement.

##### Whether the separately pleaded constitutional innocence claim warrants exceptional original relief

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming a possible constitutional claim based on actual innocence, this considered submission does not establish exceptional circumstances warranting the Court's original habeas intervention. Original relief is denied without establishing a universal freestanding-innocence burden, rejecting every such constitutional claim, or requiring an additional return merely because new expert opinions are offered.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** O’Connor’s Part IV-B, unanimous; independently sufficient original-writ ground.

**Material application:** Herrera supplies a particular-submission insufficiency holding, not one general constitutional innocence burden. The full medical dispute receives consideration; the conviction and circuit refusal alone do not replace examination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal freestanding-innocence threshold, categorical denial of every such right, presumed adequate Georgia remedy or mandatory further return from new expert opinions.

##### Whether A-890 warrants protection after claim-specific consideration is complete

**Holding:** Execution's irreversibility does not alone establish entitlement to a stay. Because the independently considered claims show no sufficiently substantial basis for relief through the available proceedings and no identified unfinished legally required process warrants protection, application A-890 is denied.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part V, unanimous, in the reasoned combined threshold decision.

**Material application:** Bowersox’s substantial-ground assessment remains distinct from Lonchar’s protection of legally required process and McFarland’s counsel/preparation predicates. Neither the petition’s existence nor retained jurisdiction supplies relief by itself.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic lateness penalty, execution date, altered prior stay, exhaustion of all other possible avenues or execution legality determination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The pending stay request is denied, 9–0, after independent examination; no unfinished identified required process or sufficiently substantial relief basis remains.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Ex parte McCardle:** Applied to the specified withdrawn appellate route; no conclusion follows that every separate habeas source is repealed.
- **Ex parte Yerger and Ex parte Bollman:** Applied to the retained statutory habeas channel and its constitutional appellate character. They do not make issuance of an original writ automatic.
- **Landgraf and Rivers:** Their controlling provision-specific temporal inquiry remains in force; the application here concerns post-enactment authorization/review proceedings, not all pending habeas litigation.
- **McCleskey v. Zant:** Its recognition of lawful successive/abuse restrictions supports consideration of the new standards; it does not eliminate their separate textual requirements or settle their mandatory reach here.
- **United States v. Klein:** Its jurisdiction/rule-of-decision distinction is preserved; the present channel and standards do not direct a factual result under unchanged law.
- **Victor and Sandoval:** The whole-charge reasonable-likelihood test is applied by the eight Associates, with its existing limits on isolated terminology and automatic cures intact.
- **Sullivan:** Its structural-error rule is preserved; the Court finds no constitutional vitiation of the proof standard and conducts no harmless-error substitute.
- **Lisenba:** Its fundamental-fairness inquiry governs the presented medical-testimony trial claim. **Ake, Ford and Eddings** do not supply the proposed automatic physician-only testimony requirement.
- **Schlup:** Its gateway remains distinct from a substantive innocence claim and the new statutory conditions; the eight Associates find the present showing insufficient under its governing probability standard.
- **Herrera:** Its particular-submission holding is preserved without converting any separate theory into a general Court rule. The present original denial likewise assumes the possible constitutional claim without deciding its universal merits threshold.
- **Bowersox, Lonchar and McFarland:** Their distinct substantial-ground, required-process and counsel/preparation rules retain their actual triggers. None guarantees additional proceedings or a stay from filing alone.

## Authority source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md — Lindh v. Murphy

#### Lindh v. Murphy

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6298.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application

**Holding:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments, including amended §2254(d), do not govern Lindh's noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The application-date construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case rule and the successive-petition provisions governed by Felker; it does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the statutory rule, its limits and reversal with remand.

**Material application:** The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 in §107(c), contrasted with its omission for chapter 153 enacted alongside it, supplies the temporal construction. Lindh filed his noncapital application on July 9, 1992.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary statutory construction may establish temporal direction without a prescribed formula. No universal substantive/procedural classification, permanent constitutional entitlement to filing-date judicial rules, chapter 154 State-qualification ruling, or decision on confrontation, bias, harmlessness, exhaustion or entitlement to a writ is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is reversed and must consider the appeal under pre-AEDPA habeas law; no writ is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf supplies the statutory-direction inquiry and Rivers its provision-specific operation. Felker is distinguished because its authorization-review proceedings began after enactment and its other Title I timing questions were reserved; its successive-petition and retained-original-authority holdings remain. Hughes Aircraft requires attention to the actual statutory change and does not displace the chapter comparison.

## Authority source: state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md — Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States, 516 U.S. 137

#### Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States, 516 U.S. 137

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-7448 and 94-7492.
**Decided:** December 6, 1995.
**Companion or consolidated matters:** Both reviewed §924(c) use convictions receive the same statutory construction and bounded carrying remand.

##### Active employment under the use prong of §924(c)(1)

**Holding:** Use requires actual, active employment of the firearm as an operative instrument of the predicate offense. Possession, proximity, accessibility, protective storage, emboldening or placement for possible later employment alone is insufficient; firing or use as a weapon is unnecessary, and Smith's actual firearm barter remains use.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** Bailey's gun was stored in a locked vehicle trunk and Robinson's in her bedroom near drug proceeds. The possession-and-availability use theories and instructions cannot sustain the appellate affirmances.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No closed list of uses, full carrying definition, retroactivity rule or additional knowledge-of-illegality element is adopted. Distinct possession offenses remain untouched.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith's barter holding is applied while resolving its previously unaddressed passive-possession boundary; its original five-Justice authority is unchanged. Staples and Ratzlaf preserve the elements Congress enacted without creating a new mental element here or displacing ordinary interpretation with automatic lenity.

##### Independent proof of conduct and relation to the offense

**Holding:** Use or carrying must be established independently of occurrence during and in relation to the predicate offense. Actual or potential facilitation may establish the required relation under Smith but does not make mere presence or availability into active use.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith requires a trafficking purpose or effect beyond accident or coincidence and separately supplies active use through the actual exchange. Harris requires independent §924(c) conduct even when the predicate offense itself involves a firearm; its carjacking and cumulative-punishment holdings are unchanged.

##### Bounded consideration of an independently available carrying ground

**Holding:** The use-based appellate affirmances are reversed, and the D.C. Circuit may consider a legally sufficient and properly available carrying basis for either firearm conviction. Carrying language in the charges and instructions establishes neither lawful determination of all elements, harmlessness nor permission for a particular remedial course.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela agrees to remand but dissents from retaining the trial convictions for this consideration without first vacating them.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse each use-based appellate affirmance unanimously; remand without immediate Supreme Court vacatur of the trial firearm convictions, 8–1. No carrying conviction, acquittal or retrial is ordered. Separate drug and felon-possession judgments remain intact; no sufficiency, waiver or harmlessness determination follows from instruction excerpts.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Robinson's aiding-and-abetting route requires an actual substantive firearm offense and intentional assistance; presence, association or awareness alone is insufficient. A charge label supplies neither element nor procedural availability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harris's independent-conduct and intentional-assistance rules remain operative during the permitted carrying review; no new carrying or automatic remedy rule is announced.

## Authority source: state/holdings/federal-courts.md — Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

#### Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136; review of 90 F.3d 1237.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question

**Holding:** A federal court cannot assume disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim. Section 11046(c) describes enforcement and relief without making every statutory liability element jurisdictional; the holding preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences and does not prescribe an exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins, including O'Connor and Kennedy's nonexhaustive sequencing reservation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether EPCRA authorizes suit over reporting violations cured before filing remains undecided. Norton and Avrech retain their distinct settings involving already-controlled merits.

##### Requested relief cannot redress this completed informational deprivation

**Holding:** Assuming past informational injury and traceability, Article III requires requested relief likely to repair that injury or prevent a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. When reports were current before suit and no continuing or imminent violation is alleged, a declaration of past lateness, Treasury penalties, monitoring, future report copies and litigation expenses supply no such redress.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support dismissal on statutory grounds without joining this constitutional rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than rejects informational injury. Standing is absent at commencement, rather than lost by later cessation. Deterrent penalties may redress a supported threatened injury; annual reporting duties alone do not allege one. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party costs on a final order, not independent compensation. A properly presented replacement-information loss claim, monitoring in a live action, recoverable prefiling expenses, associational requirements and statutory temporal reach remain open. Section 11022(f) gives the fire department no transferable automatic inspection entitlement for this plaintiff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand with directions to dismiss, unanimously as to judgment. No merits relief, standing discovery, amendment entitlement or newly inferred recurrence inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to distinguish a substantial federal claim from its ultimate success; a contested statutory element is not automatically jurisdictional.
- **Young v. Northern Illinois Conference:** Applied at that same distinction; its constitutional-defense holding is not expanded into permission to assume Article III.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Its controlling standing elements and stage-appropriate support remain governing. Its three-Justice absent-agency application is neither enlarged nor needed.
- **Church of Scientology v. United States:** Meaningful partial relief remains enough; no effectual relief of that kind is available on these allegations.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** Congress's representative-enforcement choice does not eliminate personal injury and likely redress.
- **Bennett v. Spear:** Supported pleading-stage causal and remedial links remain sufficient; its ongoing constraint differs from the completed reporting deprivation here.
- **Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona:** Applied for the live-controversy requirement and limited corrective authority to vacate without reaching merits.
- **Norton v. Mathews; Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech:** Their already-controlled merits and distinct jurisdictional settings remain intact; neither decides this unresolved EPCRA dispute.
- **Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation:** Its different statutory wording and enforcement scheme do not settle the merits question the Court leaves open.
- **Meghrig v. KFC Western:** Its RCRA cause-and-remedy limits remain statute-specific; it supplies no automatic EPCRA current-violation rule.

### Triestman v. United States; lower-court No. 96-2563, 124 F.3d 361; no Supreme Court docket number assigned. — 1998-03-12

[Public source](../records/triestman_v_united_states_jurisdictional_dismissal_1998-03-12.md#public-projection).

#### Event

Triestman v. United States; Second Circuit No. 96-2563, 124 F.3d 361. Jurisdictional disposition, March 12, 1998. The Court considers attempted ordinary review of the Second Circuit's August 28, 1997 refusal on reconsideration to authorize a successive §2255 motion. The presented savings-clause question concerns an intervening statutory interpretation, a substantial claim of noncriminal conduct, and the absence of an effective earlier opportunity to test the claim. The order presented is an authorization refusal, not a judgment deciding a separate §2241 petition.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

#### Public Action

The attempted ordinary review is dismissed for want of jurisdiction. The Court does not affirm the authorization refusal or decide the savings-clause question.

#### Judgment & Remedy

Dismissal for want of jurisdiction, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No Justice opposes. No remand, transfer, release, independent original writ or other relief is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Per curiam | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | The statutory bar requires dismissal of this attempted ordinary review; independent collateral questions are not decided. |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Joins the Court's opinion and states a separate savings-clause view of what dismissal leaves open. |

#### Holdings

##### The authorization refusal is not subject to ordinary Supreme Court review

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 2255's successive-motion certification cross-reference incorporates §2244(b)(3)(E)'s bar on ordinary certiorari review of a court of appeals' authorization decision. Because the only order presented is the Second Circuit's refusal to authorize a successive §2255 motion, this attempted review must be dismissed for want of jurisdiction, without affirming that order or adjudicating the underlying claim.

**Authority.** The per curiam opinion, joined by all nine participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the nature of the order presented determines the available review route. Felker v. Turpin applies the authorization-review bar while preserving a distinct statutory original-habeas channel. The existence of that separate channel does not permit review of an authorization refusal under a different label. No independent original application or judgment on a §2241 petition is presented here. An independent federal-prisoner proceeding would still require its own lawful forum and savings-clause basis; an original Supreme Court petition would also face Rule 20's separate requirements. Section 1651 assists jurisdiction otherwise held and creates none. The concern that an intervening interpretation may reveal noncriminal conduct cannot supply the missing appellate jurisdiction. Bailey's firearm-use rule therefore does not permit the Court to reach the firearm merits through this vehicle. Savings-clause eligibility, effective earlier opportunity, retroactivity, procedural defenses, carrying, aiding and abetting, factual development and ultimate relief remain undecided. This dismissal determines no person's guilt or innocence and does not foreclose a genuinely available independent proceeding.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996), is applied without alteration: the authorization-review prohibition and independent original-habeas jurisdiction remain distinct. Retained original authority does not establish a filed application, eligibility or entitlement to relief.
- Bailey v. United States, 516 U.S. 137 (1995), is preserved: active firearm use remains distinct from passive possession and protective storage, and any properly available carrying ground requires its own consideration. The substantive rule supplies no collateral jurisdiction and is not applied here to decide Triestman's liability.
- Lonchar v. Thomas, 517 U.S. 314 (1996), retains its protection of process actually required under governing habeas law in an available proceeding; it does not create a forum barred by statute.
- Schlup v. Delo, decided January 23, 1995, retains its reliable-new-evidence probability gateway, distinct from §2255 certification and the proposed statutory-innocence claim. Herrera v. Collins, 506 U.S. 390 (1993), retains its record-specific insufficiency holding without establishing a universal freestanding-innocence entitlement.
- Trest v. Cain, decided December 9, 1997, concerns an omitted state procedural-default defense. Its no-mandatory-duty holding does not convert a statutory jurisdictional prohibition into a forfeitable defense.

#### Law After Decision

The ordinary-review bar continues to govern successive-motion authorization orders. The Court establishes no savings-clause test or firearm-merits rule in this case. A genuinely available independent collateral route retains its own statutory conditions, defenses and remedial requirements. The concurrence supplies no binding rule on eligibility or relief.

#### Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the jurisdictional dismissal and would give effect to both Congress's restrictions on repetitive litigation and the retained savings clause. In his view, an intervening interpretation that applies to the conviction and supports a substantial claim that the conduct was outside the criminal statute can permit §2241 consideration when no effective earlier §2255 opportunity existed. Inability to satisfy successive-motion criteria alone is insufficient. The prisoner must establish eligibility and entitlement to relief separately, and the Government retains its lawful defenses. Neither Article III nor a preference for review creates jurisdiction; Felker's distinct original route remains subject to its statutory and Rule 20 conditions. Complete foreclosure of meaningful review would raise a separate Suspension Clause question, which this disposition does not decide.

He regards the reported protective-storage plea account as presenting a substantial question under Bailey's active-employment rule that warrants a genuine opportunity to establish the asserted claim through an available savings-clause process. Intent, availability, possession and an officer's perception of danger do not substitute for active use. Failure of a use theory does not establish innocence of every alternative: carrying must be legally available and proved under its own elements, and aiding and abetting requires a substantive firearm offense and intentional assistance rather than presence or awareness. No finding establishes attempted firing, innocence, or savings-clause eligibility.

Triestman's earlier factual-basis objections and his supplemental Bailey submission must be acknowledged. They do not conclusively establish whether the earlier legal framework and procedures afforded an effective opportunity. The proper court may decide on an adequate existing record, obtain appropriate submissions or allow necessary factual development under governing law. An allegation does not satisfy every gateway or merits element, but eligibility does not require already prevailing on ultimate relief. No new burden, exhaustion requirement, unlimited retries, universal oral or evidentiary hearing, automatic release or favorable outcome follows. The plea's legal effect, available defenses and appropriate remedy require their own lawful determination; separate drug convictions are not resolved by the firearm question. These are his views of what dismissal leaves open, not holdings of the Court or relief ordered in this proceeding.

#### Procedure After Action

This attempted ordinary review ends. The Court neither affirms nor vacates the circuit's authorization refusal and directs no further proceeding. Triestman retains only whatever independent §2241 process the law actually provides, where he must establish its predicates and any entitlement to relief and the Government may assert lawful defenses.

These are his noncontrolling views of what remains open, not an eligibility finding or a remand ordered by this Court.

#### Procedure After Action

This attempted ordinary review ends. The Third Circuit's authorization refusal is neither affirmed nor vacated. No further proceeding is directed. Dorsainvil retains only whatever independent §2241 process the law actually provides, where eligibility, entitlement to relief and lawful Government defenses require their own determination.

## Current-term public authority: 1998-05-18 — Bousley v. United States, No. 96-8516.

Source: terms/OT1997/workspace/neutral-projection.md; the linked Record Public Projection is controlling. The following is copied without independent paraphrase.

### 1998-05-18 — Bousley v. United States, No. 96-8516.

[Authoritative Public Projection](../records/bousley_v_united_states_merits_1998-05-18.md#public-projection).

#### Event

Bousley v. United States, No. 96-8516. Argued March 3, 1998; decided May 18, 1998. On certiorari to the Eighth Circuit's denial of relief in a first §2255 proceeding.

Bousley pleaded guilty to a drug offense and using a firearm during and in relation to drug trafficking. He appealed his sentence without challenging the plea's validity. After Bailey explained that use requires active employment, he challenged the understanding of the firearm charge underlying his plea. The questions are whether that claim is available on collateral review, how procedural default affects it, and what factual innocence requires. The indictment charged use, not carrying.

#### Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

#### Public Action

The Court reverses and remands for an opportunity to establish factual innocence and, if that gateway is satisfied, adjudication of the plea-intelligence claim.

#### Judgment & Remedy

| Component | Supporting | Opposing or different remedy | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Set aside the Eighth Circuit's rejection | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | Reversed, 7–2 |
| Conditional gateway remand | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stevens would immediately vacate the firearm conviction and permit a new plea; Scalia and Thomas would affirm | Six adopt the controlling mandate |

The firearm conviction is not immediately vacated. Neither factual innocence nor constitutional invalidity is found. The drug conviction is unaffected. The remand does not automatically require an oral evidentiary hearing, release or dismissal of the firearm charge.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer throughout; Stone-Zsela in I–III and IV.B; Stevens in I | I: informed plea and substantive meaning; II: default; III: factual gateway; IV.A: actually forgone more-serious charges; IV.B: use-only indictment and conditional remand |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stevens | None | Joins I; rejects the default/innocence prerequisites and seeks immediate vacatur with permission to plead anew |
| Dissent | Scalia | Thomas | Would affirm because cause is absent and the trial-conviction innocence gateway should not extend to this plea |

#### Holdings

##### 1. Correct knowledge of the offense is necessary to an intelligent plea

**Holding and operative rule:** A guilty plea requires real notice of the charge's essential nature. A materially mistaken description of a statutory element can render the plea unintelligent; Bailey's explanation of what the firearm statute prohibits is substantive law, not a new constitutional procedure barred by Teague.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Parts II–III and IV.B are joined by the same coalition except Stevens. Part IV.A has O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each stated rule has direct majority support.

**Controlling explanation:** Bailey requires active employment of a firearm; proximity, accessibility or possession alone is not use. Henderson and Smith require an understanding of the actual charge. A statutory construction states the substantive reach of the crime, as Rivers explains, rather than imposing a new trial procedure. Teague's procedural restriction therefore supplies no answer to this claim.

Broce distinguishes claims waived by an otherwise valid guilty plea from a challenge to the plea's own validity. Brady's concern with later disappointed expectations does not excuse a materially mistaken account of the offense's essential elements. Godinez likewise separates capacity to choose from the informed choice itself.

**Precedent treatment:** Bailey supplies the offense's meaning; Henderson and Smith supply notice; Rivers explains substantive interpretation; Teague does not bar this substantive claim; Broce, Brady and Godinez retain their distinct limits.

##### 2. Direct-review default requires cause and prejudice or factual innocence

**Holding and operative rule:** Failure to challenge plea intelligence on direct review defaults this claim. Neither anticipated defeat nor adverse precedent establishes cause where the legal argument was available; absent cause, the claim may proceed only through a sufficient factual-innocence showing, not a showing of legal error alone.

**Controlling explanation:** The distinction between using and possessing a firearm was available before Bailey. Reed's unavailable-claim concern does not encompass an argument thought unlikely to prevail, and Engle rejects expected futility as cause. Prejudice cannot replace missing cause.

Schlup nevertheless permits review where, considering the whole probative record under the correct offense, it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. That exceptional inquiry applies here despite the plea. The absence of a trial record does not justify treating an unsupported conviction as conclusive, but neither does it shift the inquiry to whether the existing plea record was formally sufficient. Carriger preserves the difference between passing this gateway and proving the underlying constitutional violation. The district court has not adjudicated innocence, and the Government does not contend that omission of that issue below independently waived this opportunity. The Court does not now decide constitutional invalidity; the gateway and underlying claim must be resolved in that order.

**Precedent treatment:** Reed and Engle delimit cause; Schlup supplies the demanding whole-record gateway; Carriger keeps the gateway separate from the constitutional merits.

##### 3. The evidence and actual plea bargain define the innocence inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government may offer admissible evidence outside the plea record to rebut factual innocence. Where it actually forgave an identified more-serious charge in the plea bargain, the innocence showing must also cover that charge; hypothetical charges, unproved bargains and alternative statutory verbs create no added burden.

**Controlling explanation:** Factual innocence concerns what occurred, not merely what the plea papers recite. Limiting the Government to those papers would convert the gateway into a legal-sufficiency test. The five Justices joining IV.A also require attention to an actual bargain relinquishing more-serious exposure, so that the inquiry does not disregard the exchange that produced the plea.

This indictment alleged use, not carrying. No bargain relinquishing carrying or another identified more-serious offense is established. Bousley therefore need establish absence of use under Bailey on this record. The Government cannot preserve the conviction simply by relabeling it an uncharged carrying conviction. A proved gateway permits adjudication of the plea-intelligence claim; it does not itself require vacatur or acquittal.

**Precedent treatment:** Schlup's factual inquiry is applied to a guilty plea with evidence beyond the plea record; Bailey continues to define the charged use offense.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Bailey:** Active employment remains necessary for the charged use offense; possession alone is insufficient.
- **Henderson and Smith:** Applied to real notice of the charge's essential nature.
- **Rivers:** Supplies the substantive effect of an authoritative statutory construction.
- **Teague:** Its new-procedure limitation does not bar this substantive statutory claim.
- **Broce and Brady:** Distinguished from an attack on the plea's informed character; a disappointed expectation and misinformation about an element remain different.
- **Godinez:** Competence does not itself establish informed assent.
- **Reed and Engle:** An available argument's expected failure does not establish cause.
- **Schlup:** Extended to this defaulted plea claim with its demanding factual test intact.
- **Carriger:** Passing the gateway remains distinct from proving constitutional error and entitlement to relief.
- **Hyde:** Its presentence withdrawal setting does not decide this collateral claim.

#### Law After Decision

A defaulted challenge to a materially misdescribed guilty plea may receive collateral review upon the required factual-innocence showing. Admissible proof beyond the plea record is available. Actual relinquishment of a more-serious charge matters; an imagined charge does not. Passing the gateway opens consideration of plea validity and supplies no automatic merits relief.

#### Separate Writings

Stevens joins the substantive and constitutional principles in Part I but would vacate the firearm conviction and permit Bousley to plead anew. Timmreck and Hill distinguish formal plea-taking defects from a constitutionally invalid plea. In his view, the erroneous understanding of the statutory element establishes the constitutional defect here, and failure to appeal does not require Bousley to prove innocence before obtaining correction. The Government would retain its ordinary burden if prosecution proceeds. His position does not require acquittal or dismissal with prejudice and does not affect the drug conviction.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, would affirm. He agrees that no cause excuses the default but rejects extending Schlup's trial-conviction gateway to guilty pleas. A plea leaves no comparable trial record of facts that participants may then have thought immaterial, may exchange admission for relief from greater charges, and may leave stale evidence insufficient to reconstruct either side of that exchange. Those features make the proposed exception materially different from Schlup's setting. The dissent does not treat possession as active use, find actual carrying, or decide Bousley's innocence.

#### Procedure After Action

The district court must permit the bounded factual-innocence inquiry and resolve any factual development needed under ordinary procedures. If Bousley satisfies that gateway, it must consider whether his plea was voluntary and intelligent and determine any lawful relief. No automatic live hearing, release or acquittal is ordered.

The published record at 523 U.S. 614 supplies the charged offense, plea and review history, and the Government's position on the separate omission of innocence below. The original plea transcript and bargain agreement are not independently supplied. No relinquished carrying or more-serious charge is established by the available record, and no such bargain is presumed.

## Current-term public authority: 1998-05-18 — Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal; No. 97-300.

Source: terms/OT1997/workspace/neutral-projection.md; the linked Record Public Projection is controlling. The following is copied without independent paraphrase.

### 1998-05-18 — Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal; No. 97-300.

[Authoritative Public Projection](../records/stewart_v_martinez_villareal_threshold_1998-05-18.md#public-projection).

#### Event

Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal, No. 97-300. Argued February 25, 1998; decided May 18, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 118 F.3d 628. The Court considers whether §2244(b)(3)(E) permits review of the circuit's determination that authorization was unnecessary and whether this renewed Ford incompetency-to-be-executed claim is second or successive.

Three earlier federal applications ended for nonexhaustion. The March 1993 application included the Ford claim, dismissed as premature at the State's request. The 1996 appellate mandate reversed relief on other grounds while preserving later litigation of that claim. After an execution warrant and state competency proceedings, the District Court dismissed the renewed motion for lack of jurisdiction. The Ninth Circuit held authorization unnecessary and returned the claim for district consideration.

#### Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

#### Public Action

The Court affirms the Ninth Circuit's judgment returning this claim for consideration in the District Court.

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

All nine agree that ordinary review of this threshold classification is available. The seven-Justice judgment permits first federal merits consideration of this particular renewed claim under otherwise applicable law. It grants no writ or stay and orders no new evidentiary hearing or execution.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer throughout; Scalia and Thomas in Part I | Affirms the district-return judgment | Part I: ordinary review channel; Part II: previously presented premature claim and resulting return for consideration |
| Dissent in part | Scalia | Thomas | Would reverse and require dismissal under §2244(b)(1) | Both join Part I but reject Part II's continuation classification |

#### Holdings

##### I. The circuit's threshold classification is reviewable

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars ordinary review of a circuit decision granting or denying authorization to file a second or successive application, but it does not bar review of this distinct determination that authorization is unnecessary. The Court may decide whether the circuit correctly placed this proceeding outside that mechanism; the circuit's label does not itself establish the answer.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is adopted by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Felker gives effect to the statutory bar for an actual authorization decision. Triestman and Dorsainvil apply that restriction to authorization refusals. Here the circuit did not decide whether the applicant satisfied the statutory prima-facie permission criteria. It held those criteria inapplicable and transferred the proceeding back. Calderon likewise distinguishes an independently reviewable first-application action from the authorization process. Ordinary review therefore reaches this classification without opening review of a true authorization refusal or allowing a court to evade the statute by relabeling its action.

##### II. This previously presented premature claim may receive its first federal merits consideration

**Holding and operative rule:** A Ford claim actually presented in the initial merits application, dismissed as premature and expressly preserved for later consideration, is not a second or successive application when renewed after the impediment of prematurity has ended. This renewed claim may therefore return to the District Court without circuit authorization, subject to otherwise applicable habeas requirements.

**Authority:** Part II is adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author; these seven directly establish the rule and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The claim was before the federal court in March 1993, but could not then receive a decision on competency to be executed. The State requested its dismissal on that ground, and the later mandate preserved its return. The renewed filing seeks the federal merits consideration previously unavailable. It does not repeat a decision already made on that claim.

Second or successive is a procedural term, not simply a count of papers filed. The treatment of an application dismissed for nonexhaustion illustrates why an earlier nonmerits filing need not consume the available opportunity. Lonchar requires attention to the actual legal source and its dismissal predicates; it does not authorize a court to ignore an applicable statute. This conclusion construes the statute's class rather than creating an equitable exception to it. The final disposition of other claims does not turn this claim's prematurity dismissal into a merits adjudication. Calderon's rule for reopening completed merits therefore supplies no contrary answer.

The Court does not adopt the circuit's broader exemption for every competency-only application. A Ford claim first presented after an earlier federal application was rejected, other later-arising claims and every other type of nonmerits dismissal remain undecided. Ford identifies the constitutional claim; this judgment establishes neither incompetence nor any defect in Arizona's competency proceedings. Lindh and Carriger retain their provision-specific limits, and neither this classification nor first-review status guarantees a new live evidentiary hearing, a writ or a stay.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Felker v. Turpin: applied to actual authorization decisions; its review bar and distinct original-habeas route remain unchanged.
- Triestman v. United States and In re Dorsainvil: distinguished because they concerned actual authorization refusals; this case creates no exception permitting ordinary review of those refusals.
- Calderon v. Thompson: its action-specific channel distinction is applied; its completed-merits recall rule is distinguished from a claim dismissed only as premature.
- Lonchar v. Thomas: its requirement of legally supported dismissal predicates informs the classification; no equitable power to disregard enacted successive limits is recognized.
- Ford v. Wainwright: supplies the incompetency-to-be-executed claim without deciding competency or procedural sufficiency here.
- Lindh v. Murphy and Carriger v. Stewart: their provision-specific temporal treatment is preserved; they do not independently exempt all later filings from AEDPA.

#### Law After Decision

The particular previously presented and premature Ford claim remains available for first federal consideration when ripe. The statute continues to govern genuinely second or successive applications, and its ordinary-review prohibition continues to govern actual authorization decisions. No general competency-claim exemption, constitutional invalidation of AEDPA or substantive entitlement to habeas relief follows.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, dissenting in part.** They agree that the Court may review the circuit's threshold classification, but would reverse it. Section 2244(b)(1) addresses a claim presented in a second or successive application and requires dismissal when that claim appeared in the earlier application. Here the earlier merits application ended after other claims received merits adjudication; the Ford claim was dismissed, not retained in an ongoing proceeding. A wholly unexhausted application dismissed without prejudice presents a different situation.

For the dissenters, the court's reservation cannot create an exception that the enacted text does not supply, and the State's earlier prematurity request does not waive the statutory restriction. Ex parte Bollman's requirement of written legal authority reinforces their objection to restoring prior judicial practice after Congress changed the governing law. They would require dismissal of this repeated claim under §2244(b)(1), not an opportunity for discretionary authorization under an invented exception. They decide no competency question, order no execution and express no disposition of a genuinely separate lawful proceeding.

#### Procedure After Action

The circuit's return of the claim to the District Court stands. That court must consider the renewed Ford claim through otherwise applicable habeas procedures; the proper form of consideration and entitlement to relief remain for determination. No competency finding, new evidentiary-hearing requirement, stay, writ or execution direction is entered by this judgment.
