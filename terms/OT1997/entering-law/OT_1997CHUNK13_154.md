## Standards and Tests selection: Unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing

### Unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing

**Governing question:** What instructions govern unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing?

**Current rule:** A governmental barrier preventing persons able and ready to bid from competing on equal terms supplies injury without proof that they would have won a contract. An association may sue when a member has individual standing, the interest is germane to its purpose, and, absent clear congressional displacement, neither the claim nor relief requires individual-member participation. That third Hunt condition is prudential; WARN §2104(a)(5) displaces it by authorizing the exclusive employee representative to enforce represented employees’ back-pay and benefit claims. The representative enforces the employees’ entitlement without becoming its beneficiary. Individual proof remains necessary as applicable; its necessity alone does not bar the authorized action. Concrete injury, traceability and redressability for the represented members remain constitutional requirements. Accepted allegations suffice only at the appropriate stage; genuinely disputed elements require stage-appropriate proof. Replacing an ordinance does not moot the dispute when the successor continues the challenged exclusion from particular competitions; a replacement actually ending that injury may have a different effect.

**Authority by component:**

- Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury: Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville — Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury, 1993-06-14. Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.
- The replacement continues the controversy: Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville — The replacement continues the controversy, 1993-06-14. Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, The replacement continues the controversy portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.
- Brown Group — Parts I–II, express WARN authority and prudential third condition, 1996-05-13; Souter for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Brown Group, May 13, 1996, for congressional displacement of the third condition; Northeastern Florida’s competitive-injury and mootness rules survive.

**Holdings navigation:** Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville, 508 U.S. 656 (1993); United Food & Commercial Workers Union Local 751 v. Brown Group, Inc., 517 U.S. 544.

**Exceptions and limits:** The union receives no right to its own damages or the local-government penalty; employee eligibility, ordinary defenses, individual amounts and WARN’s exclusive statutory remedies retain their requirements. No closing or layoff injunction is authorized by WARN. Standing germaneness does not itself establish adequate representation or binding effect, and no mandatory certification, joinder or jury procedure follows.

**Open question:** Whether Hunt’s germaneness condition is prudential and displaceable, or a sufficiently simplified damages claim could satisfy the third condition without abrogation, remains unresolved.

## Standards and Tests selection: Race-conscious public-university admissions and individual relief

### Race-conscious public-university admissions and individual relief

**Governing question:** Justification of a State university’s admissions classification and remedies for a proved unequal process.

**Current rule:** A State university’s racial admissions classifications require a compelling objective and narrowly fitted means. Concrete educational benefits of genuine student diversity can supply that objective on a supported record, but a label, numerical racial balance or assumptions that race determines viewpoint cannot. Applicants must receive genuinely competitive individual consideration; unjustified separate racial decision systems fail. After an unconstitutional admissions process is established, the school must prove by a preponderance that each applicant would have been rejected under a specified constitutional process to defeat relief for denied admission and its consequences. Plaintiffs must still prove actual compensable loss under an available cause against a proper defendant. A successful same-decision defense does not erase the process injury; failure of the defense awards neither automatic damages nor unconditional admission. Deliberate unequal racial treatment satisfies the presented Title VI intent requirement despite good faith or a beneficial aim; remedial elements, defendants and immunity remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Strict scrutiny and equal competition: Hopwood v. Texas — Hopwood v. Texas — I. The established unequal admissions process, November 25, 1996; O’Connor, all nine.
- Affirmative educational-interest rule: Hopwood — Hopwood v. Texas — II. A supported educational-diversity interest is constitutionally available; O’Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy’s judgment vote is not an affirmative-interest join.
- Same-decision burden: Hopwood v. Texas — IV-A. Individual causation and the same-decision burden; Title VI intent: Hopwood v. Texas — IV-B. Purposeful treatment and statutory intent; O’Connor and all seven other Associates. Source-specific process relief: Hopwood v. Texas — IV-D. Source-specific relief and undecided defenses, all nine. 
- Preserved punitive-culpability alternatives: Hopwood, November 25, 1996, O’Connor and all seven other Associates. Hopwood v. Texas — IV-C. The punitive request on this record. 
- Separate prospective-exposure and equitable requirements: Hopwood, November 25, 1996, O’Connor for all nine. Hopwood v. Texas — V. Prospective relief and the bounded remand.

**Present operation effective:** Hopwood, November 25, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** No particular later holistic program is approved and no institution must use race. Prospective relief independently requires concrete future exposure, a supported record and an equitable basis. Existing federal-classification rules remain distinct. Punitive liability retains Smith v. Wade’s evil-motive-or-intent or reckless-or-callous-indifference alternatives; beneficial purpose is not a universal defense and purposeful treatment alone does not prove punitive culpability.

**Related current rules:** Dismantling a formerly segregated public university system supplies the State’s separate, causally bounded remedial duty.

**Holdings navigation:** Hopwood v. Texas (1996-11-25) — The established unequal admissions process; Hopwood v. Texas (1996-11-25) — A supported educational-diversity interest is constitutionally available; Hopwood v. Texas (1996-11-25) — Individual causation and the same-decision burden; Hopwood v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Program-wide athletic opportunity and independent sex-classification review

### Program-wide athletic opportunity and independent sex-classification review

**Governing question:** Title IX athletic accommodation, independent review of governmental sex classifications and corrective relief.

**Current rule:** Title IX does not guarantee a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Lawful separate teams remain subject to equal opportunity. Effective accommodation may be shown by any one of substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex’s interests and abilities; other regulatory duties remain independently binding. A public university’s sex-conscious restructuring separately must serve an actual important objective and be substantially related to it. Genuine fiscal or programmatic change may preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality, but ratios, budget pressure or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient. Stereotypes, pretext and measures materially broader than demonstrated need are forbidden.

For the full-accommodation route, actual demonstrated interests, abilities and competitive viability govern. Aggregate comparative interest cannot excuse viable unmet demand when neither proportionality nor continuing responsive expansion is satisfied; numerical imbalance alone does not establish liability or compel preferential treatment. Independent important-objective/substantial-relationship scrutiny also applies to sex-conscious federal implementation and judicial enforcement, without deeming a private recipient a state actor. After a proved athletics violation, the court must require prompt effective correction and initially permit the institution to choose among effective lawful means. It may reject an ineffective plan and impose necessary specific relief upon supported findings that institutional choice will not cure the violation; liability alone creates no entitlement to a selected team list.

**Authority by component:**

- Program-wide participation opportunity: Program-wide participation opportunity, 1995-05-22. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.
- Independent constitutional scrutiny: Independent constitutional scrutiny, 1995-05-22. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.
- Actual opportunity, federal-enforcement scrutiny and remedial choice: Cohen v. Brown University, Cohen v. Brown University — I. Effective accommodation concerns actual opportunity through three independent routes, Cohen v. Brown University — II. The challenged federal enforcement satisfies independent sex-classification scrutiny and Cohen v. Brown University — III. Effective relief preserves a genuine institutional choice before a necessary specific command, Scalia for all nine, 1997-06-23.

**Present operation effective:** Cohen v. Brown University, 1997-06-23, for federal-enforcement scrutiny and remedial choice; Kelley's alternative routes retain their 1995-05-22 operation.

**Holdings navigation:** Program-wide participation opportunity; Independent constitutional scrutiny. Cohen v. Brown University (1997-06-23) — Civil Rights / Equal athletic opportunity and sex-conscious restructuring.

## Standards and Tests selection: Program-specific prison comparisons and sex classifications

### Program-specific prison comparisons and sex classifications

**Governing question:** Equal-protection claims concerning particular prison program opportunities.

**Current rule:** Assess whether prisoners are similarly situated in relation to the challenged opportunity. Population, security, confinement duration, feasible delivery and actual resources may distinguish a particular program, but whole-institution differences cannot defeat every comparison categorically. An actual sex classification in terms or administration requires the State to establish an important objective and substantial relationship to the sex distinction. For a genuinely sex-neutral choice, plaintiffs must prove that it was made at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences for that sex; disparity and knowledge alone do not establish purpose, although circumstantial evidence may do so. Segregated housing does not settle which branch governs a program decision.

**Authority by component:**

- Opportunity-specific comparison and separate classification/purpose branches: Similarity must concern the challenged opportunity and Actual sex classifications and neutral decisions require distinct inquiries, 1995-12-04; O’Connor’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Klinger, 1995-12-04.

**Exceptions and limits:** No identical-services right, prison exemption from sex-classification scrutiny, impact presumption, least-restrictive-means duty or entitlement to a selected program is created. Unequal programs and separated housing alone do not establish liability; immunity, entity responsibility and remedies remain separate.

**Holdings navigation:** Klinger v. Department of Corrections.

## Standards and Tests selection: Effective correction of sex-based educational exclusion

### Effective correction of sex-based educational exclusion

**Governing question:** The adequacy of a proposed alternative remedy for an unconstitutional exclusion from a public educational opportunity.

**Current rule:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, considering material tangible and intangible benefits. A shared general purpose or equal annual public subsidy alone does not establish adequacy. Government must promptly eliminate the denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, retaining initial responsibility for lawful design under judicial supervision. Continued provision of the same public opportunity without an adequate lawful alternative requires consideration of qualified applicants without the unconstitutional categorical sex bar; promises of improvement do not permit indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Authority by component:**

- Substantive remedial adequacy and lawful government choice: Part II — Adequacy of the proposed alternative and Part III — Effective relief and lawful State choice, 1996-06-26; O’Connor, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven); Thomas did not participate.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Virginia / Virginia v. United States, 1996-06-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** Legitimate qualifications, privacy, physical training and operational accommodations remain available. No identical campus, numerical resource ratio, expenditure minimum, guaranteed career outcome, quota or categorical prohibition of all separate education is prescribed. Admission scrutiny remains demanding intermediate review: a genuine important objective and substantial relationship, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means; neither litigation timing alone nor lack of an original written rationale establishes pretext.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, 518 U.S. 515 (1996).

#### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1841.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

For the other questions in Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, decided June 12, 1995, No. 93-1841. see Federal Courts — Equal-competition standing and continuing injury (Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing).

##### The inherited federal remedial standard sustains this challenge

**Holding:** Under Metro Broadcasting, a benign federal racial classification supported by Congress must serve an important governmental objective and be substantially related to that objective; Fullilove retains its supported judgment recognizing Congress's remedial authority at the scope actually sustained there. Applying that meaningful review of purpose, fit and burdens, the Court affirms the judgment rejecting this preserved challenge to the subcontractor compensation arrangement, without deciding every certification route or conferring a general exemption on measures called benign.

**Proposition-level authority:** Merits portion of Justice Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this rule and bounded application, not merely the judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The optional incentive differs from STURAA's expenditure command and a mandatory subcontracting duty. The Court preserves Metro's actual federal standard and Fullilove's supported fractured judgment, without a general benign-purpose exemption, unconditional concessions, a finding of Gonzales's certification route, or approval of all future applications. The distinct certification and payment limits below remain; conflicting section 8(d) regulations are unresolved.

**Material application:** The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. DBE subcontracts must total more than 10% of the original prime contract; compensation is 10% of approved final DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE and 2% for two or more. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Other governmental, county or city certification is accepted individually only on the contracting officer's finding of an acceptable and viable certification program. Mandatory §8(a) review, urged but nonmandatory DOT state review, and the absence of an identified separate §8(d) review provision remain distinct. Gonzales's actual route is unknown. The Court does not find irrebuttability, lack of actual disadvantage, or resolve the conflicting §8(d) rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547 (1990): retains the important-objective/substantial-relationship standard for benign federal classifications at its actual scope.
- Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448 (1980): retains its supported remedial judgment and congressional-competence recognition; no single universal rationale is attributed to the fractured decision.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469 (1989): distinguished as state/local remedial classification law; its standard is not displaced or extended to erase Metro here.
- Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education, 476 U.S. 267 (1986): retains attention to evidence, remedial fit and burdens; remedial purpose alone supplies no exemption.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit judgment rejecting the preserved constitutional challenge, 5–4. No remand, program redesign, injunction, damages or award of a subcontract follows.

#### Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-783.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Program-wide participation opportunity

**Holding:** Title IX does not guarantee preservation of a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Lawful separate teams remain subject to equal opportunity; effective accommodation may be established through substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex's interests and abilities, with any one route sufficient and distinct regulatory requirements independently binding.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Separate teams are permitted for competitive-skill selection or contact sports while equal opportunity remains required. When a noncontact team is offered only to one sex, the excluded sex must receive tryouts if its athletic opportunities previously were limited. Section 1681(b) rejects required preference merely because of imbalance while permitting relevant statistics. Proportionality is an alternative safe harbor, not a compulsory quota. Men's participation remained above substantial proportionality after the cut; complete compliance for women and independently regulated facilities, coaching, equipment, scheduling and benefits are not decided or excused.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: applies lawful delegated interpretation; authorizes no quota contrary to statute.
- Martin v. Occupational Safety & Health Review Commission, 499 U.S. 144: applies reasonable regulatory interpretation within its terms.

##### Independent constitutional scrutiny

**Holding:** A public university may consider sex during genuine fiscal or programmatic restructuring to preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality only when the choice serves an actual important objective and is substantially related to it. Ratios, budget pressure or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient; stereotypes, pretext and measures materially broader than the demonstrated need are forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The record includes demonstrated earlier denied opportunities, persistent inequality and preservation of actual women's swimming places during genuine restructuring, not percentages alone. The rule operates symmetrically for a demonstrably disadvantaged sex. Sexual orientation, gender identity, transgender status and individual team eligibility are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applies intermediate scrutiny to sex-conscious treatment.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applies genuine-objective and connected-disadvantage requirements.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547: its racial-treatment analysis supplies no changed sex standard or federal-compliance immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm summary judgment for the University on the distinct Title IX and equal-protection claims. No reinstatement, damages or remand is ordered, and no broad section 1983 preclusion rule is adopted.

#### Klinger v. Department of Corrections

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 93-2928; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 31 F.3d 727.
**Decided:** December 4, 1995.

##### Comparison tied to the prison opportunity challenged

**Holding:** Prisoners' similarity for an equal-protection program claim must be assessed in relation to the treatment challenged. Population, security, confinement duration, feasible delivery and actual resource choices may distinguish a particular program; general differences between entire institutions cannot defeat every benefit comparison categorically.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous, as are the next two Klinger holdings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither identity of institutions nor duplication of the larger prison's entire program mix is required. Higher spending or more programs elsewhere does not alone prove constitutional injury.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hogan requires scrutiny of the actual sex distinction; Turner preserves genuine prison-administration constraints without exempting sex classifications from equality review.

##### Actual sex classifications and purposeful discrimination in neutral program choices

**Holding:** For a program decision that actually classifies by sex in its terms or administration, the State must establish an important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between the objective and the distinction. For a genuinely sex-neutral choice, plaintiffs must prove a decision made at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences for that sex; disparity and awareness alone do not establish purpose, though circumstantial proof may.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sex-separated housing does not itself classify each program choice. No written admission is required to prove discriminatory administration; no impact presumption, least-restrictive-means test or right to a chosen program follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Craig and Hogan supply classification scrutiny; Davis, Arlington Heights and Feeney preserve the effect/purpose distinction and circumstantial proof. Kelley requires independent constitutional scrutiny of sex-conscious allocation without exporting Title IX athletic alternatives; J.E.B.'s sex-classification principle applies without importing its jury-specific burden sequence.

##### Correction of both the deficient liability premise and categorical dismissal

**Holding:** Liability cannot stand merely on sex-separated housing and unequal programs without the necessary classification or purpose findings. Inadequate findings under that mistaken approach also do not justify dismissal under an overbroad whole-institution comparator rule; the preserved program claims require adjudication under the proper standards.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of existing liability; vacate the direction to dismiss and remand. Legally adequate findings may be used; further evidence or proceedings are neither automatically required nor forbidden. No liability, damages, funding formula or systemwide injunction is ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Title IX, independent access-to-courts claims, other male institutions excluded by the denied amendment, unpleaded funding claims, any subsequent final judgment and other-defendant judgments are not reopened. Gunter's individual responsibility and immunity, Clarke's official-capacity responsibility, damages and equitable relief remain distinct and undecided.

#### United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, 518 U.S. 515 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-1941 and 94-2107.
**Decided:** June 26, 1996.

##### Whether VMI’s categorical exclusion of women satisfies equal protection

**Holding:** A State excluding persons from a public educational opportunity expressly because of sex must establish a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the exclusion. Virginia has not carried that burden for VMI's categorical exclusion of women, including women able and willing to undertake its demanding program; the liability judgment in No. 94-2107 is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** Accepted benefits of adversative education and predicted effects of coeducation remain findings; their legal sufficiency is independently examined. General preferences attributed to most women do not justify denying consideration to capable willing women. A genuine present purpose need not have been stated in 1839 or contemporaneous writing; litigation timing alone proves no pretext.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Demanding intermediate scrutiny is unchanged; exceedingly persuasive justification describes the State’s burden, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. Real physical differences and appropriate accommodations remain relevant; no universal ban on single-sex education.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Liability judgment in No. 94-2107 affirmed, 7–1.

##### Whether full performance of VWIL’s approved plan cures the denied opportunity

**Holding:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, taking account of its material tangible and intangible benefits; a shared general objective or equal annual public subsidy does not alone establish adequacy. VWIL's approved design does not cure this exclusion even assuming full performance of its funding and alumni commitments, so the approval judgment in No. 94-1941 must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** The Court credits equal annual public subsidy, ordinary student cost, separate endowment and full promised alumni recruitment, placement, externship and advancement access. Even so, cumulative differences in integrated adversative/military training, accessible curriculum, facilities, resources, degree and institutional standing leave the opportunity materially unequal. The engineering alternative requires three Mary Baldwin years plus two at Washington University at private tuition without the proposed final-two-year public discount.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No breached promise or unreported implementation failure is found; no isolated disparity, fixed endowment ratio, identical campus or guaranteed career outcome is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** VWIL approval in No. 94-1941 reversed, 7–1; monitoring cannot cure a substantively inadequate design.

##### What effective relief and State choices remain

**Holding:** Virginia must promptly eliminate the established denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, with responsibility for lawful remedial design remaining initially with the State and implementation supervised below. If Virginia continues the same publicly supported VMI opportunity without establishing an adequate lawful alternative, qualified women must receive consideration without the categorical sex bar; the existence of VWIL or promises of future improvement cannot justify indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** State design responsibility survives within prompt effective correction; genuine privacy, physical-training and operational accommodations remain possible. A lawful change in State involvement differs from relabeling the same public institution, but no withdrawal proposal is preapproved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No abandonment of genuine qualifications, named-person admission, closure, quota, identical programs or particular physical standard is ordered. Private single-sex education, every parallel undergraduate or sex-conscious opportunity arrangement and independent Title IX claims are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remand for prompt effective cure without indefinite exclusion under the rejected plan; no precise admissions date, damages, special master or newly required hearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applied for important-objective and substantial-relationship scrutiny; the scrutiny tier is unchanged.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applied for the State's demanding burden and genuine justification for unequal allocation. Its nursing-school facts do not decide this integrated military program automatically, and its reservation of broader separate-education questions survives. A statutory admissions exemption supplies no constitutional permission.
- J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127: its prohibition on using sex as a conclusive individual-capacity proxy informs this application. Its peremptory-strike procedure is not made the proof framework for college admissions.
- Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois, decided May 22, 1995: its genuine-objective and substantial-fit rule remains governing; its permitted sex-conscious restructuring does not create an admissions exemption or require a compensatory objective for every lawful distinction.
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections, decided December 4, 1995: applied to the particular public opportunity. Material institutional differences remain relevant, but whole-institution difference supplies neither categorical immunity nor automatic liability.
- Sweatt v. Painter, 339 U.S. 629: its consideration of tangible and intangible educational opportunity informs remedial adequacy; its racial setting is not extended into a categorical ban on all single-sex education.
- Missouri v. Jenkins, decided June 12, 1995: applied for effective, violation-linked repair rather than mere program installation or generalized educational improvement; its distinct desegregation obligations are unchanged.
- Lewis v. Casey, decided June 24, 1996: its requirement of effective relief tied to the proved injury and genuine government choice of means informs the remand. Its prisoner-claim and prison-conditions statutory rules are not transferred to this admissions dispute.
No earlier holding is overruled.

#### Hopwood v. Texas

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 94-50664; related No. 94-50569 is outside the grant.

**Decided:** November 25, 1996.

##### The established unequal admissions process

**Holding:** The State’s racial admissions classifications must satisfy strict scrutiny: the asserted objective must be compelling and the means narrowly fitted to it. The 1992 law-school process fails because it subjected applicants to unjustified, separate racial decision systems rather than genuinely competitive individual consideration; unequal opportunity to compete is an injury without proof that an applicant would have received a seat.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not find that every index difference independently fails, that enrollment aspirations were fixed reserved seats, that race determined every final seat, or that any plaintiff would have been admitted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Croson and Wygant require concrete justification, narrow fit and attention to individual burdens for state classifications. Bakke supplies the distinction between separate allocation and competitive consideration. Adarand supplies equal-competition injury while its separate federal merits rule remains untouched.

##### A supported educational-diversity interest is constitutionally available

**Holding:** A public university’s interest in concrete educational benefits from a genuinely diverse student body can be compelling on a properly supported record. The university must establish the educational objective and demonstrate that its actual racial classification is narrowly fitted to it; a diversity label, racial balance, or an assumption that racial identity determines viewpoint does not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. Kennedy supplies a seventh vote to vacate the categorical prohibition without joining this affirmative rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular holistic program, universal admissions design or later Texas program is approved. The decision requires no racial consideration and leaves federal classifications, other educational levels and employment outside its scope.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bakke's five express Part V-C joins permitted properly designed competitive consideration and rejected an absolute race-use injunction; Powell's complete diversity rationale was not a prior majority. This decision newly adopts the six-Justice supported-diversity rule. Metro and Adarand retain their distinct federal scope.

##### The responsible State and the continuing dismantlement duty

**Holding:** A State that operated a legally segregated university system must eliminate traceable policies that continue to foster segregation when their effects can practicably be removed consistently with sound educational practices; good faith and facial neutrality alone do not discharge that duty, and the State bears the showing of dismantlement. The responsible actor cannot categorically be confined to the law school as an administrative unit, but a remedial racial classification requires a demonstrated connection and fit to a continuing wrong rather than generalized societal discrimination or racial imbalance alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No current Texas remnant or justified preference is found. Inquiry is confined to the lawful admissions counterfactual and properly presented relief in these four individual claims; no general structural suit is reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fordice and Ayers supply the State's continuing dismantlement duty and burden, including traceable interacting policies, continuing effects and practicable removal consistent with sound education. Croson and Wygant require supported wrong and fitted voluntary repair without invariably requiring a fresh formal adjudication. Jenkins preserves violation-linked and causally limited remedies.

##### Individual causation and the same-decision burden

**Holding:** Once these applicants have established the unconstitutional admissions process, the school must prove by a preponderance of the evidence that each applicant would not have been admitted under a specified constitutional process to defeat relief for the denied admission and its consequences. Plaintiffs still must establish the existence and amount of compensable loss under an available cause against a proper defendant; failure of the school’s defense yields neither an automatic damages amount nor unconditional admission.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-A, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the individual-remand disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The lawful counterfactual must permit the uses of race that controlling law permits. Elliott's uncommunicated offer is not treated as communicated or accepted, and Carvell's tuition testimony establishes no awarded sum. A successful same-decision defense does not erase unequal-process injury.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mt. Healthy and Arlington Heights supply the school-borne same-decision defense. Carey separates actual compensable loss from nominal recognition. Bakke's admission award depended on a burden concession absent here.

##### Purposeful treatment and statutory intent

**Holding:** Deliberately using race to give applicants unequal admissions treatment is intentional racial discrimination for the Title VI issue presented, even when officials act in good faith and intend educational or remedial benefits. That conclusion does not establish every element of a statutory remedy, a proper defendant, or a waiver of immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-B, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the component disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sections 1981 and 1983 and Title VI keep their separate remedial requirements. No independent index disparate-impact claim or claim belonging to the unsuccessful intervenors is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bakke's individual statutory protection is applied without collapsing purpose, justification and available remedies.

##### The punitive request on this record

**Holding:** Smith v. Wade permits punitive damages in its applicable civil-rights setting for evil motive or intent, or for reckless or callous indifference to federally protected rights; lack of malice alone is not the whole standard. The punitive denial is affirmed on this record’s accepted good-faith finding and unsettled legal setting, without establishing a universal good-purpose defense or deciding the availability of punitive relief under every pleaded statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-C, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with denial of punitive damages.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purposeful unequal treatment and punitive culpability are distinct. The decision adopts neither an animus-only standard nor a good-purpose immunity and decides no general punitive entitlement under every pleaded statute.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith v. Wade retains both evil motive or intent and reckless or callous indifference; the denial creates neither an animus-only rule nor benevolent-purpose immunity.

##### Source-specific relief and undecided defenses

**Holding:** The established unequal process supports declaratory and nominal recognition independently of proof of entitlement to admission, subject to the available cause, proper defendant and immunity rules. Preserve that relief and fee-free lawful reconsideration if still practically available, while remanding the remaining claim-specific questions; no compensation, admission, immunity waiver, or personal-capacity liability is awarded here.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-D, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Official-capacity pleadings do not become personal-capacity claims. Claim-specific statutory relief, proper defendants, Eleventh Amendment immunity, waiver, fees and any properly arising personal-immunity question remain for decision. Fee-free reconsideration is not a right to a seat.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Carey preserves nominal recognition without proved compensable harm. Adarand's competition injury does not establish entitlement to any particular remedy.

##### Prospective relief and the bounded remand

**Holding:** The refusal to issue a broad prospective injunction remains intact, and directions purporting to govern unexamined future programs beyond resolution of these 1992 claims are vacated. Any later request for prospective relief requires its own sufficiently concrete future exposure, supported record and equitable basis; neither the discontinued procedure nor the existence of retrospective claims alone supplies those requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The continued retrospective claims establish no future exposure. The Court decides no replacement program and enters no broad prospective injunction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Adarand preserves the distinction between past competitive injury and actual renewed exposure needed for prospective relief.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The 1992 process invalidity stands unanimously. The diversity prohibition is vacated 7-2 and the law-school-only remedial restriction 8-1, with the proposition-level coalitions stated above. Unanimously preserve the individual-remedy remand, purposeful-treatment disposition and punitive denial; preserve refusal of a broad injunction and vacate directions purporting to govern unexamined future programs. No compensatory sum or admission is awarded.

#### Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville, 508 U.S. 656 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1721.
**Decided:** June 14, 1993.

##### Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury

**Holding:** An association may challenge a governmental barrier preventing members who are able and ready to bid from competing on equal terms, without proving that a member would have won a contract, when a member has individual standing, the interest is germane to the association’s purpose, and neither the claim nor the requested relief requires individual-member participation. The unchallenged allegations accepted here establish the required connection; genuinely disputed elements remain subject to stage-appropriate proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Turner v. Fouche, 396 U.S. 346 — equal eligibility applied.
- Bakke, 438 U.S. 265 — opportunity injury distinguished from guaranteed selection.
- Warth, 422 U.S. 490 — concrete causal connection retained.
- Hunt, 432 U.S. 333 — association requirements retained.
- Lujan, June 12, 1992 — concrete injury and stage-specific proof preserved.

##### The replacement continues the controversy

**Holding:** Repeal does not moot this dispute because the successor’s sheltered-market method continues to exclude the association’s members from particular competitions on the challenged basis. Changes in groups, percentages and methods do not remove that injury; a replacement actually ending it could produce a different result.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, The replacement continues the controversy portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Stage-appropriate proof remains necessary for genuinely disputed elements. No equal-protection merits rule, guaranteed contract or categorical rule about every replacement enactment is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for merits and lawful relief concerning the operative ordinance; no automatic reinstatement of the repealed-ordinance injunction.
