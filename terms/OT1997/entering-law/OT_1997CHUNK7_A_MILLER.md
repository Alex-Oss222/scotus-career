#### United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, 518 U.S. 515 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-1941 and 94-2107.
**Decided:** June 26, 1996.

##### Whether VMI’s categorical exclusion of women satisfies equal protection

**Holding:** A State excluding persons from a public educational opportunity expressly because of sex must establish a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the exclusion. Virginia has not carried that burden for VMI's categorical exclusion of women, including women able and willing to undertake its demanding program; the liability judgment in No. 94-2107 is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** Accepted benefits of adversative education and predicted effects of coeducation remain findings; their legal sufficiency is independently examined. General preferences attributed to most women do not justify denying consideration to capable willing women. A genuine present purpose need not have been stated in 1839 or contemporaneous writing; litigation timing alone proves no pretext.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Demanding intermediate scrutiny is unchanged; exceedingly persuasive justification describes the State’s burden, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. Real physical differences and appropriate accommodations remain relevant; no universal ban on single-sex education.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Liability judgment in No. 94-2107 affirmed, 7–1.

##### Whether full performance of VWIL’s approved plan cures the denied opportunity

**Holding:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, taking account of its material tangible and intangible benefits; a shared general objective or equal annual public subsidy does not alone establish adequacy. VWIL's approved design does not cure this exclusion even assuming full performance of its funding and alumni commitments, so the approval judgment in No. 94-1941 must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** The Court credits equal annual public subsidy, ordinary student cost, separate endowment and full promised alumni recruitment, placement, externship and advancement access. Even so, cumulative differences in integrated adversative/military training, accessible curriculum, facilities, resources, degree and institutional standing leave the opportunity materially unequal. The engineering alternative requires three Mary Baldwin years plus two at Washington University at private tuition without the proposed final-two-year public discount.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No breached promise or unreported implementation failure is found; no isolated disparity, fixed endowment ratio, identical campus or guaranteed career outcome is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** VWIL approval in No. 94-1941 reversed, 7–1; monitoring cannot cure a substantively inadequate design.

##### What effective relief and State choices remain

**Holding:** Virginia must promptly eliminate the established denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, with responsibility for lawful remedial design remaining initially with the State and implementation supervised below. If Virginia continues the same publicly supported VMI opportunity without establishing an adequate lawful alternative, qualified women must receive consideration without the categorical sex bar; the existence of VWIL or promises of future improvement cannot justify indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** State design responsibility survives within prompt effective correction; genuine privacy, physical-training and operational accommodations remain possible. A lawful change in State involvement differs from relabeling the same public institution, but no withdrawal proposal is preapproved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No abandonment of genuine qualifications, named-person admission, closure, quota, identical programs or particular physical standard is ordered. Private single-sex education, every parallel undergraduate or sex-conscious opportunity arrangement and independent Title IX claims are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remand for prompt effective cure without indefinite exclusion under the rejected plan; no precise admissions date, damages, special master or newly required hearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applied for important-objective and substantial-relationship scrutiny; the scrutiny tier is unchanged.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applied for the State's demanding burden and genuine justification for unequal allocation. Its nursing-school facts do not decide this integrated military program automatically, and its reservation of broader separate-education questions survives. A statutory admissions exemption supplies no constitutional permission.
- J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127: its prohibition on using sex as a conclusive individual-capacity proxy informs this application. Its peremptory-strike procedure is not made the proof framework for college admissions.
- Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois, decided May 22, 1995: its genuine-objective and substantial-fit rule remains governing; its permitted sex-conscious restructuring does not create an admissions exemption or require a compensatory objective for every lawful distinction.
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections, decided December 4, 1995: applied to the particular public opportunity. Material institutional differences remain relevant, but whole-institution difference supplies neither categorical immunity nor automatic liability.
- Sweatt v. Painter, 339 U.S. 629: its consideration of tangible and intangible educational opportunity informs remedial adequacy; its racial setting is not extended into a categorical ban on all single-sex education.
- Missouri v. Jenkins, decided June 12, 1995: applied for effective, violation-linked repair rather than mere program installation or generalized educational improvement; its distinct desegregation obligations are unchanged.
- Lewis v. Casey, decided June 24, 1996: its requirement of effective relief tied to the proved injury and genuine government choice of means informs the remand. Its prisoner-claim and prison-conditions statutory rules are not transferred to this admissions dispute.
No earlier holding is overruled.

#### Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-783.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Program-wide participation opportunity

**Holding:** Title IX does not guarantee preservation of a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Lawful separate teams remain subject to equal opportunity; effective accommodation may be established through substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex's interests and abilities, with any one route sufficient and distinct regulatory requirements independently binding.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Separate teams are permitted for competitive-skill selection or contact sports while equal opportunity remains required. When a noncontact team is offered only to one sex, the excluded sex must receive tryouts if its athletic opportunities previously were limited. Section 1681(b) rejects required preference merely because of imbalance while permitting relevant statistics. Proportionality is an alternative safe harbor, not a compulsory quota. Men's participation remained above substantial proportionality after the cut; complete compliance for women and independently regulated facilities, coaching, equipment, scheduling and benefits are not decided or excused.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: applies lawful delegated interpretation; authorizes no quota contrary to statute.
- Martin v. Occupational Safety & Health Review Commission, 499 U.S. 144: applies reasonable regulatory interpretation within its terms.

##### Independent constitutional scrutiny

**Holding:** A public university may consider sex during genuine fiscal or programmatic restructuring to preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality only when the choice serves an actual important objective and is substantially related to it. Ratios, budget pressure or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient; stereotypes, pretext and measures materially broader than the demonstrated need are forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The record includes demonstrated earlier denied opportunities, persistent inequality and preservation of actual women's swimming places during genuine restructuring, not percentages alone. The rule operates symmetrically for a demonstrably disadvantaged sex. Sexual orientation, gender identity, transgender status and individual team eligibility are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applies intermediate scrutiny to sex-conscious treatment.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applies genuine-objective and connected-disadvantage requirements.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547: its racial-treatment analysis supplies no changed sex standard or federal-compliance immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm summary judgment for the University on the distinct Title IX and equal-protection claims. No reinstatement, damages or remand is ordered, and no broad section 1983 preclusion rule is adopted.

#### Klinger v. Department of Corrections

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 93-2928; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 31 F.3d 727.
**Decided:** December 4, 1995.

##### Comparison tied to the prison opportunity challenged

**Holding:** Prisoners' similarity for an equal-protection program claim must be assessed in relation to the treatment challenged. Population, security, confinement duration, feasible delivery and actual resource choices may distinguish a particular program; general differences between entire institutions cannot defeat every benefit comparison categorically.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous, as are the next two Klinger holdings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither identity of institutions nor duplication of the larger prison's entire program mix is required. Higher spending or more programs elsewhere does not alone prove constitutional injury.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hogan requires scrutiny of the actual sex distinction; Turner preserves genuine prison-administration constraints without exempting sex classifications from equality review.

##### Actual sex classifications and purposeful discrimination in neutral program choices

**Holding:** For a program decision that actually classifies by sex in its terms or administration, the State must establish an important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between the objective and the distinction. For a genuinely sex-neutral choice, plaintiffs must prove a decision made at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences for that sex; disparity and awareness alone do not establish purpose, though circumstantial proof may.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sex-separated housing does not itself classify each program choice. No written admission is required to prove discriminatory administration; no impact presumption, least-restrictive-means test or right to a chosen program follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Craig and Hogan supply classification scrutiny; Davis, Arlington Heights and Feeney preserve the effect/purpose distinction and circumstantial proof. Kelley requires independent constitutional scrutiny of sex-conscious allocation without exporting Title IX athletic alternatives; J.E.B.'s sex-classification principle applies without importing its jury-specific burden sequence.

##### Correction of both the deficient liability premise and categorical dismissal

**Holding:** Liability cannot stand merely on sex-separated housing and unequal programs without the necessary classification or purpose findings. Inadequate findings under that mistaken approach also do not justify dismissal under an overbroad whole-institution comparator rule; the preserved program claims require adjudication under the proper standards.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of existing liability; vacate the direction to dismiss and remand. Legally adequate findings may be used; further evidence or proceedings are neither automatically required nor forbidden. No liability, damages, funding formula or systemwide injunction is ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Title IX, independent access-to-courts claims, other male institutions excluded by the denied amendment, unpleaded funding claims, any subsequent final judgment and other-defendant judgments are not reopened. Gunter's individual responsibility and immunity, Clarke's official-capacity responsibility, damages and equitable relief remain distinct and undecided.

#### Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1424.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### What evidence of Article III injury, causation and redressability is required at summary judgment

**Holding:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury, a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, and a likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. At summary judgment, the plaintiff must supply specific facts by affidavit or other evidence on the challenged elements; favorable reasonable inferences remain available, but allegations cannot substitute for the missing evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the elements and litigation-stage evidentiary requirements. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining this section.

**Material application:** Earlier survival of the pleading stage does not establish the specific facts needed at summary judgment. The Court resolves this action on insufficient threatened personal injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general redressability element controls. Its application to absent funding agencies and uncertain foreign-project responses appears only in the three-Justice Part III-B and is not controlling. Favorable reasonable inferences remain available; the decision does not replace Rule 56 or the separate APA programmatic-review holding of National Wildlife Federation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The merits declaration, mandatory rulemaking relief and dependent §1540(g)(4) fee award are vacated, and the action is remanded for jurisdictional dismissal. Six Justices support dismissal for lack of standing; seven support reversal, with Stevens relying on a noncontrolling statutory ground. No independent fee-entitlement rule or substitute award is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975): applied for the plaintiff's personal stake and burden to establish standing; its statutory-rights discussion remains subject to concrete injury.
- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied for the constitutional elements and the limits on generalized judicial supervision.
- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976): applied for fair traceability and likely redress; no new majority application concerning these absent agencies follows.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied for the evidentiary distinction between pleading and summary judgment; its separate APA programmatic-review holding is not enlarged.
- Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986): applied to require evidence of challenged essential elements while preserving genuine material disputes and reasonable inferences.

##### Whether past visits, indefinite return intentions and general wildlife interests establish imminent personal injury

**Holding:** Aesthetic, recreational and professional interests in observing wildlife can support standing, but these plaintiffs must show threatened interference with their own concrete use. Prior visits and an indefinite intention to return, without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure, do not establish standing for these prospective claims; an ecosystem, animal or professional connection that supplies only general interest in distant species does not cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone's separate concurrence supplies a sixth vote for the record-specific personal-injury conclusion without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** The affidavits describe visits to Egyptian and Sri Lankan project areas but no sufficiently definite return undertaking or continuing activity showing imminent exposure. General ecosystem, animal and professional connections supply no missing evidence of these members' own threatened use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document prerequisite is imposed. Actual use of an affected ecosystem and demonstrated injury transmitted across geographic distance remain undecided. Aesthetic, recreational and professional harm remains cognizable when it affects the claimant's own concrete activity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jurisdictional dismissal leaves the statute's geographic reach unresolved and does not preclude a later action supported by sufficient standing facts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): applied; aesthetic injury is cognizable, but an organization's special interest cannot replace injury to a member.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977): applied to require an injured member for this associational claim; no other associational requirement is changed.
- Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95 (1983): applied for the distinction between past exposure and a present or imminent injury supporting prospective relief.
- Whitmore v. Arkansas, 495 U.S. 149 (1990): applied for actual or imminent rather than conjectural injury.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied to specific affected use; no categorical bar to geographically transmitted injury is established.
- Japan Whaling Association v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): distinguished because its whale-watching and studying members alleged an adverse effect on their own activities; concrete professional or ecological injury remains possible.

##### Whether ESA citizen-suit permission and an asserted procedural right replace threatened concrete injury

**Holding:** ESA §11(g)'s authorization for any person to sue does not give Article III standing to a plaintiff asserting only the public's common interest in lawful administration. A procedural right may protect a plaintiff's threatened concrete interest without requiring proof that the procedure will certainly change the ultimate agency decision, but the procedural label cannot replace that personal interest; these respondents have not shown the required connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the citizen-suit and concrete-interest rule. Stone does not join this section.

**Material application:** Section 11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), permits enforcement but identifies no concrete injury to these members independent of the unsupported future use. The public's common interest in lawful administration cannot supply the missing personal connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may recognize concrete factual injuries previously inadequate in law; the outer reach of that power is not settled. Procedural protections tied to a threatened concrete interest remain cognizable without certainty of a changed ultimate decision. Neither §7(a)(2)'s geographic reach nor the binding effect of a revised rule on absent agencies is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The action is dismissed for want of Article III standing; no new consultation rule or cancellation of any foreign project is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126 (1922): applied to reject a general right to compel lawful administration as a sufficient personal injury.
- Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447 (1923), and Ex parte Levitt, 302 U.S. 633 (1937): applied for the distinction between direct injury and an undifferentiated public grievance.
- United States v. Richardson, 418 U.S. 166 (1974), and Schlesinger v. Reservists Committee to Stop the War, 418 U.S. 208 (1974): applied to generally shared governmental grievances without distinctive concrete harm.
- Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United for Separation of Church and State, Inc., 454 U.S. 464 (1982), and Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied; a claimed right to lawful government conduct alone does not satisfy Article III.
- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), and Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): preserved for Congress's power to recognize injuries and broaden protected interests while retaining the plaintiff's own concrete injury.
- Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Insurance Co., 409 U.S. 205 (1972), and Hardin v. Kentucky Utilities Co., 390 U.S. 1 (1968): distinguished as statutory recognition of concrete personal interests, not authorization of a wholly abstract grievance.

### Effective correction of sex-based educational exclusion

**Governing question:** The adequacy of a proposed alternative remedy for an unconstitutional exclusion from a public educational opportunity.

**Current rule:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, considering material tangible and intangible benefits. A shared general purpose or equal annual public subsidy alone does not establish adequacy. Government must promptly eliminate the denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, retaining initial responsibility for lawful design under judicial supervision. Continued provision of the same public opportunity without an adequate lawful alternative requires consideration of qualified applicants without the unconstitutional categorical sex bar; promises of improvement do not permit indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Authority by component:**

- Substantive remedial adequacy and lawful government choice: Part II — Adequacy of the proposed alternative and Part III — Effective relief and lawful State choice, 1996-06-26; O’Connor, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven); Thomas did not participate.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Virginia / Virginia v. United States, 1996-06-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** Legitimate qualifications, privacy, physical training and operational accommodations remain available. No identical campus, numerical resource ratio, expenditure minimum, guaranteed career outcome, quota or categorical prohibition of all separate education is prescribed. Admission scrutiny remains demanding intermediate review: a genuine important objective and substantial relationship, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means; neither litigation timing alone nor lack of an original written rationale establishes pretext.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, 518 U.S. 515 (1996).

### Program-wide athletic opportunity and independent sex-classification review

**Governing question:** Title IX athletic accommodation, independent review of governmental sex classifications and corrective relief.

**Current rule:** Title IX does not guarantee a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Lawful separate teams remain subject to equal opportunity. Effective accommodation may be shown by any one of substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex’s interests and abilities; other regulatory duties remain independently binding. A public university’s sex-conscious restructuring separately must serve an actual important objective and be substantially related to it. Genuine fiscal or programmatic change may preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality, but ratios, budget pressure or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient. Stereotypes, pretext and measures materially broader than demonstrated need are forbidden.

For the full-accommodation route, actual demonstrated interests, abilities and competitive viability govern. Aggregate comparative interest cannot excuse viable unmet demand when neither proportionality nor continuing responsive expansion is satisfied; numerical imbalance alone does not establish liability or compel preferential treatment. Independent important-objective/substantial-relationship scrutiny also applies to sex-conscious federal implementation and judicial enforcement, without deeming a private recipient a state actor. After a proved athletics violation, the court must require prompt effective correction and initially permit the institution to choose among effective lawful means. It may reject an ineffective plan and impose necessary specific relief upon supported findings that institutional choice will not cure the violation; liability alone creates no entitlement to a selected team list.

**Authority by component:**

- Program-wide participation opportunity: Program-wide participation opportunity, 1995-05-22. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.
- Independent constitutional scrutiny: Independent constitutional scrutiny, 1995-05-22. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.
- Actual opportunity, federal-enforcement scrutiny and remedial choice: Cohen v. Brown University, Cohen v. Brown University — I. Effective accommodation concerns actual opportunity through three independent routes, Cohen v. Brown University — II. The challenged federal enforcement satisfies independent sex-classification scrutiny and Cohen v. Brown University — III. Effective relief preserves a genuine institutional choice before a necessary specific command, Scalia for all nine, 1997-06-23.

**Present operation effective:** Cohen v. Brown University, 1997-06-23, for federal-enforcement scrutiny and remedial choice; Kelley's alternative routes retain their 1995-05-22 operation.

### Program-specific prison comparisons and sex classifications

**Governing question:** Equal-protection claims concerning particular prison program opportunities.

**Current rule:** Assess whether prisoners are similarly situated in relation to the challenged opportunity. Population, security, confinement duration, feasible delivery and actual resources may distinguish a particular program, but whole-institution differences cannot defeat every comparison categorically. An actual sex classification in terms or administration requires the State to establish an important objective and substantial relationship to the sex distinction. **Authority by component:**

**Exceptions and limits:** No identical-services right, prison exemption from sex-classification scrutiny, impact presumption, least-restrictive-means duty or entitlement to a selected program is created. ### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, for the identified citizen-suit and procedural-right clarification; ordinary Article III elements and evidentiary burdens are retained.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

**Holdings navigation:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992) — Standing and personal injury.
