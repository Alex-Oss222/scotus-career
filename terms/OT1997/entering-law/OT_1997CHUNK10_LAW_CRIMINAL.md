## Standards and Tests selection: Knowing unlawfulness in willful currency structuring

### Knowing unlawfulness in willful currency structuring

**Governing question:** What §5322(a)’s willfulness requires for the charged §5324(3) structuring offense.

**Current rule:** The Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowledge of the financial institution’s reporting duty and a purpose to evade it. Reasonable circumstantial inferences may establish knowledge; knowledge of a section number or having read the Code is unnecessary. If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity on that additional knowledge requirement, lenity independently requires this narrower criminal construction; conflicting history or general purpose cannot enlarge the crime beyond sufficiently clear enacted language.

**Authority by component:**

- Knowledge elements and proof: Ratzlaf v. United States — Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful, 1994-01-11. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of nine participants adopt the rule and instructional-error disposition.
- Independent residual-lenity ground: Ratzlaf v. United States — Residual ambiguity independently requires the narrower criminal construction, 1994-01-11. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; the same six Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding.

**Present operation effective:** Ratzlaf v. United States, 1994-01-11.

**Exceptions and limits:** The §5322(a) exception for §5315 and its regulations is separate from §5324(3). This is no universal ignorance-of-law defense, legal-proceeds requirement or civil-forfeiture rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful.

## Standards and Tests selection: Knowledge of machinegun characteristics in unregistered possession

### Knowledge of machinegun characteristics in unregistered possession

**Governing question:** What must be proved when a conventional-looking rifle falls within the whole-weapon machinegun definition.

**Current rule:** For §5861(d) possession of an unregistered rifle prosecuted as a whole-weapon machinegun under §5845(b), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the characteristics making it a machinegun: it shoots, is designed to shoot, or can readily be restored to shoot automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, by a single function of the trigger. Knowledge that the weapon is unregistered or that possession is illegal is not required. Ordinary possession of a rifle is not enough to presume knowledge of its automatic-fire characteristics.

**Authority by component:**

- Knowledge of the regulated weapon characteristics: Staples v. United States — Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun, 1994-05-23. Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, supplies five direct joins to this rule and its explanation. Ginsburg, joined by O'Connor, independently concurs in the judgment on the same narrow factual-knowledge requirement; the Court's authority does not depend on combining different rationales under Marks.

**Present operation effective:** Staples v. United States, 1994-05-23.

**Open question:** The holding does not decide every separately defined machinegun part or combination, every other regulated weapon, or the sufficiency of proof in every conversion setting.

**Holdings navigation:** Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun.

## Standards and Tests selection: Criminal fair warning under §242

### Criminal fair warning under §242

**Governing question:** Whether pre-existing law gave adequate warning that an official's charged conduct was constitutionally unlawful under 18 U.S.C. §242.

**Current rule:** The statute alone or as authoritatively construed must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; novel or unforeseeable criminal applications are forbidden. Relevant pre-existing law may include lower-court authority, without a Supreme-Court-only requirement, automatic sufficiency of a single lower decision or fixed numerical consensus. An obviously covered application needs no factually matching precedent, but abstract invocation of liberty or bodily security does not settle an actually unresolved application. Criminal fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Lanier — Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted, 1997-03-31; Stone-Zsela for all nine supplies conduct-time notice and the limit on reliance on bad purpose
- United States v. Lanier — Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition supplies the permissible legal landscape
- United States v. Lanier — Obvious applications need no factual twin, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition rejects a factual-twin requirement while preserving unsettled boundaries
- United States v. Lanier — The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition identifies the shared clarity protection without merging criminal liability

**Present operation effective:** Lanier, 1997-03-31.

**Exceptions and limits:** Willful bad purpose cannot cure an unknowable constitutional prohibition. The Government must still establish constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness and every other element under the criminal burden. Due-process rights and direct official assaults outside custody are not categorically excluded, but no particular count's merits or notice is predetermined.

**Related current rules:** Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review retains its civil review, timing and preservation mechanics.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Lanier (1997-03-31) — Criminal Procedure / Criminal civil-rights liability and fair warning.

## Standards and Tests selection: Firearms offered as consideration in a drug transaction

### Firearms offered as consideration in a drug transaction

**Governing question:** What instructions govern firearms offered as consideration in a drug transaction?

**Current rule:** Under the applicable §924(c)(1), use requires actually and actively employing the firearm as an operative instrument of the predicate offense. Firing or employment as a weapon is unnecessary; offering a firearm as consideration in a drug exchange is use despite its employment as merchandise. Possession, proximity, accessibility, protective storage, emboldening or placement for possible later employment does not by itself satisfy use. The separate during-and-in-relation-to condition requires a purpose or effect respecting trafficking, not accident or coincidence: at minimum facilitation or potential facilitation. Offering the gun as consideration satisfies that relation. Use or carrying must be established independently of the offense connection; actual or potential facilitation alone does not establish active employment. The outer boundary of sufficient facilitation and a comprehensive definition of carrying remain open. No general possession offense, retroactivity rule or automatic remedial consequence is adopted.

**Authority by component:**

- Offering a firearm in exchange for drugs is use within the charged statute: Smith v. United States — Offering a firearm in exchange for drugs is use within the charged statute, 1993-06-01. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.
- The offered firearm has the required connection to the trafficking crime: Smith v. United States — The offered firearm has the required connection to the trafficking crime, 1993-06-01. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

- Active employment and separate conduct/connection requirements: Use requires active employment of the firearm; Connection to the offense cannot substitute for using or carrying, 1995-12-06. O'Connor Part I, joined by all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Bailey and Robinson, 1995-12-06, defining active employment and preserving Smith’s separate relation requirement.

**Holdings navigation:** Smith v. United States, 508 U.S. 223 (1993); Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States, 516 U.S. 137.

## Scope

Hopkins; Muscarello / Cleveland; Bryan. Each authority retains its own scope, date and qualifications; inclusion does not decide its application.

#### Bowersox v. Williams, 517 U.S. 345

**Docket or dockets:** Application No. A-828.
**Decided:** April 9, 1996, final application disposition.

##### The identified grounds do not sustain this successive-petition execution stay

**Holding:** A stay pending a second or successive federal habeas petition requires substantial grounds on which relief might be granted. On the identified claims and expressly bounded assumptions below, no sufficient ground sustains this stay, which is vacated under abuse-of-discretion review.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–V, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: five Justices adopt all following propositions at the limited application-assessment level. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Petition count, certification, scheduled argument and denied rehearing do not establish substantial grounds automatically; an unexplained stay does not establish their absence. The first petition's nonexhaustion dismissal was not a merits denial; the second's actual merits adjudication bears on the repeated instruction ground. No universal appellate-explanation duty, habeas merits judgment or categorical bar to further review is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Grant the application and vacate the March 8 Eighth Circuit stay, 5–4. No substitute Supreme Court stay, fixed grace period, writ, appeal dismissal, execution direction or finding of execution is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Barefoot and Delo v. Stokes supply the successive-petition substantial-grounds inquiry and review of its actual basis. Barefoot's initial-certified-appeal and certiorari tests retain their distinct triggers; Delo v. Blair's equal division supplies no controlling rationale. Gomez, McFarland and Lonchar preserve independent equity and actual necessary-process protection but establish no unidentified further obligation or necessary interval here.

##### The reported death preference supplies no substantial cause ground by itself

**Holding:** A death preference leading a prisoner to withhold permission for a sentence challenge does not by itself establish an external objective factor preventing presentation. On the reported account, that issue supplies no substantial available ground for maintaining this stay.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, the same five Justices.

**Material application:** Contrary evidence that prison conditions caused the preference remains relevant, but preference alone does not establish preventive impairment. The reported no-causation finding, conditions litigation and representation that legitimate sentence challenges were raised support the limited assessment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No expert-credibility, incapacity, independent ineffective-assistance, undisclosed-mitigation or constitutional-prejudice finding is made. The 1990 opportunity bears on diligence without a categorical duty to request remand. Clear, particular State abuse pleading must precede the prisoner's burden; missing filings establish no defect or concession.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** McCleskey distinguishes an actual external impediment from inexcusable neglect; intentional abandonment is not indispensable to abuse.

##### Genuine supported noncapital options defeat the asserted all-or-nothing instruction ground

**Holding:** Where genuine, evidence-supported noncapital verdict choices are supplied, Beck does not require every supported lesser-offense instruction. Williams's ordinary second-degree murder and manslaughter options defeat the all-or-nothing premise, and old trial evidence and prior judicial disagreement do not independently require redetermination or sustain this stay.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, the same five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Nominal unsupported alternatives would not suffice. Both trial parties requested the omitted first-degree instruction, and disputed intent, kidnapping and witness evidence remain real disputes, not conclusive prosecution proof. No new evidence, changed Missouri law or separately current first-degree equal-protection claim is supplied.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Schad Part III supplies the genuine-supported-alternative limit; Beck and Hopper preserve the rational lesser-conviction-and-greater-acquittal requirement. Sanders retains constrained reconsideration discretion and the applicant's ends-of-justice burden, but discretion creates neither a federal merit nor an obligation to reopen the previously adjudicated ground.

##### The bounded second-degree instruction assumptions do not establish substantial due-process or equal-protection grounds

**Holding:** Assuming the omitted second-degree felony-murder instruction was available under then-applicable state rules, differently withheld from materially similar cases and procedurally available for review, that limited inconsistency does not present a substantial Hicks arbitrary-deprivation claim when genuine supported noncapital options remain without a separate posited defect. Separately, the assumed differing treatment alone supplies no substantial unconstitutional unequal-administration ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV, the same five Justices, independently adopting both constitutional assessments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** These are assessment assumptions, not merits findings. State-law availability alone does not establish a mandatory instructional duty. The Court requires no protected class, finds no absence of discriminatory purpose, and announces no general immunity for arbitrary state adjudication. The second-degree theories remain distinct from the prior first-degree ground; ultimate habeas relief is undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Schad resolves the Beck demand for an additional option, not by itself the separate constitutional theories. Hicks protects state-created opportunities from arbitrary deprivation; its invalid mandatory rule removed statutory jury discretion, unlike the bounded omission leaving genuine lawful noncapital choices here.

##### Missing comparison files and the proposed late new subject do not sustain this stay

**Holding:** An allegation of approximately two hundred missing comparison files does not by itself establish arbitrary deprivation of Missouri's mandated proportionality-review opportunity. Introducing that distinct statewide comparative-review dispute after completed discovery and preparation of the magistrate report, when the district court was ready to rule, supplies an apparent discretionary justification for declining expansion; elapsed years alone would not.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, the same five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The state opinion reports mandated review and actual comparison of crime and defendant. Neither every missing file's irrelevance nor the sufficiency of every stated review is found. No bad faith, specific prejudice, early discoverability or actual district rationale is attributed. A meritorious newly discovered deprivation could warrant amendment despite the stage, but that predicate is not established; no categorical deadline is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pulley rejects an invariable federal comparative-proportionality right without displacing Hicks's protection of state-created opportunities. Foman preserves liberal amendment while allowing a justification apparent in the record or declared by the court; Habeas Rule 11 permits civil rules only when appropriate and consistent with habeas rules.

Pre-AEDPA Rule 9(b) distinguishes repeated grounds after merits determination from new grounds omitted through abuse. Section 2244(b)'s refusal permission follows federal denial after a material factual evidentiary hearing or legal merits hearing; the exception requires an unadjudicated ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; permission is not mandatory dismissal. No delay-prejudice, exhaustion or undisclosed-default ground is added. Schlup's requirement of reliable new evidence, assessed with the whole probative record, showing that more likely than not no reasonable properly instructed juror would convict, and Sawyer's requirement of clear and convincing proof that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find death eligibility, are not satisfied. Discretionary mitigation alone is insufficient under Sawyer. Neither gateway establishes an underlying violation or automatic relief, and the available second-degree claim requires neither gateway. No new §2251 construction is adopted.

#### United States v. Gaudin

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-514.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### The jury decides application of the materiality element

**Holding:** When materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense, the Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to determine it beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge states statutory meaning and the governing legal standard; labeling its application a mixed question does not remove an element from the jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materiality concerns a statement's natural tendency or capacity to influence the relevant governmental decision. The Government conceded that it was an element and did not contest the lower court's error classifications. Clause-specific materiality requirements, universal structural-error treatment and exact unverified preservation history remain undecided. Statutory interpretation and threshold evidentiary sufficiency remain judicial functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- In re Winship: applies proof beyond a reasonable doubt to facts establishing guilt.
- Sinclair v. United States: overruled to the extent it assigns a criminal pertinency element to the judge; unrelated propositions are not displaced.
- Kungys v. United States: distinguishes civil denaturalization and its appellate allocation.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana: preserves the actual existing constitutional jury/burden holding; this case creates no universal omitted-element error classification.
- Hubbard v. United States: preserves §1001 entity coverage at its actual scope; supplies no authority to remove HUD offense elements from a jury.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of the reviewed false-statement convictions. Lawful retrial submitting every element, including materiality, to the jury remains available; no acquittal, disposition of the separate equity-skimming conviction or sentence calculation follows.

#### Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States, 516 U.S. 137

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-7448 and 94-7492.
**Decided:** December 6, 1995.
**Companion or consolidated matters:** Both reviewed §924(c) use convictions receive the same statutory construction and bounded carrying remand.

##### Active employment under the use prong of §924(c)(1)

**Holding:** Use requires actual, active employment of the firearm as an operative instrument of the predicate offense. Possession, proximity, accessibility, protective storage, emboldening or placement for possible later employment alone is insufficient; firing or use as a weapon is unnecessary, and Smith's actual firearm barter remains use.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** Bailey's gun was stored in a locked vehicle trunk and Robinson's in her bedroom near drug proceeds. The possession-and-availability use theories and instructions cannot sustain the appellate affirmances.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No closed list of uses, full carrying definition, retroactivity rule or additional knowledge-of-illegality element is adopted. Distinct possession offenses remain untouched.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith's barter holding is applied while resolving its previously unaddressed passive-possession boundary; its original five-Justice authority is unchanged. Staples and Ratzlaf preserve the elements Congress enacted without creating a new mental element here or displacing ordinary interpretation with automatic lenity.

##### Independent proof of conduct and relation to the offense

**Holding:** Use or carrying must be established independently of occurrence during and in relation to the predicate offense. Actual or potential facilitation may establish the required relation under Smith but does not make mere presence or availability into active use.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith requires a trafficking purpose or effect beyond accident or coincidence and separately supplies active use through the actual exchange. Harris requires independent §924(c) conduct even when the predicate offense itself involves a firearm; its carjacking and cumulative-punishment holdings are unchanged.

##### Bounded consideration of an independently available carrying ground

**Holding:** The use-based appellate affirmances are reversed, and the D.C. Circuit may consider a legally sufficient and properly available carrying basis for either firearm conviction. Carrying language in the charges and instructions establishes neither lawful determination of all elements, harmlessness nor permission for a particular remedial course.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela agrees to remand but dissents from retaining the trial convictions for this consideration without first vacating them.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse each use-based appellate affirmance unanimously; remand without immediate Supreme Court vacatur of the trial firearm convictions, 8–1. No carrying conviction, acquittal or retrial is ordered. Separate drug and felon-possession judgments remain intact; no sufficiency, waiver or harmlessness determination follows from instruction excerpts.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Robinson's aiding-and-abetting route requires an actual substantive firearm offense and intentional assistance; presence, association or awareness alone is insufficient. A charge label supplies neither element nor procedural availability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harris's independent-conduct and intentional-assistance rules remain operative during the permitted carrying review; no new carrying or automatic remedy rule is announced.

#### Ratzlaf v. United States, 510 U.S. 135

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1196.

**Decided:** 1994-01-11.

##### Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful

**Holding:** For the charged offense under 31 U.S.C. §§5322(a) and 5324(3), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowing the financial institution's reporting duty and acting for the purpose of evading it. Knowledge may be established through reasonable circumstantial inferences; the prosecution need not prove knowledge of a section number or that the defendant read the Code.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of nine participants adopt the rule and instructional-error disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Spies v. United States, 317 U.S. 492 (1943): applied for contextual interpretation of willfulness and permissible circumstantial proof.
- Cheek v. United States, 498 U.S. 192 (1991): its recognition of a statutory known-duty requirement informs this distinct scheme; its tax rule is not made universal.
- Liparota v. United States, 471 U.S. 419 (1985): applied for context-sensitive knowledge requirements where apparently lawful conduct may otherwise be swept into a crime.
- Potter v. United States, 155 U.S. 438 (1894): the criminal statute's separate willfulness term receives operative effect rather than disappearing into other elements.

**Independent alternative holding:** If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity about whether §5322(a) requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful, the rule of lenity independently requires that narrower construction of this charged criminal offense. Legislative purpose and conflicting legislative history do not justify dispensing with the additional knowledge requirement when the enacted criminal language does not clearly do so.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; the same six Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hughey v. United States, 495 U.S. 411 (1990): applied to resolve residual criminal statutory ambiguity in favor of the accused.
- Crandon v. United States, 494 U.S. 152 (1990): applied to prevent policy or legislative materials from enlarging a crime beyond sufficiently clear text.
- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336 (1971): applied for fair warning and Congress's responsibility to define criminal liability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The bank's reporting duty and the customer's structuring prohibition are distinct. Section 5322(a)'s exception for §5315 and its regulations remains an exception to the penalty provision, not to §5324(3). No universal ignorance-of-law defense, requirement that proceeds be lawful or civil-forfeiture rule is announced.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed Ninth Circuit judgment for both Waldemar and Loretta Ratzlaf is reversed and remanded. Waldemar's conspiracy, four structuring and interstate-travel counts and Loretta's conspiracy and interstate-travel counts require count-specific implementation of the erroneous instruction; no count is declared automatically invalid. Hunt's convictions are outside review. No acquittal or unconditional discharge is ordered.

#### Staples v. United States, 511 U.S. 600

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1441.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### Knowledge of the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun

**Holding:** To convict a defendant under 26 U.S.C. §5861(d) for possessing an unregistered machinegun in the circumstances presented here, the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the characteristics bringing the rifle within the complete-weapon machinegun definition—a weapon that shoots, is designed to shoot, or can be readily restored to shoot automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, by a single function of the trigger; circumstantial evidence may establish that knowledge. It need not prove knowledge of nonregistration, the registration requirement, or illegality, and the omitted-element instruction requires reversal and remand without a finding of evidentiary insufficiency or an order of acquittal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, supplies five direct joins to this rule and its explanation. Ginsburg, joined by O'Connor, independently concurs in the judgment on the same narrow factual-knowledge requirement; the Court's authority does not depend on combining different rationales under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morissette v. United States, 342 U.S. 246 (1952): applies the presumption of criminal fault despite statutory silence.
- United States v. United States Gypsum Co., 438 U.S. 422 (1978): applies contextual examination before dispensing with fault.
- United States v. Freed, 401 U.S. 601 (1971): distinguishes knowledge of the object's regulated characteristics from knowledge of nonregistration; its registration holding remains intact.
- United States v. Balint, 258 U.S. 250 (1922): preserves the bounded public-welfare category without extending its narcotics construction automatically to this rifle.
- Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994: distinguishes its express-willfulness and knowledge-of-illegality rule; no universal legal-knowledge requirement follows.
- United States v. Thompson/Center Arms Co., June 8, 1992: leaves its making rule and limited parts-package disposition unchanged; neither decides this possession element.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The separate frame/receiver, conversion-part, conversion-parts and one-person-controlled assembly-parts branches of §5845(b) remain undecided. Section 5845(a)'s antique exclusion and its separate exclusion for Secretary-designated collector's items unlikely to be used as weapons remain; the collector exclusion does not encompass machineguns or destructive devices and depends on manufacture date, value, design and other characteristics. No exclusion is established for this rifle. No Second Amendment, facial validity, ultimate knowledge or error-review result is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit is reversed and remanded, 7–2, for the required factual-knowledge instruction. No acquittal, mandatory retrial or new sentence is ordered; properly available sufficiency and error-review questions retain their own requirements.

#### United States v. Lanier

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1717.
**Decided:** March 31, 1997.

##### What law must supply fair warning under 18 U.S.C. §242

**Holding:** Section 242, read with authoritative constructions, must have made the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably apparent when the defendant acted. Neither a later decision nor willful bad purpose can supply missing conduct-time notice, and novel or unforeseeable criminal enlargement is forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Moral reprehensibility and state criminality do not replace a definite federal right and notice. Vagueness, lenity, and the bar on unexpected retrospective enlargement retain related but distinct functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* supplies definite-right and separate willfulness requirements; courts construe the criminal statute rather than create common-law offenses.

##### Whether notice requires a Supreme Court decision on fundamentally similar facts

**Holding:** Fair warning may come from relevant pre-existing lower-court authority and from an obviously covered application of an established rule without a factual twin. Neither a Supreme-Court-only requirement nor a fundamentally-similar-facts prerequisite is lawful; conversely, an abstract liberty or bodily-security proposition does not settle an uncertain application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** One lower-court decision or a fixed numerical consensus is not automatically sufficient. Actual conflicts, authority, particular context, and express reservations matter. No count's constitutional basis, notice, personal responsibility, or official nexus is resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Elder* supplies examination of all relevant existing authority. *Anderson* requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not repeated factual identity. *Pinder* and *Uhlrig* preserve unsettled-application and attribution limits; later holdings cannot supply earlier notice.

##### Whether the civil clarity standard substitutes for criminal elements

**Holding:** Criminal fair warning under §242 and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: pre-existing law must make the constitutional unlawfulness apparent. The common threshold does not displace proof of deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the criminal burden of proof, or any other statutory element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Civil immunity analysis cannot itself convict, and judicial office alone does not prove that every act occurred under color of law. No categorically stricter criminal clarity standard is adopted.

##### Whether due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody are categorically excluded

**Holding:** Appellate review may not exclude due-process rights from §242 categorically or treat the victim's lack of custody as automatic immunity for an official's own assault. Where a specific constitutional provision covers the claim, that provision governs; the Fourth and Eighth Amendments do not exhaust every potentially covered abusive official act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the en banc judgment and remand for count-specific consideration under the proper conduct-time notice rule and all remaining properly presented issues. The mandate does not reinstate convictions or sentence, order acquittal or retrial, or require a new evidentiary hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal constitutional-assault formula or factual assault finding is made. Constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation, omitted protection, and supervisory attribution remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* and *Price* preserve §242's due-process reach. *DeShaney*'s private-violence rule does not immunize direct official wrongdoing. *Graham* supplies the specific-provision rule. *Collins*, *Doe v. Taylor*, *Pinder*, *Uhlrig*, and *Nabozny* retain their distinct deprivation and attribution boundaries, without supplying notice retrospectively.

## Holdings

### Effective consideration of mitigation under this complete charge

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments require an effective sentencing route for the material independent culpability significance of supported mitigation; a reasonable likelihood that the charge, evidence and trial as a whole prevent that use requires correction. This charge supplies that route, so the Constitution did not require the requested additional general mitigation definition or four factor-specific instructions.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with six direct votes. Scalia joins the application of governing law while separately reserving his disagreement with its constitutional foundation.

**Controlling explanation:** Lockett and Eddings protect meaningful mitigating effect, and Graham and Johnson require a lawful means of expressing independent reduced culpability even when aggravating facts are true. Boyde asks whether the complete charge and trial create a reasonable likelihood of prohibited operation, not whether a restrictive reading is merely possible. This jury was first told to choose death or life. The Commonwealth then had to prove statutory vileness beyond a reasonable doubt. Even after that finding, paragraph three permitted death rather than requiring it and directed life if all the evidence made death unjustified. Failure to prove vileness separately required life; death also required unanimous agreement. The latter directions did not convert the preceding discretionary choice into a mandatory death sentence. Background and emotional-disturbance evidence could therefore support life without a false answer about an aggravating fact.

The contrary reading treats the disjunctive wording and later failure-of-aggravation paragraph as confining life to absence of vileness. Read together with the initial choice and paragraph three's express post-aggravator alternative, they do not create the required reasonable likelihood. Two days of testimony, both sides' explanation of mitigation, the prosecutor's acknowledgment that vileness did not require death, and the verdict form's reference to mitigation confirm the judicially authorized route. Counsel and admitted evidence do not cure an otherwise restrictive instruction. Delo permits an evidentiary predicate for a particular-factor instruction; Tuilaepa permits individualized selection without fixed labels or weights. Neither makes admissibility alone sufficient. The Court finds no instruction error, no actual juror mental state, and no evidentiary predicate for every requested factor. It prescribes no universal wording or favorable weight and does not decide an unpresented mitigation-unanimity challenge.

### The distinct statutory-factor due-process contention

**Holding and operative rule:** Virginia's identified mitigation factors and provision permitting relevant mitigation evidence do not by themselves create a federal due-process entitlement to each requested instructional formulation. No independent state-law entitlement to specified wording or separately preserved arbitrary refusal to honor such an entitlement is established here.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls with eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees in rejection of this distinct claim only.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory allowance of offense circumstances, history, background and other mitigation preserves a broad evidentiary field; its list is not exclusive. That permission does not require the judge to give the requested formulations for prior criminal history, emotional disturbance, impaired capacity and age. The constitutional effective-route inquiry remains separate and is answered in Part I. The Court neither determines every factor's factual support nor decides a different claim that Virginia arbitrarily disregarded an established instruction entitlement.

## Event

Brogan v. United States, No. 96-1579. Plenary merits decision, January 26, 1998; argued December 2, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, United States v. Wiener, 96 F.3d 35 (1996). The question is whether the version of 18 U.S.C. §1001 governing Brogan's October 4, 1993 statement excludes a bare false denial of wrongdoing during federal investigative questioning.

Department of Labor and Internal Revenue Service agents made an unannounced visit to Brogan's home. He agreed to answer and denied receiving cash or gifts from JRD Management. ## Holdings

### A false negative answer has no categorical exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** The offense-date §1001 does not categorically exclude an otherwise covered, knowingly and willfully false material denial merely because it is a bare response to investigative questioning about the speaker's wrongdoing. Every actual statutory predicate remains necessary; this decision adopts no universal materiality rule for every §1001 clause and decides no immaterial-statement application.

**Authority:** Thomas's Parts I–II, III-A and IV are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, providing six direct votes for the principal statutory framework. Ginsburg independently agrees with the narrow no-exception proposition in her judgment concurrence. Part III-B(1)'s limited response to the abuse argument has Thomas, Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy; III-B(2)'s further response has only four votes and is not a holding.

**Controlling explanation:** A negative answer communicates a factual proposition. The statute's reference to any false statement is not limited to volunteered narratives, documents, benefit claims or statements under oath. The separate concealment clause's trick, scheme or device requirement does not become an element of every false-statement charge. The Labor and IRS investigation concerns included executive agencies, unlike the court-entity defect in Hubbard.

Gilliland describes protection of authorized governmental functions; it does not add a requirement that a lie successfully deceive investigators or independently pervert their work. Their prior knowledge therefore does not create the claimed categorical exemption, without deciding every distinct materiality question.

Text must be read in context. Ratzlaf preserves contextual fault and residual lenity, and Wells, Bates and Salinas distinguish enacted predicates from unsupported additions. The statute's claims and regulatory-reporting origins, longstanding lower-court doctrine and executive restraint make the proposed limitation substantial, but do not adequately support excluding this category from the enacted false-statements language. Generally applicable interpretive principles differ from a particular exemption lacking a sufficient statutory basis. Circuit disagreement alone supplies neither an amendment nor residual ambiguity requiring this exemption. Internal charging policy and unenacted proposals likewise create no statutory defense, and congressional silence does not ratify the doctrine.

The five Justices joining III-B(1) further conclude that potential investigative or charging abuse does not authorize this categorical exclusion. Actual materiality and culpability requirements, lawful charging discretion and Congress's responsibility remain distinct responses; discretion cannot replace proof or cure ambiguity. This conclusion neither approves every investigative tactic nor decides a claim of actual charging abuse. The Court affirms the reviewed construction without importing later statutory language, supplying unreported trial findings or treating the postanswer warning as proof of earlier knowledge.

### The privilege does not authorize a deliberately false answer

**Holding and operative rule:** The privilege against compelled self-incrimination provides no general right to answer falsely instead of invoking lawful protection against answering. It therefore supplies no constitutional-avoidance basis for the categorical exculpatory-no exception on this record; actual coercion, a valid privilege claim and unlawful demands for waiver remain distinct questions.

**Authority:** Thomas's principal analysis outside III-B has Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, providing six direct votes. Ginsburg's judgment concurrence is not counted as a join in this entire explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Bryson rejects deliberate falsehood as a privileged substitute for lawful resistance to an official inquiry. Its benefit-affidavit facts do not themselves resolve every oral interview, but the privilege's function does not become a general license to lie. Brogan could remain silent; the difficulty of choosing among admission, silence and denial does not establish the claimed entitlement to false speech.

LaChance preserves the same distinction between a meaningful response and proved knowing falsity in employment proceedings, while expressly leaving this criminal statute to its own construction. Its administrative burden and service requirements do not migrate here. Garrity and Gardner retain their actual compelled-testimony and waiver protections. The Court creates no confession duty, universal warning requirement or rule approving every use of silence, and finds no custodial compulsion or waiver of a valid privilege in this interview.

### Affirmance leaves unpresented element and trial questions unresolved

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the presented statutory exception leaves the reviewed conviction in force without reopening the separate payment conviction or ordering a proceeding on hypothetical trial errors. The meaning of willfulness concerning knowledge of illegality, actual surprise or lack of reflection, materiality, jury allocation and conduct-time notice remain subject to their own properly presented claims and supported records.

**Authority:** Part IV has the same six direct principal-opinion joins; Ginsburg independently concurs in affirmance and these limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Brogan's reported concession that the answer was false and knowingly and willfully given permits resolution of the categorical exception; it does not define culpability for every future case. The Second Circuit's reservation about knowledge of illegality and its discussion of possible surprise are not findings of error in this prosecution. Gaudin requires a jury determination beyond reasonable doubt when materiality is an actual element, but supplies no finding that this jury lost such an issue. Wells decides materiality under §1014, not every §1001 clause. Lanier protects conduct-time warning; a later decision cannot supply missing notice in 1993, but no new notice violation is adjudicated here. Literal truth, ambiguous questions, immaterial statements, coercion and actual charging abuse are not decided. The original denial preceded the agents' warning; failure to correct is not treated as an additional charged statement.

## Law After Decision

A bare exculpatory denial is not a categorical exception to the offense-date false-statements provision. This adds a criminal statutory coverage holding distinct from LaChance's employment rule. The actual included-agency matter, conduct, culpability, applicable materiality and criminal proof requirements remain necessary; negative phrasing does not establish immunity or automatically prove those predicates. ## Separate Writings

**Thomas's Part III-B(2), joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy.** These four Justices add that a simple-denial exception would not reliably prevent the feared escalation: an interrogator could press for a more detailed false account outside the proposed exemption. This policy response has no majority and establishes no rule approving investigative tactics or rejecting an actual abuse claim. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional argument; Souter joins neither part of III-B.

**Ginsburg, joined by Souter, concurring in the judgment.** The unqualified false-statements language forecloses the judicial exemption, but permits a serious problem requiring congressional attention. Officials who already know the answer can elicit a predictable denial and generate a new felony when the underlying conduct cannot be proved, limitations have run, or the conduct is innocent. This is different from merely imposing an additional punishment for an established underlying crime. The reported interview's surprise, absent prior warning and existing records illustrate the concern without proving that this prosecution was unlawful or that Brogan's underlying conduct was innocent.

The statute began with fraudulent claims in 1863, expanded in 1918 under a financial-fraud limitation and broadened in 1934 to reach regulatory falsehoods. Its present reach into informal criminal questioning extends well beyond those original problems. That history supports legislative attention, though it does not overcome the enacted language. In Nunley, the Court vacated a conviction at the Government's request because prosecution lacked normally withheld internal approval; that disposition established no statutory exculpatory-no defense. The charging-era manual said the statute did not apply to exculpatory denials; the 1996 and September 1997 versions stated restrictive charging policies. They evidence restraint, not judicially enforceable immunity. The sentencing policy excluding ordinary nonperjurious denials from an obstruction enhancement likewise does not amend the substantive offense.

Affirmance does not require conviction whenever a suspect denies wrongdoing. Wiener left open whether willfulness requires knowledge that the answer is unlawful and recognized that surprise or lack of reflection can bear on actual intent. Those reservations supply no finding of error or new proceeding here. Materiality language added in 1996 governs its own temporal scope and cannot be substituted for the 1993 statute. Proposed oral-denial protections and warnings were never enacted; they illustrate possible legislative choices, not present defenses. Congressional silence does not ratify the lower-court exception. The concern about creating a criminal offense also differs from adverse employment consequences for a proved knowing false denial under LaChance; accepting that employment rule does not eliminate this concern.

**Souter, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the principal opinion except its discrete response to prosecutorial-abuse concerns in III-B, and joins Ginsburg's concurrence calling for congressional attention. His statement identifies that limited withheld assent; it does not withdraw his support from the remaining statutory, privilege or interpretive analysis.

**Stevens, joined by Breyer, dissenting.** They would reverse only insofar as the judgment sustains the §1001 conviction. A bounded statutory exception should cover a bare reactive oral denial of the questioned person's own suspected wrongdoing during an unsolicited criminal inquiry. Volunteered deception, affirmative misdirection, benefit submissions, regulatory reports and embellished accounts remain outside that exception. The Second Circuit accepted that Brogan's answer fell within the doctrine's core, so resolving its disputed margins is unnecessary here.

The dissent relies on intended statutory coverage, purpose, ordinary criminal-law background and sustained concrete judicial and executive practice. Williams illustrates that a claimed false instrument must actually make the statutory factual assertion; Sorrells and Palmer illustrate generally applicable limits concerning entrapment and statutory territorial reach. Their different holdings do not themselves create this defense, but demonstrate that broad language does not exhaust interpretation. The history of claims and regulatory reporting, together with longstanding treatment of ordinary defensive denials, supports the limited construction here. The dissent does not make executive policy binding law, infer congressional ratification from silence, adopt an unenacted bill, or announce a constitutional right to lie. Its substantial agreement with Ginsburg's concern leads to a different statutory judgment. The proposed reversal would not disturb the separate payment conviction, command release or prescribe an unverified sentence.

## Event

Bousley v. United States, No. 96-8516. Argued March 3, 1998; decided May 18, 1998. On certiorari to the Eighth Circuit's denial of relief in a first §2255 proceeding.

Bousley pleaded guilty to a drug offense and using a firearm during and in relation to drug trafficking. He appealed his sentence without challenging the plea's validity. After Bailey explained that use requires active employment, he challenged the understanding of the firearm charge underlying his plea. The questions are whether that claim is available on collateral review, how procedural default affects it, and what factual innocence requires. The indictment charged use, not carrying.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses and remands for an opportunity to establish factual innocence and, if that gateway is satisfied, adjudication of the plea-intelligence claim.

## Holdings

### 1. Correct knowledge of the offense is necessary to an intelligent plea

**Holding and operative rule:** A guilty plea requires real notice of the charge's essential nature. A materially mistaken description of a statutory element can render the plea unintelligent; Bailey's explanation of what the firearm statute prohibits is substantive law, not a new constitutional procedure barred by Teague.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Parts II–III and IV.B are joined by the same coalition except Stevens. Part IV.A has O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each stated rule has direct majority support.

**Controlling explanation:** Bailey requires active employment of a firearm; proximity, accessibility or possession alone is not use. Henderson and Smith require an understanding of the actual charge. A statutory construction states the substantive reach of the crime, as Rivers explains, rather than imposing a new trial procedure. Teague's procedural restriction therefore supplies no answer to this claim.

Broce distinguishes claims waived by an otherwise valid guilty plea from a challenge to the plea's own validity. Brady's concern with later disappointed expectations does not excuse a materially mistaken account of the offense's essential elements. Godinez likewise separates capacity to choose from the informed choice itself.

**Precedent treatment:** Bailey supplies the offense's meaning; Henderson and Smith supply notice; Rivers explains substantive interpretation; Teague does not bar this substantive claim; Broce, Brady and Godinez retain their distinct limits.

### 2. Direct-review default requires cause and prejudice or factual innocence

**Holding and operative rule:** Failure to challenge plea intelligence on direct review defaults this claim. Neither anticipated defeat nor adverse precedent establishes cause where the legal argument was available; absent cause, the claim may proceed only through a sufficient factual-innocence showing, not a showing of legal error alone.

**Controlling explanation:** The distinction between using and possessing a firearm was available before Bailey. Reed's unavailable-claim concern does not encompass an argument thought unlikely to prevail, and Engle rejects expected futility as cause. Prejudice cannot replace missing cause.

Schlup nevertheless permits review where, considering the whole probative record under the correct offense, it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. That exceptional inquiry applies here despite the plea. The absence of a trial record does not justify treating an unsupported conviction as conclusive, but neither does it shift the inquiry to whether the existing plea record was formally sufficient. Carriger preserves the difference between passing this gateway and proving the underlying constitutional violation. The district court has not adjudicated innocence, and the Government does not contend that omission of that issue below independently waived this opportunity. The Court does not now decide constitutional invalidity; the gateway and underlying claim must be resolved in that order.

**Precedent treatment:** Reed and Engle delimit cause; Schlup supplies the demanding whole-record gateway; Carriger keeps the gateway separate from the constitutional merits.

### 3. The evidence and actual plea bargain define the innocence inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government may offer admissible evidence outside the plea record to rebut factual innocence. Where it actually forgave an identified more-serious charge in the plea bargain, the innocence showing must also cover that charge; hypothetical charges, unproved bargains and alternative statutory verbs create no added burden.

**Controlling explanation:** Factual innocence concerns what occurred, not merely what the plea papers recite. Limiting the Government to those papers would convert the gateway into a legal-sufficiency test. The five Justices joining IV.A also require attention to an actual bargain relinquishing more-serious exposure, so that the inquiry does not disregard the exchange that produced the plea.

This indictment alleged use, not carrying. No bargain relinquishing carrying or another identified more-serious offense is established. Bousley therefore need establish absence of use under Bailey on this record. The Government cannot preserve the conviction simply by relabeling it an uncharged carrying conviction. A proved gateway permits adjudication of the plea-intelligence claim; it does not itself require vacatur or acquittal.

**Precedent treatment:** Schlup's factual inquiry is applied to a guilty plea with evidence beyond the plea record; Bailey continues to define the charged use offense.

## Law After Decision

A defaulted challenge to a materially misdescribed guilty plea may receive collateral review upon the required factual-innocence showing. Admissible proof beyond the plea record is available. Actual relinquishment of a more-serious charge matters; an imagined charge does not. Passing the gateway opens consideration of plea validity and supplies no automatic merits relief.

## Separate Writings

Stevens joins the substantive and constitutional principles in Part I but would vacate the firearm conviction and permit Bousley to plead anew. Timmreck and Hill distinguish formal plea-taking defects from a constitutionally invalid plea. In his view, the erroneous understanding of the statutory element establishes the constitutional defect here, and failure to appeal does not require Bousley to prove innocence before obtaining correction. The Government would retain its ordinary burden if prosecution proceeds. His position does not require acquittal or dismissal with prejudice and does not affect the drug conviction.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, would affirm. He agrees that no cause excuses the default but rejects extending Schlup's trial-conviction gateway to guilty pleas. A plea leaves no comparable trial record of facts that participants may then have thought immaterial, may exchange admission for relief from greater charges, and may leave stale evidence insufficient to reconstruct either side of that exchange. Those features make the proposed exception materially different from Schlup's setting. The dissent does not treat possession as active use, find actual carrying, or decide Bousley's innocence.

## Procedure After Action

The district court must permit the bounded factual-innocence inquiry and resolve any factual development needed under ordinary procedures. If Bousley satisfies that gateway, it must consider whether his plea was voluntary and intelligent and determine any lawful relief. No automatic live hearing, release or acquittal is ordered.
