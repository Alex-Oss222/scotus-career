### United States v. Kirk (William Joseph Kirk v. United States), Fifth Circuit No. 94-50472

Plenary merits decision, February 14, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Fifth Circuit's en banc affirmance by an equally divided court. The available record identifies no separate Supreme Court docket or oral-argument date. The question is whether 18 U.S.C. §922(o)'s possession prohibition is within the commerce power as applied to Kirk's preserved Count One conviction without transaction-specific interstate proof. Kirk pleaded guilty conditionally after denial of his constitutional motion; dismissed transfer counts and other appellate issues are outside the grant. Rybar, decided November 24, 1997, is earlier precedent, not a consolidated companion. The judgment below is reported at 105 F.3d 997.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

Count One remains effective against the Commerce Clause challenge. No Commerce-based vacatur, evidentiary remand, new trial, or transaction-specific interstate allegation is required. The Court does not reinstate dismissed transfer counts or reach the ungranted plea-agreement, double-jeopardy or sentencing issues.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. No separate writing is filed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Possession of prohibited post-1986 machinegun stock

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(o) may reach possession of a completed machinegun within the prohibited post-May 19, 1986 civilian stock without proof of a defendant-specific interstate or commercial transaction, because the possession prohibition is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of Congress's valid national restriction on additions to that stock. The Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause supplies that class-and-regime connection; no additional transaction-specific interstate element is required.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule, its application and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Hale's possession rule does not disappear when a particular transfer cannot be proved. Local conversion creates completed prohibited stock that can be retained, concealed, acquired and kept available for circulation. Excluding that possession would materially impair the stock restriction itself. Rybar applied the same rule where gun-show sales made the market connection concrete; its facts did not convert a proved sale into a universal constitutional prerequisite. Kirk's possession conviction therefore stands without treating the reported sales as admitted elements or reviving dismissed transfer charges.

Lopez requires an identified interstate mechanism before aggregation can measure its magnitude. Here the mechanism is the specific transfer-and-stock regime, its grandfathered lawful stock, and the fitted possession prohibition. It is not dangerousness, national marketability, remote interstate movement, generalized gun crime or downstream productivity. Nor does administrative convenience alone establish necessity. A particular interstate element may supply a connection in another statute, but courts need not insert one where this valid regime supplies the connection. Formal findings are not universally required and cannot create power by recital; the identified enacted structure supplies the necessary relationship here.

**Operative exceptions and limits:** Section 922(o)(2) preserves transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. It separately preserves lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986; earlier manufacture alone is insufficient. Neither exception is established for Count One. The holding establishes no general federal firearms police power, no rule for every locally manufactured object, and no automatic justification for every product-market possession prohibition. It decides neither Second Amendment protection nor every conversion-parts, readily-restorable or wear-induced application of the machinegun definition.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lopez supplies the connection-before-magnitude architecture and the necessary-inclusion route; its limit on generalized downstream effects and judicial insertion of elements remains intact.
- Hale is applied to completed prohibited stock produced by local conversion; its anti-evasion and material-impairment rule and statutory exceptions are preserved.
- Rybar is applied without treating its gun-show sales as a new element or a constitutional condition for every possession prosecution; its separate arms holding is not before the Court.
- Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class aggregation scope; neither supplies an independent disposition here.
- Parker's cross-border payment obligation is a distinct commerce mechanism and supplies no substitute firearms rule.

#### Law After Decision

Hale's existing possession authority includes this locally converted completed machinegun even without transaction-specific interstate proof. The decision clarifies that application rather than adding a new Commerce category. It leaves ordinary firearm possession, other statutory applications and independent constitutional challenges subject to their own rules.

#### Source Notes

The indictment, conditional plea, reported conversion and test firing, scope of conviction, and divided appellate posture appear in [70 F.3d 791](https://static.case.law/f3d/70/html/0791-01.html), [105 F.3d 997](https://static.case.law/f3d/105/html/0997-01.html), and the [rehearing order, 78 F.3d 160](https://static.case.law/f3d/78/html/0160-01.html). The reported sales are background recitations, not additional admitted elements of Kirk's plea. No interstate movement of the weapon, parts or charged possession is established or required by the holding.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed in the reviewed respect. Count One is not vacated or remanded on the Commerce Clause ground; no new trial, resentencing, transfer prosecution or factual inquiry is ordered.

**End of entry: United States v. Kirk (William Joseph Kirk v. United States), plenary merits decision, February 14, 1998.**

### Johnson v. Rodriguez, Fifth Circuit Nos. 95-50879 and 96-50382

Plenary merits decision, February 17, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254. The available record identifies no separate Supreme Court docket or oral-argument date. After a consent bench trial under §636(c), the trial court entered relief concerning confidential parole protest letters and writ-writers. The Fifth Circuit reversed the protest-letter decree and directed dismissal with prejudice, reversed and remanded the writ-writer claim, including retaliation and equality, and vacated attorney fees. The questions concern initial-parole liberty, protest-letter equality, protected litigation, causation, injury and the scope of class relief. This is an official-capacity prospective-relief action, not a personal-damages immunity appeal. The judgment below is reported at 110 F.3d 299.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I-A. Initial parole and confidential-file process

**Holding and operative rule:** Texas's parole scheme creates no entitlement to initial release because its substantive eligibility conditions and required procedures do not meaningfully restrict the Board's authority to deny release. Absent that entitlement, claimed unreliability in confidential protest material does not establish the asserted procedural due-process right to file correction, disclosure or an adversarial parole hearing.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I-A, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Sandin requires a genuine substantive constraint, not mandatory words alone. The Court examines the whole scheme: eligibility restrictions and guidelines limit when the Board may grant release but do not require release when particular facts exist. Young protects community freedom already conferred; Johnson remains in ordinary sentence-authorized custody. Article 42.18 §8(f)(2) requires victim-related notice, permits written and oral participation without a numerical ceiling on written speakers, and requires consideration of the victim-impact statement. Those duties do not compel parole. Section 8(e)'s file-inclusion commands do not require reliance on every allegation; §18(a)'s confidentiality likewise creates no release entitlement. This holding concerns the asserted procedural-liberty theory and does not excuse a separate unconstitutional penalty for protected conduct.

##### I-B. Protest-letter equal protection

**Holding and operative rule:** The protest-letter system establishes no purposeful unconstitutional differential treatment or irrational parole criterion on this record. The distinct class claim is therefore dismissed with prejudice; inaccurate or vindictive statements do not alone justify a prohibition on receiving or considering every protest statement.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I-B, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Even assuming prisoners receiving protest letters supply a comparison, receiving relevant information, allowing victim participation, protecting sources from reprisal and avoiding a full adversarial parole proceeding are rational purposes. The trial court's concerns about some letters do not invalidate that entire process or establish the required purposeful discrimination. The Court does not adopt a universal rule that an equal-protection class must exist before government acts. Nor does it decide claims of knowing official falsification, racial discrimination, retaliation for protected litigation, use of an invalidated conviction contrary to a binding order, or every possible individual false-statement claim.

##### II-A. Retaliation for protected litigation

**Holding and operative rule:** A parole board may not deny discretionary parole because a prisoner engaged in protected, nonfrivolous litigation. The claimant must prove the protected conduct, adverse action and its substantial or motivating causal role; officials may then defeat the claim by proving by a preponderance that they would have taken the same action without that conduct.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II-A, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Crawford-El applies Mt. Healthy's separate burdens. A merely conceivable lawful parole reason is not proof that the same decision would actually have occurred. Competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available under the ordinary preponderance standard; no heightened quantum or categorical direct-evidence rule is added. Lack of a parole entitlement does not authorize a protected-right penalty. A retaliatory parole denial itself supplies adverse injury when causally established: a claimant complaining of punishment for completed protected litigation need not additionally prove that the lawsuit failed. Nonfrivolous direct appeals, habeas claims and conditions litigation are protected, while frivolous filings and unrestricted jailhouse lawyering are not protected merely by their labels. Further findings must identify Johnson's own protected conduct, its causal role and the Board actors responsible. The Court finds neither ultimate retaliation nor entitlement to parole.

##### II-B. Necessary assistance and independent petition activity

**Holding and operative rule:** The remand may not categorically exclude all secondary legal assistance or independently protected communication and petition activity. Necessary assistance must be assessed under the recipient's meaningful-access right, effective alternative arrangements and valid penological restrictions; no free-standing right to act as another prisoner's chosen lawyer follows.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II-B, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela joins the corrective disposition without this additional rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Lewis requires actual or evidence-supported imminent hindrance of a qualifying nonfrivolous claim for a claim of inadequate access assistance. It neither terminates all assistance protection once a case is filed nor makes independent retaliation depend on a failed lawsuit. Johnson v. Avery and Wolff preserve assistance needed for meaningful access while allowing effective alternatives and legitimate restrictions. The lower court must distinguish necessary help, Johnson's own filings, frivolous conduct and any separately grounded communication or petition theory actually within the case. Mere reference to litigation does not make all conduct protected, but a categorical exclusion would discard potentially protected activity without examining its predicate. Supported findings and common evidence remain available; the remand adds no new evidence code.

##### III. The separate writ-writer equality claim

**Holding and operative rule:** The remanded writ-writer equality claim requires relevant similarly situated treatment and purposeful discriminatory adverse effect under the actual right asserted. A penalty for independently protected litigation is not justified merely by describing all litigiousness as unreformed behavior; generalized litigation references or historical bias by another agency do not establish the claim.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III, joined by all seven other Associates: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees in preserving this component on remand and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The protest-letter dismissal does not resolve this different claim. Courts must identify the comparison, protected-right predicate when invoked, purposeful differential treatment and causal injury. A conceivable rational basis for an ordinary classification is not a same-decision defense to proved retaliation. Comparative evidence need not always be statistical; missing statistics alone do not resolve every claim. The Court adopts no new strict-scrutiny rule and makes no liability finding.

##### IV-A. Standing, Board attribution and class relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Johnson must establish his own redressable injury and basis for prospective relief. Systemwide relief for the challenged parole practice requires a proved ongoing official policy or widespread authorized practice, corresponding class injury and relief fitted to that violation; class certification and isolated employee references to litigation establish none of those predicates by themselves.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part IV-A, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Lewis links common relief to a demonstrated common barrier and identified affected group; ordinary standing requires the named plaintiff's own injury and prospective exposure. The record does not yet connect identified protected conduct and attributable Board retaliation to Johnson's parole decision or justify the decree's statewide reach. Earlier Ruiz findings about other agencies and subjective perceptions do not prove the challenged Board practice. Common evidence may establish a common wrong, but its functional and geographic reach must be supported. Any relief must respect valid security interests and applicable prospective-relief law. The Court imposes no universal Monell prerequisite on every state-officer injunction, does not purge all references to litigation, and does not order a particular discovery method or trial.

##### IV-B. Dependent fees

**Holding and operative rule:** The attorney-fee award cannot rest on the reversed trial decree and remains vacated. Any later prevailing-party entitlement must follow the lawful judgment ultimately entered.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part IV-B, joined by all seven other Associates: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees in this disposition without an added ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The present correction preserves the need for new findings and supplies no merits victory establishing fees. It determines neither a later amount nor a permanent inability to recover a fee.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Sandin is applied to genuine substantive constraints; mandatory procedures alone do not require initial parole.
- Young and Morrissey are distinguished because community liberty already conferred is different from initial discretionary release.
- Crawford-El and Mt. Healthy supply ordinary motive proof and the officials' distinct preponderance same-decision defense; neither is altered.
- Lewis supplies meaningful assistance, qualifying access injury and fitted common relief; its noncontrolling categorical secondary-assistance and initial-filing restrictions are not adopted.
- Johnson v. Avery and Wolff preserve necessary assistance and effective alternatives; they establish no unrestricted entitlement to serve as jailhouse counsel.
- Lujan and Lyons preserve personal injury, causation and the prospective basis for relief.
- Turner preserves restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests; an asserted security label alone is insufficient.
- Umbehr and Valot preserve the distinction between absence of benefit entitlement and a protected-right penalty; their separate employment or contractor rules are not imported into this prison case.
- Monell's governmental-attribution principle is not converted into a universal condition for every state-officer injunction. No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Discretionary initial parole creates no federal accuracy code for the confidential file. That absence of entitlement remains separate from protected-litigation retaliation. Existing proof and access rules govern the remand, and the distinct equality claim survives for its own required showing. Neither a statewide violation nor a right to parole follows. The Court preserves the unappealed ex post facto, tentative-parole-month/progress, nonresident-furlough and corporate-entity-immunity dispositions.

#### Source Notes

The lower judgment, bench-trial posture, Texas provisions, unresolved findings and limits on the appeal appear in [Johnson v. Rodriguez, 110 F.3d 299](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/110/110.F3d.299.95-50879.96-50382.html). The descriptions of unreliable letters and litigation references are record findings or submissions at their stated scope, not a finding that every letter is false or all of Johnson's litigation is frivolous. The appellate account identifies the findings still needed on remand; it supports no present personal-damages immunity ruling.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The protest-letter dismissal and fee vacatur stand. The appellate writ-writer directions are vacated only insofar as they conflict with the protection and burden rules above; further proceedings otherwise remain required on retaliation and writ-writer equality. The statewide decree is not restored. Lower courts must determine protected activity, causation, Johnson's injury, attributable practice and the lawful scope of relief without reopening the unappealed claims.

**End of entry: Johnson v. Rodriguez, plenary merits decision, February 17, 1998.**

### Newsweek, Inc. v. Florida Department of Revenue, No. 97-663

Certiorari and summary merits disposition, February 23, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided without oral argument. The First District Court of Appeal affirmed summary judgment rejecting Newsweek's postpayment remedy claim because Florida offered a prepayment challenge. Newsweek invoked Fla. Stat. §215.26 for sales taxes paid during 1988–1990 under the newspaper/magazine distinction invalidated by the Florida Supreme Court. The question is whether the availability of an unused prepayment procedure permits Florida to withdraw the clearly offered postpayment route.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

Certiorari is granted on the postpayment-remedy question. No certiorari tally is disclosed.

**Vacated and remanded, 8–1 as to the choice of vacatur rather than reversal.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, who would reverse and remand.

All nine support the controlling postpayment-remedy rule and a remand for the constitutionally adequate opportunity it requires. No refund amount, interest award or particular equivalent correction is selected. Lawful reasonable preannounced conditions and ordinary proof of payment and amount remain applicable.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Per curiam | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Requires the postpayment opportunity and bounded remand | Reich rule, actual application, reasonable preannounced conditions and open remedial choices |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Per curiam | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Vacates and remands | Formal disposition leaves the claim's merits and lawful conditions for adjudication |
| Concurrence in Part I and remand; dissent from vacatur in place of reversal | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Would reverse and remand | Same governing rule and remedy limits; distinct formal judgment |

The merits coalitions above disclose no certiorari poll.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### A clearly offered postpayment remedy cannot be withdrawn after payment

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State clearly holds out a postpayment procedure for recovering unconstitutionally collected taxes, due process forbids denying that opportunity after payment solely because the taxpayer could have pursued an adequate prepayment challenge. The State must afford the promised clear and certain postpayment opportunity, subject to lawful reasonable conditions announced in advance and ordinary proof of payment and amount; neither automatic repayment of every dollar nor a particular equivalent correction is prescribed.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices. Part II's vacatur and remand is supported by the eight Associates; Stone-Zsela would reverse and remand under the same rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Reich answers Florida's stated reason for refusing relief. Newsweek used §215.26 for its 1988–1990 payments. Florida had long allowed constitutional refund claims through that statute, as Hardaway and Osceola recognized. Section 72.011's opportunity to deposit the disputed sum, post a bond or obtain approval for another arrangement did not retrospectively displace that offered route.

Florida distinguishes McKesson because Newsweek could have contested the tax before payment without onerous penalties. Even assuming that prepayment procedure was meaningful, the argument does not answer Reich's independent rule against withdrawing a clearly offered postpayment remedy, including after voluntary payment. The Court need not declare §72.011 inadequate. Fulton, Camps Newfound and Lunding preserve lawful state remedial choices; they do not authorize denial of all adequate correction on the ground rejected here. The existing Florida invalidity ruling supplies the substantive premise, so the Court makes no new First Amendment tax determination. Ordinary preserved conditions and the amount or form of adequate relief remain for remand; no independent default is adjudicated, general government-estoppel rule created or final judgment reopened.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Reich directly governs the withdrawal of an offered postpayment route and remains distinct from the adequacy of unused prepayment review.
- McKesson's compelled-payment rule is distinguished, not enlarged into automatic refund entitlement; Reich independently suffices even assuming meaningful prepayment review.
- Fulton preserves constitutionally adequate lawful remedial choices, subject to the separate McKesson and Reich predicates.
- Camps Newfound preserves open remedial choices without authorizing denial of all adequate correction.
- Lunding applies the same prepayment/postpayment distinction and ordinary conditions; its separate substantive tax holding is not extended here.

#### Law After Decision

Florida may not reject this postpayment claim solely because Newsweek failed to use an earlier prepayment option. The lawful refund procedure and adequate equivalent relief remain to be applied under ordinary preserved conditions. Prospective procedural changes with fair notice remain possible; no particular check amount or tax exemption follows from this decision.

#### Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that Reich forbids the procedural switch and joins the required postpayment opportunity and remand limits. He would reverse the judgment and remand for constitutionally adequate relief, rather than vacate it. He leaves lawful preannounced conditions, calculation and the form of adequate correction to the ordinary proceedings and orders no fixed refund or interest.

#### Source Notes

The payments, §215.26 invocation and Florida proceedings are recited at [522 U.S. 442, 442–445](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-522/pdf/USREPORTS-522-442.pdf). The state invalidity predicate is Department of Revenue v. Magazine Publishers of America, 565 So.2d 1304 (1990), reaffirmed at 604 So.2d 459 (1992). The established refund route is supported by State ex rel. Hardaway Contracting Co. v. Lee, 155 Fla. 724, 21 So.2d 211 (1945), and Osceola v. Florida Department of Revenue, 893 F.2d 1231, 1233 (1990). No independent timeliness or refund-condition default is established by the reported posture.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The First District's judgment is vacated. On remand, Newsweek must receive the clear and certain postpayment opportunity the State held out; the lower court must address applicable ordinary conditions and adequate relief without treating the unused prepayment option as a complete answer. No amount or interest is awarded by this Court.

### Regions Hospital v. Shalala, No. 96-1375

Merits decision, February 24, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 1, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, which affirmed summary judgment sustaining the Secretary's graduate-medical-education reaudit regulation. The Hospital obtained expedited review after the Provider Reimbursement Review Board stated that it could not invalidate the regulation. The questions concern authority to correct the fiscal-1984 allowable-cost base for later Medicare payments, retroactivity, reasonableness and the effect of the final original payment determination. The reaudit began in late 1990; no recoupment of the closed 1984 payment is sought.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas.

The Secretary may use the corrected allowable 1984 base in future reimbursement calculations and later payment determinations that remain independently open. The decision authorizes no recoupment or revision of a closed payment determination, disregard of reopening regulations, or new cost-allowability standard. No precise payment award or expense-level disallowance is decided.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg in full | Supports affirmance | I-A: statutory permission; I-B: construction and permissible deference; II: temporal use and finality; III: rule reasonableness; IV: preclusion; mandate |
| Same opinion, partial joins | Breyer | Stone-Zsela | Supports affirmance | I-A's authority to correct the lawful base and II's antecedent-input/finality rule; the mandate's corresponding closed-payment and open-year limits. Does not join I-B, III or IV's additional grounds. |
| Dissent | Scalia | O'Connor, Thomas in full | Would reverse | Statutory recognized-cost ground; remand for lawful calculations without the disputed cost redetermination |

Kennedy's Part II join concerns later reimbursement as the relevant activity and does not adopt a general retroactivity framework beyond that application. Each controlling proposition has a direct majority; no combination of separate rationales supplies the Court's law.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Authority to determine the lawful base

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1395ww(h)(2)(A) permits the Secretary to correct the allowable fiscal-1984 graduate-medical-education cost figure used in the later statutory per-resident reimbursement formula. This authority to determine a lawful historical input does not itself reopen the final payment for the base year or authorize departure from the cost rules governing that year.

**Authority:** Part I-A's operative permission has Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six votes. Part I-B's following construction and permissible-deference explanation has Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five votes, independently sufficient to control that rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress directed the Secretary to determine the hospital's average amount recognized as reasonable under the Act for direct GME costs in its fiscal-1984 reporting period. Read with the reasonable-cost system and the shift from annual determinations to an enduring formula, that language does not unambiguously freeze every expense an intermediary once reimbursed into future payments. The Secretary's construction permits the new base determination to exclude amounts not allowable under the governing cost principles. Brown requires this statutory inquiry before respect for administrative interpretation; agency expertise alone creates no authority. The express instruction concerning resident numbers does not negate the accompanying command to determine average lawful cost. The contrary reading gives past recognition decisive effect even when an erroneous annual allowance becomes a permanent formula input. The Court concludes that Congress did not compel that result, while preserving the ordinary finality of the annual payment itself.

##### II. Antecedent facts, later payments and finality

**Holding and operative rule:** Applying the existing base-year cost principles to antecedent data for future reimbursement does not impose a new legal consequence on the closed 1984 payment and is not impermissibly retroactive. A later already-paid year may be corrected only if its own payment determination remains independently open under governing reopening rules; closed determinations cannot be revised or recouped under this holding.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices, on this specific later-payment analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf distinguishes new burdens on completed conduct from present use of earlier facts; Bowen preserves the limits on retroactive rulemaking. The relevant rule changes neither which costs were allowable in 1984 nor the final amount paid for that closed year. It changes the input used in the distinct later reimbursement calculation. Regions retains finality for the earlier payment, but that protection does not establish an entitlement to carry an unallowable expense into every later calculation. The three-year reopening limit continues to protect each particular payment determination. Neither referring to an old fact nor labeling a calculation prospective answers the whole inquiry: the agency must use the governing base-year law and respect each affected year's finality. No general authority to issue retroactive legislative rules or revise final decisions is recognized.

##### III. Reasonableness and the asserted reliance burden

**Holding and operative rule:** The challenged reaudit rule is reasonable on the supported administrative record because it corrects an enduring formula input under existing cost law and provides the stated two-direction corrections and documentation accommodations. This facial conclusion does not validate every particular expense disallowance, allocation or future evidentiary accommodation.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Secretary explained that annual errors would otherwise become recurring formula errors and that competing Medicare changes delayed scrutiny. Chenery requires that actual rationale, not a new reason invented by a reviewing court. The rule excludes unallowable or misclassified costs, permits requested inclusion of GME costs incorrectly classified as operating expenses, and permits an upward hospital-specific prospective-payment operating adjustment for costs the reaudit shows belonged to operating costs instead of GME. When base-year physician-time records are missing, later reporting-period evidence may document the allocation; Regions used 1989 and 1990 studies. These accommodations answer the categorical claim that the rule can only disadvantage hospitals or demands unavailable original records in every case. They do not find every disputed expense correctly disallowed or make reliance immaterial in all applications. Good Samaritan and Guernsey keep cost correction within lawful methods, and Allentown requires compliance with the actual governing standard. No indefinite serial reaudit power, new 1984 allowability rule or procedurally defective audit is approved.

##### IV. The intermediary notice does not establish adjudicated issue preclusion here

**Holding and operative rule:** On this record, the intermediary's final annual reimbursement notice and payment-finality regulations do not conclusively adjudicate every underlying cost fact for the distinct statutory base determination. No Provider Reimbursement Review Board adjudicatory hearing resolved the reasonableness of these costs, so the asserted issue-preclusion objection does not bar this use.

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The February 28, 1986 notice fixed the annual reimbursement within its applicable finality rules. Payment finality and an adjudicated issue are different. The record establishes no adjudicatory cost determination whose preclusive force would have to be displaced. The Court therefore rejects the asserted bar without announcing that administrative adjudications can never preclude later issues or permitting reconsideration of a completed Article III judgment.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Brown requires statutory authority before deference; the agency may not add a condition contrary to the governing text.
- Landgraf is applied to the particular consequence for later payments; its rule against unauthorized new retrospective burdens remains intact.
- Bowen preserves the limit on retroactive legislative rules and rejection of unsupported litigation rationalizations; neither authority is expanded.
- Good Samaritan preserves correction within authorized methods and legal boundaries, not an entitlement to every claimed historical expense.
- Thomas Jefferson supplies the distinct limits on interpreting an agency's own regulation and on reliance on its actual ground; that standard does not replace statutory construction here.
- Guernsey preserves lawful accounting allocation without permission to contradict a binding regulation.
- Chenery preserves review on the agency's actual rationale.
- Allentown requires use of the actual announced standard and the whole record; no substituted base-year cost standard is approved.

#### Law After Decision

The Secretary may determine an accurate allowable 1984 input for the enduring GME formula while leaving closed payment awards intact. The rule's legality does not settle each expense dispute, excuse defective procedures, reopen adjudicated cost issues on a different record, or create general power to redetermine final agency decisions.

#### Separate Writings

Justice Scalia, joined by Justices O'Connor and Thomas, would reverse on the statutory ground. Congress selected the amount already recognized as reasonable under the existing reimbursement machinery; directing an average did not authorize a new reasonable-cost determination after the ordinary revision period. Congress expressly delegated resident-count rules without similarly directing recomputation of the settled cost figure. Accuracy and administrative practicality cannot supply the missing delegation. The dissent would remand for lawful calculations using the recognized base and applicable resident-count and inflation rules, without ordering a precise award. It need not decide every other hospital objection and does not claim that every use of an antecedent fact is impermissibly retroactive.

#### Source Notes

The administrative history, amounts, regulatory accommodations and proceedings below appear at [522 U.S. 448, 454–463](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-522/pdf/USREPORTS-522-448.pdf), including the cited 42 C.F.R. §413.86 provisions and 55 Fed. Reg. 36063. The original and revised aggregate GME amounts were $9,892,644 and $5,916,868; reported per-resident amounts were $70,662 and $49,805. Those reported figures do not establish an unchanged resident denominator or prove any particular expense correctly classified. No closed-1984 repayment is sought, and no prior PRRB hearing adjudicated the cost issues.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Eighth Circuit judgment is affirmed. Use of the corrected base in future and independently open later determinations may proceed within the governing rules. The final 1984 payment remains untouched; the Court orders no recoupment, new audit procedure, exact payment or adjudication of an individual expense.

**End of entry: Regions Hospital v. Shalala, merits decision, February 24, 1998.**

### Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana, No. 96-1971

Merits decision, February 24, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 21, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit. Louisiana mortgage holders sued under Louisiana law to enforce their mortgage or obtain damages. Respondents removed on the theory that earlier federal bankruptcy orders extinguished the rights asserted. The district court refused remand and entered merits judgments; the Fifth Circuit affirmed through Carpenter's complete-claim-preclusion reading of Moitie. The question is whether that defense supplies federal-question removal jurisdiction.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The district court must vacate the merits judgments entered without federal jurisdiction and remand the whole action to state court. That includes the Bank and Fountainbleau Storage Associates judgments based on preclusion and the Browns' separately reasoned failure-to-state-a-claim judgment. The state court may decide the earlier federal judgments' preclusive effect under governing federal preclusion law; no federal merits ruling in this removed action remains.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Scalia delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate writing is filed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Federal claim preclusion remains a defense

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 28 U.S.C. §§1331 and 1441, a prior federal judgment's claim-preclusive effect is a defense and does not transform a complaint presenting only state-law claims into an action arising under federal law. Complete statutory preemption differs: Congress must displace the state claim with the exclusive federal cause that supplies its legal character; the completeness of an asserted judgment defense does not perform that work.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join the rule, its application and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that jurisdiction comes from the claim and governing jurisdictional law, not from the strength of an anticipated defense. These petitioners seek recognition and enforcement of Louisiana mortgage rights, or alternatively damages under Louisiana law. The bankruptcy orders may defeat those rights, but even a conclusive defense would not supply the absent federal cause. Calling the complaint artfully pleaded does not answer whether federal law creates its controlling character.

Moitie's second footnote does not establish a general exception for federal claim preclusion. Relabeling an essentially federal claim differs from asserting an independent state claim that may be barred by an earlier federal judgment. Moitie's preclusion rule remains intact, but the broader removal rule drawn from it by Carpenter cannot sustain this judgment. Peacock requires independent authority for a later action, while City of Chicago had substantial federal claims on the complaints' face. Recognition principles in Matsushita, Richards and Baker likewise do not provide a universal jurisdictional grant. The Court decides neither whether petitioners actually retain mortgage rights nor the validity or preclusive effect of the bankruptcy orders.

##### Dependent merits judgments must be vacated

**Holding and operative rule:** Because this action lacks the asserted original federal jurisdiction and no later jurisdictional cure is established, the dependent federal merits judgments must be vacated and the whole action returned to state court. A different merits ground for one respondent does not cure the same jurisdictional defect.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion, with all nine Justices joining the remedy and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Caterpillar preserves a completed judgment where actual diversity jurisdiction arose before trial and continued through judgment; consent, efficiency and finality do not substitute for that jurisdiction. Here all parties are Louisiana parties, the complaint relies exclusively on Louisiana law, and the federal defense supplies no cure. The Browns' judgment rested on failure to state a claim rather than the Bank and FSA's preclusion ground, but it too lacked the needed federal foundation. Returning the case leaves the competent state forum to determine the defense under applicable law. The Court orders no damages, mortgage enforcement, new injunction or collateral attack on the bankruptcy orders.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Moitie's ordinary claim-preclusion rule is preserved; its footnote is not extended into a general removal exception for prior federal judgments.
- Peacock supplies the independent-authority requirement for a later action while preserving ordinary enforcement within its proper sphere.
- City of Chicago is distinguished because substantial federal claims appeared on the complaints' face there; supplemental jurisdiction has no comparable original anchor here.
- Caterpillar is distinguished because actual jurisdiction arose before trial there; finality alone cannot save this action's merits judgments.
- Matsushita preserves judgment recognition without equating that effect with adjudicatory power.
- Richards preserves constitutional limits on binding parties; it supplies no removal authority and no finding about these petitioners' binding status.
- Baker preserves the distinction among recognition, execution and new duties in another forum; that distinction creates no universal removal grant.

#### Law After Decision

Federal claim preclusion may defeat a state-law action in a competent forum but cannot itself remove that action to federal court. Complete statutory preemption retains its separate requirements. The jurisdictional decision supplies no mortgage-priority, cancellation, damages, bankruptcy-order-validity or actual preclusion holding. Otherwise available enforcement proceedings retain their own authority and prerequisites.

#### Source Notes

The mortgages, bankruptcy orders, surviving record inscription, 1993 transfer, December 29, 1994 state complaint, Louisiana parties and distinct judgments below appear at [522 U.S. 470, 472–478](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-522/pdf/USREPORTS-522-470.pdf). The Fifth Circuit's theory was complete claim preclusion under Carpenter; it did not find that a federal statute replaced these causes of action. The Browns' merits ground was failure to state a claim, distinct from the other respondents' preclusion ground.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The appellate jurisdictional judgment is reversed. On remand, the district court must vacate the dependent merits judgments and return the entire action to Louisiana state court. The state court may adjudicate the defense and remaining claims; no party's substantive recovery is ordered or predicted.

**End of entry: Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana, merits decision, February 24, 1998.**

### Alaska v. Native Village of Venetie Tribal Government, No. 96-1577

Plenary merits decision, February 25, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 10, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Ninth Circuit, 101 F.3d 1286. Review concerns whether the former Venetie Reservation lands conveyed under the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act are a dependent Indian community under 18 U.S.C. §1151(b), supplying the territorial authority asserted for the nonmember school-construction tax. The District Court rejected that classification in Alaska’s action to enjoin collection; the Court of Appeals reversed.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Reverse, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lands at issue lack dependent-community status. The asserted territorial basis for the approximately $161,000 nonmember construction tax therefore fails. Further proceedings below must conform to that ruling; no new hearing, title adjudication or region-wide jurisdictional decree is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt both holdings and the bounded disposition. No separate writing issues.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Dependent-community status requires a federal land relationship

**Holding and operative rule:** A dependent Indian community under 18 U.S.C. §1151(b) requires land set aside by the Federal Government for Indian use and federal superintendence over that community’s land relationship. Tribal recognition, Indian ownership and federal assistance to residents do not independently supply those territorial predicates.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this rule; no fractured-opinion synthesis is involved.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reads the statutory category through Sandoval, which recognizes federal protection and supervision of Pueblo lands despite fee ownership, and McGowan, which treats a federal Indian-use set-aside under continuing supervision as Indian country. The rule does not require federal fee title, a reservation label or a prescribed administrative form. It does require the federal relationship those cases describe. Political dependence and territorial jurisdiction are distinct: Cherokee Nation and Worcester do not make every parcel owned by a federally recognized tribe Indian country. The Ninth Circuit’s six-factor approach cannot excuse the absence of either statutory predicate by balancing cohesiveness, government services and general dependence. Hagen and Yankton Sioux preserve the difference between title and territory; neither opening land nor fee ownership alone determines status.

##### ANCSA removed both predicates from these lands

**Holding and operative rule:** These former-reservation lands, conveyed in unrestricted fee after ANCSA expressly revoked the reservation and without continuing federal supervision of their use or disposition, are not a dependent Indian community. The Tribe’s asserted territorial authority to tax this nonmember construction consequently fails.

**Authority:** All nine join the Court’s complete statutory construction, application and geographic limit.

**Controlling explanation:** ANCSA §1618(a) expressly revoked this reservation, §1603 extinguished aboriginal land claims, and the settlement authorized fee conveyances without continuing federal restrictions on transfer. The corporations’ §1618(b) election selected former-reservation land instead of monetary and other land benefits; it did not preserve the revoked reservation or impose a new Indian-use restriction. Their later conveyance to the Tribe likewise did not recreate a federal set-aside. Section 1636(d) protects land against adverse possession, real-property taxes and certain judgments while it remains unsold, unleased and undeveloped. Those conditional asset protections do not require federal approval of ordinary transfers or federal supervision of land use. Health, welfare, social and development programs benefit people without supplying the missing territorial supervision. The Tribe’s self-determination argument cannot substitute political recognition for the land relationship Congress ended. No allotment is at issue. The Court leaves other Native parcels, trust land, tribal identity, benefits, member-based powers and independently established consensual authority untouched.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Sandoval and McGowan supply the federal set-aside and supervision predicates; their allowance for nonfederal fee ownership remains intact.
- Hagen and Yankton Sioux retain the operative-congressional-act inquiry and the distinction between title and territorial status; ANCSA’s express revocation and unrestricted settlement disposition supply the relevant change here.
- Cherokee Nation and Worcester do not convert recognized political dependence into territorial jurisdiction over every tribal-owned parcel.

#### Law After Decision

Section 1151(b) supplies no territorial tax authority on these former Venetie Reservation lands. The two-predicate rule governs dependent-community classification; it leaves the reservation and allotment categories distinct. No universal rule against tribal fee land, federal benefits or tribal government follows.

#### Source Notes

The land conveyance, tax dispute, ANCSA provisions and lower-court test are reported in [522 U.S. 520](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-522/pdf/USREPORTS-522-520.pdf) and 101 F.3d 1286. The material statutory protections are conditional; no Indian allotment or other Alaska parcel is before the Court. The original petition, briefs and conveyance instruments are not separately reproduced; the decision rests on the published statutory and record recitations.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

This Supreme Court review is complete. The Ninth Circuit’s contrary classification is reversed; implementation below must reject the asserted territorial tax basis consistently with this opinion. No private title, independent source of authority or different parcel is adjudicated.

**End of entry: Alaska v. Native Village of Venetie Tribal Government, plenary merits decision, February 25, 1998.**

### National Credit Union Administration v. First National Bank & Trust Co. and AT&T Family Federal Credit Union v. First National Bank & Trust Co., Nos. 96-843 and 96-847

Plenary merits decision, February 25, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 6, 1997. On writs of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the D.C. Circuit, 90 F.3d 525. The paired cases concern the banks’ right to seek APA review and whether §109 of the Federal Credit Union Act, 12 U.S.C. §1759, permits one federal credit union to add multiple unrelated occupational groups. The Court of Appeals allowed the challenge and rejected the agency’s construction after the District Court had sustained it.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

| Docket | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| National Credit Union Administration v. First National Bank & Trust Co., No. 96-843 | Affirmed, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer | Existing appellate invalidation remains operative |
| AT&T Family Federal Credit Union v. First National Bank & Trust Co., No. 96-847 | Affirmed, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer | Existing appellate invalidation remains operative |

The challenged approvals cannot stand under the occupational common-bond provision. The existing appellate invalidation remains operative. The dissenters would vacate and remand for dismissal of this APA challenge on the statutory zone ground. No new member-removal order, liquidation, monetary award, transition date or enlarged injunction is entered.

#### Opinion Topology

Stone-Zsela delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Its Article III, statutory-zone and occupational common-bond conclusions have those five direct joins. O’Connor dissents, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer, on the statutory zone of interests. The dissent reaches no common-bond merits question. No legislative-history rationale or conditional merits opinion is published.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### These banks may seek review of the membership expansion

**Holding and operative rule:** Directly competing banks injured by these completed charter approvals have Article III standing and fall at least arguably within the interests implicated by §109’s particular membership boundary for APA §702 review. They need not be the statute’s specially intended beneficiaries, but competitive injury alone does not establish a right to challenge every regulatory limitation.

**Authority:** The five-Justice Opinion of the Court supplies direct authority for the provision-specific statutory-zone rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The approvals permit customers who otherwise might use the respondent banks to obtain services from ATTF; injury is undisputed, is traceable to the approvals and can be redressed by setting them aside. Lujan supplies the distinct constitutional elements, and Bennett requires examination of the particular provision rather than the Act’s general purpose. Section 109 limits credit-union membership and thus the customers the institution principally serves. These competitors challenge the agency’s expansion across that precise boundary. Clarke and Data Processing permit that direct statutory connection without a special-beneficiary requirement. Air Courier separately requires a statutory interest beyond injury, but its collateral employment interests differ from this challenge by participants in the regulated customer market. The Court does not find that protecting bank profits was Congress’s purpose. Nor does it eliminate APA finality, adequate-remedy, preclusion or other applicable conditions; these completed, operative approvals present no established independent defect of that kind.

##### Unrelated occupational groups cannot replace the common bond

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 109’s occupational route requires a common occupational bond connecting the membership of the occupationally defined credit union; it does not authorize aggregation of unrelated employer groups merely because each has its own internal bond. The challenged agency policy crosses that statutory boundary and receives no deference at Chevron’s first step.

**Authority:** The same five Justices adopt the common text-and-structure rationale, its application and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The sentence limits federal credit-union membership, not merely the labels assigned to each component group. The plural groups permits multiple groups sharing a common occupational bond; it does not make a succession of unrelated bonds sufficient. The agency’s reading retains internal group and service-area conditions, but those are different from the connection Congress imposed on the membership. The parallel geographic alternative supports that reading. NCUA’s concession that the geographic groups must occupy the same defined community is an attributed argument, not a separate holding on geographic charters. MCI requires enforcement of an enacted boundary before deference. NationsBank permits reasonable financial classifications within an authorized domain and does not permit removal of its boundary. General rulemaking authority, serviceability and administrative efficiency cannot replace the required bond. A changed interpretation is not invalid merely because it changed. The decision imposes no single-employer requirement and does not determine the full breadth of a genuine occupation or association, geographic charters, or individual membership-retention rights.

##### The disposition enforces the challenged boundary without rewriting membership law

**Holding and operative rule:** The statutory invalidation applies to the challenged unrelated employer-group additions; the other membership commands and permissions in §1759 remain intact. Affirmance supplies no independent enlargement of the injunction reported below.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice opinion adopts this scope of construction and relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1759 permits elected persons and organizations within Board rules, subject to the statutory membership limitation, and requires each member to subscribe to at least one share and pay its initial installment. A uniform entrance fee is required only if the board of directors requires it. The distinct final sentence permits survivorship joint-tenancy shares with a member’s designated person; that person may not vote, obtain loans or hold office unless within the field of membership and a qualified member. The joint-tenancy permission is not unlimited membership or authority to add unrelated employer groups. None of those provisions is displaced. The Court resolves the challenged legal interpretation, not every approval condition or remedial detail. Future applications remain subject to the statutory limits then lawfully governing them.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lujan preserves injury, traceability and redressability as separate constitutional requirements.
- Bennett is applied to the interests arguably protected by the particular provision and preserves APA review conditions.
- Clarke and Data Processing supply the permissive, provision-connected competitor inquiry without a special-beneficiary requirement.
- Air Courier preserves the distinction between injury and the statutory zone; its derivative employment interest is distinguished.
- Chevron requires enforcement of the unambiguous statutory boundary before agency deference.
- MCI applies the prohibition on eliminating an enacted requirement through interpretation. NationsBank’s lawful-classification holding remains intact within statutory boundaries.

#### Law After Decision

The banks’ APA action may proceed, and the challenged unrelated occupational-group construction is invalid. The decision neither abolishes statutory-zone screening nor requires Congress to have enacted the FCUA especially to protect banks. It supplies no general ban on multiple employers sharing a genuine common occupation, no geographic-charter ruling and no rule invalidating administrative change as such.

#### Separate Writings

**O’Connor, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer, dissenting.** The dissent accepts the banks’ concrete economic injury and rejects any requirement that Congress expressly identify them as special beneficiaries. It nevertheless finds their asserted protection against competition only incidental to §109. The common-bond rule organizes a cohesive cooperative; it does not keep credit unions collectively out of an identifiable market, since a group may join another qualifying union or form one. Clarke’s permissive inquiry and Bennett’s provision-specific focus therefore do not reduce to the economic effect of enforcing any restriction. Air Courier preserves the need for this additional statutory relationship. The dissent would vacate the reviewed judgment and remand for dismissal of the APA action, removing relief dependent on it without approving the agency’s interpretation or denying Article III injury.

#### Source Notes

The administrative policies, litigation history, specific geographic concession and undisputed competitive injury are reported in [522 U.S. 479](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-522/pdf/USREPORTS-522-479.pdf). The governing membership text appears in the [1997 United States Code, 12 U.S.C. §1759](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1997-title12/pdf/USCODE-1997-title12-chap14-subchapI-sec1759.pdf). The reported nationwide injunction’s full wording is not separately available in the record described here; no omitted condition or enlarged remedy is inferred. The District Court’s statement that second-step reasonableness was not seriously disputed is not a universal waiver or an independent finding by this Court.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Review of both dockets is complete. The D.C. Circuit’s invalidation stands, and the agency must act consistently with the occupational common-bond limitation. The Court enters no new remedial program and decides no individual member’s status.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### A material fabrication dispute cannot be resolved by relying on the warrant it produced

**Holding and operative rule:** An officer’s personally responsible knowing or reckless falsehood material to probable cause cannot obtain qualified immunity merely from the warrant that deception procured. Competent conflicting sworn accounts of the essential observation require ordinary factual resolution; negligence, a failed search or ulterior purpose alone does not establish this claim.

**Authority:** The Court opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices, directly adopts the complete rule and this summary-judgment application. Stone-Zsela joins the deliberate-falsehood application, without joining the complete knowing-or-reckless and retained-immunity rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Franks requires culpable material falsehood; Malley prevents judicial issuance from automatically insulating the responsible officer. Clanton preserves both propositions and the corrected-affidavit inquiry. Laboy asserted that he saw two men exchange a firearm and enter the house. The resident swears that no man entered then, and her husband supports her account that he was elsewhere. The other officer reports what Laboy told him rather than an independent observation. This evidence presents a credibility dispute about the asserted observation necessary to probable cause; it is not merely an inference from an empty search. The Court assumes the competent plaintiff-favorable account for summary judgment without finding a lie. Crawford-El retains ordinary proof rather than an enhanced motive threshold. The alleged fabrication must still be proved, together with materiality, responsibility, causation and injury. No liability, damages, mandatory trial or automatic discovery entitlement follows.

##### Each defendant must be connected to the asserted constitutional wrong

**Holding and operative rule:** Participation in a later search or supervisory rank alone does not establish responsibility for an affiant’s fabrication. Summary judgment stands where the supported record supplies no knowledge, assistance, agreement or affirmative causal link connecting the other officers or Puerto Rico supervisors to the alleged falsehood.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this severable personal-responsibility ground and its record application.

**Controlling explanation:** The other officers’ presence supplies no evidence that they knew of, helped create or agreed to the alleged invention. The monthly warrant expectation and remote judicial criticism of Laboy do not establish a supervisor’s encouragement, acquiescence or deliberate indifference to this alleged deception. Fletcher separates actual evidentiary conduct from official role; Crawford-El preserves personal responsibility and causation. These principles reject collective liability without immunizing knowing assistance or a supported supervisory wrong in a different record. The disposition concerning federal supervisor Ilario rests instead on the untimely appeal, not these merits grounds.

##### The timely execution claims fail on their own record and conduct-date grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** Qualified immunity on the timely search-scope, announcement and force components is sustained under the law and information relevant to December 6, 1993, without deciding that every challenged act was constitutional. Neither a Supreme-Court-only notice rule nor a physical-injury prerequisite governs.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join these distinct applications of the retained Harlow/Anderson defense.

**Controlling explanation:** Garrison and Ross confine the weapons search to places capable of containing the authorized objects; no evidence identifies a search beyond that scope. Uncompelled questions about money do not establish the claimed clearly unlawful expansion and authorize no unrestricted money search. On announcement, the supported preconduct legal setting left reasonable uncertainty concerning this entry’s constitutional requirement. Elder requires all relevant pre-existing law, and Lanier permits obvious coverage without a factual twin; later Wilson and Richards do not themselves provide 1993 notice. The ultimate reasonableness of the unannounced entry remains open. Graham governs force from the contemporary security perspective. The ax entry, officers, dogs and blockade formed an active weapons search; the reported conduct inside was orderly. Assuming that Colón pointed his gun and threatened the returning daughter who insisted on passing the blockade, this record does not establish conduct plainly outside the latitude allowed by 1993 law. Smith concerned considerable force against a wholly unresisting arrestee and does not turn injury into a necessary element. The security context, not the absence of touching alone, supports this immunity result. No universal permission to threaten a person follows.

**Holding and operative rule:** The untimely appeals from the separately final Plichta and Ilario judgments do not permit review of their merits, and the undeveloped counsel-access claim remains waived. **Authority:** The eight-Justice Court opinion adopts these grounds. Stone-Zsela agrees only with these dispositions and adds no rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The reported Rule 54(b) judgments were entered May 29 and July 22, 1996; the May 5, 1997 notice came after the applicable 60-day period. The computer component therefore supplies no holding that an unsuccessful attempt to access files cannot be a search, and the federal-supervisor component supplies no supervisory-merits ruling. Counsel access was not specifically developed on appeal. Section 1367 and City of Chicago keep supplemental power separate from discretionary retention: remand of one federal claim does not determine state claims against every defendant. This is the plaintiffs’ appeal from final judgments, so Johnson and Behrens’ limitation on officers’ interlocutory factual appeals does not bar ordinary final-judgment evidentiary review. The Court neither compels retention nor directs dismissal of every state claim.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Private association requires its own protection and employment inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The actual selective, enduring intimate relationship and its religious expression cannot be denied constitutional protection merely because they lack civil-marriage recognition or include an invitation-only ceremony. A public employer’s genuine operational interests remain relevant, but generalized forecasts, presumed lawbreaking and presumed government endorsement do not conclusively justify this defense summary judgment.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Roberts and Rotary distinguish close personal association from association for protected expression and require examination of the actual relationship and activity. On the plaintiff-favorable record, the selective, enduring relationship and its religious expression satisfy those protected predicates; their protection is not left open merely because the relationship lacks civil-marriage recognition. Shahar seeks protection against an employment penalty, not marital status or benefits. Umbehr separates entitlement to a benefit from an unconstitutional reason for withholding it. Jeffries requires attention to actual functions and institutional needs: prevention need not await disruption, but a reasonable forecast does not alone settle the legality of penalizing protected activity. Confidentiality, credible representation, assignment flexibility and professional trust remain substantial concerns. Neither a general office label nor speculation about endorsement establishes inability to perform lawful duties. No lawbreaking or refusal of duties is established, and later insurance or name-change evidence cannot supply contemporaneous knowledge. Waters protects reasonable factual reliance only when the substantive rule permits disciplining the conduct described and establishes no universal interview requirement. The remaining employment justification, causal application and relief return for adjudication; the Court orders no retention of a lawyer unable to perform necessary work.

##### II. Free exercise cannot be absorbed into a general employee-speech balance

**Holding and operative rule:** A public-employment decision alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise must be examined for neutrality and general applicability under its own governing rule. If the actual restriction is nonneutral or not generally applicable, the employer must establish a compelling justification and narrow tailoring; religious impact alone does not establish that trigger.

**Authority:** Part II has Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith preserves neutral, generally applicable rules. Lukumi requires examination of objective targeting and comparable secular conduct; Brown applies the required justification to religion-specific employment restrictions while preserving control of work, resources, coercion and official representation. The lower court assumed religious protection and treated the claim as resolved by the same general employment balance. That does not answer whether this withdrawal imposed the prohibited religious disability. The contrary argument is substantial: Bowers may have responded to the represented relationship and perceived job consequences regardless of religious form. That is an argument requiring proper assessment, not an established hypothetical fact that disposes of the independent claim. Sincerity does not prove targeting, and the absence of theological compulsion does not defeat sincere exercise. All six joiners reserve the trigger’s ultimate application and any properly supported justification. No finding of discrimination, general religious exemption or new statutory claim follows.

##### III. The actual equality disadvantage requires ordinary constitutional justification

**Holding and operative rule:** An adverse civilian employment classification does not escape ordinary equal-protection review by being described as conduct-based or as a hiring choice. The actual disadvantage must bear a rational relation to a legitimate governmental interest; no new orientation-based heightened tier is adopted.

**Authority:** Part III has Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, five direct joins. Stone-Zsela’s different proposed scrutiny standard is not controlling and is not combined with this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Romer applies ordinary rationality to the actual disability, allowing reasonable predictions and generalizations while excluding bare harm or irrational prejudice. Valot distinguishes that inquiry from justification for penalizing protected activity and from a proved same-decision defense. The lower court’s conduct/employment characterization cannot itself resolve the claim. The office’s professional and representative responsibilities remain legitimate considerations; remand does not make every predictive judgment invalid or require proof of actual disruption. Walmer, Thomasson and Richenberg address military and conduct-related settings, not an irrebuttable civilian inference from a religious commitment ceremony. Bowers v. Hardwick concerns its specified conduct; orientation or the ceremony alone proves no violation. The Court neither decides liability nor requires intermediate scrutiny. The five-Justice ordinary-rationality instruction governs the equality remand.

##### IV. Causation, conduct-date immunity and relief remain independent

**Holding and operative rule:** Remand requires claim-specific constitutional predicates, personal responsibility, causation and lawful relief. Where retaliation analysis applies, protected activity must be a substantial or motivating factor and a same-decision defense requires proof by a preponderance; ordinary rationality’s conceivable basis is not that proof.

**Authority:** Part IV is joined by the five common Court-opinion Justices; O’Connor joins its application to the free-exercise remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Umbehr and Jeffries preserve actual causal responsibility and the counterfactual defense. Bowers pleaded qualified immunity, but neither lower court decided it and he did not request appellate affirmance on that ground. The Court does not award the defense, decide waiver or automatically revive it. Any procedurally available personal-damages defense concerns notice in July 1991; later Romer, Brown, Umbehr, Jeffries, Valot and Danahy cannot supply retrospective notice. Prospective relief, any proper successor, personal damages and official-capacity relief retain separate predicates. Later evidence may have a lawful remedial relevance without becoming contemporaneous motive. Elapsed time establishes neither mootness nor a continuing job entitlement. The mandate compels no interview, discovery plan, trial, reinstatement, backpay, fees or damages award.

### Bogan v. Scott-Harris, No. 96-1569

Plenary merits decision, March 3, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 3, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the First Circuit. The Court reviews personal damages judgments under 42 U.S.C. §1983 against Fall River’s mayor and city-council vice president for introducing, approving and signing the ordinance abolishing Scott-Harris’s department. The question is whether those acts receive absolute legislative immunity despite the retaliatory motive found by the jury. Only Bogan and Roderick petition; the city judgment, which the Court of Appeals set aside, is not restored.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Reverse, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Bogan and Roderick receive absolute legislative immunity for the challenged legislative acts. The reviewed personal damages claims must be disposed of consistently with that immunity. No new factfinding mandate, municipal immunity, reinstatement of the city verdict or ruling on prospective relief follows.

#### Opinion Topology

Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt the functional immunity rule and its application to both officials. No separate writing issues.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Local legislative work receives absolute personal damages immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** Local officials performing genuine legislative functions have absolute immunity from personal damages under §1983 for those acts. The protection follows the function, so an executive official participating in that legislative process receives it for legislative work without immunity for all executive conduct.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts the rule and the distinction between function and title.

**Controlling explanation:** Tenney protects legislative independence against damages litigation over legitimate legislative activity. Lake Country Estates establishes that the institutional justification does not disappear below the state legislature. Local scale likewise does not remove the need to deliberate and enact policy without personal damages exposure. Antoine requires historical and functional support rather than immunity based on importance or title; that support exists for the lawmaking process. Consumers Union recognizes that executive participation in legislative work can carry the corresponding immunity. The historical municipal-liability examples do not establish an exception for these particular lawmaking functions merely because municipal officials were defendants. The Court decides personal damages immunity for the acts before it, not municipal liability or every form of prospective relief.

##### This budget ordinance is legislative in substance and form

**Holding and operative rule:** Roderick’s committee work and vote, and Bogan’s introduction of the budget and signature on the abolition ordinance, are legislative acts here because they enact prospective policy concerning the city’s departments, offices and resources. The retaliatory-motive verdict does not transform those otherwise legislative functions into administrative acts or defeat absolute immunity.

**Authority:** All nine join the complete classification, motive rule and bounded reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** The budget anticipated reduced state aid, froze salaries and proposed eliminating 135 positions. The ordinance eliminated an entire department and its office prospectively, even though Scott-Harris was its sole employee. It therefore determined future structure and resource allocation as well as affecting the incumbent. Forrester’s individualized administrative personnel decision is materially different. The Court examines substance as well as formal enactment and does not decide whether formal legislative character alone would suffice in another case. Once this function is correctly classified, Tenney’s protection cannot depend on a jury’s assessment of motive. Fletcher likewise separates protected function from allegedly improper purpose; its personal-attestation exception illustrates that different acts remain independently classified. The jury’s rejection of racial discrimination and finding of retaliation are not reweighed. Immunity does not declare the acts constitutional. Separate administrative execution, personal discharge decisions outside this lawmaking function, ministerial enforcement and unclassified threats receive no automatic protection.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Tenney supplies protection of the legislative process and the rule that improper motive does not defeat immunity for legislative acts.
- Lake Country Estates supports function-based legislative immunity below the state-legislature level; the principle extends here to local lawmaking.
- Consumers Union supports equivalent protection for executive participation in the legislative function.
- Antoine preserves the obligation of the official claiming immunity to establish historical and functional justification; importance or office alone remains insufficient.
- Forrester’s administrative personnel action is distinguished from prospective abolition of a department and office.
- Fletcher preserves separate classification of actual functions and the motive/function distinction. Richardson’s different private-prison setting does not negate historically supported public legislative immunity. Clinton’s function-based analysis is consistent, but its distinct presidential constitutional rationale is unnecessary here.

#### Law After Decision

Absolute legislative immunity defeats the reviewed personal damages claims for these local legislative acts. The functional inquiry remains necessary, and the Court leaves formal legislative character alone undecided. The decision creates no general personnel immunity, municipal absolute immunity or defense for all administrative implementation.

The city-council vote was a municipal act, distinct from this Court’s vote. The jury’s retaliation finding is accepted for the immunity issue; it is not a new merits finding by this Court. The full trial record and original pleadings are not separately reproduced.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

This Supreme Court review is complete. The lower courts must give effect to the officials’ absolute immunity on the reviewed legislative damages claims. No liability or proximate-cause ruling is needed, no additional evidentiary hearing is directed and no claim against a nonpetitioning defendant is restored.

### Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach, No. 96-1482

Plenary merits decision, March 3, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 10, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Ninth Circuit. The question is whether an MDL transferee court may use 28 U.S.C. §1404(a) to assign a transferred action to itself for trial despite §1407(a)’s remand command. Lexecon’s Illinois diversity tort action was transferred to Arizona for coordinated pretrial proceedings. The Arizona court entered pretrial dispositions, denied requests to suggest remand and retained the defamation claim for a defense trial judgment; the Ninth Circuit sustained the self-transfer. Lexecon preserved the objection through remand requests, mandamus and direct appeal.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Reverse and remand, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The defamation trial judgment resulting from the preserved unlawful self-transfer must be set aside and the continuing action returned to the originating district through the proper §1407 process. The authorized pretrial summary judgments, including the distinct Cotchett Rule 54(b) judgment, are not automatically void; their properly remaining appellate issues retain their own posture.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Reverses and remands | All eight Associate Justices join Parts I–III; Stone-Zsela joins Parts I–II on statutory return and the trial-forum remedy, and agrees only with Part III’s ancillary dispositions |

The opinion rests on text, structure and the preserved objection. It adds no legislative-history rationale. No separate writing issues.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The MDL transferee cannot defeat mandatory return through self-transfer

**Holding and operative rule:** Unless the transferred action has previously terminated, §1407(a) requires the Panel to remand it to the original district at or before completion of coordinated pretrial proceedings. Section 1404(a) does not authorize the transferee court to retain that continuing action for trial by transferring it from itself to itself.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by all nine Justices and supplies direct statutory authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1407 authorizes coordination for pretrial proceedings and commands return of each continuing action. The Panel may separate and remand particular claims earlier; an action that has actually terminated needs no return. Changing the asserted venue basis does not terminate the action. Addressing the command to the Panel does not authorize the transferee court to prevent its performance. Section 1404(a)’s general permission to transfer to another eligible district or division coexists with, rather than defeats, this specific temporary allocation. Section 1407(f) permits only rules consistent with Acts of Congress, so the cited self-assignment practice cannot alter the return obligation. Subsection (h)’s specific Clayton Act trial-transfer authorization does not confer that power in this tort action. The investment in coordinated pretrial work is substantial, but efficiency cannot delete the enacted sequence. A later lawful transfer by the proper court and consent or waiver questions not presented remain open.

##### II. The preserved trial-forum error requires correction of this judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** A party that preserved its §1407 return objection need not prove that a different jury would have reached a different verdict to obtain correction of the resulting unauthorized trial forum. This uncured violation requires setting aside the defamation trial judgment and enforcing statutory remand.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by all nine Justices; its remedy follows the mandatory return right asserted here.

**Controlling explanation:** Lexecon repeatedly requested remand, sought mandamus and then pursued the promised direct appeal. The possibility that Arizona could originally have been a proper venue does not supply the procedure Congress required after an MDL transfer. Caterpillar preserves a completed judgment when the jurisdictional defect actually disappeared before trial; the prohibited retention for this trial never received an equivalent cure. Good Real Property rejects inventing dismissal of a substantive action as a sanction for administrative delay. This case instead directly enforces a particular allocation of the trial forum without extinguishing the cause of action. No constitutional or subject-matter-jurisdiction label is necessary, and the Court adopts no universal rule that every procedural error defeats a completed judgment. The statutory return must occur before any independently lawful later transfer; no permanent right against such a transfer is adjudicated.

##### III. Authorized pretrial work and unreviewed tort issues remain separate

**Holding and operative rule:** The improper self-transfer does not automatically invalidate decisions made within authorized pretrial proceedings or decide the merits of the underlying tort claims. Properly remaining appellate challenges must be addressed in their own posture; the unpreserved request for prospective application does not alter this disposition.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees with these dispositions without joining an additional rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The malicious-prosecution and abuse-of-process rulings and the Cotchett Rule 54(b) judgment are pretrial dispositions, not the defamation trial verdict. Lexecon disputed the Rule 54(b) exercise of discretion without denying power to enter an authorized pretrial judgment. Those distinctions prevent wholesale reinstatement of every claim merely because trial retention was unlawful. The argument that the defamation claim should independently have been dismissed before trial remains for proper consideration, not a new factual or tort-law ruling here. The prospectivity request was not presented below or in opposition to review and supplies no basis for a different result in this case. No comprehensive retroactivity doctrine, new waiver finding or adjudication of a later transfer is adopted.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Caterpillar’s completed-judgment protection requires an actual pretrial cure; the preserved trial-forum defect here is distinguished as uncured. Its statutory remand duty remains mandatory.
- Good Real Property preserves mandatory administrative duties while rejecting an unstated forfeiture-dismissal sanction; direct enforcement of the specified trial allocation is different.
- City of Chicago retains the distinction between statutory power and convenience or discretionary retention; it supplies no authority to enlarge §1407’s temporary pretrial grant.

#### Law After Decision

Section 1407’s return command cannot be displaced by a transferee’s §1404 self-transfer. Authorized pretrial adjudication remains distinct from unauthorized trial retention, and a preserved objection secures the statutory forum remedy without independent proof that the verdict would differ. No general subject-matter-jurisdiction invalidity, bar on consensual arrangements or decision about a later lawful transfer follows.

#### Source Notes

The transfer, preservation history, trial and distinct pretrial judgments are reported in [523 U.S. 26](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-26.pdf). Sections 1404 and 1407 appear in the official 1997 United States Code. The decision uses the enacted statutory text and the reported contemporary rule argument; later Panel rule revisions supply no premise. The original motions, trial record and complete briefs are not separately reproduced, and no additional waiver or trial error is inferred.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for proceedings consistent with mandatory remand to the Northern District of Illinois and correction of the defamation trial judgment. The proper courts must keep the distinct pretrial and remaining appellate issues separate. The Court decides no tort liability, automatic new verdict or permanent prohibition on lawful later transfer. This Supreme Court review is complete.

**End of entry: Lexecon Inc. v. Milberg Weiss Bershad Hynes & Lerach, plenary merits decision, March 3, 1998.**

### Spencer v. Kemna, No. 96-7171

Decision after plenary review, March 3, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 12, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Eighth Circuit, which affirmed dismissal of the §2254 petition. Spencer challenges the September 1992 parole revocation, not the original burglary and stealing convictions. He filed on April 1, 1993 while imprisoned under the revocation; the challenged sentence expired October 16, 1993. Review concerns statutory custody, continuing Article III injury, mootness exceptions and the asserted effect of possible civil-remedy limits.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Affirm dismissal as moot, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens.

The dismissal rests on absence of a continuing Article III controversy, not failure of custody when the petition was filed or a decision on the revocation’s merits. Stevens would reverse and remand the live petition for its remaining lawful adjudication.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg | Affirms mootness dismissal | O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join all parts; Stone-Zsela joins Part II’s actual-continuing-injury ground and mootness result, but agrees only with Part II’s capable-of-repetition and litigation-delay dispositions and Parts I and III’s ancillary dispositions, without those additional rationales |
| Dissent | Stevens | — | Would reverse and remand | Recognizes the particular official adverse adjudication’s continuing reputational consequence; does not decide the revocation merits or an affirmative section 1983 exception |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Custody at filing and a continuing controversy are separate requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2254’s custody requirement is satisfied when the petitioner is held under the challenged revocation at filing; later expiration does not retroactively defeat that threshold. Article III nonetheless requires a concrete continuing injury after the challenged sentence expires.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, seven direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with the custody disposition without an additional ground; Stevens also accepts custody at filing.

**Controlling explanation:** Spencer was imprisoned under this revocation on April 1, 1993. The District Court’s alternative suggestion that expiration removed statutory custody is incorrect. The later seven-year imprisonment arises from an unrelated conviction, rather than the unexpired consecutive aggregate addressed in Garlotte. A timely filing therefore satisfies the statutory entry condition without itself supplying an enduring constitutional controversy. Lujan and Arizonans require an actual personal interest throughout adjudication, not simply an interest that existed when suit began.

##### II. The asserted collateral consequences and exceptions do not keep this petition live

**Holding and operative rule:** After this parole-revocation sentence expires, collateral injury is not presumed merely from the revocation; the petitioner must show an actual continuing consequence redressable in this action. Speculative future parole, sentencing or evidentiary uses, the possibility of recurrence and litigation delay do not supply that showing on this record.

**Authority:** Part II’s continuing-injury rule and mootness result have eight direct joins: Stone-Zsela and the seven Associates joining the Court opinion. The seven Associates also directly adopt the specific exception and delay applications.

**Controlling explanation:** An attack on a conviction is distinct from this challenge to a revocation of an otherwise valid sentence. Lane supports examining the actual surviving interest, although Spencer, unlike Lane’s respondents, challenges the adverse revocation finding itself. Future parole use depends on discretionary treatment, sentencing use on later legal developments, impeachment on future testimony and permissible use, and other-crimes evidence on additional contingencies. No present mandatory disability or demonstrated imminent adverse use is established. MacArthur’s actual statutory information interest and existing damages action do not make every retained adverse record a live habeas controversy; the Court adopts no categorical exclusion of reputational injury. The capable-of-repetition exception requires both a duration too short for full review and a reasonable expectation that this claimant will again face the action. Neither the asserted delay nor possible injury to others establishes those conditions. State extensions and delayed judicial action cannot create Article III power through fault or estoppel. Expiration occurred by the sentence’s terms, not a newly established strategic cessation. The Court decides no revocation claim, criminal guilt or exoneration.

##### III. An unresolved future civil remedy does not create this habeas controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** Heck and Edwards do not establish an unavoidable favorable-invalidation bar for every former prisoner: their controlling rules expressly reserve that category and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity. The unresolved scope of a possible later civil action does not create a damages claim in this habeas case or dispense with continuing Article III injury.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, seven direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition of this ancillary contention without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Heck’s decided prerequisite concerns the specified in-custody, conviction-negating damages claim; Edwards preserves that limit while requiring genuinely independent injuries to be classified by their consequences. Neither decision supplies the universal former-prisoner bar assumed by Spencer’s argument. A claim whose success does not necessarily invalidate the revocation would also require its own analysis. The Court need not choose an affirmative former-prisoner exception to reject the asserted inevitable bar. No damages claim is pleaded here, and possible difficulty obtaining another remedy does not empower the Court to decide an otherwise moot habeas petition. The existing reservations remain open rather than becoming either a categorical bar or a newly adjudicated exception.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lane is applied for the continuing-interest inquiry while its unchallenged-revocation posture is distinguished; no automatic consequence is inferred for this revocation.
- Lujan and Arizonans preserve an actual personal controversy throughout review. Arizonans’ vacatur remedy does not require clearing a nonexistent intervening merits judgment here.
- Garlotte’s continuous consecutive-sentence aggregate is distinguished from Spencer’s unrelated later custody; custody at filing remains sufficient for the statutory threshold.
- MacArthur preserves supported actual personal information interests without creating a universal reputation-based habeas rule.
- Heck and Edwards retain their bounded invalidation prerequisite and express former-prisoner/realistic-opportunity reservations; no affirmative section 1983 exception or universal bar is added.

#### Law After Decision

A timely custody-based habeas filing does not survive expiration of the challenged sentence without continuing Article III injury or a valid exception. This record establishes neither. The revocation’s merits remain unadjudicated, and the separate civil-remedy questions expressly reserved by Heck and Edwards remain open.

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens distinguishes Lane because Spencer challenges the official adverse revocation finding itself, not merely completed custody while leaving that finding untouched. He considers the particular government adjudication and the State’s continuing reliance on the incorporated serious-misconduct report a present personal reputational consequence that invalidation could meaningfully remove. He does not presume every revocation carries statutory disabilities or depend on a future crime, audience reaction or invented damages claim. The written order found condition violations and incorporated a report; it was not an express rape conviction, and the State’s characterization remains distinct. MacArthur’s requirement of a supported personal interest does not, in his view, erase this already imposed official determination. He would remand for unresolved lawful adjudication, without granting a writ, finding procedural wrongdoing or deciding the civil-remedy question.

#### Source Notes

The revocation order, incorporated report, custody dates, claimed procedural defects and later separate sentence are reported in [523 U.S. 1](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-1.pdf). The woman’s allegations, Spencer’s admitted cocaine use and denial of force, the written condition-violation order and the State’s description remain distinct. The underlying reports and complete record are described through published recitations; they establish no criminal conviction for the alleged conduct. No reputation-only submission or damages demand by Spencer is attributed to him. [Lane, 455 U.S. 624](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-455/pdf/USREPORTS-455-624.pdf), supplies the earlier posture and continuing-interest analysis.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The habeas action remains dismissed as moot, without constitutional-merits preclusion. No writ, new revocation hearing, expungement, damages or exoneration is ordered. This Supreme Court review is complete, and no continuing return condition is created.

**End of entry: Spencer v. Kemna, decision after plenary review, March 3, 1998.**
