### Benjamin v. Jacobson, Second Circuit No. 96-7957, 124 F.3d 162

Decision, May 7, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided May 7, 1998, on review of the Second Circuit's judgment concerning the termination of prospective relief in New York City jail consent decrees. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is recorded.

The questions are whether §3626(b) validly changes the conditions for continuing relief, what it terminates, and how the preservation inquiry must proceed. The district court vacated the decrees and declined to consider current evidence beyond the existing record. The Court of Appeals upheld the statute, rejected vacatur, treated the decree duties as surviving for state enforcement, and required an evidentiary opportunity. Only the termination framework and the presented constitutional objections are before the Court.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms in part, vacates in part, and remands for application of the amended termination statute with a meaningful opportunity to present material current evidence.

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presented constitutional objections and retrospective destruction of completed effects | Affirm rejection of the objections and of retrospective annulment, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Preserve the distinction between changed prospective duties and completed effects. |
| Automatic survival of the same prospective decree duties for state enforcement | Vacate, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | No automatic conversion of terminated judicial duties into enforceable state contractual duties. |
| Statutory preservation inquiry | Remand, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Permit the necessary current evidentiary presentation and determine the amended statutory findings; neither continuing relief nor a violation is predetermined. |

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III and V | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Supports all components | Statutory operation; Article III, vested-right, procedural and equality grounds; evidentiary remand and mandate. |
| Opinion of the Court, Part IV | Scalia | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Rejects the separate assumed contract-impairment objection | Federal-nonparty rationality ground. Stone-Zsela agrees in rejecting that objection and adds no ground. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The enacted conditions determine which prospective duties continue

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3626(b)(2) permits termination of covered prospective prison relief granted without substantive findings of necessity, narrowness and least intrusiveness; the movant must establish that predicate, and actual sufficient findings need no ritual statutory wording. Under amended §3626(b)(3), relief remains only on written, record-based findings that it is necessary to correct a current **and** ongoing federal-right violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn, and is the least intrusive means necessary to correct the violation.

**Authority:** Scalia's Parts I–III and V have all nine Justices; that common coalition governs Holdings 1–3 and 5. Holding 4 identifies the distinct eight-Justice ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Rouse supplies the amended conditions and the independent statutory termination route. Plyler establishes that Rufo's separate significant-change requirement is not an added obstacle to that route. The November 26, 1997 amendment applies to this pending case; the earlier current-or-ongoing formulation is not the governing text.

Consent decrees are covered relief. Consent alone does not establish that every term remains necessary, but a corrective remedy may properly extend beyond the minimum conduct rule when the statutory findings support it. Neither exceeding that minimum nor omitting prescribed words alone justifies termination. The court must identify supported current violations, test the retained relief against each statutory condition, and end the unsupported remainder. The Court does not determine whether any present New York jail condition satisfies those requirements.

**Precedent treatment:** Rouse is applied under the amended text; Plyler and Lewis retain their original-text holdings and scope limits; Rufo remains a distinct equitable modification route.

##### 2. Termination reaches future judicial obligations without erasing completed effects

**Holding and operative rule:** Termination ends the decree's unsupported prospective judicial duties themselves, not merely one forum's power to enforce identical continuing duties. It neither retrospectively declares the original decree erroneous nor erases completed determinations, payments or other lawful historical effects; a truly independent state-law agreement or accrued claim requires its own legal basis.

**Controlling explanation:** Rouse rejects both automatic survival and retrospective annulment. The statutory inclusion of consent decrees in prospective relief prevents relabeling the same judicial obligations as contracts to preserve them unchanged in another court. Section 3626(d) preserves state relief based solely on state-law claims; it does not itself establish an independent contract or require enforcement of a terminated federal decree.

Kokkonen distinguishes a private settlement from a judicial command and requires a proper basis for federal enforcement. The decrees' negotiated origin therefore does not answer which duties continue. Equally, a valid change to future obligations does not rewrite the lawfulness of completed judicial acts. The panel's automatic state-enforcement premise must be vacated while its refusal to destroy completed effects is preserved. The existence, breach, enforceability and proper forum of an actual independent contract remain undecided.

**Precedent treatment:** Rouse controls the prospective/historical distinction; Kokkonen preserves independent jurisdiction and the decree/contract distinction.

##### 3. The presented constitutional objections do not invalidate this framework

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may alter the conditions for prospective prison relief while courts independently determine violations, factual eligibility and remedial scope; this framework does not adjudicate the original dispute under unchanged law or cancel accrued damages and completed effects. Beneficiaries have no perpetual right to unchanged future enforcement, and the presented remedial classification satisfies ordinary rational review within the protections preserved below.

**Controlling explanation:** Rouse resolves the presented Article III, vested-right and equality objections. Robertson and Wheeling Bridge distinguish a change in governing law from legislative decision of a case; Klein does not forbid a real legal change that preserves the judicial task. Morgan Stanley and Plaut permit their bounded reinstatement of securities limitations dismissals, supplying neither absolute finality nor unrestricted reopening here.

Due process requires adjudication under the new conditions, not permanent insulation from prospective remedial change. The statutory restriction relates the actual supervisory disadvantage to legitimate institutional responsibility and resource concerns while preserving enforceable federal rights. Beach permits legitimate conceivable grounds; Heller and Romer still require a rational relationship to the actual classification. Absence of suspect status alone is insufficient. Bell preserves pretrial detainees' protection against punishment, and Lewis requires meaningful access to lawful remedies. A demonstrated necessary opportunity to present evidence cannot be withheld, as Holding 5 explains. A supported application excluded solely by the amended conjunction, an accrued independent claim, and appropriate protection against an actually supported threatened injury are not decided away.

**Precedent treatment:** The constitutional holdings of Rouse apply at their bounded scope; the changed-law, rationality and detainee-protection precedents retain the limits just stated.

##### 4. The assumed independent contract-impairment objection supplies no additional bar

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming for this objection alone an independent contract and substantial impairment, a rational federal restriction on continuing supervision is permissible where the United States is not a contracting party. Federal judicial approval and legislation benefiting States do not themselves make the United States a party to the bargain.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplying eight votes for the rationale. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejecting the objection.

**Controlling explanation:** Rouse applies National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Atchison's distinction between federal nonparty regulation and impairment of the Government's own contractual undertaking. Allocating institutional responsibility and resources rationally supports the limited supervision rule while federal-right remedies remain available. The argument incorrectly treats judicial approval as a federal contractual promise of permanent enforcement. The Court neither finds that a separate contract exists nor cancels accrued contractual liability, and it does not decide any actual state-law enforcement claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Rouse's assumed-contract holding and Atchison's federal-nonparty framework are applied without enlargement.

##### 5. This record requires a genuine opportunity to present current proof

**Holding and operative rule:** Where a court treats the existing record's silence as dispositive while legally refusing relevant current evidence necessary to the §3626(b)(3) inquiry, it must permit a meaningful evidentiary presentation and decide the statutory conditions on that record. This case-specific correction neither establishes a violation nor requires a new hearing in every termination proceeding.

**Controlling explanation:** The reported district-court treatment confined consideration to old material and declined an updated record. That restriction prevented a genuine decision of the preservation condition. Rouse involved actual evaluation of findings and monitoring reports, with only speculative deterioration offered as a reason for a new inquiry; its holding does not approve the different restriction here.

The prisoners must have the opportunity to supply material current evidence, and the court must determine its sufficiency and the necessity and scope of any retained obligation. A hearing or other evidentiary procedure follows the supported needs of this record and ordinary lawful adjudication, not the mere possibility that more information could be useful. Neither permanent continuation of the decrees nor unconditional immediate termination is ordered. The automatic-stay provisions and any general hearing entitlement remain outside the decision.

**Precedent treatment:** Rouse's evidentiary-opportunity limit is applied to an actual refusal of relevant proof, while its rejection of a speculative compulsory inquiry remains intact.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Rouse:** Applied to the amended termination framework, prospective duties, completed effects and constitutional objections; distinguished on the documented refusal of current evidence.
- **Plyler and Lewis v. Casey:** Preserve the independent statutory route, remedial safeguards, statutory scope and meaningful access under the text each construed; their original conjunction is not rewritten.
- **Rufo:** Retains significant-change and tailored-modification requirements for its distinct equitable route, without becoming an additional statutory prerequisite.
- **Kokkonen:** Preserves the distinction between judicial commands and separate agreements and the need for independent enforcement jurisdiction.
- **Morgan Stanley and Plaut:** Their defined securities-dismissal reinstatement holdings supply neither absolute immunity for future supervision nor a general congressional reopening power.
- **Robertson; Wheeling Bridge; Klein:** Preserve the distinction between changed governing law and legislative adjudication under unchanged law.
- **Beach; Heller; Romer:** Supply rational review with an actual relationship between classification and legitimate ends.
- **Bell v. Wolfish:** Preserves the independent substantive protection of pretrial detainees against punishment.
- **National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Atchison:** Supplies the federal-nonparty contract framework applied only on the assumed-contract premise.

#### Law After Decision

The amended termination conditions govern the decrees' prospective judicial operation. Completed lawful effects survive; identical judicial duties do not automatically survive by transfer to state enforcement. The statutory findings must be genuinely adjudicated after the necessary current evidentiary presentation. No current violation, independent contract, accrued liability, automatic-stay consequence or universal hearing right is established. No precedent is overruled.

### Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10, Second Circuit No. 96-9633, 127 F.3d 207

Decision, May 11, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided May 11, 1998, on review of the judgment sustaining the school district's worship and religious-instruction exclusions. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is recorded.

The church sought to rent the M.S. 206B gymnasium each Sunday for services including hymns, communion, Bible reading, preaching and teaching. The school serves grades 5–7. The district opened facilities for educational, civic, social, recreational and community-welfare activities, including secular ethical and character instruction; it had rented to the church for sports and a banquet. Its policy barred religious services and instruction but permitted religious discussion and viewpoints. The stipulated use record covers one school year and establishes consistent exclusion of worship services. The questions concern viewpoint discrimination and the proposed establishment justification; the separate free-exercise, equality and RFRA rulings are outside review.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exclusion of the requested private Sunday worship | Reverse, 5–4 | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg | Remand for consideration on lawful neutral terms and appropriate relief. |
| Exclusion of religious instruction on subjects admitted for comparable secular instruction | Reverse, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela | Remand within the admitted-subject boundary. Stone-Zsela accepts the viewpoint rule but would affirm this worship application without a separate instruction judgment. |

No final rental permit, damages award or fixed injunction text issues from this Court. Agreement that establishment does not categorically compel exclusion is not a separate vote for worship access.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Rejects categorical establishment justification | Entire establishment analysis. |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Breyer | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; Stone-Zsela joins only the general viewpoint rule | Supports instruction reversal | Eight join the application and remedy; all nine agree that admitted instruction cannot exclude an otherwise eligible religious perspective. |
| Opinion of the Court, Parts III and IV on worship and its remedy | Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Supports worship reversal and remand | Entire applied worship-access rule and its limits. The instruction portion of Part IV has the eight-Justice Part II coalition. |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stevens | Souter, Ginsburg; Stone-Zsela joins only the worship-activity analysis | Supports instruction reversal; opposes worship reversal | The integrated-service distinction; no establishment requirement to exclude. |
| Dissent from the disposition, with specified joins in the Court's reasoning | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Would affirm narrowly without remand | Worship as a distinct excluded use; instruction rule as a limit on affirmance, not a separate judgment on this request. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Private worship is not government worship merely because it uses school space

**Holding and operative rule:** Permitting this outside congregation to use school space for its own Sunday worship does not by itself establish religion. The district cannot justify this exclusion as constitutionally required, or rely on an identification of the school with the church, without facts establishing genuine governmental adoption rather than private access.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I is joined by all eight other Justices. It answers the establishment question without determining the separate scope of the speech forum.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini retains Lemon as a single inquiry in which purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats a claim on that ground; an otherwise legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives an attribution-and-function inquiry. The earlier access cases reserved standalone worship, so their results do not automatically settle this application.

Here the governmental act would be administering rental access. The pastor and congregation would arrange the services. No official selection, control, adoption, preference or obscured sponsorship is established. Tanford and Chaudhuri address the different act of incorporating devotional prayer into an official university program. Chabad and Pinette reject location alone as a sufficient attribution rule; Good News shows that the students' ages do not themselves turn independent expression into the school's speech.

The Second Circuit did not require reliance on the Establishment Clause, but treated avoidance of identification with a particular church as supporting the exclusion. That rationale cannot make religious identity and physical location decisive without actual governmental adoption. This conclusion neither immunizes every religious use nor opens every public building. Genuine future adoption or preferential operation would require its own inquiry, and government organization of worship as its own activity remains forbidden.

**Precedent treatment:** Agostini's modified Lemon inquiry is applied to the reserved worship setting; Tanford and Chaudhuri are distinguished as official programs; the contextual limits of Chabad, Pinette and Good News remain intact.

##### 2. An admitted instructional subject cannot exclude its religious perspective

**Holding and operative rule:** Once this forum admits instruction on a subject, the district may not exclude an otherwise eligible speaker because the instruction presents a religious perspective. Permitting religious discussion does not cure a ban on religious instruction where comparable secular ethical and character teaching is admitted.

**Authority:** All nine Justices support the general rule in Part II. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer also join its application and the instruction reversal; Stone-Zsela does not join that disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Lamb's Chapel protects a religious perspective on an admitted subject even assuming a nonpublic forum. Good News and Church on the Rock apply that principle to teaching and related devotional expression within admitted programs; Rosenberger protects viewpoint equality within the actual program. The district admitted broad education and secular ethical and character instruction. It cannot require the comparable religious speaker to abandon instruction and confine itself to a different permitted format of discussion.

The Court does not convert this limited forum into an unrestricted curriculum or require access for every subject. It reverses the judgment sustaining the challenged instruction exclusion only insofar as it reaches subjects actually admitted for comparable instruction. The church's integrated worship request presents a separate access question, decided in Holding 3; the instructional rule does not make every worship service an admitted use by definition.

**Precedent treatment:** Lamb's Chapel, Good News, Church on the Rock and Rosenberger are applied to the actual instructional comparator, preserving their forum and program limits.

##### 3. This broad private community forum cannot exclude the requested use solely for its devotional form

**Holding and operative rule:** In this actual after-hours educational and community forum, the district may not disqualify the church's otherwise comparable private Sunday meeting solely because its expression takes the form of worship. The district retains reasonable, viewpoint-neutral operational and genuine activity limits, but it cannot define comparable admitted uses simply by removing their religious expression.

**Authority:** Breyer's Parts III and IV are joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, a five-Justice controlling majority. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg oppose this application; no narrower rationale is assembled from their votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Perry Education and Cornelius permit a limited forum and genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral access restrictions. The actual forum, however, broadly admits community meetings and education, including moral and character development. A private meeting does not cease to serve those admitted functions merely because participants express their convictions devotionally. Widmar distinguishes religious exercise in a forum from government religious organization; Lamb's Chapel and Rosenberger prohibit subtracting the religious perspective from otherwise admitted expression.

The district's consistent exclusion proves that it maintained the challenged rule. It does not establish the rule's neutrality. The opposing approach treats the integrated service, particularly communion, as a categorically different activity. The Court rejects the premise that a religious meeting must have an exact secular sacramental counterpart before its educational and community expression is comparable. The denial rests on its worship character, not an established neutral scheduling, capacity, safety or supervision problem.

This is an applied holding on the described broad forum. It creates no right to every public building, continuous rental, preferential control, public financing of devotion or a permanently open forum. A genuinely different neutral activity boundary remains permissible. No compulsory disclaimer or universal sign rule is imposed. The absence of a categorical establishment bar is a distinct conclusion and is not itself the source of the access right.

**Precedent treatment:** The viewpoint principles of Lamb's Chapel and Rosenberger extend to this otherwise comparable worship use; the earlier standalone-worship reservations are resolved only at this scope. Perry Education and Cornelius retain genuine forum limits; Widmar supports the private/government distinction.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Agostini:** The governing modified Lemon inquiry is applied without reinstating independent former prongs or replacing it with a coercion-only rule.
- **Lamb's Chapel; Good News/Good Sports; Church on the Rock:** Their instruction and viewpoint holdings apply; their standalone-worship reservations are answered only for the present comparable use.
- **Rosenberger:** Viewpoint neutrality within the actual program supplies the speech principle; its printing-benefit setting is not converted into a general funding obligation.
- **Pinette and Chabad:** Preserve bounded private access and contextual attribution; location and religious identity alone are insufficient.
- **Tanford and Chaudhuri:** Official organization of devotional prayer remains prohibited and distinct from independent rental use.
- **Perry Education and Cornelius:** Preserve reasonable, viewpoint-neutral genuine subject and activity limits; consistency alone does not validate an exclusion.
- **Widmar:** Supports the distinction between private religious exercise in a forum and a governmental religious program.
- **Perry v. Los Angeles Police Department:** Supplies no traditional-public-forum classification or transplanted tailoring standard for this school.

#### Law After Decision

Private Sunday worship may not be excluded from this broad forum solely for its devotional form, and comparable religious instruction receives the same access as admitted secular instruction. Establishment does not categorically require the exclusion, while actual governmental adoption remains legally distinct. Neutral management and genuine forum boundaries survive; no unrestricted facility entitlement or funding obligation arises.

#### Separate Writings

Stevens, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, agrees that the instruction exclusion impermissibly disadvantages a religious perspective on admitted subjects. He disagrees with the worship holding: an integrated recurring church service, expressly including communion, is a genuinely distinct activity rather than merely instruction from a religious perspective. Perry Education and Cornelius permit limiting the forum to the instructional, civic, social and recreational uses actually opened. Consistent exclusion is relevant to identifying that forum, although it is not sufficient by itself to establish constitutionality. The partial dissent would affirm the worship exclusion while reversing instruction. It does not claim that establishment requires exclusion or that incidental prayer in an admitted program may automatically be banned. Stone-Zsela joins this worship-activity reasoning only.

Stone-Zsela would affirm the entire judgment narrowly and order no remand. The application sought regular Sunday worship, and the stipulated record supplies no separate request for permitted discussion or instruction; he therefore treats the agreed religious-instruction rule as a limit on affirmance, not a separate judgment. He finds no comparable worship activity in the actual one-year use record and accepts a reasonable, viewpoint-neutral activity boundary. If comparable activity were established, he would reverse. His affirmance rejects the identification-with-a-church rationale and does not approve excluding a religious perspective on an admitted subject. Under Agostini, school location and religious identity do not establish governmental worship; actual official adoption could change that answer. Neither his forum position nor the Court's ruling creates a right to every building, a duty to keep the forum open or public financing of devotion.

The complete Second Circuit opinion, 127 F.3d 207, including Cabranes's separate instruction analysis, supports the request, stipulated use record, policy and actual comparators. The record covers one school year and supplies no finding that the service would be closed to the public or that an admission fee was proposed. No separate Supreme Court argument date or docket is recorded. The questions describe the reviewed dispute; the petition text is unavailable.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Second Circuit for nondiscriminatory consideration and appropriate declaratory or injunctive relief within the two reviewed questions. The lower courts must apply the component holdings and otherwise lawful neutral conditions. The Court issues no permanent permit, damages award, fixed disclaimer, monopoly rental, required new evidentiary hearing or rewritten school policy. The ungranted free-exercise, equality and RFRA issues receive no new holding, and no automatic Supreme Court return is directed.

### Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc., Fourth Circuit No. 96-2412, 128 F.3d 233

Decision, May 14, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided May 14, 1998, on review of the judgment reversing defendants' summary judgment in the civil action arising from three murders. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is recorded.

The agreed issue for the Rule 56 motion was whether the First Amendment supplied a complete legal defense. For that motion only, defendants stipulated a purpose to assist criminal readers in murder for hire and actual operational assistance to the perpetrator through the publications involved. They did not concede specific knowledge of the plot, victims or conspiracy. Plaintiffs also stipulated intended legitimate readership, including writers, law-enforcement personnel, entertainment readers and researchers. Both the identified murder manual and the separate silencer manual were involved. All other issues of law and fact remained reserved.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The absolute constitutional defense fails on the assumed facts. No tort liability, damages, injunction against a publication or final factual determination is entered.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Kennedy delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Purposeful, materially effective assistance to murder has no categorical publisher immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** The First Amendment does not categorically bar civil liability where a publisher intentionally supplies detailed operational assistance to facilitate murder, intends criminal use, and the communication materially assists the resulting crime, subject to the applicable tort's substantive and causal requirements. This conclusion rests on the motion-only stipulated combination; publication, offensiveness, abstract advocacy, knowledge of possible misuse, foreseeability or a reader's independent imitation does not alone establish it.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion is joined in full by all eight other Justices and controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Giboney distinguishes expression that participates in independently unlawful conduct from protected advocacy. Claiborne Hardware bars civil responsibility based merely on association, advocacy or another person's violence; Brandenburg preserves its own imminent-lawless-action requirements for advocacy. Those protections cannot be bypassed simply by calling a book dangerous or a tort generally applicable.

The stipulated facts supply the different combination at issue here: a purpose to furnish operational help for murder for hire and actual material assistance to these completed crimes. The constitutional boundary therefore rests on intentional participation and its real assisting function, not persuasion, a disliked viewpoint or a judicial assessment of literary value. Dawson and Wisconsin v. Mitchell require a legally relevant connection rather than punishment of belief. Madsen and Schenck likewise preserve responsibility and actual-concert limits; sympathy or shared ideas do not establish participation.

The absence of knowledge of this particular plot does not create absolute immunity where purpose to assist criminal use and actual assistance are assumed. Nor does the existence of legitimate readers erase the stipulated assisting conduct. The Court rejects neither fact. It rejects only the proposition that public distribution necessarily immunizes purposeful participation regardless of those predicates.

The stipulations remain confined to the motion. Plaintiffs must establish the legally necessary facts through operative admissions or admissible proof in the reserved proceedings; defendants retain their factual and legal defenses. The Court decides no lesser-mental-state standard, general exception to Brandenburg's imminence requirement, prior restraint, or liability rule for news, fiction, historical works, ordinary technical information or generalized dangerous ideas. It does not adopt the lower opinion's broader statements about the publications' value or establish exclusive criminal readership.

**Precedent treatment:** Giboney's participation distinction and Claiborne Hardware's responsibility limits apply; Brandenburg's advocacy rule remains unchanged. Dawson, Mitchell, Madsen and Schenck preserve the required connection to actual unlawful conduct.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Brandenburg:** Retained for advocacy; no general immediacy exception is created.
- **NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware:** Applied to preserve protected advocacy, association and responsibility limits against liability for others' violence.
- **Giboney and Brown v. Hartlage:** Supply the distinction between expression and its function as participation in independently unlawful conduct, without a category of unprotected dangerous ideas.
- **Dawson and Wisconsin v. Mitchell:** Preserve the requirement that expression have a legally relevant connection to misconduct rather than serve as punishment for belief.
- **Madsen and Schenck:** Their responsibility and actual-concert limits remain; the decision does not turn shared viewpoint into participation.
- **Cohen v. Cowles Media:** No categorical press exemption follows, but the general-law label alone does not establish constitutional validity.

#### Law After Decision

Purposeful, materially effective operational assistance to murder may be subject to otherwise valid tort liability. The absolute defense fails only on the stipulated motion facts and stated constitutional predicates. Advocacy protections, ordinary tort elements, causal proof, defenses and the distinction between motion assumptions and final findings remain intact; no publication is banned and no liability is adjudged.

#### Source Notes

The Fourth Circuit's opinion, 128 F.3d 233, and its complete note 2 stipulation supply the motion-only concessions, lawful intended readership and lack of specific knowledge of the plot. Original filings are not reproduced. The stipulations neither settle later trial facts nor authorize a finding that only criminals were intended readers.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Fourth Circuit for ordinary proceedings on the reserved law and facts. The defendants' summary judgment on the complete First Amendment defense remains reversed. The Court directs no particular trial finding, damages award, evidentiary hearing or further Supreme Court event.

**End of entry: Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc., decision, May 14, 1998.**

### Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes, No. 96-779

Decision, May 18, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 8, 1997; decided May 18, 1998. On review of the Eighth Circuit's judgment, 93 F.3d 497, reversing judgment for the Commission on Forbes's First Amendment challenge to exclusion from a televised candidate debate.

The state broadcaster planned a selective, moderated debate program. Its spring 1992 selection category covered major-party candidates and other candidates with strong popular support. Forbes later qualified for the ballot, requested inclusion and was denied. Staff cited lack of campaign organization, appreciable voter support and press recognition as a serious candidate. The jury found that political pressure and disagreement with his views did not cause the exclusion. The question is whether this actual program created a forum requiring his admission and whether the exclusion was constitutionally permissible.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

The case returns for implementation of judgment for the Commission on this claim. No prospective publication requirement, fixed admission formula or compelled future broadcast is imposed.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. Justice Stevens dissents, joined by Justices Souter and Ginsburg; they would sustain the appellate judgment because the government's candidate-selection standards were insufficiently definite, without requiring universal ballot access.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. This selectively composed candidate debate is a nonpublic forum

**Holding and operative rule:** A public broadcaster's candidate debate is a nonpublic forum when its actual program retains editorial selection and has not been opened generally to every qualified candidate. Exclusions must be reasonable in light of the program's journalistic purpose and viewpoint neutral; neither public ownership nor the label journalism supplies immunity from those requirements.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. That six-Justice coalition controls both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The candidates express their own political views in this debate, so the broadcaster cannot treat their speech simply as the State's message. Yet a selective invitation program is not a general opening of airtime. Cornelius and Perry Education distinguish selective access from a designated public forum and preserve reasonable, viewpoint-neutral management.

The Commission planned a limited, moderated exchange and selected individual participants. Ballot eligibility did not itself open the program to every candidate. Rosenberger protects viewpoints within the opportunity actually created; it does not enlarge every opportunity into general access. Bronx likewise protects comparable private expression in a broad community forum while preserving genuine program boundaries. Its rental setting does not remove the broadcaster's supported selective function here. Denver Area supplies no categorical broadcasting classification or government editorial immunity.

The holding concerns this program, not every public broadcast or every candidate debate. A debate genuinely opened to all qualifying candidates would present a different access question. The State may neither disguise viewpoint exclusion as program definition nor rely on its own classification without examination of actual operation.

**Precedent treatment:** Cornelius and Perry Education supply the selective-access rule; Rosenberger and Bronx preserve viewpoint equality within genuine boundaries; Denver Area is not expanded beyond its distinct components.

##### 2. The supported exclusion is reasonable and viewpoint neutral

**Holding and operative rule:** A genuine assessment of public support and journalistic significance may justify exclusion from this selective debate when it reasonably serves the program's purpose and is not a pretext for viewpoint discrimination. The established criteria and application satisfy that rule here; the Constitution imposes no additional prerequisite that future criteria be published.

**Controlling explanation:** Limited discussion time supports selecting a manageable group that will provide a useful exchange. Reasonableness does not require the best or only conceivable debate design. The advance selection category and staff's supported assessment of organization, voter support and press recognition bear on the program's purpose. The jury's express rejection of political pressure and viewpoint disagreement confirms the absence of those asserted improper causes; it does not by itself substitute for examining the criteria's reasonable relationship to the program.

The contrary standards argument rightly identifies the danger of officials controlling political access through vague judgments of seriousness. It does not establish an additional constitutional requirement of a numerical formula, universal written policy or prospective publication for this otherwise reasonable, neutral selection. Courts retain authority to test genuine viewpoint neutrality and pretext.

Ballot qualification alone neither establishes strong public support nor defeats a legitimate selective format. This holding does not authorize incumbent favoritism, sham criteria, selective administration, exclusion because a viewpoint is unpopular or denial from a program actually opened on terms the candidate satisfies. It decides no damages amount, official immunity, election remedy or future broadcast obligation.

**Precedent treatment:** The nonpublic-forum reasonableness and viewpoint rules are applied to the established record without adding a new publication or formula requirement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Cornelius and Perry Education:** Applied to actual selective access and reasonable, viewpoint-neutral restrictions.
- **Rosenberger:** Preserves private viewpoints within the actual program rather than compelling universal access.
- **Good News/Good Sports and Bronx Household:** Preserve actual comparators and genuine forum boundaries; the planned selective debate differs from broad rental access.
- **Denver Area:** No universal public-broadcast classification or government editorial immunity is drawn from its separate components or incompatible rationales.

#### Law After Decision

This selective debate may use reasonable, genuinely viewpoint-neutral judgments about public support and journalistic significance. Forbes's exclusion satisfies that standard on the established record. Judicial review for pretext and discrimination remains; no universal candidate-access, published-criteria or numerical-threshold rule is created.

#### Separate Writings

Stevens, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, would sustain the appellate reversal on a narrower ground than an automatic right of every ballot-qualified candidate to participate. The State has created a platform for candidates' competing private political speech, and sufficiently definite, reviewable selection standards are necessary to constrain its discretion. Cornelius's access principles, Shuttlesworth's objection to uncontrolled permit discretion and Rosenberger's neutral-allocation requirement inform that conclusion. The spring category existed, but major-party status and strong popular support leave too much room for officials' judgments about seriousness. The jury's rejection of improper motive does not answer this distinct objection to the governing standards. The dissent permits reasonable selection, prescribes no particular polling number, finds no viewpoint bias contrary to the verdict, and would order no automatic damages or future broadcast.

#### Source Notes

The reported record at 523 U.S. 666 and the underlying Eighth Circuit judgment identify the advance selection category, invitations, ballot qualification, staff testimony and jury findings. The original trial papers are not reproduced. The verdict rejects improper motive; it is not a finding about every conceivable access standard. No broader past selection practice or later election fact is supplied.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower courts must implement judgment for the Commission on Forbes's reviewed First Amendment forum claim. No additional criterion-publication requirement applies. The abandoned statutory-access claim is not revived, and no separate damages, immunity, costs or future-election determination is made.

**End of entry: Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes, decision, May 18, 1998.**

### Bousley v. United States, No. 96-8516

Decision, May 18, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 3, 1998; decided May 18, 1998. On certiorari to the Eighth Circuit's denial of relief in a first §2255 proceeding.

Bousley pleaded guilty to a drug offense and using a firearm during and in relation to drug trafficking. He appealed his sentence without challenging the plea's validity. After Bailey explained that use requires active employment, he challenged the understanding of the firearm charge underlying his plea. The questions are whether that claim is available on collateral review, how procedural default affects it, and what factual innocence requires. The indictment charged use, not carrying.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices or different remedy | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Set aside the Eighth Circuit's rejection | Reversed, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | Set aside the rejection; the mandate is stated separately below. |
| Conditional gateway remand | Six adopt the controlling mandate | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stevens would immediately vacate the firearm conviction and permit a new plea; Scalia and Thomas would affirm | Opportunity to establish factual innocence, followed by adjudication of the plea-intelligence claim only if that gateway is satisfied. |

The firearm conviction is not immediately vacated. Neither factual innocence nor constitutional invalidity is found. The drug conviction is unaffected. The remand does not automatically require an oral evidentiary hearing, release or dismissal of the firearm charge.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer throughout; Stone-Zsela in I–III and IV.B; Stevens in I | Supports reversal and the conditional remand; Stevens seeks a different remedy | I: informed plea and substantive meaning; II: default; III: factual gateway; IV.A: actually forgone more-serious charges; IV.B: use-only indictment and conditional remand. |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stevens | No other Justice | Would immediately vacate the firearm conviction and permit a new plea | Joins I; rejects the default and innocence prerequisites. |
| Dissent | Scalia | Thomas | Would affirm | Cause is absent; the trial-conviction innocence gateway should not extend to this plea. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Correct knowledge of the offense is necessary to an intelligent plea

**Holding and operative rule:** A guilty plea requires real notice of the charge's essential nature. A materially mistaken description of a statutory element can render the plea unintelligent; Bailey's explanation of what the firearm statute prohibits is substantive law, not a new constitutional procedure barred by Teague.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Parts II–III and IV.B are joined by the same coalition except Stevens. Part IV.A has O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each stated rule has direct majority support.

**Controlling explanation:** Bailey requires active employment of a firearm; proximity, accessibility or possession alone is not use. Henderson and Smith require an understanding of the actual charge. A statutory construction states the substantive reach of the crime, as Rivers explains, rather than imposing a new trial procedure. Teague's procedural restriction therefore supplies no answer to this claim.

Broce distinguishes claims waived by an otherwise valid guilty plea from a challenge to the plea's own validity. Brady's concern with later disappointed expectations does not excuse a materially mistaken account of the offense's essential elements. Godinez likewise separates capacity to choose from the informed choice itself.

**Precedent treatment:** Bailey supplies the offense's meaning; Henderson and Smith supply notice; Rivers explains substantive interpretation; Teague does not bar this substantive claim; Broce, Brady and Godinez retain their distinct limits.

##### 2. Direct-review default requires cause and prejudice or factual innocence

**Holding and operative rule:** Failure to challenge plea intelligence on direct review defaults this claim. Neither anticipated defeat nor adverse precedent establishes cause where the legal argument was available; absent cause, the claim may proceed only through a sufficient factual-innocence showing, not a showing of legal error alone.

**Controlling explanation:** The distinction between using and possessing a firearm was available before Bailey. Reed's unavailable-claim concern does not encompass an argument thought unlikely to prevail, and Engle rejects expected futility as cause. Prejudice cannot replace missing cause.

Schlup nevertheless permits review where, considering the whole probative record under the correct offense, it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. That exceptional inquiry applies here despite the plea. The absence of a trial record does not justify treating an unsupported conviction as conclusive, but neither does it shift the inquiry to whether the existing plea record was formally sufficient. Carriger preserves the difference between passing this gateway and proving the underlying constitutional violation. The district court has not adjudicated innocence, and the Government does not contend that omission of that issue below independently waived this opportunity. The Court does not now decide constitutional invalidity; the gateway and underlying claim must be resolved in that order.

**Precedent treatment:** Reed and Engle delimit cause; Schlup supplies the demanding whole-record gateway; Carriger keeps the gateway separate from the constitutional merits.

##### 3. The evidence and actual plea bargain define the innocence inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government may offer admissible evidence outside the plea record to rebut factual innocence. Where it actually forgave an identified more-serious charge in the plea bargain, the innocence showing must also cover that charge; hypothetical charges, unproved bargains and alternative statutory verbs create no added burden.

**Controlling explanation:** Factual innocence concerns what occurred, not merely what the plea papers recite. Limiting the Government to those papers would convert the gateway into a legal-sufficiency test. The five Justices joining IV.A also require attention to an actual bargain relinquishing more-serious exposure, so that the inquiry does not disregard the exchange that produced the plea.

This indictment alleged use, not carrying. No bargain relinquishing carrying or another identified more-serious offense is established. Bousley therefore need establish absence of use under Bailey on this record. The Government cannot preserve the conviction simply by relabeling it an uncharged carrying conviction. A proved gateway permits adjudication of the plea-intelligence claim; it does not itself require vacatur or acquittal.

**Precedent treatment:** Schlup's factual inquiry is applied to a guilty plea with evidence beyond the plea record; Bailey continues to define the charged use offense.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Bailey:** Active employment remains necessary for the charged use offense; possession alone is insufficient.
- **Henderson and Smith:** Applied to real notice of the charge's essential nature.
- **Rivers:** Supplies the substantive effect of an authoritative statutory construction.
- **Teague:** Its new-procedure limitation does not bar this substantive statutory claim.
- **Broce and Brady:** Distinguished from an attack on the plea's informed character; a disappointed expectation and misinformation about an element remain different.
- **Godinez:** Competence does not itself establish informed assent.
- **Reed and Engle:** An available argument's expected failure does not establish cause.
- **Schlup:** Extended to this defaulted plea claim with its demanding factual test intact.
- **Carriger:** Passing the gateway remains distinct from proving constitutional error and entitlement to relief.
- **Hyde:** Its presentence withdrawal setting does not decide this collateral claim.

#### Law After Decision

A defaulted challenge to a materially misdescribed guilty plea may receive collateral review upon the required factual-innocence showing. Admissible proof beyond the plea record is available. Actual relinquishment of a more-serious charge matters; an imagined charge does not. Passing the gateway opens consideration of plea validity and supplies no automatic merits relief.

#### Separate Writings

Stevens joins the substantive and constitutional principles in Part I but would vacate the firearm conviction and permit Bousley to plead anew. Timmreck and Hill distinguish formal plea-taking defects from a constitutionally invalid plea. In his view, the erroneous understanding of the statutory element establishes the constitutional defect here, and failure to appeal does not require Bousley to prove innocence before obtaining correction. The Government would retain its ordinary burden if prosecution proceeds. His position does not require acquittal or dismissal with prejudice and does not affect the drug conviction.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, would affirm. He agrees that no cause excuses the default but rejects extending Schlup's trial-conviction gateway to guilty pleas. A plea leaves no comparable trial record of facts that participants may then have thought immaterial, may exchange admission for relief from greater charges, and may leave stale evidence insufficient to reconstruct either side of that exchange. Those features make the proposed exception materially different from Schlup's setting. The dissent does not treat possession as active use, find actual carrying, or decide Bousley's innocence.

#### Source Notes

The published record at 523 U.S. 614 supplies the charged offense, plea and review history, and the Government's position on the separate omission of innocence below. The original plea transcript and bargain agreement are not included in the available source record. No relinquished carrying or more-serious charge is established by the available record, and no such bargain is presumed.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The district court must permit the bounded factual-innocence inquiry and resolve any factual development needed under ordinary procedures. If Bousley satisfies that gateway, it must consider whether his plea was voluntary and intelligent and determine any lawful relief. No automatic live hearing, release or acquittal is ordered.

**End of entry: Bousley v. United States, decision, May 18, 1998.**

### Montana v. Crow Tribe of Indians, No. 96-1829

Decision, May 18, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 14, 1998; decided May 18, 1998. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 92 F.3d 826, which required transfer to the Tribe of the entire disputed state tax collection from Westmoreland.

The state taxes on coal from the Tribe's ceded-strip minerals were held invalid. The present dispute concerns approximately $46.8 million in severance taxes collected during 1975–1982 and $11.4 million in gross-proceeds taxes during 1975–1987, before the respective registry deposits. The question is whether the established invalidity requires payment of all those receipts to the Tribe, rather than an inquiry into its entitlement and the proper equitable remedy.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

No amount is awarded. The invalidity judgment and completed registry distributions remain undisturbed. Proper consideration of an independent partial restitution claim remains open; the decision orders neither a new compensatory award nor an automatic reopening of evidence or allowance of amendment.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The opinion rejects automatic total recovery and expressly preserves properly supported independent partial relief. Justice Souter concurs, joined by Justice O'Connor; his concurrence treats the existing prayer as encompassing partial restitution of excessive receipts and gives additional property-based grounds for considering that claim.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Invalidity of these taxes does not establish entitlement to every receipt

**Holding and operative rule:** An invalid state tax does not automatically entitle a tribe to all sums a nontribal taxpayer paid. The claimant must establish entitlement to the particular fund or remedy; lawful tribal taxing authority and a protected interest in the taxed resource do not alone transfer ownership of the whole state collection.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined in full by all eight Associate Justices. That coalition controls both holdings; the additional partial-restitution framework in Souter's concurrence is not adopted by the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The tax-invalidity judgment remains binding. Cotton Petroleum nevertheless distinguishes concurrent state and tribal taxing authority from taxation that unlawfully burdens the tribal interest. Rejection of these excessive taxes therefore does not establish that every possible state levy would have been unlawful or that the Tribe owns every dollar collected.

California separates the taxpayer's claim, economic burden and a different claimant's direct entitlement. Fulton and Reich require lawful correction in their proper claimant and procedural settings; neither commands payment of every invalid tax to any economically affected entity. Chickasaw Nation identifies statutory tax incidence but supplies no universal award of receipts to a different claimant.

The Tribe's resource interest and the United States' trustee role matter, but they do not prove the particular total award. Who paid, who bore the relevant loss, the actual agreements and preserved claims, and prior distributions must be examined. The taxpayer's failed or relinquished refund opportunity is not automatically the Tribe's claim or a release of it. Conversely, labeling the State's gain unlawful does not establish the amount belonging to this claimant. No compensatory measure is pleaded and proved for an appellate award here.

**Precedent treatment:** The earlier tax-invalidity judgment remains intact; Cotton Petroleum preserves concurrent taxation; California, Fulton, Reich and Chickasaw retain their claimant-specific and remedial limits.

##### 2. Equitable analysis must use the actual legal and procedural interests

**Holding and operative rule:** The remand must distinguish lawful tribal tax coverage, actual payment arrangements, independent claims against the State and funds already distributed. None of the approval dispute, taxpayer refund history or Tribe-taxpayer agreement categorically extinguishes a properly supported independent tribal claim on this record; further relief must follow its lawful basis and proven scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The 1988 ruling treated Interior's original reservation-tax approval as covering the ceded-strip minerals. Its contrary exclusion was legally mistaken. That ruling distributed registry funds under the 1982 agreement; it did not determine Westmoreland's liability before that agreement. Actual refusal to approve, the company's pass-through difficulty and its unwillingness to pay remain relevant practical facts, but they cannot be recast as a conclusive legal absence of tribal authority.

The United States intervened in June 1983. The severance and gross-proceeds taxes entered the registry at different times, and the completed distributions must be considered without awarding the same benefit twice. Neither the agreement's treatment of Westmoreland nor its refund difficulties establish a tribal release of the separate claim against Montana.

The contrary full-recovery theory neglects these distinctions; a categorical no-recovery theory would do the same. Proper consideration of independent partial restitution remains open in this action. The district court may take a lawful course under the Federal Rules, but this decision does not compel reopening proof, allowing an amendment, measuring compensatory injury or paying a fixed lesser sum. Equity must explain entitlement; it may not become an unexamined excuse to retain an unlawful gain.

**Precedent treatment:** California's distinction between derivative and independent entitlements governs without barring a properly established direct tribal claim; the earlier distribution ruling retains its actual, limited scope.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Prior Crow Tribe tax-invalidity judgment:** Preserved; no relitigation of these taxes' invalidity.
- **Cotton Petroleum:** Preserves possible concurrent state and tribal taxation and distinguishes all receipts from an excessive share.
- **United States v. California:** Separates taxpayer refunds, economic burdens and another claimant's independent entitlement.
- **Fulton and Reich:** Preserve adequate, procedurally lawful correction for the appropriate claimant without predetermining this award.
- **Chickasaw Nation:** Its incidence analysis does not create a general right to another taxpayer's entire payments.
- **1988 registry-distribution ruling:** Retains its approval-coverage determination and distribution effect, without becoming a decision of pre-agreement taxpayer liability.

#### Law After Decision

Established tax invalidity does not itself establish a tribe's right to the entire collection from another taxpayer. The court must examine the claimant, fund, agreements, prior distributions and lawful theory of relief. A properly supported independent partial claim remains open; neither a total award nor a complete bar follows automatically.

#### Separate Writings

Souter, joined by O'Connor, agrees that the whole-collection award cannot stand because Montana could impose a tax below the excessive level. He emphasizes that the existing request for illegally collected money encompasses a lesser claim to excessive receipts, grounded in the Tribe's own protected property interest. Interior's legally erroneous refusal and the agreement with Westmoreland do not dispose of that independent claim against the State. A dollar-for-dollar compensatory-loss measure is not the only possible basis for restitution of an improperly obtained benefit.

He would require genuine consideration of that existing claim: determine the excessive share, consider registry distributions and the remaining equities, and decide whether all, some or none of that share should be restored. This does not permit treating every state tax dollar as excessive or establish entitlement to an additional penny. The concurrence joins the Court's bounded opinion because it leaves that inquiry substantively open; its affirmative restitution framework goes beyond the Court's common holding.

#### Source Notes

The reported record at 523 U.S. 696 and the underlying judgments supply the tax periods, registry chronology, June 1983 intervention and limited 1988 ruling. The current demand is restitution with interest; no proved compensatory measure or ruling allowing a proposed amendment is established. Legal tax coverage is distinct from the commercial effects of Interior's actual refusals.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Ninth Circuit's compulsory transfer of the whole preregistry collection is reversed. Further district-court proceedings must respect the preserved independent claim and the actual scope of existing rulings and agreements. No refund claim is revived for Westmoreland, no registry distribution is disturbed, and no additional amount, amendment or evidentiary proceeding is directed.

**End of entry: Montana v. Crow Tribe of Indians, decision, May 18, 1998.**

### Ohio Forestry Association, Inc. v. Sierra Club, No. 97-16

Decision, May 18, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 25, 1998; decided May 18, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, which reversed summary judgment for the Forest Service and held aspects of the Wayne National Forest plan unlawful. The question is whether the present substantive planning challenge is ripe before a site-specific logging authorization.

The complaint challenges: approval of the plan and below-cost clearcutting under NFMA, NEPA and the APA; below-cost timber sales under public-trust duties; and timber-suitability regulations' failure to identify economically unsuitable land under NFMA and the APA. It seeks declaratory and injunctive relief, costs and fees.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded for dismissal without prejudice, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

All three substantive complaint theories are dismissed as unripe. The District Court's merits summary judgment is displaced as well. The Court orders no plan invalidation, logging injunction, plan revision or fee award.

#### Opinion Topology

Scalia delivers the opinion of the Court. All nine Justices join it in full.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The present substantive planning challenge is premature

**Holding and operative rule:** A substantive challenge to a programmatic forest plan is unripe when the plan authorizes no immediate logging, establishes no sufficient present legal hardship, and leaves further project decisions and meaningful review before cutting. On this record that rule requires dismissal without prejudice of the plan/clearcutting, public-trust sales and timber-suitability-regulation claims, without deciding their substantive validity.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine Justices, supplies direct controlling authority for this rule and the limits below.

**Controlling explanation:** Abbott Laboratories directs courts to consider fitness for decision and hardship from withholding review. Texas applies that inquiry to a dispute whose operative details remain unresolved. The Wayne plan identifies logging goals, suitable areas and probable methods; it does not authorize cutting. Before logging, the Forest Service must identify a site and method, ensure consistency with the plan, provide notice and an opportunity to be heard, conduct environmental analysis and make a final project decision subject to review. Those choices can alter the anticipated effects and provide a concrete basis for adjudication. Review need not wait until trees are cut; the relevant planning premise may be challenged with the project that applies it.

The suitability regulations are completed rules, but Toilet Goods distinguishes finality from a presently fit controversy. The complaint establishes no sufficient present consequence requiring resolution of this substantive logging dispute now. Bennett shows why retained agency discretion alone is not decisive when completed action already has supported consequences. New York and Pic-A-State likewise preserve review of actual present burdens; no comparable burden is established here. Possible plan amendment is a consideration, not an automatic bar.

NEPA's inclusion identifies no particular omitted statement, denied participation or other completed procedural deprivation. The public-trust label supplies no different timing premise. The requests for an injunction, costs and fees do not establish current cutting authority or an independent ripe injury. Neither lower court's substantive forestry ruling can survive as the disposition of this unripe action.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither standing, APA finality, statutory or public-trust merits, nor an independent fee entitlement. It does not adopt the lower court's exceptional-circumstances formulation for even-aged management or equate an optimum-method inquiry with a resource-protection condition. A site-specific authorization, an immediately binding provision or regulation, or a properly presented completed procedural violation may receive review when its independent requirements are satisfied. Lujan's protection of a procedural right tied to a threatened concrete interest remains intact. No optional-appeal exhaustion requirement or future limitations ruling is adopted.

Assertions in later briefing concerning motorcycles, heavy machinery, road closures and hiking trails supply no established basis for resolving the reviewed substantive dispute differently. The Court does not find those activities or injuries proved, find intentional waiver, announce a general forfeiture rule, or adjudicate a distinct present-recreation claim on either ripeness or merits.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner and Texas v. United States: applied to fitness and present hardship; neither requires an advisory answer about an insufficiently concrete implementation.
- Toilet Goods Association v. Gardner: applied to distinguish completed agency action from ripeness for judicial review.
- Bennett v. Spear: distinguished at its supported present-consequences premise; later implementation discretion alone does not defeat review.
- New York v. United States and Pic-A-State Pennsylvania v. Reno: distinguished because their challenged rules imposed established present burdens; pre-enforcement review remains available on that basis.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife: its concrete-interest procedural protection is preserved; this decision supplies no separate standing determination.

#### Law After Decision

Forest plans and suitability regulations receive no categorical immunity from review. This substantive planning dispute must await a sufficiently concrete, reviewable application or another independently established present consequence. Distinct completed procedural injuries retain their own timing requirements. No NFMA, NEPA, APA or public-trust merits rule changes, and no rule requires physical injury before a challenge may proceed.

#### Source Notes

The plan's operation, further project procedures, pleaded counts and requested relief appear in the published record account at [523 U.S. 726–739](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-726.pdf); the judgment below is 105 F.3d 248. The later recreational assertions are reported submissions, not findings of present activity or injury. The reference to NEPA is not a finding that a specified procedure was denied.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for dismissal without prejudice as unripe, replacing the lower merits adjudications. The plan is neither judicially approved nor invalidated. A later properly presented challenge must establish its own jurisdictional and procedural requirements; no future project, injunction, fee award or result is directed.

### Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal, No. 97-300

Decision, May 18, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 25, 1998; decided May 18, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 118 F.3d 628. The Court considers whether §2244(b)(3)(E) permits review of the circuit's determination that authorization was unnecessary and whether this renewed Ford incompetency-to-be-executed claim is second or successive.

Three earlier federal applications ended for nonexhaustion. The March 1993 application included the Ford claim, dismissed as premature at the State's request. The 1996 appellate mandate reversed relief on other grounds while preserving later litigation of that claim. After an execution warrant and state competency proceedings, the District Court dismissed the renewed motion for lack of jurisdiction. The Ninth Circuit held authorization unnecessary and returned the claim for district consideration.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

All nine agree that ordinary review of this threshold classification is available. The seven-Justice judgment permits first federal merits consideration of this particular renewed claim under otherwise applicable law. It grants no writ or stay and orders no new evidentiary hearing or execution.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer throughout; Scalia and Thomas in Part I | Affirms the district-return judgment | Part I: ordinary review channel; Part II: previously presented premature claim and resulting return for consideration |
| Dissent in part | Scalia | Thomas | Would reverse and require dismissal under §2244(b)(1) | Both join Part I but reject Part II's continuation classification |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The circuit's threshold classification is reviewable

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars ordinary review of a circuit decision granting or denying authorization to file a second or successive application, but it does not bar review of this distinct determination that authorization is unnecessary. The Court may decide whether the circuit correctly placed this proceeding outside that mechanism; the circuit's label does not itself establish the answer.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is adopted by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Felker gives effect to the statutory bar for an actual authorization decision. Triestman and Dorsainvil apply that restriction to authorization refusals. Here the circuit did not decide whether the applicant satisfied the statutory prima-facie permission criteria. It held those criteria inapplicable and transferred the proceeding back. Calderon likewise distinguishes an independently reviewable first-application action from the authorization process. Ordinary review therefore reaches this classification without opening review of a true authorization refusal or allowing a court to evade the statute by relabeling its action.

##### II. This previously presented premature claim may receive its first federal merits consideration

**Holding and operative rule:** A Ford claim actually presented in the initial merits application, dismissed as premature and expressly preserved for later consideration, is not a second or successive application when renewed after the impediment of prematurity has ended. This renewed claim may therefore return to the District Court without circuit authorization, subject to otherwise applicable habeas requirements.

**Authority:** Part II is adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author; these seven directly establish the rule and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The claim was before the federal court in March 1993, but could not then receive a decision on competency to be executed. The State requested its dismissal on that ground, and the later mandate preserved its return. The renewed filing seeks the federal merits consideration previously unavailable. It does not repeat a decision already made on that claim.

Second or successive is a procedural term, not simply a count of papers filed. The treatment of an application dismissed for nonexhaustion illustrates why an earlier nonmerits filing need not consume the available opportunity. Lonchar requires attention to the actual legal source and its dismissal predicates; it does not authorize a court to ignore an applicable statute. This conclusion construes the statute's class rather than creating an equitable exception to it. The final disposition of other claims does not turn this claim's prematurity dismissal into a merits adjudication. Calderon's rule for reopening completed merits therefore supplies no contrary answer.

The Court does not adopt the circuit's broader exemption for every competency-only application. A Ford claim first presented after an earlier federal application was rejected, other later-arising claims and every other type of nonmerits dismissal remain undecided. Ford identifies the constitutional claim; this judgment establishes neither incompetence nor any defect in Arizona's competency proceedings. Lindh and Carriger retain their provision-specific limits, and neither this classification nor first-review status guarantees a new live evidentiary hearing, a writ or a stay.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Felker v. Turpin: applied to actual authorization decisions; its review bar and distinct original-habeas route remain unchanged.
- Triestman v. United States and In re Dorsainvil: distinguished because they concerned actual authorization refusals; this case creates no exception permitting ordinary review of those refusals.
- Calderon v. Thompson: its action-specific channel distinction is applied; its completed-merits recall rule is distinguished from a claim dismissed only as premature.
- Lonchar v. Thomas: its requirement of legally supported dismissal predicates informs the classification; no equitable power to disregard enacted successive limits is recognized.
- Ford v. Wainwright: supplies the incompetency-to-be-executed claim without deciding competency or procedural sufficiency here.
- Lindh v. Murphy and Carriger v. Stewart: their provision-specific temporal treatment is preserved; they do not independently exempt all later filings from AEDPA.

#### Law After Decision

The particular previously presented and premature Ford claim remains available for first federal consideration when ripe. The statute continues to govern genuinely second or successive applications, and its ordinary-review prohibition continues to govern actual authorization decisions. No general competency-claim exemption, constitutional invalidation of AEDPA or substantive entitlement to habeas relief follows.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, dissenting in part.** They agree that the Court may review the circuit's threshold classification, but would reverse it. Section 2244(b)(1) addresses a claim presented in a second or successive application and requires dismissal when that claim appeared in the earlier application. Here the earlier merits application ended after other claims received merits adjudication; the Ford claim was dismissed, not retained in an ongoing proceeding. A wholly unexhausted application dismissed without prejudice presents a different situation.

For the dissenters, the court's reservation cannot create an exception that the enacted text does not supply, and the State's earlier prematurity request does not waive the statutory restriction. Ex parte Bollman's requirement of written legal authority reinforces their objection to restoring prior judicial practice after Congress changed the governing law. They would require dismissal of this repeated claim under §2244(b)(1), not an opportunity for discretionary authorization under an invented exception. They decide no competency question, order no execution and express no disposition of a genuinely separate lawful proceeding.

#### Source Notes

The procedural chronology and reported prior rulings appear in [523 U.S. 637–652](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-637.pdf), 118 F.3d 628 and 80 F.3d 1301, 1309 n.1. [AEDPA §106](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-104publ132/html/PLAW-104publ132.htm) supplies the successive-application provisions. The state competency determination is a reported prior ruling, not a competency finding by this Court.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The circuit's return of the claim to the District Court stands. That court must consider the renewed Ford claim through otherwise applicable habeas procedures; the proper form of consideration and entitlement to relief remain for determination. No competency finding, new evidentiary-hearing requirement, stay, writ or execution direction is entered by this judgment.

**End of entry: Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal, decision, May 18, 1998.**

### Textron Lycoming Reciprocating Engine Division v. United Automobile Workers, No. 97-463

Decision, May 18, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 23, 1998; decided May 18, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Third Circuit, 117 F.3d 119. The question is whether LMRA §301(a), 29 U.S.C. §185(a), supplies jurisdiction over this formation-fraud and contract-validity action without an alleged violation of the collective agreement.

The agreement prohibited strikes and required seven days' notice before subcontracting agreements. After Textron announced subcontracting plans affecting roughly half the approximately 500 represented employees, the Union alleged that Textron had concealed those plans during bargaining. It sought compensatory and punitive damages and a declaration that the agreement was voidable, but alleged no violation by either party. The District Court dismissed for lack of the asserted jurisdiction; the Third Circuit reversed. The reported record establishes no threatened strike or other actual threatened violation controversy.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The presented action cannot proceed on its asserted §301(a) basis. The remand leaves independently supported claims or jurisdictional grounds to their own requirements and decides no fraud, contractual validity or damages question.

#### Opinion Topology

Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court. All nine Justices adopt it in full.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Contract validity alone does not establish a suit for violation

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 301(a) supplies jurisdiction over suits for violation of covered labor contracts, not every suit concerning their formation or validity. A standalone formation-fraud action alleging no violation and establishing no actual or threatened violation controversy does not satisfy that gateway; validity may nevertheless be decided when necessary to resolve a properly brought violation dispute, including any declaratory action that independently satisfies the applicable jurisdictional and controversy requirements.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court is adopted by all nine Justices and directly controls this rule, application and reservation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court begins with the dispute Congress authorized federal courts to hear. The complaint alleges concealment during bargaining and asks to make the agreement voidable. It does not allege that Textron violated the notice provision or that the Union violated its no-strike obligation. The contract's importance under federal labor law does not supply that missing violation controversy.

Wooddell permits a member-beneficiary to enforce a qualifying labor contract, but retains the requirement of an alleged violation. Kokkonen likewise distinguishes a contract's connection with federal law or litigation from an actual source of federal jurisdiction. Uniform federal interpretation can operate within Congress's category; it cannot enlarge the category to every formation dispute.

The Union's reliance on cases permitting validity defenses does not require a different result. A court adjudicating an alleged contractual violation may have to determine whether an enforceable agreement exists. That necessary question differs from a standalone request to avoid a contract without an actual enforcement controversy. Nothing in this judgment requires enforcement of a fraudulent contract.

A genuine declaratory dispute concerning an actual or threatened violation presents a different predicate. The Court does not categorically bar that vehicle or decide its requirements in the abstract. Here no threatened strike or corresponding employer enforcement action is established, and the requested declaration cannot itself create the missing controversy. The jurisdictional ruling must therefore be reversed without deciding the alleged fraud.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no breach, fraud, validity, unfair-labor-practice or state-remedy issue; no general federal-common-law jurisdiction theory or broad §1331 question is resolved. The alternative general-common-law argument lies outside the granted §301 question. The Court neither determines that Textron violated a bargaining duty nor orders a Board proceeding. A different actual or threatened violation dispute must satisfy its own jurisdictional and justiciability requirements.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71: applied at its member-beneficiary enforcement rule; permission for that plaintiff to sue does not convert every validity dispute into a suit alleging violation.
- Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America: applied by analogy for the need for an actual jurisdictional basis rather than a contract's general connection to federal law; its settlement-enforcement rules remain unchanged.

#### Law After Decision

Section 301 remains available for qualifying labor-contract violation disputes, with formation and validity issues adjudicated when necessary to resolve them. This action's standalone fraud and voidability theory does not establish that statutory jurisdiction. Genuine declaratory enforcement controversies are not categorically excluded, but neither a declaratory label nor the possibility of a different future lawsuit establishes one. No labor-contract merits or independent-jurisdiction rule is otherwise changed.

#### Source Notes

Section 301(a)'s statutory category and the complaint and procedural account appear in [523 U.S. 653–663](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-653.pdf); the judgment below is 117 F.3d 119. The concealment theory remains an allegation, and the requested declaration does not establish an actual threatened strike or employer enforcement controversy. The separate general-common-law theory lies outside the question reviewed.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for dismissal of the presented action for want of the asserted §301(a) jurisdiction, with any independently supported claim or basis treated according to its own requirements. No fraud or validity judgment, damages, injunction, strike permission, subcontracting authorization or NLRB remedy follows.

**End of entry: Textron Lycoming Reciprocating Engine Division v. United Automobile Workers, decision, May 18, 1998.**

### Gavin v. Branstad, Eighth Circuit Nos. 96-3746 and 96-3748, 122 F.3d 1081

Decision, May 21, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided May 21, 1998, on full merits briefing; no Supreme Court oral-argument date is recorded. On writ of certiorari under §1254(1) to review the Eighth Circuit's August 5, 1997 judgment on a certified §1292(b) interlocutory appeal.

Iowa prisoners challenged segregation conditions in 1978. A 1984 consent decree and 1988 library supplement retained federal enforcement. After the PLRA, the district court acknowledged missing original remedial findings but held termination unconstitutional without deciding §3626(b)(3). The Court of Appeals reversed and remanded, declining immediate termination. The questions concern the presented constitutional objections and lawful adjudication of termination. Benjamin and Hadix are separately reviewed matters.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The district court's constitutional bar remains reversed. Its unperformed statutory inquiry must proceed; this Court neither terminates all relief nor orders its unconditional continuation. All nine reject the separate assumed-contract objection, with the reasoning distinction below.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III and V | Scalia | All other participating Justices | Supports affirmance and remand | Statutory conditions, prospective effects, presented constitutional grounds, and remand. |
| Opinion of the Court, Part IV | Scalia | Every other Associate Justice | Rejects the assumed independent-contract impairment objection | Federal-nonparty rationality ground; Stone-Zsela agrees in rejecting this objection and adds no ground. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The amended statutory conditions govern prospective relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3626(b)(2) permits termination of covered prospective prison relief granted without findings that the relief was narrowly drawn, went no further than correction of the federal-right violation required, and used the least intrusive means necessary for that correction; the movant must establish this original-findings predicate, and sufficient substantive findings need no ritual wording. Amended §3626(b)(3) preserves relief only on written, record-based findings that it is necessary to correct a current **and** ongoing federal-right violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn, and is the least intrusive means necessary to correct the violation.

**Authority:** Scalia's Parts I–III and V are joined by all nine participating Justices and control Holdings 1–3 and 5; Part IV's distinct coalition is stated in Holding 4.

**Controlling explanation:** Rouse and Benjamin supply the amended conditions. Plyler establishes that the statutory route is independent of Rule 60(b)(5); Rufo's significant-change requirement is not an additional prerequisite. The November 26, 1997 amendment applies to this pending proceeding, so the earlier current-or-ongoing formulation does not govern.

Consent decrees fall within covered relief. Neither negotiated origin nor relief exceeding the minimum substantive conduct rule determines continuation: a corrective remedy may exceed that minimum when all statutory findings support its necessity. Missing original findings do not establish present compliance. Every retained obligation must satisfy the preservation conditions; the unsupported remainder must end. The district court has not performed that inquiry.

##### 2. Prospective termination does not erase completed effects

**Holding and operative rule:** Lawful termination ends unsupported future judicial obligations themselves, not merely federal enforcement while identical decree duties automatically survive as state contracts. It does not invalidate completed determinations, payments, or other lawful historical effects; independent agreements and accrued claims require their own legal basis.

**Controlling explanation:** Benjamin and Rouse distinguish prospective judicial commands from completed effects. Kokkonen distinguishes a private agreement from a judicial obligation and requires a proper basis for enforcement. Consent alone therefore establishes neither perpetual decree duties nor a separate enforceable contract. Section 3626(d) preserves relief based solely on state-law claims without creating such a claim. No independent agreement, accrued liability, breach, or enforcement forum is adjudicated here.

##### 3. The presented constitutional objections fail

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may alter prospective remedial conditions while courts independently decide eligibility, violations, and relief; beneficiaries possess no permanent entitlement to unchanged supervision. The presented classification rationally relates the actual remedial restriction to legitimate institutional and resource interests while preserving meaningful remedies for federal violations.

**Controlling explanation:** Rouse and Benjamin govern these objections. Robertson and Wheeling Bridge distinguish changed governing law from legislative adjudication; Klein preserves independent judicial decision. Morgan Stanley and Plaut permit bounded reinstatement of specified securities limitations dismissals, establishing neither absolute finality nor unrestricted reopening.

The statute supplies new conditions, not a legislative finding of compliance. Judicial application preserves substantive federal rights and the opportunity for necessary proof. Beach allows legitimate conceivable purposes; Heller and Romer require a rational relationship to the actual classification, not merely absence of suspect status. The retained adjudicatory and remedial protections support that relationship here. Completed effects and accrued claims remain distinct. A supported violation excluded solely by the amended conjunction, or an independently supported threatened injury requiring protection, is not decided away.

##### 4. The assumed contract objection creates no further bar

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming solely for this objection an independent contract and substantial impairment, rational federal regulation of continuing supervision is permissible where the United States is not a contracting party. Judicial approval and benefit to a State do not make the United States a party.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV has the support of Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejecting the objection.

**Controlling explanation:** Rouse and Benjamin apply National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Atchison's federal-nonparty framework. Institutional responsibility and resources rationally support the limited supervision rule while federal-right remedies remain. Judicial approval supplies no federal promise of permanent enforcement. This assumed premise establishes neither an actual independent contract nor cancellation of accrued contractual liability.

##### 5. Remand must supply the missing statutory adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** A court that has rejected termination solely on constitutional grounds must determine the statutory predicate and preservation conditions before ending or retaining the disputed relief. Ordinary lawful procedure must permit the material proof needed for that inquiry, without a hearing requirement in every case or a presumed outcome.

**Controlling explanation:** Benjamin corrects an actual legal refusal to consider necessary current proof; Gavin presents an inquiry never performed. No identical evidentiary refusal is found here. Immediate termination would bypass preservation, while indefinite continuation would bypass the enacted limits. Lewis v. Casey requires actual court-access injury and remedial fit for library relief; the library supplement proves neither injury nor its absence. Other obligations must rest on their own federal rights and the statutory findings.

The Rules Enabling Act argument was not renewed on appeal. Automatic stay, new-relief provisions, state-court application, prisoner release, present conditions, and independent contractual enforcement remain outside this decision.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Rouse; Benjamin:** Applied to amended conditions, constitutional objections, prospective duties, completed effects, and the assumed-contract ground; Benjamin's proof-refusal remedy is distinguished.
- **Plyler:** Preserves the separate statutory route and its original-text limits.
- **Rufo:** Retains its significant-change and tailored-modification requirements for the independent equitable route.
- **Lewis v. Casey:** Requires actual access injury and fit for library remedies; its original statutory scope remains intact.
- **Kokkonen:** Preserves the judicial-command/private-agreement distinction and independent enforcement requirements.
- **Morgan Stanley; Plaut:** Preserve bounded reinstatement and independent adjudication, without resolving all reopening questions.
- **Robertson; Wheeling Bridge; Klein:** Preserve genuine changed-law adjudication and forbid legislative substitution for the judicial task.
- **Beach; Heller; Romer:** Supply rational review tied to the actual classification and legitimate ends.
- **National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Atchison:** Supplies the limited federal-nonparty contract framework.

#### Law After Decision

The amended termination framework governs the Iowa decrees. Continuing relief requires actual statutory findings; neither automatic termination nor permanent consent-based supervision follows. Completed effects and independent claims retain their separate legal footing. No precedent is overruled.

#### Source Notes

The complete reported Eighth Circuit opinion, 122 F.3d 1081, supports the procedural and decree history. Original decrees and current reports are not reproduced in that report. Pub. L. 105-119, §123(a)(2) and (b), 111 Stat. 2470–2471, supplies the amended conjunction and its pending-case application.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Eighth Circuit for the district court's statutory adjudication and consistent further proceedings. No current violation, retained term, release order, new stay, filing deadline, or automatic return to this Court is prescribed.

**End of entry: Gavin v. Branstad, decision, May 21, 1998.**

### United States v. Thompson, CAAF No. 96-1412-AF, CCA 31803, 47 M.J. 378

Decision, May 21, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided May 21, 1998, on full merits briefing; no Supreme Court oral-argument date is recorded. On writ of certiorari under §1259 to the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces, which granted the marital-privacy question, specified the closed-session question, and affirmed on December 16, 1997.

The questions are whether marital privacy bars this Article 125 conviction on the proved assault record, and whether the military judge's closed-session contact violated Article 39(b) and materially prejudiced substantial rights. Thompson seeks relief on both; the United States seeks affirmance. The court-martial also convicted him of assault and aggravated assault. Its approved sentence was a bad-conduct discharge, thirty months' confinement, total forfeitures, and reduction to the lowest enlisted grade.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed on both reviewed questions, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The court-martial convictions and approved sentence remain undisturbed. No remand or new hearing is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by every other participating Justice. All nine support both controlling holdings, their qualifications, and the affirmance.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The asserted marital-privacy protection does not cover this assault-induced act

**Holding and operative rule:** Constitutional protection invoked for voluntary private marital intimacy does not bar an Article 125 conviction where the act was undertaken to divert an ongoing deadly assault rather than as a voluntary intimate choice. Marriage and physical initiation alone do not establish that protected premise; the constitutionality of punishment for genuinely voluntary private conduct between spouses or other adults remains undecided.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion has all nine participating Justices, whose common full join controls both holdings directly.

**Controlling explanation:** Griswold protects marital privacy, while Eisenstadt and the Court's autonomy decisions locate the relevant protection in personal choice. Those principles do not make marriage a defense for conduct arising from this ongoing violent attack.

Thompson held a loaded pistol against his wife's forehead and attempted to fire it. When it failed to discharge, he began loading another magazine. She initiated the charged act to divert him during that assault. Physical initiation in this setting supplies no voluntary intimate choice. The Court therefore rejects the claim on its missing premise, without asking whether judges approve the practice or think it benefits marriage.

The holding neither denies marital privacy generally nor grants military proceedings an exemption from constitutional review. Weiss preserves independent constitutional scrutiny of military justice. Richenberg's distinct service question and Scheffer's category-specific evidentiary ruling do not supply a broader rule. Genuinely consensual conduct is outside this decision.

##### 2. The unlawful closed-session contact was nonprejudicial on the recorded facts

**Holding and operative rule:** Article 39(b) requires the accused and counsel to be present at court-martial proceedings other than members' deliberation and voting; the judge's entry to answer members' questions without them violated that requirement. Under Article 59(a) and Allbee's rebuttable presumption of prejudice against the Government, the complete reported exchange establishes no material prejudice to substantial rights because it concerned only recording findings already reached and supplied no substantive deliberative instruction.

**Controlling explanation:** The deliberation exception does not authorize the judge to conduct proceedings inside the members' room without the accused or counsel. Nor does the violation automatically invalidate the judgment. Allbee places the burden of rebutting prejudice on the Government. Rushen and Gagnon show that unauthorized judge-juror contact is not invariably structural error; their distinct circumstances and constitutional limits remain intact. Sullivan addresses the absence of a valid jury verdict, which appellate review cannot manufacture, whereas the members here had already decided their findings.

The military judge took the reporter into the room. The resulting verbatim transcript was supplied to the parties at trial. The members asked only how to enter lesser-included offenses on the findings worksheet; the answers did not address evidence, guilt, or deliberative substance. Nothing said would have been objectionable in open session. These affirmative facts permit complete review and rebut the presumption here. Neither trial nor appellate defense counsel identified a matter requiring challenge or clarification, which corroborates the record without shifting the burden to Thompson.

Recording alone does not make a contact harmless. The Court authorizes no substantive, unrecorded, or unreconstructable judicial exchange and decides no distinct unpresented Sixth Amendment claim or universal constitutional classification of closed-session contacts. Weiss's statutory safeguards remain operative; Scheffer does not license their disregard.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Griswold; Eisenstadt; Casey:** Their protection of personal choice supplies the voluntary-intimacy distinction; the Court announces no categorical rule for genuinely consensual conduct.
- **Weiss:** Preserves independent military constitutional review and operative statutory safeguards.
- **Richenberg:** Its distinct military-service holding supplies no general constitutional exemption.
- **United States v. Scheffer:** Its category-specific evidentiary permission does not authorize ex parte proceedings or erase participation protections.
- **Rushen v. Spain; United States v. Gagnon:** Support assessable-error treatment without making every judge-member contact harmless or deciding their distinct constitutional conditions here.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Distinguished because the supported record establishes completed findings rather than an absent valid verdict.

#### Law After Decision

The assault record defeats the asserted voluntary-intimacy premise. Article 39(b)'s participation command remains enforceable, and the Government bears the burden of rebutting the contact's presumed prejudice under Article 59(a). The confined recorded exchange satisfies that burden. No precedent is overruled, blanket military exemption created, or general Article 125 privacy question resolved.

#### Source Notes

The complete issuing-court opinion, United States v. Thompson, 47 M.J. 378, December 16, 1997, supports the assault facts, granted and specified issues, statutory violation, sentence, and reported contents of the verbatim exchange furnished at trial. The underlying transcript is not reproduced in that opinion; its detailed judicial account supplies the stated record basis. The questions describe the reviewed issues; the Supreme Court petition text is unavailable.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The CAAF judgment stands, leaving the convictions and approved sentence in force. The Court orders no remand, new trial, evidentiary hearing, sentence alteration, or further scheduled proceeding. No separate sentencing error is decided.

**End of entry: United States v. Thompson, decision, May 21, 1998.**
