### Kawaauhau v. Geiger, No. 97-115

Plenary merits decision, March 3, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 21, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit, sitting en banc. The question is whether a malpractice debt arising from negligent or reckless medical treatment is for willful and malicious injury under 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(6). The Bankruptcy Court held the debt nondischargeable, the District Court affirmed, and the Eighth Circuit reversed. The creditors seek reversal of that appellate judgment. All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The malpractice debt remains dischargeable under §523(a)(6). The Court does not revisit the jury’s malpractice finding or damages calculation; it decides dischargeability under §523(a)(6). No new intent finding, remand for relitigation of malpractice or decision under another exception is ordered.

#### Opinions and Joins

Ginsburg delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. The unanimous opinion covers the injury-intent rule, its application, precedent treatment and limits.

#### Holdings

##### Deliberate injury is required; a deliberate act causing negligent or reckless harm is insufficient

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 523(a)(6) requires a deliberate or intentional injury, in addition to the separately stated malicious character; intentionally doing an act that negligently or recklessly causes injury does not satisfy willfulness. This malpractice debt, established on negligence or recklessness rather than injury-directed intent, is therefore dischargeable under that paragraph.

**Authority:** Ginsburg’s opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight other Justices named above, directly controls the rule, application and stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory modifier describes injury. Moving it to the treatment decision would make many voluntary acts producing negligent harm sufficient and deprive the injury requirement of independent work. The medical choices here were deliberate, but that does not establish deliberate injury. The exceptionally deficient standard of care found below establishes malpractice; calling it willful and malicious does not answer the federal question.

Tinker involved an intentional invasion of a legally protected right, and McIntyre involved deliberate unauthorized appropriation of property. Neither requires personal hostility, but neither makes a voluntary act alone enough. Tinker itself distinguished negligent driving from intentionally running over a victim. To the extent its broader language is read to include merely reckless injury under the present statute, that reading is rejected. Davis confirms that even conversion liability does not automatically establish willful and malicious injury. Field likewise keeps a federal discharge predicate distinct from a state tort label.

The creditors’ cumulative account of inferior treatment, canceled transfer and discontinued antibiotics establishes the gravity of the alleged wrong, but supplies no additional finding of intended injury. Nor does uninsured practice authorize a judicially created exception. Congress fixes the exceptions. The Court reserves whether subjective knowledge of substantial certainty suffices, the independent meaning of malicious, any other independently pleaded discharge exception and the treatment of proved injury-directed harm. No categorical exemption for medical debts or new criminal-law proof requirement follows.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current Law

- Tinker v. Colwell, 193 U.S. 473, retains its intentional-invasion holding and rejection of a personal-hostility requirement; a reading that makes merely reckless injury sufficient under §523(a)(6) is rejected.
- McIntyre v. Kavanaugh, 242 U.S. 138, is distinguished as deliberate appropriation; it does not make all conversions or negligent professional acts nondischargeable, and its old-Act imputation question is not decided.
- Davis v. Aetna Acceptance Co., 293 U.S. 328, is applied to preserve the distinction between tort liability and willful and malicious injury.
- Field v. Mans is applied for the independent federal discharge predicates; its actual-fraud reliance rule is unchanged. In re Cohen’s debt-extent rule remains distinct because a qualifying injury predicate must first exist here.

**Current law:** From March 3, 1998, §523(a)(6) excludes debts for negligently or recklessly inflicted injury from its willfulness category even when the underlying act was intentional. Actual deliberate injury presents a different question. The independent malicious requirement, substantial certainty, other discharge exceptions and state-law liability retain their separate roles.

#### Source Notes

The [reported proceeding, 523 U.S. 57](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-57.pdf), supplies the treatment history, malpractice and bankruptcy sequence, argument date and appellate posture. The [1994 Code text of §523](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title11/pdf/USCODE-1994-title11-chap5-subchapII-sec523.pdf) preserves the pertinent paragraph. The published recitations establish no subjective-substantial-certainty finding or state requirement that Geiger carry insurance.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Eighth Circuit’s discharge determination under §523(a)(6) stands. This review is complete; no additional hearing, intent finding or reconsideration of malpractice is directed.

**End of entry: Kawaauhau v. Geiger, Plenary merits decision, March 3, 1998.**

### United States v. Ramirez, No. 96-1469

Plenary merits decision, March 4, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 13, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit. The United States challenges the affirmance of an order suppressing firearms evidence under the Fourth Amendment and 18 U.S.C. §3109 because officers broke a garage window while executing a no-knock warrant. The questions concern the announcement standard when property is damaged, the reasonableness of that damage and the separate statutory ground. All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The suppression judgment cannot stand on a heightened announcement standard triggered by damage, or on the challenged entry and single-window damage under the standards applied here. This decision orders no acquittal, conviction or sentence and establishes no automatic exclusionary exception for later statements, a later warrant or recovered evidence.

#### Opinions and Joins

Breyer delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. The unanimous opinion covers all constitutional and statutory holdings, their application and the remedy.

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Breyer’s opinion of the Court is joined in full by all eight other Justices named above. That direct unanimous coalition controls each holding and its limits; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

##### I. Property damage does not raise the announcement threshold

**Holding and operative rule:** Damage during warrant execution does not categorically require a greater exigency than particularized reasonable suspicion that ordinary announcement would be dangerous or futile, or would inhibit effective investigation, including through destruction of evidence. The facts supporting that exception must be known when the challenged force begins, and the means and extent of any destruction remain independently subject to Fourth Amendment reasonableness.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson makes the common-law announcement protection part of constitutional reasonableness while preserving supported exceptions. Richards requires particular facts and rational inferences rather than an offense category, unsupported hunch or a no-knock label on a warrant. Breaking property does not change that announcement inquiry into a separate higher tier. The contrary rule correctly recognizes that destruction aggravates an intrusion, but addresses that concern in the wrong place. Whether the particular destructive act is reasonable remains a separate question; justified surprise does not authorize needless or disproportionate damage. The Court adopts no universal waiting period and revisits no disputed standard-of-review rule.

##### II. The contemporaneous danger and limited damage justified this execution

**Holding and operative rule:** The reliable report of a dangerous escapee, corroborating observation and location-specific information about garage weapons supplied reasonable suspicion that ordinary announcement would create danger. Breaking one garage window to control access to those weapons, with a firearm directed through the opening for that purpose, was reasonable on this record; the justification permits neither gratuitous destruction nor any amount of damage merely because announcement may be excused.

**Controlling explanation:** Richards requires circumstances known before force. The informant’s report was partly corroborated by the agent’s observation, and the reported weapons gave the officers a concrete reason to prevent a rush into the garage. The limited window break addressed that identified danger. The number of officers outside did not eliminate a possible armed person’s access to weapons inside. Their security purpose is supported by those facts; purpose alone would not suffice. Ramirez’s later shot supplies no retrospective justification, and the later failure to find Shelby does not erase the information reasonably available beforehand. The Court decides the challenged entry and window damage, not every subsequent act. Gratuitous, punitive, disproportionate or objective-unrelated destruction receives no approval, and the remedy for materially different damage remains open.

##### III. Section 3109 preserves a supported common-law danger exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3109 ordinarily permits breaking to execute a search warrant after notice of authority and purpose and refused admission, and separately permits breaking when necessary to liberate the officer or someone assisting execution. Its codification of the common-law announcement rule also preserves a danger exception satisfied by particularized reasonable suspicion that following the ordinary process would expose officers or occupants to danger; the pre-entry escapee and garage-weapons information satisfies that exception here.

**Controlling explanation:** Simultaneous announcement and breaking do not establish prior refused admission. Nor does this record establish an officer or assistant needing liberation. The entry instead rests on the incorporated common-law danger qualification. Miller identifies the common-law source of the statutory protection but reserved a general exigency question. Sabbath treats opening an unlocked door as breaking and, in its discussion of common-law exceptions, supplies direct interpretive support without having held that every asserted danger suffices. Indeed, its unsupported danger claim failed. The Court now adopts the danger construction for the concrete supported circumstances here; it does not attribute that holding to those earlier reservations.

The statutory protection carries its traditional safety qualification, rather than automatically acquiring every constitutional exception. Wilson’s common-law account and Richards’s particularized standard guide this bounded construction. The express liberation alternative concerns freeing an impeded officer or helper and remains distinct. Because breaking already includes entry without physical damage, the statute creates no greater announcement threshold merely because property is damaged. No statutory rule for every possible futility, escape or evidence-destruction situation is necessary. Both asserted grounds for suppression fail, so the Court need not decide attenuation, independent source, inevitable discovery or the exclusionary consequence of an established violation.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current Law

- Wilson v. Arkansas is applied for the common-law announcement protection and supported exceptions; it does not constitutionalize every federal statutory detail.
- Richards v. Wisconsin is applied for particularized reasonable suspicion and contemporaneous justification; the level of suspicion required to dispense with announcement is not increased because damage occurs, and its separate review-method holding is unchanged.
- Miller v. United States, 357 U.S. 301, supplies the statutory common-law background. Its reservation of a general exigency exception is respected rather than described as an earlier holding.
- Sabbath v. United States, 391 U.S. 585, retains its rule that opening a closed unlocked door is breaking and its rejection of unsupported danger. Its common-law discussion supports the danger qualification adopted here; physical damage creates no new announcement tier.
- Aponte Matos v. Toledo Dávila retains its separate conduct-date qualified-immunity scope and supplies no direct suppression immunity here.

**Current law:** The constitutional announcement inquiry retains Richards’s particularized reasonable-suspicion standard when execution damages property, while the destructive means receive independent reasonableness review. Section 3109 separately incorporates the supported common-law danger exception applied here. Neither the validity of a warrant nor the availability of an announcement exception authorizes unreasonable damage. No suppression-causation doctrine or rule for materially different destruction is resolved.

#### Source Notes

The [reported proceeding, 523 U.S. 65](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-65.pdf), supplies the argument date, entry facts, lower-court suppression posture and later statements and warrant. The [official text of §3109](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title18/pdf/USCODE-1994-title18-partII-chap205-sec3109.pdf) supplies its notice, refusal and liberation terms. The factual account rests on published recitations; the Court draws no additional causal finding from unproduced testimony. Miller and Sabbath remain relevant at the limits stated in the opinion.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for further proceedings under the determination that the challenged entry and limited window damage violated neither the Fourth Amendment nor §3109. Those asserted grounds cannot sustain the suppression judgment. Any genuinely separate and properly preserved question retains its own requirements; the Court decides no such additional claim and orders no particular prosecution result.

**End of entry: United States v. Ramirez, Plenary merits decision, March 4, 1998.**

### Glendora v. Porzio, No. 97-7300

Filing-administration order, March 9, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

The full Court considers the present Rule 39 financial-leave motion and a separate proposed fee-and-form restriction on future noncriminal certiorari petitions. The petition seeks review after the Second Circuit declined financial leave in the appeal from dismissal of Glendora's federal action. No oral argument occurs and the underlying certiorari petition is not decided. All nine Justices participate. The financial-leave motion seeks permission to proceed without prepaying the docketing fee.

#### Judgment

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Present financial-leave motion | Denied, 7–2 | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Stevens and Stone-Zsela | Glendora may pay the Rule 38 fee and submit the petition in Rule 33.1 form by March 30, 1998 |
| Proposed future noncriminal fee-and-form condition | Declined, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | — | No new prospective restriction |

This order neither decides the underlying petition nor directs automatic denial if the compliance date passes. It does not reverse or reopen any lower judgment, award damages, remand the civil action, release anyone, or appoint counsel.

#### Opinions and Joins

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to action | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion and order of the Court, Part I | Per curiam | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Denies present financial leave | Assessment of the present petition and paid-compliance opportunity |
| Opinion and order of the Court, Part II | Per curiam | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Declines future restriction | Independent justification for the future category and for the inadequacy of reviewing filings individually |
| Dissent from Part I, joining Part II | Stevens | Stone-Zsela | Would grant present financial leave | Discretionary access for ordinary petition screening |

No vote on the underlying certiorari petition is announced.

#### Holdings

##### I. The present fee motion

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 39.8 permits, but does not require, denial of financial leave when the particular submission is frivolous or malicious, even if the applicant cannot prepay. The Court exercises that discretion here because the present petition supplies no arguable route through the independent state-action, conspiracy and judicial-immunity obstacles to the federal action; it allows payment of the Rule 38 fee and submission in Rule 33.1 form by March 30, 1998, without deciding certiorari.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt this rule, application and limited filing opportunity. Stevens and Stone-Zsela would grant the motion as a permissible discretionary choice.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court examines the grounds Glendora actually presents. She alleges false service, lack of personal jurisdiction and denial of a hearing to challenge service, and contests the appellate financial warning and clerk-signed order. Those allegations are not rejected as false. They nevertheless supply no developed arguable challenge connecting the service dispute to the independent immunity, state-action and conspiracy grounds defeating this federal action. A clerk's signature and the challenged panel composition do not themselves establish a federal defect. The Court does not confuse its own appellate jurisdiction with the District Court's inability to review a state judgment.

Martin requires particular-paper assessment and preserves a discretionary denial where an independent obstacle receives no arguable answer. Demos and Anderson permit an arguable access allegation to clear the fee threshold without proving entitlement to relief, but do not make the allegation's label conclusive. Arteaga applies the same distinction between considering an actual procedural explanation and finding an arguable route through the operative obstacle. The lower court's characterization, financial warning and unsuccessful filing history do not substitute for this examination. Financial denial is not a finding of wealth, dishonesty or default, and it decides neither the alleged misconduct nor any claim's ultimate merits. Payment and proper form permit ordinary processing, not a promised grant of review.

##### II. The proposed restriction on future noncriminal petitions

**Holding and operative rule:** A prospective fee-and-form restriction requires demonstrated applicant-specific abuse, a supported fit to the future category restricted, and support for the inadequacy of individual-paper treatment. The present record does not establish those predicates for Glendora's unidentified future noncriminal certiorari petitions, so the Court imposes no new restriction.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Martin, Day, Sassower, Anderson, Brown and Arteaga require an independent justification for future restraints. The present petition's deficiencies and the lower-court warning do not demonstrate repeated applicant-specific abuse fitted to all future noncriminal petitions, or why individual treatment is inadequate. A count of unsuccessful filings cannot establish the content of unidentified future papers. Keeping paid and criminal routes available would narrow the proposed condition but would not supply the missing justification. This refusal creates no numerical threshold, universal prior-warning requirement, duty to exhaust every conceivable lesser measure, or categorical prohibition on adequately supported restrictions. Targeted controls remain available on their own demonstrated predicates.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current Law

- Zatko preserves discretionary denial on a supported current-paper record and a paid-compliance opportunity; its limited remedy does not decide certiorari or justify every future restraint.
- Martin is applied to separate current financial leave from prospective control; it permits particular-paper denial without commanding denial whenever relief is doubtful.
- Demos and Anderson are distinguished on whether the concrete access allegation supplies an arguable route through the independent obstacle; their low financial-access threshold remains intact.
- Day and Sassower preserve the need to justify the particular future category on its own record.
- Brown and Arteaga are applied without change: current allegations receive individual examination and future restrictions require independent fit and inadequacy support.
- McDonald and Sindram retain their targeted extraordinary-writ authority; they do not automatically authorize this new noncriminal-certiorari condition.

**Current law:** Existing Rule 39 doctrine is applied. Supported present denials remain discretionary; financial permission, certiorari and underlying relief remain separate. No new future filing category is restricted, and supported targeted controls remain available under their own requirements.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, dissenting from Part I and joining Part II.** The dissent would accept the present papers under the financial-leave rules for ordinary petition screening. The concrete service and hearing allegations warrant that discretionary course despite the serious independent obstacles. Alleged false service and judicial acceptance do not themselves establish state action by private counsel, conspiracy, or an available collateral challenge to the state judgment. Martin permits supported present denials but does not compel denial in every difficult case; the dissent's preferred exercise of discretion does not displace the Court's rule. Granting financial leave would neither excuse a jurisdictional defect nor revive the Second Circuit appeal, establish the allegations, grant certiorari, appoint counsel, or award relief. The dissent joins the separate refusal of prospective controls because present deficiencies and filing volume do not establish inadequacy of individual treatment.

#### Source Notes

The [current petition, financial papers and appended lower orders](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0200) supply the service, hearing, warning and order-form content. False service and judicial misconduct remain allegations. The lower dismissal rests on independent immunity, state-action, conspiracy and jurisdictional grounds; its characterization does not establish this Court's finding without the individual examination stated above. Rules 39.8, 38 and 33.1 govern the present order. The [reported proceeding, 523 U.S. 206](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-206.pdf), supplies the docket and procedural setting.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The fee motion is disposed of and the underlying certiorari petition remains at the post-fee stage. Payment and Rule 33.1 compliance by March 30, 1998 permit ordinary processing under the Court's jurisdictional and review requirements. Any later disposition needs its own procedural basis; the date alone supplies no automatic denial.

**End of entry: Glendora v. Porzio, Filing-administration order, March 9, 1998.**

### Gray v. Maryland, No. 96-8653

Merits decision, March 9, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 8, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Maryland Court of Appeals, which reinstated Gray's conviction after the intermediate court set it aside. The question is whether a nontestifying codefendant's confession remains within Bruton when Gray's name is replaced by conspicuous blanks or spoken deletions. Gray seeks relief from the conviction; harmless error remains a distinct question. All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The state court receives the case for proceedings consistent with the confrontation holding, with any properly available Chapman harmless-error issue preserved. The Court makes no harmlessness finding and orders neither automatic retrial nor acquittal. Stone-Zsela agrees with vacatur and remand but would expressly direct the state court to determine harmless error.

#### Opinions and Joins

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Supports vacatur and remand | Direct-accusation redaction rule and application |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | O'Connor | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Supports vacatur and remand | Preservation-qualified Chapman review and absence of an automatic retrial or acquittal |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | — | Vacate and remand; would direct determination of harmless error | Joins Part I; states immediate-context application and a distinct affirmative remand instruction |
| Dissent | Scalia | Kennedy and Thomas | Would affirm on the preserved redaction question | Richardson facial-versus-inferential line, limiting instruction and separate preservation of prosecutorial-use questions |

#### Holdings

##### I. Obvious deletions that retain a direct accusation

**Holding and operative rule:** When a nontestifying codefendant's confession replaces a defendant's name with a conspicuous blank, spoken deletion, symbol or equivalent substitution that directly identifies the defendant from the statement and its immediate trial context, admitting that confession at their joint trial with only an instruction to consider it against the declarant violates the other defendant's right of confrontation under Bruton. Richardson continues to govern a confession neutral on its face that becomes incriminating only through later evidentiary linkage; this decision does not condemn every neutral pronoun, redaction or joint trial.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices adopt the rule and this application. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent. This is direct majority authority, not a combination of unlike rationales under Marks.

**Controlling explanation:** Bruton recognizes that a powerful direct accusation by an unavailable codefendant cannot reliably be confined to the declarant by an instruction. Richardson upheld a different kind of statement: the redaction removed every reference to the other defendant's existence, and only other evidence later supplied the incriminating link. Its reservation concerning symbols and substitute words leaves this question open.

Here the spoken deletions and written blanks leave the accusation's grammar and obvious missing identities in place. At this joint trial they invite the jury immediately to supply the other accused's name. Two omissions and testimony describing additional participants do not convert that transparent accusation into Richardson's otherwise neutral narrative. The distinction concerns the kind of inference, not whether any inference is involved. The limiting instruction therefore cannot cure the preserved confrontation violation.

Zafiro preserves a specific trial right even when instructions ordinarily provide adequate joint-trial protection; the benefits of joinder do not answer whether this particular accusation can be admitted. The holding rests on the challenged redaction itself. The prosecutor's follow-up question connecting Bell's information with Gray's arrest is not adjudicated as a separate constitutional wrong, and no separate preservation requirement is bypassed. The Court does not prescribe substantive rewriting of confessions or decide the validity of every neutral substitute, an independently admissible statement, or testimony subjected to cross-examination.

##### II. Constitutional error and the remand remain separate

**Holding and operative rule:** The Maryland judgment reinstating Gray's conviction is vacated and the case remanded for proceedings consistent with the confrontation holding. Any properly available harmless-error contention requires the State to establish beyond a reasonable doubt under Chapman that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict; this Court makes no harmlessness finding and orders neither an unconditional new trial nor acquittal.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt the preservation-qualified remand. Stone-Zsela supplies the sixth vote to vacate and remand, but would affirmatively direct the state court to determine harmless error and does not join Part II's narrower instruction.

**Controlling explanation:** Establishing an admission error does not itself establish the remedy required on the whole trial record. Chapman supplies the State's demanding harmlessness burden for constitutional trial error. Williamson preserves the distinction between determining an evidentiary or constitutional wrong and deciding its consequences, without substituting the standard for nonconstitutional error. The present materials do not permit this Court to find the confrontation error harmless. Further proceedings must respect the holding and any properly available error review; a materially different redaction or lawful trial-management alternative presents its own question. No conviction is reinstated by the possibility of harmlessness, and the State receives no favorable finding on an unexamined record.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current Law

- Bruton v. United States, 391 U.S. 123, is applied to obvious name deletions retaining a direct accusation; its limiting-instruction exception remains bounded to that confrontation risk.
- Richardson v. Marsh, 481 U.S. 200, is distinguished because its redaction removed reference to the defendant's existence; its rule for neutral statements requiring later evidentiary linkage remains effective.
- Zafiro v. United States, 506 U.S. 534, preserves specific confrontation protection alongside discretionary joint-trial management; it supplies no universal instruction cure.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18, supplies the State's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt harmlessness burden for constitutional trial error, without an application finding here.
- Williamson v. United States, 512 U.S. 594, preserves separate analysis of error and remedy; its assertion-specific evidentiary holding and different nonconstitutional harmlessness standard are not displaced.
- White v. Illinois, 502 U.S. 346, retains its exception-specific confrontation rules; no new categorical testimonial definition or exception for an accusatory police confession is adopted.

**Current law:** An obvious name deletion that retains a direct accusation falls within Bruton. Richardson's neutral-statement rule remains available, and no general prohibition on pronouns, lawful redaction, joint trials or cross-examined testimony follows. Preservation, constitutional harmlessness and lawful trial management remain separate inquiries.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Stone-Zsela joins Part I. The blanks, grammar, limited named participants and prosecutor's immediate connection between Bell's information and Gray's arrest directly identify whose name was removed. The jury need not wait for later independent evidence to identify the accused, so an instruction cannot cure the uncross-examined accusation. His reliance on immediate context explains the preserved admission objection; it creates no separate ruling that the follow-up question itself was a preserved independent constitutional wrong. He would direct the state court to determine harmless error, without deciding it here, and permit consideration of materially different redactions or lawful trial-management alternatives. He does not join Part II's limitation of the remand instruction to a properly available harmless-error contention. Joint trials, genuinely neutral wording, independently admissible confessions and cross-examined testimony remain outside his prohibition.

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** The dissent would apply Richardson's distinction between a confession that identifies the accused on its face and one that becomes incriminating through inference from other evidence. Bell's statement contains two deletions, while other testimony describes six participants and only one other defendant is at this trial. A blank does not uniquely identify Gray without a further inference. Conspicuous editing alone is not the powerfully incriminating express accusation for which Bruton displaces the ordinary presumption that jurors follow limiting instructions. The dissent objects to a rule encouraging concealed edits or substantive rewriting, and would affirm on the preserved confession-admission issue. It does not treat instructions as a cure for every confrontation problem or eliminate Rule 14 severance and evidentiary protection beyond the constitutional minimum. The prosecutor's follow-up question presents a separate issue: the State says an arrest warrant preceded the confession and characterizes the question as a transition, and no contemporaneous objection to that question is identified. The dissent would not use it to circumvent preservation or to transform the preserved redaction question into a different adjudication.

#### Source Notes

The [reported record and confession appendix, 523 U.S. 185](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-185.pdf), supply the joint-trial setting, two deleted names, additional-participant testimony, limiting instruction and appellate sequence. The [Bruton opinion, 391 U.S. 123](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-391/pdf/USREPORTS-391-123.pdf), and [Richardson opinion, 481 U.S. 200](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-481/pdf/USREPORTS-481-200.pdf), supply their distinct rules. The State's account of the prior warrant and reason for its follow-up question remains a submission, not a new factual finding. The record identifies objections to severance and admission, but no contemporaneous objection to that particular question; no new waiver or harmlessness finding is inferred from that limit.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Maryland judgment reinstating Gray's conviction is vacated. Further state proceedings must apply the redaction holding and, for any properly available harmless-error contention, Chapman. Materially different wording and lawful trial-management alternatives may be addressed in those proceedings on their own records. No present new-trial, acquittal or harmlessness result is directed.

**End of entry: Gray v. Maryland, Merits decision, March 9, 1998.**

### Quality King Distributors, Inc. v. L'anza Research International, Inc., No. 96-1470

Merits decision, March 9, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 8, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed summary judgment for L'anza after holding the first-sale defense unavailable against §602(a). The question concerns copyrighted labels on copies made in the United States, sold abroad and reimported without renewed copyright permission. Quality King seeks application of the first-sale rule and reversal. All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

#### Holdings

##### I. The first-sale limitation governs these domestically made copies

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 602(a) defines unauthorized importation as infringement of the distribution right in §106(3), and that right remains subject to §109(a). For a particular copy lawfully made in the United States and sold abroad with the copyright owner's authorization, its lawful owner, or a person authorized by that owner, may sell or otherwise dispose of that copy without renewed copyright permission; reimportation does not revive the exhausted distribution right.

**Authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt the complete owner-or-authorized-person rule and its domestic-manufacture boundary. Stone-Zsela joins the rule only as it concerns reimportation by the lawful owner, giving that narrower proposition nine votes; he does not join the extension to a person authorized by that owner.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted provisions operate together. Section 602(a) does not declare a freestanding right immune from the limits on the distribution right it expressly invokes. Section 106 subjects its rights to §§107–120, including §109(a), which permits the owner of a qualifying copy to dispose of it without the copyright owner's authority. A sale abroad does not, by itself, remove that limit for these domestically made copies. L'anza's interest in maintaining different domestic and foreign prices cannot supply an exception Congress did not enact.

The contrary argument that this reading makes §602(a) meaningless overlooks its remaining operation when the copy is not lawfully made, or the importer possesses it without ownership or the owner's authorization. The Court need not determine every remaining application to reject an independent importation right exempt from §109.

Bobbs-Merrill supplies the first-sale background: copyright's distribution authority does not itself enforce a continuing resale restriction after the owner's authorized sale. That principle does not decide contract liability. Campbell and Fogerty likewise respect Congress's actual rights and limitations; general market-policy preferences cannot enlarge the enacted entitlement. Here the claim concerns distribution and importation of copyrighted labels on domestically manufactured goods, not unauthorized reproduction. The Court leaves foreign-manufactured copies unresolved and does not decide counterfeiting, reproduction rights, independent contractual duties, trademark confusion, labeling or consumer-protection law.

##### II. The categorical importation judgment cannot stand

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's judgment, which treated §602(a) as barring the first-sale defense to this domestic-manufacture round trip, is reversed and the case returned for proceedings consistent with Part I. This disposition supplies no new finding about the first purchasing intermediary, exact place of title transfer or actual importer, and directs no damages, contract judgment or final judgment on an independently preserved theory.

**Authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt that limited remedial direction. Stone-Zsela joins reversal of the legal rule but would direct judgment for Quality King on the copyright-importation theory.

**Controlling explanation:** The District Court rejected the first-sale defense and entered summary judgment for L'anza; the Court of Appeals affirmed because it regarded the importation right as independent of §109. That categorical legal premise is incorrect. Reversal restores the statutory limitation without making new findings about the transaction's precise title sequence or resolving claims governed by different law. The published account supports the domestic-origin export-and-return setting but does not settle every intermediary detail. The Court therefore states the governing rule and returns the case for its consistent application, without turning the rejection of L'anza's legal theory into an unexpressed adjudication of every remaining issue.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current Law

- Bobbs-Merrill Co. v. Straus, 210 U.S. 339, supplies the first-sale principle limiting copyright control over a particular copy after an authorized sale; its resale-notice rule is not enlarged into a general contract holding.
- Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569, preserves enforcement of enacted copyright limits rather than dispositive market-policy presumptions; its four-factor fair-use inquiry is unchanged and is not substituted for §109.
- Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc., 510 U.S. 517, preserves copyright's actual statutory boundaries; its discretionary fee rules do not determine first sale or any fee award here.

**Current law:** The first-sale limit applies to the §106(3) distribution right invoked by §602(a) for the domestically made copies at issue. Foreign sale alone does not recreate that right on reimportation. Ownership or authorization by the copy's owner and lawful manufacture remain necessary; foreign manufacture and the distinct legal regimes identified above remain unresolved. No infringement, damages, fee or independent contract rule is added beyond the stated holding.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Stone-Zsela joins Part I only insofar as it addresses copies made in the United States, sold abroad with the copyright owner's authorization and later reimported by their lawful owner. He does not join its broader rule for a person authorized by that owner. Section 602(a) invokes the §106(3) right, which §109(a) limits; crossing the border cannot recreate an exhausted distribution right, and territorial-pricing policy cannot do so either. He would direct entry of judgment for Quality King on the claim that this reimportation infringed that distribution right through §602(a), rather than leave that theory for further proceedings. Any preserved independent contract, trademark, labeling or consumer-protection claim remains governed by its own law. His disposition would not decide foreign manufacture, unauthorized reproductions, counterfeits, reproduction rights, trademark confusion or contractual duties enforceable against a contracting party.

The exact foreign intermediary first purchasing, place of title transfer and importer identity are not newly found. No unauthorized reproduction of the labels is alleged. No particular damages amount, contract result or final judgment on an independent claim is directed. Stone-Zsela's preferred direction of judgment on the importation theory is not the Court's mandate.

### Aktepe v. United States, Eleventh Circuit No. 96-2167

Plenary decision on justiciability, March 10, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Judgment below: 105 F.3d 1400. The Court reviews under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) the February 20, 1997 affirmance of summary judgment for the United States. The question is whether the pleaded maritime negligence controversy is nonjusticiable, including whether a separable operational claim can be adjudicated without choosing military policy. No separate Supreme Court docket or oral-argument date is established. The sailors seek adjudication of two wrongful-death and 299 personal-injury claims arising when the Saratoga fired live missiles at the Muavenet during an intended mock NATO attack. All nine Justices participate at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

The political-question summary judgment remains effective. No negligence, damages, statutory-coverage, sovereign-immunity or Feres ruling, discovery order or new trial follows.

#### Opinions and Joins

Kennedy delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer. Stevens dissented, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg. The Court adopts the complete claim-specific ground for nonjusticiability and its limits; the dissent would vacate and remand for separate examination of execution allegations and policy choices, without guaranteeing a surviving claim.

#### Holdings

##### The pleaded duties cannot be adjudicated without choosing military training policy

**Holding and operative rule:** A military setting alone does not make a damages action nonjusticiable. These claims nevertheless cannot proceed because determining the alleged duties concerning drill notification, command terminology, live arming and questioning orders would require choosing among military training, obedience and safety policies without an independently manageable legal standard on the record before the Court.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer; five of nine participating Justices directly adopt the rule, application and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker requires examination of the particular claim, not rejection because its subject is military. Nixon preserves that issue-specific inquiry. Here ordinary negligence terminology does not identify a duty independent of the disputed training choices. Deciding which personnel should know that an attack is a mock exercise, what live-weapons commands should mean, and when operators should question an apparent firing instruction would select the balance between realistic preparation, command obedience and safety that the pleaded allegations challenge.

The contrary argument has force: this was an unintended live discharge during an intended mock exercise, and compensation after an accident differs from continuing supervision. Gilligan expressly left specific-injury controversies distinct from the ongoing supervision it rejected. But that reservation neither makes every damages action justiciable nor supplies a manageable standard for these proposed duties. Retrospective relief can still depend on judicial selection of training policy. No independently fixed operational command is established in the record before the Court that separates the alleged breach from that selection; this is not a finding that no such command exists or that plaintiffs withheld evidence.

Weiss and Loving preserve enforcement of actual constitutional and enacted limits. Dalton concerns a particular statutory review scheme and supplies no general executive immunity. Richenberg likewise combines substantial military weight with an enforceable legal standard. None supplies the independent duty missing from this claim-specific inquiry. NATO participation, possible embarrassment and military status alone would be insufficient. The Court affirms the political-question judgment without finding that the firing was lawful, deciding negligence or damages, or resolving statutory coverage, sovereign immunity or Feres. A materially different claim enforcing an independent legal duty remains outside this holding.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current Law

- Baker v. Carr supplies the claim-specific political-question inquiry; military subject matter alone remains insufficient.
- Nixon v. United States preserves review of independently fixed legal limits while recognizing the confined nonjusticiability of an actually committed procedural choice; its impeachment holding is not military immunity.
- Gilligan v. Morgan, 413 U.S. 1, is applied to the policy-selection concern, while its reservation of specific-injury controversies remains intact and supplies no categorical damages exception.
- Weiss v. United States and Loving v. United States preserve constitutional and statutory review in military matters; their appointment and delegation holdings do not establish the maritime duty proposed here.
- Dalton v. Specter retains scheme-specific statutory preclusion and is not extended to these maritime statutes.
- Richenberg v. Cohen preserves substantial military weight within actual legal constraints, without creating a military-tort immunity. No precedent is overruled.

**Current law:** The issue-specific political-question rule applies to these pleaded training and communication duties. Military status does not alone bar judicial review, and the decision establishes no categorical military-tort immunity. Independent statutory defenses and any materially different claim resting on an independently manageable duty retain their own requirements.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, dissenting.** The dissent would vacate and remand for separate examination of the claims. The intended mock exercise and accidental live firing supply a concrete reason to distinguish execution from design; listing training and command purposes does not establish that every allegation requires choosing policy. Gilligan's reservation of specific-injury cases and Nixon's claim-specific limitation oppose the blanket effect of the judgment. Weiss and Loving preserve enforceable legal constraints despite military subject matter, and Dalton does not supply general executive insulation.

The dissent accepts that a court cannot invent a safer training rule, call it operational, and thereby evade the political-question doctrine. The remand would require the Eleventh Circuit to identify which allegations actually require selection of constitutionally committed military policy or lack manageable standards, without guaranteeing a surviving negligence claim, discovery, liability or compensation. No particular written protocol is found or presumed violated. Statutory coverage, sovereign immunity, Feres, causation, damages and the justiciability of any specifically identified high-level policy challenge remain unresolved. The dissent's proposed examination is not the Court's mandate.

#### Source Notes

The [official Eleventh Circuit opinion, 105 F.3d 1400](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/19962167.OPA.pdf), supplies the summary-judgment posture, uncontested drill and communication facts and the unadjudicated Feres alternative. [Gilligan, 413 U.S. 1](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-413/pdf/USREPORTS-413-1.pdf), distinguishes continuing training supervision from reserved specific-injury controversies. The original maritime pleadings and evidence are not separately reproduced; no particular written operational protocol is established.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

This Supreme Court review is complete. Summary judgment on the political-question ground remains in force; the Court directs no further merits proceedings.

**End of entry: Aktepe v. United States, Plenary decision on justiciability, March 10, 1998.**

#### Holdings

##### I. The challenged notification is not punishment on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** For the class whose offenses preceded October 31, 1994 and who face Tier 2 or Tier 3 notification, New Jersey's civil public-protection purpose remains unrebutted by the clearest proof of punitive purpose or effect in the scheme's supported combined operation. The challenged notification therefore supplies no Ex Post Facto or Double Jeopardy ground for relief; the Court decides no same-offense question.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices directly adopt the common civil-classification ground. Scalia and Thomas support rejection on their separate grounds; Stevens dissents on Ex Post Facto punishment and does not reach independent Double Jeopardy identity.

**Controlling explanation:** Ursery supplies civil intent followed by demanding contrary proof, and Hendricks and Hudson require examination of actual combined operation. A civil label is insufficient. Here risk grading, individual exceptions and audience tailoring connect the warning to public protection. Tier 2 reaches qualifying schools, child-care facilities and organizations serving children or supporting women, subject to the state court's likely-encounter construction; Tier 3 reaches members of the public likely to encounter the registrant. The scheme directly imposes no residence, movement, employment or licensing prohibition.

The contrary shaming argument is substantial. Government actively distributes conviction and predicted-danger information, with potentially severe effects on housing, employment, relationships and safety. Those consequences cannot be dismissed categorically because some involve private acts. But the supported tailoring and causal record do not establish the clearest proof of punishment. The reported nineteen experiences, six state-compelled disclosures and separate 135-notification figure cannot be merged into one incidence calculation. Confidentiality instructions for Tier 2 and warnings against threats, vandalism and assault matter without guaranteeing perfect containment.

Hendricks found a different cumulative operation: prisonlike additional custody sustained on a future treatment structure. Its punitive result supplies no automatic classification of notification, just as its civil designation supplied no immunity there. Hudson and Kurth Ranch preserve meaningful aggregate examination; Halper's personal-monetary-penalty accounting is not a notification formula. The holding neither makes reputation irrelevant nor treats every public-safety measure as civil. Materially different punitive operation remains a different case. Registration alone, Tier 1, post-enactment offenses and every later registry are not decided.

##### II. The State must justify both tier and notification plan before ordinary notification

**Holding and operative rule:** In the ordinary circumstances presented, Tier 2 or Tier 3 notification may not proceed without a meaningful advance opportunity to contest both the risk classification and the proposed notification plan, with the prosecutor bearing persuasion for each. The protected interest rests on New Jersey's substantive restriction requiring disclosure to serve a legitimate and substantial interest with minimized invasion, rather than on reputation alone or a promise of procedures.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by the seven other Associate Justices; eight Justices directly adopt this protected-interest, hearing and State-persuasion ground. Stevens joins conditionally because civil notification remains available under the Court's judgment. Stone-Zsela supports the disposition on his stated procedural grounds without joining this distinct state-entitlement explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Sandin directs attention to a real substantive constraint and meaningful deprivation; its prison setting supplies no universal noncustodial threshold. Paul v. Davis distinguishes reputation alone, which is not the sole interest here. The actual state disclosure restraint and irreversible distribution make erroneous classification or an excessive audience a protected deprivation.

Mathews requires consideration of the interest, error risk and practical burdens. The State selects the tier and audience and already assembles an initial case. Requiring the registrant ultimately to disprove that justification wrongly allocates the risk of an unsupported disclosure. An initial prosecutorial showing does not replace the State's ultimate persuasion on both determinations. Public protection and prompt warning remain substantial interests, but do not justify making the affected person disprove the affirmative basis for disclosure.

Individual review must permit challenges to factual accuracy and calculation, atypical cases and excessive plans. A credible supported indication of likely reoffense requires high-risk treatment regardless of score; a physical condition minimizing risk, including advanced age or debilitating illness, requires low-risk treatment regardless of score. The Scale is not conclusive. This individual proceeding does not authorize a new facial attack on the Scale or its weights. No individual tier or plan is approved, and no general federal informational-privacy right is decided. The distinct question of the standard of proof is addressed below.

##### III. Risk classification requires clear and convincing proof; the plan injunction is retained on a narrower authority basis

**Holding and operative rule — risk classification:** Before the ordinary Tier 2 or Tier 3 notification presented here, the State must establish the factual basis and risk classification by clear and convincing evidence. Preponderance alone is constitutionally insufficient for that classification in this setting; proof beyond a reasonable doubt and certainty of future conduct are not required.

**Authority:** Part III-A of Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct supporters. Stevens's join operates because the Court permits civil notification. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would hold State preponderance sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathews requires a practical comparison of error risks, not automatic transplantation of every civil burden. Addington and Santosky show that a civil label does not end the inquiry and that sufficiently serious erroneous deprivation can require greater assurance. The classification here combines disputed historical facts, unadjudicated circumstances and predictive judgments with disclosure that cannot effectively be recalled. Overclassification can alter housing, work, safety and status before an error is repaired.

Underclassification can endanger potential victims, and that concern is not discounted. But continuing registration and law-enforcement monitoring remain protective measures, while the State already marshals its initial factual case. A prompt adversarial determination under the heightened standard reduces erroneous classification without demanding perfect prediction or disabling warnings justified by the evidence. The required high-risk and low-risk overrides and individual factual review give the additional assurance practical work. Existing hearing procedures do not answer the separate question of ultimate persuasion and its strength. Sahhar preserves different burdens for different legal operations; this holding derives from this notification setting, not from treating its initial-commitment and discharge provisions as interchangeable. The Court imposes no universal heightened burden for every civil prediction.

**Judgment without a controlling rationale for the plan's standard of proof:** The injunction's requirement of clear and convincing State proof for the notification plan is also affirmed, 5–4, by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Only Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part III-B's plan-specific error-allocation reasoning. Stone-Zsela supports retention of that injunction component without adopting a plan-specific ground. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would retain State persuasion but permit preponderance. The injunction binds this litigation; the judgment establishes no five-Justice rationale for the plan's standard of proof or broader general rule. The agreement in judgment without an accompanying rationale and the four-Justice explanation cannot be combined under Marks as broader and logically narrower grounds.

##### IV. The advance-notice emergency question remains unripe

**Holding and operative rule:** The present class challenge does not decide whether a concrete emergency may justify notification without ordinary advance notice because no representative alleges such notification and no prosecutor is shown to have done or planned it. The ordinary-process injunction is retained subject to that express reservation; it neither approves every emergency bypass nor prohibits every judicially authorized exception.

**Authority:** Part IV of Souter's opinion, joined by the seven other Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition of the separately reserved emergency component, adding no emergency rationale or new exception.

**Controlling explanation:** The described mechanism permits a prosecutor to seek a judicial order when release information arrives only after a Tier 3 offender's release or the registrant evades service despite every good-faith effort; after three days without completed service the prosecutor may apply. Permission to apply is not a command to bypass notice. Without a concrete use or planned use affecting the class, the Court cannot determine the constitutional adequacy of that exceptional procedure or any narrowing state construction. A later concrete application must stand on its own facts and legal basis. The ordinary holding therefore resolves neither that future controversy nor an imagined automatic emergency exemption.

- Ursery, Ward and Mendoza-Martinez supply rebuttable civil classification and combined purpose/effect examination; neither labels nor a single indicator is conclusive.
- Hendricks retains its as-applied punitive-confinement holding and actual-operation method; its outcome is distinguished, not replaced with an approval of that custody.

A genuine substantive state privacy constraint supports ordinary advance review and State persuasion for both tier and plan. **Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Stone-Zsela joins the civil-classification and heightened risk-classification portions. Public protection and a civil structure do not remove the need for timely notice, disclosure of the basis, an opportunity to contest, a neutral decisionmaker, written reasons and clear and convincing State proof before the broad notification at issue. The State controls the evidence and proposes the classification; an erroneous high tier can irreversibly affect housing, work, safety and status. **Stevens, dissenting in part.** Stevens would reverse the denial of Ex Post Facto relief and require an injunction against retrospective Tier 2/3 notification for this defined class. The affirmative official broadcast of conviction and predicted dangerousness, its criminal-history trigger and enduring cumulative burdens resemble imposed shaming. Risk and audience limits deserve weight but do not alter that combined character on his assessment. Ursery's dissenting approach and the actual-operation holdings of Hendricks and Hudson support looking beyond the civil label. **Souter's Part III-B, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer.** These four would apply clear and convincing persuasion to the plan as well as the classification because unnecessary geographic or audience expansion causes the same irreversible disclosure. The State prepares the plan and its initial justification; factual disputes, atypical risks and the actual likely-encounter limit make stronger assurance useful without demanding certainty. Continuing registration and monitoring reduce the comparative cost of mistaken undernotification. This is the rationale of four Justices, not a controlling general rule for the plan's standard of proof.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas; O'Connor and Kennedy join the standard-of-proof portion only.** Scalia and Thomas reject the Ex Post Facto challenge because the particular risk-tailored warning scheme is genuinely civil and protective on this record. They separately reject the Double Jeopardy challenge because that civil warning process is not a successive criminal prosecution. All four Justices dissenting on the standard of proof accept the substantive state interest, advance opportunity and State persuasion for both tier and plan, but would permit preponderance. Mathews also accounts for potential victims, the danger of underclassification and existing safeguards. Noncustodial tailored warnings do not impose Addington's physical custody or Santosky's family-status severance; neither serious consequences nor a protected interest mechanically requires heightened certainty. Sahhar's different commitment and discharge rules demonstrate the need to preserve the actual legal operation. Their proposed modification would remove only the heightened standard of proof, preserving the remainder of the ordinary-process injunction.

### Caterpillar Inc. v. International Union, UAW, No. 96-1925

Dismissal under Rule 46.1, March 23, 1998

### Hetzel v. Prince William County, No. 97-954

Summary merits decision, March 23, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Following the separate same-date limited grant, the Court reviews the Fourth Circuit's mandamus command requiring an unconditional reduced compensatory judgment and staying a damages retrial. No oral argument is held. A $750,000 jury award was first reduced to $500,000 because one supporting claim was legally insufficient; after appellate excessiveness review, the District Court set $50,000 and permitted a new damages trial when Hetzel rejected it. The challenged mandamus foreclosed that option. A later $15,000 judgment and its separate pending appeal are not the judgment reviewed here. All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Reversed in the reviewed respect and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The mandamus constraint is removed insofar as it requires an unconditional reduced compensatory award and bars the jury election. Further proceedings must permit acceptance of a properly determined remittitur or an appropriate new trial, with damages-only retrial dependent on separability. No amount is selected, original verdict restored, claim revived or separate pending appeal decided.

#### Opinions and Joins

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Per curiam | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Reaches later mandamus challenge | Limited earlier-petition/mandate-scope ground; Stone-Zsela agrees with disposition only |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Per curiam | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Reverses the constraint and remands | Common remittitur election, remedy and stated limits |

The merits joins do not disclose the vote on certiorari.

#### Holdings

##### I. The earlier-petition objection does not bar review of this later restriction

**Holding and operative rule:** Respondents' objection that the earlier petition omitted the jury-election claim does not prevent review of the later mandamus command here. The earlier mandate required recalculation without necessarily eliminating a remittitur election; the constitutional objection was before the Court of Appeals when its later order expressly required final judgment and stayed retrial.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices directly adopt this confined threshold ground. Stone-Zsela agrees with reaching the later challenge but adds no preservation ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Recalculation and compulsory acceptance are different instructions. A reduced award can remain conditional on the plaintiff's choice, so the earlier instruction did not necessarily impose the no-election restriction now challenged. After Hetzel rejected the revised sum, the District Court addressed the Seventh Amendment question and permitted a new damages trial. That memorandum and issue were before the Court of Appeals when mandamus required an unconditional final judgment and stayed the trial.

Sanford Fork & Tool distinguishes matters actually settled by a mandate from those left open and permits examination of the opinion to determine its reach. Messenger distinguishes law-of-the-case practice from a jurisdictional limit on the rendering court's power. Neither excuses every omitted issue in an earlier petition. They support identifying what the earlier mandate actually determined; this sequence identifies the later no-election command as the challenged restriction.

Respondents correctly observe that a later enforcement order does not always reopen an earlier conclusive decision. That principle does not establish such a prior decision here. The Court does not reopen the earlier excessiveness determination, infer an unquoted contrary mandate term or find a general waiver exception. The separate pending appeal from the later $15,000 judgment neither erases this concrete restriction nor becomes part of this review.

##### II. An excessiveness remittitur must preserve the new-trial election

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal court may condition retention of a jury's compensatory judgment on acceptance of a lawful reduction for excessiveness, but it may not impose that substituted valuation unconditionally while denying the plaintiff the option of a new jury trial. The challenged mandamus restriction is reversed insofar as it requires unconditional reduced recovery and forecloses that election, and the case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the choice between a properly determined remittitur and an appropriate new trial.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. This required choice between remittitur and a new trial controls without requiring adoption of each Justice's distinct position on the broader appellate-allocation question in Gasperini.

**Controlling explanation:** Gasperini expressly preserves the federal remittitur election. The choice allows lawful correction of excessiveness while preventing a court's valuation from becoming an unconditional replacement for the jury's. Dimick preserves the traditional remittitur distinction, and Kennon v. Gilmer recognizes the need to leave the new-trial choice when a court reduces a jury's award. Calling the restriction a mandate does not remove the jury protection.

The initial reduction from $750,000 to $500,000 because one supporting claim was legally insufficient differs from the later excessiveness-based reductions. Neely's liability-insufficiency setting does not justify replacing a supported-liability jury valuation without the election. No legally fixed recovery cap is established for the challenged restriction; comparisons with awards in other cases do not create one. BMW's state punitive-damages remedy likewise supplies no exception to this federal compensatory-jury rule.

The Court does not find the original award reasonable, restore $750,000 or $500,000, select $50,000 or $15,000, revive the legally insufficient claim or reverse the earlier excessiveness determination. The scope of any new trial must follow ordinary law, including separability from liability before a damages-only trial. The later $15,000 judgment and pending separate appeal are not themselves vacated or decided. This remand removes the unlawful mandamus constraint; it does not promise a particular amount or an impermissibly partial retrial.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current Law

- Gasperini's remittitur election is applied; its distinct diversity/state-compensation holding and existing federal appellate-allocation rule remain unchanged.
- Dimick v. Schiedt preserves traditional remittitur and the separate prohibition on federal additur.
- Kennon v. Gilmer, 131 U.S. 22, supplies the election principle when judicial reduction substitutes for the jury's damages valuation.
- Neely v. Martin K. Eby Construction Co., 386 U.S. 317, is distinguished as a liability-insufficiency decision, not authority for compulsory excessiveness remittitur.
- BMW v. Gore retains its state punitive-damages remedy and does not displace this federal compensatory-jury protection.
- In re Sanford Fork & Tool Co., 160 U.S. 247, supplies the settled/open mandate distinction; Messenger v. Anderson, 225 U.S. 436, distinguishes law-of-the-case practice from jurisdiction. Neither creates a general excuse for omitted earlier-petition issues. No precedent is overruled.

**Current law:** The existing federal remittitur election remains enforceable against an unconditional appellate mandate. Review of this later restriction does not create a general waiver exception or reopen every earlier mandate. The original excessiveness determination, legally insufficient claim and pending separate damages appeal retain their stated boundaries.

#### Source Notes

The [reported proceeding, 523 U.S. 208](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-208.pdf), supplies the mandamus sequence, respondents' earlier-petition objection and the distinct pending appeal. Original earlier-petition and mandamus filings are known through published recitations rather than reproduced originals. No separate damages appeal, additional claim or dollar amount is resolved by this review.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The matter returns for lawful proceedings preserving the choice between a properly determined remittitur and an appropriate new jury trial. Any damages-only trial requires separability from liability. No award is selected or automatically restored, and the later $15,000 judgment and separate appeal are not themselves vacated or decided.

**End of entry: Hetzel v. Prince William County, Summary merits decision, March 23, 1998.**

### United States v. Robinson, Fifth Circuit No. 96-11165

Plenary merits decision, March 23, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Judgment below: 119 F.3d 1205. On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the August 8, 1997 judgment. The grant concerns facial and as-applied Commerce Clause objections to the Hobbs Act convictions, including the proposed requirement of a substantial individual effect and the derivative firearm objection. The convictions comprise conspiracy, aiding and abetting three business robberies, and aiding and abetting one firearm offense. The Fifth Circuit affirmed the convictions but separately vacated the sentence and remanded on the vulnerable-victim enhancement; that sentencing component is outside the grant. No separate Supreme Court docket or oral-argument date is established. All nine Justices participate at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy or limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facial and as-applied commerce challenge to the Hobbs Act robbery and conspiracy convictions | Affirmed in the reviewed respect, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Convictions remain effective against this commerce objection |
| Wholly derivative firearm commerce challenge | Affirmed in the reviewed respect, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Stone-Zsela agrees with disposition only; no independent firearm defect is adjudicated |

The Fifth Circuit's separate sentencing vacatur and resentencing mandate remain undisturbed. The Court does not reinstate the vacated sentence or enhancement, order a new trial or new sentencing proceeding, or adjudicate an unrelated indictment, instruction or sufficiency objection.

#### Opinions and Joins

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Rejects the Hobbs Act commerce objections | Direct commercial-interference mechanism, aggregation after connection, robbery/conspiracy application and limits |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Kennedy | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Rejects the derivative firearm objection | Dependent constitutional ground only; Stone-Zsela agrees with this judgment component without joining its reasoning |

#### Holdings

##### I. Direct interference with operating commercial assets supplies the required commerce connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may apply the Hobbs Act to robbery directed at the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce when the taking directly impairs its commercial operations and the Act's enacted commerce element is actually established. The effect of one robbery may be slight: aggregation measures the magnitude of this directly connected economic class, but neither a business label nor generalized economic consequences of crime supplies the antecedent connection.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt the rule, application to the Hobbs Act components and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Lopez requires the enacted class and its actual interstate mechanism before magnitude can establish federal authority. The relevant class here is robbery affecting commerce through direct interference with operating business capital, not all robbery. The targeted businesses purchased interstate inventory and cashed out-of-state, payroll and government-benefit checks. The takings removed money withdrawn for those operations and impaired restocking and check-cashing capacity; the published record also reports one business closing for lack of capital. Taking the working money in a bank drive-through lane or store parking lot does not change its established business function.

American Life League supplies the closest mechanism: conduct directed at an actual interstate market's operations can directly obstruct that market. Once that relationship exists, Wickard and Perez permit evaluation of class-wide effects without demanding a substantial effect from each individual act. The statutory commerce element still requires an actual offense-specific nexus. Its words alone would not validate a constitutionally inadequate connection, and its character as an element does not make it subject-matter jurisdiction.

The contrary argument properly rejects multiplying local losses as a substitute for a constitutional mechanism. But this record identifies that mechanism before aggregation. A theory treating every victim as a potential consumer or every dollar as eventual interstate spending would lack this boundary and is not adopted. Hale, Rybar and Kirk concern the distinct inclusion of possession necessary to a valid machinegun regime; no such anti-evasion rationale is needed here.

The facial commerce objection fails because the Act lawfully reaches this defined class; the Court does not validate every imagined application. The actual business-robbery components and conspiracy directed at those robberies likewise require no substantial individual effect. This is no universal conspiracy-elements rule. Purely personal or household robbery, remote historical movement, every asserted depletion theory, a universal dollar floor and constitutional validity without the enacted commerce connection remain undecided.

##### II. The wholly derivative firearm objection fails with the predicate commerce challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented constitutional objection to the firearm conviction supplies no ground for relief because it depends entirely on the rejected claim that the underlying Hobbs Act business robberies exceed the commerce power. This disposition adjudicates no independent firearm element, instruction, sufficiency, aiding-and-abetting or collateral defect.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices directly adopt this derivative ground. Stone-Zsela agrees with the firearm disposition only and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower opinion identifies the firearm constitutional challenge as dependent on invalidity of the Hobbs Act predicates. Part I rejects that asserted invalidity through the actual business-interference mechanism and proved commerce nexus. The derivative argument therefore fails on its own stated premise. Neither this conclusion nor Salinas's distinct statutory-conspiracy analysis resolves an independent defect that is not presented. The Court does not infer a new admission, revise the indictment or approve the whole jury charge. The separate lower sentencing vacatur remains effective; the commerce affirmance neither reinstates the vulnerable-victim enhancement nor creates a new resentencing or retrial order.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current Law

- Lopez is applied without changing its connection-before-magnitude sequence, actual statutory-class inquiry, fit requirement or rejection of a general police power. An enacted commerce element is relevant but no automatic safe harbor.
- American Life League supplies the direct-market-interference mechanism; its service-market application is extended to the actual impairment of these businesses' commercial operations, not to all crime costs.
- Wickard and Perez preserve class-wide magnitude analysis once a genuine economic-market connection exists; neither requires substantial individual magnitude or manufactures a missing connection.
- Hale, Rybar and Kirk retain their distinct anti-evasion/material-impairment ground for the machinegun regime. That ground is not transferred to robbery.
- Parker and Mussari/Schroeder retain the distinct cross-border-payment mechanism, and Pic-A-State retains its actual interstate-transmission ground; none supplies an all-money rule.
- Salinas's agreement analysis remains statute-specific and supplies no universal conspiracy code. Robertson's direct-enterprise alternative is not reached. No precedent is overruled.

**Current law:** Robbery directly impairing the actual commercial operations of an interstate business may be regulated when the enacted Hobbs Act commerce connection is established. Aggregation addresses class-wide magnitude after that connection exists; no substantial individual effect is constitutionally required for this class. Purely personal robbery, generic consumer-spending chains and other reserved applications remain distinct. The firearm result is confined to the derivative objection, and the lower sentencing vacatur remains effective.

#### Source Notes

The [official Fifth Circuit opinion, 119 F.3d 1205](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/96/96-11165.CR0.wpd.pdf), supplies the businesses' interstate inventory and check-cashing functions, targeted working-capital takings, commerce objections and separate sentencing vacatur. The [reported text](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/119/119.F3d.1205.96-11165.html) includes the complete lower analysis and footnotes. The record supplies no exact dollar threshold, independent firearm defect, additional indictment finding or basis for general approval of the instructions.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

This Supreme Court commerce review is complete. The convictions stand against these objections. The separate lower-court resentencing mandate continues under its own terms, without reinstatement of the vacated sentence or a new Supreme Court sentencing order.

**End of entry: United States v. Robinson, Plenary merits decision, March 23, 1998.**
