## Decisions and Dispositions

### Almendarez-Torres v. United States, No. 96-6839

Merits decision, March 24, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 14, 1997; decided March 24, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, reviewing its affirmance of an 85-month sentence. The question is whether the prior aggravated-felony conviction in 8 U.S.C. §1326(b)(2) is an element of aggravated unlawful reentry or a sentencing factor that need not appear in the indictment.

The September 1995 indictment charged unlawful reentry under §1326 without alleging the prior aggravated-felony convictions. At his December 1995 plea, Almendarez-Torres admitted the prior convictions and deportation; he preserved his objection that the indictment authorized no more than §1326(a)'s two-year maximum. The District Court imposed the enhanced sentence in March 1996.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

The enhanced sentence is vacated. On the existing indictment and plea, the sentence may not exceed §1326(a)'s two-year maximum. The guilty plea is not withdrawn and no acquittal is entered. Whether another source of law permits a further proceeding on a properly charged aggravated offense is not decided.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg | Supports reversal and resentencing | Statutory element classification, charging consequence and resentencing remedy |
| Opinion announcing the judgment, Part II | Scalia | Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg | Supports reversal and resentencing | Constitutional-avoidance explanation; no definitive constitutional classification rule |
| Concurrence in Part I and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Concurs in Part I and in the judgment | Definitive constitutional ground for the element classification; does not join Part II's reservation of that question |
| Dissent | O'Connor | Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer | Would affirm | Penalty construction and bounded constitutional permission for this recidivist enhancement; would affirm |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Section 1326(b)(2) requires an aggravated-offense allegation before its higher maximum may apply

**Holding and operative rule:** The prior aggravated-felony conviction specified in §1326(b)(2) is an element of the aggravated unlawful-reentry offense. It must be alleged in the federal indictment and, unless validly admitted as an element of the charged offense, proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt; an admission to the fact of a prior conviction does not cure its omission from this indictment, so the existing conviction permits no sentence above §1326(a)'s two-year maximum.

**Authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. These five expressly adopt the statutory rule, its ordinary element consequences and the remedy. Four explain the construction through constitutional avoidance; Stone-Zsela gives a definitive constitutional ground separately. No majority adopts a general constitutional rule governing all facts that increase a statutory maximum.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that subsection (a)'s complete unlawful-reentry offense and two-year maximum must be distinguished from subsection (b)(2)'s aggravated offense carrying a twenty-year maximum. The prior conviction authorizes a different punishment ceiling; it does more than inform selection of punishment within the ordinary range. The provisions' cross-references permit that element construction. Hamling requires the indictment to state the offense's elements, and Gaudin requires proof of an actual element to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt when it has not validly been admitted. Almendarez-Torres admitted the prior convictions, but the indictment did not charge that aggravating element. His factual admission therefore does not answer the preserved objection to the punishment legally authorized by this charge.

The traditional use of recidivism at sentencing does not decide this statute's classification. McMillan addressed a minimum within an unchanged maximum, and Watts permits ordinary sentencing findings within conviction-based authority while reserving element evasion and other constitutional limits. Custis addresses collateral attacks on existing convictions, not the indictment's content. Old Chief confirms that prior-conviction status can be proved without unnecessary prejudicial detail; that evidentiary concern does not require treating status as a sentencing factor. The decision does not determine a subsidiary standard-of-proof claim, invalidate the admitted prior convictions, select a replacement sentence, or decide facts that do not increase the statutory maximum.

**Precedent treatment:** Hamling and Gaudin govern the consequences of the statutory classification; McMillan, Watts, Custis and Old Chief retain the distinct boundaries stated below.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Hamling v. United States:** Applied to require an indictment alleging each element of the aggravated offense.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applied after statutory classification; its jury-and-proof rule for actual elements remains controlling without deciding all sentencing classifications.
- **McMillan v. Pennsylvania:** Distinguished because its mandatory minimum left the statutory maximum unchanged; its limited holding remains intact.
- **United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra:** Preserved for ordinary sentencing within punishment authorized by the conviction; it does not resolve this higher-maximum classification.
- **Custis v. United States:** Distinguished as a collateral-review decision; the existing limits on attacking prior convictions remain intact.
- **Old Chief v. United States:** Applied to the distinction between element status and the form of adequate prior-conviction proof; no broader compulsory-stipulation rule is created.

#### Law After Decision

Section 1326(b)(2)'s higher range requires the aggravated-offense element and the corresponding charging and proof protections. This statutory rule binds lower courts. The Court establishes no general constitutional prior-conviction exception and no general constitutional rule that every maximum-increasing fact is an element. Ordinary consideration of prior convictions within an independently authorized range remains available, subject to governing law.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Scalia, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, Part II.** These Justices regard the tenfold increase in maximum punishment as presenting serious doubt about Congress's ability to assign the aggravating fact solely to a sentencing judge. The element reading is fairly available, so they apply constitutional avoidance and decline to decide that ultimate question. McMillan's unchanged ceiling and Watts's ordinary sentencing setting do not eliminate the doubt. The risk of prejudice from prior-conviction evidence can be addressed through adequate admissions or other proper proof rather than removal of an element from the charge.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and in the judgment.** The constitutional allocation turns on the fact's legal effect. A fact necessary to increase the authorized maximum from two years to twenty years performs the work of an element and must be charged and either validly admitted as an element of that offense or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. Legislative placement cannot transfer that work entirely to the sentencing judge. Gaudin protects element determination; Watts permits ordinary judicial sentencing findings only within conviction-based authority. Custis concerns collateral attack, and Old Chief demonstrates how adequate status proof can avoid unnecessary prejudice. The admitted facts here do not cure an indictment that never charged the aggravated offense. This position permits consideration of prior convictions within a separately authorized range, preserves Custis's collateral-review limits, requires no prejudicial details when adequate admission or stipulation establishes the element, and decides no sentencing fact that leaves the statutory maximum unchanged.

**O'Connor, joined by Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, dissenting.** Subsection (b)(2)'s recidivist subject, reciprocal cross-references and punishment structure make it a penalty provision for the alien already described in subsection (a). Existing conviction status differs from facts about commission of the present crime, and Congress may treat that prior adjudication as a sentencing factor in this setting. The admission supports the application but is not a substitute for their statutory classification. These Justices acknowledge that McMillan and Watts do not alone resolve an increased ceiling; the distinct recidivism ground does that work. They would affirm without adopting unlimited legislative power to remove offense elements or resolving an unpresented subsidiary proof-burden question.

#### Source Notes

The applicable provision is the [1994 text of 8 U.S.C. §1326](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title8/pdf/USCODE-1994-title8.pdf), which increased the earlier fifteen-year aggravated-felony maximum to twenty years. The published account of the indictment, plea, preserved objection and sentence appears in [523 U.S. 227–229](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-224.pdf). That account does not establish the exact charged found-in date or an intentional waiver of the indictment objection. No temporal challenge or broader waiver is decided.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Fifth Circuit for resentencing. The enhanced sentence cannot remain in effect under the existing indictment and plea; the sentencing court must act within §1326(a)'s maximum. No new aggravated-offense proceeding or particular replacement term is ordered.

**End of entry: Almendarez-Torres v. United States, Merits decision, March 24, 1998.**

### Ohio Adult Parole Authority v. Woodard, No. 96-1769

Merits decision, March 25, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 10, 1997; decided March 25, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, reviewing 107 F.3d 1178. The questions concern due process in Ohio's capital-clemency procedure and whether the offered interview compels self-incrimination.

Woodard brought this §1983 action after receiving notice of a voluntary interview and a clemency hearing. He did not request the interview. The District Court granted judgment on the pleadings. The Sixth Circuit rejected an independent entitlement to clemency, but ordered further proceedings on an established-process theory of due process and on a possible unconstitutional condition on silence.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Due-process remand | Reversed, 8–1 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stevens | Reject the present process claim; no entitlement to clemency or new hearing follows |
| Self-incrimination remand | Reversed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Reject the claim based on the offered voluntary interview |

The Sixth Circuit's rejection of an independent entitlement to clemency is undisturbed. The case returns for proceedings consistent with these rulings. The Court neither grants nor denies mercy, sets an execution date, adjudicates a stay, nor orders immunity or a new clemency hearing.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I-A | O'Connor | Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | No entitlement to mercy | No state-created substantive entitlement to clemency; Stone-Zsela joins the conclusion that discretionary mercy creates no right to its grant |
| Opinion of the Court, Part I-B | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Rejects the requested process theory | Appellate analogy supplies no basis for the requested procedure; Stone-Zsela joins only the distinction between discretionary mercy and another criminal trial |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II-A | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Establishes the minimum safeguard | Minimum safeguards against fundamentally arbitrary capital-clemency procedure |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II-B | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Rejects the pleaded due-process claim | The pleaded process objections do not establish violation of that minimum; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas agree in this disposition on Parts I-A and I-B |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III-A | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Rejects the voluntary-interview claim | The offered voluntary interview does not establish compelled self-incrimination |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III-B | O'Connor | Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Additional ground rejecting self-incrimination claim | The conclusion also survives an assumed ordinary civil adverse inference, with actual-compulsion and automatic-penalty limits; Stone-Zsela does not join this additional ground |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Kennedy | Scalia and Thomas | Concurs in part and in the judgment | Joins Parts I and III; rejects the present process claim without joining Part II's affirmative retained-life framework |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stevens | No other Justice | Concurs in part; would retain the due-process remand | Joins I-A, II-A and III; would preserve the due-process remand and does not join I-B or II-B |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Required clemency procedures do not create an entitlement to mercy or triallike review

**Holding and operative rule:** Ohio's required investigation, hearing and advisory recommendation do not create a substantive entitlement to clemency because they do not constrain the Governor to grant it on specified facts. The analogy to a first appeal as of right does not itself require appellate representation, triallike evidence procedure or a new determination of guilt in this discretionary executive process.

**Authority:** Part I-A has all eight Associates, with Stone-Zsela also joining the no-right-to-mercy conclusion. Part I-B has O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela joins only the mercy-versus-criminal-trial distinction. Stevens does not join the rejection of the established-process analogy.

**Controlling explanation:** Dumschat distinguishes seeking clemency from entitlement to its grant. Sandin requires a genuine substantive constraint on a meaningful deprivation; Doe and Johnson likewise distinguish required administration from an entitlement to a favorable discretionary decision. Ohio must investigate, hold the required hearing and recommend action even after an execution stay, but its recommendation is advisory and the Governor retains complete discretion. A later hearing or revised recommendation on new information is permitted, not required. These provisions do not compel clemency.

Evitts protects the first appeal as of right within adjudication of guilt. Clemency performs a different executive function. Its availability after conviction does not itself import appellate or trial procedure. The Court rejects the Sixth Circuit's general integral-stage theory as a basis for the requested remand, while preserving independently retained constitutional rights. Herrera's record-specific innocence ruling establishes no rule that executive mercy is the exclusive constitutionally sufficient substitute for every claim of innocence.

##### II. Capital-clemency procedures retain a constitutional minimum, satisfied on these pleadings

**Holding and operative rule:** An established capital-clemency process must afford minimal safeguards against fundamentally arbitrary procedure, including arbitrary denial of any access or substitution of an entirely arbitrary device such as a coin flip for consideration. The notice and offered interview here, considered with the pleaded restrictions, satisfy that minimum; they establish no entitlement to clemency, trial protections, a favorable recommendation or judicial review of mercy's merits.

**Authority:** Part II-A's minimum safeguard has six direct joins: O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part II-B's sufficient-process application has five: O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens disputes the application; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas agree with rejection of the claim on their distinct entitlement/adjudication ground. Both controlling propositions rest on direct majority joins, without combining separate rationales under Marks.

**Controlling explanation:** A valid death sentence reduces the process owed before discretionary mercy is denied, but the prisoner remains alive. That retained interest supplies a minimum protection independent of a state-created entitlement to release. Ford illustrates the distinction between the interest protected and the flexible procedure appropriate to it; this case does not import Ford's separate prohibition on executing an incompetent prisoner or its particular hearing requirements.

Woodard alleges three days' interview notice, ten days' hearing notice, difficulty preparing while postconviction proceedings continued, exclusion of counsel from the interview, chair-controlled counsel participation at the hearing, and inability to testify or submit documents at the hearing. Accepting that pleaded account, the notice and offered personal interview provided access, and those restrictions do not establish fundamentally arbitrary procedure requiring further adjudication. The Court does not find an unmentioned alternative document channel or reject the allegations as untrue. It establishes neither a fixed list of required safeguards nor an accuracy code for every disputed item in a clemency file. Johnson's rejection of a parole-file process entitlement does not extinguish independent constitutional protection.

##### III. The optional interview does not establish compelled self-incrimination

**Holding and operative rule:** An optional clemency interview does not compel self-incrimination merely because the prisoner must choose between providing potentially harmful information and remaining silent; absence of counsel, confidentiality or immunity does not itself convert this invitation into compulsion. The conclusion also holds if an ordinary civil adverse inference from silence is assumed, but it does not authorize an automatic privilege penalty, compelled waiver or criminal use of compelled answers.

**Authority:** Part III-A's present voluntary-interview holding has all nine Justices. Part III-B's additional ordinary-inference ground and its limits have all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join that additional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Murphy distinguishes a difficult evidentiary choice from a command backed by a genuine threatened penalty for asserting the privilege. Woodard was invited, not required, to attend or answer. The State's refusal to promise confidentiality or immunity does not establish that it compelled answers. No automatic loss of clemency eligibility for silence, compelled waiver or criminal use of compelled testimony is established.

The eight Associates additionally apply Baxter's distinction between ordinary adverse inferences in civil proceedings and automatic sanctions attached to silence. Even assuming such an inference in this civil clemency evaluation, the voluntary choice does not become compelled testimony. The record does not establish Ohio's actual use of silence or answers; the assumption supplies an alternative legal ground, not a factual finding. LaChance preserves separately supported compulsion and waiver claims, and Johnson confirms that absence of entitlement to a benefit does not excuse an independent unconstitutional penalty. A materially supported coercive condition would therefore require its own analysis.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Connecticut Board of Pardons v. Dumschat:** Applied to discretionary mercy and the distinction between seeking relief and entitlement to its grant; not extended to extinguish independent constitutional interests.
- **Sandin v. Conner:** Applied at its substantive-constraint rule. Mandatory procedure alone is insufficient, and directly retained constitutional interests remain separate.
- **Doe v. District of Columbia:** Applied to distinguish investigative duties from a substantive entitlement.
- **Johnson v. Rodriguez:** Applied to the same procedure/entitlement boundary and preservation of independent constitutional wrongs; its ordinary initial-parole rule does not displace the retained-life floor.
- **Evitts v. Lucey:** Distinguished at the adjudicatory first appeal as of right; it does not itself require the requested capital-clemency procedures.
- **Ford v. Wainwright:** Its distinction between a protected interest and appropriate flexible procedure informs the minimum-process rule; its separate incompetency protection remains unchanged.
- **Herrera v. Collins:** Preserved as a record-specific innocence disposition; no exclusive-clemency rule is attributed to it.
- **Minnesota v. Murphy:** Applied to distinguish voluntary choices from supported threats of a privilege penalty.
- **Baxter v. Palmigiano:** Applied by the eight Associates to ordinary civil adverse inferences, distinct from automatic punishment or compelled criminal testimony.
- **LaChance v. Erickson / McManus:** Preserved for separately established compulsion and waiver; it supplies no general prohibition on lawful civil inferences.

#### Law After Decision

Capital clemency remains discretionary executive mercy, separate from judicial collateral review. Required administrative steps create no right to a favorable outcome. A directly controlling minimum protects remaining life against fundamentally arbitrary clemency procedure, but the pleaded restrictions here do not breach it. The optional interview establishes no compelled self-incrimination, including on the eight-Justice alternative assumption of an ordinary civil adverse inference. No general absence of constitutional protection is declared.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Ohio's procedure does not constrain the ultimate mercy decision, and the appellate analogy supplies no right to the process requested. Dumschat and the substantive distinction applied in Sandin and Johnson resolve these pleaded process claims. These Justices do not join the affirmative retained-life framework or its application; their judgment agreement adds no join to Part II. They preserve independently grounded constitutional prohibitions and fully join the self-incrimination analysis, including its actual-compulsion and automatic-penalty limits.

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** A prisoner remains alive, and an established procedure integral to the State's final decision to take life must afford basic fairness and meaningful participation. His Dumschat position and Evitts's established-process principle support bounded inquiry into the process as a whole, even though clemency need not replicate a trial and no right to mercy exists. He joins the minimum safeguard but would preserve the due-process remand to consider the relevant materials rather than hold these pleadings insufficient. He finds no violation already established and prescribes none of the requested safeguards automatically. He joins both self-incrimination grounds, distinguishing this voluntary invitation from a command backed by a serious threatened sanction.

#### Source Notes

The state procedure, judgment-on-the-pleadings posture, Sixth Circuit rulings and asserted deficiencies appear in [523 U.S. 276–279 and 289–290](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-272.pdf). The same source at 286 identifies the absence of a record showing actual use of answers or silence and the Authority's refusal to promise confidentiality or immunity. The pleaded limitations are evaluated as allegations, not resolved against Woodard through new factual findings. No equal-protection claim is presented.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Sixth Circuit must conform its disposition to rejection of the presented due-process and self-incrimination claims. Its no-entitlement ruling stands. The decision neither retries guilt nor prevents lawful executive clemency review under Ohio's existing duties; it orders no particular recommendation, hearing, release, stay or execution.

**End of entry: Ohio Adult Parole Authority v. Woodard, Merits decision, March 25, 1998.**

### Feltner v. Columbia Pictures Television, Inc., No. 96-1768

Merits decision, March 31, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 21, 1998; decided March 31, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, reviewing 106 F.3d 284. The question is whether §504(c) of the Copyright Act or the Seventh Amendment entitles Feltner to a jury determination of statutory damages.

After license termination, the stations continued broadcasting Columbia's programs. The District Court granted summary judgment on infringement liability, denied Feltner's jury demand and, after a bench trial, found willfulness and 440 infringements. It selected $20,000 for each, totaling $8.8 million. The Ninth Circuit affirmed the relevant damages rulings but separately vacated the costs-and-fees award for explanation. Only Columbia's copyright claims against Feltner remained.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The judge-selected statutory-damages determinations are vacated. On the timely demand, factual issues pertinent to statutory damages, including willfulness and the amount within the lawful range, must be submitted to a jury. The court retains its legal, sufficiency and lawful review functions. Unreviewed infringement liability is not reopened; the separate costs-and-fees remand remains. No lawful work count, willfulness finding, replacement amount or lower ultimate award is predetermined.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Supports reversal and the jury-damages remand | No statutory jury entitlement; constitutional allocation of statutory-damages facts and amount; bounded reversal and remand |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Scalia | No other Justice | Supports the judgment on statutory grounds | Reads §504(c) itself to preserve the jury function and avoids deciding the constitutional question |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Section 504(c) supplies no independent statutory jury entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 504(c)'s authorization of statutory damages does not itself confer a right to a jury determination. That statutory conclusion does not settle the separate Seventh Amendment allocation.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The provision supplies a damages election, ranges and specific conditions for adjustment. Its references to what the court considers just, the court's findings and its discretion contain no independent jury command. Reading those references in the context of the whole subsection yields no statutory entitlement to jury assessment. The contrary institutional reading of court cannot displace that conclusion merely to avoid the constitutional issue. The Court must therefore determine whether the Constitution preserves the jury's role despite the statute's allocation; statutory silence about a jury does not eliminate constitutional protection.

##### The Seventh Amendment protects the initial determination of statutory-damages facts and amount

**Holding and operative rule:** On a timely jury demand in this private copyright statutory-damages action, the Seventh Amendment assigns disputed factual issues pertinent to the award, including willfulness and the amount within the lawful statutory range, to the jury. The court continues to interpret the statute, identify the lawful range, decide legal sufficiency and conduct constitutionally permissible review; the ruling does not assign injunctions, fees, costs, equitable accountings or historically judicial questions to juries.

**Authority:** The same eight Justices join the Court's constitutional holding and damages remand. Scalia supports the judgment on the separate statutory ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Copyright damages have a historical legal analogue, and the monetary remedy seeks compensation or punishment of the kind traditionally assessed by a jury. A range requiring judgment does not turn that task into equitable relief. Markman's historical and functional inquiry distinguishes a legal action's jury protection from the assignment of a particular legal-meaning function; its judicial patent-construction rule does not transfer disputed damages valuation to a judge.

Tull's allocation of the amount of a governmental civil penalty does not control this private copyright-damages function. Congress may identify the lawful range without removing the jury's task of deciding the pertinent facts and selecting the amount within it. Gasperini and Hetzel concern lawful review and the new-trial election after an award; they do not authorize initial judicial substitution for the demanded jury. Their applicable review limits remain intact.

Feltner demanded a jury, but the judge decided willfulness and the statutory amount. Those determinations cannot stand on that procedure. The remand changes the decisionmaker for the protected work, not the statute's ranges, burdens, per-work unit or exceptions. It does not resolve every challenge to the count of works, reopen unreviewed liability or guarantee any particular award.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** The owner may elect statutory damages before final judgment instead of actual damages and profits. The ordinary range is $500–$20,000 for all infringements of any one work for which one infringer is individually liable or two or more infringers are jointly and severally liable; all parts of a compilation or derivative work are one work. Owner-proved willfulness permits, but does not require, an increase to $100,000. Infringer-proved lack of awareness and of reason to believe the acts infringed permits, but does not require, reduction to no less than $200.

Mandatory remission is a separate rule. It requires belief, with reasonable grounds, that the use was fair use under §107, and either (1) reproduction in copies or phonorecords by an employee or agent of a nonprofit educational institution, library or archives acting within employment, or by that institution, library or archives itself; or (2) performance of a published nondramatic literary work, or reproduction of a transmission program embodying its performance, by a public broadcasting entity as defined in §118(g), or by a person as a regular part of that entity's nonprofit activities. Innocence or broadcaster status alone does not satisfy this rule. The Court finds no exception established here.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Markman v. Westview Instruments:** Applied at its history-and-function method and distinction between legal construction and protected factual work; its patent-construction allocation remains intact.
- **Tull v. United States:** Distinguished at the governmental civil-penalty amount function; it is not overruled or extended to private copyright damages.
- **Gasperini v. Center for Humanities:** Preserved for lawful trial-court review and bounded appellate review; its diversity-law rule supplies no copyright valuation formula.
- **Hetzel v. Prince William County:** Preserved for the choice between lawful excessiveness remittitur and an appropriate new jury trial, with separability required for damages-only retrial; it does not authorize the initial bench assessment here.

#### Law After Decision

A timely jury demand protects the statutory-damages facts and amount in a private copyright damages action. The statute's substantive limits remain in force, and ordinary legal and constitutionally permissible review functions remain judicial. The prior description of this monetary relief as equitable cannot sustain the denial of the demanded jury. No constitutional ruling about every civil penalty, fee, cost or equitable remedy follows.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Scalia, concurring in the judgment.** The word court can refer to the lawful tribunal, including its jury. Reading §504(c) to preserve jury determination of damages facts and amount fits that institutional use and avoids an unnecessary constitutional decision. His Tull position distinguishes Congress's power to fix an amount from authority to remove an unfixed legal award from the jury. He therefore agrees with reversal and the jury-damages remand, but does not join the Court's contrary statutory premise or reach its constitutional holding. This difference does not alter the statutory ranges, exceptions or the remand's limits.

#### Source Notes

The applicable damages amounts and separate qualifications appear in the [1994 text of 17 U.S.C. §504](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title17/pdf/USCODE-1994-title17-chap5-sec504.pdf). The party, liability and bench-damages history and separate fees disposition are reported at [523 U.S. 342–345](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-340.pdf). The 440-infringement and willfulness determinations are those of the lower court; they are not findings newly made here.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for a jury determination of the protected statutory-damages issues on the timely demand, subject to lawful rulings on statutory meaning, sufficiency and review. The existing damages award is not reinstated, a replacement amount is not selected, and the separate costs-and-fees remand remains for its own consideration.

**End of entry: Feltner v. Columbia Pictures Television, Inc., Merits decision, March 31, 1998.**

### Texas v. United States, No. 97-29

Ripeness decision, March 31, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 14, 1998; decided March 31, 1998. On appeal from the three-judge United States District Court for the District of Columbia. Texas seeks a declaration that §5 of the Voting Rights Act does not apply to school-district interventions authorized by Texas Education Code §§39.131(a)(7) and (8). It argues both that these interventions do not change voting and that they accord with federal education-grant conditions. The District Court dismissed the action as unripe without deciding either merits theory.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The requested declaration remains unavailable in this unripe action. Neither the voting-coverage theory nor the federal-funding theory is adjudicated. No injunction, exemption or merits remand is ordered; review remains available when a sufficiently definite intervention produces a concrete controversy.

#### Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate writing is filed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### A categorical preclearance exemption is unripe before the challenged intervention is sufficiently defined

**Holding and operative rule:** A request to exempt possible future school-district interventions from §5 is unripe when no sufficiently definite intervention, affected district or allocation of powers establishes a fit legal controversy and no substantial present hardship justifies deciding before those details exist. Texas's challenge therefore must be dismissed without deciding whether any particular intervention changes voting or whether a supported federal-funding conflict affects that question.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Abbott Laboratories requires attention to fitness for decision and the hardship of withholding review. The Commissioner has not selected a currently foreseen or likely district for the challenged master or management-team appointments. Under the recited state policy, lesser sanctions would first be tried; even then the Commissioner selects intervention only to the extent necessary and defines the appointees' actual powers. The legal significance of that implementation cannot be resolved through a universal exemption.

Presley requires a direct relation to voting and leaves extreme displacement of elective authority unresolved. Texas law independently prohibits these appointees from acting concerning a district election, changing the board's size or selection method, setting a tax rate, or adopting a budget with a spending level different from the level set by the board. Those limits do not themselves establish every future appointment's effect. The Attorney General's warning that some implementations could require preclearance is not a determination that an identified voting change occurred.

New York permitted review because the future obligation required substantial present preparations; Pic-A-State involved an existing business stopped by a real criminal constraint. Texas shows no comparable present burden. Its earlier Dallas County request and ninety-day approval period do not establish a current appointment, inevitable delay or present voting change. The Court does not require a party to violate the law before challenging a concrete obligation. It requires the controversy here to become sufficiently definite. Neither federal-grant compatibility nor voting coverage is reached, and Raines's rule for individual legislators is not a prohibition on suits by States.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner:** Applied at the fitness-and-hardship inquiry; uncertainty alone does not require an advisory declaration.
- **New York v. United States:** Distinguished because its future command already required substantial present siting action; timely pre-enforcement review remains available in that setting.
- **Pic-A-State Pennsylvania v. Reno:** Distinguished because an actual criminal constraint had already stopped the plaintiff's existing business; no prosecution prerequisite is added.
- **Presley v. Etowah County Commission / Mack v. Russell County Commission:** Preserved at its direct voting-relation rule and reservation concerning effective replacement of elected authority; no future intervention is classified here.
- **Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment:** Applied to require the Article III inquiry before contested statutory merits.
- **Raines v. Byrd:** Its concrete-injury discipline is respected without extending its individual-legislator rule into a categorical bar on governmental plaintiffs.

#### Law After Decision

The dismissal creates no substantive exemption for school-district interventions and no blanket prohibition on pre-enforcement review. Existing §5 law continues to govern an actual covered change. A sufficiently definite later intervention and real dispute may support review through the proper statutory channel; its coverage and any supported federal-funding question remain open.

#### Source Notes

The statutory powers and restrictions, administrative correspondence, two grounds for the requested declaration, implementation contingencies and prior Dallas County example appear in [523 U.S. 297–301](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-296.pdf). Trying lesser sanctions first is a recited state policy and complaint premise, not a new command attributed to §§39.131(a)(7) or (8). The separate election, board-composition, tax-rate and budget prohibitions arise from §39.131(e).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The District Court's ripeness dismissal stands. Texas receives no advisory coverage ruling or authorization to implement an unidentified change. Any later controversy must satisfy the ordinary jurisdictional and statutory requirements on its own actual facts.

### United States v. Scheffer, No. 96-1133

Merits decision, March 31, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 3, 1997; decided March 31, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces under 28 U.S.C. §1259, reviewing 44 M.J. 442. The question is whether Military Rule of Evidence 707's categorical exclusion of polygraph evidence violates the accused's constitutional right to present a defense.

An Air Force investigator administered the examination before the urinalysis result was known. The examiner considered Scheffer's answers to show no deception. At his court-martial, Scheffer denied knowing drug ingestion and sought the examiner's testimony to support his credibility after impeachment. The judge excluded it under Rule 707 without an individualized reliability hearing. The military appellate court removed that categorical obstacle on constitutional grounds; it did not hold this proffer necessarily admissible.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens.

The constitutional ruling invalidating the categorical exclusion is set aside. The decision neither automatically reinstates all convictions and the sentence nor resolves actual remaining claims. It orders no acquittal, compulsory admission, new trial or individualized reliability hearing.

#### Opinion Topology

Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These eight Justices join the reliability-based constitutional ground, its application and limits, and the reversal and bounded remand.

Scalia concurs, joined by Souter and Thomas, adding the category-specific credibility-role and collateral-litigation grounds. Kennedy concurs, joined by O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, distinguishing constitutional permission from evidentiary policy and declining those two additional grounds. Stevens dissents; he would affirm removal of the categorical obstacle and permit lawful consideration of the evidentiary foundation, without commanding admission.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Supported reliability concerns justify this categorical polygraph rule against the presented constitutional challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** The constitutional right to present a defense permits evidentiary restrictions justified by legitimate trial interests and proportionate to their purposes. Serious, supported concerns about polygraph reliability justify Rule 707's categorical exclusion in this setting, where the accused can present his own factual account and underlying evidence; the Constitution does not require individualized admissibility proceedings for this credibility proffer.

**Authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. The credibility-role and collateral-litigation rationales are separate, three-Justice grounds and do not become holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Chambers and Rock protect a meaningful defense while allowing sufficiently justified evidentiary limits. Egelhoff applies that protection to rebuttal of the mental-state element actually retained by law and expressly preserves reliability restrictions. Here the rulemakers could respond to substantial disagreement about the reliability of polygraph conclusions with a uniform rule. An examiner's scientific certification that the accused is truthful differs from the accused's own account of the events. Scheffer testified to lack of knowing ingestion; Rule 707 did not remove that factual defense or deem the disputed element established.

The Government's administration of this examination and the importance of credibility do not establish that the Constitution requires an individualized foundation hearing. Daubert and Joiner govern the general Federal Rules' scientific-evidence inquiry; they do not constitutionalize that particular screening arrangement for every military evidence rule. The categorical form alone therefore does not make this restriction arbitrary or disproportionate.

The Court does not find that every polygraph is unreliable, determine an accuracy percentage, or resolve this test's truthfulness. Nor does permission to testify alone necessarily establish a complete defense in every case. The holding concerns this justified restriction and this proffer, leaving ordinary evidence rules and actual remaining claims intact. A separate Article 36(a) statutory-validity question is not decided.

**Rule's scope and exception:** Rule 707(a) excludes polygraph results, examiner opinions, and references to offering, failing to take or taking an examination. Rule 707(b) preserves statements made during an examination when they are otherwise admissible; it does not make every examination statement admissible.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Chambers v. Mississippi:** Preserved for meaningful presentation of a defense subject to justified evidentiary limits.
- **Rock v. Arkansas:** Distinguished at the sufficient reliability justification and nature of this credibility proffer; no absolute scientific-evidence entitlement is inferred.
- **Montana v. Egelhoff:** Applied at its protection for rebuttal of retained elements and express reservation for justified reliability rules; the substantive element is not redefined here.
- **Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals:** Preserved as construction of the original general Federal Rules, with flexible reliability-and-fit screening; its architecture is not imposed as a constitutional requirement.
- **General Electric Co. v. Joiner:** Preserved for inferential fit and abuse-of-discretion review under those Rules; it does not invalidate Rule 707's separate categorical scheme.

#### Law After Decision

Rule 707 survives this constitutional challenge. A meaningful defense remains protected, but the Constitution permits this supported reliability restriction without a mandatory case-specific polygraph hearing. No constitutional jury-monopoly rule, general bar on credibility-related expertise or general authority to exclude inconvenient defense evidence follows from the three-Justice concurrence. Statutory validity under Article 36(a) remains undecided.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Scalia, joined by Souter and Thomas, concurring.** In addition to reliability, two features independently justify this category-specific rule: preserving the court members' responsibility to judge credibility against a scientific certification of the accused's truthfulness, and avoiding recurring collateral litigation over examiner qualifications, question design, countermeasures and interpretation. These grounds permit this polygraph rule; they do not reject every expert opinion bearing on credibility or every defense proffer requiring time. All three fully join the Court's reliability ground. Their additional grounds have three votes and are not controlling.

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, concurring.** Reasonable disagreement about polygraph reliability permits the present rule, but constitutional permission does not establish that a categorical ban is the best evidentiary policy. These Justices decline to make protection of the factfinder from ultimate-issue assistance or collateral-litigation concerns independently sufficient grounds here. Their qualification preserves the possible significance of a stronger future proffer without finding this one admissible, requiring a new hearing or altering the present judgment.

**Stevens, dissenting.** A total prohibition prevents an accused from showing that an important defense proffer has a sufficient foundation. The Government administered this examination, and its results bear on the credibility of the denial of knowing ingestion. Egelhoff's meaningful opportunity to rebut the actual element and Rock's treatment of unsupported categorical exclusion favor lawful foundation review. Allowing Scheffer to speak does not by itself answer the exclusion of corroborative support. Stevens would sustain removal of the per se obstacle without deciding ultimate admission, acquittal or entitlement to a new trial before that inquiry. He preserves a concern under Article 36(a), which directs general federal criminal evidence principles so far as the President considers practicable and bars inconsistency with the military code, but does not rest his disposition on that unbriefed statutory question.

#### Source Notes

The investigation, examination, testimony, rule-based exclusion and lower review appear in [523 U.S. 305–308](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-303.pdf); the same report's note 11 explains that the lower ruling removed the categorical obstacle rather than guaranteeing admission. The complete rule is in the [1995 Manual for Courts-Martial, M.R.E. 707](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/llmlp/manual-1995/manual-1995.pdf). The examiner's reported conclusion and the contrary urinalysis remain evidence in the record, not Supreme Court findings about truth or scientific accuracy.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the military appellate system for proceedings consistent with the validity of Rule 707 against the presented constitutional challenge. Actual remaining claims retain their own posture. The Court supplies no unsupported final disposition of every charge or sentence component.

**End of entry: United States v. Scheffer, Merits decision, March 31, 1998.**

### United States v. United States Shoe Corp., No. 97-372

Merits decision, March 31, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 4, 1998; decided March 31, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Federal Circuit, reviewing 114 F.3d 1564. The questions are whether the Court of International Trade has jurisdiction over this Harbor Maintenance Tax refund action and whether the levy on exports is a tax prohibited by the Export Clause or a permissible service charge.

U.S. Shoe paid the levy on exports during April–June 1994, submitted a protest to Customs and received a form response defending the statutory assessment. Its complaint invoked §1581(a) or alternatively §1581(i). The Court of International Trade sustained jurisdiction under §1581(i) and entered an export-refund judgment; the Federal Circuit affirmed. The Government does not contest that the goods were in export transit.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The existing export-refund judgment stands for the claim and payments before the Court. No new refund computation, interest award, limitations ruling or disposition of another taxpayer's claim is entered. Nonexport applications and independently lawful service charges are unaffected.

#### Opinion Topology

Ginsburg delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. All nine join both the jurisdictional and Export Clause holdings. No separate writing is filed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The Court of International Trade has exclusive residual jurisdiction over this levy challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1581(i)(4), read with §1581(i)(1) and 26 U.S.C. §4462(f)(2), gives the Court of International Trade exclusive jurisdiction over this challenge to administration and enforcement of the Harbor Maintenance Tax. The challenge attacks the legislatively fixed exaction rather than a protestable Customs decision under §1514, so §1581(a)'s protest-denial channel is not the necessary route.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress directs that the levy be treated as a customs duty for jurisdictional purposes. The statute provides revenue from imports as well as imposing other applications of the same levy; §1581(i)(4) covers its administration and enforcement. That direction supplies the appropriate exclusive forum before the constitutional question is reached.

Section 1581(a) concerns denial of a protest against a Customs decision of the kind governed by §1514. Here Customs passively collected a charge fixed by Congress; its form response did not supply a discretionary valuation, classification or other decision that determines this constitutional challenge. A document called a protest does not change the action's nature. Residual jurisdiction therefore does not excuse a missed protest deadline or circumvent an otherwise governing specific channel.

Section 1581(i)'s exclusion for specified antidumping or countervailing determinations reviewable by the Court of International Trade or the designated binational panels, and §1581(j)'s exclusion of actions under Tariff Act §305, do not encompass this levy claim. Section 4462(f)(1)'s regulation-qualified application of customs administration and its penalty rule, and (f)(3)'s exclusion of internal-revenue administration, remain distinct from (f)(2)'s jurisdictional direction. Calling the levy a customs duty for jurisdiction does not decide its constitutional character.

##### II. The value-based charge on export loading is an export tax, not a compensatory service fee

**Holding and operative rule:** The Export Clause prohibits this Harbor Maintenance Tax as applied to exports because its ad valorem assessment does not fairly measure services or facilities used by the exporter. A genuine compensatory charge may use reasonable administrative approximations, but dedicated revenue and a general connection to useful harbor work do not convert a cargo-value export tax into a permissible fee.

**Authority:** The same nine Justices join the Court's merits holding and affirmance of the export-refund judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** IBM establishes that nondiscrimination cannot save a federal tax on exports. The Government accordingly defends this levy as a user fee. Pace permits compensation for actual governmental services and safeguards, allowing workable approximation rather than exact individualized billing. The distinction turns on the charge's relationship to the service, not its name.

The levy imposes 0.125 percent of cargo value at export loading. Its receipts enter a dedicated harbor-maintenance fund, but cargo value does not fairly measure the exporter's use of the relevant facilities or services. The fund's useful purpose supplies no missing relationship between the tax base and that use. The Court need not invent a benefit ratio or find each particular payment excessive to identify this structural defect.

Northwest Airlines concerns a different statutory airport-fee regime and does not replace the Export Clause with a general reasonableness or nondiscrimination test. IBM's service reservations likewise do not exempt every charge related to shipping. This decision concerns the uncontested goods in export transit and the export payments claimed. It reaches neither import or domestic applications, nor a differently measured service fee, nor general taxes on legally independent events. Reasonable inspection, storage, wharfage and facility-use charges are not prohibited by this holding.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. International Business Machines Corp.:** Applied to reject nondiscrimination as a defense to an actual federal export tax; its reservation for genuinely distinct service transactions remains intact.
- **Pace v. Burgess:** Applied to preserve genuine compensation for governmental services and safeguards through reasonable approximation; a service label or dedicated fund alone is insufficient.
- **Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. County of Kent:** Distinguished as construction of the Anti-Head Tax Act; its facility-use analysis supplies an analogy, not a substitute constitutional test.
- **Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment:** Its jurisdiction-before-merits discipline is respected by determining the statutory channel before the Export Clause claim.

#### Law After Decision

The Court of International Trade is the exclusive forum for this administration-and-enforcement refund claim under §1581(i). The existing value-based Harbor Maintenance Tax cannot be applied to exports on the theory that dedicated harbor spending makes it a user fee. The decision preserves genuine service charges, nonexport applications and legally independent taxable transactions. No other case is automatically transferred or adjudicated.

#### Source Notes

The payment period, complaint's alternative jurisdictional allegations, Customs response, lower jurisdictional rulings, levy mechanics and uncontested export-transit posture appear in [523 U.S. 363–367](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-360.pdf). The operative jurisdictional provisions are 28 U.S.C. §1581(a), (i) and (j), 19 U.S.C. §1514, and 26 U.S.C. §4462(f). The source establishes the value basis and dedicated fund, not a quantitative service/cargo correlation or a newly adjudicated refund limitation.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The export-refund judgment remains in force within the claim and payments already adjudicated. Further implementation follows that judgment; the Court directs no new amount, interest calculation or disposition of unpresented claims.

**End of entry: United States v. United States Shoe Corp., Merits decision, March 31, 1998.**

### In re Dorsainvil, Third Circuit No. 96-8074

Jurisdictional disposition, April 2, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

The decision below is reported at 119 F.3d 245. The Court considers attempted ordinary review of the Third Circuit's July 23, 1997 refusal, amended August 15, to authorize a second §2255 motion. The question concerns a possible savings-clause route for an intervening statutory-innocence claim, but the only order presented is the authorization refusal, not a judgment on a §2241 petition. No Supreme Court argument date is established.

#### Judgment

**Dismissal for want of jurisdiction, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

No remand, transfer, original writ, hearing, retrial, release or acquittal is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

The Court issues a per curiam opinion joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs separately; no other Justice joins his concurrence.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The authorization refusal is outside ordinary Supreme Court review

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 2255's certification cross-reference incorporates §2244(b)(3)(E)'s prohibition on certiorari review of a court of appeals' successive-motion authorization decision. Because the only order presented is the Third Circuit's refusal to authorize a second §2255 motion, the attempted ordinary review is dismissed for want of jurisdiction; the Court neither affirms that refusal nor decides the underlying statutory-innocence claim.

**Authority.** The per curiam opinion, joined in full by all nine participating Justices, controls this threshold question.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the order presented determines the available channel. Triestman applies the incorporated review prohibition to a federal prisoner's authorization refusal, precisely the order presented here. Felker preserves a genuinely distinct original-habeas channel; it does not permit ordinary review under another name. No independent original application or judgment deciding a §2241 petition is presented. Section 1651 assists jurisdiction otherwise held and supplies none. The intervening construction of firearm use and the Government's lower-court retroactivity concession cannot confer the missing jurisdiction. Nor does the Government's decision not to assert abuse below supply a waiver in a different, unfiled proceeding. The Third Circuit's statement that a savings-clause route may remain available does not turn its authorization order into a judgment in that route. The seriousness of the claim therefore permits no merits adjudication here. A genuinely available independent proceeding retains its own forum, eligibility, defenses and remedial requirements. An original Supreme Court application would separately require Rule 20's exceptional circumstances, lack of adequate relief elsewhere, explanation for not seeking district relief, and applicable exhaustion showing. Nothing here promises a hearing or a favorable result.

Savings-clause eligibility, effective earlier opportunity, retroactivity, active use, carrying, the charge, procedural defenses and ultimate relief remain undecided. The separate drug convictions are not adjudicated.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Triestman v. United States, decided March 12, 1998, is applied: the federal-prisoner certification cross-reference bars ordinary review of the authorization refusal.
- Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996), is applied to preserve the distinction between prohibited authorization review and separately invoked original habeas; retained jurisdiction establishes neither a filing nor entitlement to relief.
- Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States, 516 U.S. 137 (1995), remains the active-employment rule for firearm use and preserves a legally available carrying ground. It supplies no jurisdiction to adjudicate either ground here.
- Lonchar v. Thomas, 517 U.S. 314 (1996), protects process legally required in an available proceeding; it does not create this barred forum. Schlup v. Delo, decided January 23, 1995, and Herrera v. Collins, 506 U.S. 390 (1993), retain their distinct innocence-gateway and record-specific innocence-relief limits without deciding this statutory claim.

#### Law After Decision

The ordinary-review prohibition continues to govern federal successive-motion authorization decisions. This decision supplies no new savings-clause test, firearm-merits holding or right to relief. An actually available independent proceeding retains its own eligibility requirements, defenses and remedies. The concurrence does not establish governing law.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's dismissal and states his view of the independent process left open. Congress's restrictions on repetitive litigation and its retained savings clause must both have practical effect. An intervening interpretation that applies to the conviction and supports a substantial claim of noncriminal conduct may permit §2241 consideration when no effective earlier §2255 opportunity existed. Failure to satisfy the successive-motion criteria alone is insufficient. The prisoner must establish eligibility and entitlement to relief separately, and the Government retains its lawful defenses. Neither Article III nor the gravity of detention creates a new forum. Complete foreclosure of meaningful review would raise a distinct Suspension Clause question that this dismissal does not decide.

In his view, the first §2255 proceedings' completion before Bailey and the described firearm account present a substantial question for an actually available savings-clause process. Intent, protective storage, availability and possession do not replace active use. The reported reaching movements establish neither firing nor attempted firing; an officer's perception of danger is not the offense element. Failure of a use theory does not establish innocence of carrying or another legally available ground. The Government must establish that ground's elements and legal availability, and the court must examine the evidence and charge rather than treat the inclusion of carrying language as a complete verdict or harmlessness analysis.

An appropriate court may decide on an adequate existing record, receive appropriate submissions or permit necessary factual development under governing law. An allegation alone does not establish the required predicates, but access does not require already prevailing on ultimate relief. No new burden, exhaustion requirement, unrestricted retries, universal oral or evidentiary hearing, automatic release or favorable outcome follows. These are his noncontrolling views of what remains open, not an eligibility finding or a remand ordered by this Court.

#### Source Notes

The lower proceedings, firearm account and separate carrying view are reported at [119 F.3d 245](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/119/119.F3d.245.96-8074.html). The lower court left a possible §2241 route open without deciding a petition under that provision. The materials supporting this disposition establish no independent original Supreme Court application or later §2241 judgment. The Government’s concession and nonassertion below do not establish waiver or relief in another proceeding.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

This attempted ordinary review ends. The Third Circuit's authorization refusal is neither affirmed nor vacated. No further proceeding is directed. Dorsainvil retains only whatever independent §2241 process the law actually provides, where eligibility, entitlement to relief and lawful Government defenses require their own determination.

**End of entry: In re Dorsainvil, Jurisdictional disposition, April 2, 1998.**

### United States v. Hinds, Fourth Circuit No. 97-4030; district case CR-96-148

Plenary merits decision, April 6, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the Fourth Circuit's unpublished October 15, 1997 judgment affirming the §844(i) conviction. The questions are whether the Pleasant Ridge United Church of Christ building was used in interstate commerce or an activity affecting commerce under that statute and, if the statute reaches it, whether that application lies within Congress's Commerce Clause authority. Hinds also presented an Establishment Clause objection below. The public record establishes no separate Supreme Court docket or argument date.

Hinds and an accomplice burned the church in Greensboro, North Carolina. Hinds waived a jury and stipulated the elements other than the commerce connection. After hearing evidence on that issue, the District Court denied his motion to dismiss and found him guilty. The Fourth Circuit recited the property-use element, held the evidence sufficient, and rejected the religious objection. The church sent annual funds through its national denomination, employed one full-time and two part-time workers, provided the full-time employee insurance procured from out-of-state companies, and purchased utilities, insurance, religious materials and other goods and services.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela.

The Fourth Circuit's affirmance is vacated. That court must apply the statutory construction to the existing trial record and determine the lawful disposition of the §844(i) conviction. No additional evidence or new trial is authorized. An actual finding of evidentiary insufficiency requires reversal for entry of acquittal; any supported adequate use basis and necessary, properly presented constitutional question must be addressed under current law. The Court does not itself direct acquittal, release, a substitute conviction or resentencing. Stone-Zsela would instead reverse and direct vacatur of the conviction and immediate entry of acquittal on this count.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Vacates the Fourth Circuit's affirmance and remands | Part I's complete property-function construction and application; Part II's existing-record mandate and constitutional nonreach |
| Same opinion, partial joins | Stevens | Stone-Zsela | Agrees that the affirmance cannot stand; opposes the Court's operative remand | Part I only as to independent work for the property-use element, insufficiency of owner relationships by themselves, and absence of a religious exemption; Part II only as to the independent Burks rule that evidentiary insufficiency requires acquittal and bars retrial to repair proof |
| Concurrence in part and dissent from the judgment and remand | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Would reverse and direct vacatur of the §844(i) conviction and entry of acquittal on that count | Active-commercial-use construction; present insufficiency and immediate acquittal; separate Agostini-based reason for rejecting the Establishment objection as a construction aid or alternative ground |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The damaged property's use must connect it to commerce

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 844(i) requires a connection between the function performed by the damaged property and interstate or foreign commerce, or an activity affecting that commerce. The owner's national affiliation, purchases, an employee's out-of-state insurance and recurring payments do not establish the element merely by existing; their relation to the property's use must be established, without making religious or nonprofit ownership an exclusion or profit-making, rental, sales or an exclusively business purpose a universal prerequisite.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies eight direct joins to the complete construction and its application. Stone-Zsela joins only the propositions that the property-use element has independent work, that the owner's identified economic relationships by themselves do not establish it, and that religious identity creates no exemption. He does not join the complete formulation's rejection of an exclusively commercial-use requirement; his active-commercial-use construction is stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute asks what the damaged property was used to do and how that function connects with commerce. Russell sustains coverage of a rented apartment building because renting the premises placed the property itself in the rental market. Its broad account of statutory coverage does not erase the enacted use limitation, and its rental example does not establish an exclusive class of covered property. The words extending coverage to an activity affecting commerce retain both their breadth and their connection to the property's use.

The Fourth Circuit correctly recited the property-use element. Its application nevertheless treated the congregation's economic relationships as sufficient without establishing their relation to the function of the damaged premises. Annual transfers through a national denomination are actual movements of money, and thus the Government's strongest asserted connection. But the existence of those transfers does not itself explain the building's qualifying function. The same distinction applies to the employees, employee insurance, utilities, religious materials and other purchases. The Court neither doubts those reported relationships nor assumes every purchase came from outside the State.

Bailey/Robinson supplies the statutory distinction between required conduct and another related condition: satisfying one does not dispense with the other. Its firearm-use rule is not transplanted into this statute. Section 844(i)'s own use language does the work here. The Court adds no interstate-travel requirement, no profit-making condition and no requirement that this individual fire substantially affect the national economy. The panel's stated rationale cannot sustain its affirmance; the complete trial proof must be considered under the correct construction. No constitutional ruling or categorical exclusion of religious premises follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Russell remains controlling for actual rental use and is applied without turning that example into exclusive coverage. Bailey/Robinson is applied at the distinction between separately required statutory predicates, not as an independent definition of property use.

##### II. The appellate judgment is vacated for review of the existing trial record

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Circuit's affirmance is vacated, and that court must apply the property-use construction to the complete existing trial record and determine the lawful disposition of the §844(i) conviction; this mandate authorizes neither additional evidence nor a new trial. If the existing evidence fails to establish the required use connection, the court must reverse for entry of acquittal, because evidentiary insufficiency cannot be repaired through another trial; if existing proof supports a legally adequate use finding, the court must identify that basis and address any necessary, properly presented constitutional question under current law.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion has the same eight Associate joins. Stone-Zsela joins the independent Burks proposition that actual evidentiary insufficiency requires acquittal and bars a second opportunity to supply missing proof, but rejects this judgment and remand: he finds the proof insufficient now and would direct acquittal immediately. No condition of the Court's further-review mandate is attributed to his position.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court corrects the legal application supporting the appellate affirmance. It does not decide that the complete trial record contains no other evidence relevant to the damaged building's function. Those are different determinations. The lower court must examine existing proof under the rule announced here; it may not treat this remand as permission to build a new prosecution case.

Burks requires acquittal after a determination that the prosecution's evidence is legally insufficient. Jacobson applies that consequence and forbids another opportunity to supply missing proof. Those authorities control if the existing record establishes only institutional relationships without the necessary property-use connection. Conversely, a legally adequate use finding cannot be assumed merely because the conviction once stood. Any such basis must actually be supported, and any necessary preserved constitutional question must then be addressed.

Bailey/Robinson illustrates the distinction between correcting an erroneous statutory premise and deciding every remaining lawful basis or remedial predicate. Its separate carrying branch creates no alternative element here. The remand determines neither acquittal nor retrial in advance, and orders no release, substitute §247 conviction, amended penalty or independent sentencing disposition. The Court reaches neither Congress's outer Commerce Clause authority nor the Establishment Clause objection.

**Precedent treatment:** Burks and Jacobson preserve the mandatory acquittal consequence of actual insufficiency. Bailey/Robinson supplies a bounded remedial analogy, without a new carrying theory, new evidence or an automatic retrial rule.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Russell v. United States, 471 U.S. 858 (1985):** Applied at its actual rental-use holding and retained statutory use limitation. Rental remains sufficient in its established setting, without becoming an exclusive class or establishing automatic church coverage.
- **Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States, December 6, 1995:** Applied at the separation of independently enacted conditions and used as a bounded remedial analogy. Its active-firearm-use and carrying rules are not imported into §844(i).
- **Burks v. United States, 437 U.S. 1 (1978), and Jacobson v. United States, April 6, 1992:** Applied to require acquittal after actual evidentiary insufficiency and prohibit another trial to repair missing proof.
- **United States v. Lopez and its current applications, including United States v. Robinson, March 23, 1998:** Preserved without a new constitutional application. Statutory jurisdictional words remain no automatic constitutional safe harbor, and each statute retains its actual elements.
- **Agostini v. Felton and Tanford v. Brand:** Unchanged. The Court does not decide the Establishment objection; Stone-Zsela's separate application of Agostini and Everson establishes no additional Court holding.

#### Law After Decision

Section 844(i)'s property-use element requires the relationship between the function of the damaged premises and commerce or a commerce-affecting activity. The owner's economic relationships alone do not suffice. Religious and nonprofit premises receive neither categorical exclusion nor automatic coverage. Existing-record review must distinguish a legally adequate use basis from insufficient proof; the latter requires acquittal and cannot support another evidentiary opportunity. The Court changes no Commerce Clause or Establishment Clause standard and decides no §247, penalty-amendment or other-charge question. Stone-Zsela's exclusive active-commercial-use formulation, immediate-acquittal application and separate Establishment explanation do not bind lower courts.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting from the judgment and remand.** The enacted use requirement demands active employment of the building itself in commercial activity. The congregation's purchases, utilities, insurance, staff, donations, mail and national affiliation are economic relationships; they do not transform premises devoted to worship into property actively employed in commerce. The national denomination's annual redistribution of funds to missionaries, seminaries, institutions of higher education and other programs is the Government's strongest argument, but it still describes what the congregation paid and gave in support of worship, rather than commercial use of this building. Russell's rented premises illustrate the distinction because the property itself operated in the rental market. The Fourth Circuit stated the use element but answered it through the congregation's economic connections. Stone-Zsela considers the Government's proof insufficient now. Under Burks and Jacobson he would reverse, direct vacatur of the conviction and entry of acquittal, and deny another trial to repair that proof.

Lopez supplies avoidance context, not a constitutional judgment. Its connection-before-magnitude sequence raises doubt about a reading that reaches every building through an owner's insurance, purchases, payroll or gifts and their downstream economic consequences. Avoidance is available because Congress enacted a use limitation to construe; it permits no inserted element. The existence of jurisdictional wording is not itself a constitutional safe harbor. Lopez preserves genuine economic-class analysis under Wickard and Perez and does not require a substantial effect from each individual act. Stone-Zsela leaves Hinds's particular substantial-effect contention and the outer Commerce question undecided. Hale, Rybar and Kirk's protection of a valid machinegun regime does not dispense with this statute's different property-use element. Robinson's direct interference with operating business assets likewise supplies no all-purchases rule. Robertson's commercial enterprise transactions and Allied-Bruce's broader transaction-involving-commerce language do not replace §844(i)'s property-use text.

This is no religious exemption. Stone-Zsela reserves the reach of the statute over religious property itself used for a commercial enterprise, rental activity, substantial commercial leasing or materially mixed commercial use. He decides no particular application within those categories, no authority under another federal statute, including FACE's unchallenged worship branches, and no reach of differently worded statutes such as the Hobbs Act. Ordinary state arson law and independently valid federal charges retain their own elements and limits.

He separately rejects the Establishment objection as a reason to construe the statute differently for religious property or as an alternative ground for reversal. Under Lemon as Agostini modified it, a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine resolves conduct it covers; an established permissible relationship defeats a covered claim; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved claim proceeds through attribution and function. Purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within that inquiry, not independent or fallback prongs. The governmental act here is prosecution of an alleged arsonist, and its function is ordinary property protection. Everson's established relationship of general public-welfare services, including ordinary police and fire protection, resolves the objection, provided the criminal statute validly applies.

The prosecution organizes no worship, prescribes no religious profession or observance, adopts no doctrine, solicits no religious adherence, governs no theology, confers no religiously allocated civil authority and finances no devotion. Examining a congregation's operations to determine an offense element is not regulation of its religious teaching or governance. Tanford involved the government's own organized commencement worship and therefore a different function. Facial neutrality and the beneficiary's identity are contextual evidence, not independently decisive grounds. Stone-Zsela reaches neither the residual route nor an accommodation, funding, legislative-prayer or ceremonial question; free exercise and religious autonomy retain their distinct sources and burdens. His answer creates no rule permitting every governmental benefit to religion, supplies neither statutory coverage nor an enumerated power, and does not ground his reversal. The second granted question remains unreached; insofar as the Establishment objection falls outside the grant, this discussion states only his reason for declining to rely on it. No other Justice joins this separate explanation.

No penalty-amendment question is decided.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Intentional force and threats directed at the provision of reproductive services fall within the established commerce connection

**Holding and operative rule.** Congress may apply §248(a)(1) to intentional force and true threats directed at preventing a person from providing reproductive-health services because that conduct operates directly on access to an actual interstate service market. The connection is the conduct's object and operation, not generalized costs of violence; neither the offender nor each affected clinic must separately cross a state line.

**Authority.** Kennedy's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices, controls the Commerce question and the affirmance on that ground.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that American Life League supplies the governing connection. Its sustained class is direct interference with provision or receipt of reproductive-health services in an interstate market. Lopez requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate effects can justify federal regulation; it does not permit Congress to turn every local crime into a national economic problem. Bird's bottle attack and threat to kill a physician entering the clinic to provide services directly obstruct that service activity. The absence of proof that this physician, clinic staff, patients or supplies crossed a state line does not sever the conduct from the class American Life League sustained. FACE's conduct and purpose elements do the necessary work; the Court inserts no omitted jurisdictional element. Hinds concerns a different statute's property-use requirement and supplies no such element here. The defense's local-violence objection thus fails on the access-directed mechanism, without a new theory based on fear, healthcare expense or lost productivity. This holding does not federalize ordinary assault, trespass, protest or disturbance merely because it occurs near a medical facility, and does not decide materially different mixed-purpose conduct. Alternative §5 authority and reconsideration of protected-choice law are unnecessary.

##### II. The supervision component requires a determination of a continuing controversy before its merits

**Holding and operative rule.** When the actual operation of a challenged supervision condition and any continuing redressable consequence remain unresolved, a live challenge to the conviction does not itself establish jurisdiction to decide that condition's merits. The appellate disposition affirming the condition is vacated, and the matter returns through the Fifth Circuit to determine that threshold on the actual supervision record; the district condition itself is neither vacated nor rewritten by this decision.

**Authority.** Kennedy's Part II-A, joined by all eight other Justices, controls the continuing-controversy requirement and the limited appellate vacatur. Part II-B's precise remand and reservation of the condition's merits is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices including the author. Stone-Zsela does not join Part II-B and would add mandatory replacement if the dispute is live.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that jurisdiction must attach to the relief actually sought. Spencer distinguishes a live custody-related injury from presumed consequences after the particular restraint ends. That requirement informs this distinct sentencing component without making every direct-sentencing appeal equivalent to Spencer's revocation case. The sentencing date and stated terms do not establish commencement, operative delay, tolling, expiration or an actual present burden. Nor does a live conviction challenge establish them. Section 2106 authorizes appropriate further proceedings but creates no merits jurisdiction. A bounded remand allows the lower courts to use judicial materials that establish status, without demanding duplicative production. Only proceedings supported by the actual jurisdictional finding may follow. If the dispute is live, the condition remains subject to §3583(d)'s reasonable relationship to the relevant sentencing purposes, no greater liberty deprivation than reasonably necessary, and pertinent policy-statement requirements. If it is moot, no condition-merits ruling may issue; the proper mootness disposition and any equitable treatment of the lower judgment depend on actual timing and circumstances. The Court presently requires neither retention nor narrowing nor removal of the condition. Past violence supports a serious protective interest but cannot resolve unknown jurisdiction or predetermine the appropriate breadth. No general resentencing or extension of supervision follows.

The separate lower-court familial-classification, overbreadth, vagueness and written-judgment-label disputes are not independently within the questions adjudicated here. Their lower dispositions are not new holdings of this Court.

#### Precedent Treatment

- American Life League v. Reno, decided November 13, 1995, is applied to the service-directed force and threats. Its conduct, purpose and speech-protection boundaries remain intact; this decision adds no new facial speech ruling.
- United States v. Lopez, decided April 26, 1995, is applied to require an identifiable commerce connection before aggregation. Direct interference with the established service market supplies that connection here.
- United States v. Hinds, decided April 6, 1998, is distinguished: its statutory property-use inquiry does not insert an element into FACE or change the Commerce rule.
- Spencer v. Kemna, decided March 3, 1998, supplies the actual-continuing-injury principle. Its revocation holding is not enlarged into a categorical rule about every direct sentencing challenge or a finding that this condition has expired.
- Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc., 512 U.S. 753 (1994), and Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network, decided February 19, 1997, remain relevant fit analogies if condition merits become reachable. Their injunction rules establish neither an automatic criminal-supervision test nor a universal permissible distance here.

#### Law After Decision

The access-directed Commerce rule remains unchanged and governs the force and threats proved here. The Court establishes no new facial FACE rule, §5 holding, general federal violent-crime power or supervision-distance rule. The separate condition cannot receive a merits disposition without an actual continuing controversy. Its proper eventual scope remains open under the governing sentencing law; Stone-Zsela’s proposed if-live replacement does not bind the lower courts.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and Part II-A and dissenting from Part II-B.** He agrees that American Life League controls the access-directed force and threat, that liveness must be determined on the actual record, and that only the appellate disposition of the condition should now be vacated. He would prescribe a further conditional remedy: if the dispute is live, the district court must replace the blanket 1,000-foot exclusion with a restriction supported by findings and reasonably necessary under §3583(d); if moot, that part must be dismissed without a merits ruling.

He regards the violence, threat and prior misconduct as supporting no-contact, no-threat, no-trespass and site-specific protection, but finds no present findings justifying exclusion from every clinic for every purpose. A temporary, strong and fact-supported restriction remains available. His position supplies no right to threaten, intimidate, obstruct, trespass or impermissibly contact providers, patients, staff or clinics. He does not presume that supervision continues or has ended and does not make a present merits ruling on a moot dispute. The Court's remand preserves the jurisdictional inquiry but does not adopt his mandatory if-live replacement instruction.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. A-738 does not establish the necessary prospect of prospective relief for a current consular violation

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming the invoked notice duties were binding and violated, and applying the applicants' favorable formulation requiring a reasonable probability that four Justices would grant review, a significant possibility of reversal, likely irreparable harm and appropriate consideration of competing equities, A-738 does not establish the necessary prospect that the older consular duties require the requested execution restraint as relief for a current violation. A continuing consequence of past nonnotification is not by itself a continuing denial of these applicants' presently available consular rights; the distinct most-favored-consul notification theory does not itself establish the requested remedy.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by all seven other Associate Justices, supplies eight direct joins to this complete rule and application. Stone-Zsela independently agrees that the current domestic duty and remedial showing is insufficient, without adopting the complete explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court considers Paraguay's own rights, not a supposed default of Breard's personal claim. Article 36 protects communication and access; consular notification must occur without delay if the national requests it, while advice to the national and forwarding covered communications must also occur without delay. It protects visits, correspondence and arranging representation, including after judgment, but requires consular officers to abstain from action on the national's behalf if the national expressly opposes that action. Its receiving-State-law clause is qualified by the requirement that those laws give full effect to the rights' purposes. Neither half may be omitted.

The most-favored-consul provision of the 1859 Treaty supports a distinct notification theory without the same request condition. Even accepting that theory for this inquiry, consular access has been available since 1996. The current presentation does not sufficiently connect the old omission to a present duty requiring this execution restraint.

Young supplies prospective officer relief for an ongoing federal violation. Seminole and Coeur d'Alene preserve that route within actual duties and remedies; they remove categorical immunity shortcuts without supplying this treaty remedy. Papasan and Milliken permit relief for a legally continuing violation produced by past events, but not every continuing harm establishes a current violation of each claimant's right. The full-effect argument therefore deserves consideration but does not establish the required interim prospect. Irreversibility and the international interests are material, not substitutes for that showing. The Court decides neither the ultimate treaty remedy nor a categorical immunity rule, and does not import A-771's newly pleaded ICJ duty into this older claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Young, Seminole and Coeur d'Alene retain the connected-officer, ongoing-duty and prospective-remedy boundaries; Papasan and Milliken retain the distinction between a continuing violation and continuing effects. Coleman supplies the separate review/reversal/irreparability framework used on the applicants' favorable assumption; no universal full-Court affirmative-injunction standard is selected.

##### II. A-738's older civil claim requires no further protected jurisdictional interval

**Holding and operative rule:** A-738 also supplies no basis for an administrative execution injunction merely to obtain additional coordinated consideration of the older treaty claim. Even assuming an otherwise lawful §2283 route in the applicants' favor, that claim's interim duty-and-remedy question can be assessed now; no necessary additional jurisdictional or injunctive-power inquiry is established by the prospect that execution-specific relief will become unavailable.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela denies interim relief on his own stated ground and does not adopt this additional analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** Shipp and United Mine Workers permit necessary preservation while a court determines a substantial question about its authority. They do not require delay whenever further consideration is desired, and restraint must cease if the relevant want of power is determined. Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction keeps §1651 auxiliary and subordinate to specifically governing statutes.

Hill and Atlantic Coast Line treat enforcement of a state judgment, including ministerial execution, as a state proceeding for §2283. Directing an order to officers, giving it a short duration or calling it administrative supplies no exception. The exceptions are express congressional authorization, necessity in aid of the issuing court's jurisdiction, and protection or effectuation of its judgments. The ICJ measure is not this Court's judgment, and foreign sovereignty does not confer the United States-plaintiff exception.

Hale and County of Imperial preserve a genuinely independent nonparty route, but absence from a caption does not decide party or privity status. Mitchum classifies §1983 as expressly authorized; a pleaded theory still requires the particular right, defendant and interim showing. It does not attach the Consul General's exception automatically to Paraguay's separate claims. Those predicates remain unresolved, not decided against applicants.

Even favorable answers would not supply the missing interim showing or need for another interval here. The jurisdiction at issue is review of a federal civil judgment. Atlantic Coast Line's distinct discussion of potential Supreme Court review of state decisions does not turn this civil petition into review of Virginia's criminal judgment. The older claim is presently assessable; the new original claim has its own channel. No general exception from §2283, factual default, political-question bar or final merits adjudication on assumed Article III jurisdiction follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Shipp and United Mine Workers retain necessary, substantial-question preservation; Pennsylvania Bureau preserves statutory limits; Hill and Atlantic Coast Line govern the restraint's character and exceptions; Hale, County of Imperial and Mitchum retain their distinct coverage and authorization routes without a present finding satisfying or defeating them.

##### III. A-767 does not warrant a stay through the original-habeas channel

**Holding and operative rule:** After independent consideration of the retained original-habeas route, A-767 presents no sufficiently substantial prospect of extraordinary domestic habeas relief and identifies no unfinished process actually required by governing law that demands interim protection. The stay is denied without denying the original petition, treating the new ICJ order as a defaulted or previously adjudicated claim, or finding an unverified Rule 20 condition unsatisfied.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas: five direct joins to the complete explanation. Stone-Zsela separately adopts the insufficient-exceptional-relief and no-identified-required-process grounds, supplying a sixth denial vote without joining every additional proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Felker preserves original habeas under §2241 independently of review of successive-application authorization. Circuit permission is not a prerequisite here. Rule 20 nevertheless requires exceptional circumstances, no adequate relief elsewhere, reasons for not applying to the district court, and exhaustion or the §2254(b) alternatives of unavailable state corrective process or circumstances rendering that process ineffective. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform discretion; their direct mandatory force in this channel remains reserved. The Court assumes unresolved procedural matters favorably for this interim inquiry rather than inventing nonexhaustion or waiver.

The April 9 measure receives separate consideration. Even assuming international bindingness, its preservation of an interstate dispute does not on this showing establish a sufficiently substantial personal domestic custody claim. Compulsory international jurisdiction, Charter Article 94's compliance undertaking, Statute Article 41's provisional authority and a domestic habeas remedy are distinct questions. Comity does not itself create that custody right or enlarge §2251. The ICJ reserved the treaty remedy and prejudice; notice failure and the disputed plea counterfactual establish neither causal prejudice nor an innocence gateway or hearing entitlement. The earlier treaty claim retains its own defenses, which are not mechanically attached to the new order.

Section 2251 permits a stay of state proceedings involving the detained person and a matter in pending habeas before final judgment, after a judgment of discharge, or pending appeal; filing alone suspends nothing. Lonchar protects an identified legally required opportunity when lawful expedition cannot finish before execution. No such unfinished obligation is identified here, and Rule 20 makes an original petition ex parte unless a response is required. #### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The stay requires both meaningful review prospects and irreparable harm

**Holding and operative rule.** A stay of execution pending certiorari requires a reasonable probability that four Justices would accept review, a significant possibility of reversal and likely irreparable harm without protection. Execution's irreversibility supplies the last requirement, but does not dispense with the two review requirements or independently overcome an applicable barrier to relief.

**Authority.** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's signed Circuit Justice explanation, Part I, joined by all eight Associates, states the Court's governing interim standard. These joins reveal no final vote on the pending petition.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court applies Coleman v. Thompson, decided May 20, 1992, which uses Barefoot's distinct requirements for protection pending review. The issue is whether the actual claims and legally available remedies justify a stay, not whether execution is serious or the asserted violation deserves attention. Each claim requires its own assessment, including any independent obstacle to a legally consequential reversal. The Court's decision on this application does not decide the petition or finally adjudicate the claims. Lonchar protects process actually required by law when lawful expedition cannot complete it before execution; first-petition status and difficulty alone establish no unfinished legal duty or automatic pause.

##### II. The present treaty/default showing does not justify a stay

**Holding and operative rule.** On this submission, the asserted Vienna Convention notification violation does not establish a sufficient prospect of obtaining relief through the surviving default and remedial barriers to justify a stay. The treaty source and asserted novelty of a claim do not alone establish cause, prejudice or an applicable exception; this interim conclusion neither makes every consular claim unenforceable nor decides ultimate prejudice.

**Authority.** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supplies five direct joins to this sufficient treaty-ground assessment. Souter supports denial on the distinct causal/remedial assessment in his concurrence and does not join Part II.

**Controlling explanation.** Article 36 requires advice without delay of the detained person's consular rights, notification to the consulate on request, and forwarding of communications without delay. Consular assistance rights coexist with the duty to refrain if the national expressly opposes action. Paragraph (2) permits receiving-State procedures subject to their enabling full effect to the rights' purposes. The Court gives that proviso weight: failure to give notice can bear on timely exercise of the right. But the proviso does not itself establish that this applicant could not have presented the available claim with reasonable diligence, that novelty supplies external cause, or that correction of the alleged violation permits the requested domestic remedy. Coleman (1991), Carrier and Reed require those distinct inquiries; treaty supremacy does not collapse them. The stipulated notice failure does not concede prejudice. Counsel's advice to accept a noncapital offer, Breard's refusal and his choice to testify leave the asserted different effect of consular advice disputed. The Fourth Circuit rejected cause without deciding prejudice. The Court makes no final contrary factual finding and does not say consular advice could never change a defense. The present submission does not show the required prospect of legally consequential relief past those obstacles. A notification allegation alone also supplies no reliable new innocence evidence satisfying Schlup.

##### III. The separate challenge to Virginia's proportionality-review method does not independently justify a stay

**Holding and operative rule.** The present claim that Virginia administered its statutory proportionality review through an inadequate comparison pool does not show sufficient reversal prospects to support a stay. State-law error alone is not a federal habeas ground; a genuine arbitrary deprivation of a state-created sentencing entitlement remains a distinct federal question, but this submission does not adequately demonstrate such a deprivation.

**Authority.** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies eight direct joins. Ginsburg and Breyer nevertheless support a stay on the treaty ground. Stevens alone regards the process claim as independently sufficient for protection.

**Controlling explanation.** Pulley rejects a universal federal requirement of comparative proportionality review, and Lewis v. Jeffers does not authorize federal correction of state-law error alone. Hicks preserves protection against arbitrary deprivation of an existing sentencing entitlement. This claim therefore cannot be dismissed merely by confusing it with a different claim that the sentence itself was disproportionate. The district court recognized the federal method claim and expressly rejected default before denying relief; the parties dispute how the Fourth Circuit treated it. Virginia's §17-110.1(C) requires determinations concerning arbitrary influence and excessiveness or disproportionality compared with similar crimes and defendants. Subsection (E) permits collection within a period the court chooses, requires consideration of available records as a guide, and requires accumulated records to be available to circuit courts. Permission to accumulate records is not a command to collect every prosecution. The state court reported examining capital cases it reviewed, including life sentences. That account is not proof of an exhaustive comparison, but the offered aggregate sentencing figures likewise do not establish which crimes and defendants are legally similar or that the required determination was rendered arbitrary. The objection thus disputes how review was performed without adequately showing deprivation of the review or substantive protection the statute supplies. The Court decides no state-law construction or final due-process merits question. The possible appellate claim-treatment error does not by itself establish sufficient prospects of relief despite the independent weaknesses of the asserted federal deprivation.

#### Limits of the Interim Decision

The Court decides neither categorical individual treaty enforceability, ultimate cause or prejudice, a final default exception, nor the merits of the separate proportionality-method claim. It does not attribute the substantive sentence claim's default to District Claim IV. No blanket AEDPA hearing bar, hearing entitlement, chapter 154 qualification, final §2254(d) determination or Teague disposition is adopted. Section 2254(d) requires an actual state merits adjudication of the relevant claim; a federal district court's merits discussion alone does not supply one. No finding of that failure or satisfaction of those exceptions is made here. Teague's actual presentation and finality inquiries, its exception for substantive limits on the State's power to punish conduct or classes of persons, and its exception for watershed procedures essential to fundamental accuracy and fairness retain their separate predicates.
