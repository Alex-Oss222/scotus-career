### Operative grounds supported by the five affirming Associates

**Statutory authority.** Section 1395ww(h)(2)(A)'s instruction that the Secretary determine a hospital's average amount recognized as reasonable under the Act for direct GME costs in its fiscal-1984 reporting period permits the reaudit regulation's determination of an accurate allowable-cost base for the later statutory formula. It does not unambiguously command permanent use of every expense an intermediary once reimbursed. The Secretary's construction is reasonable in the context of an annual-cost system becoming an enduring base; the express authority concerning resident numbers does not negate the actual command to determine the average lawful cost. Brown requires the textual inquiry before deference, and its rule against administrative addition of a contrary statutory condition is preserved.

**Retroactivity and finality.** This rule applies the governing base-year cost principles to the antecedent data used for later payments. It neither demands repayment of the closed 1984 reimbursement nor changes what costs were allowable in 1984. Reusing old facts in prospective calculations does not alone attach a forbidden new legal consequence to completed conduct. Later already-paid years may be corrected only if each is independently still open under the governing reopening rules. The ordinary three-year finality of a particular reimbursement remains effective; finality of that payment does not, on this record, conclusively settle every historical cost fact for a distinct statutory calculation. Landgraf and Bowen retain their new-burden limits; the decision does not supply general retroactive legislative-rulemaking authority.

**Reasonableness and reliance.** The agency explained that errors formerly affecting an annual payment would otherwise become enduring formula inputs and that competing Medicare changes delayed scrutiny. That is an actual administrative rationale, not a substitute invented on review. The rule permits correction in both directions: it excludes unallowable/misclassified GME amounts, permits requested inclusion of costs wrongly classified as operating expenses, and permits an upward hospital-specific prospective-payment operating adjustment for costs the reaudit shows belonged to operating costs instead of GME. The later-period physician-time documentation allowance, which Regions used, addresses the asserted loss of base-year records. These features support the rule's reasonableness; they do not establish that every actual disallowance or allocation is accurate or that every reliance objection is immaterial.

**Preclusion.** The intermediary's February 28, 1986 notice fixed the annual reimbursement within its lawful finality rules. No PRRB adjudicatory hearing decided these cost issues. On this established posture, the payment-finality regulations and that notice do not create adjudicated issue preclusion binding the new base determination. This is not a categorical rule that administrative adjudications can never preclude later issues, nor permission to reopen a completed Article III judgment.

## Holdings

### I. Authority to determine the lawful base

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1395ww(h)(2)(A) permits the Secretary to correct the allowable fiscal-1984 graduate-medical-education cost figure used in the later statutory per-resident reimbursement formula. This authority to determine a lawful historical input does not itself reopen the final payment for the base year or authorize departure from the cost rules governing that year.

**Authority:** Part I-A's operative permission has Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six votes. Part I-B's following construction and permissible-deference explanation has Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five votes, independently sufficient to control that rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress directed the Secretary to determine the hospital's average amount recognized as reasonable under the Act for direct GME costs in its fiscal-1984 reporting period. Read with the reasonable-cost system and the shift from annual determinations to an enduring formula, that language does not unambiguously freeze every expense an intermediary once reimbursed into future payments. The Secretary's construction permits the new base determination to exclude amounts not allowable under the governing cost principles. Brown requires this statutory inquiry before respect for administrative interpretation; agency expertise alone creates no authority. The express instruction concerning resident numbers does not negate the accompanying command to determine average lawful cost. The contrary reading gives past recognition decisive effect even when an erroneous annual allowance becomes a permanent formula input. The Court concludes that Congress did not compel that result, while preserving the ordinary finality of the annual payment itself.

### II. Antecedent facts, later payments and finality

**Holding and operative rule:** Applying the existing base-year cost principles to antecedent data for future reimbursement does not impose a new legal consequence on the closed 1984 payment and is not impermissibly retroactive. A later already-paid year may be corrected only if its own payment determination remains independently open under governing reopening rules; closed determinations cannot be revised or recouped under this holding.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices, on this specific later-payment analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf distinguishes new burdens on completed conduct from present use of earlier facts; Bowen preserves the limits on retroactive rulemaking. The relevant rule changes neither which costs were allowable in 1984 nor the final amount paid for that closed year. It changes the input used in the distinct later reimbursement calculation. Regions retains finality for the earlier payment, but that protection does not establish an entitlement to carry an unallowable expense into every later calculation. The three-year reopening limit continues to protect each particular payment determination. Neither referring to an old fact nor labeling a calculation prospective answers the whole inquiry: the agency must use the governing base-year law and respect each affected year's finality. No general authority to issue retroactive legislative rules or revise final decisions is recognized.

### III. Reasonableness and the asserted reliance burden

**Holding and operative rule:** The challenged reaudit rule is reasonable on the supported administrative record because it corrects an enduring formula input under existing cost law and provides the stated two-direction corrections and documentation accommodations. This facial conclusion does not validate every particular expense disallowance, allocation or future evidentiary accommodation.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Secretary explained that annual errors would otherwise become recurring formula errors and that competing Medicare changes delayed scrutiny. Chenery requires that actual rationale, not a new reason invented by a reviewing court. The rule excludes unallowable or misclassified costs, permits requested inclusion of GME costs incorrectly classified as operating expenses, and permits an upward hospital-specific prospective-payment operating adjustment for costs the reaudit shows belonged to operating costs instead of GME. When base-year physician-time records are missing, later reporting-period evidence may document the allocation; Regions used 1989 and 1990 studies. These accommodations answer the categorical claim that the rule can only disadvantage hospitals or demands unavailable original records in every case. They do not find every disputed expense correctly disallowed or make reliance immaterial in all applications. Good Samaritan and Guernsey keep cost correction within lawful methods, and Allentown requires compliance with the actual governing standard. No indefinite serial reaudit power, new 1984 allowability rule or procedurally defective audit is approved.

### IV. The intermediary notice does not establish adjudicated issue preclusion here

**Holding and operative rule:** On this record, the intermediary's final annual reimbursement notice and payment-finality regulations do not conclusively adjudicate every underlying cost fact for the distinct statutory base determination. No Provider Reimbursement Review Board adjudicatory hearing resolved the reasonableness of these costs, so the asserted issue-preclusion objection does not bar this use.

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The February 28, 1986 notice fixed the annual reimbursement within its applicable finality rules. Payment finality and an adjudicated issue are different. The record establishes no adjudicatory cost determination whose preclusive force would have to be displaced. The Court therefore rejects the asserted bar without announcing that administrative adjudications can never preclude later issues or permitting reconsideration of a completed Article III judgment.

- Landgraf is applied to the particular consequence for later payments; its rule against unauthorized new retrospective burdens remains intact.
- Bowen preserves the limit on retroactive legislative rules and rejection of unsupported litigation rationalizations; neither authority is expanded.

- Guernsey preserves lawful accounting allocation without permission to contradict a binding regulation.
- Chenery preserves review on the agency's actual rationale.
- Allentown requires use of the actual announced standard and the whole record; no substituted base-year cost standard is approved.

## Law After Decision

The Secretary may determine an accurate allowable 1984 input for the enduring GME formula while leaving closed payment awards intact. The rule's legality does not settle each expense dispute, excuse defective procedures, reopen adjudicated cost issues on a different record, or create general power to redetermine final agency decisions.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia, joined by Justices O'Connor and Thomas, would reverse on the statutory ground. Congress selected the amount already recognized as reasonable under the existing reimbursement machinery; directing an average did not authorize a new reasonable-cost determination after the ordinary revision period. Congress expressly delegated resident-count rules without similarly directing recomputation of the settled cost figure. Accuracy and administrative practicality cannot supply the missing delegation. The dissent would remand for lawful calculations using the recognized base and applicable resident-count and inflation rules, without ordering a precise award. It need not decide every other hospital objection and does not claim that every use of an antecedent fact is impermissibly retroactive.

## Procedure After Action

The final 1984 payment remains untouched; the Court orders no recoupment, new audit procedure, exact payment or adjudication of an individual expense.
