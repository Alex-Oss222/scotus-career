## Holdings

### 1. The two subordinates receive personal immunity on the actual notice and role record

**Holding and operative rule:** Astle and Blank receive qualified immunity because, during the 1988–1989 processing at issue, the law did not make their own conduct's federal unlawfulness sufficiently apparent in the supported administrative circumstances. Consultation with a superior is relevant to those circumstances but is not an automatic defense, and a state regulatory deadline violation does not alone establish a clearly established federal violation.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion for all eight Associate Justices controls the complete ground. Stone-Zsela also joins the no-automatic-defense proposition but agrees with the immunity judgment on a different ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson require objective, particularized conduct-time notice. Elder requires consideration of all relevant authority; Lanier permits an obviously covered application without an identical prior licensing case. Neither those later decisions nor Lewis can supply notice retrospectively. Davis v. Scherer separates a state-law breach from the required federal obligation.

The earlier revocation was judicially sustained, while later administrative proceedings expunged the underlying indications. Expungement did not itself adjudicate automatic reinstatement. Astle sought Blank's advice about that relationship; Blank consulted Turner, and the consultation reached state regional official Klump. Klump's direction to decide went to Turner. The record does not establish that Astle or Blank personally received and defied that instruction or possessed final authority to resolve the conflict.

Prolonged inaction may deny the practical benefit of a hearing available only after denial, and asking a supervisor cannot excuse knowingly refusing a plainly required act within one's control. Those concerns do not establish these two defendants' personal liability on this supported role and notice record. The state rules required investigation within 75 days of a valid application and notification within 30 days after completion, with a hearing after denial; those commands do not themselves decide the federal process claim or entitlement to certification. The Court resolves immunity without deciding the ultimate deprivation or excusing every administrative delay.

### 2. The official-capacity judgment requires an actual Rule 50 and municipal-liability determination

**Holding and operative rule:** Denial of a defendant's summary-judgment motion is not a judgment establishing the plaintiff's liability case and cannot, merely as law of the case, justify a directed liability judgment at trial. DuRose's official-capacity judgment must instead be evaluated on the actual trial evidence under Rule 50 and the requirements of an attributable county policy or authorized final decision causing an established federal deprivation; personal qualified immunity supplies no county defense.

**Authority:** Part II has all eight Associate Justices for the complete Rule 50 correction and bounded remand. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the entity-capacity, separate-deprivation/policy/causation and no-personal-immunity propositions, but not the additional Rule 50 rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Kentucky v. Graham identifies the official-capacity judgment as the entity's judgment. Monell excludes automatic employer liability, and Pembaur requires an actual authorized final policy decision when that is the asserted basis. McMillian directs attention to the entity and the challenged function under applicable law and legally effective usage. A title, operational discretion, employment or absence of immediate supervision alone does not establish final policymaking; its Alabama holding identifies no Oneida policymaker. Collins keeps the underlying federal deprivation distinct from its municipal attribution.

The trial court treated an earlier denial of DuRose's summary-judgment request as though liability already had been established. That premise bypassed the required trial-record determination. The familiar district court must now evaluate that record and explain any supported Rule 50 ruling. Judgment for plaintiffs, judgment for the county or factual issues for a jury remain possible; the Court selects none in advance and orders no automatic new trial. The complete trial record is not reproduced here, and that limitation supplies no adverse factual finding. Neither the $300,000 judgment nor its dependent fee/cost award can remain on the erroneous premise, but the Court fixes no proper amount or independent fee entitlement.

### 3. The distinct later personal claim requires its own identified disposition

**Holding and operative rule:** An order addressing the original pre-expungement pleading cannot dispose of a distinct post-expungement personal claim added later merely by an informal assertion that all personal claims were dismissed. The district court must identify any actual ruling and its basis, or adjudicate the pending request under the standard governing its actual procedural stage.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all eight Associate Justices, controls this procedural ground. Stone-Zsela supports the remand disposition without joining the additional rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The original complaint concerned earlier acts, while the amended pleading added the later processing claim. Treating an earlier order as a disposition of a not-yet-pleaded claim fails to identify what was decided. An informal letter does not fill that gap. If a ruling occurred, its actual basis must be explained; otherwise the pending request requires decision. Pleading review cannot demand trial proof, and summary judgment must use its own record and standard. Crawford-El preserves ordinary procedural burdens and case management rather than a special evidentiary barrier. Nothing in this remand finds DuRose personally aware of the consultation chain, responsible for every subordinate act or liable for a federal violation. The Article 78 adequate-process contention remains undecided for appropriate consideration below.
