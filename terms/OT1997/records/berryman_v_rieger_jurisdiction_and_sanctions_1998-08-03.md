## Controlling holdings

### I. The actual appeal sought factual selection rather than a separable legal immunity decision

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit properly dismissed this interlocutory appeal because the arguments actually pursued required choosing disputed defense evidence over the plaintiff's sworn account rather than deciding a separable legal immunity question on the proper assumptions. An otherwise authorized legal issue accepting those assumptions remains reviewable; neither the presence of factual disputes nor a prior appeal categorically defeats jurisdiction, and this first mandate expressly left proper immunity presentation open.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins to the complete rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** Johnson excludes an immediate appeal that asks whether the evidence supports the plaintiff's factual account. Doe, Behrens, Smith and Camilo preserve legal review on the proper assumed facts without choosing truth. Section 1254 does not enlarge the Court of Appeals' §1291 authority. If necessary assumptions are unstated, a reviewing court may identify them from material already before the District Court without reweighing evidence, or seek clarification when they cannot responsibly be identified.

Here the defendants relied on disputed treatment records, Mukherjee's affidavit despite Berryman's denial that he examined him, their own denials and later treatment by nonparties. Those contentions seek factual selection. Later care by others does not itself settle what these defendants earlier did. The conclusion rests on the affirmative judicial account of the actual arguments, not on an inference that the unrecovered three-issue letter was absent, waived or contained no legal issue. No hypothetical unidentified legal contention is rejected on its merits.

The preliminary Clerk's order withheld dismissal only at that time; it did not finally establish jurisdiction. Nor did the 1995 mandate decide immunity or prohibit its proper presentation. It identified factual conflicts and expressly reserved that defense. Behrens supplies no numerical one-appeal limit, while retaining the bar on repeated unchanged legal rulings and matters actually resolved by a mandate. The later factual appeal cannot become lawful merely because the motion itself was permitted.

**Precedent treatment:** Johnson, Doe, Behrens, Smith and Camilo apply the legal/factual review boundary without a categorical disputed-facts rule or an invented earlier immunity adjudication.

### II. Section 1912 did not authorize the award after the circuit dismissed the appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1912 permits a discretionary award to the prevailing party of just damages for delay and single or double costs when the Supreme Court or a court of appeals affirms a judgment. Because the Sixth Circuit dismissed this appeal rather than affirming a judgment, that provision did not authorize its double-cost and attorney-fee award; the award and its derivative amount-setting entitlement are vacated.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion; all nine Justices join the statutory rule and this disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute's affirmed-judgment condition limits the occasion for its discretionary remedy. The panel's account of unnecessary protraction may bear on a different sanctions inquiry, but it cannot replace the missing statutory trigger. Judicial discretion operates within the authority Congress supplied.

The strongest argument for preserving the award is that repeated factual contentions imposed an avoidable appellate burden. That does not establish authority under §1912 after a dismissal. Nor does this Court's present affirmance of the jurisdictional dismissal retroactively turn the circuit's earlier action into the affirmance on which its award depended. The Court does not decide the full measure of recoverable damages when §1912 actually applies and does not itself impose a new award.

**Precedent treatment:** Section 1912 is applied according to its affirmed-judgment condition; Cooter & Gell's source-specific treatment of appellate sanctions does not permit discretion to cure an unavailable source of authority.

### III. Sanctions may be reconsidered only under independently valid authority and process

**Holding and operative rule:** On remand the Sixth Circuit may, without being required to do so, reconsider sanctions under independently valid authority and its necessary conditions. Rule 38 permits an award to the appellee of just damages, including appropriate appellate attorney fees, and single or double costs only upon a determination that the appeal is frivolous, after a separately filed motion or notice from the court and a reasonable opportunity to respond; an amount-setting opportunity after an award is imposed does not by itself supply an earlier opportunity to contest entitlement.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion, joined in full by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins to the limited remand and every stated condition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Rule 38 amendment effective December 1, 1994 requires sanctions-specific process before imposition. A request embedded in a brief is not a separately filed motion; a jurisdictional show-cause order is not automatically sanctions notice. Roadway Express requires fair notice and opportunity before fee sanctions. But the reported account does not establish that no other sufficient motion, notice or opportunity occurred. Vacatur rests on the §1912 defect, not an invented absence of process.

The circuit may examine its actual record and provide any required process before exercising discretion. It must assess the appeal actually pursued, distinguish permitted immunity presentation from factual relitigation, and address the procedural sequence, including preliminary permission to continue. That permission is neither a permanent jurisdictional ruling nor automatic protection from sanctions. Dismissal alone does not establish frivolousness. No new evidentiary hearing is mandatory if a sufficient opportunity to respond can otherwise be afforded.

Inherent power is not automatically substituted for §1912. A fee award on that ground must independently satisfy bad-faith and fair-process requirements and the governing limits of Roadway Express and Chambers; the Court finds no such predicates established and changes no broader inherent-power rule. Section 1927 addresses counsel and does not itself validate this award against defendants. Ordinary Rule 39 costs on dismissal are distinct from double costs and attorney fees. Cooter & Gell preserves the separate appellate source of sanctions authority. Possible equivalent relief under another source does not validate an unexercised alternative power. No sanction, amount or counsel liability is imposed or directed.

**Precedent treatment:** Rule 38's operative conditions and Roadway Express's process requirements govern possible reconsideration; Cooter & Gell preserves source-specific authority, and Chambers retains its existing limits without automatic substitution or enlargement.

Neither the appeal's dismissal nor the sanctions ruling establishes a constitutional violation, serious medical need, deliberate indifference, protected litigation, retaliatory causation, ultimate immunity, liability, damages or a right to trial, discovery or a special hearing.

**Authority:** Part IV of O'Connor's opinion; all nine Justices join these nonreach and remedial limits. No independent constitutional merits holding is adopted.

**Controlling explanation:** The earlier reversal addressed conflicting sworn accounts, not established medical facts. Berryman alleged that Rieger refused treatment and directed him to seek Veterans Administration care; that Kukla invoked his litigation against her friend Fox; and that Holzheuer did not supply the care her contested progress entries described. Those different allegations remain distinct. No direct retaliatory statement by Holzheuer is identified; that supplies neither a direct-evidence prerequisite nor a finding for or against her. Alleged forgery remains an allegation, and another defendant's statement is not hers.

Crawford-El preserves competent direct and circumstantial proof, ordinary preponderance and the actual Mt. Healthy same-decision defense. A merely possible lawful explanation is not the same-decision showing, and conduct otherwise lawful may still require examination under the distinct retaliation claim. Protected conduct, personal action, causation and injury remain necessary. These preserved rules decide no element here and create no automatic discovery entitlement or replacement immunity framework.

The precise treatment dates remain unresolved. The September 1992 complaint supplies an outer bound, not an exact conduct date; later Farmer, Crawford-El or Camilo law cannot supply earlier notice. Ordinary proceedings may resolve facts and supported independent defenses. The Court prescribes no medical finding, damages assessment, trial or additional merits procedure.

**Precedent treatment:** Crawford-El and Mt. Healthy retain their ordinary proof and separate causal burdens; Hunter, Elder and the existing immunity cases retain their procedural and conduct-time limits without a new merits application.

## Law After Decision

An appeal seeking selection of contested evidence remains outside immediate qualified-immunity jurisdiction, even when an earlier mandate properly permitted the immunity motion. A separable legal appeal on proper assumptions remains available under the existing rules. Section 1912 does not authorize the circuit's enhanced award following dismissal. The award may be reconsidered under another valid source only after that source's substantive and procedural conditions are satisfied; neither dismissal, delay, unreported notice nor possible alternative authority resolves those conditions automatically. The underlying medical-care and retaliation claims, each defendant's responsibility, and ordinary defenses remain unadjudicated.
