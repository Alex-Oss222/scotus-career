## Controlling holdings

### 1. Original-source status has a prefiling condition, not a prepublication condition

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the then-operative §3730(e)(4)(B), a relator invoking the original-source exception must have direct and independent knowledge of the information on which the allegations are based and voluntarily provide that information to the Government before filing the action. The statute does not additionally require government disclosure before public disclosure, first disclosure to the Government, or responsibility for the publisher's source; the dismissal resting on that added deadline is vacated.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt Thomas's Part I; nine direct rationale votes.

**Statutory setting:** Section 3730(e)(4)(A) bars an action based upon public disclosure of allegations or transactions in a criminal, civil or administrative hearing; in a congressional, administrative or Government Accounting Office report, hearing, audit or investigation; or from the news media, unless the action is brought by the Attorney General or the person bringing it is an original source. This decision applies the original-source definition without adopting a comprehensive construction of based upon.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress supplied both knowledge conditions and a specific prefiling disclosure condition. The aim of preventing derivative bounty litigation cannot substitute an earlier publication clock. Direct knowledge and independent knowledge remain distinct requirements; neither employment nor a claim to insider status proves both. Lexecon supports applying the enacted conditions without inserting an additional procedural barrier. Brogan preserves written statutory limits rather than a free-standing policy exception. Oubre likewise requires performance of statutory protections rather than replacing them with an unprovided prerequisite; none of those decisions decides original-source facts here. The Sixth Circuit reasoned that the first person to sound the alarm alone deserves the reward, but its added chronology condition excludes persons who may satisfy Congress's definition. The Court does not reward copied allegations: genuine knowledge and voluntary disclosure remain to be proved.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Lexecon is applied to written statutory conditions without importing its separate venue rule.
- Brogan preserves the enacted boundary without importing its separate criminal holding.
- Oubre is applied to statutory conditions without importing its separate waiver rules.

### 2. Correcting the deadline does not establish jurisdiction or fraud

**Holding and operative rule:** The qui tam claim returns for application of the actual public-disclosure and original-source conditions; this judgment does not declare McKenzie an original source or establish jurisdiction. Even assuming the identified public disclosures trigger §3730(e)(4)(A), the exception requires the prescribed knowledge and voluntary governmental disclosure before filing, and dismissal remains available if those conditions fail.

**Authority:** The eight Associate Justices adopt Thomas's Part II: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with vacatur and factual remand but supplies his different additional construction in a separate writing.

**Controlling explanation:** The assumed public predicates are the reported Dorris state-court suit concerning South Central Bell's practices, the Falsetti federal qui tam suit involving a different BellSouth subsidiary, and their news coverage. The earlier workplace knowledge, substantially overlapping complaint passages, and timing and content of government communication require their own record treatment. Similar language is relevant evidence; it does not automatically decide the origin of every factual allegation. Conversely, dispatcher experience does not establish every original-source fact. Sealed filing and government service under §3730(b)(2) cannot simply be assumed to satisfy voluntary disclosure before filing, and government declination proves neither original-source status nor fraud. The extra prepublication deadline is sufficient to require vacatur, so the Court does not select a comprehensive construction of based upon. Steel Co. preserves jurisdictional inquiry distinct from substantive merits without repealing the provision's express jurisdictional form. Separately, §3730(b)(5) prohibits a person other than the Government from intervening or bringing a related action based on facts underlying a pending private FCA action. Section 3730(e)(3) bars a private action based on allegations or transactions that are the subject of a civil suit or administrative civil money penalty proceeding in which the Government is already a party. Neither distinct bar is adjudged applicable merely from similar allegations.

### 3. The employee's retaliation claim has independent statutory conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** The then-operative §3730(h) protects an employee against covered employment retaliation because of lawful acts in furtherance of an FCA action, including qualifying investigation or assistance before an action is filed; protection does not depend solely on a successful qui tam recovery or survival of the public-disclosure bar. McKenzie's allegations permit the required inferences of protected activity, employer knowledge and causation at the reviewed dismissal stage, so the Sixth Circuit's reversal and retaliation remand are affirmed.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt Thomas's Part III; nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** McKenzie alleged persistent reporting of the fraud beyond ordinary dispatcher tasks, presentation to supervisors of a newspaper account of similar federal qui tam litigation, and responsive threats and harassment. These facts permit an inference that BellSouth understood her conduct as connected to contemplated federal enforcement. The employer need not hear the precise statutory label when the alleged facts provide the required notice. Ordinary performance complaints without such a connection are not automatically protected. Robinson supports reading retaliation protection in its statutory context while keeping prohibited conduct, notice and causation distinct; its Title VII rule does not supply FCA elements. Discovery may disprove any predicate. The judgment finds neither actual fraud nor retaliation, disability causation or an entitlement to reinstatement, back pay or damages. The claim proceeds under its own statutory conditions rather than falling automatically with a distinct qui tam jurisdictional ruling.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Robinson v. Shell Oil Co. supplies contextual treatment of a retaliation provision while its Title VII coverage holding and limits remain distinct.
