United States v. Cabrales, No. 97-643. Argued April 29, 1998; decided June 1, 1998. ## Holdings

### 1. The source of the proceeds does not relocate these Florida transactions

**Holding and operative rule:** When the charged conduct under §§1956(a)(1)(B)(ii) and 1957 consists entirely of financial transactions begun, conducted and completed in Florida, Missouri does not become a proper venue solely because prior Missouri crimes generated the funds. Illicit origin remains a necessary statutory circumstance, but these counts allege no Missouri laundering act or transportation by Cabrales that would make her charged offenses begin, continue or end there; the two Missouri counts must therefore be dismissed for venue.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's unanimous opinion controls the complete rule, both counts' application and the reserved charging distinctions.

**Controlling explanation:** Article III, the Sixth Amendment and Rule 18 locate trial where the charged crime was committed. Anderson directs attention to the offense's nature and constituting acts. Subject to contrary congressional provision, §3237(a) permits trial where an offense actually begins, continues or is completed; its transportation paragraph does not turn every commerce-linked transaction into transportation through the place where funds arose.

The January 1991 Florida deposit and withdrawals are the charged transactions. Missouri drug sales supplied the property's criminal origin. The Government alleges no transport of those funds from Missouri by Cabrales and no act of hers constituting these laundering transactions there.

The Government argues that illicit origin is indispensable and therefore begins the laundering offense where the predicate crime occurred. But an indispensable attribute of the transaction's object need not be prohibited conduct by this defendant at that location. Gaudin preserves every statutory element and its factfinder; it does not transform each circumstance into a geographical act. The knowledge and unlawful-source elements retain their full force. Practical access to Missouri evidence and a description of an integrated scheme cannot add charged conduct.

United States v. Thomas involved actual authorized interstate transportation completed at its recipient, not this antecedent connection. Beddow's predicate participation and transport likewise present different allegations. A source-of-funds theory alone, as suggested by Sax, cannot supply venue for these counts. Genuine acquisition and transportation, participation in the predicate crime, aiding and abetting or a differently charged conspiracy require their own allegations and law. No universal bar to multistate laundering venue follows. Count I's venue, agreement, overt acts and guilt remain undecided; the Court inserts none of those matters into Counts II and III.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Anderson, 328 U.S. 699:** Applied to identify the charged offense's nature and the acts constituting it, rather than every related historical circumstance.
- **United States v. Gaudin, June 19, 1995:** Preserved as requiring full force and proper factfinding for every statutory element; it does not make every circumstance element a separate geographical act.
- **United States v. Thomas, May 19, 1997:** Distinguished because authorized interstate transportation actually ended at the recipient there; its venue rule and limits on remote effects remain intact.
- **United States v. Beddow, 957 F.2d 1330:** Distinguished by the defendant's predicate participation and acquisition and transport of funds; no decision about such allegations is necessary here.
- **United States v. Sax, 39 F.3d 1380:** Its suggestion that the predicate crime's location alone supplies laundering venue is rejected for the transactions and charges presented here.

## Law After Decision

Venue for these substantive laundering counts follows the charged financial conduct. Proof that the funds arose from crime in another district does not by itself relocate that conduct. Every statutory element remains, and actual multistate offenses remain subject to their own venue rules. A conspiracy, aiding-and-abetting or transportation theory cannot be assumed to repair the present indictment. The result leaves properly venued prosecution available without resolving another charge or the defendant's guilt.
