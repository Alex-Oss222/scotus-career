## Holdings

### I. Separate assessment of each present motion

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 39.8 permits denial when the particular submission is frivolous or malicious, even assuming inability to prepay; earlier unsuccessful filings cannot substitute for that determination. On separate assessment of these papers, neither the lead submission nor the Wilson submission presents an arguable challenge connecting the alleged interference to the independent failure to comply with the 1996 amendment instruction; the Court exercises its discretion to deny each motion while preserving a paid-filing opportunity.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, expressly joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Stevens and Stone-Zsela would exercise the discretion differently.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court considers Arteaga's actual explanations. He sought an extension, alleges withheld papers and obstructed access, names both appeals, and later sought reopening. He was not silent about noncompliance. But the detailed nonwillful-default account concerns No. 92-15341, rather than an identified withheld item or event preventing compliance with the March–April 1996 amendment directions. Those directions required separate properly captioned complaints and extended the deadline after his competing-work explanation. The lower orders establish the nonamendment; absence of an archival item does not.

Wilson receives its own assessment. The July 21 memorandum identifies No. 96-16222 and C 94-3046, amendment opportunities, warnings and the Rule 41(b) ground. The Wilson cover, appeal reference, extension request, interference account and reopening materials do not supply the missing particular connection. The December reopening order shows what explanation was presented but does not automatically become an additional judgment under review.

Demos and Anderson preserve a low filing threshold where a specific interference claim connects to the reviewed obstacle. They do not make a generalized access allegation conclusive merely because the paper names the affected appeal. Nor does a lower-court loss itself establish frivolity. The Court assumes financial sufficiency: the sworn unemployment, income and asset statements are affirmative evidence, and uncertain caption linkage is not proof of wealth or filing default. The decision does not find the interference allegations false, make two daily library hours universally adequate, or decide certiorari or the underlying claims.

### II. No future noncriminal fee-and-form condition

**Holding and operative rule:** A prospective restriction requires demonstrated applicant-specific abuse and a supported relation between that abuse, the future category restricted and inadequacy of individual treatment. The present record does not establish those predicates for the proposed fee-and-form condition on unidentified future noncriminal certiorari petitions, so the Court imposes none.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Martin, Day, Sassower, Anderson and Brown require a separate justification for future restraints. Twenty petitions, sixteen in two terms, and the present particular-paper denials do not themselves show why ordinary treatment is inadequate or why the entire future noncriminal category fits demonstrated abuse. The contents of unknown future filings have not been adjudicated. Preserving criminal and paid routes narrows a condition but does not prove its justification. This refusal creates no numerical threshold, universal prior-warning requirement, duty to exhaust every conceivable lesser measure, or categorical prohibition of properly supported future controls.
