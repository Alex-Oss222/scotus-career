## Holdings

**Present financial leave.** Rule 39 permits the present financial motion on the established inability-to-prepay premise. Discretionary denial under Rule 39.8 requires a supported finding that the particular submission is frivolous or malicious; earlier unsuccessful filings do not supply that finding. The Court's per curiam opinion applies that rule and declines denial on this record.

Brown's allegations concern interference with judicial access as well as laundry sabotage and judicial bias. No allegation is found true. But the available account and eight previous unsuccessful petitions do not establish that the complete present paper lacks an arguable review ground or is malicious. Martin and Demos require attention to present access content; its label alone does not establish an arguable claim. In re Vey illustrates a supported grant, while Vey v. Clinton permits denial despite indigence when the particular paper's developed grounds justify it. That denial remains available on its own predicate. Granting this motion establishes neither an access violation nor entitlement to certiorari, full briefing, counsel, or relief from a lower-court default.

**Prospective control.** A future noncriminal-certiorari fee-and-form condition requires demonstrated applicant-specific abuse, fit to that filing category, and support for the inadequacy of individual-paper treatment. The Court's per curiam opinion applies those independent requirements and declines the condition here.

Eight prior unsuccessful petitions and an earlier Rule 39.8 denial do not identify relevant repetition or failed individual controls. Keeping paid and criminal channels open would limit the condition's reach, but would not supply its justification. The rule creates no numerical threshold, universal warning requirement, duty to exhaust every conceivable alternative, or categorical prohibition on supported restrictions.
