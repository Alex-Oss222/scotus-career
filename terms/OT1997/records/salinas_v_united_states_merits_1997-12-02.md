## Holdings

**Federal bribery — no demonstrable-effects requirement.** Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, holds that §666 does not require proof that the bribe demonstrably affected federal funds.

The text's institutional and transaction predicates do not add traced federal dollars as another element. Wells supports that distinction without construing §666 itself; context and Ratzlaf's residual lenity do not establish the proposed condition. The federally connected jail and corrupt contact-visit scheme permit this answer while expressly reserving whether the statute requires a distinct connection between the bribe and expenditure of federal funds. Outer constitutional applications are separately reserved. Neither reservation adopts a nexus test or burden.

The Government must satisfy agency, corrupt intent, acceptance of a thing of value to influence or reward, covered entity business or transactions worth at least $5,000, and qualifying federal benefits exceeding $10,000 in the relevant one-year period. Section 666(c)'s bona fide compensation and ordinary-course expense exception remains operative. The $5,000 condition concerns the covered business, transaction or series, not automatically the bribe, watches or truck. Qualifying-benefits status is assumed for this review; no rule makes every federal receipt a benefit. No precise annual amount is found, intangible-benefit coverage decided, or valuation reopened. These limits do not excuse proof of an actual element.

**RICO conspiracy — knowing agreement, without two personally promised predicates.** Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, holds that §1962(d) requires a knowing agreement to further an undertaking that, if carried out, would satisfy the substantive enterprise offense; it does not require personal commission or personal agreement to commit two predicate acts.

The agreement offense differs from personal completion of the contemplated crime. Its object must nevertheless be unlawful and encompass the applicable substantive elements, including Reves's governing direction requirement. That requirement does not automatically demand that every conspirator personally direct the enterprise. Mere association, knowledge, friendship or employment is insufficient. Shabani supports the agreement distinction at its own statutory scope, not a universal conspiracy code.

The Court rejects the personal-two-act instruction objection. It does not approve the entire charge, decide an independent personal-direction instruction, general evidentiary sufficiency or harmlessness, announce general aiding-and-abetting liability, or revive the substantive acquittal. No general overt-act holding is adopted.

## Precedent Treatment

Wells is applied by analogy to distinguish actual statutory conditions from the proposed additions; Ratzlaf preserves context and residual lenity. Shabani informs the agreement inquiry without supplying a universal conspiracy rule. Reves remains the governing substantive enterprise rule and had reserved conspiracy; today's construction preserves the substantive object rather than transferring every performance requirement to each conspirator. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Section 666 does not require a demonstrable effect on federal funds; any distinct statutory expenditure nexus and outer constitutional applications remain open. Section 1962(d) requires the specified knowing unlawful agreement without two personally promised predicates. Actual coverage, culpability and substantive-object requirements remain intact.
