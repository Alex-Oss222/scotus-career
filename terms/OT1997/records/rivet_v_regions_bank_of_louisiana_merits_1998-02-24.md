**Operative holding:** Under §§1331 and 1441, the assertion that a prior federal judgment precludes a complaint presenting only state-law claims is a defense and does not transform that complaint into one arising under federal law. Complete statutory preemption is different: it concerns whether federal law supplies the claim's controlling legal character, not whether an earlier federal judgment defeats an otherwise state-law claim. An artful-pleading label or the asserted completeness of claim preclusion cannot supply the missing original jurisdiction.

**Application and alternatives:** The mortgage holders seek recognition/enforcement of Louisiana mortgage rights or alternatively damages under Louisiana law; the parties are Louisiana parties. The respondents invoke the bankruptcy orders to defeat those rights. Even if the orders conclusively extinguished them, that would answer a defense, not establish removal jurisdiction. The strongest contrary path reads Moitie footnote 2 as allowing removal whenever federal claim preclusion wholly bars a later state claim. The eight decline that extension: relabeling an essentially federal claim and asserting preclusion of an independent state claim are different. Moitie's ordinary preclusion rule is preserved; the footnote supplies no general exception for this defense. No finding that these petitioners prevail on the mortgage or may disregard the bankruptcy orders follows.

Peacock supplies the need for independent statutory authority in a later action and preserves lawful enforcement within its proper sphere. ## Public Projection

## Event

Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana, No. 96-1971. Argued January 21, 1998; decided February 24, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit. Louisiana mortgage holders sued under Louisiana law to enforce their mortgage or obtain damages. Respondents removed on the theory that earlier federal bankruptcy orders extinguished the rights asserted. The district court refused remand and entered merits judgments; the Fifth Circuit affirmed through Carpenter's complete-claim-preclusion reading of Moitie. The question is whether that defense supplies federal-question removal jurisdiction.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Fifth Circuit judgment, vacates the dependent federal merits judgments, and remands with instructions to return the action to Louisiana state court.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. The district court must vacate the merits judgments entered without federal jurisdiction and remand the whole action to state court. That includes the Bank and Fountainbleau Storage Associates judgments based on preclusion and the Browns' separately reasoned failure-to-state-a-claim judgment. The state court may decide the earlier federal judgments' preclusive effect under governing federal preclusion law; no federal merits ruling in this removed action remains.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Scalia delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### Federal claim preclusion remains a defense

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 28 U.S.C. §§1331 and 1441, a prior federal judgment's claim-preclusive effect is a defense and does not transform a complaint presenting only state-law claims into an action arising under federal law. Complete statutory preemption differs: Congress must displace the state claim with the exclusive federal cause that supplies its legal character; the completeness of an asserted judgment defense does not perform that work.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join the rule, its application and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that jurisdiction comes from the claim and governing jurisdictional law, not from the strength of an anticipated defense. These petitioners seek recognition and enforcement of Louisiana mortgage rights, or alternatively damages under Louisiana law. The bankruptcy orders may defeat those rights, but even a conclusive defense would not supply the absent federal cause. Calling the complaint artfully pleaded does not answer whether federal law creates its controlling character.

Moitie's second footnote does not establish a general exception for federal claim preclusion. Relabeling an essentially federal claim differs from asserting an independent state claim that may be barred by an earlier federal judgment. Moitie's preclusion rule remains intact, but the broader removal rule drawn from it by Carpenter cannot sustain this judgment. Peacock requires independent authority for a later action, while City of Chicago had substantial federal claims on the complaints' face. Recognition principles in Matsushita, Richards and Baker likewise do not provide a universal jurisdictional grant. The Court decides neither whether petitioners actually retain mortgage rights nor the validity or preclusive effect of the bankruptcy orders.

### Dependent merits judgments must be vacated

**Holding and operative rule:** Because this action lacks the asserted original federal jurisdiction and no later jurisdictional cure is established, the dependent federal merits judgments must be vacated and the whole action returned to state court. A different merits ground for one respondent does not cure the same jurisdictional defect.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion, with all nine Justices joining the remedy and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Caterpillar preserves a completed judgment where actual diversity jurisdiction arose before trial and continued through judgment; consent, efficiency and finality do not substitute for that jurisdiction. Here all parties are Louisiana parties, the complaint relies exclusively on Louisiana law, and the federal defense supplies no cure. The Browns' judgment rested on failure to state a claim rather than the Bank and FSA's preclusion ground, but it too lacked the needed federal foundation. Returning the case leaves the competent state forum to determine the defense under applicable law. The Court orders no damages, mortgage enforcement, new injunction or collateral attack on the bankruptcy orders.

## Precedent Treatment

- Moitie's ordinary claim-preclusion rule is preserved; its footnote is not extended into a general removal exception for prior federal judgments.
- Peacock supplies the independent-authority requirement for a later action while preserving ordinary enforcement within its proper sphere.
- City of Chicago is distinguished because substantial federal claims appeared on the complaints' face there; supplemental jurisdiction has no comparable original anchor here.
- Caterpillar is distinguished because actual jurisdiction arose before trial there; finality alone cannot save this action's merits judgments.
- Matsushita preserves judgment recognition without equating that effect with adjudicatory power.
- Richards preserves constitutional limits on binding parties; it supplies no removal authority and no finding about these petitioners' binding status.
- Baker preserves the distinction among recognition, execution and new duties in another forum; that distinction creates no universal removal grant.

## Law After Decision

Federal claim preclusion may defeat a state-law action in a competent forum but cannot itself remove that action to federal court. Complete statutory preemption retains its separate requirements. The jurisdictional decision supplies no mortgage-priority, cancellation, damages, bankruptcy-order-validity or actual preclusion holding. Otherwise available enforcement proceedings retain their own authority and prerequisites.
