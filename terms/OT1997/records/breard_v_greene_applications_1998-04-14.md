## Controlling holdings

### I. A-738 does not establish the necessary prospect of prospective relief for a current consular violation

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming the invoked notice duties were binding and violated, and applying the applicants' favorable formulation requiring a reasonable probability that four Justices would grant review, a significant possibility of reversal, likely irreparable harm and appropriate consideration of competing equities, A-738 does not establish the necessary prospect that the older consular duties require the requested execution restraint as relief for a current violation. A continuing consequence of past nonnotification is not by itself a continuing denial of these applicants' presently available consular rights; the distinct most-favored-consul notification theory does not itself establish the requested remedy.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by all seven other Associate Justices, supplies eight direct joins to this complete rule and application. Stone-Zsela independently agrees that the current domestic duty and remedial showing is insufficient, without adopting the complete explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court considers Paraguay's own rights, not a supposed default of Breard's personal claim. Article 36 protects communication and access; consular notification must occur without delay if the national requests it, while advice to the national and forwarding covered communications must also occur without delay. It protects visits, correspondence and arranging representation, including after judgment, but requires consular officers to abstain from action on the national's behalf if the national expressly opposes that action. Its receiving-State-law clause is qualified by the requirement that those laws give full effect to the rights' purposes. Neither half may be omitted.

The most-favored-consul provision of the 1859 Treaty supports a distinct notification theory without the same request condition. Even accepting that theory for this inquiry, consular access has been available since 1996. The current presentation does not sufficiently connect the old omission to a present duty requiring this execution restraint.

Young supplies prospective officer relief for an ongoing federal violation. Seminole and Coeur d'Alene preserve that route within actual duties and remedies; they remove categorical immunity shortcuts without supplying this treaty remedy. Papasan and Milliken permit relief for a legally continuing violation produced by past events, but not every continuing harm establishes a current violation of each claimant's right. The full-effect argument therefore deserves consideration but does not establish the required interim prospect. Irreversibility and the international interests are material, not substitutes for that showing. The Court decides neither the ultimate treaty remedy nor a categorical immunity rule, and does not import A-771's newly pleaded ICJ duty into this older claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Young, Seminole and Coeur d'Alene retain the connected-officer, ongoing-duty and prospective-remedy boundaries; Papasan and Milliken retain the distinction between a continuing violation and continuing effects. Coleman supplies the separate review/reversal/irreparability framework used on the applicants' favorable assumption; no universal full-Court affirmative-injunction standard is selected.

### II. A-738's older civil claim requires no further protected jurisdictional interval

**Holding and operative rule:** A-738 also supplies no basis for an administrative execution injunction merely to obtain additional coordinated consideration of the older treaty claim. Even assuming an otherwise lawful §2283 route in the applicants' favor, that claim's interim duty-and-remedy question can be assessed now; no necessary additional jurisdictional or injunctive-power inquiry is established by the prospect that execution-specific relief will become unavailable.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela denies interim relief on his own stated ground and does not adopt this additional analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** Shipp and United Mine Workers permit necessary preservation while a court determines a substantial question about its authority. They do not require delay whenever further consideration is desired, and restraint must cease if the relevant want of power is determined. Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction keeps §1651 auxiliary and subordinate to specifically governing statutes.

Hill and Atlantic Coast Line treat enforcement of a state judgment, including ministerial execution, as a state proceeding for §2283. Directing an order to officers, giving it a short duration or calling it administrative supplies no exception. The exceptions are express congressional authorization, necessity in aid of the issuing court's jurisdiction, and protection or effectuation of its judgments. The ICJ measure is not this Court's judgment, and foreign sovereignty does not confer the United States-plaintiff exception.

Hale and County of Imperial preserve a genuinely independent nonparty route, but absence from a caption does not decide party or privity status. Mitchum classifies §1983 as expressly authorized; a pleaded theory still requires the particular right, defendant and interim showing. It does not attach the Consul General's exception automatically to Paraguay's separate claims. Those predicates remain unresolved, not decided against applicants.

Even favorable answers would not supply the missing interim showing or need for another interval here. The jurisdiction at issue is review of a federal civil judgment. Atlantic Coast Line's distinct discussion of potential Supreme Court review of state decisions does not turn this civil petition into review of Virginia's criminal judgment. The older claim is presently assessable; the new original claim has its own channel. No general exception from §2283, factual default, political-question bar or final merits adjudication on assumed Article III jurisdiction follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Shipp and United Mine Workers retain necessary, substantial-question preservation; Pennsylvania Bureau preserves statutory limits; Hill and Atlantic Coast Line govern the restraint's character and exceptions; Hale, County of Imperial and Mitchum retain their distinct coverage and authorization routes without a present finding satisfying or defeating them.

### III. A-767 does not warrant a stay through the original-habeas channel

**Holding and operative rule:** After independent consideration of the retained original-habeas route, A-767 presents no sufficiently substantial prospect of extraordinary domestic habeas relief and identifies no unfinished process actually required by governing law that demands interim protection. The stay is denied without denying the original petition, treating the new ICJ order as a defaulted or previously adjudicated claim, or finding an unverified Rule 20 condition unsatisfied.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas: five direct joins to the complete explanation. Stone-Zsela separately adopts the insufficient-exceptional-relief and no-identified-required-process grounds, supplying a sixth denial vote without joining every additional proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Felker preserves original habeas under §2241 independently of review of successive-application authorization. Circuit permission is not a prerequisite here. Rule 20 nevertheless requires exceptional circumstances, no adequate relief elsewhere, reasons for not applying to the district court, and exhaustion or the §2254(b) alternatives of unavailable state corrective process or circumstances rendering that process ineffective. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform discretion; their direct mandatory force in this channel remains reserved. The Court assumes unresolved procedural matters favorably for this interim inquiry rather than inventing nonexhaustion or waiver.

The April 9 measure receives separate consideration. Even assuming international bindingness, its preservation of an interstate dispute does not on this showing establish a sufficiently substantial personal domestic custody claim. Compulsory international jurisdiction, Charter Article 94's compliance undertaking, Statute Article 41's provisional authority and a domestic habeas remedy are distinct questions. Comity does not itself create that custody right or enlarge §2251. The ICJ reserved the treaty remedy and prejudice; notice failure and the disputed plea counterfactual establish neither causal prejudice nor an innocence gateway or hearing entitlement. The earlier treaty claim retains its own defenses, which are not mechanically attached to the new order.

Section 2251 permits a stay of state proceedings involving the detained person and a matter in pending habeas before final judgment, after a judgment of discharge, or pending appeal; filing alone suspends nothing. Lonchar protects an identified legally required opportunity when lawful expedition cannot finish before execution. No such unfinished obligation is identified here, and Rule 20 makes an original petition ex parte unless a response is required. The serious new-duty argument does not require another compulsory step before this interim decision. The Court neither settles every domestic enforcement question nor converts the application's stated ICJ purposes into an automatic stay through international merits.

**Precedent treatment:** Felker's retained channel, extraordinary showing and original-§2244 reservation remain intact; Lonchar's necessary-process trigger is applied without an automatic hearing or stay; Schlup's new-evidence gateway remains distinct from notice failure and ordinary prejudice. The certiorari and civil-injunction analogies offered in the application do not replace the original-habeas inquiry.

### IV. A-771 lacks the present domestic showing for its requested interim injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** A-771's emergency submission does not sufficiently establish the domestic right, ongoing duty, jurisdictional route and remedy needed for the requested injunction through the proposed original action. Denial resolves immediate protection only: the Court reserves categorical treaty enforceability, ultimate prejudice, final original jurisdiction, the separate cause-of-action questions and the leave motion.

**Authority:** Kennedy, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas expressly adopt this interim insufficiency proposition in Part III; Stone-Zsela independently adopts the same proposition in his signed explanation. These five directly support it at this limited level. No whole opinion or additional §2283 necessity application has five denial-coalition joins, and no Marks synthesis supplies one.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court must examine the domestic channel and showing for Paraguay's own request. The new asserted preservation obligation receives its own interim assessment; denial is not a declaration that the earlier notification omission exhausts every later claim. A serious international obligation and the gravity of execution are material, but neither by itself supplies an enforceable domestic right, an ongoing duty against the named officers or authority for the particular remedy.

Seminole preserves a prospective officer route in its statutory setting and leaves direct-State amenability without a controlling rationale. It therefore supplies neither the categorical immunity ground urged from a broader account of that decision nor a treaty cause of action for these applicants. The present submission does not sufficiently establish the needed domestic injunction through the original proceeding. The Court denies the immediate request while leaving final cause-of-action and original-jurisdiction questions for their own determination. The four-Justice statutory-preservation objection and the four-Justice contrary preservation analysis remain separate positions; neither becomes the Court's additional rationale merely because five Justices agree on denial.

**Precedent treatment:** Seminole's officer holding and unresolved direct-State question are preserved, without categorical immunity or an automatic treaty cause. No new controlling construction of §2283, Article 94, Article 41, §1983 personhood or international provisional-measure enforceability results from this limited shared ground.

## Holdings

### I. A-738 does not establish the necessary prospect of prospective relief for a current consular violation

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming the invoked notice duties were binding and violated, and applying the applicants' favorable formulation requiring a reasonable probability that four Justices would grant review, a significant possibility of reversal, likely irreparable harm and appropriate consideration of competing equities, A-738 does not establish the necessary prospect that the older consular duties require the requested execution restraint as relief for a current violation. A continuing consequence of past nonnotification is not by itself a continuing denial of these applicants' presently available consular rights; the distinct most-favored-consul notification theory does not itself establish the requested remedy.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by all seven other Associate Justices, supplies eight direct joins to this complete rule and application. Stone-Zsela independently agrees that the current domestic duty and remedial showing is insufficient, without adopting the complete explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court considers Paraguay's own rights, not a supposed default of Breard's personal claim. Article 36 protects communication and access; consular notification must occur without delay if the national requests it, while advice to the national and forwarding covered communications must also occur without delay. It protects visits, correspondence and arranging representation, including after judgment, but requires consular officers to abstain from action on the national's behalf if the national expressly opposes that action. Its receiving-State-law clause is qualified by the requirement that those laws give full effect to the rights' purposes. Neither half may be omitted.

The most-favored-consul provision of the 1859 Treaty supports a distinct notification theory without the same request condition. Even accepting that theory for this inquiry, consular access has been available since 1996. The current presentation does not sufficiently connect the old omission to a present duty requiring this execution restraint.

Young supplies prospective officer relief for an ongoing federal violation. Seminole and Coeur d'Alene preserve that route within actual duties and remedies; they remove categorical immunity shortcuts without supplying this treaty remedy. Papasan and Milliken permit relief for a legally continuing violation produced by past events, but not every continuing harm establishes a current violation of each claimant's right. The full-effect argument therefore deserves consideration but does not establish the required interim prospect. Irreversibility and the international interests are material, not substitutes for that showing. The Court decides neither the ultimate treaty remedy nor a categorical immunity rule, and does not import A-771's newly pleaded ICJ duty into this older claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Young, Seminole and Coeur d'Alene retain the connected-officer, ongoing-duty and prospective-remedy boundaries; Papasan and Milliken retain the distinction between a continuing violation and continuing effects. Coleman supplies the separate review/reversal/irreparability framework used on the applicants' favorable assumption; no universal full-Court affirmative-injunction standard is selected.

### II. A-738's older civil claim requires no further protected jurisdictional interval

**Holding and operative rule:** A-738 also supplies no basis for an administrative execution injunction merely to obtain additional coordinated consideration of the older treaty claim. Even assuming an otherwise lawful §2283 route in the applicants' favor, that claim's interim duty-and-remedy question can be assessed now; no necessary additional jurisdictional or injunctive-power inquiry is established by the prospect that execution-specific relief will become unavailable.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela denies interim relief on his own stated ground and does not adopt this additional analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** Shipp and United Mine Workers permit necessary preservation while a court determines a substantial question about its authority. They do not require delay whenever further consideration is desired, and restraint must cease if the relevant want of power is determined. Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction keeps §1651 auxiliary and subordinate to specifically governing statutes.

Hill and Atlantic Coast Line treat enforcement of a state judgment, including ministerial execution, as a state proceeding for §2283. Directing an order to officers, giving it a short duration or calling it administrative supplies no exception. The exceptions are express congressional authorization, necessity in aid of the issuing court's jurisdiction, and protection or effectuation of its judgments. The ICJ measure is not this Court's judgment, and foreign sovereignty does not confer the United States-plaintiff exception.

Hale and County of Imperial preserve a genuinely independent nonparty route, but absence from a caption does not decide party or privity status. Mitchum classifies §1983 as expressly authorized; a pleaded theory still requires the particular right, defendant and interim showing. It does not attach the Consul General's exception automatically to Paraguay's separate claims. Those predicates remain unresolved, not decided against applicants.

Even favorable answers would not supply the missing interim showing or need for another interval here. The jurisdiction at issue is review of a federal civil judgment. Atlantic Coast Line's distinct discussion of potential Supreme Court review of state decisions does not turn this civil petition into review of Virginia's criminal judgment. The older claim is presently assessable; the new original claim has its own channel. No general exception from §2283, factual default, political-question bar or final merits adjudication on assumed Article III jurisdiction follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Shipp and United Mine Workers retain necessary, substantial-question preservation; Pennsylvania Bureau preserves statutory limits; Hill and Atlantic Coast Line govern the restraint's character and exceptions; Hale, County of Imperial and Mitchum retain their distinct coverage and authorization routes without a present finding satisfying or defeating them.

### III. A-767 does not warrant a stay through the original-habeas channel

**Holding and operative rule:** After independent consideration of the retained original-habeas route, A-767 presents no sufficiently substantial prospect of extraordinary domestic habeas relief and identifies no unfinished process actually required by governing law that demands interim protection. The stay is denied without denying the original petition, treating the new ICJ order as a defaulted or previously adjudicated claim, or finding an unverified Rule 20 condition unsatisfied.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas: five direct joins to the complete explanation. Stone-Zsela separately adopts the insufficient-exceptional-relief and no-identified-required-process grounds, supplying a sixth denial vote without joining every additional proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Felker preserves original habeas under §2241 independently of review of successive-application authorization. Circuit permission is not a prerequisite here. Rule 20 nevertheless requires exceptional circumstances, no adequate relief elsewhere, reasons for not applying to the district court, and exhaustion or the §2254(b) alternatives of unavailable state corrective process or circumstances rendering that process ineffective. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform discretion; their direct mandatory force in this channel remains reserved. The Court assumes unresolved procedural matters favorably for this interim inquiry rather than inventing nonexhaustion or waiver.

The April 9 measure receives separate consideration. Even assuming international bindingness, its preservation of an interstate dispute does not on this showing establish a sufficiently substantial personal domestic custody claim. Compulsory international jurisdiction, Charter Article 94's compliance undertaking, Statute Article 41's provisional authority and a domestic habeas remedy are distinct questions. Comity does not itself create that custody right or enlarge §2251. The ICJ reserved the treaty remedy and prejudice; notice failure and the disputed plea counterfactual establish neither causal prejudice nor an innocence gateway or hearing entitlement. The earlier treaty claim retains its own defenses, which are not mechanically attached to the new order.

Section 2251 permits a stay of state proceedings involving the detained person and a matter in pending habeas before final judgment, after a judgment of discharge, or pending appeal; filing alone suspends nothing. Lonchar protects an identified legally required opportunity when lawful expedition cannot finish before execution. No such unfinished obligation is identified here, and Rule 20 makes an original petition ex parte unless a response is required. The serious new-duty argument does not require another compulsory step before this interim decision. The Court neither settles every domestic enforcement question nor converts the application's stated ICJ purposes into an automatic stay through international merits.

**Precedent treatment:** Felker's retained channel, extraordinary showing and original-§2244 reservation remain intact; Lonchar's necessary-process trigger is applied without an automatic hearing or stay; Schlup's new-evidence gateway remains distinct from notice failure and ordinary prejudice. The certiorari and civil-injunction analogies offered in the application do not replace the original-habeas inquiry.

### IV. A-771 lacks the present domestic showing for its requested interim injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** A-771's emergency submission does not sufficiently establish the domestic right, ongoing duty, jurisdictional route and remedy needed for the requested injunction through the proposed original action. Denial resolves immediate protection only: the Court reserves categorical treaty enforceability, ultimate prejudice, final original jurisdiction, the separate cause-of-action questions and the leave motion.

**Authority:** Kennedy, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas expressly adopt this interim insufficiency proposition in Part III; Stone-Zsela independently adopts the same proposition in his signed explanation. These five directly support it at this limited level. No whole opinion or additional §2283 necessity application has five denial-coalition joins, and no Marks synthesis supplies one.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court must examine the domestic channel and showing for Paraguay's own request. The new asserted preservation obligation receives its own interim assessment; denial is not a declaration that the earlier notification omission exhausts every later claim. A serious international obligation and the gravity of execution are material, but neither by itself supplies an enforceable domestic right, an ongoing duty against the named officers or authority for the particular remedy.

Seminole preserves a prospective officer route in its statutory setting and leaves direct-State amenability without a controlling rationale. It therefore supplies neither the categorical immunity ground urged from a broader account of that decision nor a treaty cause of action for these applicants. The present submission does not sufficiently establish the needed domestic injunction through the original proceeding. The Court denies the immediate request while leaving final cause-of-action and original-jurisdiction questions for their own determination. The four-Justice statutory-preservation objection and the four-Justice contrary preservation analysis remain separate positions; neither becomes the Court's additional rationale merely because five Justices agree on denial.

**Precedent treatment:** Seminole's officer holding and unresolved direct-State question are preserved, without categorical immunity or an automatic treaty cause. No new controlling construction of §2283, Article 94, Article 41, §1983 personhood or international provisional-measure enforceability results from this limited shared ground.
