## Controlling holdings

### I. Obvious deletions that retain a direct accusation

**Holding and operative rule:** When a nontestifying codefendant's confession replaces a defendant's name with a conspicuous blank, spoken deletion, symbol or equivalent substitution that directly identifies the defendant from the statement and its immediate trial context, admitting that confession at their joint trial with only an instruction to consider it against the declarant violates the other defendant's right of confrontation under Bruton. Richardson continues to govern a confession neutral on its face that becomes incriminating only through later evidentiary linkage; this decision does not condemn every neutral pronoun, redaction or joint trial.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices adopt the rule and this application. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent. This is direct majority authority, not a combination of unlike rationales under Marks.

**Controlling explanation:** Bruton recognizes that a powerful direct accusation by an unavailable codefendant cannot reliably be confined to the declarant by an instruction. Richardson upheld a different kind of statement: the redaction removed every reference to the other defendant's existence, and only other evidence later supplied the incriminating link. Its reservation concerning symbols and substitute words leaves this question open.

Here the spoken deletions and written blanks leave the accusation's grammar and obvious missing identities in place. At this joint trial they invite the jury immediately to supply the other accused's name. Two omissions and testimony describing additional participants do not convert that transparent accusation into Richardson's otherwise neutral narrative. The distinction concerns the kind of inference, not whether any inference is involved. The limiting instruction therefore cannot cure the preserved confrontation violation.

Zafiro preserves a specific trial right even when instructions ordinarily provide adequate joint-trial protection; the benefits of joinder do not answer whether this particular accusation can be admitted. The holding rests on the challenged redaction itself. The prosecutor's follow-up question connecting Bell's information with Gray's arrest is not adjudicated as a separate constitutional wrong, and no separate preservation requirement is bypassed. The Court does not prescribe substantive rewriting of confessions or decide the validity of every neutral substitute, an independently admissible statement, or testimony subjected to cross-examination.

### II. Constitutional error and the remand remain separate

**Holding and operative rule:** The Maryland judgment reinstating Gray's conviction is vacated and the case remanded for proceedings consistent with the confrontation holding. Any properly available harmless-error contention requires the State to establish beyond a reasonable doubt under Chapman that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict; this Court makes no harmlessness finding and orders neither an unconditional new trial nor acquittal.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt the preservation-qualified remand. Stone-Zsela supplies the sixth vote to vacate and remand, but would affirmatively direct the state court to determine harmless error and does not join Part II's narrower instruction.

**Controlling explanation:** Establishing an admission error does not itself establish the remedy required on the whole trial record. Chapman supplies the State's demanding harmlessness burden for constitutional trial error. Williamson preserves the distinction between determining an evidentiary or constitutional wrong and deciding its consequences, without substituting the standard for nonconstitutional error. The present materials do not permit this Court to find the confrontation error harmless. Further proceedings must respect the holding and any properly available error review; a materially different redaction or lawful trial-management alternative presents its own question. No conviction is reinstated by the possibility of harmlessness, and the State receives no favorable finding on an unexamined record.

## Precedent Treatment

- Bruton v. United States, 391 U.S. 123, is applied to obvious name deletions retaining a direct accusation; its limiting-instruction exception remains bounded to that confrontation risk.
- Richardson v. Marsh, 481 U.S. 200, is distinguished because its redaction removed reference to the defendant's existence; its rule for neutral statements requiring later evidentiary linkage remains effective.
- Zafiro v. United States, 506 U.S. 534, preserves specific confrontation protection alongside discretionary joint-trial management; it supplies no universal instruction cure.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18, supplies the State's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt harmlessness burden for constitutional trial error, without an application finding here.
- Williamson v. United States, 512 U.S. 594, preserves separate analysis of error and remedy; its assertion-specific evidentiary holding and different nonconstitutional harmlessness standard are not displaced.
- White v. Illinois, 502 U.S. 346, retains its exception-specific confrontation rules; no new categorical testimonial definition or exception for an accusatory police confession is adopted.
