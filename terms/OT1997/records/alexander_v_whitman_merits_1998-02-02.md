## Holdings

That controversy permits review of her own constitutional challenge; the exact child-personhood question is already controlled by Roe and falls within the confined controlled-merits setting preserved in Steel Co., without a finding that every proposed estate or class capacity exists.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees in reaching the authorized claims without joining that threshold rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires the claimant's own injury and stage-appropriate support. The alleged refusal is a present obstacle, not speculation about another pregnancy; removing it could matter even though malpractice and recovery remain uncertain. Its summary-judgment evidence requirement does not become a pleading-stage affidavit rule. Lee requires claim-specific injury and relief, while Yankton distinguishes a continuing legal relationship from merely unfavorable precedent. No later settlement, expiration or mootness is established.

The responsible surrogate's actual refusal supplies the pleaded connection. The lawyers' independent and representative claims are unnecessary to that resolution and outside review. The Court enters no new ruling on their standing or entitlement and orders no lawyer-specific remand.

### Roe's constitutional-personhood rule governs the stillbirth claim

**Holding and operative rule:** A fetus not born alive is not a person within the Fourteenth Amendment under Roe's governing definition. This constitutional category neither determines when biological life begins nor forbids States from providing broader lawful prenatal or stillbirth protections.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Roe expressly addressed the Amendment's use of person, while distinguishing that inquiry from medical, philosophical and theological accounts of life. Its discussion of prenatal tort and property protection did not turn each state-law protection into federal constitutional personhood. Accepted allegations of development, humanity and pain therefore do not resolve the legal category.

The strongest distinction is that this case concerns a wanted near-term child allegedly harmed without conflict with the mother's abortion decision. That circumstance matters to permissible state protection and her separate rights, but does not narrow Roe's express definition. Casey preserved reproductive liberty while leaving fetal personhood undecided in that case; reserving a question did not overrule the existing independent holding. The Court applies that rule without adopting a universal description of prenatal legal status or deciding any new abortion restriction.

### The remedial line does not trigger the asserted maternal-liberty protection

**Holding and operative rule:** On these allegations, limiting additional wrongful-death and survival remedies does not itself interfere with the mother's protected reproductive decision or relationship in the manner necessary to trigger heightened constitutional protection. The Court leaves the outer scope of prenatal familial liberty open and decides no categorical rule that every tort limitation is incapable of burdening a protected right.

**Authority:** Part III has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in dismissal of this component only.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey protects the decision whether to continue a pregnancy and requires attention to the actual legal duty and attributable interference. Its complete previability standard, as applied in Fargo and Miller, imposes the applicable justification burden only after its trigger is established; a law's connection to pregnancy alone is insufficient. Here the challenged statutes prohibit no reproductive choice, compel no termination or treatment, delay no necessary care, require no disclosure and confer no third-party veto on the alleged facts.

The mother argues that denying legal protection against prenatal injury burdens her choice to continue pregnancy. Generalized loss of possible tort deterrence does not itself identify the required state interference. Her existing parental tort route is relevant context, not proof of complete protection or successful recovery. The Court neither diminishes the importance of a wanted pregnancy nor recognizes a general affirmative entitlement to every requested damages remedy. The absence of such a general entitlement does not answer her independent equality claims, which require the separate analysis that follows.

### The wrongful-death line has a rational relation to its derivative remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** New Jersey may use live birth as the administrable event identifying the independent decedent whose anticipated pecuniary contributions support survivors' derivative wrongful-death recovery. On this record that relationship satisfies ordinary equal-protection review despite comparable near-boundary losses; the existence of a parental emotional-distress action is not independently sufficient to validate the exclusion.

**Authority:** Part IV-A has all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Beach permits reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds and imperfect lines without formal legislative findings. Heller requires a relationship to the actual disadvantage, and Romer prevents treating remaining general protection as an automatic answer. Neither nonsuspect status nor absence of a fundamental tort right alone proves validity.

Wrongful death compensates survivors' pecuniary loss, including qualifying support, services and advice, with separately authorized medical, hospital and funeral expenses. Live birth identifies an independently existing decedent from whom those derivative interests are measured, distinct from the parents' direct injury from loss of pregnancy. A newborn's future contributions may be as uncertain as a near-term stillborn child's; no contrary factual finding is necessary. That comparison shows an imperfect boundary without eliminating its relationship to the defined cause.

Limiting liability alone would merely repeat the exclusion, and the Court rejects any premise that all prenatal proof is impossible. The legal connection between independent decedent status and the derivative remedy supplies the sufficient ground. Giardina's parental cause has different beneficiaries and damages; Carey v. Lovett requires distress producing physical manifestations or destroying basic emotional security, not ordinary worry or upset. No recovery or equivalent compensation is presumed. A wider state remedy could be lawful, but ordinary review does not require the best or most inclusive line.

### The assumed survival line separately defines the claim transmitted to an estate

**Holding and operative rule:** On the undisputed appellate construction that excludes stillbirth from survival recovery, New Jersey may condition an estate's continuation of its decedent's own accrued cause on live birth, including where a prenatal injury is carried into that person's life. The condition rationally identifies the independent legal holder whose claim survives; it does not rest on a finding that prenatal pain is impossible or that injury begins only at birth.

**Authority:** Part IV-B has all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Survival continues the deceased's own cause through an executor or administrator, including conscious pain and suffering and other damages and expenses allowed by state law. That function differs from survivors' expected contributions, so the wrongful-death explanation alone would not suffice. Live birth supplies a legal-holder boundary for the cause the estate continues. Recognizing a prenatal injury conditionally upon live birth does not require recognition of an independently transmissible fetal estate when birth never occurs.

The strongest objection compares the same prenatal pain followed by stillbirth with pain followed by minutes of postnatal life. The allegations are accepted, and a parental distress action may compensate neither loss. Heller and Romer require more than relabeling the exclusion. The relevant relationship here is the ownership and transmission of this particular decedent-based cause, rather than medical denial of pain or residual compensation. Beach permits that relevant legal boundary without requiring a gestational threshold, individualized substitute or new legislative study.

Giardina did not decide survival. The parallel exclusion was assumed in the federal appeal without dispute, and the Court decides the federal challenge on that express premise. It announces no authoritative New Jersey construction. Because the challenge fails even on the assumed exclusion, no certification, evidentiary remand or state-law rewrite is necessary. Broader lawful state protection remains possible.
