## Operative Standards and Tests map

### Modification of institutional consent decrees

Register present operation: Rufo, January 15, 1992; additional covered-prison-relief conditions from PLRA, April 26, 1996, as applied in Lewis v. Casey, June 24, 1996.

Operative authority: Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, January 15, 1992; Stone-Zsela with White, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. The separate performance rule is New Mexico Department of Human Services v. Joseph A. and Josephine A., April 21, 1997.

Current register wording, copied unchanged:

The party seeking modification must prove a significant change in fact or law making continued enforcement inequitable. An unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary, but actual anticipation and acceptance of the relevant risk impose a heavy burden to justify relief. Dissatisfaction, a new administration, or the availability of a cheaper constitutional alternative alone does not suffice. Once the threshold is met, the modification must be suitably tailored to the proven change and preserve lawful protection of the decree's remaining purposes; the court may not simply rewrite the decree to the constitutional minimum. Practical administration and cost may inform the selection of lawful relief but cannot substitute for the threshold showing or authorize unconstitutional conditions. A clarification that the Constitution permits an alternative practice does not by itself eliminate a voluntarily accepted stricter obligation.

Application at this event: Rufo remains a distinct equitable route. Its significant-change threshold is not an extra requirement for §3626(b)(2).

### Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments

Register present operation: Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994.

Operative authority: Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc., April 18, 1995; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Current register wording, copied unchanged:

Under Morgan Stanley, Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement under §27A(b) of a qualifying private §10(b) action even though its limitations dismissal became final without appeal before the provision's December 19, 1991 enactment: the action must have commenced on or before June 19, 1991, been dismissed as time barred after that date, and been timely under the jurisdiction's law, including retroactivity principles, as it existed June 19, 1991, with a reinstatement motion filed within sixty days after enactment. The court independently determines those cumulative conditions and, when they are satisfied, must reinstate without discretionary balancing, retaining independent responsibility for all claims, defenses, and remedies; this expressly retrospective entitlement does not authorize legislative findings, dictated liability, or unrestricted reopening of completed merits judgments. A narrowly defined, exclusively retrospective class does not by itself turn this procedure into a legislative judgment: the statute covers every action meeting its legal criteria and selects no parties for legislatively determined liability.

Application at this event: These bounded reinstatement holdings foreclose importing historical absolute-finality reasoning but authorize no general reopening rule here.

### Prison-conditions prospective relief

Register present operation: PLRA, April 26, 1996; Lewis v. Casey, June 24, 1996, supplies the controlling application and qualifications.

Operative authority: PLRA §802, April 26, 1996; Lewis v. Casey, June 24, 1996, Kennedy Part IV with all eight Associates for the findings rationale; Stone joins only the statutory disposition.

Current register wording, copied unchanged:

In a civil proceeding arising under federal law concerning conditions of confinement or officials' effects on confined persons' lives, prospective relief must be narrowly drawn, extend no further than necessary to correct the particular plaintiffs' federal-right violation and be the least intrusive necessary means; the court must make those findings and give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice-system effects. Relief requiring or permitting an official to exceed state or local authority or otherwise violate that law also requires federal permission, necessity to correct the federal violation and absence of any other corrective relief. The statute confers no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves otherwise applicable remedial limits.

Otherwise authorized preliminary injunctive relief must be narrow, no broader than necessary for the identified harm and least intrusive, with substantial safety/system weight and respect for the distinct state-law conditions. It expires ninety days after entry unless the court makes the prospective-relief findings and makes the order final before expiration. An existing permanent decree does not automatically become a preliminary order.

### 1. Conditional prospective termination preserves the judicial task

**Holding and operative rule:** On the Article III objection presented, 18 U.S.C. §3626(b)(2)–(3) validly changes the conditions for continuing prospective prison relief while leaving judges to determine actual violations, statutory eligibility and remedial scope. It neither legislatively finds compliance nor retrospectively annuls completed determinations, payments or other lawful historical effects of the decree.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that consent does not freeze the law governing future judicial supervision. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail v. Rouse, Benjamin v. Jacobson and Gavin v. Branstad apply conditional prospective termination while preserving independent adjudication. Plyler v. Moore supplies the independent statutory route. The strongest contrary argument treats a final consent decree as an irrevocable adjudication of every future duty. That argument mistakes changing prospective law for deciding the original dispute under unchanged law. Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., and United States v. Klein preserve that distinction and the judicial task. Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. and Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. permit bounded reinstatement of specified securities limitations dismissals, supplying neither absolute finality nor unrestricted congressional reopening power. The statutory requirements remain for judges to apply; Michigan's original nonadmission of liability establishes neither current compliance nor absence of a violation. No due-process or equal-protection ground is decided in this Article III review.

**Precedent treatment:** Rouse, Benjamin, Gavin and Plyler are applied at their prospective-relief scope. The changed-law precedents preserve independent adjudication; Morgan Stanley and Plaut retain their separate, bounded reinstatement holdings.

### 2. The amended statutory findings must actually be adjudicated

**Holding and operative rule:** The movant must establish that covered prospective relief was entered without substantive findings of necessity, narrowness and least intrusiveness under §3626(b)(2); sufficient findings need no ritual statutory wording. If that termination route applies, amended §3626(b)(3) preserves relief only on written, record-based findings that it remains necessary to correct a current **and** ongoing federal-right violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn, and is the least intrusive necessary means.

**Controlling explanation:** Rouse, Benjamin and Gavin supply the amended conditions. The November 26, 1997 amendment applies to pending proceedings; the former current-or-ongoing text addressed by Lewis v. Casey and Plyler is not the present standard. Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail retains its distinct significant-change and suitably tailored modification rule under Rule 60(b)(5), but that threshold is not an extra prerequisite to the enacted termination route. Consent decrees are covered relief. Negotiated protections do not become independent federal substantive rights merely because they appear in a decree. Equally, a necessary corrective remedy may exceed the minimum substantive conduct rule when all required findings support it. Neither the lack of original findings nor a term's breadth alone proves current compliance or requires unconditional termination. Each retained obligation must satisfy the preservation conditions; unsupported future obligations must end. A case in which the amended conjunction alone excludes an actually supported violation protected by the former safeguard remains undecided.

**Precedent treatment:** Rouse's amended-text application controls, as applied in Benjamin and Gavin. Lewis and Plyler retain the actual original-text holdings they made. Rufo remains a distinct equitable route, neither displaced nor added to the statutory prerequisites.

## Holdings

**Authority for Holdings 1–3:** Parts I–III of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt the full, bounded statutory and Article III rationale.

### 1. Conditional prospective termination preserves the judicial task

**Holding and operative rule:** On the Article III objection presented, 18 U.S.C. §3626(b)(2)–(3) validly changes the conditions for continuing prospective prison relief while leaving judges to determine actual violations, statutory eligibility and remedial scope. It neither legislatively finds compliance nor retrospectively annuls completed determinations, payments or other lawful historical effects of the decree.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that consent does not freeze the law governing future judicial supervision. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail v. Rouse, Benjamin v. Jacobson and Gavin v. Branstad apply conditional prospective termination while preserving independent adjudication. Plyler v. Moore supplies the independent statutory route. The strongest contrary argument treats a final consent decree as an irrevocable adjudication of every future duty. That argument mistakes changing prospective law for deciding the original dispute under unchanged law. Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., and United States v. Klein preserve that distinction and the judicial task. Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. and Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. permit bounded reinstatement of specified securities limitations dismissals, supplying neither absolute finality nor unrestricted congressional reopening power. The statutory requirements remain for judges to apply; Michigan's original nonadmission of liability establishes neither current compliance nor absence of a violation. No due-process or equal-protection ground is decided in this Article III review.

**Precedent treatment:** Rouse, Benjamin, Gavin and Plyler are applied at their prospective-relief scope. The changed-law precedents preserve independent adjudication; Morgan Stanley and Plaut retain their separate, bounded reinstatement holdings.

### 2. The amended statutory findings must actually be adjudicated

**Holding and operative rule:** The movant must establish that covered prospective relief was entered without substantive findings of necessity, narrowness and least intrusiveness under §3626(b)(2); sufficient findings need no ritual statutory wording. If that termination route applies, amended §3626(b)(3) preserves relief only on written, record-based findings that it remains necessary to correct a current **and** ongoing federal-right violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn, and is the least intrusive necessary means.

**Controlling explanation:** Rouse, Benjamin and Gavin supply the amended conditions. The November 26, 1997 amendment applies to pending proceedings; the former current-or-ongoing text addressed by Lewis v. Casey and Plyler is not the present standard. Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail retains its distinct significant-change and suitably tailored modification rule under Rule 60(b)(5), but that threshold is not an extra prerequisite to the enacted termination route. Consent decrees are covered relief. Negotiated protections do not become independent federal substantive rights merely because they appear in a decree. Equally, a necessary corrective remedy may exceed the minimum substantive conduct rule when all required findings support it. Neither the lack of original findings nor a term's breadth alone proves current compliance or requires unconditional termination. Each retained obligation must satisfy the preservation conditions; unsupported future obligations must end. A case in which the amended conjunction alone excludes an actually supported violation protected by the former safeguard remains undecided.

**Precedent treatment:** Rouse's amended-text application controls, as applied in Benjamin and Gavin. Lewis and Plyler retain the actual original-text holdings they made. Rufo remains a distinct equitable route, neither displaced nor added to the statutory prerequisites.

### 3. The existing remand supplies the unperformed inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the District Court denied termination solely on constitutional grounds, the Sixth Circuit's reversal and return for the statutory inquiry are affirmed, with further proceedings consistent with the amended conditions. The court must permit the material proof needed to decide those conditions; this judgment orders neither immediate wholesale termination nor indefinite unconditional continuation of the decree.

**Controlling explanation:** Gavin requires the adjudication a constitutional-only denial left undone. Benjamin involved an actual legal refusal to receive relevant current evidence; no identical refusal is established here. Rouse involved substantive evaluation of findings and monitoring reports; those evaluated findings cannot be imported into this case. Ordinary lawful procedure must allow the evidence necessary for the real inquiry, but an unreproduced decree or the possibility of useful additional information creates no freestanding hearing entitlement. The Court decides no current Michigan condition or individual obligation. The automatic-stay issue belongs to separate appeal No. 96-1943; no new stay, expiration or statutory clock follows here. Due process and equal protection, mentioned in the lower court's footnote as grounds not before it, are not enlarged into holdings. Independent contracts, state enforcement, prisoner release, new-relief standards and complete dismissal of the original action are likewise outside this decision.

**Precedent treatment:** Gavin's missing-inquiry rule applies. Benjamin's necessary-proof rule is preserved without importing its factual predicate; Rouse's rejection of a speculative compulsory inquiry remains intact.

## Precedent Treatment

- Inmates of Suffolk County Jail v. Rouse: applies the amended preservation conditions, independent judicial role and prospective/completed-effects distinction; its completed monitoring assessment is not imported.
- Benjamin v. Jacobson: applies the statutory and Article III rules and preserves necessary evidentiary opportunity; its actual refusal-of-proof predicate is absent here.
- Gavin v. Branstad: applies the requirement to perform the inquiry left undone by a constitutional-only denial.
- Plyler v. Moore: retains its independent statutory-route holding under the original text.
- Lewis v. Casey: retains the substantive-findings and scope qualifications of the original statute; its original conjunction is not rewritten.
- Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail: retains significant change and tailored modification for the separate equitable route.
- Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co.; Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.: retain bounded securities-dismissal reinstatement, neither absolute finality nor unrestricted reopening.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society; Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co.; United States v. Klein: preserve genuine prospective legal change with independent judicial adjudication.

## Law After Decision

The amended conditional termination framework governs this decree's future judicial obligations. Actual judicial findings determine retained relief; the statute itself establishes neither compliance nor a current violation. Completed lawful effects remain intact. The decision applies existing law without adding a hearing requirement, general finality rule, due-process or equality holding, or automatic-stay consequence.
