## Holdings

### A clearly offered postpayment remedy cannot be withdrawn after payment

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State clearly holds out a postpayment procedure for recovering unconstitutionally collected taxes, due process forbids denying that opportunity after payment solely because the taxpayer could have pursued an adequate prepayment challenge. The State must afford the promised clear and certain postpayment opportunity, subject to lawful reasonable conditions announced in advance and ordinary proof of payment and amount; neither automatic repayment of every dollar nor a particular equivalent correction is prescribed.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices. Part II's vacatur and remand is supported by the eight Associates; Stone-Zsela would reverse and remand under the same rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Reich answers Florida's stated reason for refusing relief. Newsweek used §215.26 for its 1988–1990 payments. Florida had long allowed constitutional refund claims through that statute, as Hardaway and Osceola recognized. Section 72.011's opportunity to deposit the disputed sum, post a bond or obtain approval for another arrangement did not retrospectively displace that offered route.

Florida distinguishes McKesson because Newsweek could have contested the tax before payment without onerous penalties. Even assuming that prepayment procedure was meaningful, the argument does not answer Reich's independent rule against withdrawing a clearly offered postpayment remedy, including after voluntary payment. The Court need not declare §72.011 inadequate. Fulton, Camps Newfound and Lunding preserve lawful state remedial choices; they do not authorize denial of all adequate correction on the ground rejected here. The existing Florida invalidity ruling supplies the substantive premise, so the Court makes no new First Amendment tax determination. Ordinary preserved conditions and the amount or form of adequate relief remain for remand; no independent default is adjudicated, general government-estoppel rule created or final judgment reopened.
