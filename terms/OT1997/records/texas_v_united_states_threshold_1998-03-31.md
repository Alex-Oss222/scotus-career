## Controlling law of the decision

### A categorical preclearance exemption is unripe before the challenged intervention is sufficiently defined

**Holding and operative rule:** A request to exempt possible future school-district interventions from §5 is unripe when no sufficiently definite intervention, affected district or allocation of powers establishes a fit legal controversy and no substantial present hardship justifies deciding before those details exist. Texas's challenge therefore must be dismissed without deciding whether any particular intervention changes voting or whether a supported federal-funding conflict affects that question.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Abbott Laboratories requires attention to fitness for decision and the hardship of withholding review. The Commissioner has not selected a currently foreseen or likely district for the challenged master or management-team appointments. Under the recited state policy, lesser sanctions would first be tried; even then the Commissioner selects intervention only to the extent necessary and defines the appointees' actual powers. The legal significance of that implementation cannot be resolved through a universal exemption.

Presley requires a direct relation to voting and leaves extreme displacement of elective authority unresolved. Texas law independently prohibits these appointees from acting concerning a district election, changing the board's size or selection method, setting a tax rate, or adopting a budget with a spending level different from the level set by the board. Those limits do not themselves establish every future appointment's effect. The Attorney General's warning that some implementations could require preclearance is not a determination that an identified voting change occurred.

New York permitted review because the future obligation required substantial present preparations; Pic-A-State involved an existing business stopped by a real criminal constraint. Texas shows no comparable present burden. Its earlier Dallas County request and ninety-day approval period do not establish a current appointment, inevitable delay or present voting change. The Court does not require a party to violate the law before challenging a concrete obligation. It requires the controversy here to become sufficiently definite. Neither federal-grant compatibility nor voting coverage is reached, and Raines's rule for individual legislators is not a prohibition on suits by States.

## Event

Texas v. United States, No. 97-29. Argued January 14, 1998; decided March 31, 1998. On appeal from the three-judge United States District Court for the District of Columbia. Texas seeks a declaration that §5 of the Voting Rights Act does not apply to school-district interventions authorized by Texas Education Code §§39.131(a)(7) and (8).
