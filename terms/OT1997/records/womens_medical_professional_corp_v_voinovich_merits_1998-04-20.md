## Event

Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich; Sixth Circuit Nos. 96-3157 and 96-3159. Plenary merits decision, April 20, 1998; October Term 1997, chunk 7. On review of the Sixth Circuit’s final judgment, 130 F.3d 187, entered November 18, 1997. No argument date or separate Supreme Court docket is supplied.

The questions are whether Casey’s abortion-review standard or Salerno’s no-valid-applications formulation governs this facial challenge; whether Ohio’s D&X prohibition fails because of its definition, clinical reach, notice or criminal and civil culpability provisions; and whether postviability law must permit necessary abortion to avert severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury. The Court addresses the complete Casey standard rather than adopting an independent numerical facial threshold.

Ohio’s H.B.135 created an all-stage method prohibition, a separate postviability prohibition, viability-testing requirements and related clinical duties. The district court’s preliminary injunction became permanent on January 12, 1996 after consolidation with trial by stipulation. The Sixth Circuit sustained an injunction against the whole Act, relying on the method definition’s overlap with D&E, clinical-fault concerns, serious mental-health exclusion and inseverability. Those legal conclusions remain subject to review. The lower standing ruling and the State’s failure to contest it in the court of appeals supply no new standing holding here.

Under §2919.17(A), purposeful performance, inducement or attempted performance or inducement of an abortion on a viable fetus is prohibited unless a physician, acting in good faith and using reasonable medical judgment, determines either that abortion is necessary to prevent maternal death or a serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function, or that the fetus is not viable after the assessment required by §2919.18(A). The latter provision requires, beginning with the twenty-second week, a good-faith determination of nonviability using reasonable medical judgment, after examining the patient and performing or arranging the viability tests a reasonable physician would use. Its medical-emergency exception excuses that testing obligation. The separate twenty-four-week viability presumption is rebuttable.

Section 2919.16(F) defines the emergency through the physician’s good-faith, reasonable medical assessment of a pregnancy complication: immediate abortion must be needed to prevent the patient’s death or to avoid a serious risk, created by delay, of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. Section 2919.16(J) separately defines that serious-risk category by a medically diagnosed condition complicating the pregnancy that directly or indirectly causes such impairment. Its nonexclusive examples are preeclampsia, inevitable abortion, prematurely ruptured membranes, diabetes and multiple sclerosis. These enacted categories remain distinct from the constitutional protection and the method-prohibition defenses addressed below.

## Holdings

### 1. The complete Casey framework governs the facial and access claims

**Holding and operative rule.** Casey’s complete six-step standard, as applied in Fargo, governs the challenged previability regulation. Neither Salerno’s no-valid-applications barrier nor an independent numerical large-fraction threshold displaces that inquiry, and failure to establish a substantial obstacle does not end review of remaining severe or meaningful interference.

**Authority.** Part I; Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the governing standard, including its proof allocation and qualifications:

Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

**Burdens and proof.** Challengers establish the duty, population, causation and triggering interference. The State then bears the applicable justification and tailoring or fit burden. This does not alter ordinary proof of disputed historical facts or the enacted burden for a separate statutory defense. No State substantiation burden arises merely because a law concerns abortion.

**Controlling explanation.** Casey supplies the complete sequence and distinguishes protected choice from permissible regulation. Fargo applies it to an abortion-access challenge while separating clinical notice, actual fault and substantive care. The Sixth Circuit’s facial formula cannot substitute for this inquiry. Ohio’s law must be tested through its actual commands, exceptions, sanctions and practical operation. Overlap with a commonly used procedure can establish a real interference question without alone proving every application unconstitutional. Conversely, patients need not show that no one can comply before obtaining protection for a supported violation. The Court changes neither Casey’s framework nor its allocation of proof. Salerno supplies no independent barrier in this setting; its operation elsewhere is not decided. The final method applications and corresponding breadth of relief remain for the separate inquiry below.

### 2. An act-defined method and professional clinical criteria are not categorically unintelligible

**Holding and operative rule.** Ohio’s method definition and specified good-faith, reasonable-medical-judgment criteria cannot be invalidated on the categorical ground that statutory and medical procedure labels overlap or that every subsidiary clinical judgment lacks an express culpability term. Courts must examine notice and enforcement guidance, actual fault for the relevant element, criminal and civil liability, and substantive access as distinct questions; this holding neither supplies a universal fault rule nor validates every enforcement.

**Authority.** Part II; Souter and all seven other Associates. Stone-Zsela dissents.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 2919.15 defines the prohibited act through purposeful insertion of a suction device into the fetal skull to remove the brain, expressly excluding suction curettage and suction aspiration; subsection (B) addresses knowingly performing or attempting that act. Purposeful skull suction can occur during a procedure medically called D&E without making the statutory description unintelligible. The published testimony concerns purposeful conduct in some D&E procedures, not accidental contact or every D&E. Its substantive significance requires the separate analysis in Part IV.

The postviability provisions identify outcomes, good faith and reasonable medical judgment. Fargo separates an intelligible prospective clinical standard from unsettled fault concerning error. Vuitch supports professional judgment without diagnostic certainty; Colautti requires separate scrutiny of the actual trigger and culpability. X-Citement Video supplies contextual interpretation, not knowledge of illegality as a universal element. Reasonable later medical disagreement alone does not prove every element, but neither professional immunity nor a bad-faith-only safe harbor follows. No Ohio default culpability rule, concrete prosecution, civil award or materially different authoritative construction is decided. An intelligible exception may still fail to protect necessary care.

### 3. Postviability prohibition must preserve the presented necessary mental-health care

**Holding and operative rule.** A State may prohibit abortion after viability, but it must permit abortion necessary in competent individualized medical judgment to avert grave, medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury. The patient need not await catastrophe; transient distress, unsupported assertion or mere preference does not establish the protected necessity.

**Authority.** Part III.A; Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct joins. Part III.B’s particular construction and relief directions receive the same joins except Stone-Zsela’s: six direct joins.

**Controlling explanation.** Casey expressly retains physical-and-mental-health protection at the viability boundary. Doe supports individualized clinical judgment, while Fargo distinguishes a term’s intelligibility from the adequacy of the care it permits. The necessary-care protection therefore does not turn on whether Ohio’s emergency language is understandable. Pennsylvania’s emergency-delay construction does not automatically validate Ohio’s broader postviability prohibition, and Pennsylvania’s separate harmful-disclosure defense supplies no Ohio exception. The injunction is affirmed insofar as it prevents exclusion of the serious necessary care presented here.

Under Part III.B, the lower court must examine whether the actual words of §§2919.17(A) and 2919.16(F)/(J), and genuinely dependent commands, permit a protective construction fairly supported by Ohio law. If they do not, the restriction remains unenforceable to the extent of the established constitutional violation. The court may neither insert an unlimited new exception nor presume that every protective construction is impossible. The holding preserves competent medical support, individualized circumstances and the distinction between necessary care and a physician’s unsupported preference. It does not invalidate every postviability duty.

### 4. The method prohibition requires application to actual conduct, care and alternatives

**Holding and operative rule.** The final method-ban disposition must be vacated where categorical notice reasoning or the displaced facial formula substituted for Casey’s substantive inquiry. On remand, the lower court must assess actual affected applications, attributable interference, usable alternative care and the distinct statutory defenses before deciding invalidity and matching relief; previability and postviability applications remain distinct.

**Authority.** Part IV; Souter, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins.

**Controlling explanation.** Casey and Fargo require a supported assessment of what the enacted duty does. The evidence that purposeful skull suction occurs during some D&E establishes a real scope and clinical-interference issue. It does not alone determine the magnitude of interference, affected applications, practical alternatives or final remedy. The prohibition’s criminal defense first requires the physician to make a prima facie showing that every available alternative would pose greater health risk; the prosecutor must then prove beyond a reasonable doubt that at least one available alternative would not. The civil affirmative defense is separate and receives no imported criminal burden shift.

The lower court must determine whether these protections permit necessary care in practice and whether demonstrated additional interference survives the applicable substantive protection. Alternatives count only if usable for the patient and clinical circumstances. Failure to prove D&X universally superior does not settle necessity for a supported subgroup, while bare clinical preference or an assumption that every D&E is prohibited cannot establish invalidity. The remand requires the identified substantive application; it orders no new evidentiary hearing or trial absent a demonstrated need. No final determination of every method-ban application or element-specific Ohio liability follows.

### 5. Relief must follow Ohio severability law and the component judgments

**Holding and operative rule.** Independently operable provisions and applications must receive Ohio’s state-law severability analysis; invalidity of one command does not itself eliminate the Act. The lower court must conform relief to the affirmed necessary-care protection, determine the unresolved applications and dependent duties separately, and promptly decide interim protection under ordinary equitable standards without automatic blanket enforcement or permanent blanket invalidation.

**Authority.** Part V.A’s ordinary severability principles receive all eight Associates’ direct joins. Part V.B’s full remaining mandate receives Souter, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer; Part III.B separately supplies the six-Justice health-linked construction directions. Stone’s decision to leave precise relief and severability below does not add a join in these particular analyses.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 1.50 preserves provisions and applications that can operate without the invalid part. Geiger requires attention to independent operation, legislative intention and whether separation requires inserting terms; Maurer bars severance that fundamentally disrupts the scheme. Leavitt requires this state-law inquiry while preserving independently justified constitutional protection. Its distinctive Utah independent-enactment clause cannot settle Ohio’s statute.

The lower court must keep these commands separate: viability testing begins at the twenty-second week, subject to its medical-emergency exception; viability is rebuttably presumed at twenty-four weeks, not constitutionally fixed then. Section 2919.17(A) permits the necessity or nonviability determination under the actual terms described above. The five postviability duties are the attending physician’s written necessity certification; a second physician’s written certification after reviewing records and tests; a facility with access to neonatal services; a survival-favoring method unless it creates significantly greater maternal death risk or serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function; and a second physician present to care for the fetus. Their emergency exemption permits noncompliance when its terms are satisfied; it does not require omission. The method-choice exception is distinct from the alternative-method affirmative defense.

Sections 2919.16(F)/(J)’s immediate-or-delay risk and medically diagnosed substantial irreversible impairment remain subject to the necessary-care holding. Felony, misdemeanor and civil channels, dependent certifications and other duties require separate treatment; the patient is not criminally liable under the method and postviability prohibitions. No reporting, testing, certification or damages provision is automatically sustained or eliminated. No liability award, fixed constitutional week or new criminal element follows. Interim protection for unresolved applications requires prompt lower-court consideration, not automatic dissolution before that determination, an unnecessary new hearing or permanent approval of the whole injunction.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992):** Applied without changing its complete six-step rule, proof allocation, individualized viability, physical-and-mental-health baseline or tailored-relief requirement. Pennsylvania’s emergency-delay construction and distinct information-omission defense do not resolve Ohio’s separate commands.
- **Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer, February 13, 1995:** Applied to preserve complete access review, separate intelligibility from actual fault and substantive necessary-care protection, and require supported application and ordinary interim-relief consideration. No categorical express-scienter requirement is derived from it.
- **United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987):** Its all-applications formulation does not independently bar the Casey challenge; its operation outside this setting remains unaddressed.
- **Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health, 497 U.S. 502 (1990):** Its facial-review formulation remains subject to Casey’s tailored-relief qualification; it supplies no independent numerical or no-valid-applications threshold here.
- **United States v. Vuitch, 402 U.S. 62 (1971):** Applied for the intelligibility of professional clinical judgment, without importing the D.C. statute’s construction or prosecution burden into Ohio law.
- **Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979):** Applied to require independent attention to the actual statutory trigger and culpability for clinical error; it supplies no universal express fault level or automatic invalidity.
- **United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64 (1994):** Applied for contextual attention to definition and culpability; its federal construction does not create an Ohio knowledge-of-illegality rule or cure every notice defect.
- **Doe v. Bolton, 410 U.S. 179 (1973):** Applied for individualized clinical judgment and relevant physical and mental health; Casey’s retained constitutional baseline, rather than an unlimited reading of Doe’s statutory discussion, supplies the necessary-care rule.
- **Leavitt v. Jane L., June 17, 1996:** Applied to require actual state-law severability and preservation of independently supported federal protection; its Utah independent-enactment provision is distinguished and supplies no conclusion about Ohio’s entire Act.
- **Geiger v. Geiger, 117 Ohio St. 451, 160 N.E. 28 (1927), and State ex rel. Maurer v. Sheward, 71 Ohio St.3d 513, 644 N.E.2d 369 (1994):** Applied as Ohio’s severability authorities, requiring separability, fidelity to legislative intent and avoidance of judicial insertion or fundamental disruption. No final state-law severance is decided.

## Law After Decision

The complete Casey framework continues unchanged, with no independent numerical facial threshold or Salerno barrier. The Court applies the existing necessary-care baseline to severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury after viability. That controlling protection is distinct from a claim that every distress, preference or procedure choice is protected necessity.

Overlap between a statutory act and a medical procedure label does not itself establish unconstitutional vagueness. Clinical intelligibility, actual culpability and substantive access remain separate inquiries; no universal Ohio fault rule or final liability determination is adopted. The method prohibition’s ultimate applications and the breadth of final relief remain unresolved. Ohio severability and the specified interim directions govern further proceedings. No separate opinion changes these rules, and no later case or event is decided.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** Stone joins the complete Casey/Fargo standard and the required protection for grave, medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury. He rejects both Salerno’s independent barrier and an independent numerical facial threshold. He would affirm invalidity of the D&X prohibition and deficient penal medical exceptions now. In his application, the definition reaches the predominant D&E method, and courts cannot cure that reach by rewriting the definition. Intentional performance supplies no adequate culpability concerning an erroneous clinical-necessity determination; an affirmative defense does not cure the initial criminal and civil exposure. He therefore opposes the categorical-notice vacatur and the Court’s decision to leave method invalidity for further substantive application. His application does not command the Court.

Stone leaves severability and precise relief to the lower court without reopening his invalidity holdings. Ohio may regulate a genuinely distinct procedure, prohibit postviability abortion outside necessary life-and-health circumstances, punish bad-faith or sufficiently culpable violations, and enforce evidence-based safety rules. His health protection excludes transient distress and unsupported assertion and creates no unrestricted physician veto. His agreement that relief belongs below does not join the Court’s particular saving-construction analysis or its interim-only treatment of unresolved method applications.

**Justice Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** Kennedy joins the actual-wording notice holding, serious necessary mental-health protection, its lawful protective-construction inquiry and ordinary Ohio severability principles. His retained Casey protected-choice baseline and Fargo separate position distinguish the substantive adequacy of care from the intelligibility of a rule. A method-specific health defense cannot cure a separate prohibition that excludes necessary mental-health care.

He would reverse the previability method injunction under his reasonable-regulation approach. The act-defined restriction, actual alternative procedures and enacted health defense defeat this categorical challenge absent an established denial of protected choice or necessary care. He rejects the general residual lesser-burden substantiation rule and therefore does not join the six-step method remand or continued unresolved-method protection under that mandate. He neither requires failure in every application nor finds every alternative equally safe. Supported necessary-care applications remain protected; he does not approve the entire Act. His narrower regulatory approach supplies no substitute for the Court’s controlling framework.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Thomas.** Scalia and Thomas join the bounded notice/fault holding and ordinary Ohio severability principles. Their Fargo position supports attention to the purposeful act and specified professional criteria, without a universal scienter cure, a bad-faith-only safe harbor or invented criminal and civil elements.

They would reverse the constitutional injunction under their continuing objection to Casey’s heightened abortion framework and their ordinary reasonable-regulation approach. They dissent from the required constitutional mental-health protection as well as the method remand. They acknowledge that Casey’s actual necessary-care rule is the controlling premise from which they dissent; they do not redescribe it as merely Doe’s statutory construction or a numerical test. Their position neither supplies a different statutory fact nor establishes professional immunity. Agreement on notice and severability adds no vote to their proposed reversal or to the Court’s health and method-remand rationales.
