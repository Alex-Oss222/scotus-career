### I. The MDL transferee cannot defeat mandatory return through self-transfer

**Holding and operative rule:** Unless the transferred action has previously terminated, §1407(a) requires the Panel to remand it to the original district at or before completion of coordinated pretrial proceedings. Section 1404(a) does not authorize the transferee court to retain that continuing action for trial by transferring it from itself to itself.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by all nine Justices and supplies direct statutory authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1407 authorizes coordination for pretrial proceedings and commands return of each continuing action. The Panel may separate and remand particular claims earlier; an action that has actually terminated needs no return. Changing the asserted venue basis does not terminate the action. Addressing the command to the Panel does not authorize the transferee court to prevent its performance. Section 1404(a)’s general permission to transfer to another eligible district or division coexists with, rather than defeats, this specific temporary allocation. Section 1407(f) permits only rules consistent with Acts of Congress, so the cited self-assignment practice cannot alter the return obligation. Subsection (h)’s specific Clayton Act trial-transfer authorization does not confer that power in this tort action. The investment in coordinated pretrial work is substantial, but efficiency cannot delete the enacted sequence. A later lawful transfer by the proper court and consent or waiver questions not presented remain open.

### II. The preserved trial-forum error requires correction of this judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** A party that preserved its §1407 return objection need not prove that a different jury would have reached a different verdict to obtain correction of the resulting unauthorized trial forum. This uncured violation requires setting aside the defamation trial judgment and enforcing statutory remand.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by all nine Justices; its remedy follows the mandatory return right asserted here.

**Controlling explanation:** Lexecon repeatedly requested remand, sought mandamus and then pursued the promised direct appeal. The possibility that Arizona could originally have been a proper venue does not supply the procedure Congress required after an MDL transfer. Caterpillar preserves a completed judgment when the jurisdictional defect actually disappeared before trial; the prohibited retention for this trial never received an equivalent cure. Good Real Property rejects inventing dismissal of a substantive action as a sanction for administrative delay. This case instead directly enforces a particular allocation of the trial forum without extinguishing the cause of action. No constitutional or subject-matter-jurisdiction label is necessary, and the Court adopts no universal rule that every procedural error defeats a completed judgment. The statutory return must occur before any independently lawful later transfer; no permanent right against such a transfer is adjudicated.

### III. Authorized pretrial work and unreviewed tort issues remain separate

**Holding and operative rule:** The improper self-transfer does not automatically invalidate decisions made within authorized pretrial proceedings or decide the merits of the underlying tort claims. Properly remaining appellate challenges must be addressed in their own posture; the unpreserved request for prospective application does not alter this disposition.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees with these dispositions without joining an additional rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The malicious-prosecution and abuse-of-process rulings and the Cotchett Rule 54(b) judgment are pretrial dispositions, not the defamation trial verdict. Lexecon disputed the Rule 54(b) exercise of discretion without denying power to enter an authorized pretrial judgment. Those distinctions prevent wholesale reinstatement of every claim merely because trial retention was unlawful. The argument that the defamation claim should independently have been dismissed before trial remains for proper consideration, not a new factual or tort-law ruling here. The prospectivity request was not presented below or in opposition to review and supplies no basis for a different result in this case. No comprehensive retroactivity doctrine, new waiver finding or adjudication of a later transfer is adopted.
