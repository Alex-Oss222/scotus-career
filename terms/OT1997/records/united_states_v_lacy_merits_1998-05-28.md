## Holdings

### 1. Actual acquisition and supported retention sustain probable cause over this interval

**Holding and operative rule:** A ten-month interval does not defeat probable cause to search for downloaded child-pornography files when the affidavit establishes actual acquisition, durable electronic storage and a supported likelihood of continued retention. On this affidavit, those facts gave the issuing magistrate a substantial basis to find a present fair probability that the material remained at Lacy's apartment; neither elapsed time alone nor a generalized collector profile decides the issue.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion for all nine Justices controls the complete rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** Sgro requires facts sufficiently related to issuance to support a present probability. Gates asks for a practical assessment of the total circumstances and substantial-basis review of the issuing magistrate. Ornelas preserves that institutional distinction from appellate review of a warrantless search.

The affidavit described completed downloads, not a subscription, inquiry or attempted order. BAMSE records identified the number and downloaded files, and telephone records linked matching calls to the apartment. At least two specifically described files supplied acquisition evidence. The experienced agent explained that possessors commonly value such material and retain it for long periods in secure places, often at home. That account had an individual foundation here: the corroborated receipt of durable files.

The competing argument correctly warns that generalized retention habits cannot make a home perpetually searchable. This result depends on the established acquisition, the property involved and this ten-month interval. The Court assumes neither permanent possession nor an ongoing distribution business. An affidavit resting only on generalized habits without actual acquisition presents a different question. Subsequent discoveries and trial admissions supply no retrospective justification.

### 2. The reported system and records categories are sufficiently particular

**Holding and operative rule:** This warrant adequately confines the equipment, storage-media and related-record search because probable cause ties the apartment system to the downloaded material, the affidavit explains the storage and retrieval uncertainty, and the authorization supplies objective offense-related document categories, including the BAMSE connection. The ruling permits neither a general inventory of unrelated data nor unrestricted examination of everything later found in a seized device.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls this independently resolved particularity question.

**Controlling explanation:** Andresen and Coolidge require limits that prevent a general exploratory search. The necessary specificity depends on the supported offense, the objects sought and information reasonably available at issuance. Inability to name hardware more precisely cannot justify unrelated categories, but a warrant need not supply knowledge investigators could not reasonably possess.

Here the reported affidavit tied the system to actual downloads and explained why investigators could not tell whether the files were on the hard drive or disks or what equipment and software accurate retrieval would require. The authorization's objective records limits answered the separate risk of taking unrelated papers. The circuit's contrast with the unrestricted all-records warrant in Kow is persuasive on those actual differences; its three considerations are not adopted as a new mandatory national checklist.

Pinson and Myers protect concealed domestic information through lawful authority and do not create a digital exception. No antecedent unauthorized thermal or comparable search is established here. The Court decides the sufficiency of this warrant's reported categories, not an unpreserved execution challenge, an unquoted clause, incorporation of a separate instrument or every later examination after seizure. It prescribes no forensic protocol and decides no remote account, cloud or other technologically different search.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Sgro v. United States, 287 U.S. 206:** Applied to require a present probable-cause basis, with time assessed in relation to the evidence's nature and supported retention.
- **Illinois v. Gates, 462 U.S. 213:** Applied for practical totality and substantial-basis review of the issuing magistrate, without retrospective supplementation.
- **Ornelas v. United States, May 28, 1996:** Its separate nonwarrant appellate rule is preserved; it does not replace Gates review of warrant issuance.
- **Andresen v. Maryland, 427 U.S. 463:** Applied to objective offense-related limits that prevent an exploratory records search.
- **Coolidge v. New Hampshire, 403 U.S. 443:** Applied to the prohibition on general searches, without making investigative uncertainty permission to take unrelated material.
- **United States v. Pinson, May 8, 1995:** Its protection of concealed home information remains; it neither invalidates an adequately supported warrant nor licenses unrelated digital examination.
- **United States v. Myers, June 11, 1996:** Its distinction between authority for an intrusion and derivative remedies remains. No earlier unauthorized intrusion is found in this case.
- **United States v. Leon, 468 U.S. 897:** Not reached. No good-faith exception or reliance finding supplies an alternative holding.
