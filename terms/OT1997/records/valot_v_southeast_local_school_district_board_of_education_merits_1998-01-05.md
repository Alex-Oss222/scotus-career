## Holdings

**1. These lawful unemployment-benefit petitions are protected despite their private financial subject.** A public employer may not refuse continued employment solely because an employee lawfully and non-shamly invokes this established governmental unemployment-benefit mechanism and thereby imposes the benefit cost prescribed by law. That protection does not require public-concern content. Parts I and IV command six votes: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Perry separates lack of an employment entitlement from punishment for protected conduct. California Motor Transport supplies the protection of access to governmental redress, and Smith v. Arkansas State Highway Employees distinguishes protection against retaliation from an affirmative obligation to accept a claimant's demands. Mt. Healthy, Umbehr and O'Hare require identification of the particular activity and its causal role. The formal external benefits proceeding here is not merely an informal workplace complaint. Connick's public-concern limit for employee speech does not defeat this particular invocation claim. The Court does not decide that every personal request or internal grievance escapes employment doctrine, and success is not a general condition of petition protection.

The Board admitted that applications and benefit receipts caused nonrenewal. Its dollar-for-dollar expense was real, but was the lawful cost of the very invocation it punished; no separate established service disruption or fraud supplies the asserted justification. The later reasonable-assurance practice cannot prove summer 1993 assurance or ineligibility. Government may contest eligibility, reject fraudulent or ineligible claims, enforce neutral filing requirements, lawfully change prospective terms and act for independent job-related reasons. The ruling neither freezes the benefit program nor treats all operational costs as irrelevant.

**2. The independent equality and substantive-due-process claims fail.** A classification based on known benefit applications has a rational relationship to possible recurring fiscal costs when considered independently of any protected-right penalty. This record also establishes no distinct fundamental entitlement to renewal or substantive-due-process deprivation. Part II has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in these judgments.

Beach permits a conceivable legitimate basis for ordinary economic distinctions; Heller and Romer require attention to the actual disadvantage and preserve limits on irrational classifications. That ordinary fiscal conclusion does not resolve the separate protected-right theory or prove a Mt. Healthy same-decision defense. Collins distinguishes voluntary employment and state-law expectations from an actual federal deprivation. Annual contracts and an expected recommendation did not guarantee renewal. Plaintiffs expressly confined their due-process claim to substance, so the Court does not revive a procedural claim. Albright's fractured judgment supplies no universal specific-source or state-remedy bar.

**3. The access and protected-right equality branches require reconsideration.** Successful receipt of benefits does not defeat a claim that employment was withheld for invoking the process. Count 2 therefore returns within the protected-invocation rule, without creation of a freestanding general access entitlement. The rights-dependent part of count 3 likewise returns because its categorical no-protected-right premise is incorrect. Part III has Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The equality remand neither selects automatic strict scrutiny nor establishes independent liability. The ordinary fiscal classification and a penalty on protected conduct require distinct treatment. The Court preserves actual elements, defenses and relief requirements rather than treating the petition holding as judgment on every alternative constitutional theory.

**4. Personal liability and relief require their own predicates.** The case returns for lawful adjudication of causation, individual responsibility, municipal attribution, immunity, injury and remedy. The Board admission establishes its stated reason, not every individual's participation or all conditions of relief. The superintendent's favorable recommendation does not become an adverse vote. Parts I and IV have the six-Justice coalition identified above.

Crawford-El preserves competent direct and circumstantial proof under ordinary burdens. Mt. Healthy requires protected activity and a substantial or motivating role; an actual same-decision defense must be proved by a preponderance, rather than supplied by a conceivable lawful reason. Harlow and Anderson preserve particularized notice at the conduct date; Elder requires all relevant earlier authority and permits a dispositive legal defense without a mandatory merits-first sequence. For the August 16, 1993 refusal, later San Filippo, Umbehr, O'Hare, Crawford-El, Jeffries, Clanton and today's decision cannot provide retrospective notice. The Court grants or denies no ultimate personal immunity and guarantees no discovery or trial.

**5. State-claim discretion must be reconsidered on the corrected federal disposition.** Section 1367(c)(3) permits declining related state claims after all original claims are dismissed; it neither removes supplemental power nor commands nonretention. Because federal claims survive the categorical grounds used below, the Ohio claim's nonretention on that basis is vacated for lawful reconsideration. The five-Justice Part III coalition adopts this holding.

Chicago distinguishes power from discretion. The Court does not decide the Ohio claim's merits, compel retention or foreclose another valid nonretention ground whose actual predicates exist.
