## Holdings

### Possession of prohibited post-1986 machinegun stock

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(o) may reach possession of a completed machinegun within the prohibited post-May 19, 1986 civilian stock without proof of a defendant-specific interstate or commercial transaction, because the possession prohibition is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of Congress's valid national restriction on additions to that stock. The Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause supplies that class-and-regime connection; no additional transaction-specific interstate element is required.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule, its application and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Hale's possession rule does not disappear when a particular transfer cannot be proved. Local conversion creates completed prohibited stock that can be retained, concealed, acquired and kept available for circulation. Excluding that possession would materially impair the stock restriction itself. Rybar applied the same rule where gun-show sales made the market connection concrete; its facts did not convert a proved sale into a universal constitutional prerequisite. Kirk's possession conviction therefore stands without treating the reported sales as admitted elements or reviving dismissed transfer charges.

Lopez requires an identified interstate mechanism before aggregation can measure its magnitude. Here the mechanism is the specific transfer-and-stock regime, its grandfathered lawful stock, and the fitted possession prohibition. It is not dangerousness, national marketability, remote interstate movement, generalized gun crime or downstream productivity. Nor does administrative convenience alone establish necessity. A particular interstate element may supply a connection in another statute, but courts need not insert one where this valid regime supplies the connection. Formal findings are not universally required and cannot create power by recital; the identified enacted structure supplies the necessary relationship here.

**Operative exceptions and limits:** Section 922(o)(2) preserves transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. It separately preserves lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986; earlier manufacture alone is insufficient. Neither exception is established for Count One. The holding establishes no general federal firearms police power, no rule for every locally manufactured object, and no automatic justification for every product-market possession prohibition. It decides neither Second Amendment protection nor every conversion-parts, readily-restorable or wear-induced application of the machinegun definition.

## Law After Decision

Hale's existing possession authority includes this locally converted completed machinegun even without transaction-specific interstate proof. The decision clarifies that application rather than adding a new Commerce category. It leaves ordinary firearm possession, other statutory applications and independent constitutional challenges subject to their own rules.
