## Holdings

**An appellate court has no mandatory duty to raise an omitted state procedural-default defense.** Procedural default is not a defect in federal subject-matter jurisdiction. Stevens's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls this no-duty rule and its bounded application. It does not decide whether a court may raise the defense, when it could properly do so, or whether discretionary use would be permissible here.

Lambrix identifies default and nonretroactivity as distinct nonjurisdictional limits without an inflexible sequence. Its remand concerned a preserved defense, not a duty to discover an omitted one. Classification alone does not settle every initiative question, but no independent statutory or precedential command requires initiative here. Granberry shows that comity and federalism do not themselves require automatic appellate dismissal for omitted exhaustion. That analogy distinguishes institutional interests from compulsion; exhaustion and default remain different doctrines.

The Fifth Circuit treated predicted Mississippi untimeliness as compelling exclusion. The parties had not discussed the Sones ground it invoked. Affirming on a discretionary basis it did not express would replace its actual reasoning and assume undeveloped predicates. State finality interests may bear on permissible discretion; they do not establish this mandatory obligation.

Coleman's adequate-independent-ground requirements and applicable exceptions remain intact. Neither the State's omission nor this record establishes intentional waiver, automatic forfeiture, valid default, cause and prejudice or an innocence gateway. Under Lindh, the covered chapter 153 amendments do not govern this September 30, 1991 noncapital application; amended §2254(b)(3)'s exhaustion-waiver provision supplies no default rule here. Separately directed provisions retain their scope.

The predicate pleas' constitutional validity and the route for challenging expired convictions remain reserved. Any lawful later consideration must address the actual ground with an appropriate opportunity to respond; no universal constitutional requirement of additional briefing on every unargued issue is announced. No habeas relief follows from the no-duty holding itself.
