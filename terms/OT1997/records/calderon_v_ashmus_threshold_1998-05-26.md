## Event

Calderon v. Ashmus, No. 97-391. Article III threshold decision, May 26, 1998. Argued March 24, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 123 F.3d 1199. The court of appeals affirmed a preliminary injunction barring California officials from invoking chapter 154's special capital-habeas procedures against the prisoner class, with a clarification tied to current state law. Review was granted on Eleventh Amendment and First Amendment questions. The Court resolves the antecedent Article III question and does not reach those granted issues.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded for jurisdictional dismissal, 9–0.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The declaration that California is unqualified and the injunction preventing officials from asserting chapter 154 cannot remain in force. ## Opinion Topology

## Holdings

**Holding and operative rule:** A prisoner class may not obtain a standalone declaration selecting chapter 154 as inapplicable to future habeas litigation when that declaration would settle only an isolated procedural issue and would resolve neither entitlement to habeas relief nor a complete independent legal claim. This action lacks an Article III controversy and must be dismissed; chapter 154's applicability may be determined when properly presented in a concrete habeas proceeding.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion for all nine Justices controls the rule, its application and jurisdictional remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Declaratory Judgment Act supplies a remedy within an actual controversy, not authority to obtain advance answers to every legal issue that may affect another suit. Aetna permits a conclusive determination of adverse legal relations. Seminole likewise recognizes judicial power to determine an existing statutory duty conclusively even when mediation or executive steps follow. Neither an immediate coercive remedy nor the absence of later proceedings is indispensable.

This complaint does something different. It attempts to settle one procedural feature of the prisoners' eventual custody challenges, leaving the actual habeas controversies to be brought and decided elsewhere. Coffman and Wycoff require attention to that incompleteness rather than merely the importance of the desired advance answer. California's asserted eligibility and the shorter filing regime create serious practical concerns, especially given the reported delays in appointing counsel. But the usefulness of resolving a prospective litigation issue does not make that issue a complete independent legal relation suitable for this declaration. Texas and Ohio Forestry preserve anticipatory adjudication of a sufficiently concrete dispute; they do not dispense with that requirement.

The qualification issue belongs in an actual habeas case with the applicable statutory predicates before the court. The Court decides no individual clock, counsel-system deficiency or claim default. It reaches neither the Eleventh Amendment nor the First Amendment objections, California's actual qualification, underlying habeas merits, §1983 liability or fees. Dismissal does not depend on a categorical objection to declaratory relief or pre-enforcement review.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Aetna Life Insurance Co. v. Haworth, 300 U.S. 227:** Applied for a definite controversy and conclusive declaratory resolution of adverse legal relations.
- **Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, March 27, 1996:** Distinguished because its binding adjudication determines an existing statutory negotiation duty; its rule permitting conclusive declarations despite later statutory steps remains intact.
- **Coffman v. Breeze Corporations, 323 U.S. 316:** Applied to the attempted advance determination of an isolated issue rather than a complete controversy.
- **Public Service Commission v. Wycoff Co., 344 U.S. 237:** Applied to distinguish a conclusive legal relation from a separated issue expected to arise in later litigation.
- **Texas v. United States, March 31, 1998:** Preserved; actual fitness and present hardship govern anticipatory review, without making strategic importance alone sufficient.
- **Ohio Forestry Association v. Sierra Club, May 18, 1998:** Preserved at its concrete-dispute and present-hardship boundary; it does not decide this standalone habeas-procedure action.
- **Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co., June 2, 1997:** Applied in resolving the actual Article III defect before contested merits here, without an exhaustive rule for all threshold sequences.

## Separate Writings

Breyer, joined by Souter, emphasizes a possible route to prompt determination in actual habeas litigation. A timely petition may present an amendment question for which chapter 154's applicability matters; the ordinary amendment rules and the more restrictive chapter-154 rules remain distinct. An appropriate ruling may also permit an interlocutory appeal under §1292(b), but only if the district court states in writing that there is a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion and a prospect of materially advancing the litigation's termination, and the court of appeals permits an application made within ten days. An application does not itself stay proceedings. These possibilities promise no amendment, certification, appeal, exhaustion determination or additional filing time for any prisoner. They explain a concrete adjudicative route without treating convenience or aggregate efficiency as Article III jurisdiction.
