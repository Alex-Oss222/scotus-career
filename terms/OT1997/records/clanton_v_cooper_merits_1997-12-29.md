## Holdings

**1. Immediate review separates legal immunity from historical facts.** An interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal may resolve a separable legal defense on the proper facts assumed in the nonmovant's favor, but may not decide factual sufficiency, participation, credibility or which disputed account is true. Ultimate constitutional voluntariness is not wholly historical merely because its test considers circumstances. Part I is unanimous.

Johnson and Behrens protect the assumed historical account without foreclosing genuine legal review. Section 1254 permits this Court to review the circuit case but does not enlarge the circuit's §1291 authority. Miller distinguishes the ultimate voluntariness question from historical facts; Ornelas preserves task-specific allocation rather than a universal rule for mixed questions. A court therefore cannot avoid a legal immunity question by labeling the entire coercion inquiry factual, or obtain factual review by attaching a legal label. The factual portions of Cooper's appeal must be dismissed; the distinct reviewable questions remain.

**2. The assumed material warrant deception defeats the asserted legal immunity defense.** On the proper assumed facts, an officer cannot obtain immunity for knowing or reckless factual falsehoods material to probable cause merely because a judge issued the warrant. Franks and Malley supplied the relevant pre-August-1993 rule. Part II-A's complete rule has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela additionally joins its application to the assumed deliberate material falsehoods and its subsidiary-theory limits.

The rule requires actual culpable falsity, personal responsibility and materiality. A truthful corrected account may still support probable cause, and a later recantation does not itself prove that the affiant knowingly fabricated evidence. Judicial issuance does not categorically sever the responsibility of an officer whose material deception procured the warrant. Cooper may contest the facts at the proper stage, but cannot secure interlocutory reversal by substituting his account for Clanton's. The Court does not find what the affidavit actually contained or place Bobby's derivative statement into it as independent probable cause.

The three subsidiary theories remain rejected: sufficiently reliable hearsay may support probable cause; the Fourth Amendment does not universally require filed criminal charges before a warrant; and the federal grand-jury indictment requirement does not govern state prosecutions. These propositions establish neither universal reliability of accomplice statements nor federal liability from a state-law warrant defect.

**3. Deliberate material detention deception does not require a prior case using the same communication system.** The asserted categorical immunity defense fails on the assumed personal communication of deliberate material homicide and no-bond falsehoods to procure otherwise unjustified additional unreasonable physical detention actually caused by that message. The established Franks/Malley prohibition on material official deception has an obvious application to this particular seizure; electronic transmission and a different custodian do not require a factually identical earlier NCIC case. Part II-B is unanimous.

The arrest under the original warrant and the later message's alleged extension of custody are distinct events. The Oklahoma warrant's $5,000 bond provision does not itself establish a federal release entitlement, and Texas's authority to set bond does not prove that the same detention would independently have occurred. The claim requires falsehood, culpability, materiality, attributable causation and an actual unreasonable physical seizure. An inaccurate message, bad motive, state-law breach or passage of one to three days alone is insufficient. If the same lawful detention would have occurred without the message, or Cooper had no material causal role, the pertinent defense remains available. Ordinary factual resolution must answer those questions; the Court decides no general duration of Fourth Amendment coverage through every stage of a prosecution.

**4. The independent coerced-confession theory requires proper legal review and conduct-date notice analysis.** The appellate disposition of the distinct third-party-confession branch is vacated because treating ultimate constitutional coercion as wholly outside interlocutory review fails to separate the historical assumptions from the legal defense. On remand the court must identify Clanton's own protected interest, the particular use and legal voluntariness of the statement, and whether the claimed violation was clearly established at the relevant 1993 conduct date. Part III has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela dissents from this disposition.

The Tenth Circuit did discuss Clanton's own due-process interest and notice. The defect is its overbroad treatment of the ultimate coercion conclusion, not total omission of those subjects. A claim involving an involuntary but not knowingly false statement is distinct from the preserved knowing-deception claims. Michael's personal self-incrimination privilege cannot be borrowed, but that does not categorically eliminate an independent injury to Clanton's liberty. Nor does Albright establish a universal Fourth Amendment displacement rule or a general state-remedy bar: its incompatible rationales supplied no controlling synthesis.

Jackson, Blackburn, Spano, Haynes and Fulminante supply the received voluntariness background. Frazier does not make an evidence lie conclusive, and Hutto does not make every promised benefit coercion. The alleged threats, promised leniency, suggested narrative and asserted physical evidence must be assessed together under the appropriate totality rule. The earlier third-party trial-use cases identified below, including LaFrance, Merkt, Chiavola and Bradford, do not automatically establish a particular pretrial-detention damages right in August 1993; their actual holdings and relevant circuit law must be evaluated. An identical factual precedent is unnecessary for obvious coverage, but an abstract liberty principle or bad intent cannot resolve a genuinely unsettled extension. No ultimate coercion, independent due-process right, clearly established violation or immunity is decided here.

**5. Existing proof and immunity rules remain distinct from ultimate liability.** Harlow and Anderson preserve objective, particularized conduct-date immunity. Elder requires consideration of all relevant pre-existing law on the preserved issue; later adjudications cannot retroactively provide notice, and no mandatory merits-first sequence prevents use of a genuinely dispositive notice defense. The eight-Associate opinion adopts these limits throughout; Stone-Zsela joins their application to the preserved deliberate-deception claims while proposing the separate change described below.

Crawford-El and Jeffries preserve competent direct and circumstantial proof, actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation without an invented heightened motive requirement. Their retaliation-specific burden allocation does not become the burden for every fabrication claim. Fletcher distinguishes evidentiary acts from advocacy and preserves the remaining liability requirements; it does not supply Cooper with prosecutorial immunity or itself provide 1993 notice. Failure of a legal immunity defense establishes no damages, supervisory liability, automatic discovery entitlement or right to trial.
