## Decision kernel

Review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) concerns the Eighth Circuit's October 3, 1996 affirmance of summary judgment sustaining Richenberg's honorable discharge. Cohen is the successor official. The granted question concerns genuine individualized rebuttal in this identity-statement discharge. Richenberg sought reversal and reinstatement or lawful renewed separation proceedings with prospective relief. A December 1993 hearing was followed by reconsideration under the new policy and a June 1994 hearing; the Secretary ordered discharge August 28, 1995.

Richenberg denied intending prohibited conduct and offered strong service, character, integrity and discipline evidence. Cross-examination elicited attraction to men and equivocal answers about possible future sexual activity and lawful marriage. No completed prohibited act, actual marriage or express intent to disobey is established. The Board of Inquiry made a brief propensity finding; the Board of Review relied on attraction and discounted character witnesses as not specifically asked about propensity. The appellate majority interpreted the decision as a credibility assessment; Arnold disputed that reading and the adequacy of the actual explanations. No express agency demeanor finding, excluded testimony, missing hearing, additional misconduct or numerical proof burden may be added.

Section 654(b)(2) requires separation on an approved statement finding unless a further approved finding establishes that the member demonstrated he does not engage in, attempt, have a propensity toward, or intend homosexual acts. The directive requires notice of the presumption and an opportunity to rebut. Section 654(e) permits omission of processing only with both avoidance-or-termination intent and military-best-interest findings; neither is established, and permission is not a retention command. Section 654(b)(1)'s separate retention route requires all five findings: departure from usual behavior, unlikely recurrence, absence of force/coercion/intimidation, continued presence consistent with discipline/order/morale, and no propensity or intent. It and §654(b)(3)'s marriage route do not replace the statement inquiry. Section 654(f)(3)'s sexual-purpose bodily contact or contact reasonably understood to demonstrate propensity or intent does not make ordinary attraction an act. These provisions enter at their existing scope, without a new statutory construction deciding unpresented grounds.

## Holdings

**This record permits the conduct-related inference after genuine consideration of rebuttal.** Ordinary rationality permits the statement inference sustained in Thomasson only with a genuine opportunity to rebut; propensity requires likelihood beyond abstract desire. O'Connor's opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, controls the application sustaining this discharge.

Thomasson requires meaningful rebuttal, not acceptance of every denial. Neither completed prohibited conduct nor individual operational injury is necessary. Richenberg's service and character evidence mattered without automatically defeating the inference. Two hearings received that evidence and his denial; cross-examination also produced equivocal answers about future conduct. The existing finding and evidence permit a conduct-related assessment. Discounting general character testimony can reflect its probative reach rather than categorical refusal to consider rebuttal.

The strongest objection is that the brief explanations repeat attraction without explaining disbelief of the undertaking to obey. The proceedings and reasons need not be read as making orientation conclusive. Their supported reading requires neither an invented demeanor finding nor an additional agency reason. A competing inference or sparse explanation alone does not establish a constitutional violation or a general demand for additional findings. Attraction alone remains insufficient; hypothetical appellate reasons cannot replace actual agency reasons.

Section 654(b)(2) permits rebuttal by demonstrating no engagement in, attempt, propensity toward or intent to engage in homosexual acts. Section 654(e) permits omission of processing only with avoidance-or-termination intent and military-best-interest findings; neither is established, and permission is no retention command. Ordinary attraction is not itself a statutory act.

Walmer's preliminary admitted-conduct record does not decide this statement challenge. Romer preserves ordinary rationality tied to the actual disadvantage; Weiss combines military weight with independent review. Jeffries does not substitute its civilian employment balance or retaliation burden for Thomasson. Independent APA reviewability and relief, speech, due process, directive validity, substantive liberty and other ungranted claims remain unresolved. No heightened scrutiny, actual-purpose burden or universal administrative checklist is adopted.

Lower courts must distinguish a genuinely rebuttable prediction of conduct, propensity or intent from conclusive orientation exclusion. This application establishes neither an attraction-alone rule nor a general constitutional requirement of additional administrative findings and resolves no independent APA standard or claim.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens's dissent, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg.** The dissenters would reverse and require this discharge to be set aside. Thomasson permits a conduct-related prediction only with genuine rebuttal. The actual explanations do not adequately connect ordinary attraction with likelihood of prohibited conduct or explain rejecting the sworn undertaking and supporting evidence. Receiving evidence is insufficient if the reasons deprive it of meaningful effect. Hypothetical lawful relationships and uncertainty about all future conduct do not supply the missing connection.

The Government's adverse-credibility reading deserves consideration under military deference, but an appellate inference cannot replace the required connection in the actual decision. This is an as-applied equality disagreement: good service and a denial are not automatic defenses; completed acts and individual operational harm are unnecessary; the policy and ordinary scrutiny remain intact. Lawful military proceedings remain available, without deciding every retention qualification or awarding unconditional reinstatement or backpay. No independent APA, speech or due-process ground is adopted.

**Stone-Zsela's additional dissenting statement.** Joining that equality ground and complete set-aside remedy, the Chief Justice specifies that any further military action must rest on a supported individualized finding concerning actual conduct, propensity, intent or credibility under a lawful rule. Inability to predict every future relationship is not itself intent to violate military rules. Additional explanation alone would not replace his proposed removal of this discharge.
