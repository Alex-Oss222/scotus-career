### These banks may seek review of the membership expansion

**Holding and operative rule:** Directly competing banks injured by these completed charter approvals have Article III standing and fall at least arguably within the interests implicated by §109’s particular membership boundary for APA §702 review. They need not be the statute’s specially intended beneficiaries, but competitive injury alone does not establish a right to challenge every regulatory limitation.

**Authority:** The five-Justice Opinion of the Court supplies direct authority for the provision-specific statutory-zone rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The approvals permit customers who otherwise might use the respondent banks to obtain services from ATTF; injury is undisputed, is traceable to the approvals and can be redressed by setting them aside. Lujan supplies the distinct constitutional elements, and Bennett requires examination of the particular provision rather than the Act’s general purpose. Section 109 limits credit-union membership and thus the customers the institution principally serves. These competitors challenge the agency’s expansion across that precise boundary. Clarke and Data Processing permit that direct statutory connection without a special-beneficiary requirement. Air Courier separately requires a statutory interest beyond injury, but its collateral employment interests differ from this challenge by participants in the regulated customer market. The Court does not find that protecting bank profits was Congress’s purpose. Nor does it eliminate APA finality, adequate-remedy, preclusion or other applicable conditions; these completed, operative approvals present no established independent defect of that kind.

### Unrelated occupational groups cannot replace the common bond

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 109’s occupational route requires a common occupational bond connecting the membership of the occupationally defined credit union; it does not authorize aggregation of unrelated employer groups merely because each has its own internal bond. The challenged agency policy crosses that statutory boundary and receives no deference at Chevron’s first step.

**Authority:** The same five Justices adopt the common text-and-structure rationale, its application and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The sentence limits federal credit-union membership, not merely the labels assigned to each component group. The plural groups permits multiple groups sharing a common occupational bond; it does not make a succession of unrelated bonds sufficient. The agency’s reading retains internal group and service-area conditions, but those are different from the connection Congress imposed on the membership. The parallel geographic alternative supports that reading. NCUA’s concession that the geographic groups must occupy the same defined community is an attributed argument, not a separate holding on geographic charters. MCI requires enforcement of an enacted boundary before deference. NationsBank permits reasonable financial classifications within an authorized domain and does not permit removal of its boundary. General rulemaking authority, serviceability and administrative efficiency cannot replace the required bond. A changed interpretation is not invalid merely because it changed. The decision imposes no single-employer requirement and does not determine the full breadth of a genuine occupation or association, geographic charters, or individual membership-retention rights.

### The disposition enforces the challenged boundary without rewriting membership law

**Holding and operative rule:** The statutory invalidation applies to the challenged unrelated employer-group additions; the other membership commands and permissions in §1759 remain intact. Affirmance supplies no independent enlargement of the injunction reported below.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice opinion adopts this scope of construction and relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1759 permits elected persons and organizations within Board rules, subject to the statutory membership limitation, and requires each member to subscribe to at least one share and pay its initial installment. A uniform entrance fee is required only if the board of directors requires it. The distinct final sentence permits survivorship joint-tenancy shares with a member’s designated person; that person may not vote, obtain loans or hold office unless within the field of membership and a qualified member. The joint-tenancy permission is not unlimited membership or authority to add unrelated employer groups. None of those provisions is displaced. The Court resolves the challenged legal interpretation, not every approval condition or remedial detail. Future applications remain subject to the statutory limits then lawfully governing them.

## Enhanced zone-of-interests comparison

**Available path allowing review.** Section 109 itself sets the membership boundary whose alleged removal directly creates the challenged competition. These banks are participants in the same market, injured by agency expansion across that boundary. Clarke and the competitor cases it applies permit review without proof that Congress intended especially to benefit the plaintiff. This is a direct relationship to the provision's operative limitation, not a claim derived from some unrelated provision of the Act. Air Courier's postal employees had a collateral employment interest; its footnote 5 and discussion at 529 preserve the difference between employees and direct competitors.

**Strongest objection to that path.** A statute's restriction of entry or eligibility will often reduce competition incidentally. Treating that effect alone as sufficient can collapse the statutory zone into injury in fact, precisely what Air Courier forbids. The membership bond can protect the cooperative's members without protecting established commercial lenders against competition. Clarke found a statutory purpose beyond mere incidental benefit; Bennett found a particular protective data requirement.

**Available path denying review.** The occupational limitation concerns the character of a qualifying membership group. The banks' interest in keeping potential customers away from the credit union lacks the necessary protective connection to that particular membership condition. A shared preference for enforcing a restriction is not itself a sufficient statutory interest. This path does not require an express right of action in the FCUA, special-beneficiary status, or proof that Congress named banks; it requires an arguable relationship to the provision's purposes beyond economic consequences.

**Strongest objection to that path.** Clarke expressly rejects a special-intended-beneficiary requirement, and its discussion of Investment Company Institute accepts competitor challenges to restrictions primarily aimed at financial safety. A bank need not share every purpose of a statute to challenge agency removal of a statutory limit. The banks' direct market position provides a more immediate relationship than the postal employees' derivative interest.

## Enhanced common-bond and deference comparison

**Available path invalidating the policy.** Read as a limit on a federal credit union's membership, §109 requires the relevant occupational groups to share the common bond rather than letting each unrelated employer group supply a different one. The plural permits multiple groups sharing the bond; it does not render the bond requirement meaningless. The parallel geographic alternative supplies the same collective structure, consistent with NCUA's concession. On this reading, the agency's service-area requirements cannot replace the enacted occupational connection. MCI enforces a statutory boundary before deference, while NationsBank leaves permissible classifications inside it.

**Strongest objection to invalidation.** Ordinary plural grammar permits each group to have its own bond. Group-by-group qualification still excludes unaffiliated individuals and therefore need not make the statute surplusage. The occupational and geographic clauses use different grammatical forms, and NCUA retains group membership and administrative service-area requirements. Its policy therefore can be described as applying the condition to groups rather than abolishing it.

**Available path sustaining the policy.** The plural groups admits a distributive reading: every qualifying group must possess an occupational bond, but distinct groups need not share one bond with each other. Congress did not expressly say that one bond must unite the credit union's entire occupational membership. The agency may choose among the genuinely permissible readings within its express rulemaking authority over membership, and changed administrative policy alone is not a defect. That authority remains subject to the common-bond exception, whatever its proper unit of application. Clarke, NationsBank and Allentown Mack preserve agency policy and classification discretion within the statute.

**Strongest objection to sustaining it.** If every unrelated group can be added, the occupational clause ceases to connect the membership of the credit union as a whole. The geographic concession and the earlier decades of consistent common-bond administration reinforce the collective reading. An agency cannot use general access or viability objectives to remove a concrete statutory limitation; MCI controls that boundary even where the replacement has policy advantages.

## Holdings

### These banks may seek review of the membership expansion

**Holding and operative rule:** Directly competing banks injured by these completed charter approvals have Article III standing and fall at least arguably within the interests implicated by §109’s particular membership boundary for APA §702 review. They need not be the statute’s specially intended beneficiaries, but competitive injury alone does not establish a right to challenge every regulatory limitation.

**Authority:** The five-Justice Opinion of the Court supplies direct authority for the provision-specific statutory-zone rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The approvals permit customers who otherwise might use the respondent banks to obtain services from ATTF; injury is undisputed, is traceable to the approvals and can be redressed by setting them aside. Lujan supplies the distinct constitutional elements, and Bennett requires examination of the particular provision rather than the Act’s general purpose. Section 109 limits credit-union membership and thus the customers the institution principally serves. These competitors challenge the agency’s expansion across that precise boundary. Clarke and Data Processing permit that direct statutory connection without a special-beneficiary requirement. Air Courier separately requires a statutory interest beyond injury, but its collateral employment interests differ from this challenge by participants in the regulated customer market. The Court does not find that protecting bank profits was Congress’s purpose. Nor does it eliminate APA finality, adequate-remedy, preclusion or other applicable conditions; these completed, operative approvals present no established independent defect of that kind.

### Unrelated occupational groups cannot replace the common bond

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 109’s occupational route requires a common occupational bond connecting the membership of the occupationally defined credit union; it does not authorize aggregation of unrelated employer groups merely because each has its own internal bond. The challenged agency policy crosses that statutory boundary and receives no deference at Chevron’s first step.

**Authority:** The same five Justices adopt the common text-and-structure rationale, its application and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The sentence limits federal credit-union membership, not merely the labels assigned to each component group. The plural groups permits multiple groups sharing a common occupational bond; it does not make a succession of unrelated bonds sufficient. The agency’s reading retains internal group and service-area conditions, but those are different from the connection Congress imposed on the membership. The parallel geographic alternative supports that reading. NCUA’s concession that the geographic groups must occupy the same defined community is an attributed argument, not a separate holding on geographic charters. MCI requires enforcement of an enacted boundary before deference. NationsBank permits reasonable financial classifications within an authorized domain and does not permit removal of its boundary. General rulemaking authority, serviceability and administrative efficiency cannot replace the required bond. A changed interpretation is not invalid merely because it changed. The decision imposes no single-employer requirement and does not determine the full breadth of a genuine occupation or association, geographic charters, or individual membership-retention rights.

### The disposition enforces the challenged boundary without rewriting membership law

**Holding and operative rule:** The statutory invalidation applies to the challenged unrelated employer-group additions; the other membership commands and permissions in §1759 remain intact. Affirmance supplies no independent enlargement of the injunction reported below.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice opinion adopts this scope of construction and relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1759 permits elected persons and organizations within Board rules, subject to the statutory membership limitation, and requires each member to subscribe to at least one share and pay its initial installment. A uniform entrance fee is required only if the board of directors requires it. The distinct final sentence permits survivorship joint-tenancy shares with a member’s designated person; that person may not vote, obtain loans or hold office unless within the field of membership and a qualified member. The joint-tenancy permission is not unlimited membership or authority to add unrelated employer groups. None of those provisions is displaced. The Court resolves the challenged legal interpretation, not every approval condition or remedial detail. Future applications remain subject to the statutory limits then lawfully governing them.

The banks’ APA action may proceed, and the challenged unrelated occupational-group construction is invalid. ## Separate Writings

**O’Connor, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer, dissenting.** The dissent accepts the banks’ concrete economic injury and rejects any requirement that Congress expressly identify them as special beneficiaries. It nevertheless finds their asserted protection against competition only incidental to §109. The common-bond rule organizes a cohesive cooperative; it does not keep credit unions collectively out of an identifiable market, since a group may join another qualifying union or form one. Clarke’s permissive inquiry and Bennett’s provision-specific focus therefore do not reduce to the economic effect of enforcing any restriction. Air Courier preserves the need for this additional statutory relationship. The dissent would vacate the reviewed judgment and remand for dismissal of the APA action, removing relief dependent on it without approving the agency’s interpretation or denying Article III injury.
