## Event

Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10, Second Circuit No. 96-9633, 127 F.3d 207. Decided May 11, 1998, on review of the judgment sustaining the school district's worship and religious-instruction exclusions. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is stated.

The district opened facilities for educational, civic, social, recreational and community-welfare activities, including secular ethical and character instruction; it had rented to the church for sports and a banquet. ## Holdings

### 1. Private worship is not government worship merely because it uses school space

**Holding and operative rule:** Permitting this outside congregation to use school space for its own Sunday worship does not by itself establish religion. The district cannot justify this exclusion as constitutionally required, or rely on an identification of the school with the church, without facts establishing genuine governmental adoption rather than private access.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I is joined by all eight other Justices. It answers the establishment question without determining the separate scope of the speech forum.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini retains Lemon as a single inquiry in which purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats a claim on that ground; an otherwise legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives an attribution-and-function inquiry. The earlier access cases reserved standalone worship, so their results do not automatically settle this application.

Here the governmental act would be administering rental access. The pastor and congregation would arrange the services. No official selection, control, adoption, preference or obscured sponsorship is established. Tanford and Chaudhuri address the different act of incorporating devotional prayer into an official university program. Chabad and Pinette reject location alone as a sufficient attribution rule; Good News shows that the students' ages do not themselves turn independent expression into the school's speech.

The Second Circuit did not require reliance on the Establishment Clause, but treated avoidance of identification with a particular church as supporting the exclusion. That rationale cannot make religious identity and physical location decisive without actual governmental adoption. This conclusion neither immunizes every religious use nor opens every public building. Genuine future adoption or preferential operation would require its own inquiry, and government organization of worship as its own activity remains forbidden.

**Precedent treatment:** Agostini's modified Lemon inquiry is applied to the reserved worship setting; Tanford and Chaudhuri are distinguished as official programs; the contextual limits of Chabad, Pinette and Good News remain intact.

### 2. An admitted instructional subject cannot exclude its religious perspective

**Holding and operative rule:** Once this forum admits instruction on a subject, the district may not exclude an otherwise eligible speaker because the instruction presents a religious perspective. Permitting religious discussion does not cure a ban on religious instruction where comparable secular ethical and character teaching is admitted.

**Authority:** All nine Justices support the general rule in Part II. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer also join its application and the instruction reversal; Stone-Zsela does not join that disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Lamb's Chapel protects a religious perspective on an admitted subject even assuming a nonpublic forum. Good News and Church on the Rock apply that principle to teaching and related devotional expression within admitted programs; Rosenberger protects viewpoint equality within the actual program. The district admitted broad education and secular ethical and character instruction. It cannot require the comparable religious speaker to abandon instruction and confine itself to a different permitted format of discussion.

The Court does not convert this limited forum into an unrestricted curriculum or require access for every subject. It reverses the judgment sustaining the challenged instruction exclusion only insofar as it reaches subjects actually admitted for comparable instruction. The church's integrated worship request presents a separate access question, decided in Holding 3; the instructional rule does not make every worship service an admitted use by definition.

**Precedent treatment:** Lamb's Chapel, Good News, Church on the Rock and Rosenberger are applied to the actual instructional comparator, preserving their forum and program limits.

### 3. This broad private community forum cannot exclude the requested use solely for its devotional form

**Holding and operative rule:** In this actual after-hours educational and community forum, the district may not disqualify the church's otherwise comparable private Sunday meeting solely because its expression takes the form of worship. The district retains reasonable, viewpoint-neutral operational and genuine activity limits, but it cannot define comparable admitted uses simply by removing their religious expression.

**Authority:** Breyer's Parts III and IV are joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, a five-Justice controlling majority. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg oppose this application; no narrower rationale is assembled from their votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Perry Education and Cornelius permit a limited forum and genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral access restrictions. The actual forum, however, broadly admits community meetings and education, including moral and character development. A private meeting does not cease to serve those admitted functions merely because participants express their convictions devotionally. Widmar distinguishes religious exercise in a forum from government religious organization; Lamb's Chapel and Rosenberger prohibit subtracting the religious perspective from otherwise admitted expression.

The district's consistent exclusion proves that it maintained the challenged rule. It does not establish the rule's neutrality. The opposing approach treats the integrated service, particularly communion, as a categorically different activity. The Court rejects the premise that a religious meeting must have an exact secular sacramental counterpart before its educational and community expression is comparable. The denial rests on its worship character, not an established neutral scheduling, capacity, safety or supervision problem.

This is an applied holding on the described broad forum. It creates no right to every public building, continuous rental, preferential control, public financing of devotion or a permanently open forum. A genuinely different neutral activity boundary remains permissible. No compulsory disclaimer or universal sign rule is imposed. The absence of a categorical establishment bar is a distinct conclusion and is not itself the source of the access right.

**Precedent treatment:** The viewpoint principles of Lamb's Chapel and Rosenberger extend to this otherwise comparable worship use; the earlier standalone-worship reservations are resolved only at this scope. Perry Education and Cornelius retain genuine forum limits; Widmar supports the private/government distinction.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Agostini:** The governing modified Lemon inquiry is applied without reinstating independent former prongs or replacing it with a coercion-only rule.
- **Lamb's Chapel; Good News/Good Sports; Church on the Rock:** Their instruction and viewpoint holdings apply; their standalone-worship reservations are answered only for the present comparable use.
- **Rosenberger:** Viewpoint neutrality within the actual program supplies the speech principle; its printing-benefit setting is not converted into a general funding obligation.
- **Pinette and Chabad:** Preserve bounded private access and contextual attribution; location and religious identity alone are insufficient.
- **Tanford and Chaudhuri:** Official organization of devotional prayer remains prohibited and distinct from independent rental use.
- **Perry Education and Cornelius:** Preserve reasonable, viewpoint-neutral genuine subject and activity limits; consistency alone does not validate an exclusion.
- **Widmar:** Supports the distinction between private religious exercise in a forum and a governmental religious program.
- **Perry v. Los Angeles Police Department:** Supplies no traditional-public-forum classification or transplanted tailoring standard for this school.

## Law After Decision

Private Sunday worship may not be excluded from this broad forum solely for its devotional form, and comparable religious instruction receives the same access as admitted secular instruction. Establishment does not categorically require the exclusion, while actual governmental adoption remains legally distinct. Neutral management and genuine forum boundaries survive; no unrestricted facility entitlement or funding obligation arises.

## Separate Writings

Stevens, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, agrees that the instruction exclusion impermissibly disadvantages a religious perspective on admitted subjects. He disagrees with the worship holding: an integrated recurring church service, expressly including communion, is a genuinely distinct activity rather than merely instruction from a religious perspective. Perry Education and Cornelius permit limiting the forum to the instructional, civic, social and recreational uses actually opened. Consistent exclusion is relevant to identifying that forum, although it is not sufficient by itself to establish constitutionality. The partial dissent would affirm the worship exclusion while reversing instruction. It does not claim that establishment requires exclusion or that incidental prayer in an admitted program may automatically be banned. Stone-Zsela joins this worship-activity reasoning only.

Stone-Zsela would affirm the entire judgment narrowly and order no remand. The application sought regular Sunday worship, and the stipulated record supplies no separate request for permitted discussion or instruction; he therefore treats the agreed religious-instruction rule as a limit on affirmance, not a separate judgment. He finds no comparable worship activity in the actual one-year use record and accepts a reasonable, viewpoint-neutral activity boundary. If comparable activity were established, he would reverse. His affirmance rejects the identification-with-a-church rationale and does not approve excluding a religious perspective on an admitted subject. Under Agostini, school location and religious identity do not establish governmental worship; actual official adoption could change that answer. Neither his forum position nor the Court's ruling creates a right to every building, a duty to keep the forum open or public financing of devotion.
