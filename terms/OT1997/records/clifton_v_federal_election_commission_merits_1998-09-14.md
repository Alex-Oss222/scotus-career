### Independent political-party campaign expression and candidate connections

Register present operation: Colorado Republican, 1996-06-26.

Operative authority: Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission, June 26, 1996, O'Connor Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

Current register wording, copied unchanged:

Section 441a(d)(3)'s Senate-election expenditure ceiling cannot be applied to a party's own genuinely independent campaign advertisement merely because the speaker is an official political party. Party identity or a continuing electoral relationship also cannot replace §441a(a)(7)(B)(i)'s required cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion by a candidate, authorized political committee or agent for the asserted contribution theory. ## Controlling holdings

### 1. Asking a candidate about public issues does not itself finance the candidate

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 2 U.S.C. §441b, 11 C.F.R. §114.4(c)(4) cannot prohibit a publisher's mere inquiry to a candidate about positions or recorded votes as a contribution or candidate-directed expenditure. The required connection concerns campaign spending: actual cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion concerning the expenditure remains governed by its own statutory predicates.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that gathering information is not itself spending on a candidate's behalf. Buckley v. Valeo distinguishes independent expression from candidate financing; Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission requires a real candidate connection rather than a label substituting for that connection; Akins v. FEC likewise requires actual covered financing. MRLC's effort to learn the meaning of public votes supplies no finding that a candidate directed the guide's content, collaborated in distribution, or received an agreed campaign service. The Commission may investigate such arrangements when supported. Its concern that a survey could conceal collaboration cannot make every inquiry collaboration. This holding protects neither staged questions supplying campaign work nor a paid political advertisement disguised as an independent guide.

**Precedent treatment:** Buckley's contribution/expenditure distinction, Colorado Republican's actual-connection requirement and Akins's transaction-specific coverage remain in force and apply to this information-gathering setting.

### 2. The existing written-only and equal-space declaration stands

**Holding and operative rule:** In this private-petitioner review, the declaration invalidating §114.4(c)(5)'s compulsory written questions and replies and equal space and prominence remains in place. General rulemaking and enforcement authority does not authorize treating every independently edited guide involving candidate inquiry as a prohibited contribution or imposing these indiscriminate contact and editorial conditions on that premise.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's limited statutory ground respects the difference between a real contribution and dialogue used to compose independent speech. A written record can aid enforcement, but administrative convenience does not establish that an oral discussion transfers value to a candidate. Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo protects editorial allocation; Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston protects the speaker's selection of an expressive composition. Those principles reinforce the need for actual statutory authority before requiring a private publisher to give every candidate equal space and prominence. The petition seeks broader protection for the private plaintiffs; no established cross-petition seeks enlargement of the Commission's rights by restoring restrictions already invalidated. That review limit independently preserves the existing relief. The Court does not decide that Congress could never authorize a specifically fitted contact safeguard, that accurate disclosure could never be required under lawful authority, or that proved coordinated services are exempt. A government-authored guide or a condition on a properly defined public resource presents different questions.

**Precedent treatment:** Tornillo and Hurley reinforce editorial protection without becoming a universal prohibition on regulation of campaign contributions. Austin v. Michigan Chamber of Commerce is neither overruled nor treated as a delegation of every restriction on corporate speech.

### 3. The unfinished provisions and organizational status remain for the existing remand

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court does not expand the declaration to invalidate the whole regulation or uphold the unresolved electioneering-message clauses. Their operative meaning, ripeness and severability remain for the District Court; MRLC's entitlement to nonprofit constitutional protection remains undecided on its actual funding characteristics.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court cannot supply an operative definition the appellate record leaves unsettled or decide severability from the label alone. The existing remand permits those questions and any properly supported interim-relief request to be decided under their own standards. Day v. Holahan prevents automatic denial of MCFL protection solely because an ideological nonprofit accepts some corporate money; it does not establish that MRLC's funding is insignificant, noncommercial or free of conduit features. The Court therefore neither grants an organizational exemption nor assumes commercial-treasury status. Genuine-coordination restrictions, express-advocacy provisions not successfully challenged, and distinct statutory membership exclusions retain their lawful scope.

**Precedent treatment:** Day and MCFL require actual organizational and funding characteristics; Austin's surviving corporate-treasury rule remains available in its materially different setting. No prior decision is overruled.

## Holdings

**Authority for Holdings 1–3:** Parts I–III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the stated narrow propositions. Stevens and Ginsburg join Holding 2 within the private petitioners' present review and its limited statutory ground; they do not adopt a general constitutional ban on contact safeguards.

### 1. Asking a candidate about public issues does not itself finance the candidate

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 2 U.S.C. §441b, 11 C.F.R. §114.4(c)(4) cannot prohibit a publisher's mere inquiry to a candidate about positions or recorded votes as a contribution or candidate-directed expenditure. The required connection concerns campaign spending: actual cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion concerning the expenditure remains governed by its own statutory predicates.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that gathering information is not itself spending on a candidate's behalf. Buckley v. Valeo distinguishes independent expression from candidate financing; Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission requires a real candidate connection rather than a label substituting for that connection; Akins v. FEC likewise requires actual covered financing. MRLC's effort to learn the meaning of public votes supplies no finding that a candidate directed the guide's content, collaborated in distribution, or received an agreed campaign service. The Commission may investigate such arrangements when supported. Its concern that a survey could conceal collaboration cannot make every inquiry collaboration. This holding protects neither staged questions supplying campaign work nor a paid political advertisement disguised as an independent guide.

**Precedent treatment:** Buckley's contribution/expenditure distinction, Colorado Republican's actual-connection requirement and Akins's transaction-specific coverage remain in force and apply to this information-gathering setting.

### 2. The existing written-only and equal-space declaration stands

**Holding and operative rule:** In this private-petitioner review, the declaration invalidating §114.4(c)(5)'s compulsory written questions and replies and equal space and prominence remains in place. General rulemaking and enforcement authority does not authorize treating every independently edited guide involving candidate inquiry as a prohibited contribution or imposing these indiscriminate contact and editorial conditions on that premise.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's limited statutory ground respects the difference between a real contribution and dialogue used to compose independent speech. A written record can aid enforcement, but administrative convenience does not establish that an oral discussion transfers value to a candidate. Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo protects editorial allocation; Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston protects the speaker's selection of an expressive composition. Those principles reinforce the need for actual statutory authority before requiring a private publisher to give every candidate equal space and prominence. The petition seeks broader protection for the private plaintiffs; no established cross-petition seeks enlargement of the Commission's rights by restoring restrictions already invalidated. That review limit independently preserves the existing relief. The Court does not decide that Congress could never authorize a specifically fitted contact safeguard, that accurate disclosure could never be required under lawful authority, or that proved coordinated services are exempt. A government-authored guide or a condition on a properly defined public resource presents different questions.

**Precedent treatment:** Tornillo and Hurley reinforce editorial protection without becoming a universal prohibition on regulation of campaign contributions. Austin v. Michigan Chamber of Commerce is neither overruled nor treated as a delegation of every restriction on corporate speech.

### 3. The unfinished provisions and organizational status remain for the existing remand

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court does not expand the declaration to invalidate the whole regulation or uphold the unresolved electioneering-message clauses. Their operative meaning, ripeness and severability remain for the District Court; MRLC's entitlement to nonprofit constitutional protection remains undecided on its actual funding characteristics.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court cannot supply an operative definition the appellate record leaves unsettled or decide severability from the label alone. The existing remand permits those questions and any properly supported interim-relief request to be decided under their own standards. Day v. Holahan prevents automatic denial of MCFL protection solely because an ideological nonprofit accepts some corporate money; it does not establish that MRLC's funding is insignificant, noncommercial or free of conduit features. The Court therefore neither grants an organizational exemption nor assumes commercial-treasury status. Genuine-coordination restrictions, express-advocacy provisions not successfully challenged, and distinct statutory membership exclusions retain their lawful scope.

**Precedent treatment:** Day and MCFL require actual organizational and funding characteristics; Austin's surviving corporate-treasury rule remains available in its materially different setting. No prior decision is overruled.

## Precedent Treatment

- Buckley: applies the distinction between independent expression and actual candidate financing.
- Colorado Republican: applies its requirement of an actual expenditure connection; party identity or a bare contact cannot replace it.
- Akins: applies actual statutory financing predicates without expanding its distinct membership or political-committee rulings.
- Tornillo: applies protection of editorial allocation as support for the narrow statutory construction.
- Hurley: applies protection of chosen expressive composition at the same limited level.
- Day: applies factual nonprofit protection; some corporate funding alone does not defeat it.
- MCFL: preserves its expressive and organizational protections subject to actual characteristics.
- Austin: preserves its surviving corporate-treasury scope; it supplies no general delegation to the FEC.

## Law After Decision

A mere candidate inquiry is not a contribution, and the existing declaration against written-only contacts and compulsory equal space and prominence remains effective. Actual coordinated campaign financing remains separately regulable. Neither the meaning and severability of the electioneering-message clauses nor MRLC's nonprofit status is settled by this decision. The rule concerns the challenged guide regulations; it supplies no decision of the separate §100.22(b) litigation.
