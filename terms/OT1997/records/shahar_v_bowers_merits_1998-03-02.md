### I. Private association requires its own protection and employment inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The actual selective, enduring intimate relationship and its religious expression cannot be denied constitutional protection merely because they lack civil-marriage recognition or include an invitation-only ceremony. A public employer’s genuine operational interests remain relevant, but generalized forecasts, presumed lawbreaking and presumed government endorsement do not conclusively justify this defense summary judgment.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Roberts and Rotary distinguish close personal association from association for protected expression and require examination of the actual relationship and activity. On the plaintiff-favorable record, the selective, enduring relationship and its religious expression satisfy those protected predicates; their protection is not left open merely because the relationship lacks civil-marriage recognition. Shahar seeks protection against an employment penalty, not marital status or benefits. Umbehr separates entitlement to a benefit from an unconstitutional reason for withholding it. Jeffries requires attention to actual functions and institutional needs: prevention need not await disruption, but a reasonable forecast does not alone settle the legality of penalizing protected activity. Confidentiality, credible representation, assignment flexibility and professional trust remain substantial concerns. Neither a general office label nor speculation about endorsement establishes inability to perform lawful duties. No lawbreaking or refusal of duties is established, and later insurance or name-change evidence cannot supply contemporaneous knowledge. Waters protects reasonable factual reliance only when the substantive rule permits disciplining the conduct described and establishes no universal interview requirement. The remaining employment justification, causal application and relief return for adjudication; the Court orders no retention of a lawyer unable to perform necessary work.

### II. Free exercise cannot be absorbed into a general employee-speech balance

**Holding and operative rule:** A public-employment decision alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise must be examined for neutrality and general applicability under its own governing rule. If the actual restriction is nonneutral or not generally applicable, the employer must establish a compelling justification and narrow tailoring; religious impact alone does not establish that trigger.

**Authority:** Part II has Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith preserves neutral, generally applicable rules. Lukumi requires examination of objective targeting and comparable secular conduct; Brown applies the required justification to religion-specific employment restrictions while preserving control of work, resources, coercion and official representation. The lower court assumed religious protection and treated the claim as resolved by the same general employment balance. That does not answer whether this withdrawal imposed the prohibited religious disability. The contrary argument is substantial: Bowers may have responded to the represented relationship and perceived job consequences regardless of religious form. That is an argument requiring proper assessment, not an established hypothetical fact that disposes of the independent claim. Sincerity does not prove targeting, and the absence of theological compulsion does not defeat sincere exercise. All six joiners reserve the trigger’s ultimate application and any properly supported justification. No finding of discrimination, general religious exemption or new statutory claim follows.

### III. The actual equality disadvantage requires ordinary constitutional justification

**Holding and operative rule:** An adverse civilian employment classification does not escape ordinary equal-protection review by being described as conduct-based or as a hiring choice. The actual disadvantage must bear a rational relation to a legitimate governmental interest; no new orientation-based heightened tier is adopted.

**Authority:** Part III has Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, five direct joins. Stone-Zsela’s different proposed scrutiny standard is not controlling and is not combined with this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Romer applies ordinary rationality to the actual disability, allowing reasonable predictions and generalizations while excluding bare harm or irrational prejudice. Valot distinguishes that inquiry from justification for penalizing protected activity and from a proved same-decision defense. The lower court’s conduct/employment characterization cannot itself resolve the claim. The office’s professional and representative responsibilities remain legitimate considerations; remand does not make every predictive judgment invalid or require proof of actual disruption. Walmer, Thomasson and Richenberg address military and conduct-related settings, not an irrebuttable civilian inference from a religious commitment ceremony. Bowers v. Hardwick concerns its specified conduct; orientation or the ceremony alone proves no violation. The Court neither decides liability nor requires intermediate scrutiny. The five-Justice ordinary-rationality instruction governs the equality remand.

### IV. Causation, conduct-date immunity and relief remain independent

**Holding and operative rule:** Remand requires claim-specific constitutional predicates, personal responsibility, causation and lawful relief. Where retaliation analysis applies, protected activity must be a substantial or motivating factor and a same-decision defense requires proof by a preponderance; ordinary rationality’s conceivable basis is not that proof.

**Authority:** Part IV is joined by the five common Court-opinion Justices; O’Connor joins its application to the free-exercise remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Umbehr and Jeffries preserve actual causal responsibility and the counterfactual defense. Bowers pleaded qualified immunity, but neither lower court decided it and he did not request appellate affirmance on that ground. The Court does not award the defense, decide waiver or automatically revive it. Any procedurally available personal-damages defense concerns notice in July 1991; later Romer, Brown, Umbehr, Jeffries, Valot and Danahy cannot supply retrospective notice. Prospective relief, any proper successor, personal damages and official-capacity relief retain separate predicates. Later evidence may have a lawful remedial relevance without becoming contemporaneous motive. Elapsed time establishes neither mootness nor a continuing job entitlement. The mandate compels no interview, discovery plan, trial, reinstatement, backpay, fees or damages award.

### Enhanced two-path conclusions

**S1/S2, Path A — correct the protection and employment inquiry and remand.** Roberts/Rotary require attention to the actual relationship and activity, not civil recognition or the number invited to a ceremonial event alone. The five choosing Path A accept that interest and reject an actual-disruption requirement; they find the generalized endorsement, presumed lawbreaking and possible-conflict reasoning insufficient to sustain this judgment without the claim-specific inquiry. No final finding of improper motive follows.

**S1/S2, Path B — assume protection and uphold this employment judgment.** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas give the actual professional role and the employer's preventive judgment greater weight, as their Jeffries positions show. Its strongest objection is that confidentiality and representative work cannot themselves license punishment of every private relationship. These three limit their conclusion to the reported open representation of a relationship, the office's existing legal responsibilities and the particular forecasts; they do not approve a categorical lawyer exception, invent a refusal of work or find sodomy. They refuse the five's recognition/application ground and support affirmance on the stated assumed-right balance.

**S3, Path B — affirm on a secular-ground reading.** Scalia and Thomas distinguish Brown's direct restrictions on religious words and possessions from an objection to represented relationship status. No compulsory religious-practice or theology test is accepted by either path.

**S4, Path A — vacate the categorical conduct/employment exclusion and remand under Romer.** Five choose it; they do not hold the ceremony indistinguishable from every possible misconduct or import heightened scrutiny. On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the May 30, 1997 judgment affirming summary judgment for Bowers. The case concerns withdrawal in July 1991 of an accepted civilian staff-attorney offer because of a planned same-sex religious commitment ceremony. The preserved theories are intimate association, expressive association, free exercise and equal protection. Shahar seeks damages and injunctive employment relief. No separate Supreme Court docket or oral-argument date is supplied; substantive due process and an independent free-speech claim are outside this review.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate. No Justice is recused.

## Public Action

The defense judgment is vacated on the four preserved claims and the case remanded for the distinct inquiries stated in the Court’s opinion.

## Holdings

### I. Private association requires its own protection and employment inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The actual selective, enduring intimate relationship and its religious expression cannot be denied constitutional protection merely because they lack civil-marriage recognition or include an invitation-only ceremony. A public employer’s genuine operational interests remain relevant, but generalized forecasts, presumed lawbreaking and presumed government endorsement do not conclusively justify this defense summary judgment.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Roberts and Rotary distinguish close personal association from association for protected expression and require examination of the actual relationship and activity. On the plaintiff-favorable record, the selective, enduring relationship and its religious expression satisfy those protected predicates; their protection is not left open merely because the relationship lacks civil-marriage recognition. Shahar seeks protection against an employment penalty, not marital status or benefits. Umbehr separates entitlement to a benefit from an unconstitutional reason for withholding it. Jeffries requires attention to actual functions and institutional needs: prevention need not await disruption, but a reasonable forecast does not alone settle the legality of penalizing protected activity. Confidentiality, credible representation, assignment flexibility and professional trust remain substantial concerns. Neither a general office label nor speculation about endorsement establishes inability to perform lawful duties. No lawbreaking or refusal of duties is established, and later insurance or name-change evidence cannot supply contemporaneous knowledge. Waters protects reasonable factual reliance only when the substantive rule permits disciplining the conduct described and establishes no universal interview requirement. The remaining employment justification, causal application and relief return for adjudication; the Court orders no retention of a lawyer unable to perform necessary work.

### II. Free exercise cannot be absorbed into a general employee-speech balance

**Holding and operative rule:** A public-employment decision alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise must be examined for neutrality and general applicability under its own governing rule. If the actual restriction is nonneutral or not generally applicable, the employer must establish a compelling justification and narrow tailoring; religious impact alone does not establish that trigger.

**Authority:** Part II has Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith preserves neutral, generally applicable rules. Lukumi requires examination of objective targeting and comparable secular conduct; Brown applies the required justification to religion-specific employment restrictions while preserving control of work, resources, coercion and official representation. The lower court assumed religious protection and treated the claim as resolved by the same general employment balance. That does not answer whether this withdrawal imposed the prohibited religious disability. The contrary argument is substantial: Bowers may have responded to the represented relationship and perceived job consequences regardless of religious form. That is an argument requiring proper assessment, not an established hypothetical fact that disposes of the independent claim. Sincerity does not prove targeting, and the absence of theological compulsion does not defeat sincere exercise. All six joiners reserve the trigger’s ultimate application and any properly supported justification. No finding of discrimination, general religious exemption or new statutory claim follows.

### III. The actual equality disadvantage requires ordinary constitutional justification

**Holding and operative rule:** An adverse civilian employment classification does not escape ordinary equal-protection review by being described as conduct-based or as a hiring choice. The actual disadvantage must bear a rational relation to a legitimate governmental interest; no new orientation-based heightened tier is adopted.

**Authority:** Part III has Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, five direct joins. Stone-Zsela’s different proposed scrutiny standard is not controlling and is not combined with this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Romer applies ordinary rationality to the actual disability, allowing reasonable predictions and generalizations while excluding bare harm or irrational prejudice. Valot distinguishes that inquiry from justification for penalizing protected activity and from a proved same-decision defense. The lower court’s conduct/employment characterization cannot itself resolve the claim. The office’s professional and representative responsibilities remain legitimate considerations; remand does not make every predictive judgment invalid or require proof of actual disruption. Walmer, Thomasson and Richenberg address military and conduct-related settings, not an irrebuttable civilian inference from a religious commitment ceremony. Bowers v. Hardwick concerns its specified conduct; orientation or the ceremony alone proves no violation. The Court neither decides liability nor requires intermediate scrutiny. The five-Justice ordinary-rationality instruction governs the equality remand.

### IV. Causation, conduct-date immunity and relief remain independent

**Holding and operative rule:** Remand requires claim-specific constitutional predicates, personal responsibility, causation and lawful relief. Where retaliation analysis applies, protected activity must be a substantial or motivating factor and a same-decision defense requires proof by a preponderance; ordinary rationality’s conceivable basis is not that proof.

**Authority:** Part IV is joined by the five common Court-opinion Justices; O’Connor joins its application to the free-exercise remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Umbehr and Jeffries preserve actual causal responsibility and the counterfactual defense. Bowers pleaded qualified immunity, but neither lower court decided it and he did not request appellate affirmance on that ground. The Court does not award the defense, decide waiver or automatically revive it. Any procedurally available personal-damages defense concerns notice in July 1991; later Romer, Brown, Umbehr, Jeffries, Valot and Danahy cannot supply retrospective notice. Prospective relief, any proper successor, personal damages and official-capacity relief retain separate predicates. Later evidence may have a lawful remedial relevance without becoming contemporaneous motive. Elapsed time establishes neither mootness nor a continuing job entitlement. The mandate compels no interview, discovery plan, trial, reinstatement, backpay, fees or damages award.

## Precedent Treatment

- Roberts and Rotary supply distinct intimate- and expressive-association inquiries; civil-marriage recognition is not their exclusive boundary.
- Umbehr distinguishes benefit entitlement from unconstitutional retaliation and preserves actual causation and the preponderance same-decision defense.
- Jeffries preserves preventive employment judgment while rejecting conclusive reliance on generalized forecasts. Waters protects reasonable factual reliance only within the substantive constitutional rule; neither supplies a compulsory interview code.
- Smith retains neutral, generally applicable rules; Lukumi supplies objective targeting, comparable secular conduct and triggered strict justification. Brown requires the separate religion-specific employment inquiry.
- Romer supplies ordinary rationality for the actual disadvantage. Valot keeps that standard distinct from a protected-right penalty and a same-decision defense.
- Walmer, Thomasson and Richenberg retain their military and conduct-related scope; they do not establish civilian exclusion or a heightened orientation tier. Bowers v. Hardwick is not extended from specified conduct to orientation or a ceremony.
- Danahy supplies no office-wide merits exception and no retroactive July 1991 notice. NTEU’s broad prospective-restraint burden is not transferred wholesale to this single hiring decision.

## Law After Decision

The four preserved claims return under distinct constitutional standards. Five Justices establish the association and ordinary-equality grounds; six establish the separate free-exercise inquiry. Intermediate scrutiny is not adopted. No unified rationale is synthesized from different writings, and no military inference, generalized government-lawyer exception or actual-disruption prerequisite controls civilian employment.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in setting aside the equal-protection judgment and remanding, and dissenting from formal vacatur and the ordinary-rationality mandate.** Stone-Zsela would reverse the equality judgment and require intermediate scrutiny on a developed record. He treats that standard as a proposed change, not a rule already established by Romer, Walmer or Thomasson. The actual reason for withdrawing the offer, any unsupported hostility and legitimate civilian operational grounds require examination; military conduct-related precedents do not settle this religious commitment ceremony. He leaves intimate association, expressive association and free exercise unresolved, as well as liability, immunity, damages and ultimate relief.

**O’Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** O’Connor joins the separate free-exercise inquiry under Brown and Lukumi without finding targeting. On association she assumes the asserted protection but gives dispositive weight to this staff lawyer’s professional trust, representation, assignment flexibility and credible forecasts, as in her Jeffries position and Waters rationale. On equality she finds a sufficient ordinary-rationality relationship to those professional concerns without military deference or a new classification tier. She would sustain those three dispositions while allowing only the religion claim to return; she infers neither sodomy nor refusal to perform duties.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissenting.** The dissent assumes the association rights for decision and finds the particular legal office’s preventive professional judgment sufficient; it adopts no categorical immunity for government lawyers or license for bare hostility. On free exercise it reads the supported decision as responding to the represented relationship and perceived work consequences, regardless of religious form, and distinguishes Brown’s direct religion-specific commands. Smith and Lukumi forbid religious discrimination but do not convert every effect on religious exercise into prohibited targeting. The dissent also finds the particular professional concerns sufficient under ordinary rationality. It would affirm without a damages-immunity ruling or a finding that the ceremony proves unlawful sexual conduct.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for the four claim-specific inquiries and properly remaining defenses and remedies. The judgment supplies no final employment entitlement, personal liability or constitutional damages. No additional speech or substantive-due-process claim is revived. This Supreme Court review is complete.
