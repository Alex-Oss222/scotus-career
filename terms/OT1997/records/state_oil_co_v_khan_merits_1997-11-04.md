## Decision kernel

Review under §1254(1) concerns the Seventh Circuit's antitrust disposition, 93 F.3d 1358 (1996). Khan's supply agreement required return to State Oil of revenue above its suggested retail ceiling, while allowing a lower price at Khan's expense. ## Public Projection

## Event

State Oil Co. v. Khan, No. 96-871. Merits decision, November 4, 1997; argued October 7, 1997; October Term 1997, chunk 1. On certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), the Court reviews the Seventh Circuit's antitrust disposition, 93 F.3d 1358. The question is whether a vertical agreement setting a maximum resale price is unlawful per se under Sherman Act §1 and whether Albrecht should remain controlling.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The antitrust disposition is vacated and the case remanded for rule-of-reason review. Albrecht's maximum-vertical-price per se rule is overruled.

**Vacated and remanded on the antitrust component, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. **Opposing:** none. ## Holdings

**Vertical maximum-price agreements receive rule-of-reason review.** A vertical agreement imposing a maximum resale price is evaluated for its competitive effects rather than condemned per se merely for imposing that ceiling. Albrecht is overruled to that extent. Scalia's opinion has all nine Justices' full joins and controls both the replacement rule and its limits.

Per se condemnation needs a sound basis for treating the category as predictably harmful. A ceiling can restrict services or facilitate an unlawful arrangement, but it can also restrain dealer markups and promote interbrand competition. Sylvania's treatment of vertical distribution and Atlantic Richfield's distinction between price benefits and cognizable injury undermine conclusive condemnation of the entire class. The identified harmful effects remain matters for proof under the rule appropriate to the actual restraint.

Statutory precedent deserves substantial respect. Here the defect is concrete: Albrecht's categorical premise prevents examination of arrangements whose competitive effects differ. The reliance interest in retaining categorical condemnation is limited because antitrust remedies remain available under the replacement standard. Lower courts properly followed Albrecht until this Court changed it; their duty of obedience is not a reason to preserve an unsound classification here.

State Oil's rebate obligation creates an actual price agreement. It cannot be relabeled a unilateral suggestion to avoid the precedent question. The rule change does not determine market power, competitive harm, injury, damages, or the expert evidence's ultimate significance. Harmful service suppression, exclusion, exploitation of dealer dependence, disguised minimum prices, and proved horizontal arrangements remain subject to the governing rules. Whether any such circumstance occurred here remains for the appropriate further proceedings. Minimum resale-price and horizontal price-fixing rules are unchanged; a dealer's lost margin alone is not antitrust injury.

## Precedent Treatment

- Albrecht v. Herald Co., 390 U.S. 145, is expressly overruled insofar as it makes vertical maximum resale-price agreements unlawful per se.
- Sylvania and Business Electronics retain their distinct treatment of vertical restraints and actual price agreements; their principles inform the revised classification without resolving every arrangement.
- Atlantic Richfield and Brunswick retain the independent antitrust-injury requirement.
- Kodak and Brooke Group retain their separate evidence and competitive-harm rules. The decision creates no general stare decisis checklist from Hubbard's divided rationales.

## Law After Decision

From November 4, 1997, vertical maximum resale-price agreements receive rule-of-reason review. They gain no immunity: actual competitive effects, injury, and other applicable requirements remain necessary. Minimum-price and horizontal restraints keep their distinct rules.
