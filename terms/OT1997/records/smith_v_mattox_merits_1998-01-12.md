## Holdings

**1. The separable legal immunity question is reviewable on the proper assumed account.** The Court may decide whether the plaintiff-favorable conduct was clearly established as unlawful without resolving whether that conduct occurred. It may not select between accounts of compliance, continued resistance or gun-reaching, decide credibility or review evidentiary sufficiency. Part I is unanimous.

Johnson and Behrens preserve this legal/factual division, and Clanton confirms that disputed evidence does not eliminate review of a genuine legal defense. Section 1254 does not enlarge the circuit's §1291 authority. No separate factual-sufficiency component requiring dismissal is established here. The Court accepts the proper assumptions solely to decide this appeal.

**2. The particular assumed force was obviously outside the lawful bounds in June 1993.** Under Graham v. Connor, force during arrest must be objectively reasonable in the actual circumstances from the officer's contemporaneous perspective, including the severity of the crime, immediate threat and active resistance or flight. The considerable additional arm force and reported blow against Smith while wholly unresisting on the assumed account were apparently unlawful under that pre-existing rule; an identical earlier handcuffing-injury decision is unnecessary. Part II is unanimous.

The earlier bat threat, initial refusal, flight and feigned renewed flight justified caution and some restraining force. Submission did not establish completed cuffing or make all further force unlawful. But the assumed account describes docility during the maneuver, an arm pull, a complaint of discomfort, and a grunt and blow followed by multiple fractures requiring surgery. Those circumstances together, rather than the fracture alone, exceed the remaining latitude for reasonable error. The competing reliance on a dangerous encounter cannot insert continued resistance, gun-reaching or an unassumed safety mistake into this account.

Harlow and Anderson require particularized notice, not merely invocation of a general right to bodily security. Graham supplied the force rule before June 11, 1993. Elder and Lanier explain the present inquiry into relevant authority and obvious coverage; neither supplies retrospective notice. No exhaustive finding about the absence of all earlier authority is necessary, and the Court adopts no exclusive factually-similar-case route. Subjective anger is neither assumed nor required. Injury alone, bad purpose and hindsight do not determine the result.

**3. Denial on these assumptions leaves the received defense and ordinary proceedings intact.** Harlow and Anderson remain the governing objective qualified-immunity framework. Mattox may establish a materially different supported account, including continuing resistance or necessary force, through ordinary proceedings and may obtain lawful fact-dependent reconsideration of immunity. The complete retained-framework holding in Part III has all eight Associate Justices; all nine support the bounded affirmance and these ordinary-proceedings limits.

Crawford-El preserves ordinary competent proof and independent defenses but adds neither a motive element nor retaliation's burden sequence to Graham.
