## Controlling holdings

### Patient exposure and the distinct reduction-of-care theory

**Holding and operative rule:** At summary judgment, a plaintiff seeking prospective relief must support a concrete, actual or imminent personal injury with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, allowing favorable reasonable inferences. Elsner's progressive illness and prior depression, even with statutory eligibility and the general medical assertions assumed, do not establish sufficiently impending exposure to an unwanted assisted death; likewise, an assumed reduction of medical-care protection does not establish injury without supported personal exposure to changed care or threatened harm from that reduction.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part I-A has all nine Justices. Part I-B, addressing the separate care-protection theory, has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in that disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires evidence connecting the particular plaintiff to the threatened harm at the litigation stage, and Lyons distinguishes an existing personal threat from speculation about future exposure. The seriousness of death does not relax that requirement. Elsner's illness and depression make her concern personal, but do not establish the requests, medical and witness decisions, continuing impairment and ingestion necessary for the injury alleged. This conclusion does not rest on certainty, a numerical probability, a particular document, or a finding that safeguards never fail. The Court assumes rather than finds her six-month qualification and the reported general medical propositions. The separate good-faith-immunity theory fares no better: even assuming a reduction from ordinary reasonable-care protection, no actual changed care or sufficiently impending personal injury from it is established. Lujan permits a procedural protection linked to a threatened concrete interest without certainty that procedure changes the ultimate result; the asserted legal alteration cannot itself supply that missing connection. Section 4.02's retained negligence and misconduct liability is not erased by the assumed construction of §4.01(1). The statutory construction, substantive adequacy of safeguards, and patient-claim traceability and redressability remain undecided. Dismissal is without prejudice.

### Continuing controversy, representation and the proposed class

**Holding and operative rule:** Dutson's personal prospective controversy does not continue after his death on this record, which establishes no surviving personal claim, substitute or certified class. A provider's relationship to patients and a proposed class never certified cannot supply the represented patients' missing injury or an independently continuing controversy.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by all seven other Associate Justices, controls with eight votes; Stone-Zsela agrees in these dispositions only.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans requires a live personal controversy throughout adjudication and keeps corrective jurisdiction distinct from constitutional merits power. Dutson died before the District Court's judgment, and the record establishes no surviving basis for his prospective claim. That conclusion does not dispose of another claimant's independently viable controversy. Powers keeps the representative's own stake, close relationship and hindrance to the third party distinct. The doctors' relationship to vulnerable patients cannot create an injury those patients have not shown; nor can an uncertified proposed class replace a missing injured claimant. The Court adopts no categorical prohibition on provider representation, universal class relation-back rule or damages-pleading requirement.

### Compelled participation: injury and ripeness

**Holding and operative rule:** Absence of an express criminal or statutory penalty does not by itself defeat Article III injury: an actually applicable legal obligation or supported threatened civil enforcement may burden present conduct. Fitness and hardship must both be examined; predominantly legal questions do not alone establish hardship, but the possibility of a later civil-enforcement suit does not categorically require postponing every challenge to a presently burdensome legal duty. The participation claims must be reconsidered under these principles without a present finding of standing or ripeness.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part III-A states the legal boundary with all eight Associate Justices joining. Part III-B's application and remand command six direct votes: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Thomas oppose the remand; no combination of separate rationales is needed for controlling authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires concrete personal application, not punishment as a distinct element. Babbitt rejects forcing a properly situated claimant to undergo prosecution, but its applicable-prohibition setting does not establish injury under these conditional civil provisions. Abbott Laboratories requires both fitness and hardship, and Toilet Goods rests on its actual enforcement and review setting. The Court of Appeals assumed the claimed duties, made absence of penalties decisive, regarded imminence as probably sufficient if injury were cognizable, and carried the same premise into its ripeness ruling. Its acknowledgment of possible civil enforcement did not resolve a present compelled-conduct burden. The strongest basis for affirmance is the absence of a reported current request or enforcement threat. Yet the appellate court's assumed-duty analysis does not establish a sufficient independent determination of concrete application under the correct rule. Further consideration must distinguish §4.01(4)'s express freedom to refuse participation from its transfer-upon-request obligation when care transfers, §3.01(2)'s attending-physician context, and §2.02(4)'s long-term-care witness provision. The lawful record must establish any actual duty and burden, the proper defendants' relationship to it, traceability, redressability, fitness and hardship. The Court finds no duty to prescribe or recommend assistance and no present constitutional or statutory violation. Remand commands neither a new hearing nor additional evidence or filings.

### Facility association

**Holding and operative rule:** The facilities' challenge to §4.01(2)'s alleged restriction on excluding participating physicians lacks actual or imminent injury where no intending participating physician at the facilities or present operational restriction is supported. That separate dismissal remains operative despite remand of the compelled-participation claims.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part IV has all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan and Lyons require actual or sufficiently impending personal application. The allegation that a statute could restrict a facility's response to future participation does not establish that any physician there intends to participate or that the facility presently confronts the claimed conflict. An identified conflict or supported current burden would present a different case; general opposition to participation does not establish either. This application neither construes the anti-penalty provision conclusively nor adjudicates association or religious-freedom merits.

### The District Court's declaration and injunction remain vacated

**Holding and operative rule:** Limited corrective jurisdiction permits clearing a constitutional merits judgment unsupported by a live injured claimant, and the equities here require leaving vacated the District Court's patient-based equal-protection declaration and permanent injunction. Reconsideration of a distinct, previously undecided provider claim does not restore that judgment or establish a new entitlement to relief.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part V has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in leaving the judgment and injunction vacated only.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans distinguishes jurisdictional correction from merits jurisdiction; Bender and Corrick supply that limited corrective authority. Bancorp makes vacatur equitable rather than automatic, with Munsingwear retaining its judgment-clearing role in the proper setting. Here no established live injured claimant supports the patient merits adjudication, and retaining its constitutional declaration and permanent injunction would give effect to a merits determination without the necessary controversy. Section 2106 does not supply substantive jurisdiction. The possibility that a different direct-provider claim may prove justiciable neither cures the patient defect nor resolves that claim's merits. The Court adopts no rule automatically vacating every judgment whenever jurisdiction is disputed and orders no restored or replacement injunction.

Fitness and hardship must both be examined; predominantly legal questions do not alone establish hardship, but the possibility of a later civil-enforcement suit does not categorically require postponing every challenge to a presently burdensome legal duty. The participation claims must be reconsidered under these principles without a present finding of standing or ripeness.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part III-A states the legal boundary with all eight Associate Justices joining. Part III-B's application and remand command six direct votes: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Thomas oppose the remand; no combination of separate rationales is needed for controlling authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires concrete personal application, not punishment as a distinct element. Babbitt rejects forcing a properly situated claimant to undergo prosecution, but its applicable-prohibition setting does not establish injury under these conditional civil provisions. Abbott Laboratories requires both fitness and hardship, and Toilet Goods rests on its actual enforcement and review setting. The Court of Appeals assumed the claimed duties, made absence of penalties decisive, regarded imminence as probably sufficient if injury were cognizable, and carried the same premise into its ripeness ruling. Its acknowledgment of possible civil enforcement did not resolve a present compelled-conduct burden. The strongest basis for affirmance is the absence of a reported current request or enforcement threat. Yet the appellate court's assumed-duty analysis does not establish a sufficient independent determination of concrete application under the correct rule. Further consideration must distinguish §4.01(4)'s express freedom to refuse participation from its transfer-upon-request obligation when care transfers, §3.01(2)'s attending-physician context, and §2.02(4)'s long-term-care witness provision. The lawful record must establish any actual duty and burden, the proper defendants' relationship to it, traceability, redressability, fitness and hardship. The Court finds no duty to prescribe or recommend assistance and no present constitutional or statutory violation. Remand commands neither a new hearing nor additional evidence or filings.

### Facility association

**Holding and operative rule:** The facilities' challenge to §4.01(2)'s alleged restriction on excluding participating physicians lacks actual or imminent injury where no intending participating physician at the facilities or present operational restriction is supported. That separate dismissal remains operative despite remand of the compelled-participation claims.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part IV has all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan and Lyons require actual or sufficiently impending personal application. The allegation that a statute could restrict a facility's response to future participation does not establish that any physician there intends to participate or that the facility presently confronts the claimed conflict. An identified conflict or supported current burden would present a different case; general opposition to participation does not establish either. This application neither construes the anti-penalty provision conclusively nor adjudicates association or religious-freedom merits.

### The District Court's declaration and injunction remain vacated

**Holding and operative rule:** Limited corrective jurisdiction permits clearing a constitutional merits judgment unsupported by a live injured claimant, and the equities here require leaving vacated the District Court's patient-based equal-protection declaration and permanent injunction. Reconsideration of a distinct, previously undecided provider claim does not restore that judgment or establish a new entitlement to relief.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part V has all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in leaving the judgment and injunction vacated only.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans distinguishes jurisdictional correction from merits jurisdiction; Bender and Corrick supply that limited corrective authority. Bancorp makes vacatur equitable rather than automatic, with Munsingwear retaining its judgment-clearing role in the proper setting. Here no established live injured claimant supports the patient merits adjudication, and retaining its constitutional declaration and permanent injunction would give effect to a merits determination without the necessary controversy. Section 2106 does not supply substantive jurisdiction. The possibility that a different direct-provider claim may prove justiciable neither cures the patient defect nor resolves that claim's merits. The Court adopts no rule automatically vacating every judgment whenever jurisdiction is disputed and orders no restored or replacement injunction.

## Precedent Treatment

- Lujan is applied for specific summary-judgment support, reasonable favorable inferences and the concrete-interest requirement. Its ordinary injury, traceability and redressability elements remain intact; its noncontrolling absent-agency application is not adopted.
- Lyons and Whitmore retain the requirement of supported actual or imminent personal exposure; severity of a possible harm does not replace that connection.
- Powers is applied without combining its own-stake, close-relationship and hindrance requirements or creating a universal bar to provider representation.
- Arizonans is applied for continuing controversy and limited jurisdictional correction. Bender and Corrick retain their corrective role; Bancorp retains equitable vacatur, and Munsingwear its role in clearing judgments in the proper circumstances.
- Abbott Laboratories is applied to both fitness and hardship; Toilet Goods is confined to its actual enforcement and review setting. Babbitt supplies protection against requiring completed prosecution, without establishing that these conditional duties actually apply.
- Vacco and Glucksberg remain decisions permitting their specified prohibitions. Neither requires prohibition, validates Oregon's safeguards or supplies opponents' standing.
- Tanford's actual continuing exposure, Jeffries's live-relief boundary, Chicago's distinction between jurisdiction and merits, and Rouse's supported-threat requirement remain intact; none decides this statute's duties.

## Law After Decision

A civil obligation may create a concrete present burden without an express statutory penalty, but injury still requires supported personal application. Participation claims require their own injury and fitness/hardship analysis; the Court establishes no presumption of provider standing. The patient, care-protection, representation and association dismissals apply existing requirements to their separate deficiencies. The merits of Measure 16 under equal protection, due process, the First Amendment, the ADA, the Rehabilitation Act and RFRA remain unadjudicated. The statute's substantive duties and immunity scope remain open, as do the proper-defendant, traceability, redressability and ripeness questions on remand. The additional informed-consent-law theory under 42 U.S.C. §1395cc(f)(1)(A)(i) and O.R.S. §677.097 receives no new merits ruling or preserved claim.

## Separate Writings

Scalia, joined by Thomas, agrees that absence of an express penalty is not a categorical injury bar, but would affirm the participation dismissal. Assuming an obligation that arises upon a possible future request does not establish that these plaintiffs presently confront it. The reported absence of a concrete trigger, present burden or supported enforcement threat defeats injury, and absence of supported current hardship independently defeats ripeness despite predominantly legal fitness. The appellate court's conditional observation about imminence does not establish present application. Their position does not foreclose a later action supported by concrete jurisdictional facts.

Stone-Zsela would affirm dismissal without prejudice in full. The patient chain depends on future eligibility, requests, independent medical decisions, safeguard failures and ingestion contrary to true wishes. The providers and facilities identify no present request, concrete duty, compliance cost or credible enforcement threat. In his view that unformed chain prevents identification of injury, causation or a ripe dispute, so remand is unwarranted. He reaches no assisted-death merits and preserves a later appropriate challenge by an eligible patient facing imminent application, a provider confronting a concrete statutory duty and credible enforcement, or a facility with an identified participation conflict.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Court of Appeals for further proceedings confined to the compelled-participation claims. Both their standing and ripeness dispositions must be reconsidered under the correct rules, including separate statutory conditions, concrete burden, proper defendants, traceability and redressability. The other dismissals remain without prejudice, and the District Court's patient-based declaration and permanent injunction remain vacated. No merits judgment, new injunction, compulsory hearing, evidence-production order, additional filing requirement or automatic return to this Court follows. This Supreme Court review is complete.
