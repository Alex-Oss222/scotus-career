# Estelle v. McGuire, No. 90-1074

**Render form: full — 5-3 judgment and a material change from the comparator.**

## 1. Event
*Estelle v. McGuire*, 502 U.S. 62 (1991), No. 90-1074. Argued October 9 and decided December 4, 1991, on certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 902 F.2d 749. Questions: whether prior-injury evidence, or the charge governing its use, made the murder trial fundamentally unfair or relieved California of proving McGuire's identity as the killer.

## 2. Participation
Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas took no part because argument preceded his oath.

## 3. Public Action
The Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy
| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Federal habeas judgment | Reversed, 5-3 | White, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter | Stone, Stevens, O'Connor | The habeas writ remains denied; no acquittal, retrial, or release is ordered. Stone, Stevens, and O'Connor would vacate and remand for harmless-error review. |

## 5. Opinion Topology
| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | White | Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter throughout; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor as to the prior-injury-evidence proposition | Controls reversal and both holdings | Stone, Stevens, and O'Connor do not join the instruction or final-disposition portions |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | O'Connor | Stone, Stevens | Agrees on evidence; would vacate rather than reverse | Instructional error and harmless-review remedy |

## 6. Holdings
### Prior-injury evidence
**Holding and operative rule:** On federal habeas review, evidence of a child's prior nonaccidental injuries may be relevant to prove that the charged death was intentional even when the person who caused the earlier injuries is unknown. Admission for that nonidentity purpose does not itself violate due process, and a defendant's choice not to contest accident does not remove the State's burden to prove intentional killing.

**Authority:** White's opinion joined on this proposition by all eight participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Habeas relief does not lie for an error of state evidence law alone. The prosecution retained the burden to prove an intentional killing, and the repeated injuries supplied evidence that the fatal injury was not accidental. That inference did not itself identify McGuire as the abuser, but relevance to one required proposition does not disappear because the defense concentrates on another. *Winship* supplies the State's continuing elemental burden; *Lisenba* supplies the narrow fundamental-unfairness boundary. The Court does not create a general constitutional rule governing propensity evidence and does not decide whether California correctly applied its own evidence law. The Ninth Circuit therefore could not grant relief on the evidence-alone theory.

### Instructional use
**Holding and operative rule:** An ambiguous instruction violates due process only when, read with the charge and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood the jury applied it in a way that relieved the State of proving an element. This instruction did not create that likelihood because it conditioned any inference on a finding that McGuire committed the prior acts and expressly prohibited a bad-character inference.

**Authority:** White's opinion joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; five of eight participants.

**Controlling explanation:** *Cupp* requires examination of the complete charge rather than isolated words, and *Boyde* requires a reasonable likelihood of unconstitutional application rather than a conceivable misunderstanding. The charge used conditional language and told jurors not to infer guilt from bad character. It did not direct them to find that McGuire caused the earlier injuries or shift the prosecution's burden to prove identity and every element. *Francis* and *Sandstrom* remain controlling where a charge creates a mandatory presumption or shifts elemental persuasion, but this charge did neither. Because there was no constitutional error, harmless review was unnecessary. The Court does not hold every prior-act instruction valid or decide a general constitutional propensity prohibition.

## 7. Precedent Treatment
*Lewis*, *Pulley*, *Lisenba*, and *Dowling* are applied; *Winship* is applied and unchanged; *Cupp* and *Boyde* govern whole-charge and reasonable-likelihood review; *Francis* and *Sandstrom* are distinguished and preserved. The majority does not reach *Teague*, *Rose*, or *Chapman*.

## 8. Law After Decision
The evidence and instruction rules enter current law December 4, 1991. Relevance to nonaccidental injury does not establish identity, and a general constitutional propensity-evidence rule remains open.

## 9. Separate Writings
O'Connor, joined by Stone and Stevens, agrees that the evidence had a lawful nonidentity use but would hold that grouping unidentified grave injuries as acts of “the Defendant,” followed by an invited inference to the charged killing, created a reasonable likelihood of bypassing the identity finding. They would vacate and remand for harmless-error review beyond a reasonable doubt rather than order automatic reversal.

## 10. Procedure After Action
The conviction and denial of federal habeas relief stand. The Supreme Court and federal habeas proceedings are closed.

# Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni, No. 90-584

**Render form: compact — 8-0 judgment and no material comparator change.**

## 1. Event
*Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni*, 502 U.S. 81 (1991), No. 90-584. Argued October 15 and decided December 4, 1991, on certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 909 F.2d 385. Question: whether an LHWCA-listed occupation or voluntary benefit receipt forecloses a Jones Act claim by a worker who may be a vessel crew member.

## 2. Participation
Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas took no part because argument preceded his oath.

## 3. Public Action
The Ninth Circuit judgment is affirmed.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy
8-0. All eight participating Justices support affirmance. Summary judgment remains reversed; status and any viable injury claims return for ordinary determination. No damages or status finding is made, and overlapping recovery remains subject to statutory credit.

## 5. Opinion Topology
White delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by all participating Justices. No separate writing.

## 6. Holdings
**Holding and operative rule:** Employment in an occupation listed by the LHWCA does not foreclose Jones Act seaman status because the LHWCA expressly excludes a vessel's master or crew. Receipt of voluntary LHWCA benefits without a final status adjudication does not preclude a Jones Act suit; overlapping recovery remains subject to statutory credit.

**Authority:** White's unanimous participating-Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The occupational definition and crew exclusion must be read together. A ship-repair worker may fall outside LHWCA coverage by proving crew status under *Wilander*, which requires an employment-related connection to a vessel in navigation and contribution to its work without traditional navigational duties. Gizoni's claimed platform assignment, work aboard the platforms, and towing role create genuine disputes that occupational labels, lack of propulsion, and absence of living quarters do not conclusively resolve. *Tipton* establishes that voluntary benefits do not decide seaman status. Congress supplied mechanisms for moving between remedies and crediting payments, not an election rule forcing refusal of immediate compensation. A final award, settlement, conclusive record, ultimate status, negligence, unseaworthiness, maintenance and cure, and damages remain open.

## 7. Precedent Treatment
*Wilander*, *Swanson*, and *Tipton* are applied and unchanged. *MCorp* remains confined to its express bank-regulatory review statute.

## 8. Law After Decision
Effective December 4, listed occupational work and voluntary, unadjudicated LHWCA benefits are not categorical bars to a Jones Act status claim.

## 9. Separate Writings
None.

## 10. Procedure After Action
The Supreme Court proceeding is closed. The case returns for factfinding on status and any viable injury claims.

## 11. Source Notes
Official United States Reports text, 502 U.S. 81-92, read in full; Internet Archive item `micro_IA40385013_0030`; research cutoff September 14, 2026.

# Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71, No. 90-967

**Render form: compact — 8-0 judgment and no material comparator change.**

## 1. Event
*Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71*, 502 U.S. 93 (1991), No. 90-967. Argued October 16 and decided December 4, 1991, on certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, 907 F.2d 151. Questions: jury entitlement on an LMRDA damages claim and §301 coverage of a member-beneficiary's claim under an interunion constitution.

## 2. Participation
Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas took no part because argument preceded his oath.

## 3. Public Action
The Sixth Circuit judgment is reversed and the case remanded.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy
8-0 on both reviewed questions. All participating Justices support reversal. The union-constitution claim is restored for §301 proceedings, and a jury is permitted on the LMRDA legal damages claim. No liability or amount is awarded.

## 5. Opinion Topology
White delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by all participating Justices. No separate writing.

## 6. Holdings
### Jury entitlement
**Holding and operative rule:** A claim for wages and benefits lost because hiring-hall referrals allegedly were withheld seeks compensatory legal relief and carries a Seventh Amendment jury right. Accompanying equitable requests do not eliminate that right on legal issues.

**Authority:** White's unanimous participating-Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** *Terry* supplies the historical-analogue inquiry and makes the remedy's character more important. Wooddell seeks replacement for earnings that retaliation allegedly prevented him from obtaining, not return of identified funds held by the union or backpay incidental to reinstatement. That compensation has the character of legal damages. Combining it with an injunction does not allow the court to withhold a jury; legal and equitable issues instead receive proper sequencing. The holding decides the factfinder, not whether retaliation occurred or what amount is recoverable.

### Section 301 coverage
**Holding and operative rule:** Section 301 permits an individual beneficiary of a qualifying contract between labor organizations to sue for its violation. An international constitution binding the international and its local retains its interunion-contract character when a member invokes it.

**Authority:** White's unanimous participating-Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 301 identifies covered contracts, not an exclusive class of organizational plaintiffs. *Local 334* classifies an international constitution as a contract between labor organizations, and *Evening News* permits individual beneficiaries to enforce agreements within §301. Uniform federal interpretation serves the same function regardless of whether a union or beneficiary brings the claim. Ordinary contract predicates remain necessary. The Court does not federalize every internal dispute, classify standalone local bylaws, or decide breach, defenses, or recoverable loss.

## 7. Precedent Treatment
*Terry*, *Local 334*, and *Evening News* are applied and preserved.

## 8. Law After Decision
Effective December 4, compensatory lost-referral damages carry a jury right, and member-beneficiaries may enforce qualifying interunion constitutions through §301. Liability and equitable-remedy questions remain distinct.

## 9. Separate Writings
None.

## 10. Procedure After Action
The Supreme Court proceeding is closed. Both claims return for further proceedings consistent with the judgment.

## 11. Source Notes
Official United States Reports text, 502 U.S. 93-103, read in full; Internet Archive item `micro_IA40385013_0058`; research cutoff September 14, 2026.

# Gibson v. Florida Bar, No. 90-1102

**Render form: full — mixed component judgment and material departure from the comparator.**

## 1. Event
*Gibson v. Florida Bar*, 502 U.S. 104 (1991), No. 90-1102. Argued November 6 and decided December 4, 1991, on certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, 906 F.2d 624. Question: whether Florida Bar rule 2-9.3 adequately protects compulsory-dues payers who object to nongermane political lobbying. The prospective dispute remains live; an unpreserved retroactive-refund request is outside review.

## 2. Participation
All nine Justices participated: Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas.

## 3. Public Action
The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy
| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Protection before use | Reversed, 8-1 | Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas | Scalia | Prospective lawful reduction or escrow required on remand. |
| General rather than position-by-position objection | Reversed, 8-1 | Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas | Scalia | A continuing general objection suffices. |
| Facial challenge to Bar-member arbitrators | Affirmed, 9-0 | All nine Justices | None | Membership alone does not invalidate the balanced panel. |

The lower court's correction requiring interest from collection remains intact. No retroactive damages are awarded.

## 5. Opinion Topology
| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone | White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas | Controls every component | Timing, general objection, panel membership, remedy |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Scalia | — | Joins panel component and preserved interest correction; opposes reversal on timing and repeated objections | Would affirm position-specific objection and post-objection escrow |

## 6. Holdings
### Timing and scope of protection
**Holding and operative rule:** Once a compulsory-bar member makes a continuing objection to financing nongermane ideological activity, the bar may not demand repeated objections to each announced position or leave the reasonably disputed share available for use until each new objection. It must explain anticipated chargeable and nonchargeable activity and protect disputed funds before use through a reasonable reduction or adequate interest-bearing escrow; the bar bears the chargeability burden.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight of nine participants.

**Controlling explanation:** *Keller* applies compelled-dues safeguards to an integrated bar while preserving compulsory support for germane activities. *Hudson* requires explanation, prompt review, and protection of disputed funds, and *Ellis* establishes that reimbursement and interest do not undo temporary ideological use. Florida collects annual dues before its twice-monthly announcements and begins escrow only after a member objects to a particular position. Budget estimates and protection of a reasonably sufficient amount can address uncertainty without exact advance prediction. Under *Abood*, the member must make opposition known, but the organization then bears the chargeability burden. Repeated 45-day waivers improperly restore a monitoring burden to a known dissenter. Accounting detail and prospective injunction terms remain for remand; the Court adopts no universal opt-in system.

### Impartial review
**Holding and operative rule:** Bar membership alone does not establish that every arbitrator chosen through a procedure giving the bar and objector one selection each and requiring a mutually selected or judicially appointed chair is constitutionally biased. Actual bias, direct stake, or obstructive delay remains reviewable.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion joined on this proposition by all nine participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** *Hudson* requires reasonably prompt impartial review. The Bar cannot select the panel unilaterally: each side chooses one member, neither controls the chair, and judicial appointment is available when agreement fails. A shared and indirect incidence of bar dues is not by itself a direct stake in a particular award. The Court does not approve an actually biased panel, indefinite delay in constituting it, or a system that fails in practice.

## 7. Precedent Treatment
*Keller*, *Hudson*, *Ellis*, and *Abood* are applied and preserved.

## 8. Law After Decision
Effective December 4, an integrated bar receiving a general objection must protect reasonably disputed nongermane amounts before use and may not require recurring position-specific objections. Balanced selection of Bar-member arbitrators is not facially invalid on membership alone.

## 9. Separate Writings
Scalia would permit objection after each disclosed lobbying position followed by escrow and arbitration, reasoning that uncertain future activity need not be estimated in advance. He agrees that Bar membership alone does not disqualify arbitrators and that the lower court's interest correction remains.

## 10. Procedure After Action
The Supreme Court proceeding is closed. The case returns for prospective relief permitting a lawful reduction or escrow system, preserving interest from collection, and excluding unpreserved retroactive damages.

## 11. Source Notes
Eleventh Circuit opinion and rule text, 906 F.2d 624; Internet Archive item `micro_IA40385013_0071`; reporter order at 502 U.S. 104; research cutoff September 14, 2026. The merits decision, votes, and writings are simulation outputs.

# Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, No. 90-1059

**Render form: compact — 8-0 judgment and no material comparator change.**

## 1. Event
*Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board*, 502 U.S. 105 (1991), No. 90-1059. Argued October 15 and decided December 10, 1991, on certiorari to the Second Circuit, 916 F.2d 777. Question: whether New York Executive Law §632-a unconstitutionally burdens speech by escrowing income from a person's account of crime.

## 2. Participation
Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas took no part because argument preceded his oath.

## 3. Public Action
The Second Circuit judgment is reversed.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy
8-0. All participating Justices support reversal. The case returns for appropriate declaratory and injunctive relief against applying §632-a to the contract. No award to Hill or discharge of victim or creditor claims is ordered.

## 5. Opinion Topology
O'Connor delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter. Blackmun and Kennedy each concurred in the judgment.

## 6. Holdings
**Holding and operative rule:** A law selecting crime-related expression for a special financial escrow is content based and must be necessary and narrowly drawn to serve a compelling interest. Section 632-a fails because it burdens an entire covered contract, including incidental discussion and unprosecuted admissions, while leaving other assets outside its special reach; it may not be enforced against this publishing contract.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter; six of eight participants.

**Controlling explanation:** The obligation arises from what the work says and restrains payment to its source, affecting a publisher's access to protected expression without requiring suppressive purpose. *Arkansas Writers' Project* supplies scrutiny for content-based financial burdens, and *Leathers* distinguishes general economic regulation from selective burdens keyed to expression. Victim compensation and recovery of crime proceeds are compelling at their strongest, but §632-a is not fitted to them. It captures all income under a contract when crime appears incidentally and reaches admissions never prosecuted. Excluding victimless crimes and releasing limited expenses do not cure that breadth. Neutral restitution, forfeiture, attachment, and collection can pursue compensation without this classification. The Court decides only this statute and leaves materially different laws, stolen property, general judgments, and victim claims open.

## 7. Precedent Treatment
*Arkansas Writers' Project* and *Leathers* are applied; *Caplin & Drysdale* is distinguished. *Gibson* remains confined to compelled bar financing.

## 8. Law After Decision
Effective December 10, §632-a's content-based proceeds escrow is unconstitutional; neutral asset-recovery and creditor rules remain available.

## 9. Separate Writings
Blackmun generally agrees but emphasizes that the statute is underinclusive as well as overinclusive. Kennedy would invalidate the direct content-based burden on fully protected speech without using the Court's compelling-interest balancing formulation.

## 10. Procedure After Action
The Supreme Court proceeding is closed. The case returns for appropriate declaratory and injunctive relief.

Official United States Reports text, 502 U.S. 105-128, read in full; Internet Archive item `micro_IA40385013_0068`; research cutoff September 14, 2026.

# Ardestani v. INS, No. 90-1141

**Render form: compact — 6-2 judgment and no material comparator change.**

## 1. Event
*Ardestani v. INS*, 502 U.S. 129 (1991), No. 90-1141. Argued October 8 and decided December 10, 1991, on certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, 904 F.2d 1505. Question: whether an administrative deportation hearing is an adjudication under 5 U.S.C. §554 for EAJA §504.

## 2. Participation
Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas took no part because argument preceded his oath.

## 3. Public Action
The Eleventh Circuit judgment is affirmed.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy
6-2. Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter support affirmance; Blackmun and Stevens dissent. No administrative fee award is available under §504. Ardestani's asylum grant remains undisturbed.

## 5. Opinion Topology
O'Connor delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter. Blackmun dissented, joined by Stevens.

## 6. Holdings
**Holding and operative rule:** An administrative deportation hearing governed by the INA's exclusive procedure rather than by 5 U.S.C. §554 is not an adjudication “under section 554” and is outside EAJA §504's administrative fee authorization, even if it resembles a formal APA adjudication.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion joined by Stone, White, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of eight participants.

**Controlling explanation:** EAJA did not refer generally to formal or trial-type agency hearings; it chose adjudications “under section 554.” *Marcello* establishes that the INA deportation procedure supersedes APA hearing provisions and supplies the exclusive source for this proceeding. Functional similarity and agency regulations cannot enlarge Congress's fee authorization. EAJA's remedial purpose supports protection through enacted language but cannot erase the selected cross-reference. *Sullivan v. Hudson* involved administrative work integral to a qualifying court action and supplies no predicate for this standalone request. Because coverage fails, the Court does not decide whether the agency's position was substantially justified under *Pierce*. The asylum award, covered judicial EAJA claims, and any separately presented constitutional claim remain open.

## 7. Precedent Treatment
*Marcello* is applied; *Sullivan v. Hudson* is distinguished; *Shaw* and *Pierce* remain unchanged.

## 8. Law After Decision
Effective December 10, INA deportation hearings outside §554 governance do not qualify for administrative EAJA §504 awards.

## 9. Separate Writings
Blackmun, joined by Stevens, would treat “under section 554” as a functional category encompassing statutorily required, on-the-record adversary adjudications at which the United States is represented.

## 10. Procedure After Action
The Supreme Court and administrative fee proceedings are closed. The asylum grant and its factual basis remain undisturbed.

Official United States Reports text, 502 U.S. 129-150, read in full; Internet Archive item `micro_IA40385013_0074`; research cutoff September 14, 2026.

# Union Bank v. Wolas, No. 90-1491

**Render form: compact — 9-0 judgment and no material comparator change.**

## 1. Event
*Union Bank v. Wolas*, 502 U.S. 151 (1991), No. 90-1491. Argued November 5 and decided December 11, 1991, on certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 921 F.2d 968. Question: whether payments on long-term debt may qualify for the then-current ordinary-course exception in 11 U.S.C. §547(c)(2).

## 2. Participation
All nine Justices participated: Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas.

## 3. Public Action
The Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed and the case remanded.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy
9-0. All nine Justices support reversal and remand. The Ninth Circuit must review any remaining challenges to application of all statutory elements; the Court does not itself restore judgment for the Bank.

## 5. Opinion Topology
Stevens delivered the opinion for a unanimous Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. Scalia also concurred separately, resting the result directly on the enacted text without extended legislative-history or policy analysis.

## 6. Holdings
**Holding and operative rule:** Payments on long-term debt may qualify for the ordinary-course exception in the then-current §547(c)(2). Debt maturity is not an additional categorical exclusion, but the creditor must prove every enacted ordinary-course element for the particular payments.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion states the proposition for all nine Justices; Scalia's concurrence agrees with the statutory holding and judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 547(c)(2) contains no distinction between short- and long-term debt. Congress removed the former 45-day restriction and retained three express ordinary-course requirements, so a court may not recreate a maturity bar from legislative purpose or perceived motivating examples. *Ron Pair* supplies the obligation to apply operative Code text when the result is not demonstrably contrary to congressional design. Equality of distribution and discouragement of destructive collection races coexist within the preference rule and its exceptions; neither permits omission of the exception's words. Eligibility does not establish that these interest and commitment-fee payments were incurred and paid in both parties' ordinary course and according to ordinary business terms. Those questions remain for remand with the Bank bearing §547(g)'s burden.

## 7. Precedent Treatment
*Ron Pair* is applied. The Ninth Circuit's categorical *CHG International* construction is rejected; no Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

## 8. Law After Decision
Effective December 11, long-term debt payments are eligible for §547(c)(2), subject to all three contemporaneous elements and the creditor's burden.

## 9. Separate Writings
Scalia agrees with the holding and judgment but would decide directly from the unambiguous text without relying on legislative history or policy balancing.

## 10. Procedure After Action
The Supreme Court proceeding is closed. The preference litigation returns to the Ninth Circuit for review of the remaining statutory issues.

Official United States Reports text, 502 U.S. 151-163, read in full; Internet Archive item `micro_IA40385013_0094`; research cutoff September 14, 2026.
