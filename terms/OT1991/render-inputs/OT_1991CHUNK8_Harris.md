## Vasquez v. Harris, No. A-768

### Event

Vasquez v. Harris, No. A-768. October Term 1991; application to vacate execution stay, effective April 21, 1992, after Gomez A-767 and the completed A-766 action in the established sequence. Presented to Kennedy as Circuit Justice for the Ninth Circuit and referred to the Court. No oral argument or exact submission time is established. Target: the later Ninth Circuit execution stay entered April 21; its lower docket is not supplied. Questions: whether the stay can stand after the earlier Court action with no materially new claim shown in the record, and whether further federal stays of Harris's execution require this Court's order. Render form: full; the vacatur and further-stays components have different coalitions, and a separate partial dissent concerns the operative remedy.

### Participation

All nine participate at submission and decision: Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. No recusal or nonparticipation is established.

### Public Action

The application to vacate the stay of execution of sentence of death, presented to Justice Kennedy and by him referred to the Court, is granted. The order staying the execution entered by the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit on April 21, 1992 is vacated. No further stays of Robert Alton Harris's execution shall be entered by the federal courts except upon order of this Court. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents from the further-stays direction. Justice Blackmun and Justice Stevens would deny the application.

### Judgment & Remedy

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Immediate remedy and stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vacatur of the later April 21 Ninth Circuit execution stay | Granted, 7–2 | Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas | Blackmun and Stevens | The identified stay ceases to operate upon this order; no lower docket is supplied by the target recitation. |
| Further stays of Robert Alton Harris's execution | Court authorization required, 6–3 | White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas | Stone, Blackmun and Stevens | No federal court may enter a further stay of this execution except upon this Court's order. The permission route to this Court remains available. |

A-768 is concluded. The direction is immediately operative and has no stated calendar expiration; a further stay requires this Court's order. It neither grants nor prejudges such an order. It governs Harris's execution, not other prisoners or all litigation. It does not forbid filing a claim or decide its merits. No new merits remand, execution command, execution warrant, scheduled return, or actual execution is established.

The order contains two judgment coalitions; Stone joins only its vacatur component. His statement supplies no Court holding.

### Holdings

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** The Court vacates the April 21, 1992 Ninth Circuit execution stay, 7–2, with Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas supporting and Blackmun and Stevens opposing. By 6–3, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas direct that no further federal stay of Robert Alton Harris's execution may issue except upon this Court's order; Stone, Blackmun and Stevens oppose that component.

The unexplained order is binding case-specific procedure. It supplies no controlling substantive rationale, new reusable doctrine, or independently sufficient alternative holding. It leaves an avenue to obtain this Court's authorization; it does not decide the merits or sufficiency of a future request. It does not adjudicate the underlying claims, the constitutionality of gas execution, a general §1983/habeas boundary, abstention, or a new cause/prejudice or innocence rule. No fractured-decision or Marks analysis applies because no reasoned majority opinion is issued. Stone's separate qualification is noncontrolling and does not create an exception to the operative Court-authorization requirement.

### Precedent Treatment

The order announces no substantive treatment of precedent. Gomez's existing equitable holding and reservations and the governing Delo, Barefoot and McCleskey rules remain unchanged. The new authorization condition is imposed by A-768 alone; it is not attributed to Gomez or A-766.

### Law After Decision

The identified stay is vacated and the Harris-specific further-stays condition takes immediate effect. It creates no general merits rule, filing ban, or jurisdictional prohibition on presenting claims. No future claim is adjudicated.

### Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in vacating the stay and dissents from the further-stays direction. The record supplies no materially new claim or record justifying another stay that would effectively nullify the Court's disposition in Gomez. But the lower federal courts must remain able to grant a stay on a genuinely new, jurisdictionally proper claim satisfying the governing stay standard. Another attempt merely to relitigate Gomez would not warrant a stay. Blackmun and Stevens would deny the application; they issue no separate explanatory opinion.

### Procedure After Action

A-768 is concluded; the Court's authorization condition remains operative without a stated calendar expiration. Any further federal stay of Harris's execution requires this Court's order. No further application, execution, merits remand, or other sequel is established.
