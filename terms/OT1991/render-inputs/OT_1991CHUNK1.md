# United States v. Ibarra, No. 90-1713

**Render form: compact — 8-0 judgment and no material comparator change.**

## 1. Event
*United States v. Ibarra*, 502 U.S. 1 (1991), No. 90-1713. Decided October 15, 1991, without argument, on petition for certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 920 F.2d 702. Question: whether a timely Government motion to reconsider suppression postponed appellate finality despite renewing an abandoned ground.

## 2. Participation
Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. The Marshall line was vacant; Thomas had not taken the oath.

## 3. Public Action
Certiorari granted; judgment vacated; case remanded.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy
8-0. Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter supported vacatur and remand. The Tenth Circuit must consider the appeal; suppression and consent remain undecided.

## 5. Opinion Topology
Per curiam opinion joined by every participating Justice; no separate writing.

## 6. Holdings
**Holding and operative rule:** A single timely motion asking a district court to alter a suppression ruling renders that ruling nonfinal for appeal purposes until the motion is decided, even when the motion renews a ground the Government earlier abandoned. The full appeal period begins upon disposition; the rule does not decide whether the renewed ground remains waived on the merits.

**Authority:** Unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *United States v. Healy*, which supplies the rule that a timely criminal rehearing request suspends finality, and *United States v. Dieter*, which rejects classifications based on the kind or strength of asserted error. The Government filed one motion within thirty days and appealed within thirty days after denial. Earlier abandonment may matter to the merits, but the motion still sought alteration of adjudicated rights. Making time depend on assessing strength, preservation, or likely success would leave litigants guessing about the jurisdictional date. *Department of Banking v. Pink* is distinct because its filing did not seek substantive alteration. The Court decides only finality and leaves bad faith, successive motions, waiver, and the search open.

## 7. Precedent Treatment
*Healy* and *Dieter* are applied; *Pink* is distinguished. No appeal period or preservation rule changes.

## 8. Law After Decision
The timely-reconsideration rule enters law October 15, 1991.

## 9. Separate Writings
None.

## 10. Procedure After Action
The Supreme Court proceeding is closed. The case returns to the Tenth Circuit for further appellate proceedings.

## 11. Source Notes
Official United States Reports, 502 U.S. 1-8, read in full; research cutoff September 14, 2026. No quotation is required.

# Zatko v. California and grouped matters

**Render form: compact — reduced-Court 5-3 judgment and the only comparator difference is Stone replacing Rehnquist.**

## 1. Event
*Zatko v. California*, 502 U.S. 16 (1991), lead No. 91-5052 and companion Nos. 91-5111, 91-5166, 91-5167, 91-5244, 91-5246, 91-5307, 91-5331, 91-5332, 91-5401, 91-5416, 91-5476, 91-5583, 91-5594, 91-5692, 91-5730, and 91-5732. Decided November 4, 1991, on seventeen motions for leave to proceed in forma pauperis under Rule 39.8. No underlying petition merits were before the Court.

## 2. Participation
Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas took no part; no public reason was stated.

## 3. Public Action
All seventeen fee-waiver motions denied, with leave to pay fees and submit compliant petitions by November 25, 1991.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy
5-3. White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter denied fee status; Stone, Blackmun, and Stevens would allow it. No underlying petition was decided.

## 5. Opinion Topology
Per curiam opinion for the Court. Stevens dissented, joined by Stone and Blackmun.

## 6. Holdings
**Holding and operative rule:** When the Court is satisfied that pending submissions are frivolous and repeated frivolous filings constitute extreme abuse, Rule 39.8 permits denial of in-forma-pauperis status. The Court may require fees and compliant form by a deadline without deciding merits or imposing a broader ban.

**Authority:** Per curiam majority of White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 39.8 makes fee denial discretionary when a present filing is frivolous or malicious. The Court finds that predicate in these submissions and treats the applicants' extraordinary histories as evidence of continuing abuse. *McDonald* and *Sindram* permit targeted measures preserving resources for substantial claims. The Court adopts no numerical threshold and does not make old denials independently conclusive. It denies only the pending fee motions and permits paid filing in ordinary form by November 25. The Court leaves ordinary screening available upon compliance and reaches no underlying claim, conviction, petition merits, or constitutional right to unrestricted free filing.

## 7. Precedent Treatment
*McDonald* and *Sindram* are applied as authority for tailored process protection, not extended to a categorical ban.

## 8. Law After Decision
Rule 39.8 remains discretionary; extreme repetitive frivolous use may justify fee denial for identified submissions.

## 9. Separate Writings
Stevens, joined by Stone and Blackmun, argues that filing history should not replace careful review of the present paper and that a financial barrier may exclude a later colorable indigent claim.

## 10. Procedure After Action
The motions are denied. Each applicant may pay and comply by November 25; absent compliance, the submissions do not enter the ordinary paid docket.

## 11. Source Notes
Official United States Reports, 502 U.S. 16-20, read in full; research cutoff September 14, 2026.

# Hafer v. Melo, No. 90-681

**Render form: compact — 8-0 judgment and no material comparator change.**

## 1. Event
*Hafer v. Melo*, 502 U.S. 21 (1991), No. 90-681. Argued October 15 and decided November 5, 1991, on certiorari to the Third Circuit, 912 F.2d 628. Question: individual-capacity §1983 personhood for conduct undertaken through official authority.

## 2. Participation
Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas took no part because argument preceded his oath.

## 3. Public Action
The Third Circuit judgment is affirmed.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy
8-0. Every participating Justice supported affirmance. Individual claims may proceed; no damages or reinstatement are awarded now.

## 5. Opinion Topology
O'Connor delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by all participating Justices. No separate writing.

## 6. Holdings
**Holding and operative rule:** A state officer sued individually is a §1983 “person” even for acts undertaken through official authority, and state immunity does not independently bar recovery from that officer personally. Plaintiffs must prove the officer's own violation under color of law, and personal immunities remain available.

**Authority:** O'Connor's unanimous participating-Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1983 reaches a person who, under color of state law, causes a federal deprivation. *Graham* distinguishes an official-capacity claim representing government from an individual-capacity claim seeking the officer's liability. *Will* excluded States and official-capacity damages defendants; its language described litigation capacity, not every act performed with authority. *Scheuer* confirms possible personal liability, and *Monroe* confirms that misuse of official power remains under color of law. The Court of Appeals construed these damages claims as personal. Hafer's firing authority supplies the state-law nexus but does not make her Pennsylvania for every remedy. Legitimate-administration concerns belong to personal immunity doctrine, not statutory personhood. The Court leaves the constitutional violations, qualified immunity, damages, and reinstatement unresolved.

## 7. Precedent Treatment
*Will* is clarified and preserved; *Graham*, *Scheuer*, and *Monroe* are applied; *Ex parte Young* remains unchanged.

## 8. Law After Decision
The individual-capacity coverage rule enters law November 5, 1991; immunity doctrine does not change.

## 9. Separate Writings
None.

## 10. Procedure After Action
The Supreme Court proceeding is closed. The claims return for ordinary proceedings on elements and defenses.

## 11. Source Notes
Official United States Reports, 502 U.S. 21-31, read in full; research cutoff September 14, 2026.

# Board of Governors v. MCorp Financial, Nos. 90-913 and 90-914

**Render form: compact — 8-0 component judgments and no material comparator change.**

## 1. Event
*Board of Governors v. MCorp Financial*, 502 U.S. 32 (1991), Nos. 90-913 and 90-914. Argued October 7 and decided December 3, 1991, on certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 900 F.2d 852. Question: jurisdiction to interrupt two ongoing Board proceedings.

## 2. Participation
Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas took no part because argument preceded his oath.

## 3. Public Action
No. 90-913 reversed; No. 90-914 affirmed.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy
8-0 on each docket. The source-of-strength injunction is removed and the refusal to enjoin the §23A proceeding stands. Both proceedings may continue; validity and enforcement remain open.

## 5. Opinion Topology
Stevens delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by all participating Justices. No separate writing.

## 6. Holdings
**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1818(i)(1) bars a district court from enjoining an ongoing Board notice-or-order proceeding except through §1818's channels. Neither bankruptcy jurisdiction nor *Kyne* supplies an alternative where regulatory proceedings are excepted from the stay and meaningful final-order review remains.

**Authority:** Stevens's unanimous participating-Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1818(i)(1) withdraws jurisdiction to affect Board notices or orders except as provided. Congress separately authorized challenges to temporary orders, review of final orders, and enforcement; this action fits none. Section 362(b)(4) lets governmental regulatory proceedings continue, while §§362(a)(3) and (a)(6) do not stop these nonfinal proceedings before seizure of property or collection. Section 1334 does not alter the result because the Board is not another court and continuation does not displace control of estate property. *Kyne* supplied exceptional review when a statutory right otherwise would be lost. Section 1818(h)(2) preserves meaningful later review here, while §1818(i)(1) directly precludes intervention now. Regulation validity and an actual enforcement conflict remain open.

## 7. Precedent Treatment
*Kyne* is confined to absence of meaningful later review; *Boire*’s narrow understanding is applied.

## 8. Law After Decision
The statutory-channel rule enters law December 3, 1991. No agency-deference rule changes.

## 9. Separate Writings
None.

## 10. Procedure After Action
The Supreme Court proceeding is closed. Both regulatory proceedings may continue through statutory channels.

## 11. Source Notes
Official United States Reports, 502 U.S. 32-45, read in full; research cutoff September 14, 2026.

# Griffin v. United States, No. 90-6352

**Render form: compact — 8-0 judgment, 7-1 rationale, and no material comparator change.**

## 1. Event
*Griffin v. United States*, 502 U.S. 46 (1991), No. 90-6352. Argued October 7 and decided December 3, 1991, on certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 913 F.2d 337. Question: effect of a factually unsupported lawful object in a general conspiracy verdict.

## 2. Participation
Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter participated. Thomas took no part because argument preceded his oath.

## 3. Public Action
The Seventh Circuit judgment is affirmed.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy
8-0 judgment. Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter supported affirmance. No retrial or acquittal is ordered.

## 5. Opinion Topology
Scalia delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter. Blackmun concurred in the judgment.

## 6. Holdings
**Holding and operative rule:** A federal general verdict on a multiple-object conspiracy need not be set aside merely because evidence is inadequate for one legally valid object when another is supported beyond a reasonable doubt. The rule does not govern legally invalid objects, failure on every route, element-diluting instructions, or a preserved independent unanimity defect.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion joined on this proposition by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; seven of eight participants.

**Controlling explanation:** *Stromberg* and *Yates* invalidate a verdict potentially resting on a legally forbidden basis because jurors cannot be expected to correct legal error. *Turner* supplies the different rule for factual inadequacy because jurors ordinarily can reject a lawful theory lacking proof. Both conspiracy objects here were legally permissible. The Government conceded no evidence connected Griffin to the DEA object, but sufficient IRS-object evidence satisfied *Jackson*'s rational-factfinder inquiry and *Winship*'s unchanged proof requirement. A trial court should ordinarily withdraw an unsupported route when asked, but preferred management does not itself create constitutional reversal. Legal invalidity, failure on all routes, element dilution, and independent unanimity questions remain open.

## 7. Precedent Treatment
*Turner* is applied; *Yates* and *Stromberg* are distinguished and preserved; *Winship* and *Jackson* remain unchanged.

## 8. Law After Decision
The factual-versus-legal inadequacy distinction enters law December 3, 1991.

## 9. Separate Writings
Blackmun concurs in the judgment, declining broad historical or jury-competence reasoning while agreeing that governing precedent sustains the verdict.

## 10. Procedure After Action
The conviction remains affirmed; Supreme Court proceedings are closed.

## 11. Source Notes
Official United States Reports, 502 U.S. 46-61, read in full; research cutoff September 14, 2026.
