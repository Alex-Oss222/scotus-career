# International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee

## 1. Event

International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee, No. 91-155; October Term 1991. Plenary merits decision, June 26, 1992; argued March 25, 1992. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, 925 F.2d 576 (1991). The District Court, 721 F.Supp. 572, granted § 1983 declaratory and injunctive relief against the Port Authority's terminal restrictions; the Second Circuit reversed as to solicitation and affirmed as to distribution. This docket asks whether the publicly accessible Port Authority terminal areas are public forums and whether continuous or repetitive solicitation and receipt of funds can be prohibited there. ISKCON seeks restoration of relief against solicitation. The private airline-leased areas are outside the remaining case. Lee v. ISKCON, No. 91-339, separately resolves distribution on the same date; neither case acquires an entering-law priority from presentation order. Render form: full. # United States v. Fordice; Ayers v. Fordice

## 1. Event

United States v. Fordice, No. 90-1205, together with Ayers v. Fordice, No. 90-6588; October Term 1991. Consolidated plenary merits decision, June 26, 1992; argued November 13, 1991. On writs of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit sitting en banc, 914 F.2d 676 (1990), which affirmed the District Court's compliance judgment at 674 F.Supp. 1523 (N.D.Miss. 1987). The United States and private plaintiffs challenge the standard for discharging Mississippi's established duty to dismantle its state-imposed dual university system. They seek further remedial proceedings; private plaintiffs also seek relief concerning historically black institutions. The Court reviews the Equal Protection and related Title VI duty, not an unproved entitlement to any particular closure, merger, funding amount or racial quota. Render form: compact. Basis: the full remand has an 8–1 majority and there is no material change in judgment, controlling rule, non-Stone vote, remedy or precedent treatment; Scalia's narrower admissions agreement is preserved as in the primary comparator.

## 2. Participation

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participate at argument and decision. No recusal or other nonparticipation is established. Thomas's October 23, 1991 oath precedes the argument. Nine participate; six constitute a quorum and five are required for a majority judgment or controlling proposition. The review channel is certiorari under 28 U.S.C. § 1254(1).

## 3. Public Action

The Court vacates the Fifth Circuit judgment in both consolidated dockets and remands for further proceedings under the State's continuing affirmative duty to dismantle its former dual system. Facial race neutrality and good faith alone do not discharge that duty while remediable state-created segregative policies persist.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy

The judgment providing a full policy-specific and system-wide remand in both Nos. 90-1205 and 90-6588 is 8–1: Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support it; Scalia opposes that breadth. All nine agree that the ACT admissions requirements need further review, with Scalia supporting only a narrower admission-barrier remand on his own ground.

The courts below must assess traceable policies' current segregative effects, educational justification, practical elimination and interaction, including differential admissions, unnecessary duplication, mission assignments, continued institutional organization and affected funding/other challenged features. The State bears its existing dismantlement burden. If the corrected inquiry establishes an undischarged obligation, the State must be adjudged in violation of Equal Protection and the related Title VI duty and appropriate remedial proceedings conducted. The Court orders no campus closure, merger, fixed funding award, compulsory assignment or numerical racial balance. Existing factual findings are not set aside merely because the governing legal standard was wrong.

## 5. Opinion Topology

White writes the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, eight Justices. O'Connor files a separate concurrence without additional joins; Thomas files a separate concurrence without additional joins. Scalia concurs in the judgment as to further review of discriminatory ACT admissions requirements and dissents from the broader system-wide remand; he joins no part of White's opinion. His agreement on the narrow admissions disposition is not a ninth join in the Court's systemic standard.

## 6. Holdings

### 1. The continuing duty in a formerly dual university system

**Holding and operative rule.** A State that maintained a legally segregated university system does not discharge its affirmative dismantlement duty merely by adopting good-faith, facially neutral policies. It must eliminate policies traceable to that system that continue to foster segregation when their effects can practicably be removed consistently with sound educational practices; the established duty does not require fresh discriminatory intent for each such remnant, and current racial imbalance alone is insufficient.

**Authority.** White's opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, eight of nine Justices, controls the Equal Protection rule and the related Title VI disposition in these cases.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that opening a university's doors does not necessarily remove the State's old barriers to educational choice. Brown establishes the duty to end legally imposed educational separation. Green requires effective dismantlement rather than reliance on the name given a freedom-of-choice policy. Dowell and the Court's decision in Freeman preserve practical limits and require identification of effects attributable to the established violation. University attendance is voluntary, and differing institutional missions may serve legitimate educational purposes. But the alternatives among which a student chooses remain shaped by admissions requirements, available programs and State-assigned institutional roles. Bazemore does not make those arrangements private choices: its voluntary clubs presented findings that discriminatory practices had ended and choice was unfettered. Mississippi's good faith therefore does not answer whether its inherited machinery still fosters segregation. The Title VI claim follows the same established discrimination duty here. The Court leaves new policies without roots in the dual system to ordinary equal-protection analysis and does not require proportional enrollment or decide unrelated statutory theories.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Brown v. Board of Education, 347 U.S. 483, and Brown II, 349 U.S. 294: applied; university systems share the affirmative duty to dismantle established legal segregation.
- Green v. School Board of New Kent County, 391 U.S. 430: applied to effective dismantlement, with voluntary university attendance and legitimate institutional differences preserved.
- Board of Education v. Dowell, 498 U.S. 237: applied; good faith and practicable removal of vestiges remain distinct requirements.
- Freeman v. Pitts, March 31, 1992 simulated decision: applied within its actual causal and practicability limits; its school-decree holding is not replaced or mechanically transplanted.
- Bazemore v. Friday, 478 U.S. 385: distinguished; genuinely unfettered voluntary club choice does not establish removal of inherited university constraints.
- Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Development Corp., 429 U.S. 252: preserved for a new violation involving policies not rooted in the established dual system.

### 2. The State's burden and assessment of interacting policies

**Holding and operative rule.** The State bears the burden of demonstrating that it has dismantled its established dual system; a court may not shift that burden to plaintiffs by demanding that they disprove discharge. The court must examine the current effects and practical alternatives of challenged inherited policies, including their interaction, and must tie any remedy to the violation rather than infer liability or relief from racial identifiability alone.

**Authority.** The same eight-Justice opinion of the Court adopts the burden, combined-effects inquiry and violation-linked remedial boundary.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a finding of current good intentions cannot substitute for proof of completed dismantlement. Brown II places responsibility for overcoming the established unlawful system on the State. Green requires examination of whether the chosen measures actually remove its operation, and Swann ties remedial authority to the constitutional wrong. Here the District Court identified substantial unnecessary duplication yet relied on an asserted absence of plaintiffs' proof that changing it would affect enrollment. That approach reverses the burden and isolates a policy that may reinforce admissions and mission differences. The Court's Freeman decision likewise requires attention to causal and remedial connections rather than treating a finding on one question as an answer to all others. Undisturbed historical findings remain evidence; applying the wrong compliance standard does not itself require retrying every fact. On remand the courts must examine the affected policies together where their effects interact, assess educational justification and practical change, and state the findings supporting relief. Neither general racial balance nor unlimited judicial management follows.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Brown II, 349 U.S. 294: applied; the State retains the burden of showing discharge of its established duty.
- Green, 391 U.S. 430: applied; effectiveness is assessed in actual operation, not by formal policy labels.
- Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education, 402 U.S. 1: applied; the scope of the violation bounds equitable relief.
- Freeman v. Pitts, March 31, 1992 simulated decision: applied; causal burdens, practicable compliance and remedial interdependence are retained.
- Milliken v. Bradley, 418 U.S. 717, and 433 U.S. 267: preserved; remediation must address the proven violation and its consequences.

### 3. Differential and ACT-only admissions requirements require further justification

**Holding and operative rule.** Where present admissions requirements derive from deliberately exclusionary criteria and continue segregative effects, the State cannot establish dismantlement merely by attributing their later retention to neutral educational purposes. On this record it must adequately justify the differential thresholds and ACT-only method under the continuing-duty standard or replace the unjustified, practicably removable remnants; the Court does not invalidate every use of the ACT or prescribe a substitute score.

**Authority.** White's eight-Justice Court opinion controls this application. Scalia supplies a ninth vote for further admissions review only on his distinct intentional-admission-barrier ground; he does not join the broader rule.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that changing a policy's stated purpose does not by itself remove its discriminatory inheritance. Brown and Green require the State to dismantle the exclusionary system actually established. The ACT requirements began as a means of excluding black applicants from historically white institutions, and the lower findings show continuing differences in automatic admission opportunities. The asserted concern about unprepared students does not adequately explain all differences between institutions with comparable undergraduate missions or establish why different missions require these precise thresholds. The refusal to consider school performance alongside test scores also requires more than the general invocation of uneven grading. The record presents an available predictor of success and materially different effects that the courts below did not adequately assess under the State's burden. This conclusion does not replace university admissions expertise with a judicial formula. It requires an explanation adequate to the established remedial duty and examination of practicable alternatives. The State may maintain sound admissions standards that satisfy that duty; no universal ACT prohibition is announced.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Brown and Green: applied; inherited discriminatory admission barriers require effective dismantlement rather than a new neutral label.
- Dowell and the simulated Freeman: applied; practical removal and causal attribution govern the continuing compliance inquiry.
- Florida ex rel. Hawkins v. Board of Control, 350 U.S. 413: applied to equal access to higher education; no new test-score formula follows.

### 4. Remand for the full structure without a predetermined institutional remedy

**Holding and operative rule.** The corrected inquiry must examine unnecessary program duplication, inherited mission assignments, continued institutional organization, and other properly challenged features including affected funding, with their combined segregative effects and educational justifications. Neither racial identifiability nor fiscal inefficiency alone requires closing or merging a university, and improved funding alone cannot establish dismantlement while unlawful barriers remain.

**Authority.** White's opinion, joined by the same seven Justices, controls the full remand in both dockets; Scalia dissents from this breadth.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the State must examine the structure through which its universities offer educational opportunity. Green supplies the demand for effective removal of the former system, and Swann and Milliken limit remedies to its established consequences. Unnecessary duplication can preserve separate pathways, and mission assignments that carry forward the former institutions' unequal roles may reinforce admissions barriers. Maintaining all eight institutions must also be examined where that organization perpetuates the dual system; expense alone does not resolve the constitutional issue. The present record does not establish that closing any school is required, and changes to admissions or programs may make closure unnecessary. Historically black institutions' educational value and students' interests remain relevant to sound remedial design. The Court rejects an entitlement to additional funding merely to maintain publicly financed separate enclaves, but leaves funding needed for actual dismantlement for remand. The Alabama State Teachers Association summary affirmance concerned an educationally justified new campus; it did not approve every inherited policy. Concrete remedies must follow the corrected findings, not precede them.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Green and Swann: applied to effective, violation-linked structural relief rather than a numerical enrollment command.
- Milliken, 418 U.S. 717, and 433 U.S. 267: applied to connect practical educational relief to proven consequences.
- Alabama State Teachers Association v. Alabama Public School and College Authority, 393 U.S. 400: confined to the issues necessarily affirmed concerning the justified new campus; no blanket university-compliance safe harbor is recognized.
- Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, January 15, 1992 simulated decision: left intact in its consent-decree-modification setting; it supplies no different liability or automatic termination standard here.

## 7. Precedent Treatment

The holding-specific treatment above governs. Brown's affirmative duty, Green's effectiveness requirement, Swann's remedial limits and Dowell's good-faith/practicability distinction are applied to the university setting. The actual simulated Freeman supplies the State's causal burden and attention to interdependence; its separate school-supervision disposition remains intact. Bazemore's unfettered voluntary-choice finding and the narrow Alabama State Teachers Association summary disposition are distinguished, not overruled. Ordinary intent requirements remain for a new policy outside the established dual system. No precedent is overruled, and neither a general Title VI disparate-impact cause nor a new heightened proof quantum is created.

## 8. Law After Decision

Effective June 26, 1992, Mississippi must establish actual dismantlement under the traceability, current segregative effect, educational justification and practicable-cure inquiry. The rule clarifies the established affirmative duty in higher education: neutral wording and good faith are insufficient by themselves. The State's existing burden continues; the law does not turn every racially identifiable university or every difference in funding, programs or admissions into a violation. The full range of properly challenged inherited policies must be reassessed with relevant interactions, and remedies remain subject to causation and educational practicality. Equal Protection and the related Title VI disposition share that established duty here. No specific closure, merger, funding award or racial assignment rule enters law.

## 9. Separate Writings

O'Connor joins the Court and separately emphasizes careful examination of the State's claimed educational reasons. On her view, where an essential remnant must remain, the State must counteract and minimize its segregative effects as far as possible. Her additional formulation does not replace the Court's stated rule.

Thomas joins the Court and separately stresses policy-specific liability and sound education. He views tainted origins, continued adverse effect and lack of adequate justification as supporting the inference that the prior discriminatory purpose has not been overcome. He stresses that historically black institutions may have independent educational value and traditions; the decision neither commands their destruction nor guarantees their continued racial identifiability. His intent formulation is not an additional Court test.

Scalia agrees that discriminatory admissions barriers must be removed and that Mississippi has not adequately justified continued ACT requirements. He opposes the broader remand, reading Bazemore to make truly nondiscriminatory access sufficient in voluntary higher education. He objects that the wider effects inquiry gives uncertain guidance and may pressure the State to reduce institutional diversity. He rejects a constitutional entitlement to funding equality while preserving the State's freedom to support lawful institutions.

Supreme Court merits review is complete in both consolidated dockets. The cases return through the Fifth Circuit for further proceedings under the corrected compliance standard and for remedial findings and relief where an undischarged duty is established. The Court retains no supervisory docket, sets no funding sum or institutional change, and supplies no future hearing, mandate-issuance date, settlement, filing or lower-court result. No separate Ayers event is left unresolved.

# Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc.

## 1. Event

Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc.; No. 91-971; October Term 1991. Merits decision, June 26, 1992. Argued April 21, 1992. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 932 F.2d 1113 (1991), affirming the Southern District of Texas infringement judgment. The granted question is whether inherently distinctive restaurant trade dress may receive protection under the applicable pre-1988 Lanham Act §43(a) without acquired secondary meaning. The separate challenge to the nonfunctionality finding is outside the grant. Petitioner seeks reversal because the jury found no secondary meaning. This decision shares the June 26 entering-law baseline with the other independent cases released that day.

Render form: compact. The judgment is unanimous and the reviewed rule, non-Stone votes, writing structure, remedy and precedent treatment do not materially change from the historical comparator; replacing the Chief's compatible vote creates no material change.

## 2. Participation

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated at argument and decision. No nonparticipation is established. Nine participate; the six-Justice quorum is satisfied and five votes suffice for the judgment.

## 3. Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment of the Fifth Circuit.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy

The reviewed secondary-meaning component is affirmed, 9–0: Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. No Justice opposes. The infringement judgment remains undisturbed on this question. There is no new damages calculation, new remedy or merits remand. The Court does not decide the excluded functionality objection or the independently resolved trade-secret and licensing issues.

## 5. Opinion Topology

White delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven Justices. Scalia also files a concurrence, without additional joiners, agreeing with Thomas's text and common-law account as complementary to the Court's reasoning. Stevens concurs in the judgment in a separate writing, without joiners. Thomas concurs in the judgment in a separate writing, without joiners. All writings support affirmance; Stevens and Thomas do not join White's opinion.

## 6. Holdings

### Inherent distinctiveness suffices without acquired secondary meaning

**Holding and operative rule.** Under the pre-1988 Lanham Act §43(a), nonfunctional restaurant trade dress that is inherently distinctive may receive protection without proof of acquired secondary meaning, provided the remaining infringement requirements, including likelihood of confusion, are met. Protection does not expire after an unspecified period merely because secondary meaning has not developed.

**Authority.** White's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter, supplies seven votes at this level of generality. Stevens and Thomas agree with the judgment on separate statutory accounts; the Court's rule does not depend on combining their writings.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a source identifier need not acquire distinctiveness twice. Inwood distinguishes acquired public association from functionality and recognizes the broader protection available under §43(a). Park 'N Fly explains the statutory distinction between descriptive marks requiring acquired meaning and inherently distinctive marks. The same source-identifying distinction applies to the nonfunctional restaurant presentation accepted here. The jury found inherent distinctiveness and likely confusion, yet found no acquired secondary meaning; those findings concern different propositions and are not contradictory. Chevron Chemical properly applies the familiar Abercrombie categories to trade dress, whereas a universal secondary-meaning requirement has no basis in the operative statute. Requiring an initial period of unprotected copying would obstruct source identification and goodwill. The concern that protection might exhaust useful designs is answered by the independent requirements of distinctiveness, nonfunctionality and confusion. A temporary exemption followed by an unwritten deadline is equally unsupported. The Court accepts the findings for this limited review and leaves the excluded functionality challenge and other forms of product design undecided.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Inwood Laboratories, Inc. v. Ives Laboratories, Inc., 456 U.S. 844 (1982): applied for secondary meaning, functionality and §43(a)'s protection beyond registered marks; its distinct contributory-infringement holding is unchanged.
- Park 'N Fly, Inc. v. Dollar Park & Fly, Inc., 469 U.S. 189 (1985): applied for the statutory distinction between inherent and acquired distinctiveness and protection of source-identifying goodwill; no registration requirement is imported into §43(a).
- Kellogg Co. v. National Biscuit Co., 305 U.S. 111 (1938): its acquired-source-significance concept is preserved; it does not impose secondary meaning on accepted inherently distinctive restaurant dress.
- Abercrombie & Fitch Co. v. Hunting World, Inc., 537 F.2d 4 (2d Cir. 1976): its classification is used to distinguish inherent from acquired distinctiveness; this circuit authority is not represented as an earlier Supreme Court trade-dress holding.
- Chevron Chemical Co. v. Voluntary Purchasing Groups, Inc., 659 F.2d 695 (5th Cir. 1981): its treatment of inherently distinctive trade dress is approved on the question presented.

# Franklin v. Massachusetts

## 1. Event

Franklin v. Massachusetts; No. 91-1502. Argued April 21, 1992; decided June 26, 1992. Direct appeal from the three-judge District Court for Massachusetts, 785 F. Supp. 230 (1992), which set aside the inclusion of overseas federal employees in state apportionment counts and ordered corrective relief. Massachusetts and its voters challenge inclusion, the Secretary's residence methodology and the explanation for using home-of-record data after cancellation of a proposed survey. The questions concern standing, review of the Secretary's completed decision under the APA, constitutional and statutory counting authority, and arbitrary-or-capricious review.

Render form: full. Although the judgment is unanimous, five Justices now establish APA review and decide the statutory and administrative merits. Scalia accepts constitutional standing and reaches the constitutional merits under this Court's earlier express Montana holding. Those are material changes in controlling law and a non-Stone position.

## 2. Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. The quorum is satisfied; five votes suffice for judgment.

## 3. Public Action

The Court reverses the District Court’s judgment and dissolves the corrective relief.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy

Reversal of the operative judgment and rejection of the constitutional exclusion claim: 9–0, Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. The District Court's corrective relief is dissolved. No replacement allocation is ordered and no injunction against the President is issued.

Review of the Secretary's completed decision, rejection of statutory objections and rejection of the arbitrary-or-capricious challenge: Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, five. White, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would reach the same ultimate judgment through the absence of APA review; they do not join an adjudication of those APA merits. They are not four votes that the methodology violates the APA. No further factual proceedings or new allocation are required by this judgment.

## 5. Opinion Topology

Stone-Zsela delivers the Opinion of the Court. Parts I–III, stating the posture, standing for the inclusion injury and constitutional counting rule, are joined by all eight Associate Justices. Parts IV–V, establishing review of the Secretary's final action and rejecting the statutory and administrative challenges, are joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter: five Justices including the author. O'Connor files an opinion concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by White, Scalia and Thomas; they would hold the Secretary's report nonfinal and the President outside the APA. Scalia also files a concurrence, without additional joiners, explaining why the earlier express Montana holding supports declaratory redress here while leaving coercive relief against the President unresolved.

## 6. Holdings

### Standing for the continuing apportionment injury

**Holding and operative rule.** A State and its voters have standing to contest inclusion of an identified population in the operative apportionment when that inclusion costs the State a seat and authoritative relief concerning the responsible census determination would likely correct the allocation through the prescribed statutory sequence. Likely redress here does not require an injunction against the President or a general assumption that persons outside the litigation follow judicial advice.

**Authority.** Parts I–III of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight Associate Justices, control. This holding concerns the proved inclusion injury; it does not find that any particular alternative data source would independently give Massachusetts a seat.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that this continuing representational injury can be judicially redressed. Massachusetts would receive an additional seat if the challenged overseas population were excluded. The injury therefore rests on the operative allocation, not an abstract objection to governmental procedure. This Court's Montana decision establishes that authoritative declaratory adjudication can probably correct an ongoing apportionment through the prescribed governmental sequence. Lujan requires a concrete injury, causal connection and likely relief at the pertinent evidentiary stage; its absent-agency application did not command a majority and does not supply a categorical bar here. The statutory sequence and the Government's litigation position support the likelihood of correction following an authoritative determination concerning the census. The Court need not presume a power to command presidential discretion. Nor does it assume that a different residence dataset would necessarily produce another seat: that separate causal proposition has not been established. The inclusion injury and its connected legal challenges suffice for the review undertaken here.

**Precedent treatment.**

- United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, March 31, 1992, this Court: applied for continuing representational injury and likely declaratory redress through the allocation process; the express unanimous holding remains controlling and its reservation of coercive presidential relief remains intact.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, this Court: applied for injury, causation, likely redress and evidentiary demands; the noncontrolling application concerning absent funding agencies is not promoted into law. Its rule for procedure connected to concrete injury does not require certainty that a new method changes the result.

### The Secretary's completed census determination is reviewable

**Holding and operative rule.** The Secretary's completed census determination supplies final agency action under APA §704 even though the President later transmits the resulting apportionment statement. The census statutes neither preclude review nor commit the counting decision entirely to unreviewable discretion: the duty to count the proper population supplies law to apply, while leaving substantial methodological choice to the Secretary.

**Authority.** Part IV of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, controls with five votes. White, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas disagree with this rule while agreeing with reversal on their own reviewability ground.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the official responsible for the count cannot escape review merely because another official transmits its apportionment consequence. Section 141 assigns the census and its completed population figures to the Secretary. Section 2a directs transmission of numbers ascertained under that census and application of the prescribed formula; it does not confer a new presidential choice of the population to count. Abbott Laboratories supports review of a completed administrative determination producing concrete consequences. Waterman involved a statutory presidential judgment over foreign air routes; that independent decision differs from the prescribed transmission here. Possible correction and general executive supervision do not make every completed agency decision tentative. Japan Whaling confirms that broad administrative discretion remains judicially reviewable when governing statutory commands supply a legal standard. The Secretary retains latitude over census form and method, but must count the proper population under the governing law. Review of that completed administrative choice is sufficient. The Court therefore leaves the President's own APA status and susceptibility to coercive relief undecided.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967): applied for reviewability and practical completion of an agency determination with concrete consequences; the statutory transmittal here does not postpone finality.
- Chicago & Southern Air Lines, Inc. v. Waterman Steamship Corp., 333 U.S. 103 (1948): distinguished because Congress there required an independent presidential foreign-affairs judgment; its rule for that decision structure is preserved.
- Japan Whaling Assn. v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): applied for review against statutory limits despite broad administrative judgment; methodological latitude is not absence of law to apply.

### Temporarily absent residents may be included in the constitutional count

**Holding and operative rule.** The constitutional command to count persons in each State permits inclusion of federal personnel temporarily overseas whose enduring ties support residence in a particular State. It does not require a literal census-day physical presence rule, and it does not authorize attribution to a State without a supportable residence connection.

**Authority.** Part III of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight Associate Justices, controls with nine votes. The constitutional rule does not itself resolve the separate statutory finality or APA explanation questions.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that temporary government service abroad does not necessarily sever residence in a State. The constitutional text requires an enumeration of the population belonging to each State, but does not define residence exclusively by bodily presence on census day. The first census legislation treated persons temporarily absent from their usual abode as belonging there. That early implementation gives concrete content to the counting command. The challenged personnel retain identified state connections, and the Secretary may reasonably treat their absence on federal assignment as temporary for this purpose. Wesberry and Karcher enforce equality in drawing districts within a State; they do not dictate this answer to who belongs in an interstate population count. Montana likewise recognizes that interstate apportionment must respect the constitutional structure rather than import every intrastate rule unchanged. Home-of-record information is imperfect, but that does not make every supported attribution constitutionally forbidden. The Court decides this record and rejects mandatory exclusion; unsupported connections and different methodological records remain open.

**Precedent treatment.**

- United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, March 31, 1992, this Court: applied for the constitutional structure of interstate apportionment and the distinction from intrastate districting; no new tolerance for unequal districts is adopted.
- Wesberry v. Sanders, 376 U.S. 1 (1964): distinguished as an intrastate congressional district-equality rule; its equal-population command remains effective in its own setting.
- Karcher v. Daggett, 462 U.S. 725 (1983): distinguished on the same interstate/intrastate ground; the present residence decision does not excuse avoidable inequality among a State's districts.

### The statutory and administrative objections fail on this record

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 141 permits the supported inclusion of temporarily overseas personnel with enduring state ties, and the Secretary's comparative choice of supplemented home-of-record information is not arbitrary or capricious on this record. The agency must supply a contemporaneous, reasonably discernible explanation connecting its available information and residence objective to its choice; imperfection alone does not require rejection of every available dataset.

**Authority.** Part V of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, controls with five votes. The other four Justices do not join the statutory or APA merits determination.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the Secretary made a permissible comparative choice and adequately explained it. State Farm requires examination of relevant considerations and a rational connection between the record and the decision. Bowman Transportation permits an explanation whose path is reasonably discernible even if its expression is imperfect. Cancellation of the proposed survey is a serious objection because improved residence information helped motivate the change. But the contemporaneous record also contains assurances of supplementary personnel information and continuing reasons to recognize temporarily absent residents. Home of record can identify an enduring connection; legal residence may instead reflect taxation, and last duty station may identify employment rather than home. The Secretary could weigh those comparative defects under the statutory counting responsibility. The Court relies on the recorded reasons, not later declarations or a rationale invented by counsel. It does not demand a perfect dataset unavailable to the agency. Nor does it excuse persistence when an agency's only factual premise collapses. Different methodological challenges require their own records.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Assn. v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co., 463 U.S. 29 (1983): applied for consideration of relevant factors, reasoned explanation and review on the agency's actual rationale; no exemption from review accompanies census discretion.
- Bowman Transportation, Inc. v. Arkansas-Best Freight System, Inc., 419 U.S. 281 (1974): applied for a reasonably discernible decisional path despite imperfect exposition; a court may not substitute a new rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached.** No replacement allocation, judicially selected dataset, injunction against the President, or general APA status for the President is decided. No claim that another residence method necessarily restores a seat is found proved. The statutory and administrative holdings are record-specific applications; no earlier Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

## 7. Precedent Treatment

The treatments in Holdings apply unchanged. Montana supplies an express standing premise here, and Lujan is applied only at its actual controlling level. Waterman remains effective for its distinct presidential-decision structure. State Farm and Bowman govern the actual administrative record.

## 8. Law After Decision

Effective June 26, 1992, the completed Secretary census decision is reviewable under §704 despite subsequent presidential transmission; counting obligations supply law to apply. The constitutional count may include supported temporarily absent residents. The statutory and APA merits uphold this inclusion and home-of-record choice on its contemporaneous explanation. Earlier Montana declaratory-redress law is applied. No presidential immunity or presidential APA holding, new evidentiary burden, replacement formula or districting tolerance is created.

## 9. Separate Writings

O'Connor, joined by White, Scalia and Thomas, views the Secretary's report as an intermediate step because the President transmits the operative apportionment and may seek corrections; they would also exclude the President from the APA's agency definition. They concur in the constitutional rule and reversal but oppose review of the Secretary under §704. ## 6. Holdings

### The framework retaining protected choice

**Holding and operative rule:** Before viability, the patient retains the right to decide whether to continue a pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons; the State may not prohibit that protected choice, give another person effective control over it, or prevent timely care necessary for life or health. After viability the State may prohibit abortion only with the required life-and-health protection, while competent practice, genuinely informed consent, safety and lawful administration remain regulable within these boundaries.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion for the Court on protected choice, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Kennedy: six direct joins at this level of generality. This coalition does not establish a six-Justice agreement on the trimester timetable, a complete replacement test, or every challenged provision.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the decision whether to continue a pregnancy remains a protected liberty. Roe locates that decision within the Fourteenth Amendment and distinguishes the patient's choice before viability from the State's authority afterward. Griswold and Eisenstadt establish that constitutional protection of reproductive decisions concerns the individual, including an unmarried individual. The protection is therefore neither a privilege of marriage nor a permission that officials may withhold when they find the patient's reasons insufficient. The personal consequences of pregnancy make her decision constitutionally significant; disagreement with that decision does not transfer it to the State.

This liberty protects an effective opportunity to choose. Carey explains that a government can interfere with a reproductive decision by restricting access to its means even without prohibiting the decision in words. Danforth supplies the corresponding limit on another person's authority: the State may not give a spouse a controlling power over the patient's protected choice. Accordingly, the Court considers what Pennsylvania's requirements actually demand and how they operate. A formal description as information, notice or administration cannot answer evidence that the condition gives another person effective control. Conversely, a condition does not become unconstitutional merely because it concerns abortion or may persuade a patient who freely considers accurate information.

The State has legitimate interests in health, competent medical practice and potential life. Roe recognizes those interests, and the retained right does not promise access to demonstrably unsafe treatment. The clinician must still determine what care is appropriate, and the State may establish lawful professional standards. But clinical suitability is different from an official judgment about whether a patient has an acceptable reason to end a previability pregnancy. A safety label cannot itself establish that otherwise appropriate care is unsafe. The actual medical function, burden and constitutional limits must be considered under the law applicable to the challenged requirement.

Life-and-health protection must operate when treatment is needed. Doe requires attention to individualized clinical judgment and relevant physical and mental conditions; it does not reduce health to danger of immediate death. Pennsylvania's emergency definition is sustained under the prospective construction protecting significant threats before damage becomes catastrophic. That construction, together with the requirement of good-faith clinical judgment, answers the developed facial health challenge. It does not make every preference an emergency or settle an independent vagueness claim. The separate defense for omitting harmful information cannot substitute for permission to provide timely necessary treatment.

The protected decision also explains why adult spousal control differs from permissible parental involvement. Danforth prevents an effective spousal veto. Bellotti, Ashcroft and the later minors' cases address the distinct responsibility of parents and require adequate independent routes for a minor to obtain authorization. Pennsylvania's one-parent structure must be considered with its emergency exception, substitute-consent provisions and elective, confidential, expeditious bypass based on maturity and informed consent or, alternatively, best interests. Recognizing that structure does not authorize an adult spouse to control another adult's medical choice.

The principal contrary argument would return the entire subject to ordinary legislative discretion because abortion is not expressly named in the Constitution. The Court rejects that argument under the established protection of reproductive liberty and the reasons for retaining Roe stated separately. Recognition of legitimate state interests does not answer who holds the protected decision. Neither does continued disagreement erase the individual liberty those precedents secure. The foundation survives the Court's separate treatment of regulatory rules.

This holding does not select every feature of a general test for previability regulation. The controlling status of the common minimum, the competing proposals and the trimester timetable is stated separately. Nor does the Court decide constitutional fetal personhood, a general entitlement to public funding, a conscience claim, every postviability restriction, or admission of a particular drug. States may provide greater lawful protection. The Court decides the requirements actually presented and preserves the limits necessary to make the patient's protected choice real.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Roe v. Wade — Retains the protected previability decision, viability boundary and required postviability life-and-health safeguard; this holding does not itself decide the separate treatment of the trimester timetable.
- Griswold v. Connecticut — Applies protection of reproductive decisionmaking as a foundation of the retained liberty.
- Eisenstadt v. Baird — Applies the individual character of reproductive choice; marital status does not supply official control over the decision.
- Carey v. Population Services International — Applies the principle that substantial restrictions on access can burden the protected decision itself, with no general rule about every law implicating sexual freedom.
- Doe v. Bolton — Retains individualized clinical judgment and meaningful physical-and-mental-health protection; no general professional immunity follows.
- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth — Applies the prohibition on State-conferred spousal control and preserves the distinction between voluntary informed consent and interference with protected choice.
- Bellotti v. Baird, Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft, Hodgson v. Minnesota, and Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health — Preserve their particular parental-involvement and independent-authorization safeguards; they do not supply an adult spouse's veto or a universal cure for every added parental burden.

### The standard governing previability regulation after this decision

**Holding and operative rule:** Lower courts must apply the Court's protected-choice and life-and-health boundaries, its necessary minimum of compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring for other severe interference with protected access, and its specific provision holdings; the minimum does not make lesser burdens lawful or replace surviving precedent. Six Justices, including the Chief Justice, displace the trimester timetable as an independent timing restriction, but no majority adopts a complete replacement test, Stone's six-step synthesis, a general automatic substantial-obstacle rule, or his meaningful-lesser-burden rule and burden allocation.

**Authority:** Stone's account of controlling authority has the six protected-choice Justices' descriptive concurrence. Operative authority is separately counted: protected boundaries six; severe-interference necessary minimum Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (five); timing change Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas (six). A descriptive concurrence does not add a vote to a disputed rule. The minimum is stated in Stone's severable common-minimum part; the timing rule is stated in the trimester-timetable holding, which Stone now authors as well.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's decision supplies several controlling propositions, and a lower court must respect the authority of each. Six Justices retain the patient's protected previability decision and the necessary life-and-health safeguard. A State cannot prohibit that decision, place it under an effective third-party veto, or use administrative compliance to prevent timely care that the protected medical judgment requires. These boundaries do not depend on a newly selected name for the governing inquiry. They rest on the retained constitutional protection and on the particular applications the Court decides.

Five Justices—Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter—also adopt a necessary minimum for other severe interference with access to protected care; this is Stone's own Step 4(c), and he is now its author as well as a joiner. Such interference must serve a compelling permissible interest and be narrowly tailored. Carey supplies the access-based distinction: ordinary regulation is not automatically subject to the most demanding review, but substantially limiting access to a protected reproductive decision requires a correspondingly exacting justification. Roe and Doe supply the protected subject and its clinical safeguards. This minimum concerns severe interference with the established liberty, not every serious expense or disadvantage associated with pregnancy. Challengers must establish the additional state-imposed interference; its severity is not decided by first balancing the State's asserted interests against the burden. The State must then supply the exacting justification. No new universal evidentiary quantum is prescribed.

The words at a minimum are operative. Passing that inquiry cannot excuse an independently forbidden prohibition, effective third-party veto, or denial of necessary timely care. Nor does it establish that every less severe burden is valid. Blackmun and Stevens retain stronger protections; Stone reserves both his proposed automatic obstacle prohibition and his more demanding inquiry into lesser burdens; Souter applies the surviving provision-specific precedents. O'Connor accepts the necessary floor without accepting those additional general rules. Kennedy joins the protected boundaries and particular judgments but does not join a general strict-justification tier. Thus the common minimum has five direct supporters at its stated scope; it is neither a comprehensive replacement nor an aggregation of similar labels.

Six Justices—Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas—hold that the trimester timetable no longer independently forbids regulation because of its gestational timing. Stone joins that change because he now treats his own adopted Step 4 protections as the operative substitute that satisfies his prior condition; he does not require the other five to adopt his complete framework before casting his own vote, and none of them does. Blackmun, Stevens and Souter withhold the join for their stated reasons, unaffected by Stone's own changed vote. The six-Justice timing holding binds lower courts. It does not remove the six-Justice viability and life-and-health boundaries or convert every remaining question into ordinary rational review.

The specific holdings govern the challenged laws at their exact scope. Five sustain this fixed waiting period and physician-delivery requirement on this record — the same five who authored and joined the trimester-timing change other than Stone, who dissents from waiting and delivery on his own Akron-independent grounds; those holdings displace Akron's contrary rules to the stated extent. The separately supported information, parental, reporting and spousal-notice holdings govern their respective duties and remedies. Outside those decisions, surviving precedent remains applicable subject to the express timing change and the newly stated minimum. No majority creates a general safe harbor for all lesser burdens, a universal burden shift after meaningful interference, or a new numerical facial-challenge formula.

Marks supplies no additional complete formula here. Spousal notice has six direct rationale joins, so its rule requires no narrowest-grounds inference. On waiting and physician delivery, Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter dissent from sustention notwithstanding Stone's join to the trimester-timing change; joining the removal of the timetable does not require joining every subsequent application of the resulting law, and their stronger invalidation rules cannot be combined with O'Connor's rationale as grounds supporting those judgments. Within the actual sustaining coalition, O'Connor's severe-threshold approach, Kennedy's provision-specific reasonable-regulation ground and the other three Justices' broader rational-review theory differ. The particular permissions have five direct joins. Beyond them, no complete narrower rationale has been shown to be a logical subset of the other judgment-supporting rationales, yielding the same result whenever it applies and supported by the required majority of those writers. The Court therefore announces no further Marks holding. It preserves the direct rules and leaves the unresolved general framework unfilled.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Carey v. Population Services International, 431 U.S. 678, 686–689 and n.5 (1977): applied for the access-based necessary strict-justification floor; no universal scrutiny rule for every regulation is drawn from it.
- Roe v. Wade and Doe v. Bolton: retain protected choice and clinical safeguards; only the timetable and specifically identified subsidiary rules receive the treatment separately stated.
- Akron and Thornburgh: remain applicable except where the identified provision and timing holdings expressly qualify or displace their rules.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188, 193 (1977): applied as a limit on recognizing a fractured rationale; no additional complete replacement rule is inferred.

### Stare decisis and the constitutional foundation

**Holding and operative rule:** Roe's protected reproductive liberty, viability boundary and necessary life-and-health protection are retained because renewed disagreement with the constitutional judgment supplies no sufficient justification for withdrawing that settled protection. The Court may distinguish or alter a separable regulatory rule for a demonstrated legal reason, but such treatment does not silently overrule the liberty that the rule implements.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion for the Court on stare decisis, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Kennedy: six direct joins. They join retention of the constitutional foundation and this bounded method of precedent treatment, not every other opinion part or every change to regulatory doctrine.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court retains Roe's constitutional foundation. Respect for precedent does not make constitutional error beyond correction. It requires a legal reason for correction sufficient to justify the disruption that overruling causes. Arizona v. Rumsey supplies that discipline: a conclusion that an earlier constitutional decision was wrong does not alone provide the special justification for abandoning it. The issue is therefore not whether every Member of the present Court would have written Roe in the same way. It is whether the reasons now presented warrant withdrawing the protected liberty on which later decisions and individual choices have rested.

That inquiry must identify the particular rule under examination. Roe recognizes a protected decision before viability and permits prohibition afterward only with necessary life-and-health protection. Its regulatory machinery addresses how the State's interests interact with that liberty. A criticism of one implementation does not establish that the liberty itself is unworkable. Nor does disagreement about the permissible content of a consent notice demonstrate that the patient may constitutionally be deprived of the ultimate decision. The Court accordingly separates the retained foundation from each disputed regulatory proposition and states the treatment of those propositions where they are decided.

The foundation remains capable of judicial application. Courts can distinguish a protected previability choice from an unsafe procedure, voluntary persuasion from an effective prohibition, and permissible parental guidance from an adult spouse's controlling authority. Difficult factual applications do not establish that the underlying right lacks meaning. Danforth, Doe and the later regulatory cases have required examination of identifiable legal duties and their operation. Those decisions leave contested questions, as constitutional rules often do, but the present record does not show that preserving the patient's decision is impossible to administer.

Reliance also bears directly on the requested overruling. The ability to decide whether to continue a pregnancy affects intimate relationships, education, work, family responsibilities and the course of a person's life. Such reliance cannot be reduced to the terms of a commercial agreement made in express reference to a judicial opinion. Nor does the possibility of precautions before pregnancy eliminate the significance of the legal choice once pregnancy occurs. The Court need not assume that every person has arranged every part of life in reliance on Roe to recognize that withdrawal of its protection would disturb a settled and consequential freedom.

The underlying constitutional decisions have not lost their force. Griswold and Eisenstadt continue to protect individual reproductive decisions; Carey explains that restrictions on the means of exercising such a choice may burden the choice itself. The State's interest in potential life is substantial, but Roe acknowledged that interest rather than overlooking it. Its continued assertion is not a newly discovered premise requiring abandonment of all protection. Medical developments may bear on factual applications and the timing or usefulness of particular rules. They do not establish that the patient's liberty disappears or that necessary life-and-health care may be withheld.

The strongest contrary argument is that a constitutional decision cannot ordinarily be corrected by legislation and therefore deserves less rigid adherence. That consideration makes reconsideration possible; it does not make settled constitutional liberty temporary at the preference of each newly constituted Court. A change in membership is no legal defect in a precedent. Continued public disagreement likewise neither proves the precedent wrong nor makes it immune from reconsideration. The Court's duty is to give reasons tied to law, workable protection and the consequences of change, not to treat either resistance or acquiescence as a substitute for judgment.

This conclusion does not freeze every statement in Akron or Thornburgh, nor does it adopt every argument offered to preserve Roe. Particular disclosure requirements, physician delivery, fixed delay and reporting receive their own grounds and precise precedent treatment. A majority for a particular change must support that change at its actual scope; it cannot be inferred from votes retaining the constitutional foundation. The Court rejects both wholesale abandonment by implication and the claim that any refinement necessarily abandons the right. Roe's core protection remains law for the reasons given here, subject to the distinct regulatory holdings the Court actually adopts.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Arizona v. Rumsey — Applies the requirement of a special justification beyond a belief that a constitutional precedent was wrongly decided.
- Roe v. Wade — Reaffirms its protected-choice foundation, viability boundary and necessary life-and-health safeguard; regulatory implementation receives separate treatment.
- Griswold v. Connecticut and Eisenstadt v. Baird — Their protection of individual reproductive choice remains a doctrinal foundation for retaining Roe.
- Carey v. Population Services International — Retains the connection between protected reproductive choice and meaningful access to its exercise; its existence undercuts a claim that Roe's foundation has been doctrinally displaced.
- Doe v. Bolton and Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth — Their clinical-judgment, consent and third-party-control rules demonstrate separable applications of the protected liberty and remain subject to the specific holdings stated here.
- Akron v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health and Thornburgh v. American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists — No wholesale overruling follows from this stare-decisis holding; each actual qualification, retained protection or changed rule is identified in the provision-specific holdings.

### The trimester timetable and the timing of regulation

**Holding and operative rule:** The trimester timetable no longer independently bars an otherwise lawful regulation because of when during pregnancy it applies. This timing holding does not authorize a previability prohibition, remove necessary life-and-health protection, or establish a complete standard for every regulation.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 6 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The permissible function of a regulation cannot be settled by the calendar alone. Roe used a staged timetable to relate medical risks and governmental interests, but the present challenges separately concern informed choice, medical responsibility, parental safeguards and administrative information. Their legal justification and practical effects require examination of the particular duty. Webster identifies the difficulty of making permissible regulation turn on rigid medical and temporal assumptions. The Court therefore removes the timetable as an independent timing restriction while leaving the separately controlling constitutional boundaries in place.

Preserving a protected decision does not require treating the State's interests in competent practice, informed consent or potential life as nonexistent before a specified trimester. That conclusion does not establish the validity of every restriction serving one of those interests. The particular waiting, delivery, notice and reporting duties receive their own dispositions. The contrary claim that retaining choice necessarily preserves all of Roe's regulatory machinery fails because the two propositions are separable. The Court does not announce a comprehensive substitute test through this timing holding.

The Chief Justice's own vote for this removal rests on the six-step framework he adopts in his separate opinion: a substantial obstacle to protected previability care remains independently forbidden, other severe interference requires a compelling permissible interest and narrow tailoring, and a meaningful lesser burden requires the State's justification and fit, without regard to gestational stage. He does not condition his vote here on the other five Justices adopting that same substitute; each supplies a different reason of the kind stated in their own opinions for removing this particular timing rule. Joining this proposition does not itself decide any later application; the waiting, delivery, notice and reporting holdings state and, where a Justice dissents from one, keep their own separate coalitions.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Roe: the trimester timetable is displaced as an independent timing bar; protected choice and viability/life-and-health boundaries are separately retained.
- Webster: its criticism of rigid trimester implementation supports the timing change; its fractured discussion is not misrepresented as an earlier complete overruling.

### Medical emergency and the protective construction of §3203

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3203 survives this facial challenge under the prospective construction that compliance with the Act cannot create a significant threat to the patient's life or health. Its good-faith clinical standard permits prompt treatment of preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and prematurely ruptured membranes at onset, without waiting for catastrophic deterioration; the ruling does not adjudicate every other postviability restriction or every degree of clinical risk.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 9 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains the emergency definition as prospectively construed. The statute asks whether delay will create a serious risk; it does not require the physician to await the injury that timely treatment would prevent. Its reference to good-faith clinical judgment directs attention to the patient's condition when treatment is needed. The court of appeals applied that reading to preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and prematurely ruptured membranes, allowing treatment before shock, infection or irreversible injury occurs. The providers' contrary argument draws force from the consequences of a narrower construction. That construction is not the basis of today's judgment. Webster supports evaluating a medical requirement under a workable construction preserving appropriate professional judgment rather than assuming medically useless or dangerous administration. The separate information-omission defense cannot repair an inadequate emergency rule because it concerns disclosures, not postponement of treatment. The Court decides the developed construction and health-protection challenge. It does not issue a separate vagueness holding or foreclose a challenge to materially different authoritative construction or enforcement.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Webster v. Reproductive Health Services: applies its attention to a medical rule's workable, prudent construction; does not adopt every part of its fractured abortion analysis.
- Roe v. Wade and Doe v. Bolton: their retained necessary-care protection is stated separately at its own proposition-level coalition; this unanimous statutory construction does not create unanimous adherence to their entire reasoning.

**Statutory terms and source distinction:** Section 3203 defines medical emergency through the physician's good-faith clinical judgment: a condition necessitating immediate abortion to avert death, or one in which delay creates serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The significant-threat interpretation and application to the three obstetric conditions are the Third Circuit's construction, not additional statutory words or a binding Pennsylvania Supreme Court interpretation. Section 3205(c)'s separate disclosure defense is not an emergency-delay exception.

### Emergency explanation under §3205(b)

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3205(b) requires an explanation of the medical indications supporting emergency abortion before the procedure only if possible. It does not condition necessary immediate care on completing an explanation or obtaining advance permission, and it survives the facial challenge on that limited operation.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 9 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains the emergency explanation requirement according to its express limit. Before treatment, a physician must explain the medical indications when circumstances permit. When they do not, the phrase 'if possible' prevents the duty from becoming a reason to postpone necessary care. The State may require information supporting an informed treatment decision without requiring that an emergency patient wait while a physician completes an ordinary counseling process. Danforth supplies the distinction between permissible informed consent and a condition interfering with the patient's decision; the statute's own qualification resolves the alleged timing conflict here. The required explanation concerns the actual medical indications supporting the emergency judgment. It is not an application to a public official and creates no power to withhold advance authorization. Nor does this subsection replace the emergency definition or the separate disclosure defense. The Court does not decide a hypothetical enforcement practice demanding an explanation when the emergency makes one impossible; such a practice would contradict the operation sustained today.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: applies the permission for appropriate informed-consent administration while keeping interference with the treatment decision distinct.

**Statutory terms and source distinction:** When medical emergency compels an abortion, the physician shall inform the woman, prior to abortion if possible, of the medical indications supporting the judgment that abortion is necessary to avert death or substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. Source: supplied §3205(b), statutory appendix at 505 U.S.903–904.

### The distinct information-omission defense in §3205(c)

**Holding and operative rule:** A physician is protected from liability for failing to furnish §3205(a)'s information if the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would have a severely adverse effect on the patient's physical or mental health. This statutory disclosure defense does not supply a general waiting-period waiver, authorize delay of necessary emergency treatment or replace §3203's emergency protection.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 9 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court gives the disclosure defense its separate statutory function. It protects a physician who reasonably believes that furnishing the prescribed information would severely harm this patient's physical or mental health and proves that belief by a preponderance of the evidence. The defense does not require proof that the feared injury actually occurred; the inquiry concerns the reasonable belief supporting the omission. It also does not excuse all inconvenience or every unfavorable reaction to information. Danforth permits appropriate informed-consent rules, and the defense limits their operation where the specified serious health concern exists. The principal contrary argument wrongly asks this provision to do the work of an emergency exception. A defense concerning what information is furnished cannot authorize postponing care that the emergency rule permits immediately. Conversely, the defense's separate mental-health terms cannot be erased because another provision uses a different formulation. The Court preserves this enacted allocation for this defense alone. It neither creates a universal State burden of proof nor decides every possible information-related health challenge.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: ordinary informed-consent administration remains permissible subject to the actual statutory and constitutional limits; no general proof rule is derived from it.

**Statutory terms and source distinction:** The supplied statutory appendix prints the penalty paragraph as '(e)', an OCR label. The district court and Third Circuit identify the quoted paragraph as §3205(c). The paragraph's no-violation defense covers failure to furnish subsection (a)'s information when the physician demonstrates, by a preponderance, the reasonable belief concerning severely adverse physical or mental health. The user's source file remains unchanged.

### Accurate material medical information

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3205(a)(1)(i)–(iii)'s accurate, medically material information concerning the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks may be required for informed consent. Content is sustained independently of the disputed physician-only delivery and fixed waiting requirements, with the statutory disclosure-health defense preserved.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 9 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** An informed decision requires material information about the treatment being authorized and its alternatives. Danforth permits a State to require voluntary and informed consent; Roe does not make the patient dependent on whatever facts a provider elects to omit. The required procedure, risk, gestational-age and childbirth information bears directly on this medical decision. The reasonable-patient materiality limitation supplies a concrete connection between the disclosure and consent.

The Court does not assume that every prescribed statement is accurate because the legislature calls it medical. False or exaggerated risk descriptions and ideological assent fall outside the sustained obligation. Nor does approval of these facts resolve who must personally deliver them or how long the patient must wait; those are separately reviewed duties. Section 3205(c) protects the reasonable belief, proved by a preponderance, that furnishing the information would severely adversely affect physical or mental health. The principal objection to imposed counseling therefore does not justify facially invalidating this bounded factual content. Concrete misleading implementation remains open to challenge.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: applied to ordinary voluntary and informed consent.
- Akron and Thornburgh: their protection against misleading or coercive communication survives; they do not forbid this isolated accurate medical content.

### Notice of available free materials

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3205(a)(2)(i) may require truthful notice that state materials describing fetal development and alternatives exist, that the patient has a right to review them, and that a copy will be supplied free if she chooses. The duty requires notice, not acceptance, reading or endorsement of the materials or listed agencies.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 8 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The fact that information is available differs from a command to accept its advice. This provision requires a concise description of the patient's option to review free materials. It does not require her to read a pamphlet, contact an agency, or agree with a governmental preference. Danforth's informed-consent principle permits a relevant explanation of available choices. The clinician may correct the directory, recommend another agency, or explain deficiencies.

The district findings about omissions and misinformation from some listed organizations must be taken seriously. They do not establish that the distinct statement about availability and the right to a free copy is false, or that all state materials convey unlawful information. The contrary argument treats the mandatory notice and optional receipt as the same obligation. They are separate. Akron and Thornburgh are qualified only to the extent their disclosure reasoning categorically excludes this narrow accurate notice merely because it might encourage childbirth. An identified deceptive or coercive implementation remains reviewable.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: applied to relevant informed-choice information.
- Akron and Thornburgh: qualified as to categorical exclusion of this concise accurate notice; their protections against falsehood, coercion and actual burdens remain.

### Notice of potentially available medical assistance

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3205(a)(2)(ii) may require concise accurate notice that medical assistance benefits may be available for prenatal care, childbirth and neonatal care, with more information in state materials. It does not promise eligibility or payment, and remains subject to the disclosure-health defense.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 8 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** A patient's understanding of available assistance can inform her decision without transferring that decision to the State. The statute speaks conditionally: benefits may be available. It does not guarantee eligibility, a particular award, or successful collection. That limitation matters because an inaccurate promise could distort consent. Danforth permits informed choice, and this concrete financial option bears a sufficient relationship to the alternatives being considered.

The State may favor childbirth through truthful information; the resulting possibility of persuasion does not alone make this concise notice unconstitutional. The Court rejects a categorical reading of Akron and Thornburgh that would exclude such a notice solely on that ground. It preserves their protection against misleading communication and coercion. Qualified delegated personnel may give the notice, and section 3205(c)'s separate health defense remains available on its stated preponderance showing. The Court does not hold that any truthful financial fact can be imposed regardless of relevance, manner or burden. A concrete unlawful implementation remains a distinct question.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: applied to relevant information supporting voluntary choice.
- Akron and Thornburgh: qualified for this concise conditional assistance notice; no general permission for compulsory advocacy follows.

### Paternal-support notice and the rape omission permission

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3205(a)(2)(iii) may require accurate notice that the father is liable to assist in supporting the child even if he offered to pay for an abortion; in the case of rape, this information may be omitted, without a police-reporting prerequisite. Legal liability cannot be represented as guaranteed payment, and the omission permission changes no support obligation. This holding governs the disclosure alone; a married patient's separate question of notifying her spouse of the pregnancy is governed by the spousal-notice holding below, not by this provision.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 8 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The disclosure identifies a legal responsibility relevant to the patient's alternatives. It does not establish that a particular father will pay, that collection will succeed, or that the State guarantees support. Danforth permits relevant information that assists voluntary consent. The Court sustains the concise statement of liability at that scope, while preserving the patient's decision and the clinician's ability to explain its practical limits.

The rape qualification is a permission. The statute says that in the case of rape this information may be omitted; it neither requires omission nor conditions it on a report to police. This holds whether or not the assailant is the patient's spouse: the qualification asks only whether the pregnancy resulted from rape, and a spousal assailant does not remove the patient from that class. The distinct spousal-sexual-assault reporting condition in section 3209 governs a different question — whether the patient must notify her spouse of the pregnancy at all — and cannot be imported into this disclosure provision or its terms. A patient whose assault does not qualify for section 3209's reported-assault exception, because she did not report within its 90-day period, is unaffected by that fact for purposes of this holding: the paternal-support omission permission carries no reporting condition of its own and remains available to her on the same terms as any other rape survivor. Section 3205(c)'s physical-or-mental-health defense also retains its separate terms. The contrary argument against childbirth-favoring notices does not justify categorical invalidation of this accurate legal information. Akron and Thornburgh are qualified only at that narrow point. Misleading claims about payment, compelled moral endorsement, and concrete coercive implementation remain outside the holding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: applied to truthful consent-related information.
- Akron and Thornburgh: qualified for accurate concise paternal-liability information with the statutory permission; their anti-coercion and accuracy protections remain.

### Statutory publication and chosen receipt of materials

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3208's publication duties and section 3205(a)(3)'s provision of a copy when the patient chooses to review it are sustained at their objective, accurate and nonjudgmental statutory scope. **Controlling explanation:** The State may publish information about fetal development and services available during pregnancy and after childbirth. Section 3208 regulates the content and format of that information: it requires objective, nonjudgmental scientific material and accurate descriptions, with pictures appropriate to gestational stage. The woman receives a copy under section 3205(a)(3) if she chooses to review the materials. Her decision to receive them does not become a duty to read or endorse them.

### Certification of lawful receipt

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3205(a)(4) may document actual receipt of lawful information without ideological assent or certification of unchosen reading. Its particular mandatory ancillary, timing and physician-delivery links stand or fall with the separately counted duties; a nine-Justice agreement on basic documentation is not a nine-Justice vote for every retained prerequisite.

**Authority:** Souter's basic receipt-versus-assent proposition has nine direct joins. Sustaining certification of the three mandatory ancillary notices has eight supporters (all except Blackmun), while retaining timing and physician-delivery links has five (White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas), on the respective substantive grounds identified in their own holdings. Agreement with the basic documentation principle does not adopt a duty the Justice opposes.

**Controlling explanation:** A written record of informed consent may document compliance without compelling an ideological pledge. Danforth permits consent documentation, and the present certification confirms that required information was given. The distinction between receipt and agreement controls. A patient need not certify agreement with the State's preference, the advice of a listed organization, or any belief about fetal life.

The form must also respect the statutory option concerning materials. If a patient does not choose to review them, the statute does not require a copy, and the certification cannot be used to create that missing duty. The contrary compelled-assent argument therefore does not justify invalidating accurate documentation of lawful receipt. Conversely, a form cannot restore an obligation that the Court has held invalid or expand one beyond its sustained terms. The Court sustains certification at this administrative scope, leaving a materially different coercive or false implementation for a concrete challenge. The fixed timing and physician-delivery duties receive independent judgments and are not decided by this documentation holding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: applied to written consent documentation, with receipt distinguished from assent.

### Physician delivery of medical information

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3205(a)(1)'s requirement that the performing or referring physician orally provide its medical information is sustained on this facial record as a reasonable assignment of clinical responsibility. ### The mandatory 24-hour interval

**Holding and operative rule:** The fixed 24-hour interval in section 3205 is sustained on this facial record, subject to the prospective medical-emergency protection. The demonstrated additional trips, expense and delay do not establish a severe obstruction requiring invalidation of this interval, and the deliberation interest reasonably supports it; no general permission for arbitrary or medically dangerous delay follows.

**Authority:** O'Connor's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 5 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The waiting requirement imposes real burdens. ### One-parent informed consent and independent judicial bypass

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3206's one-parent informed-consent structure survives this facial challenge with its emergency and family-substitution provisions and an effective independent judicial route. A minor may seek court authorization without first approaching a parent; maturity, capacity and actual informed consent, or alternatively her best interests, support authorization through confidential, expedited proceedings with counsel.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 9 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains the one-parent structure and independent bypass presented here. Bellotti identifies maturity and best interests as distinct routes to judicial authorization; Ashcroft sustains a parental-consent arrangement with effective judicial safeguards. Pennsylvania permits the minor to choose the judicial route without first requesting a parent's consent. It supplies counsel, confidentiality and prompt rulings, and requires authorization when either applicable route is established. The statute also accounts for emergency treatment, incest involving the father, parental unavailability and custodial arrangements. These features answer the claim that the structure necessarily delegates an unreviewable parental veto. The record establishes no present failure to provide the bypass's required operation; a past concern about preparations cannot be substituted for such a finding. Sustaining this structure does not declare every safeguard constitutionally indispensable or resolve every conceivable administration dispute. The ancillary information required for parental consent and the asserted attendance operation are treated separately. No independent adult-incapacity claim is decided, and medical emergency care cannot await parental or judicial proceedings.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bellotti v. Baird: applies the distinct maturity/informed-consent and best-interests routes rather than merging them into cumulative conditions.
- Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft: follows its sustention of parental consent supported by effective expedited judicial safeguards.
- Hodgson v. Minnesota and Ohio v. Akron Center: apply their treatment of usable independent bypass without converting parental responsibility into an adult spouse's authority.

**Statutory terms and source distinction:** Unemancipated minors under eighteen ordinarily need their own informed consent plus one parent's. ### Accurate information used for informed parental consent

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3206 may use accurate material medical information and concise, nonmisleading notices concerning available materials, possible assistance and paternal support to inform a parent's consent. That conclusion does not follow when the actual command supplies accurate, limited information for informed participation and leaves the independent judicial route available. The district court's use of §3205 to explain informed parental content does not put every adult timing or delivery mechanism into §3206. The Court therefore decides content separately and supplies no new parent-specific waiting period, nondelegable medical-speaker rule or attendance command.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft, Hodgson v. Minnesota and Ohio v. Akron Center: apply the distinct permissible parental-guidance setting and actual bypass safeguards.
- Akron and Thornburgh: categorical opposition to accurate concise assistance or alternative notices merely because they favor childbirth does not control this narrow factual-content permission; other delay and medical-delivery questions remain separately counted.

**Statutory terms and source distinction:** The district court used §3205 to interpret informed parental consent at 744 F.Supp.1382–1384. That is a lower-court construction, not an express §3206 incorporation of every §3205 timing/delivery mechanism. Assistance may be available, not guaranteed. Paternal liability includes the father who offered to pay for abortion; 'In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.' This permission has no law-enforcement reporting condition and does not guarantee collection. Materials are furnished if chosen, without required acceptance, reading or ideological assent. The §3206 emergency, incest, unavailability, custody and bypass terms remain as stated in the preceding holding.

### The parental-attendance objection and the denial of special relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court denies an attendance-specific vacatur, remand and interim injunction. Five Justices further hold that, even assuming the informed-consent practice produces the parental attendance found by the district court, the one-parent scheme survives this facial objection with its usable independent bypass; four Justices concur in denying the special relief because no enacted or authoritative compulsory-visit command has been established, without adopting bypass as a universal cure.

**Authority:** O'Connor's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 5 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court rejects the facial attendance objection even assuming the operation described by the district court. The Court does not construe §3206 to command an additional clinic visit. It accepts for this issue that medical practice may require parental attendance and that an already willing parent may face real difficulty attending. Hodgson and Ohio v. Akron support evaluating parental involvement together with an effective independent judicial route. Here a minor need not first exhaust attempts to obtain parental consent. She may obtain confidential and expedited authorization on maturity and informed consent or on best interests. The record does not establish that this route is presently unavailable or malfunctioning. Its burdens are not denied, but they do not make the challenged structure facially unconstitutional on this record. This is a holding of the five Justices joining the attendance opinion. It neither declares bypass a cure for every possible added requirement nor forecloses a concrete challenge to materially different administration. No attendance-specific vacatur, special remand or interim injunction issues.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Hodgson v. Minnesota: applies its consideration of an independent bypass when assessing burdens of parental involvement; the Court does not transplant parental responsibility to adult spousal notice.
- Ohio v. Akron Center: applies the adequacy of the actual safeguarded judicial alternative without deciding every hypothetical administrative failure.

**Statutory terms and source distinction:** Section 3206 does not expressly require an additional parental clinic visit. District findings232 and238–259 describe the practices clinics anticipated for informed parental consent and resulting costs/delays; the Third Circuit's footnote23 reserved whether that interpretation was correct. Current bypass finding266 supplies no current implementation-failure evidence. Judicial access is elective without parental-request exhaustion, with maturity/informed consent or best interests, counsel, confidentiality and three-business-day trial/five-business-day appeal ruling periods.

### Spousal notice and effective control over the protected decision

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3209 is invalid because its compulsory notice and certification condition gives spouses practical control over protected previability decisions for a large fraction of the independently defined women whom it requires to disclose against their wishes; voluntary consultation does not cure that operation. Its exceptions do not adequately protect that group, so the notice requirement and dependent enforcement are severed and enjoined; no general numerical facial-review formula is adopted.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy; 6 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The State cannot place an adult patient's protected decision under her spouse's practical control. Danforth forbids a spousal veto over abortion. The present law formally requires notification rather than consent, but the distinction does not answer what the compulsory disclosure does to women whose decisions it changes. A condition that predictably enables prevention of the protected procedure cannot become valid simply because it stops short of giving the spouse a written power of refusal. The Court applies Danforth to the operation established in this record, not to an assumption that every notice requirement has the same effect.

Most married patients voluntarily discuss abortion with their spouses. Their willingness to communicate supports voluntary consultation, but it cannot measure the constitutionality of a duty imposed on those who would not disclose. The relevant group is identified independently of the constitutional conclusion: married women whom the statute requires to notify against their wishes after its actual exceptions are taken into account. The district findings establish substantial physical, psychological, economic and family pressures within that group. For a large fraction of these women, the mandatory notice would expose the decision to effective obstruction. The Court does not invent a percentage, classify every marriage as coercive, or treat an isolated difficult application as sufficient for categorical relief.

The exceptions must be considered as they operate. A woman can provide a signed substitute statement that her spouse is not the father or could not be located after diligent effort. She can also invoke pregnancy resulting from spousal sexual assault described in section 3128 and reported to law enforcement with jurisdiction. That incorporated law, section 3128(c), requires the victim or her agent personally to report the assault to an agency with jurisdiction within 90 days of commission of the offense; a survivor who does not report within that window does not qualify for this particular substitute statement, though the not-located or anticipated-injury substitutes may still be available to her. District finding 297 explains how the reporting restriction further narrows the exception for women unable to report the assault promptly. That a survivor did not meet the 90-day condition does not establish that notifying her husband is safe or that he will not obstruct her decision; the condition measures only whether one particular substitute statement is available, not the risk notice actually poses. The remaining substitute concerns reason to believe that notice is likely to cause bodily injury to the woman by her spouse or another person. A medical emergency independently excuses notice. The notice and exception statements need not be notarized, but must warn that false statements are punishable. The 90-day condition is part of the reasoning for why these exceptions do not adequately protect the compelled group; it is not itself a separate holding, and the Court does not adjudicate the validity of section 3128(c) as a criminal-law reporting requirement, which is not before it. A patient's separate paternal-support disclosure under section 3205(a)(2)(iii) is unaffected by whether she qualifies for this notice exception; that disclosure's rape-omission permission carries no reporting condition of its own, as stated in its own holding above.

Those provisions do not generally excuse threatened financial retaliation, psychological coercion, injury to children or others, exposure of private information, or divorce and custody threats. The record also shows why some endangered women cannot safely use the available certification routes. The Court neither denies the protection those exceptions provide nor rewrites likely bodily injury as every form of coercion. Their incomplete coverage matters because the State itself makes disclosure a prerequisite to lawful care. Poverty or an abusive relationship alone is not the challenged governmental act; the additional legal condition activates the demonstrated obstacle.

The State's interests in marital communication and potential life do not supply a spouse with supervisory authority over an adult patient. The minors' cases rest on parents' responsibilities for children and on independent bypass safeguards. They therefore do not support treating a wife as a minor or requiring her to establish an acceptable reason for secrecy. Protecting voluntary communication remains permissible. Enforcing an ideal of marital consultation through a condition that produces practical control over the protected choice does not.

Ohio v. Akron Center's facial-review discussion does not require counting women who voluntarily notify as constitutionally valid restrictive applications of this law. The Court confines this conclusion to the proved operation of the notice condition and the group it actually compels; it does not adopt a numerical cutoff or a new general facial-challenge test. Adding judicial exceptions for every uncovered threat would reconstruct the law and still force endangered patients to qualify for secrecy. Severance provides effective relief without that reconstruction. The judgment in No. 91-902 is affirmed, and the notice-linked report fields are separately removed. Independent spousal speech, equality and generalized privacy grounds are unnecessary to this controlling liberty judgment and remain undecided by the Court.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth, 428 U.S. 52 (1976): extended from formal spousal consent to notice proved to give effective control; voluntary consultation remains lawful.
- Ohio v. Akron Center, 497 U.S. 502, 514 (1990): its no-valid-applications formulation is qualified for this notice condition; voluntary disclosure by unaffected women does not defeat the demonstrated facial defect.
- Bellotti II, Ashcroft and Hodgson: distinguished because parental responsibility and effective bypass for minors do not establish marital authority over an adult.
- Roe and Doe: supply the protected decision and its life-and-health boundary.

### Confidential referring-source identification

**Holding and operative rule:** The challenged referring-source field in section 3214(a)(1) is sustained facially as confidential information linked to possible clinical responsibility and follow-up. The record's specific referral deterrence does not establish that the blanket field facially denies protected access or lacks the required legitimate relation; targeted abusive or materially obstructive collection remains open to challenge.

**Authority:** O'Connor's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; 6 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** Confidential reporting may identify persons who have participated in the medical process and may be able to explain the reported care. Danforth permits reasonably directed health reporting with adequate confidentiality. The referring physician, agency or service is not necessarily the performing physician, but referral can connect to counseling, assessment and subsequent inquiry. That possible relationship supplies a legitimate reason for the information; the Court does not treat every demanded identity as automatically necessary.

The district found that particular physicians would stop referring if named even though the confidentiality safeguards were adequate. The Court accepts that evidence and rejects any invented premise that no deterrence exists. The limited showing does not establish the asserted general impairment of access or require facial invalidation of this field. The contrary argument identifies a genuine cost of collection, but not a demonstrated defect of the scope of the requested relief. Confidentiality must remain effective, and a targeted arbitrary collection practice or concrete obstruction would present a distinct claim. Performing-physician identification and unappealed fields are not reopened.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: applied to reasonably directed confidential reporting.
- Thornburgh: actual deterrence and disclosure consequences are examined; neither automatic invalidity nor immunity is inferred.

### Confidential reporting of medical necessity under §3214(a)(8)

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3214(a)(8) may require a confidential account of the physician's basis for invoking §3211(b)(1)'s medical-necessity predicate. Recording that judgment does not establish a new substantive necessity test, require advance approval, adjudicate every other postviability restriction or create an offense merely because another physician later disagrees.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 9 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains a record of the reason an existing medical-necessity predicate was invoked. Danforth permits appropriately directed confidential health reporting; a physician's explanation can support both medical knowledge and lawful review of compliance. The challenged field asks why §3211(b)(1) applied. It does not itself change that provision's substantive requirements or transfer the initial treatment judgment to an official. The district court's concern that retrospective scrutiny may chill professional judgment deserves attention, but documentation alone does not establish the anticipated unlawful interference. This decision neither authorizes punishment merely because a predicted harm failed to occur nor supplies a new general immunity from enforcement. Liability continues to depend on the actual lawful offense and its required predicates. The report remains confidential under the safeguards accepted here and omits the patient's name. The Court does not decide the independent constitutionality of every later-abortion restriction, substitute this basis field for gestational-age reporting, or foreclose a concrete challenge to administration that exceeds the duty sustained today.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: applies the permissibility of appropriately directed confidential reporting rather than inferring invalidity from the existence of documentation.
- Thornburgh v. American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists: its attention to actual burdens and disclosure consequences is preserved; it does not automatically invalidate this distinct confidential medical-basis field.

**Statutory terms and source distinction:** Section3214(a)(8) requests the basis for the judgment that an abortion under §3211(b)(1) was necessary to prevent death or substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. That predicate concerns the referenced rule for abortion at twenty-four or more weeks, not merely a viability/gestational-age result. The individual report excludes patient names; actual confidentiality safeguards are accepted in this litigation. Sources: supplied §§3214(a), (a)(8); district reporting findings and Third Circuit reporting discussion.

### Confidential reporting of an emergency judgment under §3214(a)(10)

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3214(a)(10) may require a confidential after-the-fact account of the medical judgment that an emergency excused compliance with the Act. It does not require permission or reporting before treatment, narrow the prospective good-faith emergency construction, or make professional disagreement alone a newly punishable violation.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 9 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains documentation of the emergency actually invoked. Danforth permits confidential reports directed to health administration, and the State may retain an account of why ordinary statutory requirements could not be followed. The question is different from whether an official may approve treatment beforehand. This provision asks for the basis of the physician's medical judgment; it supplies no advance-permission requirement and no reason to postpone necessary care. The emergency definition remains governed by the prospective construction stated in this decision. Thus the physician need not wait until the feared injury occurs before treating the patient and later describing the judgment. The contrary claim assumes that recording reasons itself authorizes hostile or medically unreasonable enforcement. It does not. Existing lawful offense requirements continue to control; another professional's later disagreement alone is not a new offense created by the reporting provision. The Court preserves the confidential report while leaving concrete challenges to a demand for preclearance, delayed treatment or materially different enforcement for an actual dispute.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: follows its permission for appropriate confidential health administration, with substantive treatment limits independently preserved.
- Webster v. Reproductive Health Services: the medical-construction principle informs the preserved prospective emergency interpretation; the report does not revise that interpretation.

**Statutory terms and source distinction:** The field requires the basis for any medical judgment that a medical emergency existed which excused compliance with a provision of the Act. Section3203 supplies the physician's good-faith clinical judgment of immediate need to avert death or delay creating serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function, prospectively construed to prevent a significant threat to life or health. Section3205(c)'s information-omission defense remains a different provision. Individual reports exclude patient names and retain accepted confidentiality protections.

### Confidential reporting of the diagnostic basis under §3214(a)(11)

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3214(a)(11), incorporating §3210(a), may require reporting the actual inquiries, examinations, tests and diagnostic basis used to determine probable gestational age under the prudent-physician standard. It does not prescribe additional medically unnecessary tests; §3210(a)'s exception remains when medical emergency prevents compliance, and the separate age-result field is not newly adjudicated.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 9 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains the report of the diagnostic process actually used. Section 3214(a)(11) incorporates a prudent-physician inquiry, not a list of tests that every patient must undergo. Webster supports preserving useful medical assessment while rejecting an interpretation demanding unnecessary or dangerous procedures. Danforth supplies the separate permission for appropriately directed confidential reporting. Together, those principles distinguish a record of appropriate clinical work from a new command to perform additional work merely to complete a form. The providers correctly distinguish this field from the gestational-age result itself, but that distinction does not make the additional account impermissible. It allows the department to understand the basis of the diagnosis without replacing the governing medical standard. The emergency-prevents-compliance exception remains operative. The Court does not reopen the unappealed underlying ascertainment requirement or age-result field, and does not authorize punishment simply because another clinician would have selected a different defensible inquiry. A concrete demand for medically useless tests or unavailable findings would present a different question.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Webster v. Reproductive Health Services: applies prudent, useful medical assessment rather than an inflexible demand for unnecessary tests.
- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: applies confidential reporting permission to the actual diagnostic-basis field, distinct from the age-result requirement.

**Statutory terms and source distinction:** Section3210(a), quoted in district finding309, excuses compliance when medical emergency prevents it. Otherwise the referring or performing physician first determines probable gestational age through inquiries and examinations/tests a prudent physician considers necessary for accurate diagnosis. The performing physician reports types of inquiries, examinations and tests used and the diagnostic basis. Section3214(a)(11) incorporates that information; §3214(a)(5) separately records gestational age. Accepted report confidentiality excludes patient identification by name.

### Notice-compliance and nonnotification-reason reports

**Holding and operative rule:** The notice-given and reasons-for-nonnotification components of section 3214(a)(12) are invalid and severed because they enforce or reproduce section 3209's unconstitutional disclosure condition. The independent confidential marital-status question remains, and no report or certification may restore the invalid notice obligation.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy; 6 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** A reporting requirement cannot preserve the very condition that the substantive holding removes. Section 3214(a)(12) asks whether a married patient notified her spouse and, if not, why notice was withheld. Those fields track compliance with section 3209 and require an explanation for exercising the protected decision without compelled disclosure. Their operation cannot be justified merely by renaming the demand medical reporting.

Danforth's permission for confidential health information does not authorize enforcement of a constitutionally invalid spousal prerequisite. The Court therefore reverses approval of the notice-given and nonnotice-reason fields and directs their removal, with dependent enforcement, in the implementing proceedings. The marital-status question is separately operative and receives a separate holding. Act 64's severability clause permits invalid words or applications to be removed while provisions that can operate independently continue. The contrary suggestion that all marital data must either stand or fall together overlooks those distinct functions. The remedy removes notice enforcement without disabling lawful confidential administration.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: confidential-reporting permission does not validate enforcement of an invalid spousal condition.
- Brockett: supports relief confined to the unconstitutional component; state severability law governs the surviving text.

### The independently severable marital-status field

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3214(a)(12)'s confidential inquiry whether the patient was married is sustained and severed from its invalid notice-compliance and nonnotification-reason questions. The field supplies demographic information without requiring spousal disclosure or an explanation for withholding it.

**Authority:** Stone's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 9 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** Whether a patient was married is distinct from whether she notified her spouse. The former may be collected as confidential demographic information; the latter fields track an invalid legal obligation. Danforth permits appropriately directed confidential health records, and the record accepts the adequacy of the patient-protection safeguards. No patient's name is placed in the individual procedure report, and no finding establishes public release of patient identities.

Act 64 expressly preserves words and provisions capable of operating without the invalid text. The marital-status question can operate on its own and does not need a notice duty to explain its informational function. The Court therefore sustains that question while removing the notice and reasons components. The strongest contrary argument treats all three as a single mechanism, but their statutory functions are separable. This narrow holding creates no general governmental entitlement to intimate information, and it does not resolve a future claim of inadequate confidentiality or abusive collection. Forms and enforcement must conform to the surviving field rather than reconstruct the invalid notice requirement.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: applied to confidential demographic reporting.
- Brockett: supports component-limited relief, implemented through Act 64 and Pennsylvania severability law.

### Public facility and affiliation reports

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3207(b)'s public release of facility and affiliation reports is sustained facially when the facility received State-appropriated funds during the 12 calendar months preceding the inspection or copying request. Without such funding, access remains confined to law-enforcement officials and the two medical boards for official duties; collection is distinct from publication.

**Authority:** Souter's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 7 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** Public support of a facility supplies a legitimate accountability interest in its organization and affiliations. Webster permits relevant distinctions in the use of public resources, while its funding holdings do not establish a waiver of every constitutional right. Section 3207(b) concerns institutional information and ties public access to funding received during the twelve calendar months before the request. The Court preserves that actual trigger.

The district's findings of harassment and likely funding refusal are substantial contrary evidence. The Court does not dismiss lawful providers' fears or assume that funds support only abortions. The record nevertheless does not sufficiently establish the additional disclosure-related impairment necessary for the requested facial relief. The information does not identify individual patients, and the asserted accountability relation is not eliminated merely because the facility is already publicly visible. A concrete targeted or materially obstructive release may present another question. Confidential agency collection remains independently operative, and the official-duty limits govern reports of facilities outside the funding condition.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Webster: applied to relevant public-resource accountability, without a blanket constitutional-rights waiver.
- Thornburgh: disclosure consequences are examined on this full record; its different reporting scheme does not automatically decide these institutional reports.

### Public quarterly totals and trimester reports

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3214(f)'s release of quarterly facility totals, divided by trimester, is sustained facially only when State-appropriated funds were received within the 12 calendar months before filing the report. A facility's declaration that it receives no such funds keeps the report confidential unless contrary evidence establishes funding; this filing trigger differs from section 3207(b)'s request trigger.

**Authority:** Souter's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 7 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The public may receive aggregate information about services at a facility supported with public funds. The quarterly totals and trimester categories concern institutional activity, not a patient's identity. The statute links their release to funding during the twelve calendar months before the report is filed. It also directs confidentiality when the facility reports no funding unless the department receives contrary evidence. Neither qualification may be replaced by section 3207(b)'s different request-based rule.

Webster's public-resource principles permit relevant accountability distinctions without establishing that funding purchases a surrender of constitutional protection. The district's deterrence and exposure findings are considered, but the evidence does not sufficiently establish an incremental burden warranting facial invalidation of this publication condition. Confidential oversight might be another policy, yet its availability does not alone defeat aggregate public accountability on this record. The Court preserves a concrete challenge to materially obstructive or otherwise unlawful disclosure. Collection of the quarterly information remains separate, and nothing authorizes release of individual patient reports.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Webster: applied to bounded institutional accountability associated with public resources.
- Thornburgh: distinguished after examination of the different data, safeguards and demonstrated disclosure effects.

### Independent speech challenge to medical consent information

**Holding and operative rule:** The First Amendment does not forbid requiring the accurate, material medical facts in section 3205(a)(1) as part of informed consent to this treatment. This is a bounded consent rule, not a holding that paid medical speech is categorically commercial or that any truthful statement can be compelled.

**Authority:** Souter's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 8 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** A physician's speech does not lose constitutional protection because the physician is paid. Riley prevents that shortcut and requires examination of what the government actually compels. Here the required statements explain the proposed procedure, its material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and the medical risks of childbirth. Their connection to the patient's authorization of treatment supplies the relevant justification.

Danforth permits informed consent, and the First Amendment does not require the State to permit treatment without the accurate material information that makes consent meaningful. The rule requires factual disclosure, not personal acceptance of the State's beliefs. Wooley's protection against compelled ideological affirmation therefore does not invalidate these particular medical commands. The Court preserves the statutory health defense and the limits of accuracy and materiality. It neither accepts a supposed commercial-speech concession nor creates general professional-speech immunity. A false, irrelevant or ideological script, and the separately disputed delivery and timing requirements, would require their own analysis.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Riley v. National Federation of the Blind, 487 U.S. 781 (1988): payment alone does not remove speech protection.
- Danforth: supplies the legitimate informed-consent connection.
- Wooley v. Maynard, 430 U.S. 705 (1977): distinguished because factual treatment information does not require ideological affirmation.

### Independent speech challenge to the three ancillary notices

**Holding and operative rule:** The First Amendment does not facially invalidate the concise truthful notices of available materials, possible assistance and paternal liability at the scopes separately sustained. None requires ideological assent or endorsement of a listed agency; the notice of materials remains distinct from chosen receipt.

**Authority:** Souter's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 7 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court examines each command rather than calling the whole scheme commercial speech. Riley protects speech even when compensation is involved, and Wooley protects against forced ideological affirmation. Those principles do not convert the particular facts here into compelled belief. Availability is a fact distinct from an agency's advice; assistance is described as possible, not guaranteed; paternal liability is a legal responsibility, not a promise of payment. The rape omission permission and separate disclosure-health defense remain operative.

The notice does not itself require acceptance of the available materials. Clinicians may correct the information or criticize a listed agency. These limited functions bear a concrete connection to informed choice. The contrary argument rightly identifies a constitutional problem with compulsory ideology or deceptive information, but it does not establish that these bounded commands require either. The Court does not hold that truth alone permits any compelled communication. Concrete falsehood, coercive distribution or materially different enforcement remains open to challenge. Blackmun does not reach these three independent speech grounds after invalidating the mandatory ancillary commands on due process grounds.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Riley: forecloses the compensation-based shortcut; each command is examined independently.
- Wooley: protects against compelled ideological assent, absent from the sustained commands.
- Danforth: supplies the informed-choice connection.

### Independent speech challenge to chosen materials and lawful receipt certification

**Holding and operative rule:** The First Amendment does not invalidate state publication and genuinely chosen receipt of objective materials, or a certificate confined to receipt of lawful information rather than ideological assent. This principle does not supply a vote sustaining a contested underlying notice, timing or delivery obligation.

**Authority:** Souter's severable opinion part, joined at this level by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; 8 direct supporters including the author. The controlling proposition has a participating majority; judgment-only votes are separately listed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes information a patient chooses to receive from speech the State forces her to endorse. Section 3208 permits publication within its objective and accurate statutory terms. Section 3205(a)(3) supplies a copy only if the woman chooses to review it. Neither provision requires acceptance of a belief, and a clinician remains free to correct or criticize the material. Wooley's protection against compelled ideological affirmation therefore does not invalidate those limited functions.

The written certificate similarly records receipt rather than assent. Danforth permits documentation of informed consent, while Riley prevents treating compensation as an automatic answer to a speech objection. The relevant justification is the actual consent function, not the speaker's paid status. The Court sustains the basic documentation principle without converting any Justice's objection to an underlying duty into approval of that duty. The distinct votes on ancillary, timing and delivery links remain controlling for those applications. Concrete deceptive distribution, forced reading or a certificate demanding personal agreement would present a different question.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Wooley: distinguished at voluntary receipt and factual certification scope; ideological assent remains protected.
- Danforth: applied to consent documentation.
- Riley: paid status supplies no categorical exception to speech protection.

## 8. Law After Decision

Effective June 29, 1992, versions 1, 1.2 and 1.3 are superseded by this correction. Six retain protected previability choice, viability and necessary life-and-health care. Five—Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter—adopt the necessary strict-justification floor for other severe interference with access, preserving independent prohibitions and remaining provision-specific law. Six—Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas—remove the trimester timetable as an independent timing restriction; Stone's own vote rests on the affirmative substitute he adopts in his separate opinion, which the other five do not join. No complete replacement formula, generic automatic obstacle rule, universal lesser-burden allocation or additional Marks synthesis controls. This record's dedicated governing-standard holding states how lower courts apply those rules together. The specific information, waiting, physician-delivery, parental and reporting holdings govern at their actual scopes; the notice condition and dependent notice-report fields are invalid, and marital status remains. Independently sufficient speech rationales have eight and seven supporters, respectively; the incorporated parental ancillary-content rationale has seven. Stone's fallback votes add judgments only. Existing clinical culpability rules remain; the medical-report holdings create no general professional immunity.

The Court does not decide standalone emergency vagueness or waiver; an independent adult-incapacity claim; constitutional fetal personhood; a general funding entitlement; conscience claims; the unchallenged sex-selection rule; every postviability restriction; or district-upheld unappealed ascertainment, age-result and collection duties. The separate spousal First Amendment claim and its preservation are left open as unnecessary to the Court's complete liberty relief. No independent equality, generalized marital-privacy or informational-privacy rationale commands a majority. O'Connor's narrow equality and family grounds remain separate; White applies intermediate scrutiny while Scalia and Thomas assume it for their contrary application. Retention of Roe makes the providers' conditional general alternative-foundation remand unnecessary. The Court adopts no general numerical facial formula, universal lesser-burden State allocation, general professional immunity, or right to admission of a drug.

## 9. Separate Writings

### Stone — Opinion of the Court, and separately, concurring in part and dissenting in part

Stone writes for the Court on protected choice, stare decisis, the trimester-timing change, the necessary severe-interference minimum, the medical-emergency construction, the sustained medical and ancillary disclosures including the rape omission permission, publication and chosen receipt, lawful certification, the denial of special materials relief, the one-parent structure and bypass, spousal notice and its dependent reports, the three confidential medical-basis reports, and the marital-status field. Those parts state the Court's holdings at the scope and with the joins given above; they are not restated here.

Separately, Stone's own opinion states the six-step method he adopts as his definitive position and by which he reaches those results, going beyond what the Court's holdings themselves require the joining Justices to accept. It begins with the actual legal duty, identifies its function, examines concrete and cumulative interference, applies distinct constitutional rules in a stated order, protects clinical judgment and truthful communication, and tailors relief. It treats a substantial obstacle to otherwise clinically appropriate protected care as independently invalid. Other severe interference must at minimum serve a compelling permissible interest and be narrowly tailored — the necessary minimum, which Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter join as a floor without joining the complete method. His additional lesser-burden branch requires the State to substantiate justification and fit once meaningful additional interference is established, including an answer to concrete practicable substantially less burdensome alternatives; only Stevens joins that branch and the complete substantive proposal. He identifies that branch and its allocation as his own proposed extension, not an already universal rule.

Carey supports exacting justification for serious interference with the protected decision; Doe supports timely good-faith clinical care, including clinically relevant physical and mental health. Evidence and substantial professional agreement matter without granting an association a veto. Stone proposes definite duties and appropriate culpability protection, including that professional disagreement alone cannot establish wrongdoing, while preserving fraud, malpractice and lawful discipline. This proposed general protection is distinct from the Court's narrower conclusion that reporting itself creates no new offense or prior permission requirement.

His vote for the trimester-timing change is his own: he now treats the six-step method as the operative substitute that satisfies his own prior condition, and he does not require the other five Justices in that coalition to adopt it before casting his vote. Blackmun, Stevens and Souter decline that change for their own stated reasons, unaffected by his. On fixed delay and physician exclusivity, Stone dissents from the Court's sustaining judgment: Akron directly forbids both requirements on this record, and his own method independently confirms that conclusion. Doe protects clinical judgment; Danforth supplies the consent/reporting distinction; the minors' cases govern parental safeguards.

On the reports he opposes, Stone dissents from the Court's sustaining judgments: he rejects blanket referring-source identification and both automatic public-release requirements, accepting the district's deterrence evidence without inventing patient disclosure. Those reporting conclusions and their reasoning are his own applications, distinct from the opinions of the Court he does not join on those points. He confines his Ohio v. Akron qualification to the spousal-notice holding, where he writes for the Court. His complete six-step method has one other joiner, Stevens; the necessary minimum and the trimester-timing change each have their own stated coalitions, distinct from and larger than the complete method's.

### Blackmun — Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

Justice Blackmun would preserve Roe's protected decision and its staged scrutiny. His concurrence in the Court's protected-choice and strict-justification minimum does not surrender the stronger protection he finds in Roe, Thornburgh and his Webster opinion. An independently forbidden obstacle cannot be licensed by balancing, and a lesser burden can fail because its justification is insufficient. He accepts these protections as minimum requirements while rejecting a rule that makes adequate ordinary fit a complete substitute for the scrutiny his precedents require. He therefore dissents from the majority's separate elimination of the trimester timetable.

Blackmun sustains the emergency provision only under the prospective construction that protects significant threats to life and health before catastrophic injury. He preserves individualized clinical judgment, the explanation before treatment only when possible, and the distinct disclosure defense for reasonably anticipated severely adverse physical or mental effects. He also accepts accurate material medical information, optional publication and receipt of state materials, and certification limited to lawful duties. He rejects the mandatory ancillary notices because permission to refuse the ultimate packet does not remove the compulsory counseling prerequisite. The paternal-support rape provision permits omission without police reporting; his remaining objection rests on mandatory indiscriminate counseling, not a misstatement of that qualification.

Akron independently requires invalidation of the fixed adult waiting period and unnecessary exclusive physician delivery. Qualified delegation preserves medical responsibility without the record's unsupported additional costs and visits. Blackmun accepts the sufficient one-parent and elective bypass structure at the limited scope presented, but would sever mandatory ancillary content incorporated into parental consent. He does not treat an anticipated clinic visit as enacted text and seeks no attendance-specific vacatur or special remand. He likewise supports no additional materials injunction on a finding that all publications are false; particular unlawful implementation remains challengeable.

Blackmun joins invalidation of spousal notice and its notice-compliance reports, preserving confidential marital status. The actual coercive record, rather than every marriage, explains the constitutional defect. He declines unnecessary separate speech, equality and general privacy holdings. On reports, Thornburgh's concern with deterrence and his Ashcroft distinction between useful records and needless professional burdens lead him to invalidate blanket referring-source identification. He would also invalidate the two public-release duties because the demonstrated exposure and access burden lack adequate justification, while retaining confidential collection. He nevertheless sustains the three confidential medical-basis reports: recording actual necessity, emergency and prudent diagnostic judgments is not additional permission or compulsory testing. Those limited agreements do not adopt every reporting rationale. The contrary majority dispositions govern at their precise scope; his stronger rules remain noncontrolling where the Court does not adopt them.

### Stevens — Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

Justice Stevens agrees that the Constitution protects the patient's reproductive decision and that the Court should retain Roe's foundation for the reasons stated in the Court's opinion. His Thornburgh, Webster and Hodgson writings require examination of both a restriction's severity and the legitimacy and fit of its justification. He therefore joins the Chief Justice's proposed substantive six-step approach, including the independent obstacle prohibition, the necessary strict-justification minimum for other severe interference, and scrutiny of meaningful lesser burdens with justification assigned to the State. The proposal requires proof of a real causal burden and permits practical evidence; it does not demand perfect legislation, the cheapest imaginable rule or professional unanimity. Its case-specific approach to relief now supplies no new universal facial formula as a condition of that substantive agreement.

Stevens would replace mechanical trimester rules only with operative protection of the kind he accepts in that proposal. A minority proposal cannot itself satisfy that condition. He consequently dissents from the Court's independent elimination of the timetable while recognizing that the resulting majority rule, together with the Court's other controlling holdings, governs lower courts. His agreement with the proposed framework does not extend to every factual application in the Chief Justice's opinion.

Akron supplies an independent ground to invalidate the compulsory adult waiting period and exclusive physician delivery of routine disclosures. Hodgson's recognition of useful parental consultation does not justify a mandatory reconsideration period after an adult has made an informed choice. Appropriate delegation preserves the clinician's responsibility. Stevens sustains the prospective emergency construction, accurate material medical information, and concise truthful ancillary notices with the statutory safeguards. Paternal support is liability, not guaranteed collection, and rape information may be omitted without police reporting. Optional materials and lawful receipt certification require no ideological assent. He seeks no additional materials injunction or special remand based on insufficiently identified defective implementation.

Stevens accepts the one-parent structure, accurate informed-parent content and independent confidential, expeditious bypass with alternative maturity/informed-consent and best-interest grounds. He rejects an invented clinic-attendance command and supports no attendance-specific vacatur, while preserving an actual unnecessary-visit challenge. He joins spousal-notice invalidation on the record's practical control over protected choice, including severance of notice-compliance reporting while retaining marital status; further independent constitutional grounds are unnecessary.

The report provisions require separate judgments. Stevens would invalidate indiscriminate referring-source identification because documented deterrence lacks adequate demonstrated benefit. He sustains confidential documentation of the three distinct medical judgments without prior approval or additional tests. He also sustains both public-release provisions facially: funded-institution accountability and the limited proof of incremental disclosure obstruction distinguish them from the patient's private medical records. Thus he declines the Chief Justice's contrary public-release applications while reserving concrete future disclosure challenges.

### O'Connor — Concurrence explaining the scope of the common minimum

O'Connor retains the protected previability decision and the life-and-health boundary while rejecting the trimester timetable as an independent regulator of permissible legislation. Her Akron and Thornburgh dissents distinguish the severity of interference from the State's justification for it. The initial question concerns the actual restriction on access; it is not answered by balancing the State's asserted interests against a patient's burden. Her agreement with the Court's necessary severe-interference minimum preserves that distinction. It does not make an obstacle label automatically decisive in every medical case or adopt a new justification burden for every lesser interference.

She accepts the independent limits on prohibition, effective third-party veto and denial of timely necessary care. Other severe access restrictions require the exacting justification the common minimum states. Passing a necessary condition does not override an independent prohibition. She nevertheless rejects Stone's complete substantial-obstacle rule and universal lesser-burden branch. Her earlier writings permit reasonable regulation below the severe threshold. Riggins's particular bodily-liberty justification rule does not automatically transfer to all abortion regulation, just as Medina addresses its own procedural setting.

The actual statutory functions remain distinct. She sustains prospective emergency protection, accurate medical information, qualified ancillary notices, optional materials and lawful receipt documentation. She sustains physician delivery and the fixed interval because the accepted costs and delays do not establish the constitutional access defect asserted on this record, and the duties reasonably serve medical responsibility and deliberation. Her parental analysis considers the independently usable bypass even assuming the attendance operation found below; it creates no new textual clinic-visit command.

Spousal notice fails for a different reason. The State's condition enables effective control over the adult patient's decision for the actually compelled group, and the exceptions leave substantial harms uncovered. She also states narrow equality and private-family grounds, which no Court majority adopts as independent holdings. Her Hogan reasoning rejects the husband-only supervisory entitlement; the family-autonomy ground concerns compulsory reproductive communication, not every marital disclosure. She sustains the confidential medical and referral fields and bounded public-funding transparency requirements while accepting their actual burdens. Ordinary special-justification and reliance reasoning supports retaining Roe's foundation; she adds no special precedential force merely because a decision is publicly divisive.

### Kennedy — Concurrence in part and dissent in part on the governing framework

Kennedy retains the constitutional previability choice, the viability boundary and necessary life-and-health protection. His Webster joinder recognized protected liberty while criticizing rigid implementation, and his Michael H. concurrence rejected a compulsory method confined to the narrowest historical description of a claimed right. His Payne joinder supports a special justification for constitutional overruling rather than a change based solely on disagreement or membership. Those sources support the retained foundation without settling every regulatory test.

He does not join the newly stated general severe-interference tier. His approach evaluates reasonable nonpreclusive regulation in the particular constitutional setting and recognizes the independent prohibition on effective third-party control. A common judgment does not establish agreement with a universal scrutiny sequence. He likewise declines Stone's automatic substantial-obstacle branch, universal lesser-burden justification rule and general burden allocation. His separate positions in Riggins, Foucha and Medina show why a rule governing one kind of bodily or procedural liberty cannot simply be transferred to every abortion requirement. He supports removing the trimester timetable as an independent timing restriction, while leaving the separately retained constitutional boundaries intact.

The medical-emergency construction permits prompt care without waiting for catastrophic injury. The truthful medical and ancillary disclosures inform consent without ideological assent; materials are optional and paternal-support information may be omitted in rape without a police report. He sustains accountable physician delivery and the fixed interval on the actual record. His Hodgson and Ohio v. Akron writings support the distinct parental responsibility and effective independent bypass, including sustention even on the assumed attendance operation, but do not supply an adult spouse with a parent's authority.

The trial findings demonstrate why compulsory spousal notification can give effective control over the protected choice. Kennedy joins the narrow affected-population application and the qualification of Ohio v. Akron necessary to decide this notice requirement. He does not generalize that application into a numerical facial formula or adopt independent spousal equality, generalized privacy or speech holdings. He supports severing notice-compliance reports while retaining marital-status information. The remaining confidential fields and funding-linked institutional reports survive at their individual scopes. His refusal of a complete framework does not deny the force of the Court's actual majority holdings, and his agreement with particular permissions does not supply a general rational-review holding for every lesser burden.

### Souter — Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

Justice Souter's separate opinion explains the limits of his agreement with the Court and his disagreement with the dispositions sustaining the fixed waiting period and exclusive physician delivery. He retains Roe's protected choice and necessary life-and-health care. His Payne concurrence requires a special justification for abandoning constitutional precedent; the current Court's treatment of particular regulatory rules must therefore identify the rule, the demonstrated defect and the protection that remains. He agrees to a necessary minimum of compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring for severe interference with protected reproductive choice. Carey supports that limited access-based safeguard. The minimum neither authorizes independently forbidden control nor makes every lesser burden valid.

Souter does not adopt either a complete obstacle-only replacement or the Chief Justice's universal lesser-burden extension and burden allocation. His prior joins in Foucha and Riggins concern defined liberty interests and their governing conditions, while his Medina and Wright positions preserve attention to the precise constitutional and procedural question. Those decisions do not furnish an all-purpose abortion formula. He also declines bare elimination of the trimester timetable absent a sufficient explanation for the particular protections withdrawn. The majority's contrary timetable ruling nevertheless controls lower courts at its actual scope; his precedent-based disagreement cannot negate it.

For compulsory adult delay and exclusive routine counseling by a physician, Akron supplies a direct rule. The gravity of the abortion decision was already known when that case was decided, and the present record shows additional visits, expense and longer practical delays. A general appeal to reflection provides no adequate new reason to discard the adult rule. The accepted role of physicians in parental notification likewise does not establish that qualified personnel must be excluded from adult counseling. Souter would sever those requirements and their dependent certification and enforcement while retaining medical responsibility and voluntary reflection. These are independent grounds, not inferred agreement with the Chief Justice's entire proposal.

Souter sustains the prospective emergency construction, truthful material clinical disclosures, concise qualified ancillary notices, optional materials and certification of lawful receipt. He preserves the rape omission permission and rejects additional materials proceedings unsupported by an adequately identified unlawful compulsory communication. The actual one-parent structure and effective independent bypass are facially sufficient; no enacted attendance duty justifies a separate vacatur or remand. He joins spousal-notice invalidation and removal of notice-compliance reporting, retaining confidential marital status and declining unnecessary independent speech, equality and general privacy grounds.

His reporting conclusions remain provision-specific. He sustains referring-source identification for legitimate confidential follow-up despite the limited referral-deterrence finding, and sustains both public-release provisions on their institutional accountability grounds and limited demonstrated incremental obstruction. He also sustains the three medical-basis reports as documentation of actual lawful judgments. Reporting creates no prior-approval requirement, new testing duty or new offense based on later professional disagreement; the underlying statutory and constitutional standards remain. He preserves concrete unlawful-enforcement challenges without turning a shared outcome into a shared comprehensive test.

### White — Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

White, joined in the common parts by Scalia and Thomas, would reject Roe's fundamental abortion entitlement and sustain the challenged requirements on the grounds they decide. The Constitution's protection of liberty does not, in their view, establish the asserted priority of an elective previability decision over the State's potential-life interest. White's Doe and Thornburgh dissents supply his direct basis; Scalia's Webster and Ohio v. Akron writings supply his. Thomas reaches his conclusion while confronting the contrary adherence principles reflected in McCollum and Quill; adherence depends on the particular constitutional source and consequences of change.

They favor reasoned correction of the constitutional foundation and its trimester restrictions, rather than treating membership as a legal argument. Their Quill positions require attention to the precise precedent and to Congress's different capacity to alter the Commerce rule. They do not deny every form of reliance or every independently protected bodily interest. White's medical-necessity reservation remains, and none decides every hypothetical prohibition affecting life or health. They refuse the common abortion-specific severe-interference tier and Stone's complete obstacle, lesser-burden and proof-allocation proposals.

All three sustain the prospective emergency construction, accurate consent information, physician responsibility, the fixed interval, qualified ancillary notices, optional materials and receipt certification. Riley prevents treating paid professional speech as automatically unprotected; the actual consent connection and absence of ideological assent matter. Their minors analysis accepts the one-parent structure, substitutes, direct bypass and adequate safeguards, including the assumed attendance operation. They support the actual confidential fields and institutional public-release conditions without pretending referral deterrence and harassment concerns are absent or that public funding waives all constitutional rights.

They would sustain spousal notice as a limited information duty supporting marital and prospective-parent participation. They consider its actual exceptions and coercion evidence but disagree that the facial showing requires invalidation. White applies the important-objective and substantial-relationship test drawn from Caban and Michael M. O'Connor joins only that tier and reaches the opposite application. Scalia and Thomas assume the standard without deciding its applicability; their common application does not adopt White's tier choice. They also reject the separately adjudicated marital-privacy grounds, while leaving the independently presented spousal-speech question and its preservation unresolved. Their primary votes sustain notice-linked reports; they do not become conditional invalidation votes merely because the Court removes notice. All three agree that the confidential marital-status field has an independent lawful function. Their dissent does not authorize lower courts to disregard the Court's contrary component holdings.

### Statutory qualifications, with source classifications

The principal statutory source is the [supplied text of the Pennsylvania Abortion Control Act](../briefs/OT_1991CHUNK14_CASEY_STATUTE.md), reproducing the appendix at 505 U.S. 902–911. The supplementary sources are the [District Court opinion, 744 F. Supp. 1323](https://static.case.law/f-supp/744/html/1323-01.html), the [Third Circuit opinion, 947 F.2d 682](https://static.case.law/f2d/947/html/0682-01.html), and [1984 Act 230, §2, P.L. 1211](https://www.palrb.gov/getfile.cfm?file=resources%2Fpreservation-project%2Fpltiftopdf%2F19001999%2F1984%2F0%2Fact%2F0230.pdf&view=true). Statutory commands, judicial constructions, findings and contested inferences remain separate.

- **Medical emergency, §3203.** The condition must, on the physician's good-faith clinical judgment, necessitate immediate abortion to avert death, or make delay create a serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The Third Circuit's construction that compliance must not create a significant threat to life or health, including preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and premature membrane rupture, is a protective judicial construction, not additional statutory language. Section 3205(b) requires the physician to explain the supporting medical indications **before the abortion if possible**; it does not make prior explanation indispensable where the emergency prevents it. Sources: supplied §§3203, 3205(b); Third Circuit emergency analysis.

- **Medical consent and the separate timing and speaker duties, §3205(a)(1).** Voluntary informed consent is the general condition. Except for medical emergency, the performing or referring physician must orally provide, at least 24 hours before the abortion, the nature of the procedure or treatment, risks and alternatives a reasonable patient would consider material, probable gestational age when the procedure will occur, and medical risks of carrying the pregnancy to term. Content, the physician-only delivery requirement and the 24-hour interval are distinct obligations; their constitutional dispositions must not be inferred from one another. Source: supplied §3205(a)(1).

- **Ancillary notices and qualified delegates, §3205(a)(2).** The performing or referring physician, or a qualified physician assistant, health-care practitioner, technician or social worker to whom either physician delegates the responsibility, may give the notices. The statute's separate 24-hour timing and emergency qualification apply. The patient is informed that free state materials describing fetal development and alternatives are available for her review if she chooses; that medical assistance **may** be available for prenatal care, childbirth and neonatal care, with details in the materials; and that the father is liable to assist in support even if he offered to pay for abortion. Liability is not a guarantee of collection. Source: supplied §3205(a)(2).

- **Paternal-support rape permission, §3205(a)(2)(iii).** The statute states: **“In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.”** Omission is permitted, not required. This qualification has **no police-reporting condition** and does not extinguish the support obligation. It is not the reported-spousal-sexual-assault exception to spousal notice. Source: supplied §3205(a)(2)(iii).

- **Materials, certification and speech, §§3205(a)(3)–(4), 3208.** Materials are supplied if the patient chooses to view them; acceptance, reading and ideological agreement are not compelled. Before the abortion, the patient certifies in writing that the information required under paragraphs (1), (2) and (3) has been provided. Section 3208 requires easily comprehensible materials in English, Spanish and Vietnamese within 60 days after the chapter becomes law, annual updating and legible type. The geographically indexed directory includes public and private pregnancy, childbirth, dependent-child and adoption services, their services and contact information, including telephone numbers. The Department may instead supply a toll-free 24-hour number giving that list and service information for the caller's locality. Fetal-development materials describe probable anatomy and physiology from fertilization to full term in two-week increments and relevant survival information; pictures must state fetal dimensions, be realistic and suit the woman's stage of pregnancy. Information must be objective, nonjudgmental and scientifically accurate. The materials include objective information about common abortion methods and risks, possible detrimental psychological effects and childbirth risks, and are available free upon request in appropriate number to any person, facility or hospital. They state that coercing abortion is unlawful; that performing an abortion without informed consent or a private medical consultation may expose the physician to civil damages; that the father remains liable for support even if he offered to pay for abortion; and that adoptive parents may pay prenatal, childbirth and neonatal costs. Those statements and the assistance information do not guarantee financial availability. District findings 181–189 identify directory defects and misinformation by some listed organizations; they do not establish that every pamphlet is false. Sources: supplied §§3205, 3208; district findings 177–189. The supplied appendix's `(e) Free distribution` label is an OCR error; the lower-court statutory account identifies that paragraph as §3208(c).

- **The separate disclosure defense and enforcement, §3205(c).** A physician is not guilty of violating the section for failing to furnish subsection (a) information if the physician establishes by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. This defense concerns omitted information; it is not a general waiver of waiting and cannot substitute for effective emergency treatment. Violations constitute unprofessional conduct subject to license suspension or revocation through the governing procedures. Performing an abortion without the required written certificate, or with knowledge or reason to know that informed consent was not obtained, is a summary offense initially and a third-degree misdemeanor for subsequent offenses. Section 3205(d) protects a complying physician against the specified patient civil-liability claim for failure to obtain informed consent; it is not general immunity from malpractice. Sources: supplied §3205; lower courts' identification of the penalty-and-defense paragraph as subsection (c). The supplied text labels that paragraph `(e)` through OCR; its substance is preserved and the source file is not silently amended.

- **One-parent consent and substitutes, §3206(a)–(b).** The developed challenge concerns a pregnant minor under 18 who is not emancipated. The separate rule for an adult adjudged incompetent under §5511 is statutory background, not a newly developed claim. Except for medical emergency and the section's alternatives, the physician obtains informed consent from both the minor and one parent. A parent or guardian considers only the child's or ward's best interests. If pregnancy results from incest and the father participated in the incestuous act, maternal consent alone supplies the required parental consent; the minor's own informed consent remains required. If both parents are dead or otherwise unavailable to the physician within a reasonable time and manner, guardian consent suffices; after divorce the custodial parent's consent suffices; when neither a parent nor guardian is available within a reasonable time and manner, an adult standing in place of a parent may consent. Source: supplied §3206(a)–(b).

- **Independent bypass and enforcement, §3206(c)–(i).** A minor may choose judicial authorization without first asking a parent or guardian. The proper common pleas court is in the district where she lives or where abortion is sought. Maturity, capacity for informed consent and actual informed consent require authorization. Alternatively, abortion in her best interests requires authorization; maturity and best interests are not cumulative conditions. The court advises her of the right to counsel and provides counsel unless she chooses private counsel or knowingly and intelligently waives representation. A guardian ad litem may assist. Proceedings and records are confidential, her name is excluded from a public docket, and other persons are excluded unless their presence is specifically requested by her or her guardian. The court makes written findings and conclusions and maintains a sealed record. The trial court must rule within **three business days of application**; an appellate court must rule within **five business days of filing the appeal**. These are separate ruling periods, not an invented single eight-calendar-day clock. The statutory text does not expressly require another parental clinic visit: that is the district court's operational finding, and Third Circuit footnote 23 reserves its statutory correctness. The penalty provision combines knowledge, recklessness or negligence regarding covered minor/incompetent status with an intentional, knowing or reckless failure to conform to the section; it specifies unprofessional conduct and at least three months' license suspension through the applicable mechanism. Sources: supplied §3206; the Third Circuit's quoted subsections (f), (h), (i) and footnote 23. Only the source's selected statutory excerpts are used; omitted text is not assumed absent.

- **Spousal notice and substitute statements, §3209.** A married patient supplies a signed statement that she notified her spouse. It need not be notarized but must warn that false statements are punishable. Instead she may sign a statement, also nonnotarized and carrying that warning, certifying at least one condition: her spouse is not the father; diligent effort failed to locate him; pregnancy resulted from spousal sexual assault under §3128 reported to a law-enforcement agency with jurisdiction; or she has reason to believe notification is likely to cause bodily injury to her by the spouse or another individual. Medical emergency independently excuses notification. The statute supplies no general exception for psychological or economic coercion, threatened injury to a child or another person, publicity, divorce or custody retaliation. Sources: supplied §§3203, 3209; district findings 268, 273–308.

- **The incorporated spousal-assault elements and 90-day clock, §3128.** Sexual intercourse or deviate sexual intercourse with a spouse must involve forcible compulsion, a threat of forcible compulsion that would prevent resistance by a person of reasonable resolution, or an unconscious spouse. Under §3128(c), the victim or her agent must personally report the crime to a law-enforcement agency with jurisdiction **within 90 days of the commission of the offense**. The clock begins with the offense, not discovery of pregnancy, a request for abortion or notification. These statutory terms come from **1984 Act 230, §2, P.L. 1211**; district finding 268 also quotes the offense elements. **District finding 297** separately explains that the notice exception excludes coercive sexual behavior other than penetration and that the reporting limit narrows the exception because victims may be psychologically unable to report for years after the incident. The finding is not a verbatim quotation of subsection (c). None of these reporting conditions attaches to the rape-information permission in §3205(a)(2)(iii).

- **Spousal enforcement, §3209(e).** A violation is unprofessional conduct subject to license suspension or revocation through governing procedures. A knowing violation additionally creates civil liability to the spouse who is the father of the aborted child for resulting damages and $5,000 punitive damages; a prevailing plaintiff receives a reasonable attorney fee as costs. These triggers are not interchangeable with §3205(c)'s information defense or culpability rules. Source: supplied §3209(e).

# Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council

## 1. Event

Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council; No. 91-453; October Term 1991. Merits decision, June 29, 1992. Argued March 2, 1992. Certiorari to the Supreme Court of South Carolina, 304 S.C. 376, 404 S.E.2d 895 (1991), which reversed a trial award of $1,232,387.50. The presented question is whether a land-use restriction eliminating all economically beneficial use requires compensation despite its public-harm purpose, including the effect of a new permit avenue and background ownership limits. Lucas seeks reversal of the no-compensation judgment. Independent June 29 decisions share the same entering-law baseline.

Render form: compact. Stone's compatible vote replaces the historical Chief's vote. The record states the rule's specification of Agins candidly without changing the operative rule.

## 3. Public Action

The Court reverses the no-compensation judgment and remands for further proceedings.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy

Reversal and remand, 6–3: Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support; Blackmun and Stevens dissent, and Souter would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted. Souter's position is a refusal to decide this vehicle, not a merits affirmance. The state court must address applicable background property and nuisance law and its remaining unaddressed defenses, including the sustained extent and duration of economic loss. Any compensation covers an actual taking period; neither permanent loss nor the trial award is automatically restored. The post-amendment permit procedure remains available and its effect is reserved.

## 5. Opinion Topology

Scalia delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in its stated entirety by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor and Thomas: five Justices. Kennedy files a separate concurrence in the judgment, without joiners. Blackmun and Stevens each file a dissent, without joiners. Souter files a separate statement favoring dismissal as improvidently granted, without joiners. Kennedy is not counted as a joiner of the categorical rationale; the Court's express five-member opinion controls.

## 6. Holdings

### The original categorical prohibition presents a reviewable earlier-period claim

**Holding and operative rule.** A later discretionary permit procedure does not bar review of compensation for an earlier categorical building prohibition where permission under that prohibition was unavailable and the state supreme court finally rejected the compensation claim on its merits. Any compensable taking must be remedied for its actual duration; the later procedure and unresolved factual issues remain relevant to future use, duration and valuation, and the permanent-value award is not automatically reinstated.

**Authority.** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor and Thomas, controls with five votes. Kennedy separately concurs in the judgment and agrees that the earlier interval is reviewable, but does not join the Court's writing. The holding rests on the express five-Justice opinion without combining separate rationales.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a new opportunity to seek future permission cannot eliminate a completed deprivation from judicial review. Williamson County and MacDonald ordinarily require a final determination of permitted development so that a court knows the restriction it must assess. Here the Council stipulated that the original Act allowed no building permit, and the state supreme court rejected compensation on a categorical merits ground after the amendment existed. First English establishes that ending a restriction does not eliminate compensation for a period in which it effected a taking. The Council's request for further permitting therefore cannot dispose of the earlier interval. Yee preserves the difference between a distinct unpresented claim and an argument supporting the claim actually decided; the earlier deprivation belongs to the compensation dispute already presented. These conclusions do not establish that a taking occurred or fix its value. The Court leaves the new permit avenue, the extent and duration of actual loss, and the defenses the state supreme court did not decide for further proceedings.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Williamson County Regional Planning Commission v. Hamilton Bank, 473 U.S. 172 (1985): preserves ordinary finality requirements; distinguishes an absolute prohibition for an already elapsed period.
- MacDonald, Sommer & Frates v. Yolo County, 477 U.S. 340 (1986): preserves the need to determine permitted development; an unavailable original permit need not be sought to test the earlier prohibition.
- First English Evangelical Lutheran Church v. County of Los Angeles, 482 U.S. 304 (1987): applies compensation for an actual temporary taking; does not decide whether this regulation effected one or set the amount.
- Yee v. City of Escondido, simulated April 1, 1992: applies the distinction between a presented claim and a distinct unpresented taking theory; no new physical-taking rule follows.

### Complete economic deprivation requires compensation unless background ownership law already foreclosed the use

**Holding and operative rule.** A land-use regulation that removes all economically beneficial use of the relevant property effects a taking requiring just compensation unless the prohibited use was already outside the owner's title under background state property or nuisance law. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate an applicable background limitation that would independently prohibit the claimed use in the property's circumstances; a new legislative harm declaration alone does not suffice, and ordinary adjudication supplies the proof requirements without a special heightened quantum.

**Authority.** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor and Thomas, supplies five votes for the complete rule and its explanation. Kennedy supports reversal through an expectations-sensitive analysis, without joining this categorical formulation. No Marks combination is required or used.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that public authority to restrict land and responsibility to pay for its complete economic loss are separate questions. Pennsylvania Coal recognizes that otherwise valid regulation can impose a compensable taking. Agins supplies the complete-use formulation, which the Court now specifies as a categorical rule with an ownership limitation. Penn Central continues to govern ordinary partial burdens. A purpose-only exemption would let the State describe the same sacrifice as preventing harm or conferring a benefit and thereby determine its own compensation duty. Mugler and the older noxious-use cases do not establish that exemption for proved elimination of all beneficial land use. Roth and Scranton explain why the owner's entitlement remains bounded by existing law: prohibiting an entitlement never held takes nothing. The trial finding supplies the premise of this review, but does not settle the Council's preserved factual objections. South Carolina must identify genuine background principles and their application on remand. Changed knowledge may affect nuisance law's application; a new legislative label cannot replace it. The Court decides no parcel-definition rule or percentage shortcut.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Pennsylvania Coal Co. v. Mahon, 260 U.S. 393 (1922): applies its recognition that regulation can go too far despite valid public purposes; does not attribute today's exact categorical rule to that decision.
- Agins v. City of Tiburon, 447 U.S. 255 (1980): specifies and extends its complete-economic-use formulation into the rule and necessary exception stated here; does not adjudicate its distinct legitimate-interest formulation.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104 (1978): preserves contextual analysis for partial deprivation, including economic impact, expectations and the character of governmental action; no percentage automatically defeats or establishes compensation.
- Mugler v. Kansas, 123 U.S. 623 (1887), Hadacheck v. Sebastian, 239 U.S. 394 (1915), Miller v. Schoene, 276 U.S. 272 (1928), and Goldblatt v. Hempstead, 369 U.S. 590 (1962): limits a broad noxious-use reading that would make any legislative harm characterization conclusive despite complete beneficial-use deprivation; preserves their actual outcomes and lawful protective regulation.
- Keystone Bituminous Coal Association v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470 (1987): distinguishes its productive-enterprise and partial-interest setting; no reversal of its actual holding or adoption of a new denominator follows.
- Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): applies existing rules and understandings to identify the protected property interest; does not decide a procedural due-process claim.
- Scranton v. Wheeler, 179 U.S. 141 (1900): applies its antecedent-title-limit principle; a previously existing servitude is distinct from a newly imposed public burden.
- Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp., 458 U.S. 419 (1982), and Kaiser Aetna v. United States, 444 U.S. 164 (1979): preserve physical-occupation and exclusion protections and illuminate the difference between existing limitations and a new imposed servitude; this decision concerns regulatory loss.
- Webb's Fabulous Pharmacies, Inc. v. Beckwith, 449 U.S. 155 (1980): applies the prohibition on converting private property into public property by governmental assertion alone.

**Limits and questions not reached.** The Court assumes the trial court's total-loss finding to decide the presented legal issue; it makes no new appraisal and does not resolve the Council's preserved challenge to that finding. No unconditional entitlement to the trial award, final application of state nuisance law, right to anticipated profits, new parcel rule, or disposition of post-amendment permission is adopted. Legitimate environmental purposes remain sufficient to justify regulation; compensation is a distinct consequence. The Court overrules no earlier decision.

## 7. Precedent Treatment

The treatments in Holdings apply unchanged. The Court specifies Agins and limits broad purpose-only readings of the noxious-use line. Penn Central remains operative for partial burdens and First English for the remedy after an actual taking. No precedent is overruled.

## 8. Law After Decision

Effective June 29, 1992, proved complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use triggers a categorical compensation rule unless background state ownership law already forecloses the use. The State must identify and substantiate that exception under ordinary adjudication. This specifies the earlier complete-use formulation without creating a percentage rule, parcel definition or heightened proof quantum. The original absolute prohibition is reviewable despite later permitting; unresolved facts, duration, valuation and state-law defenses remain for remand. Partial-loss doctrine, public regulatory authority and ordinary finality outside this setting remain intact.

## 9. Separate Writings

Kennedy agrees that the earlier interval is reviewable and reversal appropriate, but would test complete-loss restrictions against objectively reasonable investment-backed expectations drawn from the whole legal tradition. Common-law nuisance alone is too narrow, particularly for vulnerable coastal land; actual ability and intent to develop and the duration of loss remain for remand. Blackmun disputes finality and the unsupported premise of complete economic extinction, and rejects the categorical rule in favor of the protective public purpose and contextual precedent. Stevens would withhold premature constitutional adjudication and, on the merits, sustain the general coastal measure under Penn Central and Keystone; he questions the zero-value boundary and a rigid nuisance exception. Souter would dismiss because the unreviewable and questionable total-loss premise prevents sound resolution of both the category and its exceptions. Each position is noncontrolling.

No final state-law liability, new appraisal, compensation amount, permit grant or later lower-court event is established. This Supreme Court merits event is complete; further proceedings occur on remand.

# Espinosa v. Florida

## 1. Event

Espinosa v. Florida; No. 91-7390; October Term 1991. Summary merits action, June 29, 1992, on petition for certiorari to the Supreme Court of Florida, 589 So.2d 887 (1991), affirming convictions and a death sentence. No oral argument. The Court takes the preserved question whether a vague heinous, atrocious or cruel aggravator can infect a sentence through a jury recommendation entitled to great weight despite the judge's direct use of valid aggravators. The petition seeks relief from conviction and sentence on additional grounds, but the present grant and decision are limited to this sentencing issue. The grant and contemporaneous summary merits disposition form one action.

Render form: full. Stone joins the summary reversal rather than the historical Chief's preference for argument, changing the published supporting coalition to seven. The operative remand expressly requires correction of the recommendation's influence by lawful reweighing or Chapman review and, absent lawful cure, new sentencing; the comparator issued an unelaborated remand for proceedings consistent with its indirect-weighing opinion. The substantive defect is the same, but the specified cure instruction and coalition are material.

## 2. Participation

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participate. Nine sit, satisfying the six-Justice quorum; five votes suffice for judgment. No nonparticipation is established. This is an unargued petition and summary merits action.

## 3. Public Action

The Court grants leave to proceed in forma pauperis and certiorari limited to the preserved aggravating-instruction question, reverses the rejection of the sentencing claim, and remands.

## 4. Judgment & Remedy

Summary reversal as to the death-sentencing claim and remand, 7–2: Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support. White dissents from summary disposition and would grant certiorari and set argument; Scalia dissents and would deny certiorari. White supplies no present merits vote. The guilt judgment remains outside this disposition. Further proceedings must supply lawful reweighing or Chapman harmless-error review addressing the recommendation's influence; if no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required. No automatic life sentence follows.

## 5. Opinion Topology

The per curiam Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, seven Justices, for the defect and cure instructions. No individual majority author is identified. White files a noted procedural dissent requesting grant and oral argument, without joiners. Scalia files a substantive dissent and would deny the petition, without joiners. No concurrence or additional join is supplied.

## 6. Holdings

### A legally influential recommendation cannot rest on an inadequately narrowed aggravator

**Holding and operative rule.** When a weighing State requires its sentencing judge to give great weight to a jury's recommendation, an inadequately narrowed aggravating instruction to that jury can infect the final sentence even if the judge directly finds only valid aggravators. The especially wicked, evil, atrocious or cruel instruction given here lacks the guidance the Eighth Amendment requires; the judge's separate findings do not by themselves cure the recommendation's legal influence.

**Authority.** The per curiam Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, seven Justices, for this rule and its application. White's preference for argument is not a present merits vote. Scalia rejects the weighing premise in dissent. An express majority supplies authority without any Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that constitutional guidance must follow the actual allocation of sentencing responsibility. Godfrey, Maynard and Shell require an aggravating category to distinguish the cases to which it applies; the ordinary words given this jury supply no adequate boundary. Stringer and Sochor distinguish eligibility supported by valid factors from the later weighing that selects death. Florida law makes the jury's general recommendation consequential by requiring the judge to give it great weight, whether it recommends life or death. Mills does not permit an assumption that jurors ignored an authorized invalid category, and Walton's presumption that a judge follows the law means the recommendation received the weight state law required. The judge thus cannot be treated as wholly independent of the defective recommendation. Baldwin permits a different allocation of sentencing roles; it does not eliminate an actual duty of deference. The Court does not find that particular jurors selected particular aggravators. It decides the legal effect of the unguided recommendation and preserves the State's ability to structure a constitutionally guided sentencing process.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 (1980), Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 (1988), and Shell v. Mississippi, 498 U.S. 1 (1990): apply their requirement of meaningful guidance for a capital aggravating category; the instruction here is insufficiently narrowed.
- Stringer v. Black, simulated March 9, 1992: applies the distinction between valid eligibility and constitutionally defective weighing; surviving valid aggravators alone do not remove the error.
- Sochor v. Florida, simulated June 8, 1992: applies its weighing-error rule while distinguishing its independent state preservation bar to a different jury claim and its separate factual-invalidity issue.
- Mills v. Maryland, 486 U.S. 367 (1988): applies the refusal to presume that jurors disregard an instruction's constitutionally defective route; no factor-specific verdict is invented.
- Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639 (1990): preserves genuine judicial sentencing and the presumption that judges apply legal narrowing; distinguishes a system in which a separate unguided recommendation carries required weight.
- Baldwin v. Alabama, 472 U.S. 372 (1985), and Spaziano v. Florida, 468 U.S. 447 (1984): preserve States' latitude in allocating sentencing responsibility; that latitude does not validate influential unguided weighing.
- Tedder v. State, 322 So.2d 908 (Fla. 1975), Smith v. State, 515 So.2d 182 (Fla. 1987), and Grossman v. State, 525 So.2d 833 (Fla. 1988): supply the state-law requirement to give great weight to jury recommendations of life or death.
- Smalley v. State, 546 So.2d 720 (Fla. 1989): rejects its premise that the jury's advisory designation alone makes this vague instruction constitutionally immaterial.

### Correction must address the recommendation's influence on the death sentence

**Holding and operative rule.** Reversal of the rejection of this sentencing claim requires further proceedings that remove the influence of the invalid jury recommendation through lawful reweighing or harmless-error review satisfying Chapman beyond a reasonable doubt. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required; valid remaining aggravators alone do not establish a cure, and neither an automatic life sentence nor disturbance of the guilt judgment follows.

**Authority.** The same seven Justices join the per curiam opinion's remedy instruction. They expressly apply the existing Stringer, Sochor, Clemons and Chapman correction requirements to the two-stage defect found here. No agreement is attributed to White on this unargued merits question or to Scalia on the need for correction.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that correction must reach the error actually found. Clemons permits an authorized reviewing court to reweigh valid aggravation against mitigation or determine that an invalid factor was harmless; it does not treat a surviving majority of factors as an answer. Chapman supplies the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt harmlessness standard. Stringer and Sochor require an actual lawful cure of invalid weighing, and Parker requires attention to the sentencing record that genuinely existed. Here the jury's general recommendation carried legally required weight into the judge's decision. The judge's four valid aggravators and two mitigators therefore do not establish that this indirect error had no effect. Further review must account for that channel of influence, rather than repeat the premise that the judge alone imposed sentence. The Court does not undertake its own balancing, choose a sentence, or decide that Florida law authorizes every proposed appellate cure. It leaves those proceedings to the state court under the stated constitutional limits. The conviction and unrelated petition questions are outside this disposition.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 (1990): applies lawful appellate reweighing or harmless-error correction; merely counting valid aggravators does not cure the error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applies harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt; no lesser standard or burden on the defendant is created.
- Stringer v. Black, simulated March 9, 1992, and Sochor v. Florida, simulated June 8, 1992: apply actual cure requirements to the recommendation's transmitted influence; no unreasoned harmlessness label suffices.
- Parker v. Dugger, 498 U.S. 308 (1991): applies review grounded in the real sentencing record and mitigation, not an assumed alternative record.

**Limits and questions not reached.** No automatic reduction to life, retrial of guilt, adjudication of unrelated aggravators, factor-by-factor jury finding, or collateral-review gateway rule follows. The petition's severance and mitigation/time/funding/proportionality questions are not granted or adjudicated in this limited summary action; no separate denial of them is invented. No prior Supreme Court decision is overruled.

## 7. Precedent Treatment

The treatments in Holdings apply unchanged. The Court applies the received anti-vagueness and weighing rules to the influence of a required jury recommendation, distinguishes genuine independent judicial sentencing, and makes the existing correction requirements explicit on remand. No prior Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

## 8. Law After Decision

Effective June 29, 1992, a jury recommendation that receives great legal weight is part of the constitutionally relevant weighing process. Its unguided aggravating category can contaminate the final sentence through that required influence even when the judge directly uses valid factors. Ordinary Stringer/Sochor correction must address this indirect route: lawful reweighing or Chapman review, and a new proceeding if no lawful cure exists. States retain latitude in assigning sentencing roles; no automatic life sentence, collateral-review rule or factor-specific jury presumption is created.

That substantive view has one vote and does not control.

## 1. Event

## 6. Holdings

### A substantial procedural challenge does not alone establish entitlement to mandatory return

**Holding and operative rule.** An applicant seeking to vacate an appellate stay of an order granting effectively final individual relief must show a substantial likelihood of entitlement to that ordered relief and a demonstrable error in the appellate stay. Where the asserted procedural defect would restore agency discretion rather than compel the requested substantive outcome, a strong procedural claim and serious irreparable injury do not alone establish entitlement to immediate judicially ordered return of the drug.

**Authority.** The per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: seven Justices. The Court assumes the procedural challenge's strength for this remedial inquiry; it does not adopt Stone-Zsela's separate assessment that the then-operative FDA procedural regulation was likely violated.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the requested remedy requires a legal connection the applicants have not sufficiently shown. Western Airlines supplies the demanding review of an appellate stay; the Court must assess entitlement to the relief that would follow vacatur. Benten faces a serious imminent loss of her preferred noninvasive treatment, and the record supplies no ground to invent a health danger or an alternative placement. But the District Court acknowledged that the personal-use policy permits admission rather than compels it. Even assuming a strong challenge to automatic detention, removal of that policy restores a decision assigned to the agency. Ford Motor recognizes flexibility to make lawful agency proceedings effective; it does not clearly authorize a court to select the remaining discretionary result. Section 706(1) likewise permits relief requiring lawful action without automatically deciding its substance. Urgency and the individualized medical findings deserve weight, but do not close that remedial gap. The Court leaves the procedural merits for the proper proceedings and expresses no view on the unpresented constitutional access claim.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Western Airlines, Inc. v. Teamsters, 480 U.S. 1301 (1987) (in chambers): applied for the demanding standard governing intervention in an appellate stay; urgent injury does not eliminate the required merits-and-remedy showing.
- Ford Motor Co. v. NLRB, 305 U.S. 364 (1939): its flexibility to make lawful administrative action effective is preserved; it does not clearly establish entitlement to judicial selection of the discretionary admission decision on this application.

## 9. Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela concurs in the Court's remedy-based denial. He regards the imminent injury as serious and would excuse a judicial exhaustion requirement sufficiently to examine the procedural claim. Automatic detention removed prior field discretion, and the version of §10.40(d) in force in 1989 required additional process even if the change were interpretive or procedural. He finds no qualifying published notice or explained good-cause invocation in this record. Accardi and Nixon require an agency to follow its binding rules; Vermont Yankee limits judicial invention of extra procedures but does not erase procedures the agency adopted. This substantial challenge cannot be answered by citing the narrower rule effective in 1991. He nevertheless finds insufficient authority to convert restored discretion into mandatory admission, reserves final procedural merits and §10.70, accepts the individual medical findings, and decides no constitutional claim.

Blackmun would grant the application; no further public reason is supplied. Stevens would grant because, on this individual record, the asserted federal interest does not justify the burden on Benten's protected choice and her noninvasive method. He regards the absence of a demonstrated significant health risk under the personal-use policy as central. His constitutional analysis is a dissent, not a determination of the claim by the Court.
