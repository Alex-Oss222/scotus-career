## Unresolved discrepancies

### 1. Casey's current workspace still states incompatible governing law

The [Casey record](../records/Planned_Parenthood_of_Southeastern_Pennsylvania_v_Casey_merits_1992-06-29.md), lines 220–310, supplies the complete five-Justice standard and its conditional proof allocation. Lines 344–352 establish five votes for the integrated timetable replacement and nine votes for rejecting the old timetable in result. Those provisions are also correctly reflected in portions of the [workspace](../workspace.md), including lines 4013–4019 and 4438.

Other portions of the current workspace contradict them:

| Current workspace location | Remaining conflict |
|---|---|
| Lines 3959 and 3969, governing-standard authority and explanation | Still describe six votes for the timetable result and say Blackmun and Souter join the framework without joining that result. The current record gives all nine the timetable result and identifies the five-Justice integrated replacement rationale. |
| Line 3967, governing-standard explanation | Still presents Stone's automatic-obstacle and lesser-burden rules as reserved proposals, O'Connor as declining them, and the shared minimum as noncomprehensive. These descriptions conflict with the complete framework adopted by the five named Justices. |
| Lines 3971 and 4440, current explanation and limits | Deny a universal lesser-burden State allocation, including after meaningful interference, without preserving the framework's actual conditional allocation. The record places justification and fit on the State after challengers establish the duty, affected population, causal connection and triggering interference; workspace line 3963 already acknowledges that conditional allocation in compressed form. A limitation on an automatic burden shift for every regulation must not negate that established allocation. |
| Lines 5283 and 5285, Current-Term Neutral Projection | Line 5283 expressly denies that a complete replacement formula controls; describes Stone's substitute as separately adopted and unjoined by the other timing voters; and repeats the six-person timing result. Line 5285 repeats the denial of a universal lesser-burden State allocation. These are live current-law instructions, not historical ledger receipts. |
| Lines 3969 and 5273/5281 | The current-law explanation retains the private user-direction/assembly account, while the Neutral Projection imports the Note's current-law sections as its public baseline. That process explanation belongs in the audit/provenance material, not in the neutral account of the Court's adopted rationale. |

The resynchronization receipt at workspace line 312 says these classes of problem were corrected. The remaining passages show that synchronization is incomplete. The general statement that the canonical record controls does not make conflicting current-law instructions accurate.

**Remaining qualification omissions.** The rewritten standard paragraph at workspace line 3963 restores much of the sequence but does not supply all its adopted qualifications:

- Record lines 274–276 allow the State to answer an identified alternative by establishing that it is inadequate, unavailable or carries material countervailing consequences; permit reliable experience and reasonable predictions without prescribed findings, a study in every case, scientific certainty or numerical benefit calculations; and bar judicial policy substitution after justification and fit are established. The current-law standard says only that alternatives must be answered. Some separate-writing summaries mention selected limitations, but those summaries do not supply the complete controlling instruction.
- Record line 286 requires evaluation of medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, patient circumstances and substantial contrary evidence; gives serious weight to substantial professional agreement; and denies conclusive force to either a professional association, a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. The controlling workspace standard omits that evidentiary instruction.
- Record lines 298–311 qualify Ohio v. Akron's facial-review formulation both at the general Step 6 scope and in the distinct spousal-notice holding. The workspace preserves the important indivisible-condition remedy, but its general framework precedent-treatment list at lines 3975–3981 omits that treatment. The spousal-notice-specific account does not supply the broader adopted treatment.

**Required resolution:** Reconcile all live Casey current-law and neutral-projection passages to the existing version 1.5 holdings, authority and conditions; retain earlier descriptions only as clearly superseded history. This finding does not reopen the authorized framework, require new joins, or establish that a later decision must change.

### 2. Casey's record gives an overbroad continuing-control statement to superseded material

In the [Casey record](../records/Planned_Parenthood_of_Southeastern_Pennsylvania_v_Casey_merits_1992-06-29.md), line 50 limits supersession of the earlier Stone-control section to the single question of who joins the framework, then says every other statement in that section remains accurate and controls.

That declaration is too broad. Line 42 still describes Souter reaching a different disposition from Stone on the newly invalidated requirements and refers to the former four-Justice grounds/no-established-visit concurrence. Line 44 preserves O'Connor's authorship on components where Stone dissents. The version 1.5 assembly at lines 70–74 and the current judgment, topology and holdings instead establish the changed five-Justice invalidation coalitions and their current authorship; the parental-attendance holding at lines 593–601 now identifies Stone's five-Justice no-established-command rationale.

Line 72 likewise describes the superseded old tables as limited to framework adoption and its four newly invalidated applications, without identifying the separate change to the parental-attendance rationale. The result denying special attendance relief remains unchanged, but its controlling rationale and joins are different; the supersession account must preserve that distinction.

The explicitly labeled version 1.4 assembly and the preserved earlier annex may remain as history. The defect is the current declaration that the earlier section, apart from only one question, still controls.

**Required resolution:** Accurately delimit supersession and distinguish the preserved earlier control account from operative version 1.5 judgments, joins and authorship. No change to the authorized current adjudication is indicated.

### 3. Gizoni's Standards entry converts a reserved question into an operative proposition

The [Holdings candidate](HOLDINGS.candidate.md), line 5531, likewise reserves summary judgment on a conclusive record. The [Render Input](../render-inputs/OT_1991CHUNK2.md), line 89, and [public render](../output/OT_1991CHUNK2.md), line 86, retain that boundary.

The [Standards candidate](STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.candidate.md), line 1125, instead adds: “A conclusive record may still support summary judgment.” Its authority mapping supplies Gizoni's coverage/nonpreclusion holding, not a separate admitted source deciding the reserved issue; its open-question field at line 1133 mentions only final award and settlement.

**Required resolution:** Preserve the record's conclusive-record reservation. Close may not supply an affirmative reusable instruction merely because it may be compatible with received law. This is a candidate projection issue, not a reason to change the existing judgment or crew-status rule.

### 4. Lujan's included standing instruction omits elements it claims to preserve

The [Standards candidate](STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.candidate.md), lines 162–175, includes a rule requiring evidence of challenged standing elements and maps the existing injury, traceability and redressability elements to Lujan Part II. Yet its current rule at line 166 does not state the causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, or the likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. No other current Standards entry supplies those elements.

The [Lujan record](../records/Lujan_v_Defenders_of_Wildlife_merits_1992-06-12.md), lines 58–62, expressly supplies both requirements and their five-Justice authority. The [Holdings candidate](HOLDINGS.candidate.md), lines 1925–1935, preserves them and correctly distinguishes the controlling general redressability element from the noncontrolling application to absent funding agencies. Standards' reservation concerning that absent-agency application does not supply the omitted general instruction.

**Required resolution:** Complete the elements already invoked in this entry, preserving the record's distinction between the general rule and the unresolved/noncontrolling application. This finding does not require separate entries for every unchanged standing application or derive Standards substance from the Holdings candidate.

### 5. Casey's Standards reporting instruction leaves accepted confidentiality conditions unstated

The [Standards candidate](STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.candidate.md), line 911, permits specified confidential medical-basis reports and says that individual reports omit patient names and retain the confidentiality safeguards accepted in the decision. It does not state the remaining safeguards on which that permission rests.

The [Casey record](../records/Planned_Parenthood_of_Southeastern_Pennsylvania_v_Casey_merits_1992-06-29.md), line 978, supplies them: individual reports are not public records; law-enforcement access requires a common pleas court order following an application showing good cause; and published statistics may not identify a physician, facility or patient. The record expressly identifies these as the statutory safeguards described in district finding 357 whose adequacy is accepted in this litigation. The [Holdings candidate](HOLDINGS.candidate.md), lines 3502, 3527 and 3552, states these conditions. No other current Standards entry supplies them.

**Required resolution:** State the accepted confidentiality conditions in the included reporting instruction. Preserve their source and scope: this does not declare every safeguard a constitutionally indispensable minimum everywhere, or convert a lower-court finding into new statutory text. An unnamed reference to accepted safeguards does not satisfy the completeness rule.

The current annex still identifies no such audit's result or source location, its authorized input boundary, or provenance confirming that review. The Stone-exposed hand-correction narrative cannot by itself demonstrate that this distinct required check was completed.

**Required resolution:** Supply the identifiable result and provenance if that review occurred, or complete and document the required review through the authorized correction process. This is a validation-evidence finding. It does not assert that the review never occurred or that the final votes, remand or controlling rule must change.

### 7. Zatko retains a docket-count error and a minor public-boundary mismatch

The [workspace](../workspace.md), line 5293, refers to “seventeen Zatko petitioners.” The [Zatko record](../records/Zatko_v_California_fee_motions_1991-11-04.md), lines 4–6, 24 and 32, establishes seventeen motions/dockets and distinguishes their applicants; its filing histories concern Zatko and Martin. It does not establish seventeen distinct people. The record's carry-forward paragraph has already been corrected to refer to the seventeen dockets' applicants.

**Required resolution:** Use the supported docket/submission count in the remaining Neutral Projection sentence. The open condition is otherwise carried correctly; no compliance outcome or later petition disposition is inferred.

Separately, the [public render](../output/OT_1991CHUNK1.md) heads the entry at line 128 as Zatko v. California **and companion matters**, but its closing line at 146 says **and grouped matters**. Render Contract §3 requires the closing line to repeat the displayed case or matter name. This is a minor presentation discrepancy only; all seventeen dockets and the disposition are present. The closing caption should match the displayed caption without altering the decision.
