- **R.L.C. threshold:** The record expressly assigns the live-controversy determination to Souter's Court opinion, Parts I, II-A and III, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Its precedent-treatment section applies Villamonte-Marquez to the possibility of restored detention. - **Fractured decisions:** Burson's seven-Justice speech-scrutiny proposition remains distinct from its five-vote judgment without a controlling validity rationale. Burke supplies only the recorded fractured judgment. Thompson/Center's expressly shared propositions are actual majority agreement across writings, not a new Marks synthesis. Williams's grand-jury disclosure duty and sufficient dismissal ground retain their different qualifications and recorded Marks authority.
- **Independent alternatives:** The pass preserves Amtrak's two independent failures to rebut presumed need, Alaska's published-criterion and actual-notice grounds under §552, and Morales's direct-reference and substantial-effect preemption grounds. Each alternative has its own authority and current-force field. Other independently sufficient source grounds that answer distinct questions, including Foucha's component grounds, remain separate complete question blocks.
- **Robertson source limit:** The record identifies the §318(b)(3) and (5) replacement provisions, fiscal-year 1990 and covered contracts, but does not supply all operative restrictions or precise geographic and contract boundaries. The candidate identifies the affected statutory requirements using the record's names—MBTA, NEPA, NFMA, FLPMA and OCLA—within the stated timber litigation. It admits the provision-specific Article III holding and creates no independently usable timber-compliance rule. Stating those missing restrictions as law would require the official §318 text and an authorized source correction; close does not reconstruct them.
- **Unresolved merits remain unresolved:** Suter's §671(a)(16) claim, Montana's Count II, and the regulatory-taking merits in Yee remain undecided at their recorded scope. Remand instructions do not establish rights or liabilities on those questions. The narrow Gomez and Coleman stay holdings do not decide ultimate constitutional-method or innocence claims.
