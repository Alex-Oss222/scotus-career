## Constitutional Structure

### Congressional amendment of law governing pending litigation

#### Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1596.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

##### Whether §318(b)(6)(A) changes governing timber law or directs results under unchanged law

**Holding:** Section 318(b)(6)(A) substitutes compliance with §318(b)(3) and (b)(5) for satisfaction of the requirements under MBTA, NEPA, NFMA, FLPMA and OCLA identified in the pending timber disputes, while leaving actual compliance for courts to decide. That express substantive amendment is effective although enacted in an appropriations statute and does not violate Article III on the ground decided below.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's unanimous opinion of the Court, supported by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine Justices adopt the changed-law holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the Northwest Timber Compromise’s covered sales and fiscal-year 1990 framework, with continued application to covered sales contracts; it does not find any sale compliant. The sixteen enjoined Forest Service sales sustain the controversy; the apparently moot BLM branch supplies no separate merits disposition. Klein’s full reach, the unraised challenge to narrowly targeted legislation, and final-judgment reopening remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and remanded for judicial application of the new statutory criteria to the still-live sales. No timber sale is automatically approved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Klein, 13 Wall. 128 — distinguished on the ground decided below; its full constitutional reach is not resolved.
- Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 18 How. 421 — consistent with adjudication under changed law; no broad targeted-legislation holding is drawn from it.
- Simpson v. United States, 435 U.S. 6 — applied: specific provisions qualify general ones.
- United States v. Will, 449 U.S. 200 — applied: clear substantive amendments may appear in appropriations legislation.
- TVA v. Hill, 437 U.S. 153 — distinguished: the amendment here is express rather than an inferred repeal.
- NLRB v. Jones & Laughlin Steel Corp., 301 U.S. 1 — preserved: a possible constitutionally valid construction reinforces, but does not replace, the enacted meaning.

### Federal and state submerged-land boundaries

#### United States v. Alaska, 503 U.S. 569 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 118, Original.
**Decided:** April 21, 1992.

For the other questions in *United States v. Alaska*, April 21, 1992, No. 118, Original, see Federal Courts — Standing and original jurisdiction (live title controversy), Constitutional Structure — Federal and state submerged-land boundaries (§10 condition), and Administrative Law — Coastal permits and Administrative Procedure Act review (regulatory and APA objections).

##### Whether §10 permits the Nome condition preserving existing submerged-land boundaries

**Holding:** Section 10 of the Rivers and Harbors Act permits the Corps to consider the effect of this proposed artificial coastal extension on federal submerged-land rights and to condition approval on preserving the existing federal-state boundary. The Submerged Lands Act does not require unconditional approval of the extension or invalidate this condition, which concerns only additional entitlement attributable to the Nome project and leaves existing state property and claims intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, Part II, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; all nine Justices support this statutory holding and its stated limits.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The condition addresses the same prospective boundary encroachment that supports withholding permission and preserves the existing allocation while allowing construction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Natural accretion, different permit conditions, compulsory surrender of existing state territory, general Chevron validity and States’ constitutional takings remedies remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Federal summary judgment is granted and Alaska’s competing motion and general injunction request are denied. The declaration enforces the disclaimer only as to boundary change and corresponding entitlement attributable to the permitted Nome extension. Existing state property, claims and disputes over the precise preproject line remain intact; the permit and construction are not vacated. No remand, monetary award, nationwide permitting injunction or surrender of unrelated lands is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. California, 381 U.S. 139 (1965) — applied: the artificial-coast rule and the qualification permitting protection against future encroachment through agreement remain effective together; the decision imposes no absolute domestic/international boundary identity.
- United States ex rel. Greathouse v. Dern, 289 U.S. 352 (1933) — limited supporting analogy only: its refusal of equitable mandamus did not decide the reserved general extent of §10 discretion.
- Nollan v. California Coastal Commission, 483 U.S. 825 (1987) — distinguished: this condition directly addresses the identified purpose supporting denial and does not exact an unrelated existing property interest; no general holding about a State's constitutional takings remedies follows.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984) — applied within the actual statutory delegation and controlling boundary precedent; its general validity and scope are not reconsidered.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992 — preserved: agency construction does not displace a binding judicial construction.
- Arkansas v. Oklahoma, February 26, 1992 — preserved: agency permit conditions must rest on the authority and regulations governing that particular scheme; its Clean Water Act holding supplies no independent §10 power.

### Federal preemption

#### Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc., 504 U.S. 374 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1604.
**Decided:** June 1, 1992.

For the threatened-enforcement and injunction-scope holdings in *Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc.*, June 1, 1992, No. 90-1604, see Federal Courts — Prospective equitable relief.

##### Whether the Airline Deregulation Act preempts the specified state-enforced fare-advertising requirements despite their consumer-protection form and the general savings clause

**Holding:** Section 1305(a)(1) preempts state enforcement having a connection with or reference to covered air-carrier rates, routes or services, even through a generally applicable law; the general remedies clause does not preserve enforcement within that specific prohibition. The specified fare-advertising guidelines are preempted because they directly reference fares and impose binding requirements on how tickets at those fares may be marketed; tenuous, remote or peripheral connections remain outside the ruling's resolved boundary. Directly covered requirements need not conflict with an existing federal rule to be displaced.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five of eight participants independently sustain the statutory judgment on the direct-reference ground. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining the Court's construction. Souter does not participate.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the express aviation provision and the identified fare requirements.

**Material application:** The guidelines require disclosures about restrictions and availability, regulate surcharges and round-trip prices, and restrict discount descriptions. They bind the terms on which stated-price tickets may be marketed. Section 1506 preserves remedies outside the express prohibition; treating the requirements as applications of existing consumer law does not remove their direct relation to fares.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not draw the boundary for nonprice advertising, remote effects or materially different state duties. It creates no general airline immunity, complete displacement of ordinary state remedies, damages right or field-preemption rule based solely on federal unfair-practices authority under §1381. Constitutional speech questions and unthreatened frequent-flyer, boarding, fraud, contractual and other injury claims are not adjudicated. Stone's separate reservations do not narrow the five-Justice construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm declaratory and injunctive protection against enforcement of the specified fare requirements. The removal of excess protection for unthreatened practices is stated under Federal Courts — Prospective equitable relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Shaw v. Delta Air Lines, Inc.*, 463 U.S. 85, supplies by analogy the connection-or-reference construction and remote-effects reservation, without importing ERISA's entire scheme. *Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Massachusetts*, 471 U.S. 724, supplies text, intent and whole-statute construction. *Crawford Fitting Co. v. J. T. Gibbons, Inc.*, 482 U.S. 437, and *International Paper Co. v. Ouellette*, 479 U.S. 481, establish that a general savings clause does not undo a specific federal restriction. *Nader v. Allegheny Airlines, Inc.*, 426 U.S. 290, construed the earlier aviation regime and does not override the 1978 express clause.

**Independent alternative holding:** Independently of express reference to fares, the identified compelled disclosures and advertising restrictions fall within §1305(a)(1) because their substantial effect on marketing restricted fares significantly affects the fares themselves. This ground concerns this fare-marketing package and does not make every business cost or indirect effect sufficient for preemption.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Scalia's opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; the same five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground. Stone joins neither this ground nor a freestanding economic-effects threshold.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains controlling at the scope of the identified fare-marketing package.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The package burdens short advertisements through extensive disclosures, requires market-specific surcharge presentations and constrains discount and availability advertising. *Bates v. State Bar of Arizona*, 433 U.S. 350, supplies the relationship between price information and competitive prices without deciding a speech claim; *Shaw* preserves remote-effects limits. Federal unfair-practices authority remains available but is not alone the reason these state requirements are displaced. This ground independently sustains the same retained declaratory and injunctive protection.

### Interstate commerce

#### Wyoming v. Oklahoma, 502 U.S. 437 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 112, Original.
**Decided:** January 22, 1992.

For the separate questions concerning Wyoming's Article III injury and exercise of original jurisdiction, see *Wyoming v. Oklahoma*, January 22, 1992, No. 112, Original, under Federal Courts — Standing and original jurisdiction.

##### Whether Oklahoma's ten-percent in-state-coal requirement is lawful discrimination against interstate commerce

**Holding:** Oklahoma's express in-state-coal quota discriminates against interstate commerce and cannot stand unless the State establishes a legitimate local purpose that cannot adequately be served by reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives; it has not done so here. The Federal Power Act's general preservation of state utility regulation does not clearly authorize this geographic discrimination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices support this proposition. Scalia and Thomas cast no merits vote.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The Act requires covered public and private coal-fired utilities to obtain at least ten percent of coal heat input from Oklahoma coal. The small reserved share does not cure facial origin discrimination. The record does not show that an origin quota is necessary to secure reliable energy supplies when reasonable neutral diversification measures are available; retaining benefits for the local coal industry is not a sufficient justification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neutral energy regulation remains available; the evenhanded-regulation balance does not govern this express origin quota.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Summary judgment is entered for Wyoming, Oklahoma's exceptions are overruled, and the Act is declared unconstitutional. From January 22, 1992, Oklahoma and its officials are enjoined from enforcing the ten-percent requirement against covered utilities. No tax damages are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Philadelphia v. New Jersey*, 437 U.S. 617 — applied to invalidate facial geographic discrimination regardless of the favored market share's size.
- *Hughes v. Oklahoma*, 441 U.S. 322 — applied for legitimate purpose and the absence of reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives.
- *New Energy Co. of Indiana v. Limbach*, 486 U.S. 269, and *Bacchus Imports, Ltd. v. Dias*, 468 U.S. 263 — local commercial advantage does not justify discrimination.
- *New England Power Co. v. New Hampshire*, 455 U.S. 331 — general federal permission to regulate does not clearly authorize discriminatory restrictions.
- *Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc.*, 397 U.S. 137 — its evenhanded-regulation balance is not the governing branch for this express origin quota.
- *Maine v. Taylor*, 477 U.S. 131 — a proved need without reasonable neutral alternatives may justify discrimination; that showing is absent here.

##### Whether the integrated Act can be severed to retain only its Grand River Dam Authority application

**Holding:** The Act's integrated purchasing mandate cannot be preserved solely for the state-owned Grand River Dam Authority without an adequate state-law basis for severing applications and retaining that different legislative scheme. The Court invalidates the enacted regulatory program while leaving the validity of an independently adopted state procurement policy open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices support this proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The Act's severability language concerning parts does not adequately establish legislative intent to remove all private applications and preserve a different GRDA-only scheme. State ownership of one regulated utility does not establish that an independent procurement policy was enacted or would be retained alone.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The market-participant doctrine is preserved. The validity of a separately adopted state procurement preference is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Wyoming's severability exception is sustained. The injunction covers the enacted quota as a whole, including the proposed GRDA-only application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Reeves, Inc. v. Stake*, 447 U.S. 429, and *Hughes v. Alexandria Scrap Corp.*, 426 U.S. 794 — the market-participant doctrine is preserved; no independent procurement policy is adjudicated.
- *South-Central Timber Development, Inc. v. Wunnicke*, 467 U.S. 82 — distinguishes market participation from regulatory restrictions beyond the relevant participation.
- *Dorchy v. Kansas*, 264 U.S. 286, and *Champlin Refining Co. v. Corporation Commission*, 286 U.S. 210 — severability depends on legislative intent and a workable retained enactment, not judicial preference for a substitute policy.

#### Chemical Waste Management, Inc. v. Hunt, 504 U.S. 334 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-471.
**Decided:** June 1, 1992.

##### Whether Alabama may impose an additional disposal charge on equivalent hazardous waste because it originated outside the State

**Holding:** A State may not impose an additional disposal charge on out-of-state hazardous waste that it does not impose on equivalent in-state waste unless it establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve. Alabama's $72-per-ton additional charge fails because the record supplies no origin-linked difference in the relevant risks or costs and adequate evenhanded means remain available; commercial hazardous-waste disposal receives Commerce Clause protection despite the material's dangers.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine adopt the rule, application and remedial limits. Stone's additional compensatory-tax observation is not part of the Court's holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The accepted long-term disposal, transportation, monitoring and capacity concerns do not establish that an equivalent imported ton is more dangerous or costly than a domestic ton. Evenhanded charges, total-volume limits and direct safety regulation address those mechanisms; these examples expose the missing justification rather than require a replacement program. Allowing the same waste upon payment of an extra fee does not establish a quarantine justification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The grant concerns the additional fee alone. The base fee, independent volume cap, other permitting provisions, state constitutional claims and other federal challenges are not adjudicated. The State did not press its fair-share or compensatory-tax theory; amici's congressional-authorization theory was neither decided below nor briefed or argued by the parties. No unlimited right to dispose of hazardous waste, new proof quantum, perfect-certainty requirement or least-imaginable-alternative rule is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Alabama Supreme Court's judgment sustaining the additional fee and remand for appropriate state relief from the discriminatory exaction, consistently with *McKesson* where its coerced-payment and postpayment-review prerequisites apply. Neither those prerequisites nor an automatic refund entitlement or amount is determined. The independent base-fee, cap and other rulings outside the grant remain undisturbed; no replacement fee, operating permit or compulsory acceptance requirement issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Philadelphia v. New Jersey*, 437 U.S. 617, applies Commerce protection and nondiscrimination to hazardous-waste transactions. *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, 432 U.S. 333, preserves the State's purpose-and-alternatives burden. *Maine v. Taylor*, 477 U.S. 131, is distinguished because this record lacks distinctive imported risks and unavailable adequate alternatives. *New Energy Co. of Indiana v. Limbach*, 486 U.S. 269, applies to discriminatory charges defended on environmental grounds; complete exclusion is unnecessary. *Wyoming v. Oklahoma*, January 22, 1992, supplies the express-geographic-discrimination rule without extending its other holdings. *Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc.*, 397 U.S. 137, governs a separate evenhanded-regulation branch. *McKesson Corp. v. Division of Alcoholic Beverages and Tobacco*, 496 U.S. 18, supplies the conditional state-remedy principles without fixing a refund.

#### Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill, Inc. v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, 504 U.S. 353 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-636.
**Decided:** June 1, 1992.

##### Whether county-origin restrictions at private landfills escape Commerce Clause scrutiny because they also exclude other intrastate counties

**Holding:** A State's prohibition on a private landfill's acceptance of outside-county waste unless a county plan affirmatively authorizes it discriminates against interstate commerce even when it also excludes other counties within that State and some counties admit imports. The restriction cannot stand unless the State establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve; Michigan has not done so for these Waste Import Restrictions, and planning waste services does not convert regulation of a private facility into market participation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the same rule, application and limits. Blackmun's proposed evidentiary remand supplies no controlling join.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the restrictions on privately owned and operated facilities.

**Material application:** The challenged veto gives local waste access that equivalent outside waste lacks. Neutral quantity limits, appropriate charges and facility standards can address the planning concerns; respondents identify no distinct imported hazard or inadequacy of those alternatives and neither identify further necessary proof nor request an evidentiary remand. The alternatives illustrate the failed justification, rather than require a replacement program. No statewide total exclusion or disadvantage to every outside supplier is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Only the 1988 Waste Import Restrictions in §§299.413a and 299.430(2), as applied to private landfills, are invalidated. The earlier planning statute, public-facility practices, neutral health, safety and capacity rules, different waste-risk classifications, unconditional operation and genuinely applicable congressional authorization remain outside the decision. No hazardous-waste question arises. Distinct due-process, taking and ownership-transfer licensing matters are not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining dismissal and remand through that court for appropriately tailored declaratory and prospective relief consistent with invalidation. Independent neutral planning and permitting remain; the judgment requires neither unconditional permit issuance, acceptance of particular waste nor operation beyond neutral capacity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Philadelphia v. New Jersey*, 437 U.S. 617, applies to origin-only exclusion at private waste facilities. *Brimmer v. Rebman*, 138 U.S. 78, and *Dean Milk Co. v. Madison*, 340 U.S. 349, prevent a substate boundary and exclusion of some intrastate commerce from curing geographic discrimination. *New Energy Co. of Indiana v. Limbach*, 486 U.S. 269, and *Wyoming v. Oklahoma*, January 22, 1992, distinguish the existence of discrimination from its extent. *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, 432 U.S. 333, preserves the purpose-and-alternatives burden. *Maine v. Taylor*, 477 U.S. 131, is distinguished for lack of a distinctive imported hazard and unavailable adequate alternatives. *Hughes v. Alexandria Scrap Corp.*, 426 U.S. 794, and *Reeves, Inc. v. Stake*, 447 U.S. 429, preserve market participation while leaving private-facility regulation outside it. *Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc.*, 397 U.S. 137, remains the separate rule for evenhanded regulation.

### State-tax collection and interstate commerce

#### Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, 504 U.S. 298 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-194.
**Decided:** May 26, 1992.

##### Whether purposeful solicitation can satisfy due process for use-tax collection without physical presence

**Holding:** A mail-order seller that purposefully and continuously solicits a State’s consumers may have the minimum connection required for a use-tax collection obligation even without physical presence, when the obligation is rationally related to benefits received through that market. Quill’s sustained solicitation and approximately $1 million in annual North Dakota sales satisfy due process; Bellas Hess and earlier holdings are overruled only insofar as they make physical presence indispensable for this collection duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices. Stone expressly does not reach due process and supplies no join.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Sustained solicitation and about $1 million in annual North Dakota sales establish the purposeful connection; the obligation relates rationally to benefits from that market. The distinct Commerce inquiry remains necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consumer use-tax liability, other taxes, a numerical transaction or property-value threshold, a general software or physical-presence rule, and the result under future legislation are not decided. Potential retroactive effects of overruling the Commerce rule were excluded from the grant. Congress cannot authorize a due-process violation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The collection judgment is reversed and remanded, 8–1, under the Commerce Clause. The eight Justices reaching due process reject that challenge, but this supplies no collection liability given the Commerce disposition. No tax amount, interest, penalty, retrospective liability or §1983/§1988 entitlement is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- National Bellas Hess v. Department of Revenue, 386 U.S.753 — overruled only to the extent its due-process holding requires physical presence for use-tax collection; its Commerce rule is treated separately below.
- Miller Brothers v. Maryland, 347 U.S.340 — the minimum-connection requirement remains; any indispensable physical-entry implication is displaced at the stated collection scope.
- International Shoe Co. v. Washington, 326 U.S.310 — applied for purposeful affiliation and fair connection.
- Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz, 471 U.S.462 — its nonphysical purposeful-contact reasoning is extended by analogy to the collection obligation; every personal-jurisdiction rule is not imported.
- Shaffer v. Heitner, 433 U.S.186 — applied by comparable reasoning against a mechanical presence prerequisite.
- Moorman Manufacturing Co. v. Bair, 437 U.S.267 — the requirement that the tax relate rationally to state-connected values remains.

##### Whether Bellas Hess’s specific Commerce Clause collection rule remains controlling

**Holding:** Bellas Hess’s specific Commerce Clause rule continues to bar imposing a sales-or-use-tax collection duty on a seller whose customer connections are limited to mail or common-carrier commerce without the requisite local presence. Quill’s solicitation and remote deliveries do not themselves displace that rule; Complete Auto’s separate substantial-nexus requirement is not satisfied merely by purposeful market access or the due-process showing.

**Proposition-level authority:** The common, narrower ground expressly supported by Stevens, Blackmun, O’Connor and Souter in Part IV, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas in their concurrence, and Stone in his concurrence in the judgment; eight Justices retain the particular precedent. The full Part IV substantive policy analysis has four joins and is not an Opinion of the Court. The software application has the separate five-Justice authority identified below.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Complete Auto preserves substantial nexus separately from apportionment, nondiscrimination and fair relation to state services. The specific collection precedent and reliance it created persist; the four-Justice Part IV policy analysis is not imported into the common precedent ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consumer use-tax liability, other taxes, a numerical transaction or property-value threshold, a general software or physical-presence rule, and the result under future legislation are not decided. Potential retroactive effects of overruling the Commerce rule were excluded from the grant. Congress cannot authorize a due-process violation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The collection judgment is reversed and remanded, 8–1, under the Commerce Clause. The eight Justices reaching due process reject that challenge, but this supplies no collection liability given the Commerce disposition. No tax amount, interest, penalty, retrospective liability or §1983/§1988 entitlement is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- National Bellas Hess v. Department of Revenue, 386 U.S.753 — retained for its specific Commerce Clause sales-and-use collection rule; no general immunity for interstate commerce is restored.
- Complete Auto Transit v. Brady, 430 U.S.274 — substantial nexus remains distinct from fair apportionment, nondiscrimination and fair relation to state services; its rejection of Spector’s labeling rule remains.
- National Geographic Society v. California Board of Equalization, 430 U.S.551, and D.H. Holmes Co. v. McNamara, 486 U.S.24 — their express distinctions of Bellas Hess preserve reasonable reliance on the particular collection precedent rather than establish its abandonment.
- Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 491 U.S.164, and Payne v. Tennessee, 501 U.S.808 — their congressional-correction and reliance principles support the narrower adherence ground at the stated scope.
- Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin, 328 U.S.408 — preserves Congress’s power to authorize otherwise dormant-Commerce burdens; Congress cannot authorize a due-process violation.

##### Whether the limited licensed-software interests provide sufficient local collection nexus

**Holding:** Quill’s insignificant interests in the licensed ordering software on this record do not supply the local commercial presence required to take its mail-order sales outside the retained Commerce Clause collection rule. This conclusion rejects the State’s asserted alternative nexus for this seller without prescribing a general rule for all software, licenses or in-state property.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens, Blackmun, O’Connor and Souter in Part IV, together with Stone’s expressly matching record-specific software ground in his concurrence in the judgment; five Justices adopt this application at the same scope. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support reversal but are not counted as joining this substantive software explanation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The insignificant software interests facilitate communication with the out-of-state business; they do not establish the local commercial operation or market-maintaining representative activity asserted by the State. No warehouse, employee or agent is found.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consumer use-tax liability, other taxes, a numerical transaction or property-value threshold, a general software or physical-presence rule, and the result under future legislation are not decided. Potential retroactive effects of overruling the Commerce rule were excluded from the grant. Congress cannot authorize a due-process violation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The collection judgment is reversed and remanded, 8–1, under the Commerce Clause. The eight Justices reaching due process reject that challenge, but this supplies no collection liability given the Commerce disposition. No tax amount, interest, penalty, retrospective liability or §1983/§1988 entitlement is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- National Bellas Hess v. Department of Revenue, 386 U.S.753 — applied to this mail-order collection demand after rejection of the asserted distinguishing property interest.
- Tyler Pipe Industries v. Washington Department of Revenue, 483 U.S.232 — distinguished for actual in-state market-maintaining activity, including representatives; no employee-only nexus requirement is adopted.
- National Geographic Society v. California Board of Equalization, 430 U.S.551 — Part IV’s additional rejection of a slightest-presence standard supports its four signers’ explanation; Stone’s fifth vote adopts the stated record-specific insufficiency without a whole-opinion join. Its local-office holding remains undisturbed.

### Adequate and independent state grounds

#### Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-5843.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

For the judicial aggravator, general advisory recommendation and sentence-correction holdings in *Sochor v. Florida*, June 8, 1992, No. 91-5843, see Criminal Procedure — Capital sentencing.

##### Whether Florida's express failure-to-object ground forecloses review of the jury-heinousness instruction despite alternative merits discussion

**Holding:** The Court cannot review a federal challenge to a penalty instruction when the state judgment clearly rests on an adequate independent failure-to-object ground, even if the state court also rejects the claim on its merits. Florida's express preservation ruling forecloses this jury-heinousness challenge; no sufficient basis to reject its adequacy or independence is established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; seven Justices. Blackmun and Stevens would reach the instructional claim.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Florida separately stated that the penalty-instruction claims were unpreserved and rejected their merits; its note includes heinousness. Availability of fundamental-error review does not establish that this preservation ruling depends on the federal merits, and later citations to the merits discussion do not withdraw the independent ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the instruction's constitutional merits or foreclose a supported challenge to a different procedural ground. This direct-review holding creates no habeas exception.

**Operative remedy or transition:** No relief is granted on the jury-heinousness instruction claim; the separate judicial weighing error and inadequate cure require the sentence vacatur described under Criminal Procedure — Capital sentencing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Michigan v. Long*, 463 U.S. 1032, preserves an expressly independent state disposition despite alternative federal merits discussion. *Herb v. Pitcairn*, 324 U.S. 117, supplies the jurisdictional limit. *Coleman v. Thompson*, 501 U.S. 722, retains its distinction between independent state procedure and federal merits.

### Administrative exhaustion

#### McCarthy v. Madigan, 503 U.S. 140 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6861.
**Decided:** March 4, 1992.

##### Whether Congress requires exhaustion of the general BOP grievance procedure for this federal medical-care damages action

**Holding:** An express congressional exhaustion requirement controls according to its terms. Where Congress has not required exhaustion, courts must exercise sound discretion consistent with the statutory scheme, weighing prompt access to court against the actual interests in agency authority and judicial efficiency; neither the general BOP delegation nor the then-current §1997e imposes a mandatory gate for this federal prisoner’s *Bivens* medical-care claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the governing inquiry and statutory application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated under the statutory scheme adjudicated.

**Material application:** General prison-administration authority under 18 U.S.C. §4042 contains no exhaustion command. The then-current 42 U.S.C. §1997e addresses specified state-prisoner §1983 actions through limited discretionary stays, not this federal action. The Bureau’s general grievance procedure is not a comprehensive congressional replacement for the existing medical-care damages channel.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No exception to an express congressional requirement or new *Bivens* action is created. Liability, immunity, causation and damages are not determined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Patsy v. Board of Regents of Florida*, 457 U.S. 496, makes congressional design central. *McKart v. United States*, 395 U.S. 185, supplies the agency-authority, expertise, error-correction and efficiency purposes. *McGee v. United States*, 402 U.S. 479, supports sound judicial discretion absent congressional mandate. *Coit Independence Joint Venture v. FSLIC*, 489 U.S. 561, distinguishes statutory exhaustion and the adequacy of the administrative route. *Carlson v. Green*, 446 U.S. 14, remains the established medical-care damages channel without extension. *Bush v. Lucas*, 462 U.S. 367, and *Schweiker v. Chilicky*, 487 U.S. 412, are distinguished because there is no comparable comprehensive congressional remedial replacement here.

##### Whether the actual BOP procedure justifies requiring exhaustion of this damages-only claim absent a statutory mandate

**Holding:** Absent a statutory mandate, a federal prisoner seeking only damages for the completed medical-care wrong alleged here need not exhaust the described BOP process: it cannot award compensation, its short filing periods risk forfeiture, and its limited adjudicative benefit does not outweigh access to court. The result does not decide a request for prospective relief, a procedure able to grant the sought compensation, or a materially different administrative adjudication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices join this particular balancing and application. Scalia and Thomas concur in the judgment solely because monetary relief is unavailable; their votes do not support the deadline or broader balancing rationale.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for this claim and grievance procedure.

**Material application:** The process provides investigation and written responses but no hearing, formal evidentiary findings or monetary award. It requires an initial complaint within 15 days and appeals within 20 and 30 days, with extensions for valid reasons. The rejected regional appeal illustrates forfeiture risk without establishing official misconduct. Speculative FTCA conversion or settlement does not establish an effective damages remedy under these regulations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The deadline analysis does not invalidate short filing periods categorically. No finding of bias, deliberate indifference, damages entitlement or loss of a defense is made. The holding does not convert negligence into a constitutional violation or expand the established damages action.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the exhaustion dismissal and remand for ordinary proceedings on the damages complaint without completion of this grievance process as a prerequisite. Liability, defenses and damages remain for adjudication.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *McKart*, 395 U.S. 185, supports comparing actual individual and institutional interests. *Coit*, 489 U.S. 561, informs practical adequacy without categorically invalidating deadlines. *Turner v. Safley*, 482 U.S. 78, preserves judicial cognizance of valid prisoner claims without deciding this claim’s validity. *Hillsborough Township v. Cromwell*, 326 U.S. 620, and *Union Pacific Railroad Co. v. Board of County Commissioners*, 247 U.S. 282, address uncertainty in an administrative route to the requested relief.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is reversed and the case remanded for the Fourth Circuit to examine the informal brief's actual contents.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Torres makes Rule 3 compliance mandatory while allowing functional compliance with its required information. **Holding:** Original Bankruptcy Code §106(c) does not unequivocally waive the United States’ immunity from an affirmative personal money judgment recovering an unauthorized transfer under §550. **Treatment of earlier authority:** *Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs*, 498 U.S. 89; *United States v. Mitchell*, 445 U.S. 535; and *United States v. **Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; the same seven Justices reject each alternative offered to sustain the monetary judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A genuine specific-property proceeding requires its own facts and authority and is not adjudicated here. No traceable fund is assumed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The alternative grounds do not preserve the personal money judgment. Genuinely distinct, properly supported remedies remain for ordinary proceedings; none is awarded.

IRS*, 496 U.S. 53, does not supply an identified fund here. *United States v. Shaw*, 309 U.S. 495, is preserved against inferring monetary consent from a property characterization. *Bull v. **Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both First Circuit judgments are vacated and the cases remanded for the district court to determine whether proven changed circumstances make continued enforcement inequitable and whether the proposed modification responds suitably to those changes. **Treatment of earlier authority:**

#### Wyoming v. Oklahoma, 502 U.S. 437 (1992)

##### Whether Wyoming's severance-tax loss establishes its own Article III injury

**Holding:** A documented loss of severance-tax revenue caused by a mandatory substitution away from the State's taxed coal establishes Wyoming's own Article III injury where the record supports causation and an injunction would remove the challenged purchasing mandate. Redressability does not require a guarantee that every displaced purchase will return, but generalized hopes of future tax growth do not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices support this proposition.

**Material application:** The purchasing record, the extraction tax, and evidence of available production capacity support an inference of lost severance receipts from compelled substitution away from Wyoming coal. The fiscal inference is supported on the summary-judgment record; it is not itself a stipulated fact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary injury, causation and redressability requirements apply. No sovereign exemption or standing based on unrelated future fiscal effects is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Maryland v. Louisiana*, 451 U.S. 725 — applied to a State's demonstrated injury from another State's commercial restriction.
- *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, 432 U.S. 333 — geographic market exclusion is a concrete commercial injury; Wyoming still proves its own fiscal stake.
- *Warth v. Seldin*, 422 U.S. 490, and *Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization*, 426 U.S. 26 — ordinary personal-injury and causation requirements are preserved, not relaxed for States.

##### Whether this interstate fiscal controversy warrants exercise of original jurisdiction

**Holding:** The Court exercises original jurisdiction over this substantial interstate controversy because Wyoming's own fiscal injury and the challenged statewide mandate warrant direct resolution and no equivalent pending private action would fully resolve the State's claim. **Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices support this proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The possibility of a producer's or utility's suit does not require surrender of this distinct state injury, but the holding does not promise an original forum for every indirect effect on tax receipts. Acceptance of the bill does not bar jurisdictional reconsideration on the developed record.

**Material judgment:** Original jurisdiction is exercised by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter. Scalia and Thomas would dismiss and do not reach the Commerce Clause merits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Massachusetts v. Missouri*, 308 U.S. 1 — applied for seriousness of the sovereign interest and adequacy of other available relief.

- *Arizona v. California*, 460 U.S. 605 — an earlier jurisdictional ruling is not an irrevocable bar to reconsidering jurisdiction on the developed record.

### Capital sentencing

#### Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-5843.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

For the preservation bar on the jury-heinousness instruction claim in *Sochor v. Florida*, June 8, 1992, No. 91-5843, see Federal Courts — Adequate and independent state grounds.

##### Whether the judge's conscious-victim strangulation application supplies a sufficiently narrowed heinousness aggravator

**Holding:** A sentencing judge may apply an otherwise vague heinousness aggravator through an authoritative and sufficiently specific state narrowing construction. Florida's consistently recognized conscious-victim strangulation construction supplies adequate guidance here; inconsistent applications in other factual settings do not establish that this judge lacked guidance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-B of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; seven Justices. Blackmun and Stevens do not join this disposition because they would require correction of the separate jury defect.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at the stated judicial application and record.

**Material application:** Florida's identified narrowing decisions address the fear and conscious suffering of strangulation. The victim's resistance, fear and strangulation support that concrete construction; the judge did not merely repeat the statutory adjectives.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court approves neither every Florida heinousness decision nor the bare instruction given to this jury, and it does not decide a different victim's condition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the preserved constitutional challenge to the judge's narrowed heinousness application; that factor supplies no basis for relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Godfrey v. Georgia*, 446 U.S. 420, and *Maynard v. Cartwright*, 486 U.S. 356, require meaningful narrowing. *Walton v. Arizona*, 497 U.S. 639, permits reliance on authoritative state constructions and presumes the judge's familiarity with them. *Proffitt v. Florida*, 428 U.S. 242, preserves a narrowed heinousness category without approving every later application.

##### Whether submission of a lawful but unsupported coldness aggravator establishes that a general advisory jury recommendation relied on it

**Holding:** Submitting a legally available aggravating circumstance without evidentiary support does not itself establish that a Florida advisory jury relied on it in a general death recommendation that identifies no aggravator findings. The asserted inference of jury-coldness error is rejected without a finding that the jury actually rejected the factor or resolution of a legally invalid instruction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Souter's opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices. Stone reserves this independent question, Blackmun and Stevens would include the submission in correction, and Scalia does not join this holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at this narrow factual-insufficiency inference and general advisory recommendation.

**Material application:** Cold, calculated and premeditated murder was a lawful aggravator, but proof of its heightened premeditation requirement failed. The jury identified no aggravator findings, so submission alone cannot establish its reliance. The judge's express coldness finding establishes a separate error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding makes no factual jury finding, does not assimilate every capital recommendation to a guilt verdict, does not decide that all instructional errors are harmless, and leaves broader capital-jury doctrine open. Legally invalid alternatives remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Griffin v. United States*, December 3, 1991, is extended narrowly through its factual-versus-legal distinction to this asserted inference from a general advisory recommendation. *Stromberg v. California*, 283 U.S. 359, and *Yates v. United States*, 354 U.S. 298, retain their rules for legally invalid alternatives. *Estelle v. McGuire*, December 4, 1991, retains whole-charge reasonable-likelihood review, which neither removes the preservation bar nor supplies an automatic inference of factual reliance here.

##### Whether the judge's express weighing of the evidentially unsupported coldness aggravator creates constitutional error

**Holding:** When a Florida capital sentencing judge expressly weighs an aggravator that the state supreme court holds unsupported by the evidence, that invalid factor creates Eighth Amendment error in the weighing decision. Lawful reweighing, constitutional harmless-error review or resentencing is required; other valid aggravators do not themselves cure the error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-B-1 of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices. Scalia rejects the constitutional weighing-error premise.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for documented reliance on an unsupported aggravator.

**Material application:** The judge expressly found and weighed heightened coldness, which Florida held unproved. Proof of ordinary murder premeditation does not establish this additional aggravating requirement. Surviving death eligibility therefore does not eliminate the actual weighing error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The convictions and mitigation findings are not disturbed. Removal of an aggravator does not itself require a life sentence or a new penalty jury. The no-mitigation finding does not mean no mitigating evidence was presented.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence requires a supported determination under *Chapman*, lawful reweighing if Florida law authorizes it, or resentencing. The separate holding below identifies why the existing appellate treatment fails to provide that correction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Clemons v. Mississippi*, 494 U.S. 738, requires adequate correction of capital weighing error. *Parker v. Dugger*, 498 U.S. 308, applies that principle to unsupported Florida aggravators and the actual sentencing record. *Stringer v. Black*, March 9, 1992, supplies the correction principle for this documented reliance; its habeas retroactivity and default reservations do not bar direct review. *Zant v. Stephens*, 462 U.S. 862, is distinguished because its nonweighing setting does not make surviving eligibility an automatic cure. *Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, supplies constitutional harmlessness with the State bearing the burden.

##### Whether Florida's proportionality discussion and surviving-factor count constitutionally cure the weighing error

**Holding:** A state appellate court affirming this sentence must substantively establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the invalid aggravator did not contribute to the sentence, or provide another authorized constitutional cure. Florida's existing proportionality discussion, count of surviving factors and mixed citations do not establish that inquiry; no verbal formula is either required or sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts III-B-2 and IV of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Kennedy; six Justices. White and Thomas accept the error framework but find the existing review sufficient; Scalia would affirm on a separate ground.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Proportionality compares sentences across cases; it does not itself determine whether this invalid factor influenced this sentencing balance. Three valid factors and no found mitigation may bear on harmlessness but do not establish the missing inquiry. The State bears the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither undertakes reweighing nor determines the outcome of lawful correction. It requires no prescribed phrase and rejects a bare harmless-error label as sufficient. No automatic life sentence, mandatory new penalty jury, new mitigation finding or new habeas standard is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the judgment sustaining the death sentence and remand to Florida for supported *Chapman* review, lawful reweighing if Florida law permits, or resentencing. The convictions remain intact; the state courts must choose and perform an adequate lawful correction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, retains the State's burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute. *Clemons v. Mississippi*, 494 U.S. 738, and *Parker v. Dugger*, 498 U.S. 308, require corrective analysis of the sentence and actual record rather than proportionality alone. *Stringer v. Black*, March 9, 1992, preserves the inadequacy of factor counting and proportionality as standalone cures.

### Capital sentencing and habeas retroactivity

#### Stringer v. Black, 503 U.S. 222 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6616.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether the Mississippi vague-aggravator claim seeks a new rule after February 19, 1985 finality

**Holding:** For a sentence final on February 19, 1985, Godfrey already prohibited the unguided heinousness aggravation later addressed in Maynard, and applying that protection to Mississippi’s weighing system does not create a new rule. Teague therefore does not bar consideration of this claim on the ground that Maynard or the corrective principles discussed in Clemons postdated finality.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is confined to the old-rule application established by Godfrey for this finality date. No Teague exception or unresolved procedural-default defense is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit’s Teague disposition is reversed and remanded for consideration of the preserved procedural-default defense and adequate state correction. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 — applied, not replaced: the claim seeks an old rule at the relevant finality date.
- Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied: both novelty of the later rule and novelty of its application must be considered.
- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 — applied: meaningful guidance was already required for the heinousness aggravator.
- Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 — treated as applying Godfrey’s old rule to equivalent language.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied as identifying permissible correction, not a newly created protection in this setting.

##### What correction a constitutionally invalid aggravator requires in a capital weighing system

**Holding:** When a capital sentencer weighs a constitutionally invalid aggravating factor against mitigation, remaining valid aggravators do not by themselves cure the error. The state judicial system must supply genuine reweighing without the invalid factor, where authorized, or constitutional harmless-error review establishing that the invalid factor did not affect the sentence; the adequacy of any cure here remains for further proceedings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine authorized reweighing and constitutional harmlessness are distinct corrective routes. The latter places on the State the burden of proving harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt. Remaining valid aggravators or bare proportionality review alone do not cure the error; death eligibility is not automatically negated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit’s Teague disposition is reversed and remanded for consideration of the preserved procedural-default defense and adequate state correction. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 — distinguished: its nonweighing holding does not automatically cure a weighing-system error.
- Barclay v. Florida, 463 U.S. 939 — distinguished: its actual review and state-law invalidity do not justify uncorrected constitutional aggravation.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied: genuine appellate reweighing or constitutional harmlessness can correct a weighing error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — applied to the harmless-error route: the State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231 — distinguished: duplication in narrowing does not answer vague-factor weighting and cannot supply a prefinality reliance premise.

### Compelled medication and fair trial

#### Riggins v. Nevada, 504 U.S. 127 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-8466.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### What justification is required to continue antipsychotic medication over a pretrial detainee’s objection

**Holding:** Once a pretrial detainee objects to continued antipsychotic medication, the State must establish medical appropriateness and a need sufficient to override the protected refusal interest, considering reasonable less intrusive means. The unexplained order here supplies neither an essential justification nor findings about alternatives and cannot authorize the compelled treatment through trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this rule and application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Continued medication became involuntary after objection. Medical appropriateness was accepted, but the unexplained order established neither essential safety need nor the insufficiency of less intrusive means; testimony indicated that competence might continue without Mellaril.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Washington v. Harper, 494 U.S. 210 (1990): applied for the protected refusal interest and requirement of overriding justification and medical appropriateness; its convicted-prisoner procedures do not automatically govern trial medication.
- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): relied on for the State's legitimate trial interest and the possibility of necessary courtroom measures; no comprehensive competency-medication exception is adopted.

##### Whether the unsupported treatment and unreconstructable trial risk require a new trial

**Holding:** When unjustified compelled antipsychotic medication through trial creates a supported, substantial risk of impairing the defense that the record cannot reliably reconstruct, the defendant need not demonstrate exactly how an unmedicated trial would have differed. The risk here extends beyond visible demeanor to testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel; explanatory expert testimony does not cure it, and the convictions and dependent sentence must be vacated for a new trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this record-specific rule and remedy. Kennedy concurs in the reversal and new-trial judgment on a distinct trial-integrity ground.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The supported risk concerned attention, testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel as well as demeanor. Expert explanation cannot restore an altered conversation or response, and an unmedicated trial cannot reliably be reconstructed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): applied by analogy to potentially prejudicial compelled trial measures and the need for an essential justification.
- Estelle v. Williams, 425 U.S. 501 (1976): applied by analogy to effects of compelled trial appearance that cannot be fully reconstructed from a transcript; its particular waiver disposition is not extended.

### Confrontation and hearsay

#### White v. Illinois, 502 U.S. 346 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6113.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

##### Whether accepted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions require prior production or proof of unavailability

**Holding:** Where the granted review assumes that statements were properly admitted under the firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions, the Confrontation Clause does not require the prosecution first to produce the declarant or establish unavailability. The accepted exception foundations remain necessary; the ruling does not validate a disputed foundation or every police interview.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; nine Justices endorse this rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The limited grant accepts the state-law exception foundations for the child's statements as its premise; the Court makes no finding that disputed foundations are satisfied. Their spontaneous and treatment settings supply evidentiary value that later trial testimony cannot recreate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No blanket approval of police questioning, automatic child-necessity rule, harmless-error holding, or ruling on state-law admissibility outside the granted premise is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Illinois Appellate Court judgment is affirmed on the limited Confrontation Clause claim; no new trial or evidentiary hearing is required on the unavailability contention.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Inadi*, 475 U.S. 387 — extended from coconspirator declarations to the two accepted exceptions because later testimony does not replace their contextual value.
- *Ohio v. Roberts*, 448 U.S. 56 — preserved for its received confrontation framework; its former-testimony unavailability analysis is not universalized.
- *Idaho v. Wright*, 497 U.S. 805 — preserved for constitutional reliability outside firmly rooted exceptions; no unsupported judicial assurance substitutes for its requirements.
- *Bourjaily v. United States*, 483 U.S. 171 — firmly rooted exceptions retain their established reliability significance.
- *Coy v. Iowa*, 487 U.S. 1012, and *Maryland v. Craig*, 497 U.S. 836 — distinguished as trial-witness presentation cases; their safeguards remain intact.

##### Whether confrontation is categorically limited to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes

**Holding:** The Confrontation Clause is not categorically confined to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes, so that limiting definition does not supply an alternative basis for admitting these statements. The Court resolves the accepted exceptions under existing confrontation law and leaves any broader reconstruction of that law open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the proposed categorical narrowing. Scalia and Thomas withhold that join.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not hold that every hearsay statement violates the Constitution or decide the treatment of a formal investigative accusation on a disputed record. No new categorical testimonial rule replaces the received framework.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Mattox v. United States*, 156 U.S. 237 — retained as evidence of confrontation's substantive protection and its coexistence with established exceptions.
- *California v. Green*, 399 U.S. 149, and *Dutton v. Evans*, 400 U.S. 74 — preserved; the Court's treatment of out-of-court statements is not displaced by a formal-affidavit-only definition.
- *Inadi* and *Roberts* — applied as exception-sensitive confrontation decisions, not replaced with a new categorical testimonial rule.

### Detention credit

#### United States v. Wilson, 503 U.S. 329 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1745.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### Who initially computes §3585(b) detention credit and when

**Holding:** Section 3585(b) does not authorize a district court to compute detention credit at sentencing; the Attorney General through BOP must make the initial computation after the federal sentence commences. Administrative review and proper subsequent judicial review remain available, so the calculation is not unreviewable discretion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices adopt this proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A process capable of granting detention credit is distinct from McCarthy’s damages-only claim and compensation-incapable grievance procedure. The decision leaves proper administrative and judicial review available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit’s direction to the sentencing court to award credit is reversed. Initial computation remains with the Attorney General through BOP after sentence commencement, subject to proper review; no days of credit or new detention order are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Otte v. United States, 419 U.S. 43 — applied: Congress’s choice of verb tense informs statutory timing.
- Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation, 484 U.S. 49 — applied for the same temporal-text principle.
- Russello v. United States, 464 U.S. 16 — applied as a rebuttable inference, overcome here by the rewritten text and administration scheme.
- Robertson v. Railroad Labor Board, 268 U.S. 619 — applied: a departure from long-established procedure is not lightly inferred.
- McCarthy v. Madigan, March 4, 1992 — distinguished: its damages-only process could not provide the requested relief, unlike a credit computation process.

##### Whether detention already credited to another sentence may also receive federal credit

**Holding:** Section 3585(b) forbids federal detention credit for time already credited against another sentence when the federal sentence begins. A defendant cannot avoid that condition merely because the federal sentencing hearing occurred before the other court awarded its credit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices adopt this proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Tennessee credited 429 days before the federal term began, although after the federal sentencing hearing. The Court does not fix a numerical federal credit, define every form of official detention, or decide a challenge to a completed BOP calculation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit’s direction to the sentencing court to award credit is reversed. Initial computation remains with the Attorney General through BOP after sentence commencement, subject to proper review; no days of credit or new detention order are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Turkette, 452 U.S. 576 — applied: avoid an arbitrary statutory result unsupported by the text’s purpose.

### Double jeopardy

#### United States v. Felix, 503 U.S. 378 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1599.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

##### Whether prior evidentiary use places a defendant in jeopardy for distinct uncharged substantive offenses

**Holding:** The use of evidence of an uncharged crime in one trial does not itself place the defendant in jeopardy for that crime. The later Oklahoma substantive charges concern different acts from the Missouri attempt and are not barred merely because evidence of those acts helped prove intent in Missouri.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of White's opinion of the Court, supported by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine Justices adopt the distinct-prosecution rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Missouri attempt concerned different acts from the earlier Oklahoma offenses. Ordinary objections to the admissibility of particular evidence remain.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment reversing substantive counts 2–6 and conspiracy count 1 is reversed, restoring the prosecution’s judgments against the double-jeopardy objection, subject to preserved unresolved appellate issues. The separate disposition of travel counts 9–10 is undisturbed; no automatic new trial or evidentiary ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Dowling v. United States, 493 U.S. 342 — applied: evidentiary use does not itself constitute prosecution for the described offense.
- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508 — applied and confined to essential-conduct analysis; its express rejection of a same-evidence test remains operative.

##### Whether overlapping substantive acts bar prosecution of the distinct conspiracy agreement

**Holding:** A conspiracy and its substantive objects are distinct offenses; prosecution of the substantive crime does not bar prosecution of the agreement merely because the same acts help prove both. The overlapping Missouri conduct therefore does not bar this Oklahoma conspiracy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of White's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt this rationale. Stevens and Blackmun concur only in the conspiracy judgment on a narrower ground.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Grady remains in force; no same-transaction requirement or new evidentiary exemption is adopted. A materially different single-incident successive prosecution remains for its own case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment reversing substantive counts 2–6 and conspiracy count 1 is reversed, restoring the prosecution’s judgments against the double-jeopardy objection, subject to preserved unresolved appellate issues. The separate disposition of travel counts 9–10 is undisturbed; no automatic new trial or evidentiary ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bayer, 331 U.S. 532 — applied: the agreement is distinct from its execution.
- Pinkerton v. United States, 328 U.S. 640 — applied: substantive crime and conspiracy remain separately punishable offenses.
- Iannelli v. United States, 420 U.S. 770 — applied: conspiracy’s independent character survives overlapping proof.
- Garrett v. United States, 471 U.S. 773 — applied by analogy to the treatment of multilayered criminal conduct.
- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508 — clarified without overruling: it does not abrogate the established conspiracy/substantive-offense distinction.

### Entrapment and evidentiary insufficiency

#### Jacobson v. United States, 503 U.S. 540 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1124.
**Decided:** April 6, 1992.

##### Whether the Government proved predisposition independent of its inducement beyond a reasonable doubt

**Holding:** When the Government induces the charged crime and entrapment is at issue, it must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant’s predisposition existed before and independently of its cultivation; evidence obtained after contact may prove that earlier state of mind. Even giving the prosecution its reasonable favorable inferences, the lawful earlier purchase and responses generated during this twenty-six-month operation do not establish that independent predisposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** White, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, Thomas: six votes control.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Later-collected evidence may establish prior readiness; its collection date and investigative persistence do not alone decide entrapment. No knowledge-of-illegality element, reasonable-suspicion prerequisite, general bar on undercover opportunities, or objective misconduct defense is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit’s judgment and return the case for entry of acquittal. The prosecution may not retry the charge to supply missing proof of independent predisposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sorrells v. United States, 287 U.S. 435 — applied: the Government may offer opportunities but may not manufacture the disposition and prosecute its product.
- Sherman v. United States, 356 U.S. 369 — applied: the record can establish entrapment as a matter of law despite an eventual willing transaction.
- United States v. Russell, 411 U.S. 423 — preserved: subjective predisposition remains the inquiry; no objective misconduct defense is substituted.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307 — applied: reasonable prosecution-favorable inferences remain subject to proof beyond reasonable doubt.
- Mathews v. United States, 485 U.S. 58 — preserved: a routine offered opportunity does not automatically support an entrapment instruction.

##### Whether insufficiency of proof overcoming entrapment permits retrial

**Holding:** Because the prosecution’s proof was legally insufficient to overcome entrapment, the proper disposition is acquittal rather than a new trial. Burks bars a second opportunity to supply the missing evidence of independent predisposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** White, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, Thomas: six votes support the remedial direction.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This disposition rests on insufficiency, not an erroneous instruction or another correctable trial error. A separate outrageous-government-conduct claim and statutory amendments are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit’s judgment and return the case for entry of acquittal. The prosecution may not retry the charge to supply missing proof of independent predisposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Burks v. United States, 437 U.S. 1 — applied: insufficiency requires acquittal and bars retrial to repair the proof.
- Sherman v. United States, 356 U.S. 369 — applied: matter-of-law entrapment supports termination, not a second prosecution opportunity.

### Evidence and jury instructions on habeas review

#### Estelle v. McGuire, 502 U.S. 62 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1074.  
**Decided:** December 4, 1991.

##### Whether admission of prior nonaccidental-injury evidence violates due process when the earlier abuser is unidentified

**Holding:** On federal habeas review, evidence of a child's prior nonaccidental injuries may be relevant to prove that the charged death was intentional even when the identity of the person who caused the earlier injuries is unknown. Admission for that nonidentity purpose does not itself violate due process, and a defendant's choice not to contest accident does not remove the State's burden to prove intentional killing.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, joined on this proposition by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The repeated injuries supported the inference of nonaccidental harm without themselves identifying McGuire as the abuser. The evidence-alone theory could not support habeas relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** State-law admissibility alone is not reviewed. The Court does not decide a general constitutional prohibition of propensity evidence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lewis v. Jeffers and Pulley v. Harris exclude habeas relief for state-law error alone. Lisenba and Dowling preserve the fundamental-unfairness boundary. In re Winship supplies the prosecution's continuing burden to prove every element.

##### Whether the prior-act instruction created a reasonable likelihood that jurors bypassed elemental proof

**Holding:** An ambiguous instruction violates due process only when, read with the charge and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood the jury applied it in a way that relieved the State of proving an element. This instruction did not create that likelihood because it conditioned any inference on the jury's finding that McGuire committed the prior acts and expressly prohibited a bad-character inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion joined at this level by Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; five of eight participants. Stone, Stevens, and O'Connor join only the distinct prior-injury-evidence proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The complete charge neither directed a finding that McGuire caused the earlier injuries nor shifted the prosecution's burden on identity or any other element.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not approve every prior-act instruction or decide a general propensity-evidence rule. Because the controlling majority finds no constitutional error, it does not reach Teague, Rose, or Chapman or require harmless-error review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed. The habeas writ remains denied; no acquittal, retrial, or release is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cupp supplies whole-charge review, and Boyde supplies the reasonable-likelihood standard. Francis and Sandstrom are distinguished and preserved for mandatory presumptions or shifts in elemental persuasion.

### General verdicts

#### Griffin v. United States, 502 U.S. 46 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6352.  
**Decided:** December 3, 1991.

##### Whether factual insufficiency on one lawful conspiracy object requires reversal of a general verdict supported on another object

**Holding:** A general guilty verdict on a federal multiple-object conspiracy need not be set aside merely because the evidence is inadequate for one legally valid object when another object is supported sufficiently under the unchanged beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard. **Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion for the Court, joined on the proposition by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; seven of eight participants. Blackmun's judgment-only concurrence is not counted as a join in this reasoning.

Florida*, decided June 8, 1992, extends the factual-versus-legal distinction only to rejecting an inference of reliance on a legally available but factually unsupported aggravator from a general Florida advisory death recommendation; it does not settle broader capital-jury error rules.

**Material application:** Both alleged §371 conspiracy objects were legally permissible. The Government conceded insufficient evidence connecting Griffin to the DEA object, but the IRS object had sufficient evidentiary support.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The excluded legal-invalidity, all-routes-insufficiency, elemental-instruction, and independent unanimity questions remain unresolved. Preferred withdrawal of an unsupported route on timely request creates no independent constitutional reversal rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is affirmed; the supported IRS object sustains the conviction against this challenge. No retrial or acquittal is ordered.

### Grand-jury presentation and supervisory dismissal

#### United States v. Williams, 504 U.S. 36 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1972.
**Decided:** May 4, 1992.

For the other questions in *United States v. Williams*, May 4, 1992, No. 90-1972, see Federal Courts — Preservation and questions passed upon below (reviewability) and Criminal Procedure — Grand-jury presentation and supervisory dismissal (disclosure duty and dismissal authority).

##### What known evidence a federal prosecutor must disclose to avoid materially distorting a grand-jury presentation

**Holding:** Before seeking an indictment, a federal prosecutor must present or otherwise disclose to the grand jury personally known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense when its omission would materially distort the grand-jury presentation. This duty requires neither an investigative search for undiscovered favorable evidence nor presentation of every possible defense; a violation alone does not establish entitlement to dismissal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Parts II and III, joined by Blackmun, O’Connor and Thomas, adopt the personally-known/direct-negation duty. Stone’s separate merits opinion adopts that duty where omission materially distorts the presentation. Under Marks, these five judgment supporters establish the stated common requirement; the four-Justice formulation does not remove Stone’s material-distortion qualification from the controlling rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The duty concerns substantial evidence personally known to the prosecutor, not an investigative search or a preliminary trial of every defense. Misconduct and charging prejudice are separate inquiries.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The grant concerns authority, not the accounting documents’ specific character. Affirmance does not establish that every intent-related item meets the duty or that the prosecutor fabricated evidence. Negligent discovery, state-grand-jury duties, constitutional disclosure, immunity and fabrication claims remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit’s dismissal without prejudice is affirmed, 5–4: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor and Thomas. A lawful new indictment remains available; no acquittal, dismissal with prejudice or reindictment command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Calandra, 414 U.S. 338 — its protective function and enforceable process limits support the confined duty; ordinary evidentiary freedom remains.
- Bank of Nova Scotia v. United States, 487 U.S. 250 — its misconduct and prejudice inquiries remain separate; violation does not automatically warrant dismissal.
- Costello v. United States, 350 U.S. 359 — preserved for competence and sufficiency challenges to an otherwise lawful presentation; distinguished from the specified prosecutorial omission.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 — applied to the judgment supporters’ shared direct-negation duty with the material-distortion qualification retained.

##### When a materially misleading omission permits supervisory dismissal without prejudice

**Holding:** Federal supervisory authority permits dismissal without prejudice when a prosecutor withholds personally known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense, the omission materially distorts the presentation, the evidence would plainly preclude a finding of probable cause, and the violation substantially influenced the indictment or leaves grave doubt that the indictment was free from that influence. This establishes the stated sufficient ground for dismissal; it requires neither a search for undiscovered favorable evidence nor presentation of every potential defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s merits Parts II and III, joined by Blackmun, O’Connor and Thomas, supply the narrower judgment-supporting rule. Stone concurs in affirmance under the broader material-distortion formulation, covering known evidence substantially undermining an essential element with established Bank of Nova Scotia prejudice. Under Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188, the four-Justice rule is the controlling logical subset: every known, directly negating omission that plainly defeats probable cause, materially distorts the presentation and causes the stated prejudice also satisfies Stone’s broader rule. The five judgment supporters together establish its force. Stone is not represented as formally joining the narrower qualification. The four-Justice and broader one-Justice applications are not combined to remove that qualification from the sufficient ground recognized here. Neither the plurality nor the five judgment supporters adopt an exclusive rule forbidding relief in every other circumstance.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The narrower rule adds evidence plainly precluding probable cause to the shared known-evidence, direct-negation and material-distortion requirements, and independently requires Bank of Nova Scotia prejudice. The Government presented a categorical authority objection rather than renewed review of the particular evidence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a sufficient dismissal class, not an exclusive prohibition on relief outside it. The plurality does not find the accounting documents plainly defeat probable cause; the evidentiary application was outside the grant. Stone’s broader application does not become general law. Guilt, negligent discovery, state grand juries and constitutional disclosure remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit’s dismissal without prejudice is affirmed, 5–4: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor and Thomas. A lawful new indictment remains available; no acquittal, dismissal with prejudice or reindictment command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bank of Nova Scotia v. United States, 487 U.S. 250 — its prejudice requirement is retained; limited supervisory power is extended to the specified known-evidence omission. Its evidence-reliability prohibition survives for lawful presentations.
- Costello v. United States, 350 U.S. 359 — distinguished: the confined misconduct inquiry is not a challenge to the competence or sufficiency of evidence in an otherwise lawful presentation. Its hearsay and ordinary adequacy rules remain.
- United States v. Calandra, 414 U.S. 338 — preserved for grand-jury independence and freedom from trial evidence rules; no exclusionary-rule extension occurs.
- United States v. Mechanik, 475 U.S. 66 — its completed-trial harmlessness holding is not extended to erase a prejudicial violation challenged and resolved before trial; the charging-prejudice formulation adopted in Bank remains operative.
- Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 — not transplanted into grand-jury proceedings; its trial disclosure duty remains unchanged.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 — applied to the genuine logical subset of the five judgment supporters’ rules; the shared authority supports the sufficient dismissal ground, without creating a single majority merits opinion or an exclusive boundary on other relief.
- United States v. Page, 808 F.2d 723 — the judgment applying it in Williams is sustained against the categorical no-authority challenge; Page’s full disclosure formulation and any rule requiring every fact relevant to a possible defense are not adopted.

### Guidelines departure review

#### Williams v. United States, 503 U.S. 193 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6297.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether reliance on a forbidden departure factor incorrectly applies the Guidelines

**Holding:** A departure based on a factor that the Commission has forbidden, or has already fully considered in the applicable range, rests on an incorrect application of the Guidelines under §3742(f)(1). A policy statement expressly prohibiting a sentencing action is authoritative guidance in applying the relevant Guideline, and an arrest record itself is not a permissible departure ground here.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices support the proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The policy-statement holding concerns an express prohibition guiding the relevant Guideline, not every policy statement for every purpose. Whether nonsimilar old convictions justify departure is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-affirmance judgment is vacated and remanded to the Seventh Circuit. It must decide causal harmlessness and independently reasonable extent on the whole record; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- No earlier Supreme Court decision is overruled; this is a construction of §§3553(b), 3742(f)(1) and the applicable §4A1.3 prohibition.
- Griffin v. United States, December 3, 1991 — kept distinct: unsupported facts under a valid legal theory do not establish the rule for a prohibited sentencing factor.

##### What showing permits affirmance despite reliance on an invalid departure ground

**Holding:** Once the challenger establishes reliance on an invalid departure factor, remand is required unless the reviewing court determines from the whole record that the district court would have imposed the same sentence without that factor. The challenger need not additionally prove that the error determined the sentence; the party defending the sentence must persuade the reviewing court that the error was harmless.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices support the proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The inquiry asks whether this judge would have imposed the same sentence, not whether that sentence could lawfully have been imposed. Chapman’s constitutional proof standard is not adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-affirmance judgment is vacated and remanded to the Seventh Circuit. It must decide causal harmlessness and independently reasonable extent on the whole record; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 52(a) — applied as the governing harmless-error rule for this nonconstitutional sentencing error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — not imported: the Court adopts no beyond-reasonable-doubt burden for this statutory error.

##### Whether departure reasonableness is separate from causal harmlessness

**Holding:** Even when an invalid departure ground was harmless under §3742(f)(1), a reviewing court must separately determine under §3742(f)(2) that the departure’s extent is reasonable in light of the valid grounds actually identified by the sentencing court. A reasonable sentence that the district court could have imposed does not establish that the erroneous factor was harmless.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices support the proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Review concerns valid grounds actually identified by the sentencing court; comments used to select a sentence after departure do not become new departure grounds. The lawful use of nonsimilar old convictions and the ultimate reasonable magnitude remain unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-affirmance judgment is vacated and remanded to the Seventh Circuit. It must decide causal harmlessness and independently reasonable extent on the whole record; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Solem v. Helm, 463 U.S. 277 — applied for the primary sentencing role of the trial court, not to add a proportionality issue.

##### Whether a refusal unrelated to a legitimate governmental end permits relief

**Holding:** A defendant is entitled to relief if the prosecutor's refusal to file a substantial-assistance motion is not rationally related to any legitimate governmental end. Useful assistance alone does not establish that defect, because the Government may rationally assess the costs and benefits of moving; no particular corrective remedy is selected here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Eight Justices adopt this independent constitutional ground; Stone does not join the separate formulation and resolves only the unsupported proffer under his stated ground.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Useful assistance may coexist with a rational assessment that the costs and benefits do not justify moving.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No enforceable plea promise is established. The independent operation of §3553(e) and §5K1.1, any particular remedy for a proved unconstitutional refusal, a departure amount and an independent firearm-term challenge are not decided. The motion prerequisite is not classified as an Article III jurisdictional limit.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The 180-month sentence is affirmed unanimously. No sentence reduction, discovery, evidentiary hearing, new sentencing proceeding or remand to develop an unspecified claim is ordered. The consecutive five-year firearm term is undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wayte v. United States, 470 U.S. 598 (1985): applied for constitutionally constrained prosecutorial choice; the legitimate-end requirement is adopted for the refusal at issue.

##### What showing is required for discovery or an evidentiary hearing about a refusal

**Holding:** A defendant seeking discovery or an evidentiary hearing about a refused substantial-assistance motion must make a substantial threshold showing of improper motive; useful assistance alone, with or without generalized accusations, is insufficient. Wade's assistance-only proffer does not justify discovery, a hearing, a remand to develop an unspecified claim, or a sentence reduction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: eight Justices adopt the discovery threshold and its application. Stone agrees that this proffer fails but does not join the general threshold; his separate concurrence is not a limit on the eight-Justice rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** When invited to identify evidence supporting inquiry, counsel offered only Wade’s assistance. No suspect reason was identified; useful cooperation with generalized accusations cannot justify inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No enforceable plea promise is established. The independent operation of §3553(e) and §5K1.1, any particular remedy for a proved unconstitutional refusal, a departure amount and an independent firearm-term challenge are not decided. The motion prerequisite is not classified as an Article III jurisdictional limit.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The 180-month sentence is affirmed unanimously. No sentence reduction, discovery, evidentiary hearing, new sentencing proceeding or remand to develop an unspecified claim is ordered. The consecutive five-year firearm term is undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wayte v. United States, 470 U.S. 598 (1985): its constitutional limits and concern with intrusive inquiry are applied; it reserved discovery entitlement, and the substantial-assistance threshold is adopted here.

### Child-welfare statutory enforcement

#### Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1488.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *Suter v. Artist M.*, March 25, 1992, No. 90-1488: whether the separate b.h. consent decree moots or precludes this statutory-enforcement controversy (Federal Courts — Mootness and decree scope).

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates the asserted individual reasonable-efforts right enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(15), read in the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act's plan and administrative-enforcement structure, does not confer on these beneficiaries an individual right enforceable through §1983 to compel the asserted reasonable efforts to prevent removal or reunify families. An approved plan's reasonable-efforts commitment, §671(a)(3)'s statewide-operation requirement, and the record's caseworker deficiencies do not supply that missing federal right.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this rule and reverse the §1983 ruling under §671(a)(15).

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every state-plan provision unenforceable or find that administrative funding withdrawal comprehensively forecloses an otherwise existing §1983 right. **Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. **Holding:** Section 671(a)(9)'s requirement that a state plan provide for reporting to the appropriate court or law-enforcement agency when a state agency has reason to believe a child's home or institution is unsuitable because of neglect, abuse, or exploitation, and the child's care is paid for in whole or in part under Title IV-E or IV-B, does not confer on these respondents a private federal right enforceable through §1983 on the claim presented. **Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

- Wilder, 496 U.S. 498 — applies provision-specific examination of a binding individual entitlement; no general rule against enforcement of plan provisions replaces it.

##### Whether §671(a)(9) creates the asserted separate implied action

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9) does not create an implied private action for these respondents to obtain the challenged caseworker relief: its reporting requirement and the Act's enforcement structure do not establish congressional intent to authorize that action. ### Municipal liability and public employment

#### Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1279.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether §1983 adds a freestanding governmental-power-abuse element for municipal employees

**Holding:** A municipal employee who establishes deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and municipal responsibility need not also prove an independent, freestanding abuse-of-governmental-power element. The existence of the constitutional wrong and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; unanimous statutory holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The Fifth Circuit’s additional statutory requirement cannot sustain dismissal merely because the city acted as an employer. Its rejection does not establish the underlying federal right or final municipal attribution; attribution is assumed only to resolve the constitutional question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employment alone does not establish municipal liability, and deficient training does not create a substantive constitutional right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The extra statutory element is rejected, but dismissal of the federal claim is affirmed on the separate constitutional grounds below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658, supplies municipal policy and causation requirements distinct from employment alone. *Canton v. Harris*, 489 U.S. 378, is clarified as an attribution doctrine, not a source of the underlying substantive right. *Oklahoma City v. Tuttle*, 471 U.S. 808, preserves the distinction between constitutional wrong and municipal responsibility.

##### Whether voluntary municipal employment or these pleaded safety omissions establish a substantive-due-process deprivation

**Holding:** The substantive Due Process Clause does not impose an independent general obligation on a municipality to provide its voluntary employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks of harm. Accepting public employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; unanimous duty limitation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The complaint alleges known hazards and deliberate failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers. Those allegations are accepted at dismissal; they do not make voluntary employment a custodial restraint.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not excuse violation of other applicable federal rights. Actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury, a different properly pleaded affirmative-danger claim and procedural due process are not decided. State safety and tort remedies are not adjudicated, and knowing allegations are not reduced to negligence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal substantive-due-process claim. No federal compensation or supervision order is entered; independently available state-law remedies retain their own merits and procedural requirements.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services*, 489 U.S. 189, supplies the absence of a general protective duty while leaving distinct custody and affirmative-deprivation questions. *Youngberg v. Romeo*, 457 U.S. 307, is distinguished because voluntary employment is not involuntary custody. *Daniels v. Williams*, 474 U.S. 327, preserves the distinction between constitutional due process and ordinary tort law without redescribing these allegations as negligence. *Bishop v. Wood*, 426 U.S. 341, rejects a general constitutional guarantee against employment mistakes.

**Independent alternative holding:** The pleaded municipal failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers against known hazards do not constitute arbitrary or conscience-shocking deprivation under substantive due process in this voluntary-employment setting. Even assuming the Texas safety statute creates the asserted duties and a protected liberty interest, breach alone does not establish that distinct substantive constitutional wrong.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part C of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices control this alternative rejection. Stone’s agreement in the judgment does not join Part C.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** No deliberate bodily injury or direction knowingly sending Collins into a dangerous sewer is alleged. The public-program rationale recognizes competing social, political and economic choices as a legal presumption; it is not a finding that the city acted responsibly or that its budget justified these omissions. The alternative independently supports affirmance without deciding state remedies or a different affirmative-injury claim. *Rochin v. California*, 342 U.S. 165, is distinguished from these protective omissions. *Bishop*, 426 U.S. 341, and *Daniels*, 474 U.S. 327, preserve the distinction between state employment or tort duties and substantive constitutional arbitrariness. *Harrah Independent School District v. Martin*, 440 U.S. 194, distinguishes an assumed state-created interest from arbitrary deprivation.

#### Hudson v. McMillian, 503 U.S. 1 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6531.
**Decided:** February 25, 1992.

##### Whether limited injury defeats the established nontrivial malicious beating of a restrained prisoner

**Holding:** A nontrivial beating inflicted maliciously and without disciplinary justification on a restrained convicted prisoner violates the Eighth Amendment even when the resulting physical injuries are limited, where the established conduct satisfies even *Whitley*’s demanding malicious-and-sadistic inquiry. Injury remains relevant evidence and bears on compensation; accidental harm or a merely technical battery is not enough.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt this rule and its application. Blackmun’s concurrence in the judgment is not counted as a join. O’Connor’s Part B, joined by White, Kennedy and Souter, has only four votes for extending *Whitley* to every prison excessive-force claim. No *Marks* aggregation supplies that universal rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Hudson was handcuffed and shackled; the undisturbed findings established unnecessary repeated blows and supervisory condonation. Dental and facial injuries confirmed that the force was not trivial. The findings satisfied even *Whitley*’s heightened culpability inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No comprehensive mental-state standard for every force setting, psychological-injury-only rule, immunity decision, or exhaustive rule for unusual forms of cruelty is adopted. Medical-care, confinement-conditions, legitimate-discipline and compensation rules remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand to the Fifth Circuit for further disposition consistent with the undisturbed findings and without its significant-injury bar. No increase above the existing $800 award, immunity ruling or prison-management injunction is ordered.

**Material judgment:** Reversal and remand, 7–2: Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Whitley v. Albers*, 475 U.S. 312, is applied as a sufficient ground on these findings; its disturbance rule is not extended universally. *Wilson v. Seiter*, 501 U.S. 294, is distinguished: its objective-deprivation requirement does not become a significant-injury floor for this beating. *Estelle v. Gamble*, 429 U.S. 97, supplies the prohibition of unnecessary and wanton pain while retaining the distinct serious-medical-need and deliberate-indifference requirements.

##### Whether an assumed isolated, unauthorized assault sustains affirmance on this record

**Holding:** The undisturbed finding of supervisory condonation and the limits of the granted question prevent affirmance on an assumed characterization of this beating as an isolated, unauthorized assault.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part C of O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six-Justice case-specific rejection of the alternative ground.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court announces no general definition of punishment or general rule governing isolated, unauthorized conduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The alternative characterization supplies no ground for preserving the Fifth Circuit’s judgment; the remand must respect the intact supervisory-condonation finding.

### Psychiatric confinement

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-5844.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### Whether the insanity acquittal continues to authorize psychiatric custody after the State concedes the end of mental illness

**Holding:** When the State concedes that an insanity acquittee is no longer mentally ill, it may not continue his psychiatric confinement under the existing insanity-acquittee authority solely because he cannot prove that he will be harmless. The original acquittal supports neither a suspended criminal sentence nor indefinite psychiatric custody after its illness predicate disappears.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-A, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this proposition and application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The conceded absence of present mental illness is not cured by antisocial personality or a doctor’s inability to guarantee safety. An insanity acquittal is not a conviction authorizing punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Holding:** Once the insanity-acquittal basis for holding Foucha as a mental patient has ended, Louisiana cannot preserve that psychiatric custody without constitutionally adequate proceedings establishing the grounds required for it. If the State invokes ordinary civil commitment, it must establish current mental illness and dangerousness by clear and convincing evidence; additional procedure cannot supply an absent substantive ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: five of nine participating Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground. Stone does not join this section's separate Vitek-based procedural characterization.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. ### School desegregation decrees

#### Freeman v. Pitts, 503 U.S. 467 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 89-1290.
**Decided:** March 31, 1992.

##### When supervision may be withdrawn from completed parts of an unfinished school-desegregation decree

**Holding:** A court may withdraw supervision from a completed part of a school-desegregation decree while retaining jurisdiction and supervision elsewhere only after the district shows full and satisfactory compliance and elimination of the relevant vestiges to the extent practicable. The court must assess demonstrated good faith toward the whole decree and whether retaining control is necessary or practicable to achieve compliance elsewhere; release may not obstruct the remaining remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter: all eight participating Justices support this limited rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

Jurisdiction and active authority over remaining violations continue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Eleventh Circuit’s categorical simultaneous-compliance restriction and remand, 8–0; Thomas did not participate. The court must identify issues properly open, secure findings on whole-decree good faith and remedial connections, and define any released obligations and retained supervision. This Court releases no component, orders no general retrial of settled facts, and finds no final systemwide compliance. Each party bears its own costs.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Green v. School Board of New Kent County, 391 U.S. 430 — applied: the affirmative duty and operational inquiry continue across the unfinished decree.
- Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education, 402 U.S. 1 — applied: equitable flexibility remains tied to effective repair of the violation.
- Board of Education v. Dowell, 498 U.S. 237 — clarified for partial withdrawal: good faith and practicable elimination must be assessed in that setting.
- Milliken v. Bradley, 433 U.S. 267 — applied: restoration of local control remains consistent with completion of the constitutional remedy.
- Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, January 15, 1992 — preserved within its consent-modification scope; it does not replace the Green/Dowell release inquiry.

**Holding:** The district bears the burden of showing that imbalance for which it disclaims remedial responsibility is not traceable to the constitutional violation; genuinely independent demographic change does not require perpetual racial balancing. **Proposition-level authority:** Stone, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter: eight votes; the separate opinions’ disputed factual applications are outside this holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither racial imbalance alone nor demographic movement automatically establishes a violation or completed remedy. Properly settled factual findings receive clear-error review; unexamined causal or remedial links remain open. No majority adopts the separate writings’ disputed factual applications or a revised causation presumption.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Eleventh Circuit’s categorical simultaneous-compliance restriction and remand, 8–0; Thomas did not participate. The court must identify issues properly open, secure findings on whole-decree good faith and remedial connections, and define any released obligations and retained supervision. This Court releases no component, orders no general retrial of settled facts, and finds no final systemwide compliance. Each party bears its own costs.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pasadena Board of Education v. Spangler, 427 U.S. 424 — applied with its independent-cause and completed-remedy limits.
- Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education, 402 U.S. 1 — preserved: the district’s causal showing and violation-linked remedy remain required.
- Board of Education v. Dowell, 498 U.S. 237 — applied: good faith requires demonstrated compliance, not an assurance alone.

### Section 1983 immunity

#### Wyatt v. Cole, 504 U.S. 158 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-126.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### Whether private litigants using state replevin, garnishment or attachment machinery receive Harlow immunity from suit

**Holding:** Private litigants who invoke state replevin, garnishment, or attachment procedures do not receive the objective immunity from suit recognized in Harlow merely because their qualifying joint action may be attributable to the State under §1983. Whether such defendants have a distinct good-faith or probable-cause defense, whether the plaintiff bears additional elements, and the allocation of any such burden remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion for the Court, joined in full by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia and Kennedy: six of nine participating Justices adopt the proposition covering replevin, garnishment and attachment. Stone joins the opinion only as applied to private litigants invoking state replevin machinery, including its stated reservations; seven adopt that narrower proposition. He takes no position on its extension to the other procedures. Souter and Thomas dissent. The six full joins suffice for the broader rule; no fractured-decision analysis is required.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Private commercial use of compulsory process and representation in that use do not establish a public office whose discretionary performance needs immunity from litigation. Attribution under Lugar and eligibility for Harlow immunity remain separate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The availability, elements and burdens of any independent good-faith or probable-cause defense remain open, as do each defendant’s attribution and liability. Public-official immunity, Hunter’s Rule 56 administration and Hafer’s individual-capacity rule remain unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The private-immunity dispositions for Cole and Robbins are reversed, 7–2, and remanded for each defendant’s remaining §1983 predicates and legally available defenses. Attorney status alone settles neither Robbins’s attribution nor immunity. No damages, liability determination or new-trial command issues; official defendants’ dispositions and fee matters outside the grant are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co., 457 U.S. 922 (1982) — answers its reserved immunity question while preserving its two-part attribution inquiry; joint action does not itself establish immunity or either defendant's liability.
- Owen v. City of Independence, 445 U.S. 622 (1980) — applies its historical and functional inquiry; immunity is not assumed for every person reached by §1983.
- Pierson v. Ray, 386 U.S. 547 (1967) — distinguishes its police good-faith protection from automatic private immunity from suit; no private merits defense is adjudicated.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800 (1982) — declines to extend its objective public-official immunity to these private litigants; eligible officials' protection remains unchanged.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511 (1985) — applies its distinction between immunity from litigation and a defense to liability; it does not supply private eligibility.
- Malley v. Briggs, 475 U.S. 335 (1986) — uses its complaining-witness historical analogy without turning analogous tort elements into Harlow immunity.
- Dennis v. Sparks, 449 U.S. 24 (1980) — preserves its refusal to transfer judicial immunity to private joint actors; the independent reliance theory also fails for the reasons stated here.

### Statutory discrimination remedies

#### Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-918.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether compensatory damages are available in the established Title IX private action for intentional discrimination

**Holding:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing that remedy does not create a new cause of action. In Title IX’s established private action, compensatory damages are available for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation and compensable loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the remedial rule and application. Scalia and Thomas concur only in the judgment on a narrower statutory ground.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Franklin’s allegations concern completed sexual harassment and abuse while attending a federally funded school, with alleged official knowledge and inaction. After her departure, backpay and prospective student relief could not compensate the pleaded injury. Congress’s 1986 reference to legal and equitable remedies and subsequent preservation of private enforcement supply no direction barring compensation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Allegations are not findings. No new cause of action, final recipient-attribution rule, punitive remedy, or liability for unintended discriminatory effects is established; the source of Congress’s constitutional power need not be selected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical damages dismissal and remand for ordinary adjudication of liability, attribution, causation and compensable loss. No amount is awarded or prospective school-supervision order entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Cannon v. University of Chicago*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established Title IX action and is not reopened. *Bell v. Hood*, 327 U.S. 678, supplies appropriate relief absent congressional limitation. *Davis v. Passman*, 442 U.S. 228, separates authority to sue from remedy. *J. I. Case Co. v. Borak*, 377 U.S. 426, opposes limiting relief merely because a statute does not enumerate remedies. *Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission*, 463 U.S. 582, supplies intentional-discrimination remedial support without a single unified majority rationale. *Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, supplies analogous monetary-relief support.

##### Whether Spending Clause notice categorically bars compensation for the intentional Title IX violation alleged

**Holding:** The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar compensatory relief for an intentional violation of Title IX’s express prohibition of sex discrimination within its established private action. Ambiguous funding conditions, unintentional effects and the separate requirements for attributing misconduct to a recipient are not resolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the separate funding-notice ground for dismissal.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The enacted nondiscrimination condition and established private action defeat a categorical notice objection to compensation for the pleaded intentional discrimination. This conclusion does not establish when the school is responsible for the teacher’s conduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The notice objection does not sustain dismissal; compensatory relief may be sought on remand subject to proof of the violation and resulting loss.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman*, 451 U.S. 1, retains its fair-notice rule for funding conditions but does not bar relief for the intentional violation alleged. *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, informs the identification of sex-based harassment without importing a Title VII attribution formula. *Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, defeats a categorical monetary-remedy objection based on spending legislation. *Cannon*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established enforcement premise.

### Voting Rights Act preclearance

#### Presley v. Etowah County Commission; Mack v. Russell County Commission, 502 U.S. 491 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-711 and 90-712.
**Decided:** January 27, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Presley concerns the Common Fund Resolution in No. 90-711; Mack concerns the Unit System in No. 90-712. One opinion supplies the same controlling grounds for both appealed noncoverage judgments.

##### Whether the Common Fund Resolution and Unit System require §5 preclearance

**Holding:** Section 5 does not require preclearance of these reallocations of governing authority because they do not alter voting procedures, candidacy, the electorate, or the method of selecting the relevant offices and have no direct relation to voting. This ruling does not resolve whether an extreme transfer could effectively replace an elective office with an appointive one, nor does it exempt a change merely because it occurs after an election.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support this proposition in both appeals.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The Common Fund Resolution replaces individual district road-fund authority with collective commission control. The Unit System places road operations under an appointed engineer while retaining substantial authority, including appointment and budget powers, in the elected commission. Neither changes the relevant selection mechanism or electorate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The listed electoral categories are illustrative, not exhaustive. Effective replacement of an elective office with an appointive office remains unresolved. The Court decides no racial-purpose or discriminatory-effect question, §2 claim, Title VI claim, or constitutional claim; the unappealed Road Supervision ruling is undisturbed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both appealed noncoverage judgments are affirmed. Neither challenged resolution requires §5 preclearance or is enjoined under §5 on these grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Allen v. State Board of Elections*, 393 U.S. 544 — broad coverage of changes affecting electoral choice remains; elective-to-appointive selection is distinguished from these retained elected offices.
- *Perkins v. Matthews*, 400 U.S. 379 — changes to the electorate and voting arrangements remain covered; coverage remains distinct from discrimination merits.
- *Dougherty County Board of Education v. White*, 439 U.S. 32 — candidacy restrictions directly related to electoral choice remain covered.
- *McCain v. Lybrand*, 465 U.S. 236, and *City of Lockhart v. United States*, 460 U.S. 125 — changes in electoral arrangements remain within §5; neither decides ordinary internal governing duties.
- *NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission*, 470 U.S. 166 — practical changes to election timing or procedure require proper review; postelection timing alone supplies no exemption here.

##### Whether the Attorney General's interpretation permits coverage without the statutory connection to voting

**Holding:** The Attorney General's interpretation of §5 is entitled to substantial consideration but cannot extend preclearance to governing changes that lack the statutory connection to voting. The proposed significant-transfer/different-constituencies test cannot substitute for that connection in these appeals.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support this proposition in both appeals.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The rejected test concerns significant transfers of authority between officials responsible to substantially different constituencies. That formulation can reach governing responsibilities without identifying any change in voting and therefore cannot substitute for the required connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The distinction between §5 coverage and review of discriminatory effect remains intact. Administrative expertise retains substantial weight within the statutory voting boundary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Board of Commissioners of Sheffield*, 435 U.S. 110 — administrative interpretation retains substantial weight within the statutory voting boundary.
- *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837 — preserved; deference does not authorize exceeding the statute's scope.
- *Beer v. United States*, 425 U.S. 130 — coverage and discriminatory-effect review remain separate inquiries.
- *South Carolina v. Katzenbach*, 383 U.S. 301 — §5's remedial function is preserved; it does not by itself establish coverage of every governing change.

## First Amendment

### Association evidence at capital sentencing

#### Dawson v. Delaware, 503 U.S. 159 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6704.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### When protected association may aggravate a capital sentence

**Holding:** The State may not use protected association to aggravate a capital sentence when the evidence establishes only abstract beliefs and supplies no supported connection to the crime, future danger, another legitimate sentencing issue, or the mitigation actually offered. The stipulated association evidence here lacks that connection and its admission violates the First and Fourteenth Amendments.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of White’s opinion; Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter join White at this level of generality.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal bar excludes association evidence tied to witness bias, demonstrated racial motive, future danger, another legitimate sentencing issue, or actual mitigation. A white victim does not categorically exclude racial motive in another case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining the death sentence is vacated and remanded, 8–1. Delaware may consider its harmless-error contention consistently with the constitutional relevance ruling; the Court neither finds harmlessness nor adopts a mandatory Chapman/new-sentencing sequence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- NAACP v. Alabama, 357 U.S. 449 — applied: freedom of association protects unpopular affiliations.
- Bates v. Little Rock, 361 U.S. 516 — applied: protected association cannot itself justify adverse governmental treatment.
- United States v. Abel, 469 U.S. 45 — distinguished on the facts: association established witness bias there; relevant association remains admissible.
- Barclay v. Florida, 463 U.S. 939 — distinguished: demonstrated racial motivation connected the association to the crime.
- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 — applied: protected beliefs cannot themselves supply an aggravating circumstance.
- Payne v. Tennessee, 501 U.S. 808 — applied with its relevance limit: rebuttal of mitigation does not make unrelated belief probative.
- Schware v. Board of Bar Examiners, 353 U.S. 232 — applied by analogy: an affiliation without a rational connection to the relevant character inquiry is insufficient.

##### Whether harmless-error review is available and satisfied for this association-evidence error

**Holding:** The state judgment sustaining this sentence cannot stand on the constitutional reasoning it adopted. On remand the state court may consider Delaware’s harmless-error contention; this Court decides neither whether harmless-error analysis is available for this violation nor whether the error was harmless.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of White’s opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the remand’s reserved scope.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither availability nor satisfaction of harmless-error review and does not command new sentencing automatically. The convictions remain intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining the death sentence is vacated and remanded, 8–1. Delaware may consider its harmless-error contention consistently with the constitutional relevance ruling; the Court neither finds harmlessness nor adopts a mandatory Chapman/new-sentencing sequence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — preserved: its constitutional harmless-error burden is not relaxed; applicability and satisfaction in this case remain undecided.

### Compulsory bar dues

#### Gibson v. Florida Bar, 502 U.S. 104 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1102.  
**Decided:** December 4, 1991.

##### When a continuing general objector's disputed compulsory bar dues must be protected

**Holding:** Once a compulsory-bar member makes a continuing objection to financing nongermane ideological activity, the bar may not require repeated objections to each announced position or leave the reasonably disputed share available for use until each new objection arrives. The bar must provide adequate advance explanation and protect the disputed share before use through a reasonable reduction or adequate interest-bearing escrow; it retains the burden to justify chargeability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight of nine participants.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Annual collection preceded twice-monthly lobbying announcements, and protection began only after position-specific objections. Budget estimates and protection of a reasonably sufficient amount can address uncertainty without exact advance prediction; repeated 45-day waivers cannot shift the monitoring burden back to a known dissenter.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Compulsory support for activities germane to professional regulation and improvement of legal services remains permissible. No universal opt-in requirement is adopted. Accounting details and injunction terms are left for remand; an unpreserved retroactive-refund request is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The timing and repeated-objection components are reversed and remanded for prospective reduction or escrow protection. The correction requiring interest from dues collection remains intact; no retroactive damages are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Keller applies compelled-dues safeguards to integrated bars. Hudson requires explanation, prompt review, and protection of disputed funds. Ellis rejects later repayment with interest as a cure for temporary ideological use. Abood permits requiring an objection, but after opposition is known places the chargeability burden on the organization.

##### Whether Bar membership alone makes the prescribed balanced arbitration panel constitutionally biased

**Holding:** Membership in the compulsory bar alone does not establish constitutional bias in every arbitrator selected through a procedure giving the bar and objector one selection each and requiring a mutually selected or judicially appointed chair. Actual bias, a direct stake, or obstructive delay remains subject to review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion joined on this proposition by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; all nine Justices.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Neither party controls the chair, and judicial appointment is available if agreement fails. The shared indirect incidence of bar dues does not itself establish a direct stake in a particular award.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling does not approve an actually biased panel, indefinite delay in constituting it, or a procedure ineffective in operation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The rejection of the facial panel-membership challenge is affirmed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hudson's demand for reasonably prompt impartial review is applied; Bar membership alone does not defeat that requirement under this selection arrangement.

### Content-based financial burdens

#### Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, 502 U.S. 105 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1059.  
**Decided:** December 10, 1991.

##### Whether New York's crime-related-expression escrow survives scrutiny of a content-based financial burden

**Holding:** A law selecting crime-related expression for a special financial escrow is content based and must be necessary and narrowly drawn to serve a compelling interest. Section 632-a fails because it burdens an entire covered contract, including incidental discussion and unprosecuted admissions, while leaving other assets outside its special reach; it therefore may not be enforced against this publishing contract.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter; six of eight participants. Blackmun's and Kennedy's judgment-only concurrences are not counted as joins in this rationale.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The payment restraint depends on the work's content and affects access to protected expression without requiring a purpose to suppress ideas. Excluding victimless crimes and releasing limited production or legal expenses do not cure the statute's breadth.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides this statute, leaving materially different laws open. The decision does not protect stolen property, discharge victim or creditor claims, or immunize publishing income from neutral restitution, forfeiture, attachment, judgments, and collection rules.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit is reversed, and the case returns for appropriate declaratory and injunctive relief against applying §632-a to the contract. No payment to Hill is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arkansas Writers' Project supplies scrutiny of content-based differential financial burdens. Leathers distinguishes general financial regulation from selective burdens keyed to expression. Caplin & Drysdale is distinguished because criminal proceeds differ from lawful compensation for discussing crime. Gibson's compelled-bar-financing rule does not replace this scrutiny.

### Electioneering on public sidewalks

#### Burson v. Freeman, 504 U.S. 191 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1056.
**Decided:** May 26, 1992.

##### What scrutiny governs the campaign-specific restriction on ordinary streets and sidewalks within the polling-place boundary

**Holding:** Tennessee's campaign-specific prohibition is a content-based restriction on political speech, and ordinary public sidewalks and streets within the 100-foot boundary remain public forums. The State must show that this restriction is necessary to serve a compelling interest and narrowly drawn to achieve that end; viewpoint neutrality and the availability of speech beyond the boundary do not by themselves lower that scrutiny.

**Proposition-level authority:** Seven participating Justices expressly adopt this governing proposition at the same level of generality: Blackmun's plurality, joined by Stone, White and Kennedy, and Stevens's dissent, joined by O'Connor and Souter. It is an independently majority-supported proposition, not a seven-Justice judgment coalition or an Opinion of the Court. Scalia rejects the forum classification and applies a different standard.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Viewpoint neutrality and opportunities to speak beyond the boundary do not make campaign-specific content discrimination neutral. The Justices applying strict scrutiny disagree about necessity and tailoring in this facial challenge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The majority-supported scrutiny rule supplies no shared validity rationale or numerical safe harbor. The separate 300-foot provisions, larger zones, periods outside voting, unrelated political speech, discriminatory enforcement, incidental bumper stickers, unusual highway applications and unreviewed state constitutional grounds remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment invalidating the 100-foot restriction is reversed and remanded, 5–3, by Stone, White, Blackmun, Scalia and Kennedy. The governing scrutiny proposition has a different, seven-Justice coalition; Thomas takes no part.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Ass'n v. Perry Local Educators' Ass'n, 460 U.S. 37 (1983) — applies its traditional-public-forum and content-based scrutiny framework to the ordinary streets and sidewalks reached here.
- United States v. Grace, 461 U.S. 171 (1983) — applies its protection of ordinary public sidewalks; government-building proximity does not alone remove that status.
- Boos v. Barry, 485 U.S. 312 (1988) — applies strict scrutiny to political subject discrimination; a claim of regulatory purpose does not automatically make this campaign restriction content neutral.
- Mills v. Alabama, 384 U.S. 214 (1966) — preserves its protection of general election-day advocacy and its reservation concerning conduct in and around polls; it does not automatically settle this boundary's validity.
- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991, simulated decision — preserves exacting review of content-based burdens; this case's divided application creates no general exception to that rule.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989) — distinguishes its content-neutral time, place and manner standard; it does not replace strict scrutiny here.

##### Whether this 100-foot restriction survives the facial challenge

**Authority of the judgment:** The judgment invalidating the restriction is reversed and remanded, 5–3: Stone, White, Blackmun, Scalia and Kennedy support reversal; Stevens, O’Connor and Souter oppose it. Thomas takes no part. No controlling validity rationale exists under *Marks*: Blackmun’s plurality, joined by Stone, White and Kennedy, treats the place as a public forum and finds strict scrutiny satisfied; Scalia treats the polling environs during voting as a nonpublic forum subject to reasonableness and viewpoint neutrality. The different forum predicates are not established logical subsets, and Scalia does not adopt the plurality’s necessity or tailoring conclusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment binds this facial challenge to the 100-foot boundary during voting. Neither the four-Justice application nor Scalia’s nonpublic-forum theory becomes a controlling exception. The separately majority-supported scrutiny rule appears above. The separate 300-foot provisions, larger zones, periods outside voting, unrelated speech, discriminatory enforcement, incidental bumper stickers, unusual highway applications and unreviewed state constitutional grounds remain undecided.

## Election Law

### Ballot access and political parties

#### Norman v. Reed, 502 U.S. 279 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1126 and 90-1435.
**Decided:** January 14, 1992.

##### Whether Illinois may prohibit authorized party-name expansion or invalidate qualified slate components for another district's signature deficiency

**Holding:** Illinois may require meaningful support within each separate electorate, but it may not categorically prevent authorized candidates from expanding an existing party under its name or disqualify otherwise qualified countywide and city candidates solely because candidates in a different district lack sufficient signatures.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, and Kennedy; these seven Justices supply a majority of the eight participants.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Protection against unauthorized appropriation of a party name did not justify a geographic bar that also prohibited authorized expansion. The suburban candidates' insufficient support justified their exclusion, but did not justify disqualifying countywide and city candidates who met their own constituencies' requirements.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Other district structures, cumulative burdens, and actual unauthorized name use remain open. The unaddressed state-law judicial-candidate objection is left for authoritative state-court construction; the unappealed water-district candidates are not revived, and no new election is ordered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded. The suburban exclusion is retained; name-based and whole-slate exclusions are reversed. The unresolved judicial-candidate objection is returned to the Illinois Supreme Court.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Anderson supplies examination of the burden, the State's precise interests, and the restriction's necessity. Socialist Workers Party rejects an inadequately justified local signature burden more onerous than the statewide route. Jenness is applied and preserved. Bacchus Imports supports returning the consequential unresolved state-law issue for authoritative construction.

For the election-mootness holding in Norman v. Reed, decided January 14, 1992, Nos. 90-1126 and 90-1435, see Federal Courts, “Mootness and election disputes.”

#### Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-535.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

##### What level of constitutional scrutiny applies to a restriction on voting or candidate access

**Holding:** A court must weigh the character and magnitude of the injury to First and Fourteenth Amendment rights against the State's precise asserted interests and the extent to which they require the burden. Severe restrictions must be narrowly drawn to advance a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily are justified by important regulatory interests, so strict scrutiny does not govern every election rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices control the complete rule. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone's dissent also accept the calibrated framework without making their contrary application controlling.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** A write-in prohibition must be examined with the available routes to ballot access and its role in the selection system. Neither the prohibition alone nor the general need for election administration decides the burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary administration remains subject to meaningful scrutiny. The holding neither imposes strict scrutiny on all ballot regulations nor extends *Burson*'s public-sidewalk campaign-content holding to the official ballot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, supplies the burden, precise-interest and necessity inquiry. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, preserves actual access review and permissible electorate-specific support requirements, including its protection against cross-component exclusion. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, recognizes substantial regulation of candidate selection while preserving review of severe barriers. *Burson v. Freeman*, May 26, 1992, concerns ordinary public sidewalks; neither its strict-scrutiny proposition nor its fractured judgment changes this ballot analysis.

##### Whether Hawaii's complete write-in prohibition is valid within the ballot-access system reviewed

**Holding:** Where the complete ballot-access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a write-in prohibition is presumptively valid and may be sustained by proportionate state interests. Hawaii's party and nonpartisan routes provide adequate access, and its prohibition validly supports primary finality, prevention of sore-loser candidacies and party raiding, and its treatment of uncontested primaries.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support both the rationale and affirmance. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy disagree with this application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the complete access system reviewed and its reasonable burdens.

**Material application:** New parties need one percent of registered-voter signatures 150 days before the primary; qualifying established parties retain access. Candidates need 15 signatures for legislative or county offices, or 25 for statewide or federal offices, 60 days before the primary. Nonpartisan candidates advance with ten percent of the primary vote or the votes sufficient to nominate a partisan candidate, whichever is lower; voters choose one party or nonpartisan primary ballot for all offices. These combined routes and restrictions support the limited-burden finding. Lawful selection deadlines need not accommodate every later preference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both primary and general-election applications within this system are reached. The Court does not independently validate every surrounding access rule or decide a prohibition within materially more restrictive access rules. Candidate qualifications and reasonable write-in procedures elsewhere remain open; no absolute write-in right or guarantee of competitive races is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit; its reversal of the district court's injunction remains effective. No prospective injunction or replacement election-administration scheme issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Jenness v. Fortson*, 403 U.S. 431, permits reasonable demonstrations of support. *Munro v. Socialist Workers Party*, 479 U.S. 189, permits primary winnowing and preventive regulation without proof of a particular past disruption. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supports primary finality and restrictions against circumvention. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, governs this limited-burden application; the decision creates no exception to its inquiry. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, retains protection against unjustified party-name and cross-component disqualification.

##### Whether the Constitution requires the State to count and publish fictional candidates or arbitrary protest messages

**Holding:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments do not require a State to count and publicize write-in votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent form of expression. Elections select public officials, and a desire to use the ballot for such messages does not create an entitlement to a write-in option.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas, controls. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone's dissent expressly agree on rejection of this broader claim; all nine support the component without issuing a joint opinion.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A genuine interest in supporting an actual alternative candidate requires the contextual access analysis separately stated above. Political expression outside the ballot and every restriction on eligible candidates in a different system are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the arbitrary-message tally claim; it supplies no independent ground for relief from the prohibition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supplies the office-selecting function of elections; the ballot is not a general expressive forum. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, protects electoral participation without requiring an arbitrary-message tally.

### Interstate congressional apportionment

#### United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, 503 U.S. 442 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-860.
**Decided:** March 31, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *United States Department of Commerce v. Montana*, March 31, 1992, No. 91-860: whether congress’s equal-proportions apportionment choice is judicially reviewable (Federal Courts — Political questions and constitutional review); whether count i reversal disposes of the distinct automatic-apportionment claim in count ii (Federal Courts — Scope of appellate disposition); whether count i presents a continuing representation injury redressable by review of the allocation formula (Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury).

##### Whether equal proportions satisfies the presented interstate population-equality challenge

**Holding:** In allocating the statutorily fixed House among States using whole Representatives and respecting each State's constitutional minimum, Article I, §2 and the Fourteenth Amendment permit the equal-proportions method's population-based choice of relative representational equality. **Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The House’s size of 435 is statutory, not constitutional. Wesberry and Karcher’s intrastate equality and justification rules remain; no percentage tolerance, new burden sequence, uniquely required method, or approval of every population-based method is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse Count I’s judgment, remove its declaration of invalidity and dissolve its permanent injunction, and direct judgment for the Government on Count I only. Remand Count II without deciding it. The allocation remains operative subject to further lawful order; no seat transfer, new enactment or presidential command is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wesberry, 376 U.S. 1 — preserves its intrastate equal-vote holding while distinguishing allocation of indivisible seats among States; its historical explanation does not select Montana's interstate metric.
- Karcher v. Daggett, 462 U.S. 725 (1983) — distinguishes its intrastate line-drawing problem; its good-faith and specific-justification requirements remain unchanged, with no numerical safe harbor imported here.

## Administrative Law

### Administrative fee awards

#### Ardestani v. INS, 502 U.S. 129 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1141.  
**Decided:** December 10, 1991.

##### Whether an INA deportation hearing is an adjudication under section 554 for administrative EAJA fees

### Federal permit interpretation and review

#### Arkansas v. Oklahoma, 503 U.S. 91 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1262 and 90-1266.

##### How EPA’s incorporated downstream standards govern this federal permit

**Holding:** For this interstate federal permit, EPA’s valid regulation incorporates the applicable, federally approved downstream standards into federal permitting law, and its reasonable, consistently held construction receives substantial deference. **Material application:** The standards were developed with federal guidance, approved by EPA and incorporated through 40 C.F.R. §122.4(d). **Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not decide state law for unrelated purposes, excuse unreliable scientific findings, authorize disregard of a demonstrated detectable violation, or create a general exemption for pollution called trivial. General agency-deference doctrine is not revised.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reasonable construction supports sustaining the permit in both dockets under its existing compliance, study and modification conditions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837, applies to this authorized construction. *Bowles v. **Holding:** A reviewing court must accept agency adjudicative findings supported by substantial evidence on the record as a whole and may not replace them merely because competing findings also could be supported. **Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; unanimous appellate-review holding and application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse in both dockets and sustain the supported permit determination, preserving the existing study and modification conditions as safeguards for further information.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Universal Camera Corp. v. ##### Whether politically motivated coercive recruitment necessarily establishes persecution on account of the recruit's political opinion

**Holding:** Coercive recruitment by a politically motivated organization does not necessarily constitute persecution on account of the recruit's political opinion; the protected reason for targeting the applicant must be supported by evidence concerning the particular case. **Holding:** Under §1105a(a)(4), a court may overturn the Board's supported factual determination only when the record compels the contrary finding, not merely because that finding could be supported. **Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support both the evidentiary conclusion and the operative restoration of the Board's denial. Stone does not join this holding or its remedy.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The nexus-based review here does not establish one review standard for every prima-facie determination or every reopening denial.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit must implement reversal and leave the Board's denial effective without the additional nexus or reopening review proposed by Stone. No asylum or withholding is awarded.

**Material judgment:** Six Justices—Stone, White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—support reversal of the categorical disposition; Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor would affirm. ## Federal Indian Law

### State taxation of reservation fee land

#### County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes and Bands of the Yakima Indian Nation, 502 U.S. 251 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-408 and 90-577.
**Decided:** January 14, 1992.

##### Whether state ad valorem taxation may reach reservation land patented in fee under the General Allotment Act

**Holding:** The General Allotment Act's established fee-land rule and the Burke Act proviso authorize state ad valorem taxation of reservation land validly patented in fee under that Act, including qualifying land later held by the Nation. The tax follows the land, but patent source and independently applicable federal protection must be determined parcel by parcel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; these eight Justices support the ad valorem holding. Blackmun dissents on this component.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The stipulated record did not establish every parcel's patent source. The Indian Reorganization Act did not automatically restore every existing fee parcel to tax-protected status, and the specific land-tax authority did not create general personal taxing jurisdiction over reservation Indians.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns qualifying General Allotment Act fee land and preserves independently applicable federal protections. It does not rest on alienability alone or authorize a governmental-impact inquiry under Brendale.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is affirmed and the case remanded with modified instructions for parcel-specific patent-source analysis and relief consistent with the two distinct tax holdings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Goudy is reaffirmed for taxation of qualifying General Allotment Act fee land. Blackfeet supplies the requirement of unmistakably clear congressional authorization. Mancari requires compatible enactments to operate together. Moe's rejection of general personal taxing jurisdiction is preserved. Brendale's fractured analysis of tribal regulation of nonmember land supplies no governmental-impact test for this statutory tax question.

##### Whether permission to tax the land also authorizes a sales excise imposed on an Indian owner

**Holding:** Congressional permission to tax qualifying fee land ad valorem does not unmistakably authorize a tax imposed on an Indian owner's sale of that land. Securing the transactional tax with a lien does not change its legal incidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion states this proposition for Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; all nine Justices support the sales-excise holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The annual property levy is triggered by ownership and burdens the realty; the sales excise is triggered by a transaction. A collection lien cannot enlarge Congress's permission by transforming an unauthorized transactional charge into a land tax.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sales excise remains prohibited, and remand relief must respect that prohibition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Blackfeet requires authorization clear at the level of the tax actually imposed. Capoeman's allotment-context discussion of taxation after fee patent does not supply blanket permission for every tax related to land.

### ERISA employee classification

#### Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1802.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### Which meaning of employee governs ERISA’s circular definition

**Holding:** ERISA’s circular definition of employee incorporates the general common law of agency, not a particular State’s law or a substitute test of benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship—including control over manner and means, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, regular-business integration, the hiring party’s business, benefits and tax treatment—with no single factor decisive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court, supported by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine Justices adopt the agency construction and multifactor inquiry.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A contractual label or single factor is not decisive. Benefits do not replace classification. The FLSA’s broader express definition remains distinct; Darden’s status, vesting, forfeiture, benefits and independent state-contract remedies are not decided. The denied Extended Earnings Plan cross-petition is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and remanded for application of all relevant common-law agency incidents and further proceedings consistent with the resulting classification; no employee classification or benefit award is made here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Community for Creative Non-Violence v. Reid, 490 U.S. 730 — applied to ERISA: general common-law agency is the default meaning of an unhelpfully defined employee term.
- NLRB v. United Insurance Co., 390 U.S. 254 — applied: all incidents must be assessed with no single decisive factor.
- NLRB v. Hearst Publications, 322 U.S. 111 — limited as a general interpretive model: purpose alone does not displace agency meaning in ERISA.
- United States v. Silk, 331 U.S. 704 — its general purposive emphasis is not followed for undefined employee terms; Reid supplies the governing presumption.
- Rutherford Food Corp. v. McComb, 331 U.S. 722 — distinguished and preserved: FLSA’s broader express definition supports its different reach.
- Kelley v. Southern Pacific, 419 U.S. 318; Baker v. Texas & Pacific, 359 U.S. 227; Robinson v. Baltimore & Ohio, 237 U.S. 84 — retained as supporting applications of ordinary agency meaning.

### Nonemployee organizing access

#### Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, 502 U.S. 527 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-970.
**Decided:** January 27, 1992.

##### When nonemployee organizers may obtain access despite nondiscriminatory exclusion from private property

**Holding:** For a nondiscriminatory exclusion policy, nonemployee organizers must show that employees are practically beyond reasonable offsite communication before the Board may balance a need for entry against the employer's property rights; greater convenience or organizing success does not satisfy that threshold. This workforce's community residence, public approaches and substantial direct contacts defeat the asserted exception, while discriminatory exclusion, employees' own solicitation and genuinely inaccessible workforces remain governed by their distinct rules.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices control both the statutory rule and denial of enforcement.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Employees lived in the surrounding community; organizers used public approaches and obtained direct home-contact information for roughly one fifth of the workforce. Few authorization cards did not establish an inability to communicate. Board expertise remains relevant to actual access obstacles, within the judicially established statutory rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court requires neither organizational success nor exhaustion of every imaginable communication technique. Employees' own solicitation, discriminatory exclusion, lawful public-property advocacy, and genuinely inaccessible workforces retain their separate treatment. No new general deference doctrine or constitutional right of entry is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The First Circuit judgment is reversed and enforcement denied. The Board's order is set aside insofar as it requires nonemployee-organizer access on these facts; no remand for a new balancing exercise is ordered, and the judgment permits no interference with employees' distinct organizing rights or lawful public activity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *NLRB v. Babcock & Wilcox Co.*, 351 U.S. 105 — applied and reaffirmed: employee and nonemployee activity differ, and demonstrated inaccessibility precedes balancing under a nondiscriminatory rule.
- *Republic Aviation Corp. v. NLRB*, 324 U.S. 793 — employees' own organizing rights remain distinct and are not narrowed here.
- *Central Hardware Co. v. NLRB*, 407 U.S. 539 — applied for the prior showing of a need for nonemployee access.
- *Hudgens v. NLRB*, 424 U.S. 507 — accommodation language is preserved within Babcock's nonemployee threshold, not treated as authorization for universal balancing.
- *Sears, Roebuck & Co. v. Carpenters*, 436 U.S. 180 — applied for the heavy burden concerning alternative access and the separate discrimination route.
- *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837, and *Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc.*, 497 U.S. 116 — general deference remains; a later agency approach must respect a governing judicial statutory construction.
- *Jean Country*, 291 N.L.R.B. 11 — rejected insofar as its universal balancing replaces the antecedent inaccessibility inquiry for nondiscriminatory exclusion of nonemployee organizers; other contexts are not adjudicated.

### Exemption objections

#### Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz, 503 U.S. 638 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-571.
**Decided:** April 21, 1992.

##### Whether an untimely objection may defeat a scheduled exemption lacking a colorable statutory basis

**Holding:** Under 11 U.S.C. §522(l) and the applicable Bankruptcy Rule 4003(b), property claimed as exempt becomes exempt when no party in interest objects within 30 days after the creditors' meeting concludes or a relevant amendment to the exemption list is filed, whichever is later, and the court grants no further time within that period. The trustee cannot later contest the claimed exemption merely because it lacked a colorable statutory basis; this holding concerns the scheduled proceeds claimed here and does not decide a different claim's scope, an unscheduled asset, or an unpreserved fraud or §105(a) theory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion for the Court, joined in full by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Eight participating Justices adopt the rule and its explanation at the same level of generality; no fractured-opinion analysis is needed.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Taylor knew of the lawsuit and its potentially substantial proceeds. Uncertainty about value allowed timely inquiry, objection or a request for extension; it did not suspend the deadline until collection. Rule 9006(b)(3) confines enlargement to Rule 4003(b)’s conditions; the August 1, 1991 supplemental-schedule wording does not reopen the expired period.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide §105(a), unpreserved fraud, unscheduled property, differently limited exemption claims, retroactivity, alternative valuation, liens or good faith. Rule 4003(c)’s burden on the objector remains applicable in a properly presented objection proceeding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit is affirmed. Its reversal of the $23,483.75-plus-interest turnover order stands; the scheduled proceeds remain exempt against this untimely theory. No alternative recovery, sanction or new award is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S. 235 — applied: a coherent Bankruptcy Code provision is enforced according to its terms; no general power to replace explicit procedure with an equitable exception follows from bankruptcy administration.
- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, March 9, 1992 — consistent with its text/coexistence rule: substantive exemption eligibility and the procedure for contesting a claim perform different work. Its separate bankruptcy appellate-jurisdiction holding remains unchanged.
- In re Dembs, 757 F.2d 777, and In re Peterson, 920 F.2d 1389 — rejected insofar as their colorability requirement permits a late objection of this kind; that requirement cannot govern the §522(l) consequence decided here.

### Ordinary-course preference exception

#### Union Bank v. Wolas, 502 U.S. 151 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1491.  
**Decided:** December 11, 1991.

##### Whether payments on long-term debt are eligible for the contemporaneous section 547(c)(2) ordinary-course exception

**Holding:** Payments on long-term debt may qualify for the then-current §547(c)(2) ordinary-course exception; debt maturity is not an additional categorical exclusion. Under §547(g), the creditor must prove that the debt was incurred in the ordinary course of both parties, that payment was made in their ordinary course, and that payment accorded with ordinary business terms.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion states this statutory proposition for Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; all nine Justices. Scalia's separate writing agrees with this holding and judgment.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Congress removed the former 45-day restriction while retaining the three statutory requirements. Eligibility alone does not establish that the particular interest and commitment-fee payments satisfy them.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No presumption of ordinariness attaches to long-term payments. The decision does not categorically exempt interest or commitment fees or decide challenges the maturity ruling bypassed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for review of remaining challenges to the statutory elements. Judgment for the Bank is not itself restored.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises supplies adherence to operative Code text unless demonstrably at odds with congressional intent. The Ninth Circuit's categorical CHG International construction is rejected; no Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

### Preference transfer timing

#### Barnhill v. Johnson, 503 U.S. 393 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-159.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

##### When an ordinary check transfers the debtor’s account interest for §547(b)’s preference period

**Holding:** For §547(b), an ordinary check transfers the debtor’s relevant bank-account interest when the drawee honors it, because delivery gives the payee no assigned interest in that account. The November 20 honor date therefore satisfies the ninety-day timing element here, without deciding other preference elements or defenses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt this proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

Neither timing under §547(c) nor nonordinary payment instruments is decided. Section 547(e) does not relate the transfer back before an interest in the account passed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit’s honor-date judgment is affirmed. November 20, the honor date, falls within the ninety-day period; the decision does not automatically avoid every check honored within that period.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

## Federal Taxation

### Bankruptcy fiduciaries’ filing and payment duties

#### Holywell Corp. v. Smith, 503 U.S. 47 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1361 and 90-1484.
**Decided:** February 25, 1992.
**Companion or consolidated matters:** *United States v. Smith*, No. 90-1484, consolidated with *Holywell*; both challenge the declaration of no filing or payment duty.

##### Whether the assignee of substantially all corporate property must file corporate returns and pay the associated tax

**Holding:** An assignee holding all or substantially all of a corporation’s property must make its income-tax return under §6012(b)(3), whether or not the corporation dissolves or the assignee continues its business. Smith’s receipt of the corporate estate property satisfies that category, and §6151(a) requires payment of the tax due with those returns.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; unanimous statutory construction.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The plan assigned substantially all corporate property to Smith for liquidation and distribution. The filing duty follows the statutory assignee category and does not depend on operating the business or dissolving the corporations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No alternative statutory classification, exact income, deduction, penalty or tax amount is decided. The duties concern returns and accompanying payment after appointment, not taxes already due before appointment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand in both dockets for Smith to file the prescribed corporate returns as assignee and pay the tax due with them under §6151; amounts and implementation are left to ordinary tax and bankruptcy procedures.

##### Whether Smith must file and pay as fiduciary of the separate liquidating trust holding Gould’s former estate property

**Holding:** Smith must file the separate liquidating trust’s return under §6012(b)(4) and pay the associated tax under §6151: the plan vested Gould’s estate property directly in a separate trust organized to liquidate and distribute the transferred assets and authorized Smith to perform those functions, making him its fiduciary despite limits on his discretion. Because the property did not first revest in Gould for contribution to the trust, the asserted grantor-trust theory does not displace Smith’s duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; unanimous trust-specific construction and application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The plan created a separate trust to liquidate and distribute transferred assets within Treasury Regulation §301.7701-4(d); Smith’s powers satisfy §7701(a)(6) and Regulation §301.7701-6 despite limits on his discretion. The estate transferred Gould’s property directly to the trust; Gould did not first recover and contribute it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal tax treatment for all liquidating trusts is adopted. Grantor provisions retain their operation when their predicates exist; the result of a different revesting-and-contribution arrangement is not decided. The Court computes no tax amount.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand in both dockets for Smith to file as trust fiduciary and pay the associated tax, with computation and implementation left to ordinary procedures.

##### Whether confirmation of a plan silent about taxes and the United States’ failure to object excuse later statutory duties

**Holding:** Confirmation under §1141(a) of a plan silent about taxes, coupled with the United States’ failure to object, does not excuse the statutory return and payment obligations imposed on this assignee and trust fiduciary after appointment. The binding treatment of properly encompassed preconfirmation claims does not establish an exemption from these later obligations.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; unanimous rejection of the confirmation defense.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The government seeks recognition of Smith’s postappointment duties, not payment from him of taxes already due before appointment. The plan neither stated nor adjudicated an exemption; §6151 connects payment to the required return’s due date.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes, without deciding more broadly, that confirmation can bind creditors concerning properly encompassed preconfirmation claims. Preappointment taxes and express adjudication of existing tax claims remain outside the disposition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The no-duty declaration is reversed in both dockets; confirmation supplies no defense to implementation of the postappointment filing and payment obligations.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Davis v. Michigan Department of Treasury, 489 U.S. 803 — applied: retirement income receives §111 protection and a tax-relevant significant difference must justify unequal treatment.
- Phillips Chemical Co. v. Dumas Independent School District, 361 U.S. 376 — applied through Davis: the difference must directly relate to and justify the disparity.
- United States v. Tyler, 105 U.S. 244 — confined to its statutory pay-increase context; continuing military status does not establish current compensation for all purposes.
- McCarty v. McCarty, 453 U.S. 210 — distinguished: it reserved the characterization question and its community-property prohibition was displaced by the Uniformed Services Former Spouses’ Protection Act; no surviving rule authorizes this tax disparity.

### National Firearms Act making tax

#### United States v. Thompson/Center Arms Co., 504 U.S. 505 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-164.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

##### Whether the genuine unassembled dual-use Contender package incurs the short-barreled-rifle making tax

**Holding:** The National Firearms Act does not clearly treat the segregated Contender pistol and conversion package, which has genuine lawful pistol and long-rifle uses and has not been assembled as a short-barreled rifle, as making that regulated firearm. Because the same definition carries criminal consequences, the remaining ambiguity is resolved for the manufacturer in this civil refund action as well.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's plurality opinion joined by O'Connor, Scalia's concurrence joined by Thomas, and Stone's concurrence expressly share this case-specific construction and uniform residual lenity. These five form an actual majority at this level of generality; no complete opinion controls and no synthetic *Marks* rationale combines their different accounts of the ambiguity.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the identified genuine dual-use package and the shared residual-lenity proposition.

**Material application:** Section 5845's rifle definition addresses design, making and intended shoulder firing, while other firearm definitions expressly address combinations of parts. The language and related definitions leave ambiguity for this package's lawful pistol and long-rifle configurations alongside its regulated short-rifle capability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No complete opinion controls the broader division between permissible and regulated combinations. Actual short-rifle assembly, pure regulated-firearm kits, sham lawful configurations, miscellaneous or incomplete parts, unrelated firearm categories and separate criminal offenses remain unadjudicated. No general intent-to-assemble element, new criminal proof burden, storage protocol or exemption for all parts kits is created. Souter's broader utility taxonomy, Scalia and Thomas's separate shoulder-firing account and Stevens's civil-notice exception remain noncontrolling.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Federal Circuit's refund judgment, 5–4, with Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas supporting affirmance and White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy opposing. The $200 making tax is not owed on the segregated unassembled unit; no broader injunction or new remand command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Crandon v. United States*, 494 U.S. 152, supplies one construction of criminally consequential language in civil litigation without changing criminal proof. *United States v. R. L. C.*, March 24, 1992, preserves its adult-Guidelines ceiling without establishing a shared new lenity method or firearms rule. *Marks v. United States*, 430 U.S. 188, supplies no synthesized broader rationale where the exact case-specific proposition already has five express supporters.

##### Whether making a firearm invariably requires final mechanical assembly

**Holding:** The National Firearms Act's references to manufacturing and otherwise producing a firearm are not confined to the final mechanical act of putting its parts together. The Federal Circuit's universal actual-assembly prerequisite is rejected without deciding tax liability for an unpresented pure-parts or sham arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter and O'Connor expressly adopt the construction; White's dissent, joined by Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy, adopts the same predicate; and Stone's concurrence expressly rejects a universal final-assembly requirement while reserving pure-parts and sham liability. These seven directly support the exact proposition across writings. Scalia and Thomas disagree. This is an actual majority proposition, not a *Marks* synthesis or a join in the plurality's broader taxonomy.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated, without a categorical rule for the reserved arrangements.

**Material application:** Section 5845(i) separately identifies manufacturing, otherwise producing and putting together. Requiring final assembly for every form of making would deprive the broader production language of work. Production must still meet the enacted definition; regulatory purpose alone does not impose a tax.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No holding classifies every miscellaneous collection, incomplete kit or genuine alternative-use set as a firearm. Liability for pure-parts kits, sham configurations, actual short-rifle assembly and separate offenses remains outside the decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The narrow refund judgment remains affirmed; the Federal Circuit's universal actual-assembly rationale does not survive as the governing construction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *United States v. Drasen*, 845 F.2d 731, supports the manufacturing construction without adjudicating its kits or adopting its broader notice and criminal-responsibility discussion. *Thompson/Center Arms Co. v. United States*, 924 F.2d 1041, is affirmed in judgment but its universal actual-assembly rationale is rejected.

## Tort Law

### Federal Tort Claims Act damages

#### Molzof v. United States, 502 U.S. 301 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-838.
**Decided:** January 14, 1992.

##### Whether 28 U.S.C. §2674 bars compensatory damages because of an asserted punitive effect

**Holding:** Section 2674 bars damages whose legal nature is punishment for culpable wrongdoing, not every compensatory award alleged to be excessive, duplicative, or difficult for the injured person to perceive. Rejection of that federal bar does not establish entitlement under the governing state law or determine an amount.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's unanimous opinion; Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas support the proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The claims arose from admitted simple negligence and sought categories characterized as compensatory under state law. Alleged duplication or unconsciousness did not alone change the damages' legal nature. A federal “punitive effect” inquiry could not replace the statute's traditional punitive-damages category.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Wisconsin law must determine whether future care, lost enjoyment, survival, and offsets permit recovery. The Court neither authorizes punitive payment nor directs an award.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is reversed and the case remanded without directing additional damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Morissette supplies the reading of borrowed legal terms against their established legal meaning. Massachusetts Bonding confirms the distinction between punitive recovery and compensation through the FTCA's special wrongful-death provision. Muniz, Richards, and Sheridan are applied and preserved.

## Environmental Law

### Interstate water-pollution permits

#### Arkansas v. Oklahoma, 503 U.S. 91 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1262 and 90-1266.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.
**Companion or consolidated matters:** *EPA v. Oklahoma*, No. 90-1266, consolidated with *Arkansas*; both concern EPA’s federal discharge permit for Fayetteville.

##### Whether EPA may condition this upstream federal permit on compliance with downstream state water-quality standards

**Holding:** The Clean Water Act authorizes EPA, in issuing this federal permit under §402(a), to require compliance with applicable downstream state water-quality standards through its valid regulations. A downstream State’s limited direct role in permitting does not restrict that federal authority; whether the Act itself commands identical compliance in every permitting setting remains open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; unanimous statutory-authority holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Section 402(a)(2) authorizes permit conditions, §301(b)(1)(C) identifies attainment of applicable standards, and 40 C.F.R. §122.4(d) reasonably implements that scheme through assurance of affected-state compliance. Limits on Oklahoma’s independent authority do not negate EPA’s delegated power.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither a universal statutory command governing every federal or state permit nor an independent source-state tort remedy. Technical expertise alone creates no authority.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse in both dockets and sustain EPA’s permit under its existing compliance, study and modification conditions. No new numerical limit or order concerning the separate White River discharge is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Milwaukee v. Illinois*, 451 U.S. 304, retains the Act’s displacement of federal nuisance common law. *International Paper Co. v. Ouellette*, 479 U.S. 481, is distinguished: limits on affected-state common-law control and direct permitting participation do not restrict this authorized federal condition. *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837, applies within Congress’s delegation. *Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB*, decided January 27, 1992, is preserved: deference operates within controlling statutory precedent, and no contrary construction is displaced.

##### Whether existing downstream degradation categorically prohibits a new discharge

**Holding:** The Clean Water Act does not categorically forbid a new discharge merely because its effluent will reach waters already failing a water-quality standard, irrespective of whether it will cause a detectable violation under the applicable permit standards. Courts may not substitute that categorical prohibition for the Act’s actual permitting and pollution-reduction scheme.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; unanimous rejection of the court of appeals’ independent statutory ground.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Section 303(d) provides a framework for pollution reduction, and §402(h) addresses additional pollutants accepted by a treatment plant violating its own permit; neither supplies the broader downstream prohibition the Tenth Circuit imposed. EPA applied the no-detectable-violation approach to this discharge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Existing degradation remains relevant under the actual governing standards. No detectable violation is excused and no later permit condition is decided on facts outside the record.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remove the categorical already-degraded-waters bar and sustain the permit under the existing conditions in both dockets.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Milwaukee v. Illinois*, 451 U.S. 304, describes a comprehensive statutory program; it does not authorize courts to add a categorical prohibition.

For *Arkansas v. Oklahoma*, Nos. 90-1262 and 90-1266, decided February 26, 1992, on construction of the incorporated downstream standards and judicial review of EPA’s findings, see Administrative Law, “Federal permit interpretation and review.”

## Business and Commercial Law

### Civil RICO causation and SIPA remedies

#### Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp., 503 U.S. 258 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-727.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### What causal relation civil RICO requires between a violation and business or property injury

**Holding:** A plaintiff seeking damages under 18 U.S.C. §1964(c) must establish both factual causation and proximate cause, including some direct relation between the injury asserted and the alleged §1962 violation. Injury that merely follows harm to another person is insufficient on the causal chain presented here; directness is a contextual requirement informed by attribution, overlapping recovery and the availability of directly injured plaintiffs, not a mechanical rule resolving every third-party injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this rule. Scalia agrees that proximate cause is required but joins no part of the Court's opinion; his distinct reasoning is not added to the opinion's support.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchaser-seller requirement is decided: Souter, Stone, Blackmun, Kennedy and Thomas join Part IV’s reservation. No special racketeering injury, antitrust-injury requirement, universal third-party-injury bar, or general zone-of-interests rule is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s judgment is reversed on both asserted SIPC claims and remanded with directions to enter judgment for Holmes on the derivative customer RICO claim and the asserted independent §78eee(d) administration-advance claim. Trustees’ distinct claims remain, and SIPC may share in any estate recovery only under lawful liquidation priorities; no recovery or distribution is predetermined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Associated General Contractors of California, Inc. v. Carpenters, 459 U.S. 519 (1983) — its account of common-law causal limits in the borrowed Clayton Act language is extended to §1964(c); its antitrust-specific injury requirement is not imported.
- Agency Holding Corp. v. Malley-Duff & Associates, Inc., 483 U.S. 143 (1987) — relied on for the close statutory relationship between civil RICO and the Clayton Act; its limitations holding remains unchanged.
- Sedima, S.P.R.L. v. Imrex Co., 473 U.S. 479 (1985) — preserved: no special racketeering injury or prior conviction is required; the enacted causal limitation still must be satisfied.
- Cannon v. University of Chicago, 441 U.S. 677 (1979) — applied for Congress's presumed awareness of the settled interpretation of borrowed language; no new implied action is recognized.

##### Whether SIPC may recover for the nonpurchasing customers’ derivative losses

**Holding:** Even assuming SIPC may be subrogated to the nonpurchasing customers' rights against Holmes, it cannot recover under §1964(c) on the asserted stock-manipulation theory because their unpaid claims depend on the broker-dealers' prior losses and intervening insolvency. The trustees' distinct claims remain undisturbed, and SIPC may share in any estate recovery according to the priority SIPA lawfully gives its claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part III-A, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the application and its limits. The validity and source of the asserted common-law subrogation are assumed, not established.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Customer subrogation and responsibility for coconspirators’ conduct are assumed without decision. Purchasing-customer claims, separately grounded parking claims, directly aimed injury to SIPC, fraud merits, actual causation and damages remain unadjudicated; the unappealed Rule 10b-5 judgment is not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s judgment is reversed on both asserted SIPC claims and remanded with directions to enter judgment for Holmes on the derivative customer RICO claim and the asserted independent §78eee(d) administration-advance claim. Trustees’ distinct claims remain, and SIPC may share in any estate recovery only under lawful liquidation priorities; no recovery or distribution is predetermined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Associated General Contractors, 459 U.S. 519 — applied to the derivative creditor-loss chain, attribution difficulties, overlapping recovery and available directly injured plaintiffs.
- Blue Shield of Virginia v. McCready, 457 U.S. 465 (1982) — relied on for causal limits and avoidance of overlapping damages; its recognition of a directly injured claimant's action is not displaced.
- Hawaii v. Standard Oil Co. of California, 405 U.S. 251 (1972) — applied for the concern about duplicate recovery; no state parens patriae issue is decided here.
- Southern Pacific Co. v. Darnell-Taenzer Lumber Co., 245 U.S. 531 (1918) — applied as a general direct-injury principle, without converting its first-step formulation into an exceptionless rule.

##### Whether §78eee(d) authorizes SIPC’s own administration-advance damages claim

**Holding:** Section 78eee(d) of SIPA gives SIPC party-in-interest status, a right to be heard and deemed intervention in liquidation matters; it does not itself create an independent right to recover administration advances as damages from Holmes. Neither that participation provision alone nor its combination with §1964(c) supplies a missing substantive recovery right or dispenses with RICO's own conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part III-B, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient statutory ground for rejecting SIPC's own §78eee(d) claim. This is distinct from the customer-subrogation causation holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide every other-law claim or the unbriefed theory of subrogation to trustees for expense advances. The statute’s participation rights and administration-advance duties remain; a separate RICO-causation ground for SIPC’s own advances is not a controlling alternative holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s judgment is reversed on both asserted SIPC claims and remanded with directions to enter judgment for Holmes on the derivative customer RICO claim and the asserted independent §78eee(d) administration-advance claim. Trustees’ distinct claims remain, and SIPC may share in any estate recovery only under lawful liquidation priorities; no recovery or distribution is predetermined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

No earlier decision is independently construed, extended or overruled by this provision-specific holding. Its participation/recovery distinction is consistent with the existing action/remedy distinction; it does not announce a general rule foreclosing implied or subrogated actions under other law.

## Property and Economic Rights

### Contract obligations and retroactive economic legislation

#### General Motors Corp. v. Romein, 503 U.S. 181 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1390.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether Michigan impaired a contractual obligation by requiring repayment of workers’ compensation offsets

**Holding:** A Contract Clause challenge requires an identified contractual obligation that the challenged law impairs; employment regulation does not become an immutable contractual term merely because it governs the relationship. Michigan’s repayment law impairs no offset promise or protected contractual enforcement rule in these employers’ agreements, so substantial-impairment balancing is unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine Justices adopt this threshold holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agreements predated the offset statute and contained no offset promise; no contractual enforcement rule was removed. The Court does not reach substantial-impairment balancing or deny protection to genuinely impaired employment contracts.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Michigan Supreme Court’s judgment is affirmed on both constitutional claims. The repayment obligation remains enforceable; no additional benefit award or contract reformation is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Energy Reserves Group v. Kansas Power & Light, 459 U.S. 400 — applied: identify the contractual obligation before measuring impairment.
- Allied Structural Steel v. Spannaus, 438 U.S. 234 — distinguished: the identified contractual pension obligations there do not create an offset promise here.
- Anderson v. Brand, 303 U.S. 95 — applied: federal review independently determines whether the asserted contractual obligation exists.
- United States Trust Co. v. New Jersey, 431 U.S. 1 — applied with its boundary: contractual enforceability is protected, but every workplace regulation is not an immutable contract term.
- Home Building & Loan Association v. Blaisdell, 290 U.S. 398 — preserved: law may inform actual contractual obligations without freezing all public regulation.
- Von Hoffman v. City of Quincy, 4 Wall. 535 — distinguished: withdrawal of contractual enforcement support is absent.
- McCracken v. Hayward, 2 How. 608 — distinguished: this law does not remove a remedy enforcing the employers’ contracts.

##### Whether the retroactive repayment requirement satisfies due process

**Holding:** Retroactive economic legislation satisfies due process when its retroactive operation rationally advances a legitimate legislative purpose. Requiring repayment of these offsets rationally restores Michigan’s compensation compromise and equalizes affected employers, notwithstanding their reliance on the earlier coordination rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion, supported by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine Justices adopt the due-process holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Retroactivity itself requires rational justification. Reliance does not automatically defeat this restoration and equalization measure; the Court does not validate retroactive legislation categorically.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Michigan Supreme Court’s judgment is affirmed on both constitutional claims. The repayment obligation remains enforceable; no additional benefit award or contract reformation is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pension Benefit Guaranty Corp. v. R.A. Gray & Co., 467 U.S. 717 — applied: retroactivity itself must rationally serve a legitimate purpose.
- Usery v. Turner Elkhorn Mining Co., 428 U.S. 1 — applied: reasonable retrospective economic burdens can survive reliance objections.
- United States v. Sperry Corp., 493 U.S. 52 — applied: rational allocation of comparable financial burdens is legitimate.

## Antitrust

### Tying and aftermarket monopolization

#### Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc., 504 U.S. 451 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1029.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

##### Whether demand for separate parts and service and conditional parts sales create a triable tying claim

**Holding:** For a Sherman Act §1 tying claim, functionally related products are distinct when sufficient separate consumer demand makes separate provision efficient. Evidence that Kodak parts and service meet that test, and that Kodak conditions parts sales on buying its service or forgoing independent service, creates genuine disputes about distinct products and conditioning; respondents must still prove all remaining tying elements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Independent service providers, customer preference for their services and self-servicing customers permit a finding of efficient separate provision. Restrictions on parts sales and use of independent service permit a finding of conditioning; installation of parts during service does not conclusively merge the products. The tied-commerce volume is undisputed here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final tie, market power or liability finding is made. An independent original-equipment-manufacturer conspiracy claim is not adjudicated; the rule-of-reason theory unpreserved in opposition to summary judgment is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of summary judgment on the preserved §1 tying claim; respondents retain proof of its elements and the requirements for recovery.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Jefferson Parish Hospital District No. 2 v. Hyde*, 466 U.S. 2, supplies separate demand, conditioning, tying power and tied commerce. *Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, preserves the obligation to produce evidence on challenged elements at summary judgment.

##### Whether equipment-market competition categorically precludes aftermarket power

**Holding:** Competition in an equipment market does not as a matter of law preclude power in its parts or service markets. Rule 56 does not permit summary judgment solely through an economic prediction contradicted by evidence allowing a rational inference of power, including material information and switching costs and actual price or exclusion effects; unsupported speculation remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices reject the categorical defense.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated; equipment competition remains relevant evidence.

**Material application:** Costly equipment replacement and difficulty predicting lifetime service prices permit a finding that installed customers cannot readily escape adverse terms. Evidence of price and exclusion effects supports further proceedings. Kodak may establish that informed new customers and equipment competition adequately constrain its policies, but the conclusion is not inevitable as a matter of law.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court presumes neither market power from imperfect information nor monopoly status for every durable-product seller. Prices, effects and asserted policy changes remain disputed; no universal grandfathering, deception or retroactive-policy-change finding is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The preserved claims survive summary judgment, with actual markets, power and liability remaining to be proved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp.*, 475 U.S. 574, preserves rational inference from evidence but is distinguished because immediate aftermarket gains differ from its prolonged loss-producing conspiracy. *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.*, 477 U.S. 242, preserves the factfinder's role on genuine material disputes. *Jefferson Parish*, 466 U.S. 2, continues to require proof of actual tying power.

##### Whether one brand's parts or service can constitute a relevant market on this record

**Holding:** A relevant product market may consist of one brand's parts or service when actual commercial alternatives and competitive constraints support that definition; brand identity alone neither establishes nor defeats it. Kodak-specific compatibility, very high parts and service shares, and entry and switching barriers create a genuine dispute about monopoly power sufficient to resist summary judgment under §2.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices permit a market and power finding without conclusively establishing either.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Owners cannot necessarily substitute another brand's parts, and replacing the equipment may cost much more than the service at issue. Evidence supports nearly complete control of compatible parts and an eighty-to-ninety-five-percent service share, together with impediments to independent supply. Equipment competitors remain relevant to whether lifetime prices are constrained.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal single-brand-market rule or monopoly presumption follows from patents, proprietary design or numerical share apart from actual competitive constraints. Actual market definition and power remain unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of summary judgment on the preserved §2 claims, without a final market or power finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *United States v. Grinnell Corp.*, 384 U.S. 563, requires monopoly power in a relevant market, distinct from exclusion. *United States v. E. I. du Pont de Nemours & Co.*, 351 U.S. 377, supplies practical substitution and competitive-constraint analysis without a categorical bar to one-brand markets.

##### Whether evidence of exclusion and Kodak's business explanations conclusively resolve monopoly maintenance

**Holding:** Monopolization under §2 requires monopoly power plus willful acquisition or maintenance, distinguished from success through a superior product, business acumen or accident. Parts-access restrictions that may exclude service rivals create a genuine issue of unlawful maintenance, and Kodak's quality, inventory and free-riding explanations do not conclusively eliminate that issue here; no general duty to supply rivals or final pretext finding is imposed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for evidentiary sufficiency and the distinct power and conduct requirements.

**Material application:** The quality explanation must be tested against self-service exceptions and independent-service evidence; the inventory explanation against evidence that parts needs do not depend on the service provider; and free-riding claims against the restrictions' operation and alternative-supply obstacles. Kodak may prove legitimate reasons and defeat liability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Attempted monopolization remains distinct, with its own intent and dangerous-probability requirements. Ultimate markets, power, unlawful conduct, pretext, antitrust injury, causation, damages and remedial design remain unresolved. Injury to a competitor alone establishes neither antitrust injury nor damages entitlement.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of summary judgment on the preserved §2 monopolization and attempt claims. No damages, final injunction or compulsory parts-supply order issues; respondents must prove each claim and recovery requirement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Grinnell*, 384 U.S. 563, distinguishes unlawful maintenance from lawful success. *Aspen Skiing Co. v. Aspen Highlands Skiing Corp.*, 472 U.S. 585, makes exclusion and business justification material without creating a universal supply duty. *Brunswick Corp. v. Pueblo Bowl-O-Mat, Inc.*, 429 U.S. 477, retains the distinct antitrust-injury requirement. *Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp.*, March 24, 1992, retains its RICO directness and SIPA holdings without deciding these Sherman Act elements or injury.
