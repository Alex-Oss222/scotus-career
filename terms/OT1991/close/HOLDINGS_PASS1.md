## Source limits preserved

- **King v. St. Vincent's Hospital:** The record states that enacted coverage, notice, service, and return conditions remain necessary, but does not give their statutory terms. The candidate admits only the fully supported rule against an implied duration limit and preserves nonreach of those other requirements. It creates no rule for a coverage, notice, service, or return dispute; such a dispute would require the applicable statutory text and adjudicative source. This does not prevent incorporation of the recorded duration holding.
- **Holywell Corp. v. Smith:** The record supplies the separate trust's liquidation-and-distribution purpose, direct transfer of estate property, and Smith's authority despite limited discretion. The candidate states that actual application. It does not state universal definitions under §7701(a)(6), Treasury Regulations §§301.7701-4(d) and 301.7701-6, or the grantor-trust provisions, whose full terms the record does not reproduce. Any broader definitional rule would require verified term-effective text and an adequate adjudicative source; the record identifies 503 U.S. 54–58 as its source location.
- **United States v. Nordic Village, Inc.:** The record does not reproduce the precise triggers in original §106(a) and (b), or §106(c)'s introductory exception. The candidate preserves their case-specific nonapplication without restating their general operation. Its holding concerns the particular §550 personal money judgment and the adopted reading of §106(c). A broader statement would require the original statutory text and, if material to an adjudicated proposition, an authorized source correction; the record identifies 503 U.S. 31–37 as its source location. No missing trigger or broader waiver is inferred.
