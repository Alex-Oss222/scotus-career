### 3. Current Law
- **Criminal appeal finality (October 15):** A single timely substantive reconsideration motion renders a suppression ruling nonfinal until disposition, including when it renews an abandoned ground; the full appeal period then begins. Bad faith, successive motions, merits waiver, and the search remain open.
- **Judicial immunity and emergency election relief:** *Mireles*'s equal division and *Campos*'s unexplained denial create no precedent.
- **Rule 39.8 (November 4):** Current frivolousness plus extreme repetitive frivolous use may warrant discretionary fee denial for identified submissions, with a paid-filing opportunity; no numerical threshold or prospective ban was adopted.
- **Section 1983 capacity (November 5):** A state officer sued individually is a statutory person for conduct under color of official authority; sovereign immunity does not bar personal recovery, while proof and personal immunities remain distinct.
- **Bank regulatory proceedings (December 3):** Section 1818(i)(1) forecloses district-court interruption outside statutory channels; general bankruptcy jurisdiction and *Kyne* do not overcome preclusion where proceedings may continue and meaningful final-order review exists.
- **General criminal verdicts (December 3):** A factually unsupported but legally valid conspiracy object does not require reversal when another object is sufficiently proved; legal invalidity and the reserved defects remain distinct.
- **Prior-injury evidence and instructions (December 4):** Prior nonaccidental-injury evidence may prove intentional harm without proving the abuser's identity. An ambiguous prior-act instruction violates due process only upon a reasonable likelihood, assessed from the full charge and trial, that jurors relieved the State of an element; this charge did not. A general constitutional propensity rule remains open.
- **Maritime status (December 4):** An LHWCA-listed occupation and voluntary benefits without a final status adjudication do not categorically bar Jones Act seaman status; the crew exclusion, fact-specific vessel connection, and statutory credit remain operative.
- **Union claims (December 4):** Compensatory lost-referral damages carry a Seventh Amendment jury right despite joined equitable relief. An individual beneficiary may invoke §301 to enforce a qualifying interunion constitution; liability and standalone local bylaws remain open.
- **Compulsory bar dues (December 4):** A known general objector's reasonably disputed nongermane share must be protected before use through reasonable reduction or adequate escrow; recurring position-specific objections cannot be required. Balanced selection of Bar-member arbitrators is not facially biased by membership alone.
- **Crime-related publishing income (December 10):** New York §632-a's content-based escrow fails narrow tailoring and cannot apply to the contract; neutral restitution, forfeiture, attachment, and creditor rules remain available.
- **Administrative EAJA (December 10):** A deportation hearing governed by the INA rather than §554 is not an adjudication under §554 for EAJA §504, even if functionally similar.
- **Bankruptcy preferences (December 11):** Long-term debt payments may qualify under the contemporaneous §547(c)(2); the creditor must prove all three statutory ordinary-course elements, and maturity is no separate exclusion.

- **FOIA Exemption 6 (December 16):** Identifiers may be withheld when their concrete privacy cost outweighs little demonstrated incremental oversight value after segregated release; confidentiality is relevant, not dispositive, and derivative uses are not categorically excluded.
- **Immigration release conditions (December 16):** INA §242(a) permits the narrowed unauthorized-work bond condition when authorized workers remain outside it and individual status determination and relief remain available.
- **FELA state railroads (December 16):** *Parden*'s state-railroad coverage holding remains enforceable in competent state courts; constructive federal-forum waiver remains displaced and other Article I statutes remain open.
- **Military leave (December 16):** Section 2024(d) contains no implied duration-reasonableness limit; its enacted prerequisites remain.
- **Qualified immunity procedure (December 16):** The judge applies reasonable-officer immunity to the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record under Rule 56. Neither a “most reasonable” test nor generalized security concern substitutes for offense-specific analysis. Existing immunity doctrine is unchanged.
- **Mandamus (January 13):** A petitioner ordinarily must seek focused relief from the court that entered the operative order and show that avenue inadequate; *Blodgett* otherwise creates no new habeas rule.
- **Appellate notice (January 14):** A timely brief may function as a notice if it objectively supplies every Rule 3(c) item; informal title and mistaken filing location do not independently defeat it.
- **Reservation fee-land taxation (January 14):** Qualifying General Allotment Act fee land is subject to ad valorem taxation, subject to patent source and independent protections. The identified authorization does not reach an Indian owner's sales excise, and a lien does not change incidence.
- **Ballot access (January 14):** Continuing qualification effects preserve a live election controversy. Illinois may require support in a separate electorate but may not bar authorized party-name expansion or invalidate qualified slate components solely because another district fails.
- **FTCA damages (January 14):** Section 2674 uses the traditional punitive category tied to punishment for culpable conduct; alleged excess, duplication, or imperceptibility does not alone reclassify compensation. State entitlement and amount remain distinct.
