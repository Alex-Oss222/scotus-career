# Hudson v. McMillian

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Hudson v. McMillian
* **Citation or Docket:** 503 U.S. 1 (1992); No. 90-6531.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of a judgment reversing a prisoner's §1983 damages award.
* **Simulated Event Date:** February 25, 1992, inventory scheduling date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: whether an Eighth Amendment excessive-force claim requires a significant injury even when guards use force unnecessarily and wantonly.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, 929 F.2d 1014, reversing the judgment entered after a consensual trial before a magistrate.
* **Relief Requested:** Hudson seeks reversal of the significant-injury bar; guards and their supervisor seek affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Hudson, a convicted Louisiana prisoner, was handcuffed and shackled after an argument with a guard. The magistrate found that guards McMillian and Woods used force without need and that supervisor Mezo expressly condoned it. The evidence described punches and kicks, facial swelling, bruising, loosened teeth and a cracked dental plate. The magistrate awarded $800. The Fifth Circuit treated the injuries as minor and insufficiently significant, rather than overturning the relevant findings of unnecessary force and supervisory approval.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The prisoner is convicted, so the Eighth Amendment supplies the constitutional source. Respondents also characterize the conduct as isolated and unauthorized, hence not punishment; that theory was not the basis of the decision below and conflicts with findings left intact. Qualified immunity is not the question presented.
* **Relevant Dates:** The incident occurred in October 1983; liability and any separately preserved immunity inquiry would require attention to their own temporal baselines.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Earlier Hunter and Hafer concern defenses and capacities, not this force-injury element.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before February 25, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decisions supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Revalidate actual Hunter or Hafer law only if a preserved immunity or capacity issue becomes material; neither drafted position answers the Eighth Amendment question.

### Issue 1: Unnecessary force and injury severity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated excessive-force standard supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Eighth Amendment prohibits unnecessary and wanton infliction of pain. In Whitley v. Albers, 475 U.S. 312 (1986), force used in a prison disturbance is assessed by whether it was a good-faith effort to maintain or restore discipline or was applied maliciously and sadistically to cause harm. Need, relation of force to need, reasonably perceived threat, efforts to temper force and injury inform that inquiry. Deference to genuine security judgments is not permission for gratuitous punishment.
* **Material Other Authority:** Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97 (1976), distinguishes serious medical needs and deliberate indifference from malpractice. Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294 (1991), requires an objective constitutional deprivation and appropriate subjective culpability in prison-conditions claims. Neither expressly establishes a serious-medical-injury prerequisite for every beating. The prisoner bears the ordinary civil burden of proving his claim; trial fact findings receive clear-error review, while the existence of an additional legal element is reviewed independently.
* **Open Point:** Whether significant physical injury is a separate necessary element of an excessive-force violation, and how Whitley's disturbance-specific formulation applies outside a riot. Objective wrongfulness, subjective culpability and compensable injury remain distinct, even where the same evidence informs each.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Fifth Circuit required significant injury, reasoning that minor harm did not satisfy the Eighth Amendment's objective dimension. Respondents invoke Wilson's serious-deprivation requirement and the need to keep ordinary battery claims out of constitutional litigation. They also raise the isolated-unauthorized-conduct theory.

Hudson argues that a malicious beating cannot be constitutionally permissible merely because it fortuitously causes limited lasting injury. His handcuffs, shackles, lack of a disciplinary need and supervisory approval distinguish a good-faith security mistake or a trivial contact. The injury evidence remains relevant to what happened, culpability and damages; rejecting a categorical injury floor does not make all contact actionable. The record-based answer to the unauthorized-conduct argument is different from adopting a general definition of punishment for every isolated assault.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain the injury requirement or reject it and return the case for disposition under the proper force standard. It may resolve this case because the findings satisfy even Whitley's demanding culpability formulation, without deciding whether every other prison-force context requires precisely that mental state. A merits ruling need not decide unpresented immunity issues, psychological injury alone, or damages for wholly trivial force. The findings established after trial, rather than presumed allegations, determine the legal application.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit's judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the significant-injury bar and require further disposition consistent with the undisturbed trial findings. Do not increase the award, decide an unpresented immunity defense or issue a prison-management injunction.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Gratuitous custodial violence

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the injury element that defeated the established claim.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the absence of serious injury defeats this deliberately unnecessary, nontrivial beating; a comprehensive mental-state rule for every force setting is unnecessary.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the Eighth Amendment's force doctrine in context and reject the circuit's additional injury prerequisite. Wilson's conditions analysis is distinguished, not overruled.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A nontrivial beating inflicted maliciously and without a disciplinary justification on a restrained convicted prisoner can violate the Eighth Amendment despite limited resulting physical injury.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Whitley treats injury as evidence within the force inquiry, not a license to administer cruelty so long as the body withstands it. Wilson's objective dimension is satisfied by the character and circumstances of serious custodial violence; the seriousness appropriate to a food or medical-deprivation claim cannot be transplanted as an identical injury element. Hudson's restraint, the absence of need, repeated blows and supervisory condonation satisfy the demanding wanton-force inquiry on the findings below. His dental and facial injuries reinforce that this was no trivial touch. The rule preserves room for disciplinary judgment because it requires constitutionally culpable force, while refusing to make constitutional protection depend on the victim's physical resilience.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No rule that any technical battery is cruel punishment, no strict liability for accidental harm and no general holding on isolated unauthorized conduct. That alternative cannot defeat this record, which includes supervisory approval. Compensatory damages still require their own lawful measurement; recognition of the right does not determine every remedy or defense.

# United States v. Nordic Village, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Nordic Village, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 503 U.S. 30 (1992); No. 90-1629.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, statutory sovereign-immunity review of a bankruptcy money judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** February 25, 1992, inventory scheduling date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: whether the then-operative Bankruptcy Code §106(c), or an alternative jurisdictional or property theory, permits monetary recovery from the United States of an unauthorized postpetition transfer.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, 915 F.2d 1049, affirming the $20,000 judgment against the IRS after the bankruptcy court and district court rejected immunity.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks reversal of the money judgment; the trustee seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Nordic Village entered Chapter 11 in March 1984. Several months later officer Josef Lah withdrew corporate funds and used $20,000 to pay his personal federal tax liability. The bankruptcy court avoided the unauthorized transfer under §549(a) and ordered recovery under §550(a). The judgment is an obligation to pay money, not an order returning identified property presently within the court's control.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Statutory avoidance authority does not itself settle sovereign amenability to monetary relief. Section 106(a) and (b)'s claim-linked provisions are not the asserted basis supporting this judgment. The federal sovereign issue differs from an Eleventh Amendment defense by a State.
* **Relevant Dates:** Apply the Code language governing this litigation, including the original §106(c); no later congressional amendment enters.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Earlier Wolas and Dewsnup concern other Bankruptcy Code provisions; Holywell follows this case in the controlling index and cannot supply entering law.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before February 25, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decisions supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Revalidate any actual effective Ardestani holding on strict construction of federal sovereign-immunity waivers, as well as Wolas or Dewsnup statutory-method holdings. An earlier draft or historical same-term outcome is not effective simulated law; a rule about preference or lien avoidance does not itself waive federal immunity.

### Issue 1: Waiver for monetary recovery

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated §106 immunity rule supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Federal sovereign immunity requires consent to the particular suit and remedy. Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs, 498 U.S. 89 (1990), restates the requirement of unequivocal statutory waiver. Section 106(c) provides, notwithstanding sovereign immunity and subject to (a) and (b), that a Code provision containing specified governmental terms applies to governmental units and that a determination of an issue under that provision binds them. Subsections (a) and (b) expressly address specified claim and offset circumstances. Section 550 permits recovery of transferred property or its value from relevant transferees; whether §106 extends that monetary remedy to the United States is the disputed bridge. The party invoking jurisdiction must identify an applicable waiver; this is legal construction, not a heightened evidentiary quantum for proving the transfer.
* **Material Other Authority:** Hoffman v. Connecticut Department of Income Maintenance, 492 U.S. 96 (1989), rejected a money claim against a State, but only a four-Justice plurality adopted the relevant narrow statutory reading. The fifth vote rested on state sovereign-immunity principles. Hoffman therefore supplies competing statutory reasoning, not a binding majority construction dispositive of federal immunity. A general jurisdictional grant, here 28 U.S.C. §1334(d), is distinct from a waiver of a sovereign defendant's immunity.
* **Open Point:** Whether the text applying Code provisions and making determinations binding unequivocally authorizes a personal monetary judgment, or can have operative nonmonetary and claim-determination effects without that waiver.

### Issue 2: Alternative property and trust grounds

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated exception supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** In rem jurisdiction concerns adjudication of interests in property under the court's authority. Specific-property recovery and a general obligation to pay its value are materially different forms of relief. Trust principles can identify ownership but do not independently enact sovereign consent to a money judgment.
* **Material Other Authority:** The unequivocal federal-waiver requirement identified in Issue 1 applies separately to the alternative jurisdictional characterization.
* **Open Point:** Whether this actual judgment falls within any property-based route independent of §106(c), rather than becoming such an order merely because the money originated in the estate.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Sixth Circuit understood §106(c)'s application-and-binding language to permit the recovery order and did not regard Hoffman's fractured statutory reasoning as controlling. The trustee emphasizes the clause's express reference to sovereign immunity, §550's use of covered governmental terminology, equal treatment of governmental transferees and the need to restore estate assets. He also invokes broad bankruptcy jurisdiction, in rem authority and trust principles.

The United States argues that Congress expressly used monetary language in the neighboring subsections while §106(c) speaks of binding determinations. It maintains that plausible narrower operation defeats the required clear waiver and that a jurisdictional statute or equitable label cannot convert a general money judgment into recovery of an identified res. No contention that Lah was entitled to divert corporate money is necessary to this immunity defense.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may find a statutory waiver and sustain the monetary judgment, or find the waiver insufficient and reverse without validating the transfer. An actual specific-property remedy would require its own jurisdictional basis and factual identification; it cannot be awarded on an assumed traceable fund. The case need not resolve every effect of §106(c), Congress's power over state immunity, or later statutory remedies. The record adequately identifies the form of judgment; the absence of an identified res is part of that judgment's character rather than an inference from an unavailable source.

# Holywell Corp. v. Smith

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Holywell Corp. v. Smith
* **Canonical Matter / Alias:** Includes United States v. Smith, the consolidated petition.
* **Citation or Docket:** 503 U.S. 47 (1992); Nos. 90-1361 and 90-1484.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, tax-return and payment obligations of a Chapter 11 liquidating trustee.
* **Simulated Event Date:** February 25, 1992, inventory scheduling date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: whether the trustee must file returns and pay tax on income attributable to transferred corporate and individual-debtor property, despite a confirmed plan silent about taxes.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, 911 F.2d 1539, affirming a declaration that trustee Smith had no such duty.
* **Relief Requested:** Corporate debtors and the United States seek reversal; Smith and supporting respondents seek preservation of the declaration.
* **Material Record:** Four related corporations and Theodore Gould filed Chapter 11 cases. A confirmed plan assigned their estate property to a separate liquidating trust, with Smith to liquidate and distribute it to creditors. The plan took effect in October 1985 and did not address tax filing or payment. The United States did not object to confirmation. Capital gains and interest arose from the property; a corporate return prompted a dispute about Smith's obligations, and succeeding returns were not filed. The United States seeks recognition of duties after Smith's appointment, not payment by him of taxes due before it.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Corporate assignee and individual-property trust theories require separate treatment. The United States' proposed individual-estate theory is an alternative legal characterization, not a necessary concession about the plan's trust. The issue is responsibility, not adjudication of a final tax amount.
* **Relevant Dates:** Confirmation and appointment delimit prospective statutory duties and the asserted effect of government silence.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Both petitions seek review of the same declaratory judgment. Nordic Village precedes this case in the controlling index. Revalidate its actual effective statutory holding if relevant, while distinguishing its federal-immunity question.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before February 25, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decisions supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any actual Wolas or Dewsnup Code-construction holding may require methodological revalidation; neither would itself define the Internal Revenue Code's fiduciary categories.

### Issue 1: Statutory filing and payment categories

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated tax-fiduciary doctrine supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Internal Revenue Code §6012(b)(3) requires the receiver, trustee or assignee possessing all or substantially all corporate property to make the corporation's return, whether or not the business continues. Section 6012(b)(4) places estate or trust returns on the fiduciary. Section 7701(a)(6) and Treasury Regulation §301.7701-6 describe fiduciary capacity; Regulation §301.7701-4(d) addresses liquidating trusts. Section 6151 connects the duty to file with payment of tax shown, subject to applicable statutory rules. Grantor-trust provisions §§671–677 may allocate tax differently if their predicates are met; the existence of formerly debtor-owned property alone does not establish them.
* **Material Other Authority:** Statutory words are read in their ordinary and legal context; bankruptcy labels do not create an exemption absent tax-law support.
* **Open Point:** Whether Smith satisfies the corporate assignee category and, as to Gould's property, acts for an individual estate or a separate taxable trust; whether the asserted grantor characterization defeats trustee responsibility. These are legal classification issues on established plan terms, not heightened scrutiny or a new evidentiary presumption.

### Issue 2: Effect of plan confirmation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated confirmation rule supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Bankruptcy Code §1141(a) binds specified parties, including creditors, to the confirmed plan. That binding effect must be related to the claims and provisions actually addressed.
* **Material Other Authority:** Section 101(10)'s creditor definition matters to postconfirmation liabilities.
* **Open Point:** Whether silence about tax duties and the government's failure to object eliminate obligations created by tax law after the trust begins operating.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The courts below treated the plan-created trustee as outside the asserted filing duties and gave effect to the confirmed plan's omission of a tax obligation. Smith argues that he is not operating the corporations or winding them up in the conventional sense, that his constrained liquidation duties do not make him the relevant fiduciary, and that Gould remains responsible under grantor-trust principles. He also invokes the binding plan and governmental nonobjection.

The debtors and United States emphasize §6012(b)(3)'s express assignee language and its application whether business continues or not. For Gould's property, the United States advances individual-estate fiduciary status; the trust provisions offer an alternative route supported by the actual plan. Petitioners argue that a silent plan cannot immunize future income from ordinary tax administration. Neither the trust's restricted business purpose nor the government's participation establishes an affirmative negotiated tax exemption.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm, reverse separately for corporate or individual property, or reverse both components on their respective statutory categories. A correct judgment can use trust fiduciary status without accepting the government's individual-estate theory. Remand can implement filing and payment obligations without fixing unsupported income, deductions, penalties or allocation amounts. No ruling on preappointment taxes or a plan expressly adjudicating a disputed preconfirmation tax claim is necessary.

# Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools
* **Citation or Docket:** 503 U.S. 60 (1992); No. 90-918.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of dismissal for categorical unavailability of Title IX damages.
* **Simulated Event Date:** February 26, 1992, inventory scheduling date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: whether damages are an available remedy in the recognized private action for intentional violations of Title IX.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, 911 F.2d 617, affirming dismissal on the ground that Title IX affords no damages remedy.
* **Relief Requested:** Franklin seeks reversal and proceedings on her damages claim; respondents seek affirmance of the remedial bar.
* **Material Record:** Franklin alleges repeated sexual harassment and abuse by teacher and coach Andrew Hill while she attended a federally funded high school. She alleges school officials knew, failed to act and discouraged her complaint. Hill resigned on a condition that pending matters against him be dropped, and the school closed its investigation. These are pleading-stage allegations, not judicial findings of abuse, institutional knowledge or liability. Franklin no longer attends the school; prospective student relief and employee backpay do not redress the alleged past harm.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Cannon's recognized private action supplies the cause-of-action premise. The presented question concerns remedies, not a new implied action or the final standard for attributing a teacher's acts to the recipient. Federal funding and intentional discrimination are the pleaded predicates; their ultimate proof remains open.
* **Relevant Dates:** Allegations concern the 1980s; the 1986 remedies amendment and the Civil Rights Restoration Act enacted in 1988 predate this event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No companion. Same-date tax and environmental cases do not determine this remedy.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before February 26, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decisions supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Actual earlier Hafer or Nordic Village holdings may require attention only if a preserved capacity or sovereign-remedy issue applies. A local school district is not assumed to be an arm of the State, and federal Treasury immunity is not a generic bar to statutory damages against all recipients.

### Issue 1: Remedies under the existing private action

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated Title IX remedy rule supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Title IX, 20 U.S.C. §1681(a), prohibits sex discrimination in covered federally supported education. Cannon v. University of Chicago, 441 U.S. 677 (1979), recognizes its private action. Bell v. Hood, 327 U.S. 678 (1946), provides a general remedial principle for cognizable federal rights, subject to congressional limits; existence of a cause and scope of relief remain separate. Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission, 463 U.S. 582 (1983), is fractured but includes majority support for damages for intentional discrimination under closely related Title VI. Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Darrone, 465 U.S. 624 (1984), sustains backpay under the Rehabilitation Act.
* **Material Other Authority:** The 1986 amendment, 42 U.S.C. §2000d-7, removes specified state immunity and provides remedies at law and in equity to the same extent as against other public or private entities. It does not enumerate every measure of damages. The Restoration Act addresses coverage after earlier narrowing and legislates against Cannon's existence. These enactments bear on congressional treatment of the established cause.
* **Open Point:** Whether Title IX's remedial silence excludes damages or leaves ordinary appropriate relief available, particularly for a past intentional injury for which an injunction cannot compensate. The legal availability question does not resolve causation, compensable loss or institutional responsibility.

### Issue 2: Spending Clause notice

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated notice rule supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Spending Clause authorizes Congress to attach lawful conditions to federal funds.
* **Material Other Authority:** Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981), requires clear notice of conditions attached to federal funds. The distinction between knowing intentional violation of an express nondiscrimination condition and liability for unintended effects matters to notice.
* **Open Point:** Whether that principle bars compensatory relief for the alleged intentional violation despite the established action and explicit statutory prohibition. No later Title IX recipient-liability formula enters this event.

The transaction reorganized the taxpayer's corporate structure and secured benefits beyond the tax year, so its facilitating expenses are not simply the costs of current operations. Rejecting an immediate deduction does not establish a particular amortization period or authorize a deduction unavailable elsewhere in the Code. The record supports the actual structural transaction; no quantified future profit projection is required or invented.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Public employment does not erase applicable speech, equality or bodily-liberty guarantees. The relevant constitutional source determines the wrong; §1983 supplies the action and Monell the city's responsibility. Adding a second abstract governmental-abuse requirement risks defeating recognized rights. That error does not require reversal if the complaint lacks a constitutional deprivation in the first place.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Municipal responsibility is not assumed merely because employment exists.

### Issue 2: The alleged omissions do not establish the required deprivation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the properly presented alternative ground necessary to the judgment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply source-specific duties and distinguish his affirmative-danger commitment from a general constitutional safe-workplace guarantee. No categorical rejection of affirmative-danger liability is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A voluntary public-employment relationship and a knowing policy of inadequate safety training, warnings or equipment do not, without the necessary custodial or affirmative-deprivation predicate, establish the pleaded substantive-due-process right to workplace safety.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The prior incident supports knowledge, and deliberate omission is more than negligence. Those points satisfy neither custody nor an affirmative exercise of authority creating the specific danger. The complaint does not allege coerced entry, deliberate bodily injury, obstruction of available rescue or another affirmative act materially increasing the hazard beyond the failure to provide protection. A general employment arrangement cannot itself supply that additional element without turning all knowingly deficient public workplaces into constitutional custodial settings. Stone's Master would recognize a properly presented affirmative contribution to a specific foreseeable danger with conscious disregard, practical opportunity and causation; those elements cannot be inserted into this complaint. Canton addresses when the city owns a qualifying constitutional wrong and cannot fill the missing substantive duty. The Texas statute supplies its own legal protection without converting its standards into federal substantive due process.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Preserve claims of actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury or a properly alleged affirmative danger. No factual approval of the city's conduct or assumption that budget choices justified it is needed. A broader new danger-creation rule need not be adopted to decide this omission-based pleading.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Willy v. Coastal Corp.
* **Citation or Docket:** 503 U.S. 131 (1992); No. 90-1150.
* **October Term:** 1991.

* **Simulated Event Date:** March 3, 1992, inventory scheduling date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: whether a federal district court may impose the challenged Rule 11 sanction when it is later determined that the removed civil action lay outside its subject-matter jurisdiction.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, 915 F.2d 965, sustaining sanctioning authority on the second appeal.
* **Relief Requested:** Willy seeks vacatur of the sanction for absence of jurisdiction; Coastal seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Willy sued his former employer in Texas state court, alleging dismissal from his in-house counsel job for refusing participation in environmental-law violations. Coastal removed on federal-question grounds. The district court rejected Willy's jurisdictional objection, dismissed the merits and sanctioned litigation conduct unrelated to his jurisdictional argument. On the first appeal the Fifth Circuit found no subject-matter jurisdiction but preserved sanctioning authority and remanded for determination of amount. The present appeal concerns that authority, not whether the federal court correctly adjudicated the underlying employment claim.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The absence of merits jurisdiction is settled. The sanction must be examined as a collateral procedural order; treating the jurisdictional objection itself as misconduct would contradict the record. No new determination of the amount or underlying Rule 11 violation is the presented issue.

* **Current Through:** Immediately before March 3, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decisions supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Revalidate any actual earlier jurisdictional holding, including MCorp's source-specific channel rule, without treating it as a direct decision on collateral sanctions. No earlier listed decision identified resolves this Rule 11 question.

### Issue 1: Collateral procedural authority

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated collateral-sanction rule supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The entering Rules Enabling Act and collateral-proceeding principles below control the analysis.
* **Other Governing Law:** Rules 1 and 81(c) apply the Federal Rules to civil proceedings and removed actions. Then-operative Rule 11 requires appropriate sanctions for specified signed-paper violations after the requisite reasonable inquiry; it does not authorize deciding an employment claim outside federal jurisdiction. Rule 82 disclaims enlargement of jurisdiction. The Rules Enabling Act, 28 U.S.C. §2072, permits procedural rules within constitutional limits. Whether the rule validly supports this order is independently reviewed; the ordinary discretionary review of sanction selection is a distinct question.
* **Material Other Authority:** Sibbach v. Wilson & Co., 312 U.S. 1 (1941), distinguishes valid procedural regulation. Cooter & Gell v. United States v. Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258 (1947), recognizes authority to preserve orderly proceedings while jurisdiction is examined. United States Catholic Conference v. Abortion Rights Mobilization, 487 U.S. 72 (1988), limits civil contempt supporting merits discovery when the court lacks jurisdiction over the underlying suit.
* **Open Point:** Whether this sanction remains an independently permissible procedural consequence when merits jurisdiction was absent from the outset, rather than merely lost later. The distinction between punishment of litigation abuse and coercion of compliance with invalid merits process matters.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Fifth Circuit treats the sanction as collateral enforcement of rules governing conduct before the court. Willy argues that Article III and Rule 82 leave no authority once initial jurisdiction is found absent; Cooter & Gell involved a court that possessed jurisdiction before voluntary dismissal. He relies on the limits illustrated by Catholic Conference.

Coastal argues that procedural order cannot depend retroactively on whether a jurisdictional ruling survives appeal. The challenged sanction regulates conduct while the court addressed the removed case and does not enforce the invalid merits judgment. Willy acknowledges congressional power to regulate federal courts and authorize sanctions; the dispute concerns its valid operation in this setting, not an unlimited inherent authority.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may vacate for lack of authority or sustain the collateral sanction while leaving the absence of merits jurisdiction fully intact. It may distinguish an order enforcing discovery into an unauthorized merits dispute from a completed procedural violation. Neither reinstating the employment dismissal nor deciding whether removal was substantively justified is available on this settled posture. The case supplies no basis for sanctions against the successful jurisdictional objection or a new sanction amount.

# McCarthy v. Madigan

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** McCarthy v. Madigan
* **Citation or Docket:** 503 U.S. 140 (1992); No. 90-6861.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of dismissal for failure to exhaust a federal prison grievance process.
* **Simulated Event Date:** March 4, 1992, inventory scheduling date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: whether a federal prisoner seeking only money damages through the recognized Bivens medical-care channel must first exhaust a Bureau of Prisons grievance procedure that cannot award that relief.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Tenth Circuit, 914 F.2d 1411, affirming dismissal for nonexhaustion.
* **Relief Requested:** McCarthy seeks reversal and proceedings on his damages claim; federal officials seek affirmance of the exhaustion requirement.
* **Material Record:** McCarthy alleged deliberate indifference by four prison medical or administrative employees to conditions following back surgery and to psychiatric needs. His complaint expressly seeks money damages only. The general BOP procedure provides investigation and written responses, successive administrative appeals and short filing periods, but no formal hearing or money award. An initial grievance concerned a private cell, not the medical claim; a later medical grievance was accepted despite lateness and denied on the merits, but an administrative appeal was rejected as late. These proceedings do not establish that officials actually violated the Eighth Amendment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** No statute directly mandates this general BOP exhaustion for the pleaded Bivens action. The action is not an FTCA claim subject to that statute's own administrative channel, and it requests no injunction. The question is court-imposed exhaustion, not creation of a new constitutional cause.
* **Relevant Dates:** The 1991 BOP regulations and then-operative prisoner-litigation statutes govern. No later Prison Litigation Reform Act enters the baseline.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Earlier Hudson concerns force; prison medical-care standards remain source-specific. No companion requires consolidated disposition.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before March 4, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decisions supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** An effective Hunter holding could affect a later preserved individual-defense inquiry; an effective Hudson ruling must not replace Estelle's medical-care elements. Neither prepared position resolves this procedural gate.

### Issue 1: Mandatory and discretionary exhaustion

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated BOP exhaustion rule supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Use the entering statute-first exhaustion inquiry and discretionary considerations identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Congress can require exhaustion, in which case the enacted command governs. The then-current 42 U.S.C. §1997e addresses specified state-prisoner §1983 proceedings through a limited statutory mechanism, not this federal Bivens damages claim. The general procedure in 28 C.F.R. §§542.10–542.16 provides review of prison complaints, with extensions for valid delay, but does not itself state the asserted mandatory precondition to this damages suit. The separate FTCA procedure has a different source and claim.
* **Material Other Authority:** McKart v. United States, 395 U.S. 185 (1969), weighs statutory design and exhaustion purposes including agency autonomy, expertise and efficient judicial review against the claimant's interests. McGee v. United States, 402 U.S. 479 (1971), illustrates exhaustion's stronger role when factual agency functions matter. Patsy v. Board of Regents, 457 U.S. 496 (1982), rejects a general exhaustion requirement for §1983 and emphasizes Congress's design; it does not automatically decide every Bivens question. Judicially imposed exhaustion is a procedural doctrine, not a requirement to prove the underlying constitutional claim to a special quantum.
* **Open Point:** Whether the general grievance process's investigatory and corrective benefits justify delaying or barring a damages-only action where the process cannot grant the requested remedy and its deadlines may foreclose review.

### Issue 2: Scope of the existing judicial channel

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated modification of the relevant Bivens channel supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The entering cause-of-action and medical-duty authorities remain distinct from exhaustion.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Eighth Amendment supplies the pleaded substantive protection; the asserted individual-officer damages channel requires its own recognized authority.
* **Material Other Authority:** Bivens, 403 U.S. 388 (1971), and Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14 (1980), provide the relevant background for individual federal-officer damages claims, with Carlson specifically addressing prison medical mistreatment. Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97 (1976), distinguishes deliberate indifference to serious medical needs from malpractice. Liability, causation, defenses and damages require their own later adjudication.
* **Open Point:** No decision about the ultimate sufficiency or proof of this prisoner's medical claim is required merely to resolve the exhaustion bar.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Tenth Circuit reasoned that courts creating Bivens remedies may impose reasonable filing conditions and that preliminary agency fact gathering and possible corrective action assist litigation even without a monetary award. The government stresses prison administration, opportunity to correct mistakes and reduction of needless litigation.

McCarthy argues that a procedure unable to provide his sole requested relief should not bar the established judicial action, especially where rapid deadlines and absence of formal factfinding diminish its utility. He does not claim that the grievance procedure is nonexistent or that every administrative effort is futile. The dispute concerns whether its actual benefits justify this obligatory gate, not whether prisons should maintain useful voluntary complaint procedures.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain court-imposed exhaustion, reject it for this damages-only claim, or formulate a narrower rule tied to the actual procedure's lack of remedial authority. Reversal restores the claim to ordinary litigation without establishing medical liability or immunity's availability. Requests for ongoing injunctive relief, an amended grievance system empowered to award damages, or a later statutory mandate present different cases. The record identifies both the grievance process and McCarthy's actual efforts; no assumption that he never complained or that officials refused all review is warranted.
