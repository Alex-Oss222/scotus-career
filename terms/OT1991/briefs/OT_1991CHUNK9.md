# Foucha v. Louisiana

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Foucha v. Louisiana
* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 71 (1992); No. 90-5844.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of continued confinement following an insanity acquittal.
* **Simulated Event Date:** May 18, 1992, historical decision date used for ordering, not a grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: May Louisiana continue to confine an insanity acquittee in a psychiatric hospital on dangerousness grounds after the State no longer claims that he is mentally ill, placing on him the burden of proving nondangerousness?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Louisiana Supreme Court, 563 So. 2d 1138, sustaining the confinement order.
* **Relief Requested:** Reversal and relief from confinement under the insanity-acquittee provision.
* **Material Record:** In 1984 Foucha was acquitted by reason of insanity of aggravated burglary and illegal discharge of a firearm and committed. In 1988 the hospital review panel recommended conditional discharge. The examining doctor described the original condition as a recovered drug-induced psychosis, identified an antisocial personality rather than a mental disease, and would not certify that Foucha would be harmless, partly because of institutional altercations. The court found dangerousness and continued confinement. Louisiana does not contend that Foucha was currently mentally ill at the relevant hearing. A recommendation for conditional discharge is not a finding that he is harmless.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The continuing physical restraint supplies a live injury. The challenge concerns the authority and burden for continued commitment, not the validity of the original insanity verdict. No adequate independent state ground disposing of the federal claims is identified.
* **Relevant Dates:** October 12, 1984 acquittal; March 21, 1988 review-panel report.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Riggins concerns medication and trial rights, not the same detention authority; it follows this case in the controlling index and cannot supply entering law.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no other nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before May 18, 1992, using eligible historical law not displaced by an actual simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Collins could affect only a proposed generalized substantive-due-process route; its actual simulated holding must be checked if that route becomes necessary. The specific commitment authorities below independently frame this dispute.

### Issue 1: Substantive authority and procedure for continuing psychiatric confinement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No simulated standards tracker yet exists.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; Louisiana Code of Criminal Procedure arts. 654, 655, and 657 governing insanity-acquittee commitment and discharge.
* **Material Other Authority:** Jones v. United States, 463 U.S. 354 (1983), permits initial commitment on an insanity verdict established by a preponderance and rejects an automatic limit equal to the hypothetical criminal sentence. Its explanation ties confinement to continued illness and danger and distinguishes treatment from punishment. Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418 (1979), requires the State to establish ordinary civil-commitment grounds by clear and convincing evidence, not merely a preponderance and not beyond a reasonable doubt. O'Connor v. Donaldson, 422 U.S. 563 (1975), forbids continued confinement of a nondangerous person capable of living safely in freedom. Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715 (1972), requires a reasonable relationship between the nature and duration of commitment and its purpose. United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987), sustains limited preventive detention under a carefully confined criminal pretrial scheme with government proof of dangerousness by clear and convincing evidence, an adversarial hearing, review, and temporal safeguards. These are distinct legal channels; satisfying a condition of one does not establish another.
* **Open Point:** Whether Jones's illness-and-danger limitation excludes Louisiana's distinct continued-detention rule, or leaves room for a nonpunitive dangerous-acquittee category. The ordinary civil-commitment burden does not automatically govern the initial insanity commitment, but the disappearance of its predicate requires identifying a lawful basis for continued restraint.

### Issue 2: Differential treatment of acquittees

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** None supplied.
* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause.
* **Material Other Authority:** Jones permits distinctions grounded in an insanity verdict's evidence of illness and danger. Baxstrom v. Herold, 383 U.S. 107 (1966), rejects withholding ordinary civil-commitment protections merely because commitment follows a criminal sentence. The legally relevant comparison and surviving justification must be identified; the classification is not automatically racial or otherwise suspect.
* **Open Point:** Whether the original verdict continues to justify this differential treatment once current mental illness is disclaimed. Due process can resolve the case without choosing a broader equality rule.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Louisiana's court treated Jones's release language as insufficient to invalidate its own statute and relied on Foucha's failure to prove nondangerousness. Louisiana emphasizes the verdict's demonstrated dangerous conduct, the risk of release, and legislative room to distinguish acquittees from ordinary civil committees. Its strongest limiting argument is that the confinement remains preventive, with judicial review, rather than punishment.

Foucha answers that an acquittal cannot serve as a substitute conviction and that psychiatric detention loses its authorized treatment basis when illness ends. Requiring him to disprove dangerousness allows permanent confinement on medical uncertainty rather than government proof of a present statutory condition. His equality argument is that other persons with comparable asserted dangerousness receive protections denied to him. Neither side can turn the doctor's reluctance to guarantee safety into proof of a new psychiatric diagnosis.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reverse because the existing insanity commitment cannot continue without illness, leaving ordinary lawful civil or criminal proceedings available on their own elements. It could instead uphold a distinct preventive category if it finds substantive authority and constitutionally adequate procedures, explaining the limiting principle separating that category from detention of any predicted dangerous person. A narrower procedural remand is possible if the defect is proof allocation, but cannot cure a substantively impermissible basis merely by improving the hearing. An adequate due process disposition makes the broader equal protection question unnecessary. The record supports no appellate finding of harmlessness or a new diagnosis.

# Riggins v. Nevada

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Riggins v. Nevada
* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 127 (1992); No. 90-8466.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, direct review of criminal convictions following involuntary medication at trial.
* **Simulated Event Date:** May 18, 1992; chronological event date, not a grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Did Nevada violate Riggins's protected liberty and fair-trial rights by continuing antipsychotic medication over his objection through trial without establishing its necessity?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Nevada Supreme Court, 107 Nev. 178, 808 P.2d 535, affirming murder and robbery convictions and a death sentence.
* **Relief Requested:** Reversal and a new trial.
* **Material Record:** Riggins initially requested treatment for voices and sleep difficulties. Mellaril was progressively increased, reaching 800 milligrams daily; he also received Dilantin. After a competency proceeding, he moved to stop medication until trial concluded. A hearing produced differing medical predictions, including testimony that he could remain competent without Mellaril and that the dose appeared high. The court denied the motion in a one-page order without reasons. Riggins presented an insanity defense and testified while medicated. Medical appropriateness is accepted for this review; necessity, dosage effects, and effects on trial participation must not be supplied as appellate findings.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Consent to initial treatment does not resolve the later objected-to treatment. The distinct Eighth Amendment assertion about displaying an unmedicated condition at sentencing was not raised below or in the petition. It is outside the authorized merits scope. The constitutional trial claim and state-law competency requirement are distinct.
* **Relevant Dates:** November 1987 arrest; June 1988 motion to suspend treatment; July 14, 1988 hearing.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Foucha precedes this case in the controlling index and concerns commitment rather than trial medication. Revalidate any relevant actual effective due-process holding; no such simulated disposition is supplied.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no other nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before May 18, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decisions supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Estelle may affect the general treatment of trial prejudice or state-law error, but cannot substitute for the specific forced-medication inquiry. Revalidate its actual holding if material; no same-day Foucha result is assumed.

### Issue 1: Authority and necessity for involuntary medication

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** None supplied.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; state competency law does not itself authorize every means of achieving competence.
* **Material Other Authority:** Washington v. Harper, 494 U.S. 210 (1990), recognizes the protected interest in avoiding unwanted antipsychotic medication and permits treatment of a seriously mentally ill convicted prisoner who is dangerous to himself or others when medically appropriate, using constitutionally adequate professional review. It rejects an automatic requirement of a judicial hearing and heightened proof for that prison setting. Bell v. Wolfish, 441 U.S. 520 (1979), distinguishes legitimate pretrial regulation from punishment. Youngberg v. Romeo, 457 U.S. 307 (1982), addresses professional judgment in civil custody, not prosecutorial control over trial appearance.
* **Open Point:** The precise justification needed when medication is imposed through trial on an objecting detainee. Harper supplies no automatic entitlement to medicate for litigation convenience and does not itself settle competency-restoration treatment at trial. No date-eligible authority supplied here establishes a universal clear-and-convincing burden for this question.

### Issue 2: Trial participation and error consequences

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** None supplied.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments, including meaningful assistance of counsel and a fair adjudication; direct appellate review.
* **Material Other Authority:** Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970), permits necessary courtroom control while recognizing restraints' effects. Estelle v. Williams, 425 U.S. 501 (1976), protects against compelled practices undermining trial fairness. Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967), places on the State the burden of showing constitutional trial error harmless beyond a reasonable doubt when harmless-error analysis applies. Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279 (1991), distinguishes trial errors from structural defects.
* **Open Point:** Whether proven unjustified medication calls for reversal on this record and whether the record can support harmlessness. A serious intrusion is not automatically a newly recognized structural error.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Nevada's court relied on expert testimony informing the jury about medication effects. Nevada argues that maintaining competence permits adjudication and that medical treatment need not be abandoned because an insanity defense is planned. Riggins answers that competence was not shown to require continued medication, that alternatives were not resolved, and that explaining demeanor does not repair impaired communication, attention, or testimony. He need not establish a right to display untreated illness at sentencing to raise the preserved trial claim. The strongest State limiting position requires medically appropriate treatment tied to an actual necessity, rather than a categorical authority derived from custody.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain treatment if the supported justification and trial protections satisfy the relevant constitutional requirements. It may reverse where the State did not establish necessity and cannot demonstrate harmlessness. A remand for post hoc findings must confront whether it could reliably reconstruct the actual trial and contemporaneous treatment need. The record does not justify inventing either actual trial impairment or a finding of no impairment. The unpreserved sentencing claim remains outside the decision.

# Wyatt v. Cole

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Wyatt v. Cole
* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 158 (1992); No. 91-126.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, private defendants' entitlement to qualified immunity in a § 1983 action.
* **Simulated Event Date:** May 18, 1992, chronology only.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Are private parties who invoke an unconstitutional state replevin procedure entitled to the qualified immunity from suit afforded public officials?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, 928 F.2d 718, affirming qualified immunity for Cole and his attorney, Robbins.
* **Relief Requested:** Reversal of that immunity disposition and further proceedings on the civil-rights claim.
* **Material Record:** After a cattle partnership dispute, Cole and Robbins invoked Mississippi's replevin law, posting an $18,000 bond and procuring an order that required the sheriff to seize cattle, a tractor, and other property from Wyatt. The statute gave the judge no discretion to reject the writ once formal prerequisites were met. Following a later hearing, the state court dismissed the replevin complaint and ordered return. Cole did not comply. The federal court held the procedure unconstitutional but gave the private defendants immunity for conduct preceding invalidation. It assumed Cole's potential liability and did not finally decide Robbins's status.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** State action and the underlying merits remain necessary to liability, although the certified immunity question can be resolved on the assumed predicate. Private misuse of a valid statute and participation in enforcement of an unconstitutional statute are not interchangeable. The officials' separate immunity judgments are not reopened by this question.
* **Relevant Dates:** 1986 seizure; federal invalidation in 1989; statutory amendment in 1990.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate brief.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no other nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before May 18, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated holdings supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Hunter may change public-official qualified immunity; Hafer may clarify capacity; Mireles concerns a different absolute immunity. Revalidate actual relevant holdings without extending them automatically to private defendants.

### Issue 1: Attribution and private-party immunity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** None supplied.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. § 1983 and the Fourteenth Amendment.
* **Material Other Authority:** Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co., 457 U.S. 922 (1982), requires a deprivation attributable to a state-created rule or authority and a defendant fairly treated as a state actor through joint action, significant official assistance, or another recognized route. Alleged private misuse alone does not necessarily satisfy these requirements. Lugar expressly reserved private-party immunity. Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800 (1982), gives eligible officials an objective clearly-established-law immunity; Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511 (1985), explains immunity from suit and its immediate appeal consequences. Owen v. City of Independence, 445 U.S. 622 (1980), examines historical immunities and purposes rather than assuming every § 1983 defendant receives the same protection. Dennis v. Sparks, 449 U.S. 24 (1980), does not extend a judge's immunity to private joint actors merely because they participated with the judge.
* **Open Point:** Whether common-law defenses of good faith or probable cause support an affirmative merits defense for private litigants, and whether such a defense amounts to Harlow immunity. No source establishes that attribution carries immunity with it.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Fifth Circuit extended public-official immunity to protect private reliance on presumptively valid state law. Cole and Robbins emphasize notice, access to judicial process, and the unfairness of retrospective damages after statutory invalidation. Wyatt answers that these are private commercial actors, that § 1983's text reaches persons acting under color of law, and that official recruitment and discretionary public service rationales do not transfer. A historical good-faith defense, if supported, differs from immunity against litigation itself. Both liability and any defense must be assessed separately for Cole and Robbins; the latter's attorney status supplies neither attribution nor immunity automatically.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm the private immunity rule or reverse and return the action for resolution of attribution, merits, causation, and any properly preserved defense. Reversal of immunity is not a damages judgment. An independent good-faith defense may be reserved because it was not the immunity actually adjudicated; reservation does not establish or prohibit that defense. An appellate ruling that every invocation of civil process creates state action would exceed the needed decision.

# Wade v. United States

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Wade v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 181 (1992); No. 91-5771.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, sentencing authority and review of a prosecutor's refusal to seek a substantial-assistance departure.
* **Simulated Event Date:** May 18, 1992; chronology, not grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: May a district court reduce a sentence for substantial assistance without the Government's motion, and what judicial review exists over the refusal to move?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fourth Circuit, 936 F.2d 169, affirming the sentence and rejecting inquiry into the prosecutor's motives.
* **Relief Requested:** Sentencing relief or a remand allowing inquiry into the refusal.
* **Material Record:** After his 1989 arrest, Wade supplied information leading to another dealer's arrest. He pleaded guilty to drug offenses and a firearm count. The drug counts carried a ten-year mandatory minimum; the firearm count carried a consecutive five years. The Government made no substantial-assistance motion. Asked what supporting evidence he would present, counsel described Wade's assistance rather than a suspect reason for the refusal. No enforceable Government promise to move is established in this record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The case presents statutory authority and asserted constitutional limits, not breach of a proved plea agreement. The parties treat § 3553(e) and § 5K1.1 as equivalent barriers here; their possible separate operation is not necessary to deciding this record.
* **Relevant Dates:** October 30, 1989 arrest; direct review at the stated event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no other nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before May 18, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated holdings supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Williams v. United States concerns sentencing error and remand, and Wilson concerns allocation of sentence-credit authority. Their actual holdings matter only if adopted propositions reach this statutory motion prerequisite; neither may be inferred from its draft.

### Issue 1: Sentencing authority and reviewable discretion

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** None supplied.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. § 3553(e) permits a below-statutory-minimum sentence upon Government motion reflecting substantial assistance. USSG § 5K1.1 authorizes a guideline departure upon Government motion. Section 924(c)'s firearm consequence is a separate sentence component.
* **Material Other Authority:** Wayte v. United States, 470 U.S. 598 (1985), recognizes broad prosecutorial discretion subject to constitutional limits, including proof of discriminatory effect and purpose for selective prosecution. Bordenkircher v. Hayes, 434 U.S. 357 (1978), preserves lawful plea negotiation, not a general license for forbidden discrimination. Santobello v. New York, 404 U.S. 257 (1971), requires enforcement of a material prosecutorial promise inducing a plea, a branch requiring an actual promise and breach.
* **Open Point:** What showing permits inquiry into withholding a departure motion and what remedy follows a proven unconstitutional refusal. The statute does not equate assistance with an entitlement to a motion. Its prerequisite concerns sentencing authority, not Article III subject-matter jurisdiction. A defendant alleging forbidden purpose bears that claim's evidentiary burden; the newly contested discovery threshold must not be described as already established by this case.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Wade argues that substantial assistance warrants judicial consideration and that unreviewable prosecutorial control permits arbitrariness. He also contends that the district court's view of its power prevented development of his claim. The Government relies on the enacted motion requirement and discretionary assessment of cooperation's value. Its strongest lawful position preserves constitutional and contractual constraints while resisting discovery based only on an assertion that the assistance was useful. The Fourth Circuit's categorical rejection of motive inquiry is broader than its conclusion that no motion supported a departure here.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance can rest on the missing motion and Wade's failure to identify a supported unconstitutional reason, while rejecting absolute insulation from review. Alternatively, a court could remand if the district court foreclosed an adequately proffered claim. No such factual proffer may be invented. A proven unconstitutional refusal could require a tailored correction, but the exact remedy and the interaction of statutory and guideline departures need not be selected when no violation is established. Lawful prosecutorial assessment of usefulness is distinct from race, religion, retaliation, or an independently established irrational purpose.

The jury was told a contribution alone is lawful even with donor business pending, but a demand or acceptance of money in exchange for a specific requested exercise of official power violates the Act. The jury returned guilty verdicts. Its conclusion is reviewed under the applicable favorable-to-verdict evidentiary posture, not replaced by a finding that every contribution buys an official act.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Interpret the offense and the charge as given. No freestanding challenge to federal commerce power or reopening of the rejected entrapment defense is established by this question.
* **Relevant Dates:** Investigation in 1985-1986; July 25, 1986 payment.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** McCormick is preterm authority; Jacobson's actual simulated entrapment holding matters only if a preserved entrapment issue is established, not merely because the same investigation used an undercover agent.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no other nonparticipation identified.

### Issue 1: Statutory elements and the exchange requirement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** None supplied.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. § 1951 prohibits interference with commerce by robbery or extortion; § 1951(b)(2) defines obtaining another's property with consent induced by wrongful force, violence, fear, or under color of official right. The Government bears every charged element beyond a reasonable doubt, including the required interstate-commerce connection; that element is not independently disputed here.
* **Material Other Authority:** Morissette v. United States, 342 U.S. 246 (1952), gives established legal terms their contextual common-law content absent contrary direction. McCormick v. United States, 500 U.S. 257 (1991), requires an explicit promise or undertaking for official action in exchange for campaign contributions and reserves the general inducement question. United States v. Enmons, 410 U.S. 396 (1973), interprets the statutory wrongfulness requirement in its own setting; a different category of extortion does not erase this one's text. Lenity operates after the conventional tools leave genuine ambiguity. Legal construction and instruction adequacy receive independent review, distinct from the verdict's evidentiary posture.
* **Open Point:** Whether official-right extortion requires that the official initiate the transfer, as opposed to knowingly agreeing to the exchange, and whether the charge sufficiently distinguishes a corrupt exchange from legitimate contributions.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Evans argues that “induced” requires action causing payment and that eliminating inducement converts extortion into ordinary bribery beyond the enacted scope. He invokes notice, federalism, and the danger to legitimate contributions. The Government answers that official-right extortion inherited the taking of unauthorized payment for official action, that “induced” does not mean initiated, and that the charge requires a knowing exchange for a specific exercise of power. The Eleventh Circuit treated such knowing acceptance as sufficient without an additional demand; its suggestion that office alone supplies coercion must be distinguished from proof of the exchange. Passive receipt without the requisite knowledge or agreement is not the Government's strongest supported theory.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm by holding that a separately initiated demand is unnecessary and the exchange instruction adequate. It may reverse if the offense requires inducement omitted from the charge, with the appropriate trial-error or insufficiency consequences kept distinct. A failure of an instruction does not automatically prove the evidence insufficient for retrial. The tax conviction is unaffected by a ruling confined to the Hobbs Act count. The Court need not decide the scope of every gift, gratuity, campaign payment, or later statutory bribery offense.

# Quill Corp. v. North Dakota

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Quill Corp. v. North Dakota
* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 298 (1992); No. 91-194.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of a state use-tax collection obligation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** May 26, 1992, chronology only.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: May North Dakota require this mail-order seller to collect use tax despite National Bellas Hess, under the Due Process and Commerce Clauses?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** North Dakota Supreme Court, 470 N.W.2d 203, reversing judgment for Quill.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse the judgment enforcing the collection obligation, including the challenged liability for taxes, interest, and penalties from July 1, 1987.
* **Material Record:** Quill has offices and warehouses outside North Dakota, no employees working or residing there, and no local outlets or sales representatives. It solicits through catalogs, flyers, national advertising, and telephone calls; approximately $1 million in annual sales reaches about 3,000 North Dakota customers by mail or common carrier. Its tangible property in the State is insignificant or nonexistent. The State also invokes licensed ordering software. The trial court held that title passes upon delivery despite a 90-day return guarantee; the state supreme court assumed that ruling correct. A 1987 amendment extends the collection obligation to regular or systematic solicitation, administratively defined as at least three advertisements in twelve months.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The case concerns the seller's collection duty, not whether consumers owe use tax or whether all interstate businesses are immune from state taxation. Both constitutional grounds are presented; either adequate ground can resolve enforceability.
* **Relevant Dates:** Collection liability sought from July 1, 1987; state supreme court decision in 1991.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Wyoming v. Oklahoma may affect a genuinely relevant Commerce Clause proposition once actually decided in the simulation; its discriminatory coal rule does not by itself settle collection nexus.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no other nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before May 26, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decisions supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Revalidate any actual Wyoming holding bearing on state fiscal burdens. Neither same-day tax cases nor later commerce cases supply an assumed change to Bellas Hess.

### Issue 1: Commerce Clause collection nexus and precedent

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** None supplied.
* **Other Governing Law:** National Bellas Hess v. Department of Revenue, 386 U.S. 753 (1967), rejects a use-tax collection obligation for a seller connected to customers only by mail or common carrier, on both Commerce Clause and due-process grounds. Complete Auto Transit v. Brady, 430 U.S. 274 (1977), requires substantial nexus, fair apportionment, nondiscrimination, and fair relation to state services; it rejects a general immunity for direct taxation of interstate commerce. Those four requirements are distinct. Tyler Pipe Industries v. Washington Department of Revenue, 483 U.S. 232 (1987), considers representatives' in-state activity in establishing and maintaining a market; economic magnitude alone is not an already established substitute for every nexus requirement.
* **Material Other Authority:** Bellas Hess's specific collection holding must be reconciled with Complete Auto or openly reconsidered. Congress can authorize state burdens otherwise barred by the dormant Commerce Clause. The legal validity of the obligation is reviewed independently; no heightened evidentiary quantum governs constitutional interpretation.
* **Open Point:** Whether later doctrine has displaced the mail-order collection rule, whether it should now be overruled, and whether Quill's software interests materially distinguish this record.

### Issue 2: Due-process connection

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** None supplied.
* **Other Governing Law:** Miller Brothers v. Maryland, 347 U.S. 340 (1954), requires a definite link or minimum connection with the person, property, or transaction taxed. 
* **Material Other Authority:** International Shoe, 326 U.S. 310 (1945), and Burger King, 471 U.S. 462 (1985), permit personal jurisdiction based on purposeful contacts without literal physical entry. These jurisdiction decisions inform, but do not themselves expressly overrule, Bellas Hess's tax holding. Congress cannot authorize a due-process violation.
* **Open Point:** Whether purposeful sustained solicitation supplies the required tax connection and warrants revising Bellas Hess on this separate ground. That revision may be unnecessary if commerce independently disposes of the case.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Quill invokes Bellas Hess's specific rule and settled business arrangements, distinguishing constitutional collection nexus from general minimum contacts. North Dakota argues that Complete Auto's practical approach, the growth of mail-order commerce, and easier computerized compliance eliminate the old rule's foundations. The State points to its protected consumer market and services, including disposal of mailed catalogs, and argues that remote sellers otherwise gain an advantage over local collectors. The state supreme court accepted these changes and treated economic presence and market benefits as sufficient. Quill responds that multiplying independent collection regimes remains different from the consumer's underlying tax and that benefit alone cannot replace every Complete Auto element.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may retain Bellas Hess's commerce rule and reverse without reaching due process; retain commerce while revising due process; or openly overrule the collection rule and examine all remaining constitutional requirements and retrospective consequences. Treating Complete Auto as controlling without identifying its effect on the specific precedent would leave the decisive conflict unexplained. Software licensing and the assumed title ruling must be assessed on this record, not through invented warehouses or agents. A commerce disposition does not resolve other forms of taxation or license retrospective collection under a rule not actually adopted.
