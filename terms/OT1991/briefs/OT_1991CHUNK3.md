# United States Department of State v. Ray

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States Department of State v. Ray
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 164 (1991); No. 90-747.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of FOIA Exemption 6 redactions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 16, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Does Exemption 6 permit withholding identifying information from State Department interviews with Haitian returnees when the interview content has been released?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, 908 F.2d 1549 (1990), affirming an order requiring unredacted reports.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse the disclosure order as to names and identifying details.
* **Material Record:** Under the United States-Haiti arrangement beginning in 1981, Haiti promised not to prosecute interdicted returnees for illegal departure. State Department personnel interviewed a sample to monitor compliance. Ray and asylum-seeking clients requested the reports to test Government assertions about treatment of returnees. The agency disclosed 25 documents totaling approximately 96 pages, redacting identifiers in 17. The released material described the interviews, dates, sampling, and reported treatment, including isolated reported threats or mistreatment. The reports also contain personal information about family, work, and living conditions. Both courts accepted that interviewees had been promised confidentiality. The lower appellate court recognized significant privacy interests but considered follow-up interviews valuable for checking official monitoring and candor.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The similar-files category is uncontested. Exemptions 1 and 7(C) were raised late and rejected on waiver grounds; that ruling is outside the reviewed question. This is public-record disclosure, not an adjudication of asylum, Haiti's general safety, or a right to litigation discovery.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 16, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated holding supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Earlier *Ardestani* governs a separate immigration fee provision; it does not resolve FOIA access. An effective *Simon & Schuster* speech ruling would not create a right to these records. No direct prior Exemption 6 dependency is identified.

### Issue 1: Privacy, public oversight, and segregability

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Department of Air Force v. Rose*, 425 U.S. 352 (1976), balances personal privacy against the public interest in examining agency action and permits disclosure with identifying details removed. *Department of State v. Washington Post*, 456 U.S. 595 (1982), gives similar files a broad meaning for information applying to particular persons. 
* **Material Other Authority:** *Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee*, 489 U.S. 749 (1989), identifies scrutiny of government performance as the relevant public interest and generally rejects requester-specific need as the FOIA measure; its Exemption 7(C) threshold is more privacy-protective than the text governing this case.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 552(a)(4)(B) places the withholding burden on the agency in de novo judicial review. Exemption 6 requires a clearly unwarranted privacy invasion, with disclosure favored unless that standard is met. Reasonably segregable nonexempt information must be released. A confidentiality promise informs actual privacy expectations but cannot create an exemption by contract.
* **Open Point:** How much identifiers add to oversight after extensive redacted disclosure, and whether reasonably demonstrated investigatory follow-up value counts. No categorical rule excluding all derivative public benefits has been established in the entering authorities identified here.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Department emphasizes that names connect intimate details to particular returnees, identify their cooperation with monitoring, and expose them to unwanted attention in the setting that prompted confidentiality. Requesters emphasize checking the Government's own interviews through independent contact and the importance of truthful asylum-related representations. They need not establish an asylum entitlement to invoke FOIA. The lower court's decision credits that oversight value; the Government responds that identifiers add no direct explanation of agency conduct and the proposed additional findings remain speculative. The record does not contain identified contradictions substantiating inaccurate reports.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Uphold the narrow redactions if the agency meets Exemption 6's balance on this record, or require disclosure if the incremental oversight value warrants the invasion. The Court can reserve a categorical derivative-use question. It should not revive waived exemptions, treat promises as dispositive, or announce that only requesters already proving misconduct may investigate government performance.

[Record and statutory text, 502 U.S. 164](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/502/164); [Rose](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/425/352/); [Washington Post](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/456/595/); [Reporters Committee](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/489/749/).

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the order requiring these identifying details.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Sustain the Exemption 6 redactions while preserving disclosure of the segregable substantive reports.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Protect identities without shielding the monitoring record

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the only preserved exemption question.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Rose* with the agency retaining its statutory burden.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** On this record the identifiers' limited demonstrated additional oversight value does not warrant connecting sensitive interview material to named returnees.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Redacted reports permit examination of what officials asked, what was reported, when interviews occurred, and the sample used. Identifiers would expose intimate facts and cooperation that the interviewees supplied under an accepted confidentiality assurance. The invasion is concrete even without finding that retaliation will occur. Independent verification can be a legitimate public benefit; its weight depends on the demonstrated informational connection and must be considered on the same practical footing as privacy consequences. Here no concrete additional information likely obtainable from identifiers is shown beyond a general hope of checking the reports. That does not establish dishonesty, but neither does it create an irrebuttable official presumption. Together with the extensive segregated disclosure and sensitive content, the agency's showing satisfies the more demanding Exemption 6 threshold.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No requirement to prove wrongdoing before any FOIA request; no categorical exclusion of derivative investigation; no finding that Haiti is safe or that asylum claims lack merit. The result concerns these identifiers and this disclosure record.

# INS v. National Center for Immigrants' Rights, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** INS v. National Center for Immigrants' Rights, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 183 (1991); No. 90-1090.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of a facial statutory challenge to an immigration bond regulation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 16, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Does INA §242(a) prohibit a rule generally conditioning release on refraining from unauthorized employment pending a deportability determination?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, 913 F.2d 1350 (1990), affirming statutory invalidation of 8 C.F.R. §103.6(a)(2)(ii).
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse the facial invalidation and associated classwide prohibition.
* **Material Record:** The 1983 amendment made a no-work bond condition the default, with an exception when the district director determines employment appropriate. Its heading specifies unauthorized employment; the body says employment. INS's contemporaneous explanation and operating instructions exempt authorized workers and protect colorable citizenship claims; the Government represents that asylum applicants are unaffected. Individuals and organizations sued, and a class was certified. No named individual alleged preexisting work authorization. The Ninth Circuit read the rule to bar authorized and unauthorized work, and held that bond conditions must concern appearance or active subversion and require individualized decisions. The earlier remand following the 1986 immigration legislation did not settle those issues.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Only facial statutory authority is reviewed. Individual misapplications and the complaint's constitutional claims were not decided below and are outside the presented scope.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation recorded; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 16, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Earlier *Ardestani* may define the INA's relation to a specific APA fee cross-reference; it does not decide bond authority or immigration procedure's constitutionality. *MCorp* may concern statutory review channels, but does not foreclose this established facial-review posture. Revalidate actual holdings on those precise points.

### Issue 1: Regulatory construction and statutory authority

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** INA §242(a), then §1252(a)(1), authorizes release bonds with conditions prescribed by the Attorney General. *United States v. Witkovich*, 353 U.S. 194 (1957), reads broad immigration powers in statutory context and avoids an unbounded information demand. *Carlson v. Landon*, 342 U.S. 524 (1952), sustains detention tied to congressionally identified risks and individual circumstances. Neither permits every conceivable condition. *Sure-Tan v. NLRB*, 467 U.S. 883 (1984), recognizes immigration law's labor-market protection objective; IRCA's 1986 employer-sanctions scheme reinforces that enacted concern.
* **Other Governing Law:** INA §242(a) delegates bond-condition authority, subject to the statutory scheme; the contemporaneous regulation and instructions define the challenged implementation.
* **Material Other Authority:** Regulatory headings may clarify ambiguity, as statutory headings do under *Mead Corp. v. Tilley*, 490 U.S. 714 (1989). *Bowles v. Seminole Rock*, 325 U.S. 410 (1945), supplies the entering deference rule for an agency's regulatory interpretation unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Statutory authorization remains a separate inquiry. *American Hospital Association v. NLRB*, 499 U.S. 606 (1991), permits appropriate general rules within statutory discretion while retaining relevant individualized adjudication.
* **Open Point:** Whether text, heading, promulgation explanation, and implementation support the narrower reading, and whether the actual process adequately individualizes a default condition. No criminal burden of proving deportability is imported into this facial question.

### Issue 2: Individual determination within the rule

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Same historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A general rule may structure statutory discretion while retaining the individual determinations the delegation requires; a facial challenge differs from proof of a particular erroneous application.
* **Other Governing Law:** The process includes an initial assessment of work-authorizing status, opportunities to establish authorization through documents or status identifiers, a discretionary application under §103.6(a)(2)(iii), and administrative and judicial review of bond conditions. The discretionary applicant must establish compelling reasons; listed factors include employment protection, prior immigration violations, potential relief, and dependent family equities. The Government represents that temporary relief is available for a preliminary showing of likely success where authorization is not readily documented. Those are source-grounded procedures and representations, not findings that every applicant received them.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Carlson* and *American Hospital Association*, discussed above, bear on the relationship between rulemaking and individual assessment; neither resolves an unadjudicated constitutional claim here.
* **Open Point:** Whether the narrower regulation, taken with the described process, necessarily eliminates the individual judgment required by the statute.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

INS argues the rule only adds a bond consequence to unauthorized employment, protects an enacted immigration objective, and allows individualized status and hardship determinations. Respondents emphasize the body's broad wording, the connection between bond and appearance, loss of income needed for counsel or release, and the dangers of a blanket condition. Their statutory facial theory must address the narrower reading and existing avenues for individual consideration; hardships remain relevant to reserved applications and constitutional claims but do not establish that every enforcement is unauthorized.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A narrowing construction grounded in the actual regulation permits resolution of statutory validity without approving restraints on authorized work. If that construction and process fit the delegation, reverse and remand with other claims reserved. If the regulation necessarily forbids authorized work or eliminates statutory discretion, invalidation may follow on the pertinent ground. The Court cannot convert an agency assurance into permission for contrary conduct or resolve unadjudicated constitutional questions.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit enforcement only under the supported unauthorized-work construction and the described individualized process. Leave preserved constitutional and as-applied claims for proper proceedings.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The narrower regulation falls within statutory discretion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide facial statutory authority, not detention policy generally.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply contextual construction and existing administrative doctrine without revising deference rules.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 242(a) permits this default condition against unauthorized employment, understood to exclude authorized workers and retain individualized determination and relief.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The heading, exception, contemporaneous explanation, and operating instructions support the narrower reading independently of treating the agency as final judge of its power. The enacted labor-market provisions connect unauthorized employment to immigration administration; the delegation is not restricted to physical appearance alone. *Witkovich* requires a statutory connection, which this condition has. General rules can structure discretion, but cannot replace the individual status determination. Existing authorization, colorable citizenship, and the regulation's own exceptions therefore matter in each case. The actual channels refute a theory that the text necessarily eliminates all individualized judgment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This construction does not establish a general power to deny lawful work, presume every contested migrant removable, or reject due process and practical-access claims. No administrative deference precedent is overruled; broad disagreement with such deference is unnecessary to the result.

# Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 197 (1991); No. 90-848.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of FELA liability of a state-owned railroad in state court.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 16, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Does FELA retain its cause of action against a state-owned railroad, enforceable in state court, after later sovereign-immunity and clear-statement decisions?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** South Carolina Supreme Court's summary affirmance relying on *Freeman v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission*, 302 S.C. 51, 393 S.E.2d 383 (1990).
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse dismissal and permit the negligence action to proceed.
* **Material Record:** Hilton alleges employment injury caused by the Commission's negligence. The Commission is a state agency and interstate common carrier, created in 1969 and employing approximately 300 people. Hilton initially sued federally but dismissed that action after *Welch* and filed in state court. State courts considered the earlier *Parden* liability holding displaced. South Carolina excludes FELA-covered employment from workers' compensation; the Commission acknowledged Hilton might lack an alternative remedy. Injury, negligence, causation, and damages remain allegations to be proved.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Statutory defendant coverage, state-court amenability, and federal-court Eleventh Amendment immunity are distinct. This case reaches the Supreme Court through review of the state judgment, not renewed district-court review of that judgment.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 16, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Hafer* is directly relevant if its effective holding distinguishes statutory personhood, individual liability, and state immunity when applying *Will*. Its proposed supplement is not law. *Ardestani* concerns the federal fee waiver; its actual construction must not become an automatic state-immunity rule.

### Issue 1: FELA coverage and the surviving force of precedent

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline until any relevant actual *Hafer* ruling is integrated.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Parden v. Terminal Railway*, 377 U.S. 184 (1964), read FELA's every-common-carrier language to include state-owned railroads and also found waiver of federal-court immunity through operating a railroad. *Welch v. Texas Department of Highways*, 483 U.S. 468 (1987), displaced the latter constructive-waiver theory to the extent inconsistent with an unmistakably clear abrogation requirement, but expressly reserved statutory coverage. *Will v. Michigan Department of State Police*, 491 U.S. 58 (1989), excludes States from §1983 personhood and applies a clear-statement principle; its statutory object differs from FELA.
* **Other Governing Law:** FELA, 45 U.S.C. §§51–60, supplies liability and concurrent adjudication. The Supremacy Clause governs enforcement of valid federal claims by competent state courts. *Howlett v. Rose*, 496 U.S. 356 (1990), disallows a state-law immunity defense defeating a federal claim against a defendant federally subject to liability; it does not itself create statutory liability against every State. The Eleventh Amendment governs the federal forum and is not by itself a state-court immunity rule.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Gregory v. Ashcroft*, 501 U.S. 452 (1991), protects federalism through contextual clear-statement construction. *Patterson v. McLean Credit Union*, 491 U.S. 164 (1989), emphasizes the weight of statutory precedent and Congress's corrective role. *Port Authority Trans-Hudson v. Feeney*, 495 U.S. 299 (1990), addresses express consent to federal jurisdiction rather than abolishing immunity.
* **Open Point:** Whether *Parden*'s statutory holding survives independently, and whether demonstrated reliance warrants retaining it despite tension with later immunity doctrine. No later *Seminole Tribe* or *Alden* rule enters this 1991 case.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Hilton relies on the settled interpretation of FELA, Congress's retention of the language, and compensation arrangements built around its coverage. The Commission argues that *Will* and *Welch* require unmistakable intent before a State must face damages in either forum and undermine *Parden*'s foundation. The employer's strongest concern is constitutional federalism, not simply avoiding payment; Hilton's strongest response is that the specific coverage question was reserved and the statutory construction has an independent basis. The absence of another remedy is relevant reliance, not an independent cause of action or waiver.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Preserve the surviving statutory holding and remand the state action, or expressly overrule it after confronting its text, later treatment, federalism rationale, and concrete reliance. One cannot call it already overruled merely because a separate holding failed. A decision allowing this legacy FELA action need not settle every Article I statute or revive federal-court constructive waiver. A remand establishes no negligence or damages entitlement.

[Record and issues, 502 U.S. 197](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/502/197); [Parden](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/377/184/); [Welch](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/483/468/); [Will](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/491/58/); [Howlett](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/496/356/); [Gregory](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/501/452/).

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Retain the specific statutory precedent

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve amenability under this settled FELA construction, leaving negligence merits below.

Displacing the surviving rule here would withdraw an established forum without a demonstrated need to abandon its specific statutory reading. Stone therefore retains this precedent rather than inventing a broad state-immunity exception.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general ruling that Article I always authorizes private damages against States, no implied waiver by merely entering commerce, and no proposition that lack of another remedy itself overrides sovereign immunity. The state-court result follows the particular surviving FELA precedent.

# King v. St. Vincent's Hospital

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** King v. St. Vincent's Hospital
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 215 (1991); No. 90-889.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of statutory military-leave duration.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 16, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Does 38 U.S.C. §2024(d) impose an unstated reasonableness limit on the duration of protected military leave?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, 901 F.2d 1068 (1990), affirming declaratory judgment that a three-year leave falls outside statutory protection.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse that durational bar and recognize the statute's applicability to the requested leave.
* **Material Record:** King, an Alabama National Guard member, applied for and accepted a three-year Active Guard/Reserve appointment as Command Sergeant Major. The tour's length was required by Army regulations for the position. He notified St. Vincent's, requested leave, and reported August 17, 1987. Weeks later the Hospital called the request unreasonable and brought this declaratory action. The district court applied circuit precedent imposing reasonableness and considered three years unreasonable per se. The panel affirmed; one judge agreed the particular request was unreasonable but rejected a per se duration rule.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Both parties accept that this program is covered by §2024(d). The dispute is actual and concerns their present leave and reemployment obligations. No allegation of bad faith is needed to identify the legal issue, and no such finding is supplied.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 16, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated ruling supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Union Bank v. Wolas* is a potentially relevant earlier statutory-duration case: integrate any actual holding about omitted limits while keeping different enactments distinct. Its drafted position is not precedent. *Hilton* precedes this case in the controlling index; consult its actual effective statutory or stare-decisis holding if relevant.

### Issue 1: Statutory duration and contextual interpretation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Section 2024(d) requires requested leave for the period required to perform covered duty and return with protected employment attributes afterward. Other §2024 provisions expressly limit covered periods for other service categories. The distinction between a statutory condition and a judicial preference governs the legal interpretation; no factual proof standard creates a missing duration cap.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Veterans' Reemployment Rights Act as effective in 1991, including the 1980 coverage of AGR service, controls. Later USERRA provisions must not be substituted. An employee still must establish actual statutory coverage and comply with applicable return requirements; silence about duration does not erase those enacted conditions.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Massachusetts v. Morash*, 490 U.S. 107 (1989), reads provisions within the whole statutory scheme. *Fishgold v. Sullivan Drydock*, 328 U.S. 275 (1946), supports protective construction for military reemployment without dispensing with statutory text. The Eleventh Circuit's *Gulf States Paper v. Ingram*, 811 F.2d 1464 (1987), and Fourth Circuit's *Kolkhorst v. Tilghman*, 897 F.2d 1282 (1990), present opposing circuit approaches, not a controlling Supreme Court duration holding.
* **Open Point:** Whether the words leave, employee, and return imply a reasonable-duration qualification despite Congress's express limits elsewhere.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

King invokes the mandatory language and absence of a durational condition. The Hospital argues that continued employment and immediate return are incompatible with such a long absence, emphasizes disruption for substitute employees, and reads the service categories as a hierarchy in which voluntary training receives less protection. King answers that different statutory categories do not prove the asserted hierarchy and that Congress showed how to impose duration limits where it chose them. The question is the meaning of this provision, not whether long absences impose real workplace costs.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reject the extra-textual duration condition and remand, or uphold an implied reasonableness construction if text and scheme support it. Merely removing the per se three-year rule while retaining an unstated balancing test would leave the core statutory question unresolved. Relief need not decide a later failure to return properly, an unrelated accommodation dispute, or a damages amount.

### Issue 1: Enforce the duration Congress selected

# Hunter v. Bryant

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Hunter v. Bryant
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 224 (1991); No. 90-1440.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of denial of qualified-immunity summary judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 16, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Are Secret Service agents entitled to qualified immunity on a claim of arrest without probable cause, and how should the court resolve that defense at summary judgment?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, 903 F.2d 717 (1990), affirming denial of immunity on the probable-cause claim while granting immunity on a separate warrant claim.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse the probable-cause immunity ruling and enter judgment for the agents.
* **Material Record:** On May 3, 1985, Bryant delivered a rambling letter to university offices describing a plot against President Reagan, then in Germany. The letter repeatedly associated “Mr. Image” with the National Council of Churches. Employees reported references to assassination, a throat-cutting gesture, and a statement that the President should have been assassinated in Bonn. Agents Hunter and Jordan read the letter, interviewed employees, entered and searched with Bryant's consent, and found its original. * **Current Through:** Immediately before December 16, 1991, with personal liability assessed against law governing the May 1985 conduct.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated holding supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Actual *Mireles* and *Hafer* decisions may distinguish functional absolute immunity, individual-capacity liability, and personal defenses. ### Issue 1: Probable cause and qualified immunity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Under *Beck v. Ohio*, 379 U.S. 89 (1964), probable cause depends on reasonably trustworthy information sufficient for a prudent person to believe an offense occurred at the time of arrest. *Watts v. United States*, 394 U.S. 705 (1969), distinguishes a true threat under §871 from protected expression in context. Reason to investigate danger does not automatically establish probable cause that the crime of threatening occurred. Probable cause is not proof beyond reasonable doubt and accommodates reasonable factual assessments.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment and §871(a)'s knowing-and-willful threat offense supply the substantive inquiry. *Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents*, 403 U.S. 388 (1971), supplies the recognized Fourth Amendment damages channel; no new public-entity remedy is presented.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Harlow v. Fitzgerald*, 457 U.S. 800 (1982), and *Anderson v. Creighton*, 483 U.S. 635 (1987), protect officers whose conduct does not violate clearly established law understood with appropriate specificity, including a reasonable mistaken belief in probable cause. *Malley v. Briggs*, 475 U.S. 335 (1986), preserves room for reasonable error, not plainly incompetent or knowingly unlawful conduct. These immunity decisions govern adjudication entering 1991; post-arrest decisions do not create retroactive notice of previously unclear substantive law. The Ninth Circuit placed on Bryant the showing that the right was clearly established and treated immunity as an affirmative defense the agents must establish.

* **Open Point:** Whether the agents' belief that a §871 threat occurred was within the reasonable-officer immunity protection, considering the complete contemporaneous information.

### Issue 2: Judicial decision and summary-judgment limits

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Same historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Mitchell v. Forsyth*, 472 U.S. 511 (1985), permits early legal resolution and interlocutory review of qualifying immunity denials. The judge decides the legal defense; material historical disputes retain ordinary factfinding protections. 
* **Other Governing Law:** Rule 56 governs the summary-judgment motion and retains its distinction between a genuine material dispute and unsupported assertion.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby*, 477 U.S. 242 (1986), requires the nonmovant's supported version and favorable reasonable inferences, without allowing allegations or speculation alone to create disputes.
* **Open Point:** Whether the record permits immunity as a matter of law under the correct reasonable-officer test. That another reading of the letter is more reasonable is not alone enough to defeat immunity; conversely, presidential security does not eliminate the statutory-offense connection.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The agents rely on the assassination references, witness accounts, gesture, interview, and possibility that Mr. Image was Bryant's alter ego. Bryant stresses the letter's express identification of an outside group, his cooperation, and the distinction between a delusional warning and a personal threat. The Ninth Circuit referred both to the more reasonable interpretation and to factual development, while also recognizing early judicial immunity decisions. Its language assigning reasonable-officer assessment to a trier of fact must be separated from any legitimate need to resolve actual historical disputes. A later magistrate's detention order cannot supply information the agents lacked when arresting.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may order immunity if no permissible record view defeats it, affirm a properly grounded denial, or vacate and remand to apply the correct legal standard and identify any genuinely disputed predicate facts. Remand does not require a trial automatically. A wholesale revision of qualified immunity is unnecessary to correcting the legal/factual allocation and is not supported merely by a general preference for broader remedial access. The underlying threat offense's guilt and unrelated warrant ruling remain outside the disposition.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision supplied.

### Issue 1: Functional compliance with required appellate notice

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline pending revalidation of *Ibarra*.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Torres v. Oakland Scavenger*, 487 U.S. 312 (1988), treats Rule 3 compliance as jurisdictional while allowing functional compliance rather than formalistic title requirements. *Foman v. Davis*, 371 U.S. 178 (1962), reads notices in context to preserve a manifest appeal intention when the applicable requirements are met. Liberal construction does not excuse failure to identify an appellant or other information the rule actually requires.
* **Other Governing Law:** Rule 3(c) requires the appealing party or parties, the judgment or order appealed from, and the receiving appellate court, and forbids dismissal for informality of form or title. Rule 4 provides the filing period and treats a notice mistakenly filed in the appellate court as filed in the district court on receipt, with transmittal. Rule 3(a) distinguishes the timely notice from later procedural steps. Those rules' 1988/1991 operation controls, not later amendments that changed premature-notice treatment.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Griggs v. Provident Consumer Discount*, 459 U.S. 56 (1982), explains the then-operative effect of a timely posttrial motion on a premature notice. The sources identify a circuit conflict on whether a brief can ever qualify; those lower-court categorical holdings do not supply a contrary Supreme Court rule.
* **Open Point:** Whether this informal brief actually communicates all Rule 3(c) information, an issue the Fourth Circuit left unresolved. No proof quantum or equitable excuse replaces examination of the timely document and record.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Smith argues that his timely brief communicates an appeal and that Rule 3 expressly protects informal form and title. Respondents emphasize that he filed in the appellate court, that briefing and notice are ordinarily separate, and that the document may omit required information. The Fourth Circuit relied on his response to a briefing order rather than an independent intent to initiate an appeal. Smith answers that a document's objective notice function matters, that the rules cure the filing location, and that the alleged omissions should be evaluated rather than assumed immaterial.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the categorical exclusion and remand for content examination, or sustain dismissal if an actually established, uncured content defect independently requires it. The Court need not excuse the first premature notice, deem every brief sufficient, or adjudicate the underlying prison claims. Preserving jurisdictional doctrine does not require elevating a caption over information that actually fulfills the rule.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes and Bands of the Yakima Indian Nation
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 251 (1992); Nos. 90-408 and 90-577.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of consolidated challenges concerning state taxation of reservation fee land.
* **Simulated Event Date:** January 14, 1992.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: May Yakima County tax Indian-owned reservation land held in fee and tax its sale, and does either question turn on the Ninth Circuit's tribal-impact balance?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, 903 F.2d 1207 (1990), sustaining the prohibition of the sales excise tax but remanding the ad valorem issue for a serious-impact inquiry under *Brendale*.
* **Relief Requested:** The County seeks authority for both taxes; the Nation seeks protection from both and opposes the impact-based remand.
* **Material Record:** The 1855 reservation includes trust and fee-patented land; some fee land belongs to tribal members and some to the Nation. Washington imposes an annual real-property tax and a separate excise on sales. In 1987 the County began foreclosure proceedings involving delinquent reservation parcels. The Nation sued for declaratory and injunctive relief, and the district court ruled on stipulated facts for the Nation. The ad valorem burden runs against the realty; the sales excise is imposed on the transaction and secured by a lien. The Nation disputes whether all relevant parcels were patented under the General Allotment Act rather than another allotment statute. No parcel-specific resolution of that issue is supplied.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is the Nation's suit to prevent asserted unlawful taxation of its and its members' reservation interests, through the recognized federal Indian-law channel. Taxability and the source of particular patents precede any final enforcement disposition. Neither tribal membership alone nor a fee label establishes the federal statute authorizing taxation.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation recorded; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before January 14, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated holding supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Hilton* may matter if it actually changes clear-statement or statutory-precedent analysis, but state sovereign immunity is different from tribal tax immunity. *Wolas* may concern coexistence of text and policy, but supplies no tribal-tax authorization. No earlier inventory case directly resolves reservation fee-land taxation.

### Issue 1: Congressional permission and later legislation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Montana v. Blackfeet Tribe*, 471 U.S. 759 (1985), requires unmistakably clear congressional authorization for state taxation of tribes or members on a reservation and resolves statutory ambiguity in their favor. *Goudy v. Meath*, 203 U.S. 146 (1906), sustained taxation after a General Allotment Act fee patent. *Moe v. Confederated Salish & Kootenai Tribes*, 425 U.S. 463 (1976), rejects deriving general reservation-wide personal taxing jurisdiction from allotment, and recognizes the Tribe's federal channel notwithstanding the Tax Injunction Act.
* **Other Governing Law:** General Allotment Act §§5–6 and the 1906 Burke Act proviso govern trust-to-fee conversion; the proviso expressly removes restrictions on sale, encumbrance, and taxation of the specified land. The 1934 Indian Reorganization Act ended new allotments and indefinitely extended existing trust periods, with mechanisms for acquisition into trust. The later Indian-country definition and jurisdiction statutes must be considered rather than assuming that all allotment-era jurisdiction survived unchanged. Sections 1331 and 1362 supply the relevant federal-question/tribal jurisdictional framework.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Morton v. Mancari*, 417 U.S. 535 (1974), gives effect to compatible enactments rather than assuming implied repeal. *McClanahan v. Arizona State Tax Commission*, 411 U.S. 164 (1973), supplies the reservation-tax sovereignty and statutory context. *Brendale v. Confederated Yakima Indian Nation*, 492 U.S. 408 (1989), is fractured and concerns tribal zoning over non-Indian fee land, a different direction of regulatory authority.
* **Open Point:** Whether later enactments remove the earlier specific permission concerning fee land, or preempt the challenged taxes despite surviving text. The County must identify the congressional permission it invokes; governmental convenience does not supply it.

### Issue 2: Legal incidence and scope of permitted taxation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Same historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The character and legal incidence of each tax matter. Permission to tax land does not automatically authorize every activity involving it. *Blackfeet*'s ambiguity rule applies to the scope of the permission.
* **Other Governing Law:** Washington's annual levy burdens property ownership and creates an in rem lien; its excise taxes the sale, though secured against the property. Statutory treatment of tax collection cannot by itself enlarge Congress's authorization.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Squire v. Capoeman*, 351 U.S. 1 (1956), addresses trust allotment and taxation, including language about taxes after fee patent; its discussion must be read in that context rather than as blanket authorization of every transactional tax. *Moe*'s treatment of tax incidence distinguishes burdens on Indians from collection duties for taxes on non-Indians.
* **Open Point:** Patent source and any applicable statute-specific protection remain for remand. No later decision on reacquired fee land or tribal foreclosure immunity supplies a 1992 premise.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The County invokes *Goudy*, the express taxation language, and the surviving status of fee patents. It says the sales tax is sufficiently connected to the land, particularly through its lien. The Nation argues that the IRA and later jurisdiction legislation ended the earlier assimilationist jurisdictional arrangement, and that *Moe* prevents a checkerboard of state authority. The United States supports the Nation, arguing that later statutory protections can preempt taxation without formally repealing all earlier text. Both sides reject the Ninth Circuit's *Brendale* impact test. A strong tribal argument distinguishes preemption from implied repeal and questions extending a proviso specifically addressing early fee patents to every parcel; a strong County answer separates specific land taxation from personal jurisdiction and identifies *Goudy*'s holding for ordinary fee patents.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may recognize the specific land-tax permission while rejecting the broader sales tax, or find that later law defeats the asserted permission under the applicable source analysis. *Brendale* cannot be imported simply because both disputes involve the same reservation. ## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The state supreme court read the name prohibition literally and treated insufficient suburban signatures as disqualifying the whole slate. Reed invokes protection of party identity and genuine support across the offices a new county party seeks to contest. The strongest defense distinguishes support for each separate election from an aggregate county signature demand; the response is that invalidating successful components makes each office depend on an additional constituency's support.

Organizers additionally claim city support should qualify suburban candidates. The Board disagrees: the separate district structure is unchallenged, and the suburban requirement represents only slightly more than 2% of eligible suburban voters according to its uncontested calculation. Reed's omitted-judicial-candidate objection supported an alternative circuit-court ground; the Board rejected it partly because judicial offices belonged to a different political subdivision. On the merits, the Court may sustain the name and whole-slate restrictions if the asserted interests meet the governing scrutiny, or invalidate either without eliminating independent district-support requirements. A partial reversal can leave suburban exclusions intact. The judicial-office objection permits remand for authoritative state construction and any properly necessary constitutional review. The record does not justify granting unconditional established-party status or reviving the unappealed water-district candidates.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Molzof v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 301 (1992); No. 90-838.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the Federal Tort Claims Act's limitation on punitive damages after a bench trial with liability admitted.

* **Relief Requested:** The estate seeks reversal of that federal bar and consideration of the requested damages. The United States seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Hospital employees' admitted negligence during postoperative ventilator care caused irreversible brain damage and permanent coma. The trial court found existing veterans' hospital care adequate and continued residence there in the patient's best interest. It awarded supplemental care but refused duplicative future care and loss-of-enjoyment damages. The patient died after judgment; his personal representative was substituted. The claims rest on simple negligence, not culpability warranting punishment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Liability is conceded. Review concerns a federal statutory classification, not an independent constitutional damages entitlement. The lower court did not finally resolve recoverability under Wisconsin law. Death and substitution do not authorize invented calculations or assumptions concerning state-law survival and offsets.
* **Relevant Dates:** October 31, 1986 operation; August 30, 1990 appellate decision; November 4, 1991 argument.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation identified. Stone replaces Rehnquist.

### Issue 1: Federal punitive-damages limitation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** FTCA liability generally follows the law of the place of the tort under 28 U.S.C. § 1346(b), subject to federal limitations. Section 2674 makes the United States liable like a private individual in like circumstances while withholding prejudgment interest and punitive damages. The meaning of the federal exclusion is a legal question reviewed independently; it is distinct from reviewing damage findings for clear error. Plaintiff must establish recoverable damages under the governing tort law; no heightened proof standard for ordinary compensation follows from this classification dispute.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§ 1346(b), 2674, and Wisconsin tort law. Section 2674 separately substitutes pecuniary compensation where the relevant wrongful-death law permits only punitive recovery. ### Issue 2: State-law entitlement and remedial consequence

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Removal of a federal damages exclusion does not establish the underlying state-law right or amount. Compensation, duplication, offsets and survival retain their applicable legal sources.
* **Other Governing Law:** Wisconsin rules governing medical expenses and loss of enjoyment of life, applied through the FTCA.
* **Material Other Authority:** Sheridan v. United States, 487 U.S. 392, 401 & n.6 (1988), illustrates remand for unresolved state-law issues after resolving the federal FTCA question.
* **Open Point:** Whether Wisconsin recognizes these damages on these facts and permits or requires an offset for care already furnished. The lower courts' federal ground left those matters unsettled.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Seventh Circuit reasoned that paying for already supplied care would punish through duplication and that a comatose patient could not perceive compensation for lost enjoyment. The estate argues that Congress borrowed a settled punitive-damages category tied to punishment and culpable misconduct; a disputed compensatory award does not change categories merely because it is excessive or its value is difficult to measure.

The Government argues that the waiver's compensatory purpose excludes damages exceeding or unrelated to actual loss, and invokes the special wrongful-death clause and the federal nature of other FTCA exceptions. Its position does not demand exact mathematical correspondence in every compensatory award: it accepts reasonable approximations and concedes that the wrongful-death provision's pecuniary measure does not generally govern personal-injury claims. The estate still needs Wisconsin law authorizing recovery; its statutory argument does not compel a particular sum.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may accept the broader federal punitive-effect interpretation and affirm these denials, or reject that interpretation and remand for state-law entitlement and offsets. A federal term-of-art construction does not automatically award both requested categories. Neither the record nor the presented question requires reconsidering admitted liability, the supplemental-care award, general federal immunity, or the constitutionality of damages restrictions. The incomplete Wisconsin-law determination changes the permissible remedy even if the federal question is resolved for the estate.
