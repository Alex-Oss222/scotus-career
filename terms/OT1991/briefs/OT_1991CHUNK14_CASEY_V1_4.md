**Nos. 91-744 and 91-902 — June 29, 1992**
**Version 1.4 — Stone's definitive six-step framework, trimester replacement, coalition posture and disposition**

**Source qualification that must accompany every use of the paternal-support disclosure.** Under §3205(a)(2)(iii) the paternal-support information may be omitted in cases of rape. The statute states: "In the case of rape, this information may be omitted." The permission has no police-reporting condition and is distinct from the spousal-sexual-assault exception to spousal notification in §3209, whose incorporated §3128 reporting condition runs 90 days from commission of the offense.

**A. What version 1.4 decides that version 1.3 left open**

Stone adopts the six-step framework stated in part B as his definitive position and proposes it as the governing rule for regulation of abortion before viability. Version 1.3 declined to displace Roe's trimester timetable while the substitute remained a proposal. Version 1.4 does both in the same decision: it adopts the affirmative protections and displaces the timetable, so that the substitute is operative law the moment the timetable falls. Stone would not join a freestanding elimination of the timetable that leaves no operative substitute; his vote for displacement is his vote for the protections that replace it.

Stone reaffirms the liberty Roe protects. Before viability the decision whether to continue a pregnancy belongs to the patient, who need not establish medical necessity or obtain official approval of her reasons. After viability the State may prohibit elective abortion, subject to the required protection of life and health. The State may support childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval of the protected decision does not by itself justify obstructing it, and a reduction in abortions produced by coercion, delay or lost access is not a benefit the State may claim. Roe, 410 U.S. 153–165; Doe, 410 U.S. 192–200; Carey, 431 U.S. 686–689.

Stone qualifies Akron and Thornburgh only insofar as their disclosure reasoning categorically excludes concise, accurate notice of available assistance or alternatives because the information might favor childbirth. Their protections against unjustified delay, coercion, misleading communication and unnecessary medical restriction are retained and are applied through the framework. Akron, 462 U.S. 430–450; Thornburgh, 476 U.S. 758–771.

**B. The six-step framework**

The governing question is whether an identified requirement respects the protected decision and has a justification adequate to the interference it actually causes. The steps are applied in order.

**Step 1. Identify the actual legal duty imposed.** State the statutory duty, its authoritative construction, the conduct it changes, its exceptions and the consequences of noncompliance. Separate the duty from a lower court's finding about how the duty operates in practice; both matter, but they are different facts.

**Step 2. Identify the duty's function and asserted justification.** Distinguish medical safety, informed consent, permissible parental involvement, public-health administration, financial accountability and persuasion. The asserted purpose must explain the particular obligation. A purpose of obstructing protected choice, shown by objective evidence of design, context and expected operation, independently defeats the requirement; opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Truthful information that persuades through voluntary understanding is not an obstacle.

**Step 3. Establish the concrete and cumulative interference with protected care.** Challengers must show a concrete addition attributable to the duty: added appointments, material expense, consequential delay, coercion, privacy exposure, lost provider capacity or clinical harm. The comparison is between access for the same patients in the same circumstances with and without the challenged duty; for a jointly challenged package, the package is what is removed from the comparison. Account for interaction with existing requirements and with actual provider capacity, and count each consequence once. Poverty, distance and transportation are circumstances in which the law operates; they are not themselves the State's doing, but they explain why an additional state-imposed condition may impair access. Define the affected population from the statute's operation and the record, independently of the constitutional conclusion, and examine evidence-supported subgroups; do not dilute their burdens by averaging across everyone who is pregnant. Reliable evidence of predictable effects may suffice before enforcement.

Hypothetical illustration: a 24-hour interval that interacts with a clinic operating one day a week produces a week's additional delay for that clinic's patients. That consequence is evaluated once, as the actual operation of the interval in that setting.

**Step 4. Apply the constitutional protections in the following order.** These protections overlap and operate in sequence. Once an earlier protection resolves the case, the later ones are not reached to rescue the requirement.

(a) Categorical prohibitions. A requirement that prohibits the protected previability decision, transfers effective control of it to another person, or prevents timely care necessary to preserve life or health is invalid. No compelling-interest argument rescues it. Roe; Doe; Danforth, 428 U.S. 69–75.

(b) The substantial-obstacle prohibition. A requirement whose purpose or effect is to seriously obstruct the practical opportunity to obtain timely, clinically appropriate previability care, judged in the circumstances of the people it actually affects, is invalid. Absolute impossibility is not required, and the fact that some patients overcome the restriction does not establish that access remains constitutionally adequate. The State's preference for childbirth cannot justify the obstacle. A safety label does not change the classification of an established obstacle.

Hypothetical illustrations: a mandatory second clinic visit may add only expense for some patients while seriously obstructing timely access for an identifiable group whose clinic schedules, transportation, employment or caregiving make a second trip impracticable within the clinical window. A narrowly targeted specialist-care requirement for a demonstrated medical risk may be a serious burden that is nonetheless justified, if the State establishes the medical need and shows that suitable alternative care is actually and timely available.

(c) Severe interference. Where no categorical prohibition or substantial obstacle is established, a requirement that severely interferes with protected access must, at a minimum, serve a compelling permissible interest and be narrowly tailored to it. General invocations of safety, administrative convenience or potential life do not make that showing. This protection is a fallback that operates below the obstacle prohibition, not an exception that permits substantial obstacles on a showing of compelling interest. It is access-based; it is not a rule for other constitutional claims. Carey, 431 U.S. 686–689.

(d) Meaningful lesser interference. Where interference is meaningful but not severe, the State must substantiate its permissible purpose and the connection between that purpose and the requirement, and must answer any concrete, practicable and substantially less burdensome alternative the challengers identify. Perfect legislation, professional unanimity and refutation of every imaginable alternative are not required. Stone states this rule and its allocation of the burden of justification as a proposed doctrinal development. It requires justification of its own, which he supplies: a right that permits every individually tolerable obstacle is not protected in operation, and the State that imposes an additional condition on access is the party that knows and can prove what the condition accomplishes.

The threshold for a meaningful burden is administrable. Challengers identify the legal requirement and establish a concrete, nontrivial additional burden attributable to it: an additional appointment, material expense, consequential delay or reduced usable provider capacity. Hypothetical illustrations: a receipt checkbox completed during an existing consultation, with no other demonstrated consequence, ordinarily does not trigger this review; an additional required clinic trip ordinarily does. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly cutoff is adopted; courts evaluate affected patients' actual circumstances from testimony, records, reliable clinical evidence and supported practical inferences. Crossing the threshold triggers review, not invalidation. Falling below it ends this access inquiry and leaves independent constitutional objections, such as informed consent, clinical judgment, confidentiality and compelled expression, fully available.

**Step 5. Protect professionally defensible clinical judgment and truthful communication.** A claimed medical benefit is evaluated against reliable clinical evidence, pertinent professional standards and competent contrary evidence; substantial professional agreement receives substantial evidentiary weight, and a burdensome departure from it requires an explanation. Professional associations have no legislative veto and unanimity is not required. Necessary treatment must remain available on timely, good-faith clinical judgment, assessed clinically as to physical and mental health, without requiring deterioration into catastrophe; preference alone is not an emergency. Duties must be sufficiently definite and carry appropriate culpability rules, so that a later professional disagreement, without more, is not punishable wrongdoing. Fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remain subject to lawful enforcement; no general immunity is created. Clinicians may correct or qualify state information, and patients need not endorse any view to obtain care. Doe; Colautti, 439 U.S. 394–401.

**Step 6. Tailor the disposition and remedy to the established violation.** State separately the duty and affected group, the proven or reliably predicted interference, the justification and its fit, the invalid requirement or application, and the scope of effective relief and the lawful provisions that remain. Distinguish proof of a violation affecting particular patients from the justification for facial relief; explain the scope of any injunction and its relationship to the challenged duty's contribution. A cumulative violation does not invalidate unrelated provisions. When a duty falls, its dependent certifications and enforcement fall with it, and independently valid receipt obligations remain. An individual claim does not fail because others can comply; broader relief requires a demonstrated defect of corresponding scope. Stone does not make adoption of a new general facial-challenge formula a condition of adopting his substantive reasoning; his qualification of Ohio v. Akron's no-valid-applications formulation is confined to the spousal-notice application in part E. Ohio v. Akron, 497 U.S. 514; Brockett, 472 U.S. 501–507.

**C. Replacing the trimester timetable while making the substitute protections operative**

The framework applies throughout the previability period. Crossing a trimester boundary does not change the governing constitutional standard, and the patient's protected choice does not depend on medical necessity or official approval of her reasons at any previability stage. Gestational age remains relevant where evidence connects a particular stage, procedure, risk and requirement; medically supported regulation remains possible on that showing, but advancing gestation is not automatic permission to obstruct previability choice.

Viability remains the boundary for prohibiting elective abortion. It is an individualized, medically supported determination, not a fixed week written into the Constitution. Timely care necessary for life or health is protected at every stage, on the terms of Step 5.

Precedent displaced: Roe's trimester timetable as the rule determining when health and potential-life regulation may begin, and the applications of Akron and Thornburgh that rest on that timetable or on the categorical disclosure reasoning qualified in part A. Precedent retained: Roe's protected previability choice, viability boundary and life-and-health safeguard; Doe's protection of clinical judgment; Danforth's rejection of a spousal veto and its permission for ordinary consent and confidential health reporting; Bellotti and Ashcroft on parental involvement with an effective bypass; Akron's holdings against unjustified delay and unnecessary medical restriction, now applied as instances of the framework. Existing injunctions entered under the displaced timetable are not disturbed by this decision alone; they are conformed on remand, provision by provision, under Step 6. The concrete applications to Pennsylvania's provisions are in part E.

**D. Coalition posture and authorization**

Stone presents the complete framework as his proposal and defends it as his operative position. He authorizes no change to its substance to attract a join: the order of the protections in Step 4, the categorical prohibitions, the substantial-obstacle rule, the severe-interference minimum and the proposed lesser-burden rule are what he proposes. He accepts wording accommodations that do not alter substance, and he accepts severable joins: a Justice may join the categorical prohibitions, the substantial-obstacle rule, the severe-interference minimum, the trimester replacement or a provision-specific application without joining the whole. A shared label or a shared disposition is not agreement with his reasoning, and Stone does not count it as such.

If the complete framework obtains a majority, Stone, as the Chief in that coalition, assigns the framework opinion to himself unless retaining the coalition requires another author. Where separate coalitions support other portions, their assignment follows the Court's ordinary rule. No Court-wide result is prescribed.

**E. Stone's disposition of each challenged requirement**

Each disposition is Stone's application of the framework to the actual record.

1. Medical emergency, §3203 and §3205(b): sustain under the appellate construction that compliance cannot create a significant threat to life or health, covering the disputed obstetric conditions, on timely good-faith clinical judgment. The §3205(c) disclosure defense does not substitute for an effective emergency exception. No standalone vagueness claim is decided.

2. Mandatory 24-hour interval, §3205(a)(1)–(2): invalidate. The record establishes an additional visit, expense and consequential delay attributable to the interval. Under Step 4(b), on this record the interval seriously obstructs timely access for an identifiable group of patients; independently, under Step 4(d), a general interest in deliberation does not substantiate a universal compulsory reconsideration period after every patient's informed choice, and individualized counseling is a practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative the State has not answered. Akron independently supports this result. Remove the dependent timing certification and enforcement; do not transfer the interval into parental consent.

3. Required information and its delivery, §3205(a)(1)–(4) and §3208: sustain accurate information material to consent and the concise, nonmisleading notices of available materials, possible assistance and paternal support, with the rape omission permission stated as above and legal support liability not represented as guaranteed payment; materials are supplied only if the patient chooses to review them. Invalidate the exclusion of qualified personnel from routine disclosures: it adds an appointment or scheduling burden without demonstrated benefit beyond the physician's retained responsibility for individualized assessment, which is preserved. Retain written certification only for surviving lawful duties. No materials remand or interim injunction.

4. Parental consent, §3206: sustain the one-parent structure with the confidential, expeditious independent bypass on maturity and informed consent or best interests, with emergency care not awaiting the process. No attendance-specific vacatur, remand or injunction; the statute does not expressly require an additional parental clinic visit and the disputed construction is unresolved. An established unnecessary attendance requirement remains challengeable. No adult-incapacity claim is decided.

5. Spousal notification, §3209, and §3214(a)(12): invalidate the notice requirement and its dependent enforcement under Step 4(a) and (b). Compelled disclosure gives abusive or coercive spouses effective control over the protected decision, the exceptions leave important threats uncovered, and the absence of a formal consent requirement does not cure effective control. Danforth's protection against a spousal veto extends to a notice requirement shown to produce that result. For this requirement Stone qualifies Ohio v. Akron's no-valid-applications formulation: women who would notify voluntarily do not supply valid restrictive applications defeating relief, and effective obstruction is shown for a large fraction of the independently defined group required to disclose against their wishes; no numerical cutoff is adopted. Invalidate the notice-given and reason-for-nonnotification fields; preserve the independently severable confidential marital-status field.

6. Referring-source identification, §3214(a)(1): invalidate the blanket demand. The specific findings of referral deterrence despite confidentiality establish meaningful interference; indiscriminate identification of referring physicians, agencies and services adds insufficient regulatory value beyond performing-physician identification and targeted inquiry, which are preserved.

7. Automatic public release, §§3207(b) and 3214(f): invalidate the challenged release requirements. Taxpayer accountability is legitimate, and the record supports deterrence and exposure attributable to compelled affiliation and trimester disclosures beyond facilities' existing visibility. The blanket scope is not justified when confidential oversight and public accounting tied to expenditures remain available. Institutional accountability is distinguished from patient-identifying information, which is not at issue. Confidential collection and tailored public accountability are preserved.

8. Other confidential reports, §§3214(a)(8), (10) and (11): sustain. These limited duties record relevant judgments and support lawful oversight without prior approval, without prescribing additional diagnostic tests, and without authorizing punishment for reasonable professional disagreement. Confidentiality and concrete challenges to unlawful enforcement remain.

9. Reassessed narrow outcomes: emergency protection, accurate medical content, optional materials and chosen receipt, basic receipt certification, one-parent consent with bypass, the limited clinical-basis reports and the confidential marital-status field are reassessed under the framework and sustained on the same terms. The distinctions between emergency treatment and the information-omission defense, between optional materials and mandatory notices, and between confidential reports and public institutional disclosures are preserved. No statutory parental attendance requirement and no police-reporting condition on the paternal-support rape permission are invented.

**F. Judgment Stone would enter**

In No. 91-902, affirm the invalidation of spousal notification. In No. 91-744, affirm in part, reverse in part and remand for implementation and severability consistent with part E: reverse the approval of the mandatory interval, unjustified physician exclusivity, blanket referring-source identification, the challenged automatic public-release requirements and the notice-related reporting fields; preserve the specified lawful requirements; conform the existing injunction provision by provision. Do not order the materials or parental-attendance vacaturs, remands or interim injunctions proposed in version 1.2.
