**A. Stone’s reasons for preserving the right and refining its protection**

Stone would reaffirm the liberty protected by Roe. Before viability, the decision whether to continue a pregnancy belongs to the patient. She need not establish medical necessity or obtain official approval of her reasons. After viability, the State may prohibit abortion subject to the required protection for life and health. States may provide greater protection and regulate competent practice, informed consent, safety and legitimate administration within this constitutional minimum. [Roe, 410 U.S. 153–165](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/410/113/)

The State may support childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstructing the protected decision. A decline in abortions caused by informed, voluntary choices differs legally from a decline caused by coercion, delay or loss of access. The latter cannot justify itself.

Stone proposes an affirmative replacement for Roe’s trimester machinery because a regulation’s justification and interference cannot reliably be determined by gestational stage alone. His replacement preserves the viability boundary, practical access, clinical judgment and protection against unjustified restrictions. Reliance on the ability to make reproductive decisions weighs against withdrawing those protections. A change in the Court’s membership supplies no legal reason for doing so.

**Stone does not separately endorse eliminating the trimester framework while leaving its replacement unresolved.** His preferred departure is the substitution of the substantive protections stated below. Without that substitution, his alternative reasoning applies entering precedents, except for the particular disclosure qualification he expressly identifies here. This defines Stone’s position; it does not dictate how other Justices must divide the questions.

He would qualify Akron and Thornburgh only insofar as their disclosure reasoning categorically excludes concise, accurate notice of available assistance or alternatives merely because it might favor childbirth. He preserves their protection against unnecessary delay, coercion, misleading communication and unjustified medical restrictions. The distinction permits informed choice while protecting the patient’s authority to choose. [Akron](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/462/416/); [Thornburgh](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/476/747/)

**B. Stone’s suggested undue-burden test**

The governing question is whether an identified requirement respects the protected decision and has a justification adequate to the interference it actually causes. The following sequence separates constitutional boundaries, evidence, justification and relief.

**1. Identify the protected decision and the actual legal obligation.**

Specify the statutory duty, authoritative construction, affected conduct, exceptions and consequences of noncompliance. Determine whether the law prohibits a protected previability decision, gives another person effective control, or prevents timely care necessary to preserve life or health. Those violations cannot be justified by balancing their consequences against the State’s preference for childbirth. Genuine regulation of demonstrably unsafe practice remains permissible.

**2. Identify the function and purpose of the requirement.**

Separate medical safety, informed consent, permissible parental involvement, public-health administration, financial accountability and persuasion. The asserted purpose must explain the particular obligation. Evidence that a requirement was designed to obstruct protected choice defeats it independently. Opposition to abortion, standing alone, does not establish that purpose; design, context and expected operation matter. Information that persuades through voluntary understanding is not itself an obstacle.

**3. Establish the actual and cumulative interference.**

Require a concrete showing of added delay, visits, expense, travel, coercion, privacy exposure, loss of providers or clinical harm. Examine the people upon whom the requirement operates, defined independently of the ultimate finding of unconstitutionality. Assess its additional burden in the existing setting, including interactions with other requirements. Count exceptions and alternatives only when practically usable.

Reliable evidence of predictable effects can suffice before enforcement. Poverty or distance does not automatically become a constitutional injury attributable to the State, but each may explain why a new state-imposed condition substantially impairs access. Courts must identify that causal connection and avoid double-counting. A burden need not make abortion literally impossible to be constitutionally serious.

**4. Apply distinct rules for prohibited obstacles, severe interference and lesser burdens.**

- **Protected boundary:** Stone would invalidate a requirement whose purpose or effect is to create a substantial obstacle to obtaining otherwise clinically appropriate, protected previability care. Labeling the requirement a safety measure does not establish that the care is inappropriate.
- **Other severe interference:** A severe restriction falling outside an independently forbidden category must, at a minimum, satisfy a compelling permissible interest and narrow tailoring. Satisfying that minimum does not override an independent constitutional prohibition. The reproductive-liberty cases supply a basis for demanding strict justification, rather than treating a severe burden as ordinary regulation. [Carey, 431 U.S. 686–689](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/431/678/)
- **Meaningful lesser burdens:** Absence of a substantial obstacle does not establish validity. Stone would also invalidate a demonstrated, nontrivial interference when the requirement lacks adequate justification or fit. Once challengers establish such an interference from an additional condition on access or a departure from ordinary competent practice, the State must substantiate the condition’s function and explain why its burden is warranted. A concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative serving the same function requires an answer; the mere possibility of a cheaper rule does not decide the case.

That last branch is Stone’s expressly proposed extension and allocation of the justification burden. It is not presented as an already adopted universal rule. Ordinary consent, competent medical standards and limited confidential reporting do not automatically require strict scrutiny, new legislative studies or proof of the least costly imaginable alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may substantiate a rule; a label alone cannot answer concrete contrary evidence. No new universal standard of proof governs disputed facts.

**5. Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication in implementation.**

A claimed medical benefit must be assessed using relevant clinical evidence, professional standards and competent contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement deserves substantial evidentiary weight. A burdensome departure requires an explanation. Neither professional unanimity nor approval by a particular association is a constitutional prerequisite.

Necessary treatment must remain available on timely, good-faith clinical judgment; the patient need not deteriorate until death is imminent. Health includes relevant physical and mental conditions assessed clinically, not merely a preference relabeled as an emergency. Routine documentation may record a judgment without becoming prior permission to exercise it. [Doe, 410 U.S. 191–200](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/410/179/)

Stone would require sufficiently definite duties and appropriate culpability rules. A later professional disagreement, without more, cannot establish punishable wrongdoing. That is his proposed protection for professionally defensible judgment, not a claim that Colautti created general immunity. Fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remain subject to lawful enforcement. Clinicians may correct or qualify state information; patients need not endorse an ideology to receive care. [Colautti, 439 U.S. 394–401](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/439/379/)

**6. Decide the challenged requirement and tailor relief.**

State separately the obligation, affected group, proven or reliably predicted burden, justification, fit and remedy. Preserve severable lawful duties. Distinguish confidential collection from public release, information from compelled agreement, and actual statutory commands from disputed implementation.

Stone does not make a new general facial-challenge formula a condition of adopting his substantive reasoning. Apply the relevant precedents to the particular relief requested; identify any necessary departure expressly. An individual claim does not fail merely because other people can comply. Broader relief requires a demonstrated defect of corresponding scope, not a hypothetical worst case. [Ohio v. Akron, 497 U.S. 514](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/502/); [Brockett, 472 U.S. 501–507](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/472/491/)

**C. Independent grounds and the Souter fallback**

Stone’s narrower grounds remain available independently of his proposed synthesis. In the precedent-based approach expressed by Souter in this simulation, Stone would identify the precise holding governing each requirement and demand a sufficient legal justification for departing from it.

Akron independently supports invalidating the present fixed waiting period and unnecessary exclusion of qualified personnel from routine disclosures. Doe protects against unnecessary displacement of clinical judgment. Danforth distinguishes permissible consent and confidential reporting from interference with the protected decision. The minors’ cases supply their own safeguards. Stone’s stated qualification concerning accurate assistance notices is a specific departure, not an implicit abandonment of those precedents. [Akron](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/462/416/); [Danforth](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/428/52/)

**If no complete replacement framework is adopted, Stone would decide on those provision-specific grounds.** He would not infer a general new rule from differently reasoned dispositions. Agreement on those grounds does not require agreement with his entire six-step proposal.

Stone can also agree to a common minimum preserving protected choice, effective life-and-health protection and at least strict justification for severe interference, while expressly reserving his stronger protections against substantial obstacles and unjustified lesser burdens. That agreement does not imply that every lesser burden is valid.

The fallback concerns reasoning. It does not adopt Souter’s entire opinion or change Stone’s reporting dispositions below. A contrary holding actually adopted by the Court governs lower courts at its proper scope; Stone’s reliance on entering precedent explains his disagreement rather than authorizing disregard of the resulting law.

**D. Stone’s disposition of each challenged requirement**

**1. Medical emergency — sustain under the protective construction.** Accept the appellate construction that compliance cannot create a significant threat to life or health. It must permit timely good-faith treatment, including the disputed obstetric conditions, before danger becomes catastrophic. Section 3205(c)’s disclosure defense does not substitute for an effective emergency exception. Stone does not separately decide an unnecessary standalone vagueness claim.

**2. Mandatory 24-hour waiting period — invalidate.** The record establishes additional visits, cost and delay. A general interest in deliberation does not adequately justify a compulsory reconsideration period after every patient’s informed choice. The requirement fails Stone’s lesser-burden analysis and, independently, Akron. Remove its dependent certification and enforcement requirements; do not transfer the invalid delay into parental consent.

**3. Required information and its delivery — preserve lawful content; invalidate unjustified physician exclusivity.** Sustain accurate information material to consent and concise, nonmisleading notices about assistance, paternal support and available materials. Paternal-support information may be omitted in cases of rape; legal liability must not be represented as guaranteed payment. Materials are supplied if the patient chooses to review them, without compelled reading or agreement.

Invalidate exclusion of qualified personnel from routine disclosures when it adds burden without adequate justification. Preserve the clinician’s responsibility for individualized medical assessment. Retain written certification only for surviving lawful duties.

Reject the requested additional materials remand and interim injunction on this record. Directory omissions and misinformation from some listed organizations do not sufficiently identify the unlawful state communication or compulsory distribution practice necessary for that relief. This does not adjudge every publication accurate or foreclose a concrete implementation challenge.

**4. Parental consent — sustain the permissible one-parent structure and effective independent bypass.** Retain the minor’s direct access to a confidential, expeditious decision based on maturity and informed consent or, alternatively, best interests. Emergency care cannot await that process. [Bellotti II, 443 U.S. 643–644](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/443/622/); [Ashcroft, 462 U.S. 490–493](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/462/476/)

Reject the proposed attendance-specific vacatur, remand and interim injunction. The statutory text does not expressly require an additional parental clinic visit, and the disputed construction remains unresolved. A later established or credibly threatened unnecessary attendance requirement remains challengeable; bypass does not automatically justify every added burden on an already-consenting family. Stone does not decide an unpresented adult-incapacity claim.

**5. Spousal notification — invalidate §3209 and its dependent enforcement.** The record establishes that compelled disclosure gives abusive or coercive spouses practical control over protected decisions. The exceptions leave important threats uncovered. Focus on women whose conduct the requirement actually changes; unaffected marriages cannot dilute the demonstrated constitutional injury. The absence of a formal consent requirement does not cure effective control. This application extends Danforth’s protection against spousal veto to a notice requirement shown to produce that result. Voluntary consultation remains permissible. Separate speech, equality and privacy grounds need not be reached.

For this notice requirement, Stone expressly qualifies Ohio v. Akron’s no-valid-applications formulation: women who would notify voluntarily do not supply constitutionally valid restrictive applications that defeat relief. He finds the evidence establishes effective obstruction in a large fraction of the independently defined group required to disclose against their wishes. No numerical cutoff or general facial formula is adopted. Existing exceptions do not cure that defect, and judicially adding categories of threatened harm would reconstruct the statute while still forcing endangered patients to establish an entitlement to secrecy. Severing the compulsory-notice requirement, while preserving voluntary consultation and independent reporting, supplies effective relief of the necessary scope.

Invalidate the notice-compliance and reasons-for-nonnotification components of §3214(a)(12). **Preserve the independently severable, confidential marital-status field.**

**6. Referring-source identification — invalidate the blanket demand in §3214(a)(1).** Stone credits the specific evidence of referral deterrence despite confidentiality. He concludes that indiscriminate identification of referring physicians, agencies and services adds insufficient regulatory value to justify that interference. Preserve performing-physician identification and targeted inquiry into actual clinical responsibility or suspected misconduct. This is Stone’s independent application; Souter’s fallback reasoning does not supply agreement with this conclusion.

**7. Automatic public release — invalidate the challenged release requirements in §§3207(b) and 3214(f).** Taxpayer accountability is legitimate. Stone nevertheless credits the district findings of deterrence and exposure and infers that compelled affiliation and trimester disclosures add a meaningful burden despite facilities’ existing public visibility. He finds the blanket scope insufficiently justified when confidential oversight and public accounting more closely tied to expenditures remain available.

That incremental-burden finding is Stone’s contested inference from the record. It does not assume public disclosure of patient identities, treat lawful protest itself as an injury, or claim that Thornburgh automatically resolves these different reports. Preserve confidential collection and appropriately tailored public accountability. [District court, reporting findings](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/744/1323/1797783/); [Court of appeals, reporting analysis](https://openjurist.org/947/f2d/682/planned-parenthood-of-southeastern-pennsylvania-v-casey)

**8. Other confidential reports — sustain the limited challenged duties.** Preserve ordinary clinical reporting and §§3214(a)(8), (10) and (11), concerning medical necessity, emergency judgment and gestational-age determination. They may record relevant judgments and support lawful oversight without becoming advance approval. They do not prescribe additional diagnostic tests merely because an official would prefer them, or authorize punishment based solely on reasonable professional disagreement. Preserve confidentiality and concrete challenges to unlawful enforcement.
