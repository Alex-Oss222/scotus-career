# Nordlinger v. Hahn

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Nordlinger v. Hahn
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 1 (1992); No. 90-1912.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, authorized for hearing in the simulation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1992-06-18, historical disposition date used only for event ordering, not a certiorari-grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation, not a verified verbatim petition quotation: Whether California’s acquisition-value property-tax assessment scheme and challenged reassessment exceptions violate equal protection.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** California Court of Appeal, 225 Cal.App.3d 1259, 275 Cal.Rptr. 684, affirming dismissal without leave to amend.
* **Relief Requested:** Nordlinger seeks invalidation of the unequal assessment treatment and relief from her resulting tax burden; the assessor seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Proposition 13 adopted acquisition-value assessment with limited annual increases, reassessment on ownership changes or new construction, and exceptions for certain over-55 replacement residences and parent-child transfers. Nordlinger bought a Los Angeles home after renting locally and paid substantially more property tax than longer-term owners of comparable homes. The disparity is a feature of the statewide constitutional policy, not an assessor’s unexplained departure from a uniform current-value rule.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Nordlinger has her own tax injury. She had already lived in California before purchase and alleges no personal interference with interstate movement. The record supplies neither a special relationship nor an obstacle allowing her to assert absent travelers’ claims. Distinguish unequal taxation from a fundamental right to a particular assessment method.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires consolidation in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation is identified in the historical opinion.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 1992-06-18, using the pre-term historical baseline unless displaced by an effective simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied. Earlier drafted positions are not Court holdings.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** General Motors v. Romein could affect treatment of legislative reliance interests; Burlington Northern v. Ford could affect ordinary classification analysis. Revalidate their actual holdings if issued, without importing the historical results.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Fourteenth Amendment; review on dismissal accepts properly pleaded facts but legal rationality remains an independent question. State constitutional provisions define the challenged policy.

### Issue 1: Rational assessment classifications

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment Equal Protection Clause governs the challenge to California’s acquisition-value property-tax classifications and exceptions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Ordinary property, acquisition-date, and age classifications receive rational-basis review absent a separately implicated fundamental right or suspect classification. Allied Stores v. Bowers, 358 U.S. 522 (1959), and Lehnhausen v. Lake Shore Auto Parts, 410 U.S. 356 (1973), allow broad tax classifications with the challenger bearing the showing of irrationality. Allegheny Pittsburgh Coal v. County Commission, 488 U.S. 336 (1989), invalidated gross disparities from administration inconsistent with the State’s current-value system. Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), governs assertion of third parties’ rights.
* **Open Point:** Whether intentional acquisition-based disparities rationally serve reliance and continuity interests, and how Allegheny Pittsburgh applies to an enacted assessment system.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The California courts upheld the assessment scheme. Nordlinger emphasizes extreme disparities for comparable property, unequal public-service contributions, preferential inheritance, and Allegheny Pittsburgh. The State identifies predictability for existing owners, protection against displacement from appreciation-driven taxes, neighborhood continuity, and facilitating suitable replacement housing for older owners. The challenger’s criticisms of lock-in and unfair distribution do not themselves establish suspectness or a forbidden governmental purpose.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If ordinary rational basis is satisfied, affirm dismissal even if the scheme produces serious inequities. If the classification or exceptions have no rational lawful basis, reverse for appropriate tax relief, with state-law implementation and remedial options separately considered. A right-to-travel holding requires a proper claimant or justified third-party assertion and need not be reached here.

# Georgia v. McCollum

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Georgia v. McCollum
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 42 (1992); No. 91-372.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, authorized for hearing in the simulation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1992-06-18, historical disposition date used only for event ordering, not a certiorari-grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation, not a verified verbatim petition quotation: Whether criminal defendants’ race-based peremptory challenges are attributable to the State, whether the prosecution may assert prospective jurors’ equal-protection rights, and whether prohibiting those strikes violates defense rights.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Georgia Supreme Court, 261 Ga. 473, 405 S.E.2d 688, affirming denial of the prosecution’s pretrial motion to prohibit racially discriminatory peremptory strikes.
* **Relief Requested:** Georgia seeks reversal and application of an antidiscrimination constraint during jury selection; respondents seek freedom from that constraint as criminal defendants.
* **Material Record:** White defendants were charged with assaulting two African-American persons. Before jury selection the prosecution sought an order forbidding racially discriminatory defense strikes. The trial judge refused. No actual challenged defense strike or completed discriminatory selection finding is before the Court. The merits of the criminal charges remain unadjudicated.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This authorized interlocutory merits review concerns a definitive legal ruling governing the imminent trial. It does not authorize a presumption that these defendants will discriminate. State action is necessary; a defendant’s private interest in acquittal does not itself resolve attribution.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires consolidation in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation is identified in the historical opinion.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 1992-06-18, using the pre-term historical baseline unless displaced by an effective simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied. Earlier drafted positions are not Court holdings.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Trevino v. Texas may affect the functioning Batson framework if actually decided. Wyatt v. Cole may address a different private-party state-action setting, but immunity in damages is distinct from attribution during jury selection.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Fourteenth Amendment; state-provided jury-selection procedure and peremptory authority; Sixth Amendment defense interests. Legal attribution and scope reviewed independently.

### Issue 1: Attribution and juror equality

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment Equal Protection Clause prohibits state-attributable purposeful racial discrimination.
* **Material Other Authority:** Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 (1986), prohibits prosecutorial purposeful racial strikes. Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete, 500 U.S. 614 (1991), extends the restriction to private civil litigants under Lugar’s source-of-authority and fair-attribution inquiries. Edmonson examines government assistance, governmental function, and governmental aggravation of the injury. Polk County v. Dodson, 454 U.S. 312 (1981), treats ordinary defense representation as private for §1983 purposes. The specific act, not the lawyer’s title, must be classified.
* **Open Point:** Whether selecting a governmental jury is attributable when a criminal defendant invokes the strike, notwithstanding defense counsel’s ordinary independence.

### Issue 2: Third-party assertion and defense rights

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects jurors against discriminatory exclusion; Georgia’s peremptory-challenge provisions supply the challenged selection device.
* **Material Other Authority:** Powers v. Ohio, 499 U.S. 400 (1991), recognizes jurors’ personal right against discriminatory exclusion and requires claimant injury, a close relationship, and an obstacle to rights-holders’ own litigation for third-party assertion. Batson’s stages require a prima facie inference, a race-neutral explanation by the striking party, then a determination of proved purposeful discrimination; the ultimate persuasion burden remains with the objector. Peremptories are statutory devices, not an independent constitutional entitlement; Ross v. Oklahoma, 487 U.S. 81 (1988).
* **Open Point:** Whether the State can assert the jurors’ rights, and how neutral-explanation duties coexist with confidential defense strategy and the right to an impartial jury.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Georgia Supreme Court distinguished Edmonson’s civil parties from criminal defendants opposing sovereign prosecution. Respondents invoke Polk County, effective counsel, confidential strategy, and protection against jurors with actual racial bias. Georgia and the United States emphasize that jurors suffer the same exclusion, that courts give strikes their legal effect, and that a defense role does not license discrimination in assembling a governmental body. Alleged community prejudice may justify probing actual bias, but race alone is not a finding about a particular juror.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

No state attribution would defeat this constitutional restriction, leaving other lawful procedural constraints untouched. If attribution and third-party standing exist, a narrowly administered Batson extension can govern future strikes while preserving ordinary cause challenges and genuine race-neutral peremptories. Remand concerns trial procedure, not reversal of a conviction or a finding that respondents already committed discrimination. Confidentiality problems must be handled if concretely raised, not invented as an automatic defense.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Attribution and public jury function

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach state action as the necessary antecedent to juror equal protection.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Edmonson’s act-specific attribution analysis to criminal peremptories, while retaining Polk County for ordinary representation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A criminal defendant’s invocation of state peremptory authority to obtain judicial exclusion from a governmental jury is state action for this limited purpose.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The defendant acts against the prosecutor, but the relevant relationship is to the excluded juror and the court. State law creates the power; judicial officers assemble the panel and make the exclusion effective. These features were decisive in Edmonson and remain present here. Representing a client, investigating, and advising do not become government functions by association with litigation. Stone applies Master’s source-bound attribution, not a free-standing constitutional restriction on all private prejudice.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general state-action status attaches to criminal defendants or their counsel.

### Issue 2: Enforcement and defense protections

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the State’s standing and defense objections because both are necessary to the prospective ruling.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Powers and extend Batson’s existing procedure without changing its purpose requirement or persuasion burden.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The State may vindicate excluded jurors’ rights in its own trial process; defense rights do not include purposeful racial exclusion.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The State has a concrete institutional injury in the integrity of its tribunal, a direct relationship to summoned jurors, and a practical enforcement role where an excluded person faces serious obstacles to an individual suit. At an actual objection, the State must first show facts supporting an inference; defense counsel then supplies a race-neutral reason; the judge determines whether the State proved purposeful discrimination. No reason is demanded merely because a minority juror was struck. The rule preserves challenges based on actual bias and meaningful counsel. It does not presume a juror’s loyalties from race. Specific confidentiality concerns can receive appropriate protection without defeating the rule categorically. Master §9’s rejection of unsupported racial assumptions is consistent with this native juror-rights rule; there is no occasion to replace racial scrutiny throughout equal-protection law.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve the treatment of a concrete privilege dispute, unraised nonracial strike grounds, and protective rules aimed at unconscious bias. This holding neither abolishes peremptories nor converts disparate impact alone into constitutional purpose.

# Kraft General Foods, Inc. v. Iowa Department of Revenue and Finance

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Kraft General Foods, Inc. v. Iowa Department of Revenue and Finance
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 71 (1992); No. 90-1918.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, authorized for hearing in the simulation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1992-06-18, historical disposition date used only for event ordering, not a certiorari-grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation, not a verified verbatim petition quotation: Whether Iowa may tax dividends from foreign subsidiaries while deducting dividends from domestic subsidiaries in the tax base of a unitary corporation.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Iowa Supreme Court, 465 N.W.2d 664, sustaining the challenged deficiency.
* **Relief Requested:** Kraft seeks reversal and relief from the discriminatory assessment; Iowa seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Kraft operated a unitary business in Iowa and elsewhere. Its 1981 return deducted dividends from six foreign-incorporated subsidiaries conducting business abroad. Iowa used federal net-income definitions with adjustments, allowed domestic-subsidiary dividend deductions, denied the corresponding foreign deduction, and unlike the federal system provided no credit for foreign taxes. The parties do not dispute Iowa’s general ability to tax an apportioned share of the unitary business.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is foreign-commerce discrimination, not alienage equal protection or a jurisdictional nexus challenge. Federal tax classifications and federal foreign-affairs authority are not automatically available to an individual State.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires consolidation in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation is identified in the historical opinion.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 1992-06-18, using the pre-term historical baseline unless displaced by an effective simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied. Earlier drafted positions are not Court holdings.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Quill, Chemical Waste Management, Fort Gratiot, and Allied-Signal require checking for actually adopted rules on tax structure, discrimination, and unitary income. The unitary relationship here is not the unresolved investment relationship in Allied-Signal.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Commerce Clause, Article I §8 cl.3; state deficiency/refund procedure. Constitutional nondiscrimination reviewed independently.

### Issue 1: Foreign-commerce tax discrimination

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Commerce Clause governs the state tax’s differential treatment of foreign and domestic dividends; the Iowa deduction and referenced federal tax provisions define the challenged distinction.
* **Material Other Authority:** Complete Auto Transit v. Brady, 430 U.S. 274 (1977), requires nexus, fair apportionment, nondiscrimination, and relation to services. Japan Line v. County of Los Angeles, 441 U.S. 434 (1979), identifies additional foreign-commerce concerns, including multiple taxation and national uniformity. Boston Stock Exchange v. State Tax Commission, 429 U.S. 318 (1977), and Westinghouse Electric v. Tully, 466 U.S. 388 (1984), reject tax disadvantages linked to out-of-state activity. Congress can authorize otherwise impermissible state discrimination, but a federal income-tax definition alone does not expressly do so.
* **Open Point:** Whether favoring all domestic subsidiaries rather than only Iowa subsidiaries is discrimination against foreign commerce; whether administrative convenience or claimed compensating tax burdens justify it.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Iowa held that Kraft failed to show a commercial advantage for Iowa businesses. Kraft argues that the relevant comparison is domestic versus foreign dividends and that foreign discrimination need not favor Iowa alone. Iowa and the United States defend federal conformity, administrative convenience, corporate domicile as an imperfect business-location proxy, and the possible burden of other domestic taxes. Kraft answers that unsupported taxes levied by different sovereigns cannot automatically compensate for Iowa’s differential tax.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If the dividend distinction is constitutionally permissible or specifically authorized, affirm. If it facially discriminates without a sufficient justification, reverse and remand for a nondiscriminatory tax result. The Court need not decide other Japan Line branches if discrimination suffices. State remedial choices remain subject to the due-process tax-relief requirements of McKesson, 496 U.S. 18 (1990).

# Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 88 (1992); No. 90-1676.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, authorized for hearing in the simulation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1992-06-18, historical disposition date used only for event ordering, not a certiorari-grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation, not a verified verbatim petition quotation: Whether the Occupational Safety and Health Act preempts Illinois hazardous-waste worker licensing and training requirements without an approved state plan, including requirements also protecting the public.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Seventh Circuit, 918 F.2d 671, affirming in part, reversing in part, and remanding under a rule preempting direct, clear, substantial occupational-safety regulation without federal approval.
* **Relief Requested:** Illinois seeks rejection of the categorical preemption rule; the trade association seeks protection against enforcement of overlapping state requirements.
* **Material Record:** OSHA promulgated hazardous-waste employee training standards under the OSH Act and SARA. Illinois enacted two licensing statutes with training and examination requirements intended to protect workers and the public. Illinois has no approved §18 state plan. The District Court rejected broad preemption because of the public-safety purpose but invalidated some provisions; the appellate court announced a broader rule without deciding every challenged provision individually.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Dual purpose does not by itself classify a requirement as immune from preemption. The case does not authorize invalidating every part of a licensing code without matching its provisions to the federal scheme.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 1992-06-18, using the pre-term historical baseline unless displaced by an effective simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied. Earlier drafted positions are not Court holdings.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Morales v. Trans World Airlines could alter the simulation’s construction of express preemption if actually effective. Its airline statute differs from §18; an abstract preference for uniformity cannot replace the OSH Act text.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Supremacy Clause; OSH Act §§3(8), 18, 29 U.S.C. §§652(8), 667, and §4(b)(4), with SARA’s training direction. Statutory preemption is reviewed independently.

### Issue 1: Displacement and state-plan provisions

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** OSH Act §18(a) preserves state jurisdiction where no federal standard is effective. Section 18(b) requires a State desiring to assume responsibility for standards on a federally regulated issue to submit a plan; its enacted heading concerns state standards preempting federal standards. Other subsections govern approval and federal withdrawal. Section 4(b)(4) preserves specified compensation and tort rights. The Supremacy Clause supplies the constitutional basis for statutory displacement.
* **Material Other Authority:** Rice v. Santa Fe Elevator, 331 U.S. 218 (1947), supplies the presumption respecting traditional state police regulation absent clear displacement. Florida Lime & Avocado Growers v. Paul, 373 U.S. 132 (1963), and Hines v. Davidowitz, 312 U.S. 52 (1941), distinguish impossibility and interference with federal statutory operation.
* **Open Point:** Whether §18 excludes concurrent supplemental standards or only regulates state assumption of the federal role; whether each challenged requirement conflicts with the actual federal operation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The association reads §18’s plan requirement as the exclusive route for state occupational standards on regulated issues, fearing incompatible or duplicative obligations. Illinois argues that assumption of responsibility means replacing the federal regime and that compatible supplementary protection remains lawful. The lower court’s direct-and-substantial approach rejects the District Court’s purpose-only rule. Both sides must distinguish general public-safety laws from targeted worker training; labels alone do not answer coverage.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

An exclusive-state-plan construction supports the appellate rule, with remand to identify displaced provisions. A concurrent-standards construction requires actual conflict analysis and rejects automatic displacement from overlap. Under either reading, general traffic or fire rules governing workers as members of the public stand differently, and specific saving provisions must be respected. The existing record does not supply a complete provision-by-provision conflict determination.

If the actual record satisfies Jackson even under independent review, reverse without changing habeas legal review. A Teague bar would require identifying the proposed new rule rather than calling any adverse-to-State application new. ## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The District Court treated the grand-jury and trial motives as different. The Second Circuit allowed the similar-motive element to disappear where the Government obtained immunized testimony and declined to make the witnesses available at trial. Defendants stress fairness, the exculpatory account, and the prosecution’s inconsistent theory. The Government argues that every Rule element applies and that a grand jury’s investigative objectives may differ from a trial’s burden. Neither a categorical difference nor a categorical identity of motive necessarily follows from the forum alone.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If similar motive is legally dispensable, the appellate ground could stand. If every express requirement applies, reverse that legal ruling and examine actual motive on remand. A supported finding of similar motive permits admission under this exception; absence of it requires another valid evidentiary or preserved constitutional ground. The remand must not automatically reinstate all convictions where separate appellate grounds remain.
