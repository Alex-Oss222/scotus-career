# United States Department of State v. Ray

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 8 to 0 in the judgment; Thomas did not participate.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Stevens for the Court; Scalia and Kennedy joined all but Part III and concurred separately concerning derivative uses and privacy consequences.
* **Historical Holding:** Exemption 6 protected the returnees' identifiers given serious privacy interests and insufficient additional public benefit. The majority reserved whether derivative use could ever justify disclosure.
* **Historical Authority:** 502 U.S. 164, comparator only.
* **Historical Sources:** [Historical opinion and concurrence](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/502/164).

# INS v. National Center for Immigrants' Rights, Inc.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9 to 0.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Stevens for the Court.
* **Historical Holding:** The regulation, construed to forbid only unauthorized employment and considered with individual administrative procedures, was facially within the Attorney General's statutory authority. Constitutional and as-applied claims were reserved.
* **Historical Authority:** 502 U.S. 183, comparator only.

# Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 6 to 2; Thomas did not participate.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy for Rehnquist, White, Stevens, Souter, and himself; Blackmun concurred in the judgment. O'Connor dissented, joined by Scalia.

* **Historical Authority:** 502 U.S. 197, comparator only.

# Hunter v. Bryant

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Summarily reversed and remanded, 7 to 1; Thomas did not participate.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Per curiam; Scalia concurred in the judgment; Stevens dissented.

# In re Blodgett

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Mandamus denied without prejudice; in forma pauperis granted. All nine agreed in the judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Per curiam; Stevens, joined by Blackmun, concurred in the judgment.
* **Historical Holding:** Failure first to seek modification of the August order defeated the extraordinary request. The per curiam additionally announced a duty of prompt, full and fair resolution accompanying a federal execution stay; the concurrence resisted the broader criticism and emphasized consolidation and Rule 20.1.
* **Historical Authority:** 502 U.S. 236, comparator only.
* **Historical Sources:** [Historical opinion and concurrence](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/502/236).

# County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes and Bands of the Yakima Indian Nation

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Judgment affirmed and cause remanded with revised instructions; 8 to 1 on permitting the ad valorem tax and unanimous rejection of the excise tax.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Scalia for eight Justices; Blackmun concurred on excise tax and dissented on ad valorem authority.
* **Historical Holding:** General Allotment Act fee land could be taxed ad valorem, but sales excise was unauthorized; *Brendale* balancing was inapplicable. Patent-source facts and their possible legal significance remained for remand.
* **Historical Authority:** 502 U.S. 251, comparator only.
* **Historical Sources:** [Historical opinion and partial dissent](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/502/251).

# Norman v. Reed

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded, 7–1; Thomas did not participate.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter wrote for Rehnquist, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and himself. Scalia dissented.
* **Historical Holding:** The dispute remained live; the name prohibition and entire-slate suburban-signature consequence violated ballot-access rights. The independent suburban qualification requirement survived. The omitted-judicial-candidate issue was remanded. Scalia treated the statute as requiring support in each election and disputed the majority's use of Socialist Workers Party.
* **Historical Authority:** 502 U.S. 279 (1992), particularly 287–296; comparison only, not effective simulated law.
* **Historical Sources:** [Complete historical opinion and dissent](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/502/279/).

# Molzof v. United States

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, unanimously, 9–0.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Thomas wrote for the Court; no separate opinion.
* **Historical Holding:** Section 2674 bars punitive damages in their traditional common-law sense. Alleged excess or duplication does not alone make compensation punitive. The simple-negligence claims therefore were not federally barred on that theory, but Wisconsin-law entitlement remained for remand.
* **Historical Authority:** 502 U.S. 301 (1992), especially 305–312. Historical comparison only; it does not become simulated law without an effective simulated decision.
* **Historical Sources:** [Complete historical opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/502/301/).
