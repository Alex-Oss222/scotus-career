Maine v. Thiboutot, 448 U.S. 1 (1980), permits §1983 statutory enforcement. Wilder, 496 U.S. 498 (1990), and Wright, 479 U.S. 418 (1987), ask whether plaintiffs are intended beneficiaries, the duty is binding rather than aspirational, and its content is not so vague and amorphous as to defeat judicial enforcement; Congress may separately foreclose §1983 expressly or through incompatible comprehensive enforcement. ## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The officials argue the Act requires an approved plan, leaves reasonable efforts too indefinite, and entrusts enforcement to the Secretary. The children rely on the mandatory in-each-case language and Wilder: discretion over reasonable measures does not eliminate a duty to make efforts, especially when the agency supplies no worker to provide them. Respondents also rely on the intervening B.H. decree; officials distinguish its class, defined duties, and no-admission clause. The decree’s scope, rather than its shared subject matter, determines any mootness or preclusion consequence.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A §1983 holding can sustain the available action without deciding an additional implied action. A no-right holding ends that statutory claim but does not decide every other provision in the Act. If the action is authorized, equitable relief must address demonstrated failure and be practicable; identifying a duty does not prescribe services regardless of safety or cost.

### Issue 1: When may a district court withdraw supervision over particular parts of a school desegregation decree while retaining other parts?

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No case-specific simulated holding supplied. Historical baseline authorities remain eligible unless actually displaced.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No prior Standards & Tests tracker exists; the complete material entering framework is reconstructed below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment and the operative desegregation decree, administered through federal equitable authority.
* **Material Other Authority:** Brown II, 349 U.S. 294 (1955), authorizes equitable implementation of the Equal Protection Clause. The district argues that completed remedial tasks should return to local control despite unfinished tasks elsewhere. ## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A court may recognize authority for partial release but remand for its disciplined exercise. A contrary rule would retain every component until all are complete. Reinstating the district order automatically would require confidence that its findings establish both substantive compliance and safe separation; recognizing authority alone does not establish those facts.

### Issue 1: Did the Government prove predisposition independent of its inducement beyond a reasonable doubt?

* **Material Other Authority:** Sorrells, 287 U.S. 435 (1932), distinguishes providing an opportunity to a ready offender from inducing an otherwise innocent person. Jacobson relies on prolonged persuasion and the lawful character of his earlier purchase. The Government points to his responses, later orders, and expressed sexual interests as showing readiness. The Court may find sufficient independent predisposition and affirm. If no rational factfinder could find it beyond a reasonable doubt, reversal requires acquittal rather than a new trial. The defense does not automatically prevail whenever agents initiate contact or lack prior evidence of an offense.
