# Reynolds v. International Amateur Athletic Federation

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Reynolds v. International Amateur Athletic Federation
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 1301 (1992); No. A-954.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Application to stay the Sixth Circuit's stay of a preliminary injunction. Presented to Justice Stevens as Sixth Circuit Justice and referred to the participating full Court for majority decision, by order in the Court's name.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 20, 1992. Historical decision date supplies scheduling only.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: should the appellate stay be suspended to permit Reynolds to compete in the United States Olympic Trials while his eligibility litigation continues?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit's June 19, 1992 stay of the Southern District of Ohio's same-day preliminary injunction against interference by the IAAF and The Athletics Congress with Reynolds's trials participation.
* **Relief Requested:** Suspend the appellate stay, restoring the trial-level injunction for the trials, without finally deciding Olympic eligibility or the underlying tort and contract claims.
* **Material Record:** The IAAF suspended Reynolds after a urine sample attributed to him at a 1990 Monaco meet tested positive for nandrolone. An American arbitration and the national governing body's hearing supported his challenge to sample identity and testing. An IAAF arbitration upheld the suspension. These are conflicting adjudications, not an established finding that he used steroids or that the test was fabricated. The district court held an evidentiary hearing and found preliminary grounds for relief. Other athletes and TAC intervened; IAAF declined to appear, asserting lack of personal jurisdiction. The IAAF threatened consequences for competitors racing against Reynolds.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The first action was dismissed for failure to exhaust sports remedies. The new action followed the IAAF arbitration. Diversity jurisdiction, Ohio long-arm jurisdiction, constitutionally sufficient forum contacts, exhaustion, and the source of the asserted contractual duties remain material. The emergency does not create a freestanding constitutional right to enter privately administered athletic competition.
* **Relevant Dates:** May 1992 IAAF arbitration; June 17 federal evidentiary hearing; trials begin June 20. Missing these trials could foreclose qualification before ordinary appellate review.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Earlier Reynolds litigation supplies procedural history only.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; Stevens presents but does not decide alone. No case-specific nonparticipation supplied.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 20, 1992, using pre-term historical law unless actually displaced in the simulation.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated disposition has been supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Campos and Gomez may supply relevant emergency-order rules if actually decided; McCarthy may matter only to the distinction between statutory and judicially imposed exhaustion. None establishes sports-law jurisdiction by analogy alone.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1651(a), the Court's appellate jurisdiction, and the governing stay practice permit interim protection of a case within the appellate channel. Referral to the full Court follows the express simulation instruction.

### Issue 1: Interim relief and jurisdiction

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding changes the historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulation test exists. Traditional preliminary-relief doctrine considers likelihood of success, irreparable injury, injury to others, and the public interest. Coleman v. PACCAR, 424 U.S. 1301, 1304 (1976), and Western Airlines v. Teamsters, 480 U.S. 1301, 1305 (1987), describe the more demanding posture of disturbing an appellate stay: serious irreparable injury in a matter within the prospective appellate channel and likely to receive review, together with a demonstrably erroneous application of accepted stay standards. These preterm in-chambers authorities supply the standard, while the user's instruction supplies full-Court decisionmaking. Reynolds bears the requested-relief showing; ultimate liability is not decided.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fed. R. Civ. P. 65; 28 U.S.C. §1332; Ohio Rev. Code §2307.382. Plaintiff bears personal-jurisdiction support; the district court applied a prima-facie standard on the submitted jurisdictional materials, not proof by the defendant of no contacts.
* **Material Other Authority:** International Shoe (1945), World-Wide Volkswagen (1980), Calder v. Jones (1984), and Burger King (1985) require defendant-related contacts and fairness. Hilton v. Braunskill (1987) identifies ordinary stay considerations in its own habeas setting; NCAA v. Tarkanian (1988) cautions against treating private sports governance as automatically governmental.
* **Open Point:** Whether IAAF's alleged contractual dealings, directed decisions and Ohio effects establish jurisdiction and probable contract or tort liability, and whether protecting one athlete's opportunity outweighs immediate disruption without deciding final eligibility.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court found prima-facie jurisdiction from asserted business and athlete relationships, effects in Ohio, and TAC's role; it regarded the completed proceedings as sufficient exhaustion and the contradictory testing evidence as substantial support for the contract and tort claims. Its jurisdictional analysis cannot be replaced by the bare fact that Reynolds resides in Ohio. IAAF disputes jurisdiction and maintains its authority to enforce common eligibility rules. TAC and intervening athletes invoke the consequences for their own participation. Reynolds argues that the existing hearing record supports relief and a later monetary award cannot restore the opportunity to qualify. The opposing institutional concern is that judicial intervention in one country's proceedings could undermine uniform eligibility and harm other athletes. Claimed harm caused solely by a respondent's threatened retaliation remains distinguishable from independently unavoidable scheduling or safety harm.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Denying relief leaves the appellate stay in force and does not affirm the suspension's merits. Granting relief restores only the preliminary protection needed for trials participation, leaving qualification, Barcelona eligibility, jurisdiction after full development, and damages for subsequent proceedings. A narrower, time-limited order may protect the opportunity without extending to all future events. The compressed record supports only preliminary assessment; the district opinion, not an assumed final adjudication of testing accuracy, supplies that assessment.

# Sawyer v. Whitley

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Sawyer v. Whitley
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 333 (1992); No. 91-6382.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of dismissal of successive and abusive federal habeas claims.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 22, 1992, using the historical date for scheduling.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: what showing of innocence of a death sentence permits consideration of otherwise barred habeas claims, and does Sawyer make it?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, 945 F.2d 812 (1991), affirming denial of a second federal habeas petition, including refusal to consider claims for want of cause or a sufficient miscarriage-of-justice showing.
* **Relief Requested:** Review of the constitutional claims notwithstanding the procedural bars, followed by appropriate habeas proceedings. Opening the gateway does not itself require a new trial or life sentence.
* **Material Record:** Sawyer was convicted of Frances Arwood's murder after evidence of a sustained beating, scalding and burning. The jury found three aggravators; the Louisiana Supreme Court invalidated the unrelated-prior-murder aggravator. Aggravated arson and an especially heinous, atrocious or cruel murder remained. His intoxication and mental condition were litigated, and a relative described childhood deprivation and psychiatric treatment. New submissions include records of brain damage and impaired mental development, affidavits impeaching principal witness Cynthia Shano, and a caregiver's account that a child witness said Lane set the victim afire and Sawyer tried to stop him. The affidavit conflicts with contemporaneous accounts and does not establish that Sawyer did not participate in the preceding violence. The psychiatric material is evidence offered by Sawyer, not a new judicial finding of incompetence or diminished responsibility.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Brady claim was treated as abusive; the renewed ineffective-assistance claim as successive. The adverse cause determination is part of the posture. The gateway cannot be confused with constitutional materiality, merits prejudice, exhaustion, or the bar on retroactive new rules.
* **Relevant Dates:** Murder in 1979; state affirmance in 1982 and reconsideration in 1983; first federal petition in 1986. Sawyer v. Smith (1990) resolved a different retroactivity question in the prior proceeding and is eligible pre-term history.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Earlier same-term capital and habeas matters identified below.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 22, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated result supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Keeney may affect development of facts not presented in state court; Wright may affect habeas review methodology; Stringer and Sochor may affect invalid-aggravator analysis if the actual resulting holdings reach this Louisiana posture. Do not infer displacement from their drafted briefs or their historical outcomes.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Pre-AEDPA 28 U.S.C. §§2244 and 2254, Habeas Rule 9(b), and judicial abuse-of-writ doctrine. The 1996 successive-petition statute and 1993 Brecht rule are unavailable.

### Issue 1: Gateway around procedural bars

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied beyond the opening historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Wainwright v. Sykes (1977), Murray v. Carrier (1986), and McCleskey v. Zant (1991) ordinarily require cause and actual prejudice. The State identifies abuse through the petition history and omitted claims; the prisoner must answer with cause and prejudice. Cause requires an external impediment or constitutionally ineffective assistance where legally cognizable and properly exhausted, not ordinary lawyer oversight. A narrow fundamental-miscarriage-of-justice exception remains without cause. Carrier concerns a constitutional violation that probably resulted in conviction of an actually innocent person; Smith v. Murray (1986) recognizes a sentencing application without exhaustively defining it. Kuhlmann v. Wilson (1986) contains a plurality discussion of factual innocence and successive claims; its opinion support must not be inflated.
* **Other Governing Law:** The habeas statutes govern custody and access to relief; this issue is not an Eighth Amendment scrutiny tier.
* **Material Other Authority:** Sanders v. United States (1963) addresses successive applications and the ends of justice; McCleskey supplies later pre-term abuse doctrine.
* **Open Point:** Whether capital innocence is confined to objective eligibility, extends to mitigation producing an intolerably unreliable sentence, and what probability or proof formulation implements the narrow exception. The same-matter historical clear-and-convincing rule is not entering law.

### Issue 2: Capital eligibility, selection, and the underlying claims

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied alteration of the eligible precedents.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Zant v. Stephens (1983) and Lowenfield v. Phelps (1988) distinguish narrowing eligibility from individualized selection. Lockett (1978 plurality), Eddings (1982), and Penry (1989) require consideration and effect for relevant mitigation, not automatic life imprisonment whenever mitigation exists. Louisiana required at least one valid aggravator and then a sentencing choice taking mitigation into account.
* **Other Governing Law:** Brady (1963) and Bagley (1985) require favorable suppressed evidence and materiality; Strickland (1984) requires deficient representation and a reasonable probability of a different result. Those retrospective merits standards do not automatically define the innocence gateway.
* **Material Other Authority:** Teague v. Lane (1989) and Sawyer v. Smith (1990) separately govern retroactivity. A gateway victory does not erase them.
* **Open Point:** Whether the additional submissions satisfy the gateway under a permissible definition before any claim's merits are reached.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Fifth Circuit confined capital innocence to the crime and aggravating facts necessary for death eligibility and found no adequate showing. Sawyer contends that a constitutional system requiring individualized mitigation cannot deem mitigation categorically irrelevant to innocence of the sentence; suppression and omitted psychiatric records must be considered together. The State argues that innocence means factual ineligibility, and turning every substantial mitigation claim into innocence would erase the cause requirement. The United States supported affirmance and a narrow gateway. Even a broader approach must distinguish ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice from an exceptional miscarriage of justice; even the eligibility approach must examine state-law participation and each surviving aggravator rather than assume that disproving who lit the fire disposes of the case.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the evidence fails a properly formulated gateway. Vacate and remand if the lower court excluded a legally material category and the record requires new assessment. If the gateway opens, adjudicate only properly available constitutional claims under their independent elements and retroactivity limits. No gateway determination establishes innocence of the murder, invalidates the death penalty generally, or authorizes an immediate unconditional release. The precise reliability and cumulative force of the submissions matter; no new evidentiary findings are supplied here.

Murray, 477 U.S. 527](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/477/527/), [Eddings, 455 U.S. 104](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/455/104/), and [Strickland, 466 U.S. 668](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/466/668/) support the entering frameworks. The record and procedural account are verified against the judicial text at [505 U.S. 333, pp. 335-338, 347-350 and 373-375](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/333/); its new rule and outcome remain exclusively comparator material.

# R. A. V. v. City of St. Paul

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** R. A. V. v. City of St. Paul
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 377 (1992); No. 90-7675.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the reinstatement of a juvenile charge under a municipal ordinance.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 22, 1992; historical date used for ordering.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: is the Bias-Motivated Crime Ordinance, as authoritatively construed, facially invalid for overbreadth or unconstitutional content discrimination?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Minnesota Supreme Court, 464 N.W.2d 507 (1991), reversing dismissal of the ordinance charge.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse reinstatement of that charge. Other available criminal charges are separate.
* **Material Record:** The juvenile allegedly burned a cross inside the fenced yard of a Black family. The allegation is not a finding of guilt. The ordinance prohibits displaying symbols, objects or graffiti, including a burning cross or Nazi swastika, known or reasonably known to arouse anger, alarm or resentment based on race, color, creed, religion or gender. The Minnesota Supreme Court construed the provision as limited to fighting words under Chaplinsky, including expressions inflicting injury or tending to incite immediate violence.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** First Amendment overbreadth allows a facial challenge even where this defendant's alleged conduct might be punishable under a valid narrower law. The Court must accept the state-law construction, while independently assessing whether the resulting coverage fits federal constitutional limits. Do not rewrite the ordinance into a threat statute or assume every cross burning has the same context.
* **Relevant Dates:** Alleged incident June 1990; state judgment in 1991.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The earlier scheduled speech cases may affect analysis as specified below.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 22, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None supplied as effective simulated law.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Simon & Schuster may affect content discrimination; Forsyth County may affect audience-hostility reasoning; Burson may affect a claimed specially justified speech restriction. Their historical holdings cannot be assumed.

### Issue 1: Fighting words and facial overbreadth

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied departure from eligible historical speech law.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Chaplinsky v. New Hampshire (1942) permits narrowly defined fighting-word prohibitions. Cohen v. California (1971), Gooding v. Wilson (1972), and Lewis v. New Orleans (1974) distinguish personally abusive face-to-face provocation from offensive ideas or ordinary verbal abuse. Broadrick v. Oklahoma (1973) requires substantial overbreadth relative to legitimate sweep, with a workable narrowing construction considered first. The challenger identifies substantial protected coverage; government cannot establish an unprotected category merely by naming it.
* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; St. Paul Legislative Code §292.02 (1990).
* **Material Other Authority:** Texas v. Johnson (1989) rejects offensiveness as sufficient grounds to prohibit expressive conduct. Watts v. United States (1969) distinguishes threatening expression from hyperbole in context; Brandenburg (1969) separately requires directed and likely imminent lawless action for incitement.
* **Open Point:** Whether the authoritative construction still reaches substantial expression producing injury or resentment without immediate breach-of-peace provocation. A future categorical threat mental-state rule cannot be imported.

### Issue 2: Selective regulation within a proscribable category

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied on this exact question.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Police Department v. Mosley (1972), Carey v. Brown (1980), and Boos v. Barry (1988) constrain content distinctions in protected speech and public-forum regulation. Ordinarily government bears compelling-interest and narrow-tailoring justification for content discrimination. Whether that same rule applies to subject distinctions wholly within fighting words is the contested extension here.
* **Other Governing Law:** Equal protection and free speech remain distinct claims; a city's special concern with racial intimidation is not itself a finding of forbidden purpose.
* **Material Other Authority:** O'Brien (1968) governs qualifying conduct regulation whose interest is unrelated to suppressing expression, not an automatic defense to a law keyed expressly to a message.
* **Open Point:** Whether a lawful category may be regulated selectively for distinct injury without censorship of ideas; no same-matter historical exceptions are entering rules.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The state supreme court concluded its fighting-words construction cured overbreadth and the prohibition was narrowly tailored to a compelling public-safety interest against bias-motivated threats. R. A. V. argues that indignation and hurt remain protected and that the ordinance selects disfavored topics. St. Paul argues that racial and religious intimidation inflicts particular harm and need not be accompanied by a ban on all other fighting words. The strongest limiting distinction is between punishing intimidation or violence and punishing disagreement, not between sympathetic and unsympathetic speakers. A valid prohibition on assault, arson, trespass or actual intimidation need not fail because evidence of motive includes speech. Conversely, categorizing all identity-related insult as fighting words cannot establish the required immediate provocation.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse for substantial overbreadth without deciding all selective regulation within unprotected categories; reverse on a source-grounded content-discrimination rule if that issue is necessary; or uphold the narrowed ordinance and remand for adjudication of its elements. Narrowing cannot become judicial amendment of the enacted offense. This pretrial posture supplies no finding that the alleged act satisfies every element of any alternative offense, and reversal of this ordinance charge does not immunize the incident from otherwise lawful prosecution.

## E. SOURCES

[Judicial text, 505 U.S. 377](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/377/), record and lower-court passages, verifies the charge, construction and arguments. [Gooding, 405 U.S. 518](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/405/518/), [Cohen, 403 U.S. 15](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/403/15/), [Broadrick, 413 U.S. 601](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/413/601/), and [Mosley, 408 U.S. 92](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/408/92/) supply eligible frameworks.

# Medina v. California

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Medina v. California
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 437 (1992); No. 90-8370.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review on direct review of criminal convictions and death sentences.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 22, 1992; historical date used for scheduling.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: may California presume trial competence and require the defendant to prove incompetence by a preponderance once a competency hearing is held?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** California Supreme Court, 51 Cal. 3d 870, 799 P.2d 1282 (1990), affirming.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse the judgment resting on the challenged allocation and obtain constitutionally adequate competency proceedings.
* **Material Record:** Counsel questioned Medina's ability to participate in his defense. A six-day jury hearing included conflicting psychiatric and lay testimony: one psychiatrist found incompetence; others found competence, suspected malingering, or could not decide. Medina had serious outbursts. The jury was instructed to presume competence unless he proved incompetence by a preponderance. It found him competent, and a separate jury convicted him of three murders and other crimes. Competence to stand trial differs from sanity at the time of the crimes, which was separately litigated.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Medina challenges the allocation and associated presumption, not the preponderance quantum itself. No finding establishes that the evidence was actually in equipoise or that Medina was incompetent. The constitutional effect of the allocation is a legal question; the Court is not the original psychiatric factfinder.
* **Relevant Dates:** Crimes and arrest in 1984; California decision in 1990.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Foucha and Riggins may affect related liberty or criminal-process doctrine only through actual effective rulings.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 22, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated outcomes supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Riggins, if it resolves trial-related mental-health safeguards, may bear on practical defense capacity; Foucha concerns civil confinement after insanity acquittal and cannot itself allocate the competency burden. Wright may bear on review only if the resulting rule reaches direct criminal review.

### Issue 1: Constitutional competency procedure

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied alteration of eligible historical doctrine.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Dusky v. United States (1960) asks whether a defendant has sufficient present ability to consult counsel with a reasonable degree of rational understanding and rational as well as factual understanding of proceedings. Pate v. Robinson (1966) and Drope v. Missouri (1975) prohibit trial while incompetent and require inquiry when sufficient doubt arises; a possibly incompetent defendant cannot waive the inquiry by failing to request it. Neither squarely fixes the persuasion burden at the resulting hearing.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; Cal. Penal Code §§1367-1369, especially §1369(f)'s preponderance allocation to the party asserting incompetence.
* **Material Other Authority:** Patterson v. New York (1977) affords States latitude over criminal procedure unless a fundamental principle of justice is offended. Mathews v. Eldridge (1976) weighs the private interest, risk of error and value of added safeguards, and governmental interests; Ake v. Oklahoma (1985) applied a comparable inquiry to psychiatric assistance in criminal defense. Their relationship in this setting is disputed. Leland v. Oregon (1952) concerns the insanity defense, not present competence. In re Winship (1970) allocates crime elements to the prosecution beyond reasonable doubt; competence is not an offense element.
* **Open Point:** Whether fair procedure allows the defendant to lose in equipoise after substantial doubt has triggered a hearing, and whether the statutory presumption does anything beyond allocating that risk.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

California's court treated the presumption as the allocation of proof and found adequate protection in the hearing. Medina argues that placing the decisive burden on someone whose capacity to assist counsel is itself doubtful undermines the established prohibition, and that the State should establish competence before proceeding. California invokes procedural latitude, mixed historical practice, defense access to relevant information, appointed experts and a full hearing. Its strongest distinction is that allocating a close case to one side does not itself establish that an incompetent defendant will be tried. Medina's answer concerns the legally assigned risk of such an error, not a claim that every mental illness is incompetence. The United States supported California. No empirical frequency of erroneous competency verdicts is established in the materials reviewed.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the hearing and defendant's preponderance burden satisfy due process. Reverse or vacate if the State must carry persuasion once the hearing threshold is met; remand for consideration of whether a reliable retrospective competency determination is possible and the consequences if not. The legal invalidity of a burden does not itself establish historic incompetence or require acquittal. Competence now is not proof of competence then. A new trial, if required, may occur only while the defendant is competent; interim custody requires its own lawful authority.

## E. SOURCES

The trial evidence, instruction, lower judgment and preservation are verified from [505 U.S. 437, pp. 439-442](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/437/). [Pate, 383 U.S. 375](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/383/375/), [Drope, 420 U.S. 162](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/420/162/), [Patterson, 432 U.S. 197](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/432/197/), and [Ake, 470 U.S. 68](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/470/68/) supply the eligible competing frameworks.

# Estate of Cowart v. Nicklos Drilling Co.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Estate of Cowart v. Nicklos Drilling Co.
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 469 (1992); No. 91-17.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act forfeiture.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 22, 1992, historical date used as the calendar.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: does §33(g) reach a worker whose employer is neither currently paying compensation nor subject to an award when the worker settles with a third party?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit en banc, 927 F.2d 828 (1991), reversing the administrative award.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse and restore eligibility for compensation under the asserted limited meaning of “person entitled to compensation.”
* **Material Record:** Cowart injured his hand in 1983 on an Outer Continental Shelf platform. Nicklos and its carrier paid temporary benefits, then stopped. An informal Labor Department notice identified permanent disability compensation but was not a binding award. Cowart settled a negligence claim against platform owner Transco in 1985. Nicklos funded the settlement under an indemnity agreement and knew its amount, but the prescribed prior written approval from employer and carrier was not obtained. The gross settlement was $45,000; net recovery was $29,350.60; the informal disability calculation was $35,592.77. The administrative award relied on Benefits Review Board precedent limiting the approval trigger to acknowledged or adjudicated compensation.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The review question concerns the worker's legal status at settlement. Whether employer participation independently satisfied or excused approval is outside the defined question and must not be invented as a controlling alternative. The gross-versus-net settlement comparison is not the question to be decided here.
* **Relevant Dates:** Injury July 20, 1983; 1984 statutory amendments; settlement July 1, 1985.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 22, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated outcomes supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Southwest Marine concerns other LHWCA coverage and election questions; revalidate only any actual holding bearing on compensation entitlement or statutory waiver. A general result in that case cannot create an approval exception here.

### Issue 1: Entitlement and third-party settlement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied modification of statutory interpretation or LHWCA doctrine.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Enacted definitions, the phrase's ordinary legal use, surrounding subsections and the compensation scheme determine coverage. Benefits Review Board decisions are adjudicative interpretations, not automatically controlling agency policymaking. Chevron (1984), where applicable to the responsible administrator, begins with the statute; contrary administrative interpretation cannot displace clear text.
* **Other Governing Law:** 33 U.S.C. §933(g)(1) requires prior employer and carrier written approval, in prescribed form, when a person entitled to compensation settles a covered third-party claim for less than compensation entitlement; approval must be filed within thirty days. Section (g)(2) terminates compensation and medical-benefit rights for failure to obtain and file required approval, or failure to notify the employer of a third-party settlement or judgment, irrespective of prior payments or acknowledgement. Section (f) credits net third-party recovery in computing the employer's remaining liability. 43 U.S.C. §1333(b) supplies the platform's LHWCA coverage.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Board's O'Leary (1977) and Dorsey (1986) interpretations support the estate's position but are not Supreme Court holdings. Northeast Marine Terminal v. Caputo (1977) recognizes the Act's remedial context without eliminating its express conditions.
* **Open Point:** Whether “entitled” means having a vested statutory right or currently receiving/awarded payment; what significance attaches to Congress retaining the phrase while adding §(g)(2).

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The en banc court read entitlement as an existing legal right and the new subsection as confirming that unacknowledged rights can be forfeited. The estate invokes the Board's settled interpretation, Congress's retention of its wording in 1984, the Act's remedial structure, and the practical difficulty of knowing ultimate compensation at settlement. Nicklos and Compass invoke the explicit protection of their exposure and subrogation interests and the language making acknowledgement immaterial. The government's positions during litigation changed; no stable litigation position is treated as a binding rule. The fact that the employer funded the settlement sharpens the asserted harshness but does not itself decide the separately limited interpretation question.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if statutory entitlement exists before payment or an award. Reverse if prior acknowledgement/adjudication is part of the trigger. The broad statutory reading still requires the actual subsection's conditions; it does not mean every settlement, whatever its size or status, requires approval under (g)(1). Notification is separately necessary. Leave any independently preserved participation or calculation question to its proper posture; decide no equitable exception on invented facts.

[Chevron, 467 U.S. 837](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/467/837/) and [Caputo, 432 U.S. 249](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/432/249/) supply eligible interpretive authority. The source statute is the pre-event version reproduced in the judicial record, not a later amendment.

# Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 504 (1992); No. 90-1038.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of federal preemption of specified state-law cigarette claims.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 24, 1992, historical date used for sequence.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: to what extent do the 1965 Federal Cigarette Labeling and Advertising Act and its 1969 successor preempt the pleaded New Jersey damages claims?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Third Circuit, 893 F.2d 541 (1990), applying its earlier preemption ruling and ordering further proceedings on other grounds.
* **Relief Requested:** Remove the preemption barriers to claims based on post-1965 conduct. The Court is not asked to find fraud, causation or a particular damages amount.
* **Material Record:** Rose Cipollone smoked from 1942 and died of lung cancer in 1984. Her estate and related claims are maintained by her son after both original plaintiffs' deaths. The complaint asserts failure to warn, express warranty, fraudulent misrepresentation, and conspiracy to withhold smoking-health information. The pleaded theories include allegedly neutralizing federal warnings through advertising, concealing facts through channels other than advertising, and violating express assurances voluntarily made by manufacturers. Design-defect allegations existed but are not an independently presented preemption component requiring a new merits decision here. Under the pretrial preemption restriction, trial proceeded chiefly on earlier conduct. The jury rejected fraud and conspiracy claims submitted to it, found pre-1966 warning/warranty breaches by Liggett, and assigned substantial responsibility to Rose; a separate award to her husband and other trial issues led to appellate proceedings. Those findings do not adjudicate excluded post-enactment theories.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Separate the legal duty necessary to each claim from evidence used to prove it. The pleaded label “fraud” cannot evade preemption of an additional warning duty, while an advertisement as evidence does not necessarily make the underlying duty a warning requirement. The case concerns the Acts applicable to these claims; the parties do not rely on the 1984 amendments.
* **Relevant Dates:** 1965 Act effective January 1, 1966; successor Public Health Cigarette Smoking Act of 1969 enacted in 1970. Use the applicable statutory period, not the title's year as an invented enactment date.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 24, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated preemption holdings supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Morales and Gade could materially affect express-preemption interpretation or treatment of overlapping state duties, depending on their actual holdings. Different statutory language still requires independent construction.

### Issue 1: Scope of the two express clauses

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied displacement of the historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Congressional intent expressed in text and structure controls. Rice v. Santa Fe Elevator (1947) supports a presumption against displacing traditional state police power absent sufficient congressional intent. English v. General Electric (1990) distinguishes express, field and actual conflict preemption. A specific express clause must be interpreted before resorting to generalized uniformity. The party asserting preemption must establish statutory coverage; no special proof quantum governs this legal question.
* **Other Governing Law:** Supremacy Clause; 1965 §5(b) barred requiring a statement relating to smoking and health in advertising for conformingly labeled cigarettes. The amended §5(b) instead barred a state-law requirement or prohibition based on smoking and health with respect to advertising or promotion of conformingly labeled cigarettes. Package labeling, advertising, promotion, the subject of the duty and the federal compliance condition each perform distinct work.
* **Material Other Authority:** San Diego Building Trades Council v. Garmon (1959) recognizes that damages duties can regulate conduct, without deciding these Acts. Silkwood v. Kerr-McGee (1984) illustrates why federal regulation does not automatically eliminate state damages remedies. The 1965 Act also preserved the FTC's unfair-or-deceptive-practices authority.
* **Open Point:** Whether either clause reaches common-law damages duties; if the amended clause does, which specific duties fall inside it. The existence of an express clause does not supply a universal rule that every actual conflict outside its terms is impossible.

### Issue 2: Claim-specific operation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied on these cigarette claims.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The state claim must be examined by the duty it would impose. A warning theory may demand a different promotional health statement; a warranty theory may enforce a voluntarily undertaken promise; fraud may concern either neutralization of warnings or the general duty not to deceive. Conspiracy is derivative of the unlawful conduct alleged, not a freestanding exemption from preemption.
* **Other Governing Law:** New Jersey tort and warranty law continues to supply breach, causation, reliance where required and damages unless valid federal law displaces the particular duty.
* **Material Other Authority:** The conflicting lower decisions, including the New Jersey Supreme Court's Dewey v. R.J. Reynolds (1990), show the disputed scope but do not replace Supreme Court construction.
* **Open Point:** Which allegations can proceed after the proper federal boundary is fixed; ultimate state-law liability remains for the lower courts.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Third Circuit relied on warning uniformity and the regulatory effect of tort liability to preempt post-enactment advertising and promotional theories broadly. Cipollone argues that the Acts regulate mandated statements, not compensation for personal injury; the later wording does not unmistakably abolish traditional remedies. The manufacturers argue that jury-enforced duties are state requirements and can compel precisely the warnings Congress standardized. The strongest intermediate reading distinguishes the narrower 1965 wording from the later requirement-or-prohibition clause and then examines each predicate duty. The manufacturers' argument is strongest for claims demanding extra health warnings in promotion; the estate's is strongest for express undertakings and intentional falsehoods actionable under a duty not to deceive generally.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A no-damages-preemption reading restores all disputed theories subject to state law. A broad reading could foreclose all smoking-health advertising theories, including fraud and warranty. A text-sensitive claim analysis permits some and bars others. Any partial affirmance must identify both the statutory period and the prohibited duty, not erase every use of advertising evidence. Remand follows for application, preserved trial errors and surviving claims; success against a federal defense is not proof of liability. No constitutional ruling on cigarette advertising is necessary.

# City of Burlington v. Dague

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** City of Burlington v. Dague
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 557 (1992); No. 91-810.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review limited to contingency enhancement of statutory attorney fees.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 24, 1992, historical date used for scheduling.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: may a reasonable fee under the Clean Water Act and Solid Waste Disposal Act exceed the lodestar to compensate for contingency risk, and was this enhancement permissible?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, 935 F.2d 1343 (1991), affirming a 25% enhancement.
* **Relief Requested:** Remove the enhancement. The underlying pollution injunction and substantially-prevailing-party determination are not within this review.
* **Material Record:** Dague owned land adjoining Burlington's landfill. Contingently retained counsel secured relief for statutory violations. The district court found reasonable hours and rates yielding a $198,027.50 lodestar. It added $49,506.87, finding substantial litigation risk and difficulty obtaining competent environmental counsel without possible enhancement. It regarded more than 25% as a windfall. The record's support for local market treatment of contingent representation, distinct from this case's riskiness, is disputed.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Distinguish statutory authority for any contingency enhancement from adequacy of the showing and amount in this case. The award does not concern enhancement for delay or an unreasonably low hourly rate.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 24, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated fee rule supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No earlier listed case squarely resolves contingency enhancement. Ardestani may matter to source-specific fee authority only if its actual holding extends beyond its distinct waiver statute; Department of Energy v. Ohio does not make this municipal fee dispute a federal-sovereign-immunity case.

### Issue 1: Statutory fee and contingency

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied departure from historical fee doctrine.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Hensley v. Eckerhart (1983) and Blum v. Stenson (1984) start with reasonable hours times reasonable market rate; the resulting lodestar is presumptively reasonable. The applicant documents hours and rates and bears the justification for an upward adjustment. Difficulty, skill and ordinary litigation demands generally belong in hours/rates and cannot be counted twice. Delaware Valley I (1986) reinforces the lodestar's primacy. Delaware Valley II (1987) rejected the particular enhancement without a majority resolving categorical permission: four Justices opposed risk enhancement; O'Connor allowed a limited market-based approach requiring proof that absent enhancement the client would have substantial difficulty obtaining competent counsel, rejecting case-specific risk calculations; four dissenters accepted a broader allowance. Marks (1977) governs identification of any narrow controlling ground, and lower courts differ on whether the concurrence controls.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §6972(e) and 33 U.S.C. §1365(d) authorize appropriate litigation costs including reasonable attorney fees. They do not expressly mention contingency multipliers.
* **Material Other Authority:** Missouri v. Jenkins (1989) looks to market rates/practices; Blanchard v. Bergeron (1989) rejects treating a private contingent-fee agreement as an automatic cap or measure of statutory fees. Legal authority is reviewed independently; allowable factual market findings receive ordinary clear-error review and a lawful award receives discretion review.
* **Open Point:** Whether statutory reasonableness permits contingency compensation at all, and, if so, what prevents double counting, compensation for losing cases, and arbitrary multipliers.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Second Circuit treated Delaware Valley II as leaving the question open and approved its circuit's effective-access rationale. Burlington argues that lodestar rates compensate competent work, fee statutes pay winners rather than insurers of unsuccessful litigation, and risk multipliers reward weak cases. Dague argues that payment only upon success is economically different from guaranteed hourly compensation and ignoring that difference can prevent representation Congress intended fee shifting to support. The United States supported reversal. A market-based middle position permits exceptional adjustment for demonstrated access and market differences while rejecting mechanical inverse-probability multipliers and unsupported case-specific estimates.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the enhancement categorically; affirm a supported lawful enhancement; or recognize limited statutory permission while vacating for application of the correct market showing. None requires revisiting environmental liability or the independently calculated lodestar. Finding substantial difficulty obtaining counsel does not establish the correct multiplier. The distinction between general fee-market evidence and an impression of this litigation's danger is material.

## E. SOURCES

[505 U.S. 557, pp. 559-563 and 575-576](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/557/) verifies the award, findings, question scope and record issue. [Blum, 465 U.S. 886](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/465/886/), [Delaware Valley II, 483 U.S. 711](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/483/711/), and [Jenkins, 491 U.S. 274](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/491/274/) supply eligible doctrine. Neither a later categorical rule nor a single concurrence is silently treated as an entering majority holding.

# Lee v. Weisman

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lee v. Weisman
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 577 (1992); No. 90-1014.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of permanent relief against official public-school graduation prayers.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 24, 1992; historical date used for sequence.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: may school officials select clergy and include invocations and benedictions at public secondary-school graduation ceremonies consistently with the Establishment Clause?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** First Circuit, 908 F.2d 1090 (1990), affirming the district injunction.
* **Relief Requested:** School officials seek reversal; the Weismans seek preservation of prospective protection.
* **Material Record:** Principal Robert Lee invited a rabbi for Deborah Weisman's middle-school graduation, gave him guidelines for civic prayers and requested nonsectarian content. The rabbi delivered an invocation and benediction. The family had unsuccessfully sought a temporary restraining order and then attended. Attendance was not legally compulsory. The practice was permitted at public middle and high schools; Deborah expected to encounter it at her high-school graduation. Stipulated facts establish official selection and organization; they do not establish punishment for refusing to pray.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The completed ceremony does not eliminate the requested relief if the ongoing policy threatens the same student at a later graduation. Standing rests on exposure to the official exercise, not abstract taxpayer disagreement. Institutional accommodation and Lemon's continued role are argued, but no reconsideration of every establishment doctrine is necessary if school-prayer precedents dispose of the case.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No same-term supplied holding resolves public-school prayer.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 24, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None supplied as effective simulated law.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No directly controlling earlier listed Establishment Clause matter. Later airport speech cases do not govern this case.

### Issue 1: State-organized worship in school

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied departure from eligible historical doctrine.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Engel v. Vitale (1962) prohibits official school prayer even if denominationally neutral and participation is nominally voluntary. Abington School District v. Schempp (1963) rejects school devotional exercises despite excuses from participation. Lemon v. Kurtzman (1971) asks secular purpose, primary effect neither advancing nor inhibiting religion, and absence of excessive entanglement; failure of a necessary prong defeats the policy. These are constitutional legal requirements, not a heightened factual-proof quantum. A claimant establishes the challenged governmental practice and infringement; neither physical compulsion nor denominational preference is a universal prerequisite.
* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment Establishment Clause through the Fourteenth Amendment; the official school policy and the prospective equitable remedy.
* **Material Other Authority:** Marsh v. Chambers (1983) treats legislative prayer in its distinct historical institutional setting. Lynch v. Donnelly (1984) and County of Allegheny v. ACLU (1989) address contextual religious displays, not permission to direct devotional exercises in schools. Board of Education v. Mergens (1990) protects private religious meetings under equal access, distinct from school sponsorship.
* **Open Point:** Whether graduation's ceremonial and nonmandatory character materially distinguishes official school prayer, and whether any narrower constitutional rule resolves the case without revising Lemon.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court applied Lemon's effect inquiry, finding official identification with religion. The First Circuit affirmed; the opinions differed over how many Lemon prongs were violated and the relevance of Marsh. Officials argue that graduation is a voluntary civic ceremony, prayer solemnizes it without coercion, and nonsectarian directions accommodate differing beliefs. The Weismans argue that school selection, invitation, content advice and the official program make this state worship; opting out of an important educational ceremony is not equal participation. The United States supported the school officials and urged reconsideration of Lemon. The institution can mark graduation solemnly without deciding what prayer is acceptable. Private religious speech and worship by attendees remain a distinct category.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm on the existing school-prayer line, with Lemon's effect principle reinforcing the result, leaving its entire future scope unresolved. Reverse if the civic ceremony falls within a permissible noncoercive historical accommodation, explaining why school-prayer precedents do not control. A threshold disposition is appropriate only if the record no longer supports prospective exposure, not simply because the first graduation ended. Relief may bar the challenged official practice without suppressing independent student expression or private observance.

## E. SOURCES

[505 U.S. 577, pp. 580-586](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/577/) verifies stipulated facts, lower reasoning, prospective exposure and party positions. [Engel, 370 U.S. 421](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/370/421/), [Schempp, 374 U.S. 203](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/374/203/), [Lemon, 403 U.S. 602](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/403/602/), and [Marsh, 463 U.S. 783](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/463/783/) supply the date-eligible distinctions. Later abandonment of Lemon is excluded.

# Doggett v. United States

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Doggett v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 647 (1992); No. 90-857.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of a speedy-trial challenge preserved through a conditional guilty plea.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 24, 1992, historical date used for sequence.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: does extraordinary post-indictment delay caused by government negligence violate the Sixth Amendment without a particularized showing of trial prejudice?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, 906 F.2d 573 (1990), affirming denial of dismissal.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse and dismiss the indictment for a speedy-trial violation.
* **Material Record:** Doggett was indicted in February 1980, left the country before attempted arrest, was later detained in Panama, then went to Colombia. Officials failed to follow up adequately. He returned to the United States in 1982, lived openly under his own name, married, worked and obtained a degree. A routine credit check located him in 1988. More than eight years elapsed between indictment and arrest, including approximately six years of open domestic residence. The courts found government negligence and that Doggett did not know of the indictment before arrest. He did not identify a specific lost witness or other measurable defense impairment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Sixth Amendment claim attaches to an outstanding indictment, distinct from a preaccusation due-process claim or a Speedy Trial Act calculation. The conditional plea preserves review. Negligence and lack of knowledge are findings, not assumptions that may be discarded because he traveled abroad.
* **Relevant Dates:** Indictment February 22, 1980; domestic return September 1982; arrest September 1988.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 24, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No supplied effective simulated speedy-trial decision.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No earlier listed case directly decides the post-indictment-delay question. Ibarra concerns appeal timing rather than this constitutional balance and supplies no automatic tolling rule here.

### Issue 1: Barker balance and prejudice

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied alteration of historical Sixth Amendment law.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Barker v. Wingo (1972) assesses length of delay, reasons, assertion of the right, and prejudice together; none is an indispensable standalone element. Length first triggers inquiry when presumptively prejudicial in context, then bears on the balance. Deliberate delay to hamper defense weighs heavily against government; negligence less heavily but still against it; valid reasons such as unavailable witnesses may justify delay. Assertion matters but demand-waiver is rejected. Prejudice includes oppressive detention, anxiety and impaired defense; inability to identify precisely what was lost does not make delay harmless as a matter of law. Moore v. Arizona (1973) rejects a universal demand for affirmative proof of prejudice.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth Amendment. United States v. Marion (1971) distinguishes preaccusation delay; United States v. MacDonald (1982) concerns periods after dismissal of charges; neither makes all prearrest periods exempt when an indictment remains pending.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Loud Hawk (1986) addresses delay and interlocutory proceedings in its posture. Strunk v. United States (1973) makes dismissal the remedy for an established constitutional speedy-trial violation. Findings about reasons and knowledge receive factual deference; constitutional application is independently reviewed.
* **Open Point:** How much prolonged negligent delay and inferential defense impairment can establish under Barker where the accused was unaware and unincarcerated.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The courts below recognized negligence and lack of knowledge. The Eleventh Circuit required actual prejudice unless the first three Barker factors weighed heavily for the accused; under its precedent, negligence did not carry the necessary weight, so lack of demonstrable prejudice defeated relief. Doggett argues that government could have found him through routine inquiry for years and that requiring specific proof of forgotten evidence defeats Barker's concern with hidden impairment. The United States emphasizes absence of custody, anxiety about unknown charges, or identified lost defense evidence, and characterizes negligence as insufficient without demonstrated harm. Its strongest argument is for a demanding contextual threshold, not rewriting Barker into a fixed affirmative-prejudice element. Doggett's position does not excuse a knowing fugitive or assign every overseas delay to the prosecution.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the full balance does not show a violation, without treating specific prejudice as categorically mandatory. Reverse and direct dismissal if the extraordinary negligence and unrebutted inferential impairment suffice. Remand for factual findings only if a genuinely unresolved material fact remains; do not reopen the accepted lack-of-knowledge finding by conjecture. A constitutional speedy-trial violation calls for dismissal, not sentence reduction or a new speedy trial.
