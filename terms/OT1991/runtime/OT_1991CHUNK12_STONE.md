## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment rejecting Nordlinger’s equal-protection challenge.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave her assessment governed by the lawful state scheme; reserve any properly presented independent traveler’s claim and award no judicially redesigned tax schedule.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Tax disparities and exceptions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach her own equal-protection claim; decline assertion of absent travelers’ rights on this record.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply rational basis without a heightened record-building duty or new property-tax equality test.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Acquisition-value assessment and the challenged reassessment exceptions rationally serve legitimate predictability and continuity interests and do not violate equal protection on this claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Existing owners committed to property under prior tax expectations; a purchaser can consider the acquisition-based liability before committing. That distinction can rationally support predictability even if it distributes public burdens unevenly. Over-55 replacement rules permit adaptation without loss of the protected base; parent-child continuity is a permissible familial and neighborhood policy, not a classification based on nonmarital birth. The State need not prove optimality. Allegheny Pittsburgh concerned officials defeating their own State’s equal-value policy, whereas California adopted a different policy openly. The disparity’s magnitude matters but does not by itself negate the relation to the chosen objective. Master’s ordinary-economics restraint forecloses treating disfavored tax policy as caste without supporting facts.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve a proper travel claim, discriminatory administration departing from the adopted system, and other statutory tax challenges. Approval of rationality does not declare the policy equitable or economically wise.

# Georgia v. McCollum

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The eligible baseline in Section I.B.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate against actual earlier effective simulated decisions, particularly the dependencies identified in Section I.B. No approval or prior Stone case-specific position is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Georgia Supreme Court judgment and remand for jury selection subject to the prohibition on purposeful racial defense strikes.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the State to raise supported objections under the staged Batson procedure. Make no finding of discrimination before strikes occur and prohibit no genuinely race-neutral challenge.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Attribution and public jury function

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach state action as the necessary antecedent to juror equal protection.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Edmonson’s act-specific attribution analysis to criminal peremptories, while retaining Polk County for ordinary representation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A criminal defendant’s invocation of state peremptory authority to obtain judicial exclusion from a governmental jury is state action for this limited purpose.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The defendant acts against the prosecutor, but the relevant relationship is to the excluded juror and the court. State law creates the power; judicial officers assemble the panel and make the exclusion effective. These features were decisive in Edmonson and remain present here. Representing a client, investigating, and advising do not become government functions by association with litigation. Stone applies Master’s source-bound attribution, not a free-standing constitutional restriction on all private prejudice.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general state-action status attaches to criminal defendants or their counsel.

### Issue 2: Enforcement and defense protections

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the State’s standing and defense objections because both are necessary to the prospective ruling.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Powers and extend Batson’s existing procedure without changing its purpose requirement or persuasion burden.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The State may vindicate excluded jurors’ rights in its own trial process; defense rights do not include purposeful racial exclusion.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The State has a concrete institutional injury in the integrity of its tribunal, a direct relationship to summoned jurors, and a practical enforcement role where an excluded person faces serious obstacles to an individual suit. At an actual objection, the State must first show facts supporting an inference; defense counsel then supplies a race-neutral reason; the judge determines whether the State proved purposeful discrimination. No reason is demanded merely because a minority juror was struck. The rule preserves challenges based on actual bias and meaningful counsel. It does not presume a juror’s loyalties from race. Specific confidentiality concerns can receive appropriate protection without defeating the rule categorically. Master §9’s rejection of unsupported racial assumptions is consistent with this native juror-rights rule; there is no occasion to replace racial scrutiny throughout equal-protection law.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve the treatment of a concrete privilege dispute, unraised nonracial strike grounds, and protective rules aimed at unconscious bias. This holding neither abolishes peremptories nor converts disparate impact alone into constitutional purpose.

# Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The eligible baseline in Section I.B.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate against actual earlier effective simulated decisions, particularly the dependencies identified in Section I.B. No approval or prior Stone case-specific position is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Seventh Circuit judgment insofar as it establishes categorical preemption from occupational overlap and lack of an approved state plan; remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require provision-specific examination for a demonstrated conflict with federal law; preserve independently supported rulings and do not direct wholesale enforcement or invalidation before that inquiry.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Meaning of assumption and overlap

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach statutory preemption and reject reliance solely on the statutes’ public-safety purpose.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt the concurrent-standards reading of the unresolved federal statute; this is disagreement with the lower court, not overruling an entering Supreme Court holding.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 18’s state-plan process does not automatically preempt every compatible supplementary state occupational-safety standard; displacement requires the statutory scheme’s actual exclusivity or a demonstrated conflict.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The enacted heading and approval/withdrawal structure concern a State assuming the federal regulatory role. Section 18(a)’s express preservation where no federal rule exists does not logically establish total displacement whenever one does. Federal minimum training and additional state training may be simultaneously obeyed; their overlap alone proves neither impossibility nor defeat of a congressional operation. The strongest contrary reading is that mandatory plan submission would otherwise lose work. It retains substantial work in allowing approved state responsibility to replace federal administration, while supplementation remains subject to actual federal requirements. Stone therefore rejects a general anti-duplication purpose untethered to operative language. He also rejects Illinois’s claim that a public-safety purpose alone immunizes a conflicting provision.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No presumption makes state law prevail over valid federal law. Reserve which provisions are actually incompatible, and recognize that a clearly enacted federal ceiling or exclusive arrangement would control. No new state licensing entitlement is created.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment invalidating the challenged fee scheme, on its standardless and content-sensitive features rather than a universal nominal-fee ceiling.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Prevent enforcement of this fee mechanism against protected demonstrations; leave the County free to adopt lawful neutral coordination rules and a sufficiently bounded cost policy.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Discretion and audience hostility

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach both defects in the licensing rule; the protected-expression setting supports facial review.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply received licensing and content-discrimination law, preserving Cox’s lawful cost-recovery principle.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A fee system giving officials unbounded discretion and allowing anticipated hostility to a message to raise the price of speaking is facially invalid; a numerical cap does not cure either defect.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The cap limits the maximum but not the choice among zero, $100, and larger charges. That choice can deter speakers even without a completed discriminatory denial. Security pricing keyed to predicted opposition makes expression more expensive when its opponents threaten disruption. Maintaining order is legitimate, but shifting the cost of hostile reaction to the target does not satisfy the necessary neutrality or tailoring. The actual $100 assessment need not be proved retaliatory to establish the system’s defect. Stone applies the same rule to this speaker as to the earlier civil-rights marchers and does not grant or deny speech protection by viewpoint.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve fixed neutral fees, genuine content-independent administrative costs, and a concrete indigency challenge. No absolute ban on all nonnominal fees or mandatory municipal spending formula is announced.

# New York v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The eligible baseline in Section I.B.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate against actual earlier effective simulated decisions, particularly the dependencies identified in Section I.B. No approval or prior Stone case-specific position is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the three dockets’ judgments as to the monetary and access incentives; reverse as to the take-title provision and remand for corresponding declaratory relief.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Declare §2021e(d)(2)(C)’s compulsory title, possession, and associated liability mechanism invalid and sever it. Preserve the otherwise operative statute, including lawful surcharge distributions and authorized access choices. Create no federal replacement program or duty on other States to accept New York waste.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Authority and voluntary choices

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Find the challenge ripe because siting choices must be made now; reach each incentive separately.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Dole and the commerce/cooperative-regulation authorities without a new numeric coercion threshold.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The monetary and access incentives are authorized inducements on this record; the general responsibility language adds no independent command beyond the specified mechanisms.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Waste disposal is an actual interstate market, not merely an aggregation of local inconvenience. Congress can govern its terms and authorize access restrictions. The escrow conditions are explicit and connected to disposal capacity; loss of this program’s incentive distribution does not commandeer an established state function. Access restrictions govern disposal availability to producers if New York declines action; they do not legally require the State to regulate. This is a real adverse alternative rather than an invented federal administrative fallback. No evidence or statutory feature establishes that these branches compel the State to administer another government’s program.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Preserve later as-applied challenges based on a materially different coercive operation; no fixed funding percentage or presumption that every condition is voluntary is adopted.

### Issue 2: Compulsory title and state administration

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach take-title because neither statutory avoidance nor another threshold eliminates the explicit command.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Clarify and extend Hodel’s structural limitation to the explicit choice between state administration and compulsory state ownership/liability.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress’s ordinary commerce authority does not permit making a State either regulate private generators as Congress directs or take their waste and liability into state ownership.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Neither branch is an independently permissible option merely because the other is offered. Directly regulating generators, preempting state restrictions, and regulating a State’s own waste remain available; making the State assume unrelated generators’ legal responsibilities is different. Garcia concerns compliance by a state employer and is retained. FERC’s federal-law adjudication and limited consideration holdings do not establish an unlimited duty to administer federal policy. Under Master §16, this is a stated boundary development supported by Article I’s allocation and retained state responsibility, not revival of an unworkable traditional-functions immunity. Political advocacy for legislation is not a binding compact, and New York’s benefits do not establish consent to an otherwise compulsory ownership duty. The settled bargain matters to severability, but cannot supply an enumerated means.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve valid Reconstruction enforcement, state-court duties under federal law, actual compact obligations, and direct regulation of state market conduct.

### Issue 3: Remaining constitutional claim and severance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the limited Guarantee Clause claim directed to displacement of state decisionmaking, then severability.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Treat this specific institutional claim as judicially manageable under Baker; do not declare all Guarantee Clause disputes justiciable. Apply Alaska Airlines.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The remaining incentives preserve New York’s legislative choice and electoral accountability and do not deny republican government; take-title is severable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The claim requires no selection between rival governments or judicial prescription of an ideal state constitution. It asks whether federal law displaces the State’s ability to choose its legislative policy, an identifiable issue already analyzed through structural sources. That limited reach is a proposed resolution of the open justiciability point, distinguished from Luther. On the merits the retained branches impose consequences while leaving that choice in state hands. Take-title needs no additional Guarantee Clause ruling because the structural ground supplies full relief. The remaining financing and access provisions can operate and still advance Congress’s disposal objective. Their independent force supports severance despite take-title’s bargaining importance; absence of a clause does not compel destruction of the whole program.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve other Guarantee Clause theories and wider justiciability formulations. Severance removes a command; it does not authorize the judiciary to rewrite the interstate compromise.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Wisconsin Supreme Court judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Hold §381 protection unavailable on these combined activities; permit state assessment proceedings subject to remaining valid defenses and lawful tax calculation.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Solicitation and independent local business

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory boundary and the claimed trivial-departure qualification.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the open term by its commercial function; recognize a narrow de minimis construction as a proposed statutory clarification.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Solicitation includes activities serving no independent business function apart from obtaining orders; Wrigley’s recurring replacement, delivery, and stock-maintenance operations exceed it and are not de minimis together.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A sales force needs samples, supervision, recruitment, and sales meetings to request orders. Those are part of that process even though not themselves verbal requests. Replacing defective or stale inventory and supplying stock have value independent of requesting a new order; assigning them to representatives does not change their function. Credit intervention limited to facilitating requests can differ from operating a collection office. Stone permits genuinely trivial departures because the statutory class describes a business activity, not every isolated act, but states this interpretive qualification openly. The recurring independent stock functions establish a meaningful additional local connection here. A fixed dollar cutoff would be invented, and proportional smallness beside a large company’s total business is not dispositive.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve rare isolated acts and different service records; do not require taxpayers to prove that solicitation is physically impossible without every ancillary activity. No immunity extends to all conduct ordinarily performed by salespeople.

# Wright v. West

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The eligible baseline in Section I.B.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate against actual earlier effective simulated decisions, particularly the dependencies identified in Section I.B. No approval or prior Stone case-specific position is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit judgment and remand for disposition rejecting this insufficiency ground.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny habeas relief on the Jackson claim; do not order a new trial or expand custody beyond the lawful state judgment. Any other properly remaining habeas claim retains its own requirements.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Existing Jackson standard

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the exhausted record-based claim under the already established Jackson rule; no favorable new constitutional rule is necessary to decide it.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply independent legal review with Jackson’s mandated respect for rational jury inferences.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The quantity and recency of West’s possession, considered with the jury’s permissible rejection of his account, allow a rational finding of larceny beyond a reasonable doubt.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The jury was not confined to choosing between accepting his account and treating the goods as unexplained abstractions. Multiple items from the same theft in his possession, shortly afterward, support an inference of participation in taking them. His account may be evaluated for credibility, but disbelief alone would not create missing evidence. Here it operates alongside the affirmative possession evidence. The prosecution keeps every element’s burden; West need not prove innocence or corroborate his account as an element-shifting condition. Stone would not personally retry credibility or use the felony conviction as proof of criminal propensity.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve any unsupported proposition that possession invariably proves theft; materially weaker possession evidence could fail Jackson.

### Issue 2: No conversion of Teague into a second review barrier

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Address the proposed framework change only to the extent necessary to identify the rule used; decide no unnecessary historical habeas dispute.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Retain the distinction between retroactivity and independent application of existing federal law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The State prevails under Jackson itself, so this case supplies no need to replace independent review with a general reasonable-state-decision rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Teague asks which legal rules may operate after finality; it does not by its terms revise §2254 fact presumptions or the court’s task of applying an eligible constitutional standard. A disagreement over a requested extension may raise a genuine new-rule question, but the soundness of the existing conviction can be determined here without granting an extension. No new-law exception is needed because no relief is awarded. Master’s native-doctrine and restraint instructions support deciding the sufficient ground, not using it as an occasion to close federal review more broadly.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve a habeas case requiring a genuine disputed extension of Jackson and the application of Teague’s exceptions. No later AEDPA or Brecht rule enters this brief.

# United States v. Salerno

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The eligible baseline in Section I.B.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate against actual earlier effective simulated decisions, particularly the dependencies identified in Section I.B. No approval or prior Stone case-specific position is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Second Circuit judgment insofar as it dispenses with Rule 804(b)(1)’s similar-motive requirement and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require consideration of the parties’ actual similar-motive arguments and the resulting admissibility and judgment consequences. Preserve independent appellate grounds; direct neither categorical exclusion nor unconditional reinstatement of every conviction.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Every element, actual motive

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the Rule-based exclusion question; reserve an unpresented compelled-immunity or independent constitutional ruling.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the Rule as written while rejecting categorical assumptions about grand-jury motives.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Defense-offered grand-jury testimony must satisfy similar motive as well as opportunity; fairness cannot erase that requirement, and the forum label alone does not decide whether it is met.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The prior testimony’s favorable character makes accurate application consequential but does not rewrite an exception. A prosecutor may have strong reasons to test a denial when developing the same alleged conspiracy, or may be pursuing different investigative questions; the actual examination and proceeding supply the answer. The Second Circuit did not complete that analysis under the correct rule. Government evidence contradicting the witnesses does not disclose or affirm their prior statements and thus does not by itself forfeit a hearsay objection. Dennis concerns obtaining material to use, not automatic admission. Stone’s disclosure commitments likewise do not convert access into admissibility. The defense bears this evidentiary showing, while the prosecution still bears criminal guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve independent constitutional objections properly preserved and supported, other hearsay exceptions, and the factual motive determination. A rule requiring identical motives would also be incorrect; similar is the enacted boundary.
