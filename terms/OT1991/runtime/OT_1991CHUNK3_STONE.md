# United States Department of State v. Ray

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible law immediately before December 16, 1991.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the order requiring these identifying details.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Sustain the Exemption 6 redactions while preserving disclosure of the segregable substantive reports.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Protect identities without shielding the monitoring record

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the only preserved exemption question.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Rose* with the agency retaining its statutory burden.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** On this record the identifiers' limited demonstrated additional oversight value does not warrant connecting sensitive interview material to named returnees.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Redacted reports permit examination of what officials asked, what was reported, when interviews occurred, and the sample used. Identifiers would expose intimate facts and cooperation that the interviewees supplied under an accepted confidentiality assurance. The invasion is concrete even without finding that retaliation will occur. Independent verification can be a legitimate public benefit; its weight depends on the demonstrated informational connection and must be considered on the same practical footing as privacy consequences. Here no concrete additional information likely obtainable from identifiers is shown beyond a general hope of checking the reports. That does not establish dishonesty, but neither does it create an irrebuttable official presumption. Together with the extensive segregated disclosure and sensitive content, the agency's showing satisfies the more demanding Exemption 6 threshold.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No requirement to prove wrongdoing before any FOIA request; no categorical exclusion of derivative investigation; no finding that Haiti is safe or that asylum claims lack merit. The result concerns these identifiers and this disclosure record.

# INS v. National Center for Immigrants' Rights, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible law immediately before December 16, 1991; actual earlier administrative-law holdings require revalidation if material.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the facial statutory invalidation and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit enforcement only under the supported unauthorized-work construction and the described individualized process. Leave preserved constitutional and as-applied claims for proper proceedings.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The narrower regulation falls within statutory discretion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide facial statutory authority, not detention policy generally.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply contextual construction and existing administrative doctrine without revising deference rules.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 242(a) permits this default condition against unauthorized employment, understood to exclude authorized workers and retain individualized determination and relief.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The heading, exception, contemporaneous explanation, and operating instructions support the narrower reading independently of treating the agency as final judge of its power. The enacted labor-market provisions connect unauthorized employment to immigration administration; the delegation is not restricted to physical appearance alone. *Witkovich* requires a statutory connection, which this condition has. General rules can structure discretion, but cannot replace the individual status determination. Existing authorization, colorable citizenship, and the regulation's own exceptions therefore matter in each case. The actual channels refute a theory that the text necessarily eliminates all individualized judgment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This construction does not establish a general power to deny lawful work, presume every contested migrant removable, or reject due process and practical-access claims. No administrative deference precedent is overruled; broad disagreement with such deference is unnecessary to the result.

# Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible law immediately before December 16, 1991, subject to revalidation of an effective *Hafer* ruling.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand the state-court dismissal.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit Hilton to litigate the existing FELA claim against the Commission in state court; do not revive his dismissed federal action or award compensation without proof.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Retain the specific statutory precedent

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve amenability under this settled FELA construction, leaving negligence merits below.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the surviving portion of *Parden*, despite Stone's general preference against Article I private-suit abrogation. His preference does not silently displace entering precedent.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** FELA's established coverage of state-owned interstate railroads remains enforceable in this state-court action; *Welch*'s federal-immunity holding is undisturbed.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** *Parden* actually reasoned statutory inclusion from every common carrier and the railway regulatory scheme. The later rejection of its waiver theory weakens any argument resting on that constitutional premise, but does not negate the separately reserved coverage construction. *Will* concerns a different statutory term and context. Under Master §16, continuity rests on an administrable rule, an independent statutory basis, and concrete reliance: legislatures excluded these workers from alternative compensation and workers' protection was organized around FELA. Congressional silence alone would not prove approval, nor does age establish correctness; together with retained text and actual arrangements it reinforces the statutory precedent. Displacing the surviving rule here would withdraw an established forum without a demonstrated need to abandon its specific statutory reading. Stone therefore retains this precedent rather than inventing a broad state-immunity exception.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general ruling that Article I always authorizes private damages against States, no implied waiver by merely entering commerce, and no proposition that lack of another remedy itself overrides sovereign immunity. The state-court result follows the particular surviving FELA precedent.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove both the per se three-year exclusion and the implied durational-reasonableness condition; permit further proceedings on actual statutory obligations.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Enforce the duration Congress selected

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide only the asserted implied duration limit.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply whole-statute interpretation without a new balancing test.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2024(d) contains no implicit reasonableness limit on the length of otherwise covered military service.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The subsection connects leave to the period required for service, and the Army required this tour's duration. Congress imposed time limits in adjacent categories and omitted one here. # Hunter v. Bryant

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible law immediately before December 16, 1991; May 1985 remains the notice date for the challenged conduct. Revalidate actual earlier immunity decisions.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the probable-cause immunity ruling and remand for application of the correct judicial summary-judgment standard; do not direct judgment for either side on the present legal rationale.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require prompt judicial determination using the whole contemporaneous record. Identify any genuine material historical dispute and the supported nonmovant account before deciding whether a reasonable officer could believe probable cause existed. Leave the separate warrant disposition intact.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Apply the existing immunity standard to the actual offense

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only qualified immunity on the arrest-without-probable-cause claim.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Harlow*, *Anderson*, and *Malley* as entering law. The preferred merits-first, narrow legal-reliance defense in the Master is not substituted in this case.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The defense is judicially determinable under ordinary summary-judgment limits; a competing more reasonable account alone does not defeat it, and generalized concern for presidential safety alone does not establish it.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The agents' reports must be considered together, including the troubling oral statements and gesture. But the letter's repeated identification of an external group also matters to whether its author was communicating a serious threat rather than reporting an imagined one. A refusal to elaborate does not itself admit threatening conduct. Both the statutory offense and the full reasonable-officer immunity allowance must be applied; the question is not which interpretation the reviewing court prefers. The lower opinion's mixed references to a jury and a most-reasonable reading warrant clarification through remand rather than adoption of either formulation. The remand court must decide the defense if the accepted facts permit only a protected reasonable-officer assessment; it may defer only where a specifically identified, supported factual dispute matters under that rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding of innocence, bad faith, fabrication, or constitutional violation; no automatic trial; no requirement of an identical prior case. This ruling changes neither qualified immunity nor the recognized damages channel. Broader doctrinal revision would need a properly presented issue and the full §16 analysis, which this application dispute does not require.

# Smith v. Barry

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible law immediately before January 14, 1992, with an actual *Ibarra* holding integrated if pertinent.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Direct the Fourth Circuit to determine whether the timely informal brief satisfies Rule 3(c), applying the mistaken-filing provision. No present ruling that jurisdiction over every intended issue is established.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Content, timing, and the rule's express protection govern

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve access to the appellate forum before any underlying merits.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Torres*, *Foman*, and the Rules as then written; no reclassification of their jurisdictional force is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A document styled as a brief can serve as a timely notice if it objectively supplies Rule 3(c)'s required information and manifests appellate review.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The rule itself rejects dismissal for informal form or title. Responding to a clerk's form does not negate the notice the response conveys; Smith received that form after trying to appeal. The ordinary sequence of separate papers is an administrative arrangement, not an additional condition of jurisdiction beyond the governing rule. Filing with the appellate clerk is addressed by the express transmittal provision, preserving the May 4 receipt date. The first notice remains ineffective under the rule then applicable. Actual omissions remain consequential and require the remand inquiry, so protecting substance does not abolish mandatory requirements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No equitable extension of May 13, no automatic sufficiency for every informal paper, and no decision on medical care, force, state action, or damages. Stone's preferred precision about jurisdictional terminology does not silently displace *Torres*.

# County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes and Bands of the Yakima Indian Nation

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible law immediately before January 14, 1992; revalidate any material actual statutory-construction holding in *Hilton* or *Wolas*.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment and remand with modified instructions: preserve the prohibition of the sales excise, reject the ad valorem impact-balancing instruction, and remand for application of the specific federal tax-authorization rule to the parcels.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Recognize ad valorem authority for land validly patented in fee under the General Allotment Act, including such fee land held by the Nation, without ordering foreclosure of unidentified parcels. Determine patent sources and any legally material distinctions on remand. Maintain appropriate protection against taxes outside the established authorization.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Specific land-tax permission survives without general state dominion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the governing authorization before parcel-level application.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Blackfeet*, *Goudy*, and the whole statutory scheme, distinguishing *Moe* rather than overruling it.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The General Allotment Act's established fee-land taxation rule and Burke Act's express confirmation authorize the ad valorem levy on qualifying fee-patented land; the IRA did not restore every such parcel to tax-protected trust status.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The County has more than an inference that saleability should imply taxability: *Goudy* actually decided the ordinary-patent question, and Congress expressly addressed land taxation in the early-patent proviso. The IRA halted future allotments and preserved trusts but provided a distinct means to reacquire land in trust, permitting both enactments to operate. Later protections defeat sweeping personal jurisdiction under *Moe*; the specific property rule does not reestablish that jurisdiction. The Nation's preemption argument therefore receives independent consideration, but the described legislation does not establish that merely holding the qualifying fee parcel restores the removed restriction. No assertion that taxes are harmless to tribal life is necessary. The source, not an assessment of comparative government importance, controls.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general proposition that alienability alone defeats tribal immunity; no extension to other patent statutes without examination; no decision concerning trust parcels or an independently presented defense to a particular enforcement remedy. *Brendale*'s nonmember-regulation framework is not the tax test.

### Issue 2: The sales excise exceeds that permission

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Independently classify the second tax.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing Indian-law clear-statement and ambiguity rules.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Permission to tax the land does not unmistakably authorize the tax on an Indian owner's sale of it.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Ownership triggers the annual levy, whereas a transaction triggers the excise. A lien is security for collection, not a change in the taxed activity; otherwise any unauthorized personal tax could become a land tax by adding a lien. *Capoeman*'s reference to all taxes does not erase the governing provision's object. The narrower permissible reading must govern under *Blackfeet*. The result gives effect to the actual authorization and its boundary without importing a Stone-specific balancing rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No immunity for taxes whose lawful incidence falls on non-Indians, no blanket prohibition of all transactions concerning reservation land, and no invented final tax assessment.

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** In both consolidated dockets, affirm the exclusion of suburban-district candidates for insufficient suburban signatures; reverse the name-based and whole-slate exclusions; remand the unresolved judicial-candidate objection.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the state court to determine that remaining state-law objection and consequent party status consistently with the federal ruling. ### Issue 1: Continuing controversy and state-law remand

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the decided federal questions because the 1990 results have continuing qualification consequences and the short election cycle threatens recurrence.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing justiciability and remand principles.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The election's completion does not moot this dispute; the unaddressed judicial-office objection should return to the Illinois Supreme Court.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Existing votes above 5% can change the next election's access requirements. The state court's omission of the judicial issue is not a holding that it has merit. Authoritative state construction may remove that objection without another constitutional decision.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No decision on the constitutionality of requiring judicial candidates, party leadership generally, or water-district access.

### Issue 2: Name use and whole-slate disqualification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Both grounds were used to exclude petitioners and require resolution.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Anderson and Socialist Workers Party to these restrictions; propose no new scrutiny tier or burden.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Illinois cannot categorically bar affiliated candidates from expanding under their party's name or exclude otherwise qualified countywide and city candidates solely because a separate district lacks sufficient signatures.

A permission requirement addresses the asserted confusion more directly. The Board's authorization finding and absence of a state supreme court determination that authorization is impossible do not support treating these candidates as impostors. The separate-election defense justifies support in each race; it does not explain disqualifying candidates who met the support requirement for their own constituency. Doing so effectively doubles the support needed for county access compared with statewide access. The burden is severe, and the supplied justifications do not establish the necessity of that consequence.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The State may police actual unauthorized name use. Do not approve a hypothetical replacement geographic distribution formula or require the State to adopt one particular election code.

### Issue 3: Suburban candidates

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Address the organizers' separate challenge despite the Board's contrary position.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Jenness contextually, without treating its 5% example as a universal safe harbor.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** On this record, the independent 25,000-signature requirement for suburban seats is not unconstitutional merely because the party met the city requirement.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The district arrangement is unchallenged; suburban voters choose these representatives. Only 7,800 signatures were submitted there, and no supported additional burden demonstrates that the roughly 2% requirement suppresses access. Association does not turn support in one electorate into support in another.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Preserve challenges based on a different record, cumulative burdens, or unconstitutional district boundaries.

# Molzof v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Pre-event historical law; no effective simulated decision supplied.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate against actually effective earlier simulated law. No approved alternative is asserted.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Seventh Circuit's judgment insofar as it bars the disputed damages as federally punitive; remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Lower courts must decide whether Wisconsin law permits the requested compensation and what offsets or other limitations apply. Do not direct payment, disturb the admitted liability determination, or prescribe an amount.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Meaning of punitive damages

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the statutory issue supporting the judgment, without a constitutional ruling.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted waiver and the established meaning of its borrowed tort term. This rejects the lower court's interpretation; it does not abolish the statutory immunity from punitive awards.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2674 excludes damages whose legal nature is punishment for culpable wrongdoing, not every allegedly excessive or duplicative award of compensation. These simple-negligence claims do not become punitive merely because the United States disputes their compensatory value.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute specifies a category of damages while otherwise adopting private-person liability. Congress used an established legal term, and Massachusetts Bonding confirms the connection between punitive recovery and culpability. The Government's alternative turns federal courts into general revisers of state compensation rules without corresponding statutory language. Its reasonable-approximation concession further shows why imperfect equivalence to actual loss cannot supply a complete dividing rule. The separate wrongful-death amendment solves a defined statutory problem and supplies no general pecuniary-only limitation here. The FTCA's other exceptions protect identified governmental functions; their differing language does not erase this provision's common-law meaning.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Labels alone do not control: an award actually serving the legally punitive function remains barred. No punitive award against the Treasury, new cause of action, or immunity revision is proposed.

### Issue 2: Unresolved compensation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only the consequence of rejecting the federal bar.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply state-law incorporation and ordinary appellate remand practice.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Federal permissibility is necessary but insufficient; the estate must establish Wisconsin-law entitlement before receiving additional compensation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The trial findings concerning free adequate care can matter to state-law duplication and offsets without converting the damages into punishment. Consciousness may matter to Wisconsin's recognition or measurement of lost enjoyment. Those unresolved questions belong to the lower courts; Stone cannot replace them with assumptions favoring either litigant.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No holding on whether Wisconsin allows either category, the proper amount, or the consequences of the patient's intervening death for calculation.
