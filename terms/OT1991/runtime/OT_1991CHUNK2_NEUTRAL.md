# Estelle v. McGuire

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Estelle v. McGuire
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 62 (1991); No. 90-1074.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Federal habeas review of a California second-degree murder conviction.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 4, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Did evidence of prior injuries to the victim, or instructions governing its use, render McGuire's trial fundamentally unfair or relieve the State of proving his identity as the killer?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, 902 F.2d 749 (1990), reversing denial of federal habeas relief.
* **Relief Requested:** The warden seeks reversal; McGuire seeks habeas relief from the conviction.
* **Material Record:** Six-month-old Tori died with extensive injuries. Physicians identified old fractured ribs and rectal tearing as evidence of repeated nonaccidental abuse. No direct evidence identified who inflicted those old injuries. A neighbor described McGuire's rough handling; the parents were the regular caregivers. McGuire initially suggested a fall from a couch. His wife, after receiving transactional immunity, testified at trial that she had beaten the child, contrary to earlier statements. The challenged charge introduced prior-act evidence as showing acts by “the Defendant,” forbade using it merely to show bad character, and permitted a conclusion that if he committed prior offenses he also committed the charged crime when a clear connection existed. The identity of the killer remained contested.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Habeas corrects federal violations, not errors of California evidence law. The preserved instruction claim is distinct from a general constitutional ban on propensity evidence. No independent procedural default is identified in the reviewed materials.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 4, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decisions supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Revalidate the actual preceding *Griffin* holding if it addresses instructions, factual alternatives, or the consequence of general verdicts. Its draft is not law, and a factual-alternative rule would not automatically dispose of a burden-shifting instruction claim.

### Issue 1: Evidence and the federal habeas boundary

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline absent an effective simulated adoption.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Lewis v. Jeffers*, 497 U.S. 764 (1990), and *Pulley v. Harris*, 465 U.S. 37 (1984), exclude mere state-law error. *Lisenba v. California*, 314 U.S. 219 (1941), and *Dowling v. United States*, 493 U.S. 342 (1990), demand fundamental unfairness, a narrow federal category, for an otherwise ordinary evidentiary claim.
* **Other Governing Law:** Then-current 28 U.S.C. §2254 supplies the custody remedy and rules for state factual determinations.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Winship*, 397 U.S. 358 (1970), leaves the State responsible for proving every offense element beyond reasonable doubt even when the defense does not dispute one. Absence of an accident defense therefore does not automatically make nonaccident evidence irrelevant.
* **Open Point:** Prior injury evidence can establish nonaccidental harm without identifying the abuser. Whether its permitted use crossed the federal line must be considered independently from state admissibility.

### Issue 2: Ambiguous instructions and elemental proof

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Same baseline, subject to an actual material *Griffin* adoption.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Cupp v. Naughten*, 414 U.S. 141 (1973), examines instructions in the whole trial. *Francis v. Franklin*, 471 U.S. 307 (1985), and *Sandstrom v. Montana*, 442 U.S. 510 (1979), prohibit instructions relieving the State of elemental persuasion; permissive inferences remain separately governed by *County Court v. Allen*, 442 U.S. 140 (1979). *Boyde v. California*, 494 U.S. 370 (1990), supplies reasonable-likelihood analysis of ambiguous capital instructions, a relevant interpretive approach rather than an evidentiary quantum. McGuire bears the burden of establishing the unconstitutional operation, not merely an imaginable misunderstanding.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Due Process Clause governs elemental proof; the pre-AEDPA habeas statute supplies the remedial channel.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Teague v. Lane*, 489 U.S. 288 (1989), constrains new rules on collateral review. Ordinary application of established elemental-proof law differs from a new categorical constitutional evidence rule. Under the pre-*Brecht* regime, constitutional trial error subject to harmless review requires the State to establish harmlessness beyond reasonable doubt under *Chapman*, 386 U.S. 18 (1967); *Rose v. Clark*, 478 U.S. 570 (1986), permits harmless review of an erroneous presumption. AEDPA and *Brecht* are later law.
* **Open Point:** Whether the charge, taken as a whole, permissibly conditioned an inference on jury findings or suggested that identity was already established. The question is independently reviewed; credibility is not retried.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

California courts admitted the evidence to demonstrate battered-child syndrome. The federal district court denied habeas; the Ninth Circuit found evidentiary irrelevance and an unfair instruction together. The warden argues that nonaccident remained an element and that “if” plus the anti-character warning required the jury to decide who committed prior acts. McGuire argues that grouping unidentified grave injuries with identified rough handling labeled all as his acts, then directed a supposed logical conclusion about the murder. The State's strongest limiting point is the express anti-propensity clause; McGuire must explain why the specific identity language still defeated the general warning. His wife's trial testimony makes actor identification material, though it is not conclusive proof of his innocence.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reject the state-law and evidence-alone grounds. Then reject the instruction claim and reverse habeas relief if no unconstitutional interpretation is reasonably likely; or find a violation and remand for harmless-error review before ordering a new trial or conditional release. A new constitutional propensity prohibition requires separate *Teague* analysis and is unnecessary if established proof-burden law decides the instruction. The full record must govern harmlessness; the packet cannot find which caregiver caused the fatal or earlier injuries.

## E. SOURCES

[Record, charge, and proceedings, 502 U.S. 62](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/502/62); [Francis](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/471/307/); [Boyde](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/494/370/); [Rose](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/478/570/); [Teague](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/489/288/). The current historical holding is not entering law.

# Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 81 (1991); No. 90-584.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Review of summary judgment in a maritime worker's action.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 4, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Does employment in an occupation enumerated by the LHWCA, or acceptance of voluntary benefits, foreclose a Jones Act claim by a worker who may be a vessel crew member?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, 909 F.2d 385 (1990), reversing summary judgment for the employer.
* **Relief Requested:** Southwest Marine seeks reinstatement of summary judgment; Gizoni seeks adjudication of seaman status and his injury claims.
* **Material Record:** Gizoni was a rigging foreman who worked on floating platforms used to carry and position repair equipment. Tugs moved the platforms; they lacked independent propulsion and living facilities. He rode them, sometimes acted as lookout or signaled maneuvers, and handled lines. A covered deck opening gave way, causing disabling injuries. He received LHWCA medical and compensation payments without a litigated coverage adjudication, then sued under the Jones Act and pleaded unseaworthiness and maintenance and cure. He alleged permanent assignment to the group of platforms. The district court found no vessel-in-navigation or navigational work; the Ninth Circuit found genuine factual questions.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Status is mixed law and fact. Benefits receipt, final adjudicatory preclusion, and ultimate statutory coverage are different. No final administrative award resolving seaman status is established.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 4, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated holdings supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Revalidate actual *MCorp* treatment of express administrative-review preclusion if material; its banking-regulation review channel cannot automatically migrate into the LHWCA. The index also orders same-date entries; integrate any earlier effective simulated holding to the extent relevant, without treating a draft or historical result as precedent.

### Issue 1: Status and mutually exclusive remedies

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *McDermott International v. Wilander*, 498 U.S. 337 (1991), majority, requires an employment-related connection to a vessel in navigation and contribution to the vessel's work; aiding navigation is unnecessary. A reasonable dispute over the facts or application ordinarily belongs to the factfinder. Summary judgment remains possible where only one conclusion is legally supportable.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Jones Act, then 46 U.S.C. App. §688, provides an action for a seaman's employment injury. LHWCA §§902(3)(G) and 905(a) exclude vessel masters and crew from its employee definition and make its remedy exclusive only for covered employees. The claimant bears proof of seaman status; ordinary civil fact proof applies, while legal interpretation and summary judgment are reviewed independently.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Swanson v. Marra Brothers*, 328 U.S. 1 (1946), explains the statutory separation. The occupational list does not eliminate the crew exclusion. No later percentage-of-work formula is entering law.
* **Open Point:** Whether these platforms and Gizoni's actual connection satisfy the applicable status test on developed facts.

### Issue 2: Agency priority and benefits acceptance

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Same baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Voluntary compensation receipt is not itself a conclusive seaman-status adjudication; preclusion requires the predicates of a legally operative determination, absent here.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Tipton v. Socony Mobil Oil*, 375 U.S. 34 (1963), rejects using compensation receipt as conclusive status determination.
* **Other Governing Law:** LHWCA §913(d) accommodates a rejected Jones Act claim before subsequent compensation; §903(e) provides credit to prevent double recovery. Unlike statutes with express exclusive administrative coverage determination, this scheme contains no comprehensive bar requiring a final LHWCA proceeding before a Jones Act action.
* **Open Point:** A litigated final award, settlement, or other established preclusion basis would present a different question.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court treated ship repair and absence of navigational duties as dispositive; the Ninth Circuit emphasized actual vessel relationship. The employer argues occupational coverage, administrative priority, and election of remedies from accepting benefits. Gizoni relies on the explicit crew exception and his platform assignment; the United States supports his position. His account must still establish status and negligence, not merely a shipyard injury. The employer retains summary-judgment arguments tied to actual status evidence, rather than occupational labels alone.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm reversal of summary judgment and permit status factfinding. Alternatively adopt categorical occupational exclusion or administrative priority, explaining the statutory basis and resulting effect on the crew exclusion. No adjudicated status or negligence finding can be supplied here. Credit against overlapping compensation prevents duplicate recovery if further relief is awarded; rejecting preclusion does not decide the availability or amount of each pleaded remedy.

## E. SOURCES

[Record and statutes, 502 U.S. 81](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/502/81); [Wilander](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/498/337/); [Tipton](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/375/34/); [Swanson](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/328/1/).

# Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 93 (1991); No. 90-967.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Review of civil jury entitlement and federal jurisdiction over a union-constitution claim.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 4, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Does a member have a jury right on his LMRDA damages claim, and may he sue under LMRA §301 for violation of an interunion constitution benefiting him?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, 907 F.2d 151 (1990), restoring the LMRDA retaliation claim but denying a jury and sustaining dismissal of the union-constitution claim.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse those two adverse rulings and return for adjudication.
* **Material Record:** Wooddell opposed a dues increase and a representative's appointment. He alleges Local 71 retaliated through discriminatory hiring-hall referrals. Internal charges had not culminated in a final decision. He sued the Local and officers for lost wages and benefits, other damages, and injunctive relief; the alleged contract source included the international constitution requiring locals to honor collective-bargaining agreements. The record establishes claims, not retaliation or contractual breach.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The two reviewed questions do not reopen every dismissed state-law, disciplinary, or fair-representation claim. Whether the international constitution is an interunion contract differs from whether purely local bylaws alone satisfy §301.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 4, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated holding supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Earlier *Hafer* may address keeping coverage and liability separate, but its §1983 interpretation does not establish §301 scope. No direct prior jury or union-contract dependency is identified.

### Issue 1: Civil jury entitlement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Chauffeurs, Teamsters & Helpers Local 391 v. Terry*, 494 U.S. 558 (1990), asks the historical analogue and the legal or equitable character of relief, with the latter more important. The judgment recognized a jury right for compensatory backpay in the claim there; its historical-analogy discussion had divided opinion support. 
* **Material Other Authority:** *Terry*'s compensatory-remedy analysis remains relevant where a claimant combines legal damages and equitable relief; combination does not eliminate the jury entitlement on legal issues.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Seventh Amendment and LMRDA Title I, including its enforcement provision, govern. Lost earnings from jobs never referred may be compensatory damages rather than restitution incident to reinstatement. The legal classification is reviewed independently; a request labeled damages must still be examined by function.
* **Open Point:** Classification of this claim and relief, not proof of the underlying discrimination.

### Issue 2: Interunion contract suits by beneficiaries

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Same baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Plumbers v. Local 334*, 452 U.S. 615 (1981), treats union constitutions as contracts between labor organizations within §301. The plaintiff must identify a qualifying contract and a claimed violation affecting an enforceable contractual interest.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Smith v. Evening News Association*, 371 U.S. 195 (1962), permits individual beneficiaries to enforce covered labor agreements.
* **Other Governing Law:** 29 U.S.C. §185(a) authorizes suits for violation of contracts between qualifying employers and labor organizations or between labor organizations. The text identifies covered contracts, not an exclusive list of organizational plaintiffs.
* **Open Point:** Whether individual beneficiaries of an interunion constitution are excluded despite those principles. The existence of a valid contractual duty, breach, available defenses, and recoverable loss remains for further proceedings.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Sixth Circuit treated the statutory damages claim as nonjury and read §301 as unavailable to individual members enforcing a union constitution. Wooddell invokes the compensatory nature of lost referrals and his beneficial interest in the international-local agreement. The union argues that intraunion matters belong in state law and that expanded federal jurisdiction invites trivial disputes. Wooddell's limiting answer is that only qualifying interunion contracts enter §301 and ordinary claim requirements remain. LMRDA's separate statutory protections do not necessarily repeal or narrow the earlier contract channel.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Recognize a jury right on the legal damages claim and §301 jurisdiction over the interunion contract claim, reverse, and remand without deciding liability. Either issue can be resolved separately if its counterpart fails. Purely equitable relief remains for the court; the existence of mixed relief requires proper sequencing, not blanket denial of a jury. The Court need not declare every local bylaw federally enforceable or prescribe damages unavailable under the applicable source.

## E. SOURCES

[Record and questions, 502 U.S. 93](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/502/93); [Terry](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/494/558/); [Local 334](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/452/615/); [Evening News](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/371/195/).

# Gibson v. Florida Bar

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gibson v. Florida Bar
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 104 (1991); No. 90-1102.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of mandatory-bar-dues objection procedures.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 4, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Do the Florida Bar's procedures adequately protect members who object to financing nongermane political lobbying through compulsory dues?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, 906 F.2d 624 (1990), upholding rule 2-9.3 except for requiring interest on refunds from receipt of dues rather than receipt of an objection.
* **Relief Requested:** Declaratory and prospective injunctive protection against unconstitutional compulsory financing.
* **Material Record:** Gibson's 1984 suit initially concerned a tax initiative, but retained a general challenge after that initiative left the ballot. Following an earlier appellate remand, Florida adopted rule 2-9.3 in 1988. Legislative positions are announced in the twice-monthly Bar News. A member must object to each position within 45 days or waive objection. Only after objection does the executive director escrow the disputed pro rata share, independently verified by an accountant. After the objection deadline, the Bar has 45 days to refund or arbitrate. A three-member panel consists of one Bar choice, one objector choice, and a mutually chosen chair, with a judicial appointment if agreement fails; all must be Bar members. The panel decides constitutional chargeability and must report within 45 days after constitution. The rule allowed another 30 days for payment and interest from objection; the court below corrected the interest start date.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Continuing compulsory assessments sustain the prospective dispute despite the original initiative's removal. A retroactive refund request first raised on appeal lacked a pleaded damages claim or supporting trial evidence; it is outside this review. The approved bylaw and lower-court record permit examination of procedures without deciding every lobbying expenditure.
* **Participation Matter:** No verified case-specific nonparticipation is supplied; no recusal reason is inferred. Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 4, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Earlier *Zatko* concerns access to a judicial filing process, not compelled ideological dues. No direct bar-dues dependency is identified; no earlier proposal supplies law.

### Issue 1: Chargeability and adequate protective procedure

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Keller v. State Bar*, 496 U.S. 1 (1990), permits compulsory support of activities germane to regulating the profession and improving legal services, while protecting objectors against nongermane ideological subsidies. It applies the *Abood* framework to integrated bars and identifies *Hudson*'s minimum procedures. *Chicago Teachers Union v. Hudson*, 475 U.S. 292 (1986), requires an adequate explanation, reasonably prompt impartial review, and escrow of amounts reasonably disputed. *Ellis v. Railway Clerks*, 466 U.S. 435 (1984), rejects a pure rebate that permits even temporary ideological financing; advance reduction or adequate interest-bearing escrow may protect the funds.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment applies through the Fourteenth to the State's compulsory bar-financing scheme; rule 2-9.3 supplies the challenged procedures.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Abood v. Detroit Board of Education*, 431 U.S. 209, 239–241 (1977), permits an objection requirement but rejects requiring an objector to specify every ideological expenditure. The organization bears the burden of justifying chargeability; the member must make opposition known. These are funding safeguards, not a ban on the organization's voluntarily financed expression.
* **Open Point:** Whether post-position, post-objection escrow plus retroactive interest prevents the temporary use that *Ellis* and *Hudson* prohibit; whether the recurring issue-specific waiver is compatible with a general objection; and whether this panel structure is facially impartial.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Gibson argues for advance protection of the nongermane share, objects to monitoring and challenging each position, and contends Bar-member arbitrators have a financial interest. The Bar answers that future lobbying cannot be predicted precisely, that escrow is an approved alternative to advance deduction, that members need reveal only a chargeability objection rather than their political beliefs, and that shared selection prevents unilateral control. The Eleventh Circuit accepted those answers except for interest timing. Judge Clark's dissent distinguished escrow protecting funds before use from a rebate after compelled collection and stressed *Abood*'s general-objection rule. The constitutional inquiry does not turn on whether the challenged politics are agreeable.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain an adequately protective escrow alternative without mandating advance deductions in every system. It may nevertheless require protection before known objectors' disputed funds become available and accept a general objection. Panel membership and selection can be examined separately from the financial procedure. Any remand should preserve the existing interest correction and permit the Bar to choose a lawful financing system. No independently verified vehicle defect requires withholding merits consideration.

## E. SOURCES

[Full lower-court opinion and rule text, 906 F.2d 624](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/906/624/72850/); [Keller](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/496/1/); [Hudson](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/475/292/); [Ellis](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/466/435/); [Abood](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/431/209/).

# Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 105 (1991); No. 90-1059.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of a content-based proceeds-escrow statute.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 10, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Does New York Executive Law §632-a impermissibly burden speech by placing income from a person's account of crime in escrow for victims and creditors?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, 916 F.2d 777 (1990), affirming rejection of the publisher's First Amendment challenge.
* **Relief Requested:** A declaration of invalidity and injunction against enforcement through §1983.
* **Material Record:** Henry Hill and Nicholas Pileggi contracted with Simon & Schuster to publish Hill's life story. Hill had admitted serious organized crime and received immunity in exchange for testimony. The Board ordered the publisher to turn over money owed to Hill. Section 632-a reaches contracts depicting a crime or expressing an accused or convicted person's thoughts about it. Its definition also covers a voluntary, intelligent admission without prosecution. Funds enter a five-year escrow available for specified claims, with limited releases for legal representation and production expenses. New York's highest court has excluded victimless crimes. Ordinary compensation, forfeiture, restitution, and attachment laws separately exist.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The publisher faces a concrete payment obligation affecting publication contracts and can challenge that burden. Neither guilt nor a victim's damages claim is being adjudicated here. The Court must distinguish compensation from the means selected to secure it.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 10, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated holding supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Gibson*, if decided effectively by this date, may bear on compelled financial support and timing of escrow, but its bar-dues standard does not replace scrutiny of content-based burdens. Revalidate any broader speech holding actually adopted there.

### Issue 1: Content discrimination and justification

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline pending that revalidation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Arkansas Writers' Project v. Ragland*, 481 U.S. 221 (1987), requires the State to justify content-based differential financial burdens with a compelling interest and a necessary, narrowly drawn means. *Leathers v. Medlock*, 499 U.S. 439 (1991), distinguishes general taxes from selective burdens threatening expression. A benign purpose does not alone cure a content classification. The legal scrutiny is not a criminal proof standard.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment applies through the Fourteenth. A paid book remains protected expression; its sale does not make its content merely commercial advertising. 
* **Material Other Authority:** *Caplin & Drysdale v. United States*, 491 U.S. 617 (1989), concerns forfeiture of criminal proceeds; identifying assets generated by crime differs from identifying lawful compensation for discussing crime.
* **Open Point:** Whether the speech-linked escrow's fit can be justified by victim compensation or preventing profit from wrongdoing, including incidental references and unprosecuted admissions.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The publisher contends that speech about crime receives a financial disability unavailable against other income and that withholding compensation discourages sources and publication. The Board stresses compensating victims and preventing criminals from profiting, argues that escrow postpones payment rather than suppressing a message, and notes that the law governs any contracting entity. The publisher answers that the burden still turns on content and that neutral remedies can reach a wrongdoer's assets. The victimless-crime limitation narrows coverage but does not remove the statute's dependence on subject matter or incidental discussion.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may invalidate the challenged content-based escrow requirement and enjoin its application to this contract, leaving neutral creditor and restitution remedies intact. It may assess a narrowing construction actually supplied by state law; it cannot invent a replacement statute. An assumption that every autobiographical reference is a fruit of crime would prejudge the disputed classification. Relief need not settle Hill's debts, excuse his crimes, or constitutionalize immunity of publishing income from general collection laws.

## E. SOURCES

[Record and statutory text, 502 U.S. 105](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/502/105); [Arkansas Writers' Project](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/481/221/); [Leathers](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/499/439/); [Caplin & Drysdale](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/491/617/).

# Ardestani v. INS

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Ardestani v. INS
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 129 (1991); No. 90-1141.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of administrative attorney-fee eligibility.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 10, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Is an administrative deportation hearing an adjudication under 5 U.S.C. §554 for purposes of the Equal Access to Justice Act?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, 904 F.2d 1505 (1990), sustaining the Board of Immigration Appeals' denial of fees.
* **Relief Requested:** Restore the immigration judge's $1,071.85 fee award.
* **Material Record:** Ardestani, an Iranian Bahai, sought asylum after lawful admission as a visitor. Despite a favorable State Department assessment, INS denied asylum based on supposed safe haven in Luxembourg. She said she had stayed only three days in a hotel while traveling and never sought residence. At the deportation hearing she obtained asylum. The immigration judge found her the prevailing party and INS's position not substantially justified. The Board vacated the fee award because it considered deportation hearings outside EAJA coverage. Both parties agree the Government was represented by counsel.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The issue is statutory coverage of this administrative proceeding, not asylum eligibility, the validity of the underlying immigration judgment, or a freestanding constitutional right to government-paid counsel.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 10, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated ruling supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *MCorp* may clarify respecting a specific administrative statutory structure, but involves a different review statute. *Hafer* concerns state-officer liability, not the United States' fee waiver. Revalidate any actual general statutory holding without importing its distinct result.

### Issue 1: EAJA's covered adjudication

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** EAJA §504(a)(1) provides fees for an eligible prevailing party in an adversary adjudication unless the agency position was substantially justified or special circumstances make an award unjust. Section 504(b)(1)(C) requires an adjudication under §554 with the United States represented. Coverage precedes evaluation of justification; the Government bears the justification burden in covered proceedings under *Pierce v. Underwood*, 487 U.S. 552 (1988), whose reasonableness standard is not a merits-winning requirement.
* **Other Governing Law:** INA §242(b), then 8 U.S.C. §1252(b), supplies the sole and exclusive deportation procedure. 
* **Material Other Authority:** *Marcello v. Bonds*, 349 U.S. 302 (1955), holds that this scheme supersedes the APA's hearing provisions. Similarity to formal APA procedure does not itself change the statutory source. *Library of Congress v. Shaw*, 478 U.S. 310 (1986), requires an applicable sovereign-immunity waiver for monetary fee liability. *Sullivan v. Hudson*, 490 U.S. 877 (1989), permits some administrative-remand work integral to a covered judicial action under the judicial EAJA provision; it does not make every standalone agency hearing covered.
* **Open Point:** Whether “under section 554” incorporates only a descriptive category or requires proceedings actually governed by that section. No heightened evidentiary quantum resolves this legal construction.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Ardestani argues that deportation hearings meet §554(a)'s description of on-the-record adjudications required by statute; later regulations make the procedures functionally similar. She invokes EAJA's purpose of deterring unjustified governmental action and the hardship of defending immigration cases. INS answers that *Marcello* excludes the hearing from §554's governance and Congress selected a limited statutory category. A functional reading could serve EAJA's purpose, but it must still explain why Congress used this specific cross-reference rather than a general description of contested agency proceedings.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the cross-reference requires APA governance; reverse and remand for any remaining eligibility questions if it defines a broader functional category. The immigration judge's lack-of-justification finding cannot itself create statutory coverage. Conversely, denial of coverage would not vindicate the agency's position in the asylum case. A judicial-remand fee claim requires its own predicate and cannot be assumed here.

# Union Bank v. Wolas

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Union Bank v. Wolas
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 151 (1991); No. 90-1491.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the ordinary-course bankruptcy preference exception.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 11, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Can payments on long-term debt qualify for the ordinary-course exception in 11 U.S.C. §547(c)(2)?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, 921 F.2d 968 (1990), reversing judgment for the Bank because it considered long-term debts categorically excluded.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse that categorical interpretation and restore consideration of the statutory exception.
* **Material Record:** ZZZZ Best borrowed $7 million from Union Bank in December 1986 and filed Chapter 7 on July 8, 1987. Within the preceding 90 days it paid approximately $100,000 in interest and a roughly $2,500 commitment fee. Trustee Wolas sought avoidance. The bankruptcy court found the debt incurred and payments made in the ordinary course of both parties' affairs and according to ordinary business terms, granting summary judgment for the Bank; the district court affirmed. The Ninth Circuit followed its intervening decision in *In re CHG International*, 897 F.2d 1479 (1990), excluding long-term creditors.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The §547(b) preference elements are undisputed here. Whether the particular transactions satisfy all ordinary-course elements remains distinct from categorical eligibility and open for appellate review.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation recorded; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 11, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated holding supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *MCorp* involves bankruptcy and administrative jurisdiction, but does not supply a preference rule. *Ardestani*, if effective, could bear on a claimed general textual approach, but its fee-waiver boundary is not substantive bankruptcy law.

### Issue 1: Eligibility and proof under the preference exception

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Section 547(b) requires a transfer benefiting a creditor, on antecedent debt, during insolvency, within the relevant preference period, improving the creditor's recovery compared with Chapter 7 distribution absent the transfer. Section 547(f) supplies a 90-day insolvency presumption. Under §547(g), the trustee bears the subsection (b) burden and the creditor the subsection (c) exception burden.
* **Other Governing Law:** The then-current §547(c)(2) requires all three: debt incurred in the ordinary course of debtor and transferee; payment made in their ordinary course; and ordinary business terms. Congress deleted the previous 45-day debt-age limit in 1984 without replacing it. 
* **Material Other Authority:** *United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises*, 489 U.S. 235 (1989), gives effect to the Bankruptcy Code's operative text when its application is not demonstrably at odds with congressional intent. The Ninth Circuit's *CHG* and Sixth Circuit's contrary *In re Finn*, 909 F.2d 903 (1990), frame a circuit conflict, not controlling Supreme Court law.
* **Open Point:** Whether historical current-expense practice or preference policy justifies adding a debt-maturity exclusion absent from the amended text. This is legal interpretation; no special evidentiary quantum attaches to textual arguments.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Bank relies on the absence of a maturity distinction and deletion of the 45-day requirement. Wolas argues that the exception derives from a current-expense tradition favoring trade creditors, that the amendment targeted short-term commercial-paper problems, and that protecting long-term lenders undermines equal distribution. The Bank answers that Congress enacted both a broad preference rule and distinct exceptions, and ordinary payments may discourage destructive collection races. Neither side's policy description proves that these particular payments were ordinary.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the categorical exclusion and remand for review of the remaining statutory elements, or affirm if the text is lawfully construed to preserve a maturity limitation. The Court should not equate eligibility with satisfaction of the exception. No present question requires rewriting other preference defenses or making a finding about the debtor's entire business model.
