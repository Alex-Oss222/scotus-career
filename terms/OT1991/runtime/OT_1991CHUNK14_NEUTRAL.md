# International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 672 (1992); No. 91-155.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the solicitation portion of a First Amendment judgment; the cross-case concerning literature is separately listed.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 26, 1992, historical date used for sequence.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: what forum standards govern publicly accessible airport terminals, and may the Port Authority prohibit continuous or repetitive solicitation and receipt of funds there?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, 925 F.2d 576 (1991), reversing the district court as to solicitation while affirming its invalidation of the literature-distribution prohibition.
* **Relief Requested:** Invalidate the solicitation restriction and restore corresponding declaratory and injunctive relief.
* **Material Record:** ISKCON members perform sankirtan by disseminating religious literature and soliciting donations. The Port Authority controls public portions of Kennedy, La Guardia and Newark airport terminals, which include transportation facilities and shops. Its regulation prohibits specified activities directed to passersby continuously or repetitively, including the solicitation and receipt of funds. It permits solicitation and distribution on outside sidewalks. The remaining defendant enforces the regulation; private airline defendants and their leased areas are no longer in this controversy. The district court considered the terminals traditional public fora; the appellate court considered them nonpublic fora and distinguished solicitation from leafletting.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Protected expressive solicitation and enforcement against petitioners are established features of the case. The pending controversy concerns the Port Authority's controlled areas, not every airport or every privately leased space. The construction of solicitation and receipt as an immediate transaction is material to a narrower regulatory defense.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 26, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No supplied effective simulated decision settles airport forum classification.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** If effective, Forsyth County may bear on content neutrality and burdens on protected speech, but its fee setting issue does not itself classify terminals. The separately listed Lee cross-case must not be presumed decided or incorporated into entering law merely because it shares this date.

### Issue 1: Forum classification and solicitation regulation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied beyond the historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Perry Education Association (1983) distinguishes traditional public fora, intentionally designated fora and nonpublic property. In a public forum, content-based restrictions ordinarily require a compelling justification and narrow tailoring; content-neutral time, place and manner restrictions must serve a significant interest, be narrowly tailored and leave ample alternative channels. Ward (1989) does not require the least restrictive conceivable means but forbids substantially excessive speech burdens. In nonpublic fora, restrictions must be reasonable in light of the property's purpose and viewpoint neutral. Cornelius (1985) examines policy, practice, nature and use to determine intentional designation; admission of the general public alone does not establish designation.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment protects charitable and religious solicitation; it is not reduced to commercial speech because money changes hands. Heffron (1981) permits appropriate evenhanded location rules at a crowded fair. Board of Airport Commissioners v. Jews for Jesus (1987) invalidates an airport's sweeping ban on First Amendment activity while expressly leaving its forum status undecided.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Kokinda (1990) has a plurality forum analysis of a postal sidewalk and a separate concurrence sustaining the rule under time, place and manner analysis. It does not supply a majority holding that every publicly accessible government transportation facility is nonpublic. Government bears the required justification under the applicable speech standard; no fixed evidentiary quantum replaces tailoring or reasonableness. Legal classification and constitutional fit receive independent review.
* **Open Point:** Airport classification and whether this particular restriction can satisfy the more protective public-forum standard on an immediate-exchange construction.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

ISKCON emphasizes the terminals' extensive public access, commercial and social activities, and practical importance for reaching travelers. It argues that congestion and fraud can be controlled through narrower rules and that sidewalks do not reach the same audience. The Port Authority emphasizes transportation operations, vulnerable hurried travelers, obstruction during financial exchanges and enforcement difficulty when many groups solicit. It defends nonpublic status and reasonableness. A narrower defense distinguishes immediate receipt of funds from a request to contribute later: it addresses interruption and pressure associated with a transaction rather than forbidding advocacy or donation appeals in distributed material. That construction and the independent literature restriction require separate attention; the latter cannot be silently validated in this case.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may classify the terminals and test the restriction under the resulting standard. It may instead sustain this provision under public-forum time, place and manner standards, expressly assuming the more protective classification and limiting its construction. If the construction cannot fairly be drawn from the regulation, evaluate the broader ban actually enforced rather than invent a saving limitation. Invalidation of solicitation would require corresponding relief without resolving all literature restrictions or airport access rules.

Entering sources: [Perry, 460 U.S. 37](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/460/37/), [Cornelius, 473 U.S. 788](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/473/788/), [Ward, 491 U.S. 781](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/491/781/), [Heffron, 452 U.S. 640](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/452/640/), [Kokinda, 497 U.S. 720](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/720/), and [Jews for Jesus, 482 U.S. 569](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/482/569/).

# United States v. Fordice

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Fordice
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 717 (1992); No. 90-1205, together with Ayers v. Fordice, No. 90-6588.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the governing standard for dismantling a State's formerly compulsory dual university system.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 26, 1992, historical date used for sequence.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: does adoption of facially race-neutral, good-faith policies discharge Mississippi's affirmative desegregation obligation, or must the State also eliminate continuing segregative effects of policies inherited from its dual system where practicable and educationally sound?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit en banc, 914 F.2d 676 (1990), affirming the district court's judgment for state officials, 674 F. Supp. 1523 (N.D. Miss. 1987).
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse the governing legal standard and require further proceedings to dismantle unlawful remnants; private plaintiffs also seek remedies benefiting historically black institutions.
* **Material Record:** Mississippi established five universities for white students and three for black students. Formal exclusion ended, but institutions remained strongly racially identifiable. A lengthy trial addressed admissions, missions, program duplication, funding, facilities, faculty, governance and other system features. Admissions standards differ among institutions and rely on ACT scores. The district court identified substantial unnecessary program duplication. The State's 1981 mission assignments largely reflected inherited institutional resources and roles, assigning the most expansive functions to three historically white universities. The State continued to operate all eight institutions. The courts below emphasized voluntary student choice and current good faith, finding compliance without ordering further system change.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The historic state-imposed dual system is established, not inferred from current racial imbalance. The United States intervened under Equal Protection and Title VI. The dispute concerns discharge of an existing remedial duty; not every constitutional provision initially pleaded requires an independent new ruling. No particular campus closure or funding equalization is already proved the required remedy.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 26, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated desegregation holding supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Freeman v. Pitts may affect causal attribution and release from desegregation supervision; Rufo may affect modification of decrees if relevant on remand. Neither its historical holding nor a proposed Stone position may be treated as effective law here without the simulated result.

### Issue 1: The affirmative duty in higher education

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied modification of Brown and its pre-term remedial progeny.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Brown and Brown II require dismantling state-imposed educational segregation. Green (1968) requires effective removal of a dual system, not merely a formally neutral freedom-of-choice plan when it leaves the state-created structure operative. Swann (1971) makes the nature and extent of the violation determine equitable reach. Dowell (1991) asks whether good faith and elimination of vestiges to the extent practicable justify ending supervision. The State bears the burden of demonstrating discharge of its established affirmative obligation; no special numerical proof quantum is prescribed for these equitable findings.
* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; Title VI, 42 U.S.C. §2000d, forbidding race discrimination in federally assisted programs. In this dispute the Title VI claim tracks the duty to dismantle the established intentionally segregated system; no general private disparate-impact cause need be invented.
* **Material Other Authority:** Bazemore v. Friday (1986) addresses voluntary 4-H clubs after discriminatory barriers were removed; its particular treatment of private choice is invoked by the State. The summary affirmance in Alabama State Teachers Association v. Alabama Public School and College Authority, 393 U.S. 400 (1969), must be read against the questions necessarily decided, not as blanket approval of every nominally neutral university structure. The translation of desegregation principles to university admissions and institutional missions is open.
* **Open Point:** Whether good-faith race neutrality discharges the existing university-system duty when inherited policies may still shape segregative student choices.

### Issue 2: Causation, policy assessment and remedy

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied university-specific remedial formulation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Equitable relief must address state-caused constitutional injury and practical means of curing it. Current racial ratios are relevant evidence but do not establish the whole violation or mandate proportional enrollment. Good faith matters but does not automatically remove effects of an established unlawful system. Review of the legal standard is independent; historical facts receive ordinary factual deference.
* **Other Governing Law:** Brown II's remedial allocation and Swann's violation-linked limits govern. Milliken v. Bradley (1974) limits remedies extending beyond the proved violation, while its later remedial decision, 433 U.S. 267 (1977), permits measures actually directed to consequences of segregation.
* **Material Other Authority:** The trial findings supply policy-specific evidence. A finding that an institution is racially identifiable does not by itself justify closing it, moving programs, or assigning students by race. University choice and distinct educational missions affect practicality and the means of remediation without erasing the underlying duty.
* **Open Point:** How to assess traceability, combined current effects, educational justification and feasible remedies without equating racial identifiability with liability or requiring particular campus closures.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The United States and private plaintiffs argue that facial neutrality leaves an inherited structure governing student choice: admissions thresholds, limited missions and duplicative programs can maintain segregative pathways without a new express racial command. They challenge placing the burden on victims to disprove complete dismantlement. State officials respond that students voluntarily choose among open institutions, that differentiated missions serve educational needs, and that continued racial identifiability cannot alone establish unlawful discrimination. They invoke Bazemore and the distinct character of universities. Private plaintiffs' interest in strengthening historically black institutions raises a further remedy question: dismantling segregation need not destroy institutions with independent educational and cultural value, but equalization alone may not cure operative system barriers.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if current neutrality is a sufficient discharge standard in this setting and the findings support it. Vacate and remand if the standard failed to ask whether traceable policies continue segregative effects and can practicably be changed. On remand assess admissions, duplication, missions and institutional organization in combination, alongside other challenged features; do not announce that every difference or every campus violates federal law. A decree must follow findings concerning causation, educational justification and feasible alternatives. No numerical racial balance or automatic merger follows from vacatur.

## E. SOURCES

[505 U.S. 717, pp. 721-728](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/717/) recounts the extensive trial record and lower-court standard; its discussion identifies the underlying policy findings. Entering sources: [Brown II, 349 U.S. 294](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/349/294/), [Green, 391 U.S. 430](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/391/430/), [Swann, 402 U.S. 1](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/402/1/), [Dowell, 498 U.S. 237](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/498/237/), [Bazemore, 478 U.S. 385](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/478/385/), and [Milliken, 433 U.S. 267](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/433/267/).

# Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 763 (1992); No. 91-971.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review limited to the secondary-meaning requirement for inherently distinctive trade dress.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 26, 1992, historical date used for sequence.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: may inherently distinctive restaurant trade dress be protected under Lanham Act §43(a) without proof of acquired secondary meaning?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, 932 F.2d 1113 (1991), affirming the infringement judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse because the jury found no secondary meaning.
* **Material Record:** Taco Cabana began operating Mexican restaurants in 1978. Two Pesos entered in 1985 with a similar overall restaurant presentation; their markets later overlapped. The jury found that Taco Cabana had trade dress, that the overall dress was nonfunctional and inherently distinctive, that it lacked secondary meaning in the relevant Texas market, and that the alleged infringement was likely to confuse ordinary customers about source or association. The trial court awarded damages and found intentional infringement. The appellate court sustained the instructions and evidence under circuit precedent allowing inherent distinctiveness as an alternative to acquired secondary meaning.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review does not include the separately proposed challenge to nonfunctionality. The relevant conduct and suit predate the 1988 statutory revision; analyze the applicable earlier §43(a), not later additions. No patent or copyright infringement question is presented.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 26, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No supplied effective simulated trade-dress decision.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No earlier listed case directly decides this statutory issue. Corporate personhood and ownership theories from unrelated cases do not supply its liability standard.

### Issue 1: Distinctiveness under the applicable §43(a)

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied alteration of the Lanham Act baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Section 43(a), 15 U.S.C. §1125(a), reaches false designations and representations connected to goods or services; circuit law recognizes qualifying unregistered marks and trade dress. The customary trademark taxonomy distinguishes generic matter, which cannot identify protected source by exclusive ownership, descriptive matter requiring acquired source significance, and suggestive, arbitrary or fanciful matter capable of inherent distinctiveness. Secondary meaning asks whether the feature's primary significance to the public is source rather than the product itself. Likelihood of confusion remains distinct from whether a source identifier qualifies for protection.
* **Other Governing Law:** The applicable pre-1988 text of §43(a); the registration provisions of §2, including treatment of merely descriptive marks and acquired distinctiveness, are contextual statutory guidance rather than an automatic registration requirement for suit. The claimant bears the ordinary civil burden on its infringement case; no heightened proof quantum follows from the disputed legal requirement.
* **Material Other Authority:** Inwood Laboratories (1982) explains secondary meaning and functionality; functional features include those essential to use or purpose or affecting cost or quality. Abercrombie & Fitch v. Hunting World, 537 F.2d 4 (2d Cir. 1976), supplies the familiar taxonomy as circuit authority. Chevron Chemical, 659 F.2d 695 (5th Cir. 1981), permits inherently distinctive trade dress without secondary meaning; Vibrant Sales, 652 F.2d 299 (2d Cir. 1981), supplies a contrary approach. These are a conflict to resolve, not a uniform existing Supreme Court trade-dress holding.
* **Open Point:** Whether trade dress categorically requires an additional acquired-distinctiveness showing despite inherent distinctiveness. The legal instruction is independently reviewed; the accepted findings are not retried here.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Two Pesos argues that restaurant appearance requires public association through use before exclusive protection, invokes competing circuit authority and warns against monopolizing useful or ordinary designs. It also suggests limiting any initial protection without secondary meaning in time. Taco Cabana argues that the statute has no categorical trade-dress distinction, that inherently distinctive appearance can identify source from the outset, and that functionality and confusion already supply material boundaries. The Fifth Circuit regarded absence of acquired association and presence of inherent source-identifying capacity as consistent findings, not a contradictory verdict.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if inherent distinctiveness can qualify this nonfunctional restaurant dress without acquired secondary meaning. Reverse or remand if secondary meaning is a necessary additional element. Do not reopen the excluded functionality question or adopt categorical rules for every type of product design. A new time-limited immunity from proof would require a statutory basis; neither party's policy concerns alone establish it.

## E. SOURCES

[505 U.S. 763, pp. 764-767 and n.6](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/763/) supplies the special verdict, operative text, circuit conflict and limited review. [Inwood Laboratories, 456 U.S. 844, nn.10-11](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/456/844/) supports functionality and secondary meaning; the judicial reproduction at 505 U.S. 763 identifies the contemporaneous circuit authorities and statutory provisions.

# Franklin v. Massachusetts

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Franklin v. Massachusetts
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 788 (1992); No. 91-1502.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Direct merits review of a three-judge district court's census and apportionment judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 26, 1992, historical date used for sequence.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: is allocation of overseas federal personnel to States reviewable under the APA and the Constitution, and is the decision to count them using principally home-of-record data lawful?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** District of Massachusetts, 785 F. Supp. 230 (1992), holding the challenged allocation arbitrary and capricious and directing corrective census and apportionment action.
* **Relief Requested:** Federal appellants seek reversal and relief from the district court's orders. Massachusetts and its voter plaintiffs seek correction restoring a congressional seat assigned to Washington under the challenged count.
* **Material Record:** The Secretary initially planned to exclude overseas federal personnel from state totals, then decided to include them. Anticipated Defense Department survey and recent-residence data did not materialize. The Bureau principally used home of record, designated at entry into service, with other data where unavailable. That field can be stale and does not itself determine taxation. Officials considered legal residence and last duty station less suitable proxies for usual residence. The record includes assurances that personnel information would be supplemented and improved. Earlier censuses used different policies, including overseas military allocation in 1970 but not 1980. The district court accepted that inclusion could be constitutional and statutorily permissible, but found the administrative explanation insufficient after the proposed survey failed.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Article III injury and causal allocation are concrete; redressability is contested because the Secretary reports to the President, the President transmits apportionment to Congress, and the House Clerk certifies state entitlements. APA finality, agency coverage, statutory preclusion and committed discretion precede APA merits. Direct injunction against the President raises a separate institutional and remedial question.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 26, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated census or standing result supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Department of Commerce v. Montana may affect constitutional apportionment review; Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife may affect redressability and evidentiary standing requirements. Revalidate their actual effective simulated holdings, not their historical comparators. Neither can be presumed to establish a desired result.

### Issue 1: Standing, APA reviewability and relief against officials

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied alteration of pre-term standing or APA doctrine.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Allen v. Wright (1984), Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization (1976), and Duke Power (1978) require concrete injury fairly traceable to challenged action and likely redressable by the requested judicial relief. Plaintiffs carry the standing burden; likely redress does not mean absolute certainty, but a speculative chain through independent actors is insufficient. APA §704 requires final agency action without another adequate court remedy. Abbott Laboratories (1967), FTC v. Standard Oil (1980), and Port of Boston Marine Terminal (1970) examine finality pragmatically, considering definitiveness and legal impact rather than labels alone.
* **Other Governing Law:** 13 U.S.C. §141(a)-(b) assigns the census and tabulation to the Secretary; 2 U.S.C. §2a assigns the President's congressional statement and the Clerk's certificates. APA §§701(a), 701(b), 704 and 706 govern statutory review. Citizens to Preserve Overton Park (1971) treats committed discretion as a narrow exception where there is no law to apply. Statutory silence alone does not generally overcome the presumption of review.
* **Material Other Authority:** Japan Whaling (1986) treats a Secretary's certification with independent consequences as final despite later presidential action. Chicago & Southern Air Lines v. Waterman (1948) involves a materially different presidential foreign-affairs approval regime. Mississippi v. Johnson (1867) limits judicial injunctions against the President's official discretionary duties, reserving the ministerial question. Whether this census report is final and whether the President is an APA agency remain disputed applications, not already settled holdings in this matter.
* **Open Point:** Whether §2a leaves a new presidential population-policy choice or directs application of apportionment to the completed Secretary's census, and whether lawful relief against the Secretary likely redresses the lost seat.

### Issue 2: Census authority, constitutional residence and administrative explanation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied census-specific change.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The Enumeration Clause and Fourteenth Amendment require counting persons for representation in the States, with Congress directing the manner. The 1790 enumeration statute included persons occasionally absent in their usual place of residence; physical presence at one instant is not its only residence concept. The statutory assignment gives the Secretary substantial methodological discretion within the population-counting command. Under State Farm (1983), APA review asks whether the agency considered relevant data and gave a rational explanation, including for policy change; courts cannot supply an absent rationale or substitute their preferred method. Bowman Transportation (1974) permits a reasonably discernible explanation lacking ideal clarity.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article I, §2, cl.3; Fourteenth Amendment, §2; 13 U.S.C. §141; 2 U.S.C. §2a; APA §706(2)(A). Legal and constitutional interpretation receive independent judicial review. Arbitrary-and-capricious review is a review standard, not a heightened proof quantum or de novo selection of census data.
* **Material Other Authority:** Agency materials concerning the canceled survey, improved alternative data and comparative defects in available residence proxies bear directly on State Farm. Failed bills show no enacted directive; legislative interest can be part of the recorded policy context but does not amend the statute.
* **Open Point:** Whether the available residence proxies and explanation after cancellation of the survey satisfy constitutional residence and ordinary APA review.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Federal officials argue that the President's later action prevents finality of the Secretary's report, that the President is outside the APA, and that census choices are committed to discretion. They challenge direct presidential relief and the causal sufficiency of relief against the Secretary alone. On the merits they invoke enduring ties of temporarily overseas personnel and the comparative suitability of home of record. Massachusetts argues that the Secretary has completed the census determination that drives apportionment and other government uses; excluding review would insulate the operative choice. It challenges stale data and retention of an inclusion policy after a central promise of better information failed. Its constitutional argument stresses persons in each State and actual, accurate enumeration. The district court's administrative holding does not itself establish that overseas personnel can never lawfully count.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Dismiss if likely redress cannot be established through lawful relief. If standing exists, decide the available review channel before reaching its merits. An APA bar does not automatically dispose of the constitutional claim. If the Secretary's completed action is reviewable, test statutory discretion, administrative explanation and constitutional residence separately. Uphold lawful inclusion and methodology if the record supports them; remand an unexplained administrative choice rather than judicially selecting a replacement census. Presidential injunctive relief is unnecessary if no substantive violation warrants any corrective order.

## E. SOURCES

[505 U.S. 788, pp. 791-796](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/788/) supplies statutory sequence, allocation record and lower-court ruling; its reproduced agency materials include the comparative data and post-survey explanation at pp. 821-823. Entering sources: [Allen, 468 U.S. 737](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/468/737/), [Duke Power, 438 U.S. 59](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/438/59/), [Abbott Laboratories, 387 U.S. 136](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/387/136/), [Japan Whaling, 478 U.S. 221](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/478/221/), [Overton Park, 401 U.S. 402](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/401/402/), [State Farm, 463 U.S. 29](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/463/29/), [Bowman Transportation, 419 U.S. 281](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/419/281/), and [Mississippi v. Johnson, 71 U.S. 475](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/71/475/).

# Lee v. International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lee v. International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 830 (1992); No. 91-339.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review on the Port Authority's cross-case concerning literature distribution.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 26, 1992, historical date used for sequence.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: may the Port Authority prohibit continuous or repetitive distribution of written material to passersby in its publicly accessible terminal areas?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, 925 F.2d 576 (1991), affirming invalidation of the distribution ban while sustaining the separately challenged solicitation ban.
* **Relief Requested:** The Port Authority seeks reversal of the judgment protecting literature distribution.
* **Material Record:** The Port Authority controls public terminal areas at Kennedy, La Guardia and Newark, with transportation facilities, shops and restaurants. Its regulation lists sale or distribution of printed material among activities forbidden when continuous or repetitive. Sidewalk distribution outside is permitted. ISKCON's religious practice includes offering literature. The district court treated the terminals as traditional public fora and invalidated the restrictions. The appellate court treated them as nonpublic but concluded that a complete distribution prohibition was unreasonable. Congestion, discarded leaflets, maintenance costs and enforcement burdens are the Authority's asserted operational concerns.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is the distribution branch of the same litigation as No. 91-155, but a separate inventory entry and requested judgment. Whether immediate payment can be restricted is not itself a basis to prohibit free distribution. No entitlement to airline-leased private spaces is before the Court.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 26, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No supplied effective simulated airport decision.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** The separately listed ISKCON v. Lee must be checked if an effective simulated decision is supplied before this event; its mere earlier inventory ordinal and shared date do not make its proposed or historical result law. Forsyth County may bear on speech burdens if an effective holding is supplied.

### Issue 1: Distribution and the applicable forum standard

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied that alters the historical forum framework.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Perry (1983) and Cornelius (1985) distinguish traditional or designated public fora from nonpublic property. A nonpublic restriction must be viewpoint neutral and reasonable in light of the property's purpose and surrounding circumstances; ownership does not eliminate First Amendment scrutiny. A content-neutral public-forum restriction must satisfy the significant-interest, narrow-tailoring and ample-alternatives test of Ward (1989). Government must justify the restriction under the appropriate test; constitutional fit is not a heightened factual proof quantum.
* **Other Governing Law:** Distribution is protected expression. Schneider v. State (1939) rejects ordinary litter prevention as a sufficient reason to bar leafletting on public streets. Members of City Council v. Taxpayers for Vincent (1984) permits regulation of posted signs on public property in its distinct physical setting. Neither automatically settles interior-terminal regulations. Jews for Jesus (1987) leaves airport forum classification open while invalidating an indiscriminate ban on First Amendment activity.
* **Material Other Authority:** Kokinda (1990) distinguishes the operational interruption of immediate solicitation; its fragmented opinions do not establish that passive distribution entails the same burdens. The challenged rule's breadth, terminal uses and actually asserted operational demands matter even if nonpublic status is assumed.
* **Open Point:** Airport classification and the reasonableness of complete exclusion, rather than limited anti-obstruction or location rules.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Authority argues that handing out papers can slow traffic, provoke conversation, produce litter, and complicate enforcement of a solicitation rule. It stresses cumulative access by many groups. ISKCON distinguishes accepting or declining a leaflet while walking from stopping for payment, and argues that operational concerns support conduct and placement rules rather than complete exclusion. The appellate court's disposition accepts that distinction even under nonpublic-forum review. The fact that other expressive activity may be limited does not itself prove that this particular activity is incompatible with terminal use.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the complete ban fails even the nonpublic-forum standard; then definitive forum classification need not be reached. Reverse if the prohibition is reasonable in context and nonpublic status is established. If the Court classifies the terminals as public fora, apply the more protective tailoring and alternatives inquiry. Preserve the distinction between distribution and a transaction involving sale or immediate payment; do not convert relief protecting leafletting into an unlimited sales privilege.

# Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey

# SECTION I: CORRECTED NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. Case, posture, and scope

Five clinics and a physician challenge specified provisions of Pennsylvania’s Abortion Control Act, as amended in 1988 and 1989, before their enforcement.

The district court granted broader relief than the Third Circuit. The Third Circuit sustained the challenged provisions other than spousal notice. Providers seek broader relief in No. 91-744; state officials seek restoration of spousal notice in No. 91-902.

The presented questions concern the governing abortion framework, medical emergency, informed consent and delay, parental informed consent and bypass, spousal notice, and reporting. Reconsideration of Roe is presented. A general funding entitlement, constitutional fetal personhood, and the validity of every unchallenged postviability restriction are not independently presented.

The account below distinguishes statutory terms, lower-court findings, disputed conclusions, and Stone’s proposed legal determinations.

## B. Statutory provisions and complete material qualifications

### 1. Medical emergency — §3203

The statutory definition uses the physician’s good-faith clinical judgment. It covers a condition requiring immediate abortion to avert death, or a condition in which delay creates a serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function.

The Third Circuit construed the provision to prevent compliance with the Act from creating a significant threat to life or health, including the disputed conditions of preeclampsia, inevitable abortion, and prematurely ruptured membranes. That is a judicial construction, not additional statutory language.

Section 3205(b) requires the physician, before the abortion if possible, to explain the medical indications supporting the emergency judgment. It does not make prior explanation indispensable when the emergency prevents it.

### 2. Medical information, prescribed disclosures, delay, and certification — §3205

Section 3205(a)(1) requires the performing or referring physician to provide information concerning the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age, and the medical risks of childbirth.

Section 3205(a)(2) permits those physicians or designated qualified personnel to give the additional disclosures concerning available state materials, assistance benefits, and paternal support.

Complete paternal-support qualification: Section 3205(a)(2)(iii) requires information that the father must assist in supporting the child even if he offered to pay for an abortion. It then states: “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.”

Accordingly, when the pregnancy resulted from rape, the paternal-support disclosure may be omitted. “May” permits omission; it does not require omission. This provision concerns disclosure of support information. It does not eliminate a support obligation or establish the separate exception to spousal notification.

The statutory 24-hour interval applies to the required information, subject to medical emergency. Printed materials are supplied if the patient chooses to receive them; she is not required to accept or read them. Optional receipt of the printed materials is distinct from the mandatory oral disclosures.

Section 3205(a)(4) requires written certification that the required information was provided.

Section 3205(c) separately protects a physician against liability for failing to furnish subsection (a)’s information if the physician proves, by a preponderance, a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient’s physical or mental health. This disclosure defense is distinct from the emergency exception governing timely treatment.

### 3. Parental informed consent and judicial bypass — §3206

The provision generally requires the informed consent of the pregnant unemancipated minor and one parent. It also addresses guardianship for an adult adjudicated incompetent, but this brief does not supply an independently developed challenge concerning that class.

The material alternatives and qualifications are:

* Medical emergency excuses compliance with the parental-consent requirement.
* A minor may seek judicial authorization without first requesting parental consent.
* Authorization is available if she is mature, capable of informed consent, and has given that consent.
* Alternatively, authorization is available if abortion is in her best interests. Maturity and best interests are alternative grounds.
* Proceedings provide confidentiality, counsel, and expedition. The described timetable is three days at the trial level and no more than eight business days for the entire process, including appeal.
* If pregnancy resulted from incest involving the father, the statute requires only the mother’s consent.
* When both parents are deceased or unavailable within a reasonable time and manner, a guardian may consent. Where parents are divorced, the custodial parent’s consent suffices. If neither a parent nor guardian is available, an adult standing in loco parentis may consent.

The statute does not expressly require a parental clinic visit. Whether its informed-consent requirement produces that consequence is a record and application question.

### 4. Spousal notification — §3209

The provision requires a married patient’s signed certification that she notified her spouse.

The notification certification is excused if she signs a statement establishing at least one of these statutory grounds:

* Her spouse is not the father.
* Her spouse could not be located after diligent effort.
* Pregnancy resulted from spousal sexual assault reported to a law-enforcement agency with jurisdiction.
* She reasonably anticipates that notification is likely to cause bodily injury to her, inflicted by her spouse or another person.

Medical emergency separately excuses notification.

The spousal-sexual-assault route incorporates the then-applicable reporting restriction associated with §3128; the reported litigation identifies a 90-day reporting requirement. This is different from §3205(a)(2)(iii)’s permission to omit paternal-support counseling in rape cases.

The exceptions do not expressly cover every form of psychological coercion, financial retaliation, or threatened harm to another person.

### 5. Confidential procedure reports — §3214(a)

The statute requires reports concerning individual procedures without identifying the patient by name. The litigation accepts the adequacy of the statutory confidentiality safeguards; inadequate patient confidentiality must not be invented as a finding.

The disputed components include:

* Section 3214(a)(1), insofar as it identifies the referring physician.
* Section 3214(a)(8), requiring the basis for the physician’s medical-necessity judgment for an abortion performed under §3211(b)(1), concerning prevention of death or substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function.
* Section 3214(a)(10), requiring the basis for a medical-emergency determination excusing compliance with the Act.
* Section 3214(a)(11), incorporating reports of the inquiries, examinations, tests, and diagnostic basis used to determine gestational age under §3210(a).
* Section 3214(a)(12), requiring marital and spousal-notification information, including the reason notification was omitted.

Reporting the gestational-age result under §3214(a)(5) is distinct from requiring an explanation of the diagnostic basis under §3214(a)(11).

### 6. Facility reports and public disclosure — §§3207(b) and 3214(f)

Section 3207(b) requires facility and affiliation information. Its public-access condition concerns receipt of state-appropriated funds during the twelve calendar months preceding a request to inspect or copy the report.

Section 3214(f) requires quarterly procedure totals, divided by trimester. Its public-access condition concerns receipt of state-appropriated funds during the twelve calendar months preceding the report’s filing.

The challenge to these provisions distinguishes public release from collection by the responsible agency. Public disclosure of facility information is not the same as disclosure of a patient’s identity.

Statutory source: [Casey, statutory appendix, 505 U.S. 902–911](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/833/).

## C. Additional record facts material to this revision

The district court found that obtaining informed parental consent would require an in-person visit under the medical practices established in the record. That could delay care even where the parent already agreed. The appellate court did not resolve the visit premise; it regarded the bypass alternative as sufficient.

The district court also found that some physicians would stop referring patients if their identities appeared in confidential reports. That finding differs from a finding that confidentiality was inadequate or that performing physicians generally would stop practicing.

The district court found a burden associated with public disclosure and rejected specified medical-judgment reporting demands. It nevertheless sustained other confidential medical reporting.

These findings and their legal significance were disputed on appeal. They support adjudication of the particular requirements; they do not establish that every report impairs access.

Sources: [District court, 744 F. Supp. 1323, especially pp. 1382–1384 and 1390–1395](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/744/1323/1797783/); [Third Circuit, 947 F.2d 682, parental-consent and reporting discussions](https://openjurist.org/947/f2d/682/planned-parenthood-of-southeastern-pennsylvania-v-casey).

## D. Law entering the case

The governing baseline is law available immediately before June 29, 1992. No issued replacement simulated abortion decision has been supplied.

* Roe and Doe: Protected reproductive choice, the received trimester and viability framework, and the life-and-health safeguard. Health involves individualized medical judgment rather than an exclusively imminent-death standard. [Roe](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/410/113/); [Doe](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/410/179/).
* Akron and Thornburgh: Limits on compulsory persuasion, arbitrary waiting periods, unjustified restrictions on information delivery, and interference with protected medical choice. [Akron](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/462/416/); [Thornburgh](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/476/747/).
* Danforth, Bellotti, and Ashcroft: Ordinary informed consent may be lawful; a spouse cannot receive a veto; parental involvement requires appropriate safeguards, and a sufficient bypass can make a parental-consent system permissible. [Danforth](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/428/52/); [Bellotti](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/443/622/); [Ashcroft](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/462/476/).
* Webster and the 1990 minors’ cases: Their particular holdings must be respected. They do not supply a general, already adopted replacement for Roe’s framework. [Webster](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/492/490/); [Ohio v. Akron Center](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/502/); [Hodgson](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/417/).
* Reporting: Danforth permits appropriately directed, confidential health reporting. Thornburgh requires attention to actual burdens and disclosure consequences. Neither supplies automatic validity or invalidity for every demanded item.
* Facial relief and severability: The relevant abortion precedents govern the challenged categorical requirements, while relief must preserve valid, independently operative provisions where state law permits. [Brockett](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/472/491/).

The historical opinions issued in Casey may identify the record and contemporaneous arguments. Their resolution of this same case is not entering precedent.

# Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 1003 (1992); No. 91-453.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of denial of compensation for a coastal development prohibition.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 29, 1992, historical date used for sequence.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: does a regulation found to eliminate all economic use require compensation despite its harm-prevention purpose, and does a later permit procedure defeat review of the earlier deprivation?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Supreme Court of South Carolina, 304 S.C. 376, 404 S.E.2d 895 (1991), reversing a trial-court compensation award.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse the no-compensation judgment and restore or permit a constitutionally adequate compensation determination.
* **Material Record:** Lucas bought two residential lots on Isle of Palms in 1986, intending single-family homes. They were then zoned for that use and outside the coastal building-permit restriction relevant here. The 1988 Beachfront Management Act barred permanent habitable structures; certain small nonhabitable improvements remained permitted. The trial court found no reasonable economic use, describing the lots as valueless, and awarded $1,232,387.50. Lucas did not challenge the legitimacy of protecting the beaches; he claimed that a valid public measure could still require compensation. The state supreme court relied on the legislature's unchallenged harm-prevention findings to deny compensation regardless of the economic effect. The Council stipulated that a building application under the 1988 prohibition would not have succeeded.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** A 1990 amendment created a special-permit possibility after argument below and before the state supreme court's judgment. Lucas had not pursued that new route. The judgment nevertheless disposed of the merits broadly, including the pre-amendment loss. The completeness of economic deprivation is a trial finding, not a Supreme Court appraisal of possible recreational value. No independent finding that Lucas's proposed homes constituted a nuisance is supplied.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 29, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No supplied effective simulated total-deprivation rule.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Yee concerns a physical-taking characterization and the limited scope of a granted question; an effective simulated holding may inform separation of physical and regulatory claims but does not automatically decide total economic loss. Lujan may matter to standing analysis if its actual simulated rule is supplied. No historical same-matter categorical rule belongs in this baseline.

### Issue 1: Finality, the amendment and the period of claimed taking

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied alteration of the pre-term ripeness or compensation cases.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Williamson County (1985) and MacDonald, Sommer & Frates (1986) ordinarily require a final governmental position defining permitted use before adjudicating a regulatory taking; an owner cannot claim total deprivation without knowing what development is allowed. First English (1987) recognizes compensation for a temporary period once a compensable taking is established, even if government later withdraws the restriction. It does not make every permitting delay a taking.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth Amendment Just Compensation Clause through the Fourteenth Amendment; state compensation proceedings have been pursued to judgment. The 1988 ban and the 1990 special-permit amendment govern different periods.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Council's stipulation removes uncertainty about permission under the original ban. The later route may affect prospective relief without necessarily eliminating the already adjudicated pre-amendment claim. Article III injury from actual loss is distinct from finality concerning the extent of future permission.
* **Open Point:** Whether the new procedure requires dismissal or postponement of the entire case despite the final merits judgment disposing of the earlier deprivation.

### Issue 2: Total deprivation, harm prevention and background ownership limits

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied replacement of received regulatory-takings doctrine.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Penn Central (1978) ordinarily evaluates economic impact, interference with distinct investment-backed expectations and the character of government action, considering the property as a whole. Agins (1980) describes denial of economically viable use as a taking ground; its precise categorical consequences and exceptions require resolution here. Loretto (1982) establishes a physical-occupation category, which is distinct from this restriction. Pennsylvania Coal (1922) recognizes that regulation can go too far even when a public purpose exists. Owner proof of compensable deprivation is required; calling a restriction severe does not establish total loss.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Takings Clause requires just compensation rather than invalidating every lawful public-purpose restriction. Mugler (1887), Hadacheck (1915), Miller v. Schoene (1928) and Keystone Bituminous Coal (1987) sustain specified harm-prevention restrictions without compensation. The scope of ownership under state property and nuisance law matters, but no already-settled Supreme Court formulation limits every total-deprivation exception to background principles in precisely the manner now proposed by Lucas.
* **Material Other Authority:** Agins also contains a substantially-advances formulation in the historical baseline; this case does not challenge the Act's legitimate protective purpose, and no later doctrinal deletion is imported. First English concerns remedy after liability. The two-lot record supplies the property whose asserted total loss is reviewed; neither a broader parcel aggregation rule nor segmentation of selected development sticks should be invented.
* **Open Point:** Whether complete economic deprivation warrants a categorical compensation rule, what preexisting ownership limits defeat the claim, and who must identify those limits once total loss is shown.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Lucas argues that the State may protect its beaches but must spread the cost of wholly extinguishing economically beneficial private use. His acceptance of public purpose is not acceptance that the property was valueless or legally unusable before regulation. The Council invokes the harm-prevention cases, public coastal vulnerability and legislative findings. It also argues that the 1990 procedure prevents a final permanent-taking adjudication. The state supreme court treated the unchallenged protective purpose as dispositive, whereas its dissent distinguished this preservation measure from traditional nuisance abatement. The parties' disagreement does not itself prove whether background state law already prohibited all productive use.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Address the pre-amendment claim notwithstanding unresolved future permission if the original prohibition was final. Affirm the harm-prevention approach; apply ordinary Penn Central analysis; or adopt a narrowly defined total-deprivation rule and remand for its exception and compensation questions. A finding of public benefit cannot simply decide both regulatory validity and compensation. Conversely, categorical compensation cannot assume that ownership included a use already forbidden by background law. Remand may need to address duration, actual retained use, state-law restrictions and valuation rather than mechanically restoring a permanent fee-value award after the amendment.

# Espinosa v. Florida

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Espinosa v. Florida
* **Citation or Docket:** 505 U.S. 1079 (1992); No. 91-7390.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of a preserved capital-sentencing instruction issue; the inventory authorizes hearing the matter and is not a certiorari vote.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 29, 1992, historical date used for sequence.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: does an inadequately narrowed aggravating-factor instruction to Florida's sentencing jury violate the Eighth Amendment even when the judge independently applies valid aggravators, given the legal weight owed the jury's recommendation?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Supreme Court of Florida, 589 So.2d 887 (1991), affirming conviction and death sentence and rejecting the instruction challenge by reference to Smalley v. State, 546 So.2d 720 (Fla. 1989).
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse the rejection of the sentencing challenge and require constitutionally valid sentencing proceedings or review.
* **Material Record:** After conviction of first-degree murder, the jury received an instruction permitting an aggravator if the murder was especially wicked, evil, atrocious or cruel. It recommended death without separate findings of aggravation or mitigation. The trial judge independently found four aggravating and two mitigating circumstances and imposed death. The judge did not directly rely on an invalid aggravator. Florida law nevertheless requires great weight for the jury's recommendation, including a death recommendation.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenged instruction was presented to the state supreme court and rejected on its merits. The question concerns sentencing, not guilt, and direct review rather than a successive federal habeas gateway. The State's defense concerns the jury's role; it does not identify a meaningful narrowing in the words actually given.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no nonparticipation identified.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before June 29, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated capital-weighting decision supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Stringer v. Black and Sochor v. Florida may directly affect invalid-aggravator analysis, weighing and appellate cure; their actual effective simulated holdings must be incorporated if supplied. Sawyer concerns a collateral procedural gateway, not an additional obstacle to this preserved direct-review claim. No proposed brief is itself precedent.

### Issue 1: Vague aggravation in a divided sentencing process

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied change to the pre-term narrowing and weighting cases.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Godfrey (1980), Maynard v. Cartwright (1988) and Shell v. Mississippi (1990) require constitutionally sufficient guidance for a claimed aggravating category; broad evaluative terms applying to virtually any murder do not supply it without adequate narrowing. Clemons (1990) addresses invalid aggravation in a weighing system: the error cannot be ignored merely because some other valid aggravator exists. State appellate reweighing or constitutionally adequate harmless-error review may cure it where legally and factually available. Parker v. Dugger (1991) requires review grounded in an accurate account of the sentencing factors.
* **Other Governing Law:** Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments; Fla. Stat. §921.141 (1991). Florida places weighing tasks in a recommending jury and in the judge, whose written findings determine the final sentence. Tedder (1975), Smith v. State, 515 So.2d 182 (Fla. 1987), and Grossman v. State, 525 So.2d 833 (Fla. 1988), require great weight for jury recommendations in their respective settings.
* **Material Other Authority:** Walton v. Arizona (1990) permits judicial capital sentencing and appropriate judicial application of narrowing law; it does not automatically settle whether a separate jury's legally influential but inadequately guided recommendation affects the final weighing. Baldwin v. Alabama (1985) considers the actual role of a jury verdict in judicial sentencing. Chapman (1967) places on the State the burden to establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt when constitutional harmless-error review is used. That remedial quantum is distinct from the legal vagueness inquiry or proof of aggravating facts.
* **Open Point:** Whether a legally weighted jury recommendation can carry invalid aggravation into an otherwise properly instructed judge's determination, and what cure must account for that influence.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Espinosa argues that the instruction leaves the jury unguided and that the judge's obligation to respect its recommendation makes the defect consequential. Florida responds that the jury is advisory, the judge independently finds and weighs aggravators, and its judicial narrowing construction supplies adequate final sentencing guidance. The state supreme court relied on that distinction. The strongest State position is actual independence of the final determination, not the proposition that some valid aggravators categorically excuse an erroneous weighing process. The jury returned no factor-specific verdict, so a court cannot invent a finding that it declined to use the challenged instruction.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the judge's legally independent process demonstrably removes the jury defect under the governing framework. Reverse or vacate the sentencing affirmance if the required deference imports the risk of invalid weighting. Remand for lawful appellate reweighing or harmless-error review that addresses the jury's influence, or for a new sentencing proceeding if a valid cure cannot be made. No automatic reversal of the murder conviction or wholesale invalidation of judicial capital sentencing follows.

### Issue 1: Extraordinary appellate intervention and exhaustion

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied alteration of the pre-term emergency standard.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Coleman v. PACCAR (1976) and Western Airlines (1987) address interference with an appellate stay: serious irreparable injury in a matter within the prospective appellate channel and likely to receive review, together with a demonstrably erroneous application of accepted stay standards. Ordinary interim equity considers merits likelihood, irreparable harm, harm to others and public interest. The applicant bears the showing. Johnson v. Kay, 860 F.2d 529, 540 (2d Cir. 1988), requires substantial likelihood of success for preliminary relief that effectively supplies the final individual relief sought. These are provisional remedial standards, not fact-proof quanta for final statutory liability.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fed. R. Civ. P. 65; 28 U.S.C. §1651; APA review and the prospective appellate channel. The user supplies full-Court referral and decisionmaking. The agency has a detention-objection procedure under 21 C.F.R. §1.94.

The district court relied on the same-term historical McCarthy decision; its result is only a dependency here. Irremediable expiry of meaningful relief bears on nonstatutory exhaustion, but does not create substantive approval authority.
* **Open Point:** Whether bypass of the available agency process is justified in this time-sensitive individual case, and whether restoring the return order satisfies the higher appellate-intervention threshold.

### Issue 2: APA and the agency's own procedural rules

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied change to binding-agency-procedure doctrine.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** APA §553 ordinarily requires notice and comment for substantive rules, while exempting interpretive rules, general policy statements and agency organization, procedure or practice. Characterization depends on operation and binding effect, not the document's title. An agency may bind itself to additional procedures through valid regulations. Accardi (1954), Vitarelli (1959) and United States v. Nixon (1974) require adherence to legally binding agency rules while they remain in force. Vermont Yankee (1978) forbids courts from inventing additional procedures; it does not authorize disregard of procedures the agency lawfully imposed on itself.
* **Other Governing Law:** The version of 21 C.F.R. §10.40(d) in force when the 1989 Alert issued required notice and comment for interpretive rules and agency-practice rules too. Its policy exception concerned informational notices published in the Federal Register; §10.40(e) supplied specified exceptions, including an explained good-cause route. No such invocation or qualifying publication was shown below. The 1991 narrowing to APA-required procedures does not retroactively answer how the Alert was adopted. Section 10.70 separately required files supporting significant decisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Perales v. Sullivan, 948 F.2d 1348 (2d Cir. 1991), and Bellarno International v. FDA, 678 F. Supp. 410 (E.D.N.Y. 1988), analyze substantive effect and binding detention instructions. They are lower-court authority. Heckler v. Chaney (1985) concerns presumptively unreviewable nonenforcement discretion, not automatic immunity from a claim that a binding procedural regulation was disobeyed. The district court's separate arbitrary-and-capricious/documentation ground is part of its reasoning, not an independently established Supreme Court application issue.
* **Open Point:** Whether the Alert is a substantive rule or otherwise covered by the agency's then-binding procedural regulation, and what procedural invalidation would restore.

### Issue 3: Statutory admission and the remedy for a defective Alert

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied rule equates procedural invalidity with drug approval.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act imposes approval and import restrictions on unapproved new drugs. United States v. Rutherford (1979) rejects an implied general exemption from the approval regime for terminally ill patients. A discretionary enforcement policy does not itself approve a drug or command admission in every qualifying individual case. APA §706 permits setting aside unlawful action and compelling action unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed; relief must respect the scope of the agency's lawful discretion.
* **Other Governing Law:** 21 U.S.C. §§355 and 381(a); RPM 9-71-30(C). The personal-importation policy says personnel should consider a more permissive approach in specified situations; it does not require admission. The district court expressly recognized this limit.
* **Material Other Authority:** Ford Motor Co. v. NLRB, 305 U.S. 364, 373 (1939), recognizes equitable adjustment of reviewing-court relief. The district court invoked that authority, §706(1) and contemporaneous circuit cases to order immediate return because delay could defeat relief. Whether those powers permit selecting the discretionary outcome, rather than requiring prompt lawful agency consideration, is a separate issue from defective rulemaking. An individual suitability finding does not constitute regulatory approval for general distribution.
* **Open Point:** Whether the procedural claim establishes substantial likelihood of entitlement to this immediate return remedy, rather than entitlement to lawful individualized consideration.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Applicants argue that the Alert transformed a case-by-case policy into a binding prohibition, bypassed both statutory and agency-created procedures, and now makes effective relief impossible without immediate return. They invoke the uncontroverted individual medical findings and the district court's limited, supervised order. Officials stress that the drug is unapproved, the manual created no right to import it, and the Alert therefore created no new private duty requiring legislative rulemaking. They defend enforcement discretion and challenge the legal basis for judicially ordering admission. The strongest applicants' procedural response is the independently broader 1989 FDA rule, not merely calling every enforcement policy substantive. The strongest remedial response for officials is that even an invalid categorical Alert leaves individualized agency discretion. Neither side's position warrants inventing an unacceptable individual health risk absent from the record.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Grant if the applicants satisfy extraordinary appellate intervention and establish sufficient likelihood of entitlement to the limited return order. Deny if the appellate stay is not a demonstrable misuse of discretion, including because the requested remedy does not follow from the likely procedural violation. Prompt agency consideration or relief compelling an unduly delayed decision may remain available through the proper channel; that is distinct from restoring an mandatory agency-admission outcome. Resolve only the presented statutory and regulatory grounds; the constitutional method-of-abortion claim remains reserved. Any order is a full-Court order reciting Thomas's referral, with no invented votes of other Justices.
