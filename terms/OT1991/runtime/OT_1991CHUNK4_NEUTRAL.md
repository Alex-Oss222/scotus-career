# INS v. Doherty

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** INS v. Doherty
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 314 (1992); No. 90-925.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of the refusal to reopen deportation proceedings, not adjudication of asylum entitlement in the first instance.
* **Simulated Event Date:** January 15, 1992, historical decision date used for scheduling, not a grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: whether the Attorney General lawfully denied reopening of the previously withdrawn asylum and withholding claims. Exact petition wording has not been independently verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, 908 F.2d 1108, sustaining rejection of Ireland as the designated destination but setting aside the denial of reopening.
* **Relief Requested:** INS seeks reversal and restoration of the denial; Doherty seeks an opportunity to litigate protection against return to the United Kingdom.
* **Material Record:** Doherty, a citizen of Ireland and the United Kingdom, entered the United States unlawfully after escaping detention in Northern Ireland, where he was convicted in absentia of murder and related offenses. A United States extradition proceeding found the treaty's political-offense exception applicable. In 1986 he conceded deportability, selected Ireland, and withdrew asylum and withholding claims. INS opposed that destination. Ireland subsequently implemented its 1987 extradition legislation. The Attorney General rejected the destination and ordered removal to the United Kingdom. The BIA granted reopening; the Attorney General reversed on new-evidence, withdrawal, and substantive ineligibility/discretionary grounds.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Destination selection, extraditability, asylum discretion, and mandatory withholding are distinct. The extradition ruling does not itself decide the INA's serious-nonpolitical-crime exclusion. Protection eligibility has not been established by an operative merits hearing. The present review does not reopen the separate detention litigation.
* **Relevant Dates:** Withdrawal, September 12, 1986; Irish legislation implemented December 1987; destination changed in 1988.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Prior extradition and detention proceedings provide posture only.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate in the historical matter; preserve that nonparticipation absent a supplied simulation change. Stone occupies Rehnquist's seat.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before January 15, 1992, using eligible historical law and only actually effective simulated decisions.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated dispositions have been supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Ardestani and INS v. National Center for Immigrants' Rights may affect any broader simulated ruling concerning immigration procedure or regulatory construction. Their drafted positions and historical outcomes cannot determine this case. The index also fixes same-date sequence; no actual effective disposition is supplied from which to derive an additional rule.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Former 8 U.S.C. §1105a supplies record-based appellate review of deportation orders; the applicable regulatory reopening regime precedes later statutory reopening amendments.

### Issue 1: Reopening authority and its relationship to substantive relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** None supplied on reopening; the following historical rules supply the baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Former 8 C.F.R. §3.2 requires material evidence unavailable and not discoverable or presentable at the earlier hearing. Former §208.11 requires a reasonable explanation for not claiming asylum earlier. The applicant carries the reopening showing. Under INS v. Abudu, 485 U.S. 94 (March 1, 1988), denial may rest on failure of prima facie eligibility, failure of the new-evidence requirement or applicable explanation requirement, or, for discretionary relief, denial in the exercise of substantive discretion. Abudu applies abuse-of-discretion review to the latter procedural and discretionary grounds; it expressly did not settle the review standard for every prima facie determination.
* **Material Other Authority:** INS v. Rios-Pineda, 471 U.S. 444 (May 13, 1985), recognizes substantial reopening discretion. That discretion does not authorize applying the wrong statute or regulatory predicate. Asylum under §1158 is discretionary after refugee eligibility; §1253(h) requires withholding where its protection criteria are established and no enumerated exclusion applies. INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca, 480 U.S. 421 (March 9, 1987), distinguishes asylum's well-founded fear from withholding's more-likely-than-not protection threshold. Section 1253(h)(2)(C) separately excludes a person where serious reasons support commission of a serious nonpolitical crime abroad.
* **Open Point:** Whether withdrawal and foreseeable destination risk justify refusing reopening of both forms of relief; whether the Attorney General's substantive exclusion ground independently survives review. Reopening does not itself establish the withholding merits or shift their burden.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Second Circuit treated the destination change and Irish legislation as material developments and concluded that the Attorney General used an unduly restrictive new-evidence rule. It read Abudu to prevent discretionary withholding denial once procedural requirements and prima facie eligibility were established, and rejected the use of foreign-policy considerations to deny asylum.

INS emphasizes finality, available alternative pleading, the express statutory power to reject a destination, and the deliberate tactical withdrawal despite its opposition. Its alternative ground is the serious-nonpolitical-crime exclusion and the Attorney General's denial of discretionary asylum. Doherty answers that a claim directed to the United Kingdom became operationally necessary only after the destination changed, and that regulatory discretion cannot turn Congress's mandatory nonreturn protection into diplomatic discretion. His strongest limitation is that reopening still requires a supported protection claim and remains subject to statutory exclusions. Neither the extradition judgment nor sympathy proves eligibility.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain a valid independent procedural denial; distinguish asylum from withholding; or reject the procedural grounds and remand for review of the substantive exclusion. It may uphold discretionary asylum denial without deciding that withholding is discretionary. A remand to test an exclusion does not award asylum, block all removal destinations, or decide detention legality. The record must be examined before treating the extradition and immigration political-offense inquiries as equivalent. No factual determination of ultimate persecution or statutory exclusion is supplied by this brief.

# White v. Illinois

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** White v. Illinois
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 346 (1992); No. 90-6113.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, direct review of a criminal conviction on a limited federal confrontation question.
* **Simulated Event Date:** January 15, 1992, historical date used to schedule adjudication, not certiorari.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: whether the Confrontation Clause requires production or an unavailability finding before admission of statements properly within spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment hearsay exceptions. Review is limited to the federal constitutional question; the state-law exception rulings are taken as given.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Illinois Appellate Court, Fourth District, 198 Ill. App. 3d 641, 555 N.E.2d 1241, affirming White's conviction; state supreme court review was discretionary.
* **Relief Requested:** White seeks reversal of the conviction and further proceedings; Illinois seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** A four-year-old child described an assault to her babysitter immediately afterward, to her mother approximately thirty minutes later, to an investigating officer approximately forty-five minutes afterward, and to medical personnel about four hours afterward. Those persons repeated the statements at trial. The State attempted twice to present the child, who left without testifying after apparent emotional difficulty. No unavailability finding was made or requested. The defense did not call her. The babysitter also saw White leaving the bedroom; the mother observed marks on the child's neck.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The federal objection is preserved. Correct admission under the identified state hearsay exceptions is the review premise, not a new factual finding. The scope does not present a general reform of Illinois evidence law or entitlement to conviction on hearsay alone.
* **Relevant Dates:** Trial statements precede the direct-review event; no later confrontation law is eligible.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate module.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before January 15, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Estelle v. McGuire may affect an actually adopted distinction between state evidence law and a federal constitutional claim, but does not predetermine confrontation. Griffin matters only if its actual disposition changes a relevant harmless-error principle. Same-date briefs are not effective law.

### Issue 1: Production, unavailability, and reliability

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulation rule supplied; eligible historical doctrine follows.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth Amendment, applied to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment. Ohio v. Roberts, 448 U.S. 56 (June 25, 1980), ordinarily requires prosecution proof of unavailability for absent prior-testimony witnesses, including reasonable good-faith efforts to obtain attendance. Admissibility additionally requires adequate indicia of reliability: a firmly rooted exception can supply them; otherwise particularized guarantees are necessary. United States v. Inadi, 475 U.S. 387 (March 10, 1986), rejects a universal unavailability prerequisite and permits qualifying coconspirator statements without that showing because their contemporaneous evidentiary value is not replaced by later testimony.
* **Material Other Authority:** Idaho v. Wright, 497 U.S. 805 (June 27, 1990), governs particularized guarantees for statements outside a firmly rooted exception and prevents bootstrapping reliability from corroborating evidence. Coy v. Iowa, 487 U.S. 1012 (1988), and Maryland v. Craig, 497 U.S. 836 (1990), concern face-to-face trial procedures and necessity findings for specified alternatives. They do not automatically determine the hearsay exception question. Constitutional interpretation is reviewed independently; the accepted state-law admissibility premise stays fixed.
* **Open Point:** Whether Inadi's rationale extends to these two established exceptions, or Roberts requires a preliminary unavailability showing here. Reliability, necessity, and trial confrontation are distinct questions.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The state appellate court held the statements admissible under state law and read Inadi to foreclose White's proposed across-the-board production requirement. White argues that a child whose accusation supplies the prosecution's case is a witness against him, that her emotional difficulty is not a judicial necessity finding, and that repetition by adults cannot supply cross-examination of the source. Illinois argues that spontaneous statements and treatment communications carry distinctive contemporaneous guarantees and that producing the child would not replace their evidentiary value. The United States, as amicus, urges the additional, broader theory that the Clause principally reaches formal ex parte accusation. That proposal would alter the breadth of received doctrine and is not required to uphold these admissions. White's failure to call the child does not transfer a prosecution burden if the Constitution imposes one.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may extend Inadi's no-unavailability branch while retaining Roberts reliability review, require an unavailability determination for some or all statements, or reserve a broader reconstruction of confrontation. If it finds constitutional error, Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967), places on the State the burden of proving harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt on direct review; a remand may be needed. Emotional difficulty alone supplies no appellate finding of constitutional unavailability. The granted scope forecloses relitigating whether each statement actually met Illinois's exceptions.

Wright](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/805/); [Craig](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/836/); [Chapman](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/386/18/).

# Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 367 (1992); Nos. 90-954 and 90-1004, the latter captioned Rapone v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail and included as a consolidated judgment component.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of denial of a motion to modify an institutional consent decree.
* **Simulated Event Date:** January 15, 1992, historical date used as the event schedule.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: what standard Rule 60(b)(5) requires for modification of this prospective jail decree, and whether the courts below employed the correct standard. No same-day grant is inferred.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** First Circuit, 915 F.2d 1557, affirming refusal to modify the decree.
* **Relief Requested:** Officials seek permission for double occupancy of specified cells; inmates seek enforcement of the single-cell commitment.
* **Material Record:** Litigation established unconstitutional jail conditions. A 1979 consent decree required a replacement facility with single cells for pretrial detainees. Construction delays and growth in the detainee population led officials to seek modification before completion. The trial court found population growth neither new nor unforeseen, emphasized single occupancy's centrality to the settlement, and rejected modification under both the demanding Swift standard and an asserted alternative flexible standard. It did not adjudicate that every version of double occupancy would violate the Constitution.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review concerns continuing equitable obligations, not damages or reopening the original liability judgment. A voluntary decree may require more than the constitutional minimum. Approval of a modification does not follow automatically from the proposed practice's constitutionality.
* **Relevant Dates:** The 1979 decree, subsequent construction history, and what officials anticipated when accepting or reaffirming obligations are material.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Rapone's consolidated petition is addressed here, not separately inventoried.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate historically; preserve that limitation absent supplied change. Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before January 15, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None supplied as effective simulated law.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No identified earlier listed case squarely fixes institutional decree modification. Any actual broad equitable ruling in an earlier application must be checked for relevant scope; the index fixes same-date sequence, but no earlier effective simulated disposition is supplied.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Rule 60(b)(5), in its then-operative form, permits relief when continued prospective operation is no longer equitable. The movant carries the justification for relief; legal standard selection is independently reviewed and the resulting equitable choice for abuse of discretion.

### Issue 1: Modification predicate and permissible scope

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied simulated rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** United States v. Swift & Co., 286 U.S. 106 (May 2, 1932), recognizes continuing equitable power over future conduct but demands a clear showing of grievous wrong from new and unforeseen conditions on its antitrust facts. System Federation v. Wright, 364 U.S. 642 (January 16, 1961), permits adjustment to changed governing law despite consent. United States v. United Shoe Machinery Corp., 391 U.S. 244 (May 20, 1968), explains that Swift's language must be read in its factual context and permits modification to make a decree achieve its purpose.
* **Material Other Authority:** Bell v. Wolfish, 441 U.S. 520 (May 14, 1979), evaluates pretrial conditions by whether they amount to punishment: express punitive intent or lack of a reasonable relation to a legitimate nonpunitive objective, including excessive conditions relative to that objective. Double occupancy is not unconstitutional per se. Local No. 93, Firefighters v. Cleveland, 478 U.S. 501 (July 2, 1986), recognizes consent relief exceeding what the court might order after trial within lawful subject-matter limits.
* **Open Point:** Whether Swift's demanding phrase universally controls this institutional decree; how anticipated growth, postdecree doctrine, inmates' settlement reliance and public administration affect justified modification. No special numerical population or evidentiary threshold is supplied by Rule 60.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Officials contend the expanding jail population materially altered feasibility, that Bell refutes an absolute constitutional single-cell requirement, and that prospective decrees must accommodate public administration. Inmates emphasize that officials knew population could grow, accepted single occupancy as a central bargain, and can satisfy their obligations through lawful alternatives instead of reducing protection. The courts below relied both on Swift and the importance of the undertaking; their alternative discussion raises the question whether any error in choosing the legal standard affected the denial. Bell's holding supplies a constitutional boundary, not an automatic right to rewrite an agreement.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm under Swift or conclude that the court's alternative reasoning already reflects adequate discretion. It may instead clarify a more flexible standard and remand because legal error constrained consideration of material changed conditions. Direct authorization of a double-cell plan would require findings on the plan, changed conditions, remaining obligations, and detainee treatment that an appellate court must not invent. A lawful modification must preserve constitutional compliance and fit the demonstrated reason for change; neither permanent judicial management nor termination at the bare constitutional floor necessarily follows.

# Dewsnup v. Timm

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Dewsnup v. Timm
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 410 (1992); No. 90-741.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, statutory review in a Chapter 7 adversary proceeding.
* **Simulated Event Date:** January 15, 1992, historical date used for adjudicative scheduling.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: whether 11 U.S.C. §506(d) permits a Chapter 7 debtor to void the portion of an allowed lien exceeding collateral value as determined under §506(a).
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Tenth Circuit, 908 F.2d 588, affirming dismissal with prejudice after the bankruptcy court refused lien reduction.
* **Relief Requested:** The debtor seeks reduction of the lien to the judicially determined collateral value; creditors seek preservation of the lien securing the allowed debt.
* **Material Record:** Dewsnup owed approximately $120,000 secured by real property. The bankruptcy court valued the collateral at $39,000. The underlying debt was allowed; the dispute concerns secured status for lien avoidance, not disallowance of the debt itself.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory relationship of §§502, 506(a), 506(d), and applicable Chapter 7 provisions is presented. A court must not conflate discharge of personal liability with elimination of a lien. Remaining administration or valuation questions must be resolved on the record rather than by hypothetical future appreciation.
* **Relevant Dates:** The applicable Bankruptcy Code governs the petition and lien litigation; later amendments and later lien-stripping cases do not enter.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately briefed companion.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate historically; preserve that limitation absent a supplied change. Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before January 15, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Union Bank v. Wolas could affect an actual adopted bankruptcy interpretation principle, but addresses preference avoidance rather than the meaning of §506. Same-date proposals are not law.

### Issue 1: Meaning and operation of an allowed secured claim

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated §506 doctrine supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 502 governs allowance; §506(a) divides a lien-backed allowed claim into a secured claim to the value of the creditor's interest in estate property and an unsecured remainder. Valuation accounts for its purpose and proposed disposition or use. Section 506(d) voids a lien to the extent it secures a claim that is not an allowed secured claim, subject to its express exceptions for specified disallowances and failure to file a proof of claim. Those exceptions must be addressed before relief, not supplied by a general fresh-start policy. Section 103(a) generally applies Chapter 5 in Chapter 7.
* **Material Other Authority:** Long v. Bullard, 117 U.S. 617 (1886), supplies the traditional survival of a lien through discharge. Johnson v. Home State Bank, 501 U.S. 78 (June 10, 1991), holds that a mortgage surviving personal discharge remains a claim for Chapter 13 purposes, distinguishing in personam from in rem enforcement. United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, 489 U.S. 235 (February 22, 1989), gives primacy to the Code's enacted text. These holdings do not themselves adjudicate the precise §506(d) question presented here.
* **Open Point:** Whether the same phrase in §506(d) uses §506(a)'s valuation meaning or instead independently means any claim both allowed and backed by a lien. This is independent statutory review, not scrutiny or a burden of proving constitutional injury.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The lower courts preserved the lien despite valuation below the allowed debt. Dewsnup argues that adjacent provisions deliberately use one defined secured-claim concept, and that §506(d)'s stated exceptions leave no additional general exception for Chapter 7. Creditors and the United States argue that allowance and lien security separately satisfy §506(d), that pre-Code liens passed through bankruptcy, and that a valuation cutoff would transfer future appreciation from the secured creditor to the debtor. The debtor answers that historic practice helps resolve ambiguity but cannot defeat changed enacted language. Creditors need not deny §506(a)'s bifurcation for distribution to contend that lien survival is a separate statutory function.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may read §506(a) and (d) together, reverse and remand for lien relief within the statute, or preserve the lien using the separate allowance/security reading. Any applicable express exception or estate-interest issue must be resolved before an operative avoidance order. A statutory disposition need not reach a takings claim. Future appreciation concerns may test interpretation but are not findings of future value. No new valuation date or bankruptcy administration rule is invented.

[Record and Code text, 502 U.S. 410, 411–416](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/502/410/); [Johnson](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/501/78/); [Long](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/117/617/); [Ron Pair](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/489/235/).

# Wyoming v. Oklahoma

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Wyoming v. Oklahoma
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 437 (1992); No. 112, Original.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS in the Court's original jurisdiction; exceptions to a Special Master's report and cross-motions for summary judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** January 22, 1992, inventory scheduling date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: whether Wyoming may maintain this original action against Oklahoma's requirement that coal-fired generators selling electricity in Oklahoma burn at least 10% Oklahoma coal; whether that requirement violates the Commerce Clause; and whether application to Oklahoma's own utility can be severed.
* **Question Granted:** Not a certiorari matter; the referred original-action issues govern.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** No lower-court judgment. The Special Master recommends standing, exercise of original jurisdiction, and invalidation as to private utilities, with dismissal or severance concerning the state-owned Grand River Dam Authority (GRDA).
* **Relief Requested:** Wyoming seeks declaratory and injunctive relief against the Act; Oklahoma seeks dismissal or judgment sustaining it.
* **Material Record:** The 1986 Act, Okla. Stat. tit. 45, §§939–939.1, mandates a minimum 10% Oklahoma-coal mixture calculated by energy content, with specified cost protections. Four utilities, three private and GRDA, had relied overwhelmingly on Wyoming coal. Wyoming does not sell coal; it taxes extraction. The stipulated purchases and tax record links displaced Wyoming coal to lost severance revenue. The State's legislative materials expressly promote retaining purchases and economic activity locally. The parties do not object to the Master's factual findings. A proposed stipulated decree accepting invalidity for private utilities and preserving GRDA application was submitted but not entered; the Court requested briefing and argument on constitutionality.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Injury to Wyoming's own treasury, rather than representation of coal producers alone, supplies the asserted standing. State-versus-state jurisdiction and its discretionary exercise remain contested. GRDA purchasing authority and severability are distinct questions.
* **Relevant Dates:** The 1986 enactment displaced the preceding voluntary-purchase request; post-enactment purchases and revenue evidence matter to causation.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring joint disposition.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before January 22, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decisions supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None earlier identified that determines these original-jurisdiction or discrimination questions. Later standing and Commerce Clause matters cannot be imported.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Article III, §2 and 28 U.S.C. §1251(a) establish original, exclusive jurisdiction over controversies between States. The Court considers seriousness and dignity of the sovereign claim and availability of an alternative forum. On stipulated material facts, summary judgment turns on law, not credibility weighing.

### Issue 1: State injury and original jurisdiction

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated rule supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III requires concrete injury fairly traceable to the challenged act and redressable by relief. Wyoming bears the burden of establishing its own injury; a speculative generalized decline in prosperity is different from identified lost taxes on displaced transactions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Maryland v. Louisiana, 451 U.S. 725 (1981), recognizes state fiscal injuries in a discriminatory-energy dispute. Massachusetts v. Missouri, 308 U.S. 1 (1939), and Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 (1983), inform genuine state controversies and appropriate original jurisdiction.
* **Open Point:** Whether the record establishes direct fiscal injury and a sufficiently substantial sovereign controversy despite coal producers' possible private suits.

### Issue 2: Geographic discrimination and remedial scope

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated Commerce Clause rule supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Facial economic discrimination receives the demanding discrimination inquiry of Philadelphia v. New Jersey, 437 U.S. 617 (1978), and Hughes v. Oklahoma, 441 U.S. 322 (1979): the State must show a legitimate local purpose that cannot adequately be served by reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives. Maine v. Taylor, 477 U.S. 131 (1986), illustrates that narrow exception. Pike v. Bruce Church, 397 U.S. 137 (1970), governs incidental burdens of evenhanded regulation and is a different branch, not a reduced version of the discrimination test.
* **Material Other Authority:** Reeves, Inc. v. Stake, 447 U.S. 429 (1980), protects some state market participation; South-Central Timber Development v. Wunnicke, 467 U.S. 82 (1984), limits using participation to regulate a distinct market. Congressional permission for otherwise prohibited discrimination must be unmistakably clear. Severability depends on state law and legislative design, not simply the federal court's ability to write a narrower provision.
* **Open Point:** Whether the local-source mandate is justified or congressionally authorized; whether GRDA's procurement can be detached from an act regulating all generators.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Master finds concrete tax loss and facial discrimination unsupported by an adequate justification, but proposes a different disposition for GRDA. Wyoming emphasizes that a mandatory home-state purchase displaces taxable extraction and that ordinary private litigation cannot resolve the entire sovereign controversy. Oklahoma characterizes the tax injury as derivative of private transactions and invokes the Court's restraint in original cases.

On the merits, Oklahoma invokes energy-supply diversification and the Federal Power Act's preservation of state regulation. Wyoming answers that preserving state authority is not affirmative congressional permission for geographic discrimination and that a local purchase quota protects local producers rather than ensuring neutral supply reliability. Oklahoma relies on GRDA's status as a market participant. Wyoming's severability exception concerns preserving a materially different state-only purchasing law the legislature did not separately enact. The proposed compromise decree does not itself decide these questions or eliminate the need for a lawful judgment.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Dismissal remains possible for failure of state injury or refusal to exercise original jurisdiction. If jurisdiction is exercised, the Court can uphold the law, invalidate discriminatory regulation, or preserve a genuinely severable GRDA provision if state law supports that result. A ruling about this Act need not decide the constitutionality of every separate state procurement preference. Declaratory relief and an injunction against enforcement can redress displaced purchases without awarding speculative tax damages; jurisdiction may be retained for implementation. The uncontested findings permit legal resolution without inventing additional market evidence.

Taylor](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/477/131/); [Reeves](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/447/429/).

# INS v. Elias-Zacarias

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** INS v. Elias-Zacarias
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 478 (1992); No. 90-1342.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of an immigration reopening dispute involving statutory refugee status.
* **Simulated Event Date:** January 22, 1992, inventory scheduling date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: whether attempted recruitment by guerrillas necessarily establishes persecution on account of political opinion, and whether the agency record requires that statutory conclusion.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, 921 F.2d 844, reversing the Board of Immigration Appeals' disposition and treating its refusal to reopen as an effective merits determination.
* **Relief Requested:** INS seeks reversal; Elias-Zacarias seeks preservation of relief from the agency's denial and further consideration of protection.
* **Material Record:** In January 1987, armed, masked guerrillas asked Elias-Zacarias and his parents to join them; the family refused. The recruiters said to reconsider and that they would return. Elias-Zacarias testified that joining could expose his family to government retaliation. He left Guatemala in March and was apprehended in the United States in July. After an immigration judge denied asylum and withholding, the Board dismissed an initial appeal on procedural grounds. A reopening motion presented evidence that guerrillas had returned twice seeking him. The Board found no prima facie eligibility; the Ninth Circuit used a categorical political-persecution analysis. Fear that recruiters might take or kill him is not a finding that they expressly made that threat.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Identify the actual agency ground and reopening posture. Statutory eligibility, discretionary asylum, and mandatory withholding have different functions and must not be collapsed. No criminal-offense exclusion is at issue on these facts.
* **Relevant Dates:** Recruitment and flight occurred in 1987; the further visits supply the asserted additional evidence.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** INS v. Doherty is an earlier inventory matter concerning reopening and the distinction between asylum and withholding.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before January 22, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** An effective Doherty decision must be consulted for reopening grounds and review. Its prepared Stone position is not law and cannot decide this case's political-opinion nexus.

### Issue 1: Political-opinion nexus and review

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated refugee-status rule supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Under 8 U.S.C. §§1101(a)(42)(A) and 1158(a), asylum eligibility requires refugee status: persecution or a well-founded fear of persecution on account of a listed ground, including political opinion. The applicant bears eligibility and nexus burdens. Refugee status permits, but does not compel, discretionary asylum. Section 1253(h) withholding instead requires the stricter probability showing described in INS v. Stevic, 467 U.S. 407 (1984), subject to statutory exclusions. INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca, 480 U.S. 421 (1987), rejects equating a well-founded fear with the more-likely-than-not withholding threshold.
* **Material Other Authority:** Section 1105a(a)(4) makes agency findings conclusive if supported by reasonable, substantial and probative evidence on the whole record. Judicial disagreement is insufficient to overturn a supported factual inference. INS v. Abudu, 485 U.S. 94 (1988), distinguishes failure to show prima facie eligibility, failure to offer qualifying new material evidence, and discretionary denial where discretion exists; it does not turn withholding into discretionary asylum. The regulation permits reopening for material evidence previously unavailable.
* **Open Point:** Whether refusal of recruitment is itself political opinion, whether a persecutor's political aims establish the required nexus, and what direct or circumstantial evidence supports the particular applicant's actual or attributed opinion. Credibility, feared harm, protected ground and persecutor motive remain separate inquiries; no special numerical proof quantum resolves motive.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit regarded forced recruitment by nongovernmental guerrillas as political persecution and treated refusal to fight as political neutrality. Elias-Zacarias argues that compelled participation suppresses a person's decision to remain outside the conflict, and that repeated recruitment supports his fear. INS argues that a group's political purpose does not establish that it targeted this individual because of his political opinion; a need for personnel may explain recruitment regardless of the recruit's beliefs. The applicant need not prove motive through an express confession by a recruiter. Conversely, unwillingness to fight cannot establish every statutory element without attention to the evidence. The Board's specific prima facie ruling, rather than a newly imagined credibility rejection, is the administrative decision under examination.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can sustain a record-specific inference of protected political neutrality, reject the categorical rule and remand for proper review, or uphold the agency if the evidence does not require a contrary conclusion under the applicable posture. A nexus holding against the applicant does not establish that guerrilla harm is permissible; it identifies the limits of this statutory protection. A remand does not award asylum or bypass its discretionary phase. The exact petition wording is not independently verified; the judicial record establishes the nexus and review issues. No missing-source inference is used to find the applicant incredible or the recruiters apolitical.

# Presley v. Etowah County Commission

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Presley v. Etowah County Commission
* **Canonical Matter / Alias:** Includes Mack v. Russell County Commission, the consolidated appeal.
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 491 (1992); Nos. 90-711 and 90-712.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, direct appeals concerning Voting Rights Act §5 coverage.
* **Simulated Event Date:** January 27, 1992, inventory scheduling date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: whether the challenged reallocations of authority among elected county commissioners and to an appointed engineer are changes in a standard, practice or procedure with respect to voting requiring §5 preclearance.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Three-judge Middle District of Alabama court; denial of §5 relief for Etowah County's Common Fund Resolution and Russell County's Unit System.
* **Relief Requested:** Appellants seek a declaration of §5 coverage and relief against implementation without preclearance; counties seek affirmance of the noncoverage judgments.
* **Material Record:** Etowah County expanded its commission and elected its first Black commissioner, Lawrence Presley, under a district system. Its 1987 Road Supervision Resolution retained road authority in four holdover commissioners, excluding both newly elected commissioners, one Black and one white. A separate Common Fund Resolution pooled funds previously allocated to individual districts for collective allocation. Russell County's 1979 Unit System transferred road operations from individual commissioners to an engineer appointed by the commission; the change followed corruption indictments. A 1985 electoral consent decree created seven single-member districts and was precleared, without submission of the older Unit System. Black commissioners were first elected in 1986.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The district court held Etowah's Road Supervision Resolution covered by §5; that ruling was not appealed and is not before this Court. The Common Fund and Russell Unit System rulings are the two appealed components. Coverage is distinct from whether a change has discriminatory purpose or effect; other constitutional and statutory claims remain separate.
* **Relevant Dates:** Compare post-baseline changes against the applicable §5 benchmark; do not infer that Russell adopted its 1979 system in response to the later 1986 election.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Mack is included here, not an additional inventory case.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before January 27, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decisions supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No earlier inventory ruling identified that resolves §5 governmental-power coverage. Future term voting-rights rulings cannot supply the answer.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 5, then 42 U.S.C. §1973c, requires covered jurisdictions to obtain administrative preclearance from the Attorney General or a declaratory judgment from the District Court for the District of Columbia before implementing a covered change. The local three-judge enforcement court decides whether a change is covered and unprecleared, not the ultimate preclearance merits.

### Issue 1: Changes in authority attached to elective office

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated coverage rule supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 5 reaches changed voting qualifications, prerequisites, standards, practices and procedures relative to the statutory benchmark. The covered jurisdiction bears the preclearance burden of showing absence of discriminatory purpose and effect; that burden is not proof that challengers must carry to establish coverage. Beer v. United States, 425 U.S. 130 (1976), concerns retrogressive effect at the preclearance stage.
* **Material Other Authority:** Allen v. State Board of Elections, 393 U.S. 544 (1969), reads the voting language broadly, including changes between elective and appointive office and district and at-large election. Perkins v. Matthews, 400 U.S. 379 (1971), separates coverage from discrimination merits. Dougherty County Board of Education v. White, 439 U.S. 32 (1978), addresses indirect burdens on candidacy and gives substantial weight to the Attorney General's administration of §5. None expressly decides every redistribution of powers after an election.
* **Open Point:** Whether removing or collectivizing authority formerly exercisable by an elected district representative changes the effective elective choice sufficiently to fall within §5, or instead concerns ordinary governing after voting has concluded. No equal-population formula or entitlement to proportional policy victories decides this statutory question.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court distinguished electoral changes from transfers of governing authority that leave the selection process and elected offices intact. The commissioners challenge that distinction: voters' ability to select an official can be hollowed out by moving the office's established substantive authority elsewhere. They invoke Allen's functional reading and the danger of preserving election forms while reducing newly elected representatives' practical power.

The counties argue that §5 is a voting safeguard, not federal advance review of every budget, personnel decision or reallocation of responsibilities. The Unit System predates Russell's first Black commissioners, and the Common Fund applies to the commission collectively; these facts oppose any automatic inference of racial purpose but do not by themselves answer coverage. The United States supports coverage for the challenged transfers, relying on the statute's protective purpose and administrative interpretation. A coverage ruling must specify a workable boundary; neither side can substitute a presumed racial motive or a categorical immunity for all post-election changes.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm noncoverage, find one or both changes covered, or distinguish pooling district-controlled funds from transferring operating authority to an appointed official. Coverage would require preclearance, not an immediate holding of racial discrimination or an award of a prescribed share of road spending. Any enforcement order must accommodate the posture of an already implemented change through appropriate lower-court relief. The unappealed Road Supervision ruling remains untouched. The exact petition language was not separately obtained; the consolidated record clearly identifies the two challenged rulings and excludes that third resolution.

# Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 527 (1992); No. 90-970.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, review of enforcement of an NLRB unfair-labor-practice order.
* **Simulated Event Date:** January 27, 1992, inventory scheduling date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: when §7 of the National Labor Relations Act requires an employer to admit nonemployee union organizers to private property, and whether the Board's Jean Country approach correctly governs this record.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** First Circuit, 914 F.2d 313, enforcing the Board's order requiring access to a retail parking lot.
* **Relief Requested:** Lechmere seeks reversal of enforcement; the Board seeks affirmance of its access order.
* **Material Record:** Organizers sought to reach approximately 200 retail employees through handbills on cars in a privately owned shopping-plaza lot. Lechmere enforced a longstanding nonemployee solicitation ban, including against charitable groups. Organizers then distributed material from a mostly public grassy strip adjoining the highway, advertised, picketed and obtained 41 employee names and addresses from license-plate records. Four mailings, home visits and calls produced one authorization card. The Board found access warranted using Jean Country's balancing of organizational impairment, property interests and alternative means. Employees did not reside on the employer's property.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** These are nonemployee organizers; employee solicitation and public First Amendment access are distinct. Uniform enforcement makes discriminatory permission for other nonemployee groups an unavailable factual basis here.
* **Relevant Dates:** The 1987 organizing effort preceded the Board's 1988 Jean Country formulation, which it applied in reviewing this dispute.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring consolidated judgment.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation identified; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before January 27, 1992.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decisions supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No earlier inventory decision identified as resolving nonemployee access. An actual earlier adopted general agency-review rule, if supplied, would require revalidation; draft preferences are not precedent.

### Issue 1: Nonemployee access and administrative authority

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No adopted simulated NLRA standard supplied.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted runtime standard supplied; the operative historical framework and its limits are identified below.
* **Other Governing Law:** NLRA §7, 29 U.S.C. §157, protects employees' organizational rights; §8(a)(1), §158(a)(1), prohibits employer interference. NLRB v. Babcock & Wilcox Co., 351 U.S. 105 (1956), permits exclusion of nonemployee organizers where reasonable alternative means communicate with employees and exclusion does not discriminate against union solicitation. Where employee location makes reasonable communication ineffective, accommodation can require access. The party seeking nonemployee access carries the burden of bringing the case within the exception; improved efficiency or organizational success is not the same factual question as practical inaccessibility.
* **Material Other Authority:** Republic Aviation Corp. v. NLRB, 324 U.S. 793 (1945), concerns employees' own solicitation. Central Hardware Co. v. NLRB, 407 U.S. 539 (1972), and Hudgens v. NLRB, 424 U.S. 507 (1976), require statutory rather than general First Amendment analysis. Sears, Roebuck & Co. v. Carpenters, 436 U.S. 180 (1978), describes the heavy burden supporting nonemployee access. Chevron, 467 U.S. 837 (1984), supplies the entering statutory-deference framework; Universal Camera Corp. v. NLRB, 340 U.S. 474 (1951), requires substantial-evidence review on the whole record for Board facts. Neither turns a conflicting Supreme Court statutory holding into an optional consideration.
* **Open Point:** Whether Jean Country permissibly accommodates the protected interests through general balancing or bypasses Babcock's threshold restriction, and whether the particular alternatives satisfy that restriction. This is statutory interpretation and factual review, not constitutional strict scrutiny.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Board and First Circuit regarded the retail lot's property interest as limited and alternative contacts as insufficiently effective, applying Jean Country rather than treating inaccessibility as a separate prerequisite. The Board, represented by the Solicitor General, defends its expertise in adjusting property and organizational interests and points to the practical difficulty of reaching the workforce.

Lechmere argues that Babcock directly governs nonemployee access and that the Board cannot transform preferred or more successful communication into statutory entitlement. The union's identification of only one fifth of employees supports its practical concern but also demonstrates actual offsite access. Neither the absence of employee residence onsite nor a single failed advertising effort alone proves all possible communication effective or ineffective. A union need not win employee support to establish it communicated, and the statute does not promise organizational success.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain Jean Country as a lawful application of existing accommodation doctrine, reject general balancing but remand for a proper accessibility inquiry, or reverse enforcement because the established facts do not satisfy Babcock. A ruling limited to nondiscriminatory nonemployee exclusion need not curtail employee solicitation rights. No remedy against union advocacy on public land is presented. The primary record establishes the actual contact methods, physical setting and policy enforcement; no assumptions about employees' reactions or union sincerity are needed.
