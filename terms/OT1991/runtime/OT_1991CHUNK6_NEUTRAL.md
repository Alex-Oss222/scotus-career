The court below treated association as character information within broad sentencing discretion. Dawson argues that membership alone invites punishment of ideas. Delaware responds that gang membership rebuts good character and can indicate future danger. The State’s strongest lawful theory requires a record link to conduct or danger; material available elsewhere about prison gangs does not establish the missing link in this trial.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A categorical exclusion of all associational evidence would conflict with Abel and Barclay. Conversely, admission based solely on ideological disapproval raises the direct constitutional defect. A finding of error permits vacatur limited to sentencing and remand for a supported harmless-error determination or a new penalty proceeding. The present packet makes no finding that the existing error was harmless.

* **Material Other Authority:** Under Teague, 489 U.S. 288 (1989), and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 (1990), a procedural rule not dictated by precedent when conviction became final ordinarily cannot support habeas relief; the then-recognized exceptions cover substantive rules placing conduct or punishment beyond state authority and watershed procedural protections essential to fundamental fairness and accuracy. Merely showing that a later decision is old-rule in its own setting does not answer whether the requested extension is new. Godfrey, 446 U.S. 420 (1980), required meaningful narrowing of a vague capital aggravator; its rationale originated in a plurality, with the judgment and later majority treatment supplying the relevant authority. Zant, 462 U.S. 862 (1983), sustained a nonweighing system’s sentence where an invalid aggravator did not introduce impermissible evidence or distort the selection rule; it expressly reserved weighing systems. Mississippi requires weighing statutory aggravators against mitigation. Maynard, 486 U.S. 356 (1988), applied vagueness protection to heinousness language. Clemons, 494 U.S. 738 (1990), permits a state appellate court either genuinely to reweigh valid aggravation against mitigation or to conduct constitutional harmless-error review. A bare count of remaining aggravators is neither. Chapman assigns the State harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt. No later Brecht standard governs this event.
* **Open Point:** Whether the particular Mississippi application was dictated at finality and, if so, which adequate sentencing correction remains available.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Fifth Circuit treated differences between Georgia and Mississippi as sufficient to make the requested application new. Stringer argues that an undefined adjective cannot guide capital selection and that weighting it makes Godfrey more directly applicable. Mississippi argues that narrowing accomplished elsewhere and the presence of valid aggravators made the sentence constitutionally defensible before Clemons. Its strongest finality argument is that a reserved question can admit reasonable competing readings; the response is that Zant’s reservation did not endorse uncorrected distortion in a weighing system.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may find the rule dictated by pre-finality authority, reject the categorical Teague bar, and remand for genuine constitutional review. If it finds the application new, it must examine the contemporaneous Teague exceptions rather than equate seriousness with retroactivity. On an eligible merits claim, lawful reweighing, proven harmlessness, or a new sentencing hearing are distinct corrective routes. This record does not authorize a federal appellate court to supply a missing state-law reweighing decision. The exact state judgment and instructions govern the final remedial choice.
