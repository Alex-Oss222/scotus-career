### Issue 1: May the Corps condition approval of an artificial port extension on preservation of the existing federal-state submerged-land boundary?

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require nondiscriminatory treatment under §111 and consideration of preserved retroactivity and refund issues under then-governing law; award no unsupported sum.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: May Kansas tax federal military retirement while exempting state and local retirement benefits?

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only the question and necessary antecedents identified in I.A, subject to its threshold limitations.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Davis.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Military retirement is deferred compensation for the relevant §111 comparison; retained status and recall obligations do not justify Kansas’s source-based disparity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Apply Davis. The calculation and ordinary operation of these benefits make past service the basis of payment. Continued status and possible recall do not supply a sufficient connection to taxing all military retirement while exempting comparable public retirement. A formally true distinction is not necessarily a legally relevant justification under the governing source-neutrality requirement. Stone would enforce that existing rule without inventing a heightened constitutional tier for veterans or revisiting intergovernmental immunity generally.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve remaining retrospective remedy questions and different compensation specifically tied to new service. No later Harper rule enters this event.

# United States Department of Energy v. Ohio

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The entering-law baseline in I.B, not the historical comparator.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any specifically identified earlier simulated dependency when its actual holding becomes effective. No prior approved case-specific position has been supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** In No. 90-1341, reverse the challenged waiver rulings. In No. 90-1517, sustain the rejection of waiver under RCRA’s federal-facilities provision and reject the preserved alternative CWA citizen-suit ground. Remand the consolidated action for disposition of the disputed fines consistently with those holdings.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Bar the disputed punitive fines for past violations under these provisions; preserve coercive compliance relief and other obligations within the enacted waivers. Apply the contingent stipulation only if a lawful remaining basis warrants it.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Did the Clean Water Act or RCRA waive federal immunity from punitive fines for past pollution violations?

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only the question and necessary antecedents identified in I.A, subject to its threshold limitations.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the source-specific waiver rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The invoked citizen-suit and federal-facilities provisions do not unequivocally waive the federal immunity from the requested punitive past-violation fines, while coercive compliance relief remains distinct.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Apply the source-specific waiver rule. The citizen-suit clauses authorize proceedings against federal agencies, but their cross-references do not unequivocally impose the punitive monetary liability that the separate penalty definitions assign to persons. Otherwise an express defendant authorization would silently rewrite the incorporated remedial limitation. The RCRA facilities clause concentrates on compliance and sanctions enforcing injunctions, supporting coercive remedies without clearly extending to punishment for completed conduct.

The CWA text presents the closer question because it expressly mentions civil penalties. Stone would read that language within the surrounding enforcement provision and its federal-law limitation, not as an independent all-penalties grant. Ohio’s approved state program remains state law for this liability question; federal approval does not itself make every state fine one arising under federal law. The remaining textual tension does not provide the unequivocal consent required by Mitchell. This conclusion rests on the wording Congress used at this event, not on a general view that public polluters deserve immunity. Congress can specify retrospective penalties clearly, and every clear compliance obligation remains enforceable. No broad revision of sovereign immunity is required to resolve this statutory scope question.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve later amendments, other express penalty waivers, and the merits of settled pollution allegations. Do not convert environmental seriousness into an implied cause of action or monetary waiver.

# Gomez v. United States District Court for the Northern District of California

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The entering-law baseline in I.B, not the historical comparator.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any specifically identified earlier simulated dependency when its actual holding becomes effective. No prior approved case-specific position has been supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Deny California’s application to vacate the stay.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Proposed Court order: “The application to vacate the stay presented to Justice O’Connor and by her referred to the Court is denied.” Preserve the existing stay for prompt adjudication below; do not issue a permanent injunction or adjudicate the constitutionality of cyanide gas on this application.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Should the full Court vacate the Ninth Circuit’s stay of Harris’s execution pending his cyanide-gas challenge?

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only the question and necessary antecedents identified in I.A, subject to its threshold limitations.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply interim-relief principles to the existing stay.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** On this application, the supported unresolved method claim and contested channel and default questions warrant preserving the appellate stay for prompt adjudication; no final method ruling is made.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Apply interim-relief principles to the existing stay. The identified medical submissions present a substantial question about unnecessary pain whose adjudication becomes impossible after execution. California’s timing objection deserves serious weight, including scrutiny of why a method long in use was not challenged sooner. But timing does not establish the correct litigation channel or satisfy every abuse-of-writ element by itself. The recent evidence strengthens the present need for assessment without automatically excusing prior omissions. The full-Court question is whether the appellate stay lacks substantial support or abuses equitable discretion, not whether a lone Circuit Justice should defer to colleagues. Stone would find a substantial, supported possibility of relief on the unnecessary-pain claim and no sufficiently established channel bar on this application to make the stay an abuse under Delo. This is a preliminary assessment of the actual claim and posture, not a rule that an unresolved procedural question always defeats vacatur.

California’s independent equitable-delay ground also warrants a decision. Harris had a long opportunity to challenge this established method, and four earlier federal proceedings make the failure to do so a serious reason against disrupting the execution setting. Stone would give that consideration weight independently of cause and prejudice and would not deem the recent Harding account an automatic excuse. Nevertheless, the combined medical submissions and recent concrete account substantiate a risk of severe unnecessary pain that would become irreversible at execution. On this record, preserving a focused, expedited adjudication imposes a limited postponement while permitting the State promptly to press every channel and merits defense. Stone would judge that balance sufficient to sustain the existing stay despite the delay. This conclusion depends on the supported claim and confined duration; a bare last-minute allegation or indefinite postponement would not receive the same treatment.

This is a limited assessment of the stay, not a finding that Harris will prevail. The lower court can require focused, expedited submissions on channel, default, and the evidence supporting the method claim, and can dissolve the stay if the actual governing rules foreclose relief. Irreversibility cannot produce an automatic stay in every capital case, but it explains why a supported unresolved claim and a contested procedural bar warrant completion of that adjudication before vacatur. Stone proposes no categorical abolition of capital punishment and no assumption that the recent account proves a constitutional violation. His remedial choice follows from the procedural state of this application and the evidence identified in it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve final Eighth Amendment merits, a universal alternative-method requirement, and a general rule excusing abusive delay. No present vote is a sole Circuit Justice disposition or an effective Court order.

# Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The entering-law baseline in I.B, not the historical comparator.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any specifically identified earlier simulated dependency when its actual holding becomes effective. No prior approved case-specific position has been supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the requirement of a federal evidentiary hearing under Townsend on the mens rea explanation alone.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for a hearing confined to whether the manslaughter mens rea element was adequately explained, followed by adjudication of that constitutional claim. Preserve the denial of a new hearing on translation of the plea form and plea proceeding and the supported findings underlying it. If the mens rea claim is proved, use a conditional writ permitting a lawful plea or trial process; do not order immediate unconditional release on the present allegations.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Does negligent failure to develop facts in state postconviction proceedings require cause and prejudice before a federal habeas hearing?

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only the question and necessary antecedents identified in I.A, subject to its threshold limitations.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Retain the directly applicable Townsend rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Townsend’s deliberate-bypass rule remains applicable to this inadequate-development claim; ordinary counsel negligence does not bar a hearing on the inadequately developed mens rea explanation, while the supported plea-form and plea-proceeding findings remain undisturbed.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Retain the directly applicable Townsend rule. The State correctly identifies costs to comity and factfinding when counsel leaves an incomplete record. Those costs are already relevant to deliberate bypass and to the adequacy of state fact development; they do not make a claim-default holding interchangeable with an evidentiary-hearing holding. Here the claim was brought to the state process, and the neglected facts are necessary to decide whether the plea was knowing. A federal court cannot treat the absence of adequately developed evidence as both the reason no constitutional violation can be proved and the reason no hearing is available without a legal rule authorizing that consequence. Townsend supplies the operative rule and does not make ordinary negligence sufficient.

Under Master §16, Stone would identify rather than conceal the State’s proposed change: replace Townsend’s deliberate-bypass treatment of inadequate development with cause and actual prejudice, ordinarily attributing counsel negligence to the prisoner, subject to miscarriage of justice. Sykes, Carrier, Coleman, and McCleskey establish that approach in adjacent contexts and give the proposal substantial doctrinal support. They did not themselves decide the factual-development question, however. Their source-bound holdings can coexist with a rule requiring federal facts for a claim properly submitted to state adjudication. Uniformity is an administrative virtue, but it is not by itself a demonstration that Townsend’s specific rule has become unworkable, contradicted the statute, or lost its legal foundation.

The protected interests are procedural rather than a claimed entitlement to benefit from counsel’s neglect. A prisoner depends on a lawful opportunity to establish facts necessary to the court’s custody judgment; the State depends on orderly use of the first available forum and reliable finality. Townsend accommodates both by distinguishing inadequate development from deliberate bypass and requiring a genuinely material factual dispute. The record characterizes the omission as negligence, not deliberate circumvention. Stone would not invent a deliberate-bypass finding to avoid the precedent or presume constitutional error to avoid adjudication.

Administration remains bounded: identify the pleaded constitutional claim, identify facts material to it that state proceedings did not adequately resolve, examine the recognized hearing exceptions and bypass question, then hear only what is needed. Existing supported findings keep their statutory treatment. In particular, the state court did make an affirmative finding of accurate translation; it cannot be described as having found nothing. The Ninth Circuit nevertheless identified inadequate development concerning the mens rea explanation while upholding the adequately developed findings on the plea form and proceeding. The federal hearing addresses the former identified deficiency only. It is not a de novo retrial of every aspect of the conviction and does not guarantee a writ. This preserves a workable specific rule without reopening Coleman or creating a constitutional right to state postconviction counsel.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve the truth of the mens rea-explanation allegations, ultimate validity of the plea on that branch, and different records showing deliberate bypass; preserve the separate plea-form and plea-proceeding determinations. Do not import AEDPA, later ineffective-assistance exceptions, or an automatic innocence finding.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Neitzke’s distinction and give the statutory discretion a reviewable scope.

Limiting screening to facts capable of judicial notice is too narrow to reach genuinely irrational allegations. Unbounded disbelief would be too broad: an extraordinary allegation can be true, especially where evidence is controlled by custodians. Stone would adopt abuse-of-discretion review of the screening judgment while preserving independent review of its legal boundary. This interpretation supplies the unresolved review rule openly; it does not raise a prisoner’s proof burden at pleading or demand corroboration absent a source. Here corroborative material was offered: the reviewing court must account for Esquer’s affidavit and the proposed Pierce submission, without deciding their truth merely from the broader pattern of allegations. The magistrate’s concession that the individual complaints were not necessarily frivolous also bears on whether their aggregation lawfully justifies dismissing each of the three appealed actions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve merits, damages, and the truth of each event. No presumption of institutional honesty or prisoner dishonesty applies.

# United States v. Williams

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The entering-law baseline in I.B, not the historical comparator.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any specifically identified earlier simulated dependency when its actual holding becomes effective. No prior approved case-specific position has been supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal without prejudice under a confined supervisory obligation to disclose known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the Government to seek a fresh indictment after a presentation complying with that limited obligation. Do not dismiss with prejudice, direct acquittal, or require presentation of every potential defense.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: May a federal indictment be dismissed for failure to present substantial exculpatory evidence to the grand jury?

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only the question and necessary antecedents identified in I.A, subject to its threshold limitations.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reach the issue because the appellate court passed upon the rule and the Government’s adherence to binding circuit precedent was not a merits concession.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Limited supervisory authority supports disclosure of known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense where omission materially distorts the presentation; supported Bank of Nova Scotia prejudice warrants dismissal without prejudice here.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reach the issue because the appellate court passed upon the rule and the Government’s adherence to binding circuit precedent was not a merits concession. Stone would retain the circuit’s narrow protective rule, while explicitly recognizing that this Court has not previously imposed it. The proposed source is the limited supervisory authority over federal criminal administration recognized, but constrained, in Bank of Nova Scotia and Hasting. It is not Brady transplanted wholesale into a secret accusatory proceeding, nor a new constitutional right to a preliminary adversarial trial.

The operative duty should cover known evidence that substantially undermines an essential element, where omission would materially distort the prosecution’s presentation. It does not require an investigative search for every helpful fact, a witness-by-witness defense presentation, or judicial reassessment of whether the grand jury weighed sufficient evidence. Costello’s refusal to test the quality or quantity of an otherwise lawful presentation is therefore preserved. A defendant seeking dismissal must establish a violation of this confined duty and the Bank of Nova Scotia prejudice consequence; judicial disagreement with the indictment is insufficient.

The contrary institutional argument is substantial: the grand jury is constitutionally distinct, and judicial innovation can obstruct its accusatory function. Stone’s response is equally limited. Supervision addresses the conduct of the federal prosecutor who controls a materially one-sided presentation, and the remedy permits lawful resubmission. It does not prescribe how jurors must deliberate or import trial evidence rules. Under Master §16 this is a proposed resolution of an open supervisory boundary, with direct attention to the limiting precedents, rather than an unannounced claim that an existing Supreme Court holding compels disclosure. The established district findings identify exculpatory material bearing on intent and grave doubt about the indictment’s independence from its omission. On those supported findings the prejudice condition is met.

The practical limit matters: the rule cannot become a means to litigate every disputed inference before trial. A motion must identify the known omitted evidence, its direct relation to an element, the material distortion, and prejudice under the established remedial standard. The case supplies findings after a hearing, rather than a dismissal based solely on the defendant’s assertion. Stone would uphold the available without-prejudice remedy on that confined footing.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve a constitutional disclosure duty, state grand juries, negligent failure to discover evidence, and immunity or fabrication claims not presented. No guilt or innocence finding follows from dismissal without prejudice.
