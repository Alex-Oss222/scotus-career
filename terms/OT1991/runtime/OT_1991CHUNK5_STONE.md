# Hudson v. McMillian

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before February 25, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any effective same-term holding actually bearing on the presented force claim; defenses outside the grant remain outside this position.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit's judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the significant-injury bar and require further disposition consistent with the undisturbed trial findings. Do not increase the award, decide an unpresented immunity defense or issue a prison-management injunction.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Gratuitous custodial violence

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the injury element that defeated the established claim.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the absence of serious injury defeats this deliberately unnecessary, nontrivial beating; a comprehensive mental-state rule for every force setting is unnecessary.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the Eighth Amendment's force doctrine in context and reject the circuit's additional injury prerequisite. Wilson's conditions analysis is distinguished, not overruled.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A nontrivial beating inflicted maliciously and without a disciplinary justification on a restrained convicted prisoner can violate the Eighth Amendment despite limited resulting physical injury.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Whitley treats injury as evidence within the force inquiry, not a license to administer cruelty so long as the body withstands it. Wilson's objective dimension is satisfied by the character and circumstances of serious custodial violence; the seriousness appropriate to a food or medical-deprivation claim cannot be transplanted as an identical injury element. Hudson's restraint, the absence of need, repeated blows and supervisory condonation satisfy the demanding wanton-force inquiry on the findings below. His dental and facial injuries reinforce that this was no trivial touch. The rule preserves room for disciplinary judgment because it requires constitutionally culpable force, while refusing to make constitutional protection depend on the victim's physical resilience.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No rule that any technical battery is cruel punishment, no strict liability for accidental harm and no general holding on isolated unauthorized conduct. That alternative cannot defeat this record, which includes supervisory approval. Compensatory damages still require their own lawful measurement; recognition of the right does not determine every remedy or defense.

# United States v. Nordic Village, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before February 25, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any effective simulated bankruptcy or immunity rule; no draft creates a waiver.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment permitting this monetary recovery from the United States.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Set aside the personal money judgment for want of the required sovereign consent. Leave genuinely distinct, properly supported remedies for ordinary proceedings; invent neither a traceable res nor a substitute award.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Consent to the particular remedy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the sovereign-immunity threshold before enforcing §550 against the Treasury.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entering unequivocal-waiver rule, with independent attention to the whole enacted provision. Hoffman is persuasive on text, not a binding statutory majority.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The original §106(c) does not unequivocally authorize the personal monetary judgment entered here.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The clause's reference to immunity is significant and must do work, but its specified consequence is that applicable provisions and determinations bind governmental units. That consequence can resolve claims and rights without authorizing every available monetary remedy. Neighboring express claim and offset waivers reinforce the distinction. The broader reading is plausible; the existing clear-consent rule requires more than plausibility. Section 550 creates recovery authority in ordinary cases, but it does not eliminate the separate question whether this sovereign has consented to that remedy. Respect for creditors' lawful entitlements does not authorize a court to fill the statutory gap by equitable preference.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No validation of the diversion, no holding that §106(c) has no effect, and no decision about state immunity or the validity of a later express waiver. There is no occasion to replace the native immunity rule.

### Issue 2: No conversion of a money judgment into a res

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Address each alternative offered to sustain the judgment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the distinction between jurisdiction over estate property and sovereign consent to pay money.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Neither §1334(d) nor trust terminology supplies the missing waiver for this order.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The order demands $20,000 from the United States, rather than adjudicating an identified fund in the bankruptcy court's possession. Its historical origin in corporate assets does not change the ordered remedy. A trust theory about beneficial ownership likewise cannot alone waive immunity.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Genuine specific-property proceedings remain a different question requiring their actual facts and legal authority.

# Holywell Corp. v. Smith

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before February 25, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any actual applicable simulated Code-construction rule.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse in both Nos. 90-1361 and 90-1484 and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Declare Smith responsible for the prescribed corporate returns as assignee and the relevant trust returns as fiduciary, with accompanying lawful tax-payment obligations after appointment. Leave amounts and implementation to ordinary tax and bankruptcy procedures.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Corporate assignee and trust fiduciary

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve both property categories necessary to the declaration.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted tax classifications and operative regulations without a bankruptcy-policy exception.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An assignee of substantially all corporate property falls within §6012(b)(3), and the fiduciary of this separately established liquidating trust falls within §6012(b)(4).
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The plan assigned the corporate property to Smith; the statute expressly makes business continuation unnecessary. Limiting an assignee to a conventional operating receiver would delete the broader term and its qualification. Gould's assets were vested in a separate trust, not simply left in an unchanged individual bankruptcy estate with a substituted manager. The liquidation purpose and Smith's authority match the regulation's trust and fiduciary descriptions even though the plan limits his discretion. The asserted grantor theory does not overcome the actual transfer and governing tax predicates. Section 6151 supplies the corresponding duty to pay tax required with the returns; the Court need not compute it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No universal rule that every bankruptcy trustee has identical tax duties or that every liquidating trust is taxed without regard to the grantor provisions.

### Issue 2: Silence is not a prospective tax exemption

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Address confirmation because it is offered to defeat otherwise applicable duties.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply §1141(a) to its actual subject.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A plan silent about taxes does not remove statutory duties arising from postappointment trust activity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The government did not agree to an exemption, and the plan did not adjudicate these prospective liabilities. Protecting reliance on a confirmed allocation does not permit the court to insert immunity from a different federal statute. The requested declaration therefore cannot stand even if confirmation binds creditors as to properly encompassed preconfirmation claims.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Preappointment taxes and express adjudication of existing tax claims remain outside this disposition.

# Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before February 26, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate actual earlier simulated remedies decisions to the extent their defendant, source and relief rules apply.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for proceedings on Franklin's damages claim.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit compensatory relief for proved intentional Title IX discrimination; determine liability, attribution, causation and amount through ordinary further proceedings. Award nothing now and prescribe no unnecessary injunctive regime.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Effective relief under a recognized federal action

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Accept Cannon's established cause and reach the categorical remedy bar actually decided below.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary remedial principles and Congress's statutory treatment of the action; no overruling or new cause of action is required.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A private plaintiff may seek compensatory damages for intentional violation of Title IX; the statute does not confine this recognized action to relief incapable of addressing a completed personal injury.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Cannon supplies enforcement authority. Bell and the analogous discrimination cases support appropriate relief unless Congress limits it. The 1986 law's reference to remedies at law and in equity, and subsequent legislation retaining the private-action structure, oppose a categorical implication of no damages. Franklin's departure from school illustrates the concrete remedial gap without itself creating judicial power: statutory enforcement and traditional remedies supply the source. Compensation must remain tied to a proved violation and resulting loss, rather than an abstract interest in punishing institutions. The pleaded official knowledge and conduct must still be tested; they are not assumed to establish a final recipient-liability standard.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No decision on punitive relief, liability for unintended effects, or every possible harassment-attribution rule. Reopening Cannon would exceed the necessary remedial question.

### Issue 2: Notice of intentional discrimination liability

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Address the Spending Clause objection because it is advanced as a bar to the requested remedy.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Pennhurst's notice principle without converting it into a universal requirement that every compensatory consequence be individually enumerated.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The notice principle does not categorically bar relief for the pleaded intentional violation of the express nondiscrimination condition.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An institution accepting education funds has statutory notice that intentional sex discrimination is forbidden, and the established private action gives the remedial inquiry a concrete legal foundation. A theory imposing unexpected liability for otherwise permissible conduct is a different case. This resolution rests on the statutory condition and entering enforcement law, not a generalized judicial duty to remedy every wrong.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Preserve the separate proof of recipient responsibility and questions about genuinely ambiguous funding conditions.

# INDOPCO, Inc. v. Commissioner

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before February 26, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any actual applicable simulated tax holding.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of the claimed immediate deductions.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve capital classification of the disputed transaction expenses; determine no new amortization schedule or tax liability beyond the reviewed judgment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Character of this acquisition expenditure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory classification and the taxpayer's proposed necessary condition.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply §§162 and 263 and read Lincoln Savings according to the proposition it decided. No statutory precedent is overruled.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Creation of a distinct asset is sufficient but not necessary for capitalization; these expenses facilitating a transaction with established enduring corporate benefits are not deductible as current ordinary operating expenses.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Code distinguishes the time and character of an outlay, not simply whether management acted sensibly. Lincoln Savings explains why its particular expenditure was capital; it does not convert that explanation into the exclusive definition for all transactions. Here the company changed from independent public ownership to subsidiary status and obtained access to a larger enterprise's resources. Those established benefits and the expenses' direct relation to the restructuring support the courts' classification without needing speculative profit estimates. The shareholder estate-planning preference alone would not establish a benefit to the corporation; the record supplies separate corporate grounds. The answer follows ordinary tax law and carries no additional constitutional or anti-corporate premise.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Incidental future benefit does not automatically capitalize a routine expense. No categorical rule for every acquisition or professional fee is adopted, and the immediate-deduction issue does not itself determine later cost recovery.

# Arkansas v. Oklahoma

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before February 26, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate the actual effective Lechmere decision for any material agency-review proposition.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Tenth Circuit in both Nos. 90-1262 and 90-1266.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the categorical already-degraded-waters bar and sustain the reviewed permit determination under its existing compliance and modification conditions. Do not prescribe new numerical limits or adjudicate the unrelated White River discharge.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Enforce the delegated interstate safeguards

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide authority for EPA's actual conditions and the court-imposed prohibition.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether EPA was authorized to use the downstream-compliance approach here, not whether every permit must use an identical formulation.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted scheme and entering administrative doctrine. The sources independently establish EPA's authority, so no overhaul of Chevron or regulatory deference is necessary.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** EPA may impose downstream state water-quality compliance through this federal permit; the Act does not supply the Tenth Circuit's categorical prohibition irrespective of detectable violation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Interstate effluent predictably crosses boundaries, and Congress provided a federal scheme in which affected-state standards and participation constrain permits. Recognizing those authorized conditions respects the downstream State without converting its common law into direct extraterritorial control. The scheme does not say that existing degradation automatically forbids every additional source regardless of the permit's demonstrated effect. A court cannot manufacture that prohibition from a broad environmental goal after the agency has applied the governing standards. This conclusion depends on enacted authority, not an assumption that technical expertise creates power.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve mandatory compliance in all conceivable settings and any distinct source-state tort remedy preserved by law.

### Issue 2: Respect supported technical findings within the rule

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the administrative determination necessary to resolve the reviewed permit.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing proof requirement and whole-record review; change neither burden nor scientific threshold.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The supported no-detectable-violation determination cannot be displaced by the reviewing court's separate categorical policy judgment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** EPA corrected the initial undue-impact formulation and required the city to carry a preponderance burden concerning actual detectable violations. The ALJ made component-specific findings meeting that instruction. The Tenth Circuit did not identify an evidentiary defect sufficient to overturn them. Technical uncertainty still matters through the permit's study and modification provisions; it does not authorize a court to invent a different statutory prohibition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No blanket tolerance for pollution labeled de minimis; detectable violations, unreliable findings and failure to apply valid standards remain reviewable.

# Collins v. City of Harker Heights

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before February 26, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate actual effective simulated custodial or municipal-liability holdings. The Master states a personal affirmative-danger commitment, not existing simulated precedent.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal substantive-due-process claim on the constitutional-duty ground, rejecting the Fifth Circuit's separate governmental-abuse element.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No federal damages or supervision order. Leave any independently available state-law remedies and their procedural treatment to the proper courts.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: No employer exemption from constitutional responsibility

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the §1983 element relied on below.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply §1983 and Monell; reject an additional categorical circuit requirement unsupported by the statute.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A municipality acting as employer can violate federal rights; a plaintiff who establishes such a violation and municipal responsibility need not prove an independent, additional sovereign-power-abuse element.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Public employment does not erase applicable speech, equality or bodily-liberty guarantees. The relevant constitutional source determines the wrong; §1983 supplies the action and Monell the city's responsibility. Adding a second abstract governmental-abuse requirement risks defeating recognized rights. That error does not require reversal if the complaint lacks a constitutional deprivation in the first place.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Municipal responsibility is not assumed merely because employment exists.

### Issue 2: The alleged omissions do not establish the required deprivation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the properly presented alternative ground necessary to the judgment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply source-specific duties and distinguish his affirmative-danger commitment from a general constitutional safe-workplace guarantee. No categorical rejection of affirmative-danger liability is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A voluntary public-employment relationship and a knowing policy of inadequate safety training, warnings or equipment do not, without the necessary custodial or affirmative-deprivation predicate, establish the pleaded substantive-due-process right to workplace safety.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The prior incident supports knowledge, and deliberate omission is more than negligence. Those points satisfy neither custody nor an affirmative exercise of authority creating the specific danger. The complaint does not allege coerced entry, deliberate bodily injury, obstruction of available rescue or another affirmative act materially increasing the hazard beyond the failure to provide protection. A general employment arrangement cannot itself supply that additional element without turning all knowingly deficient public workplaces into constitutional custodial settings. Stone's Master would recognize a properly presented affirmative contribution to a specific foreseeable danger with conscious disregard, practical opportunity and causation; those elements cannot be inserted into this complaint. Canton addresses when the city owns a qualifying constitutional wrong and cannot fill the missing substantive duty. The Texas statute supplies its own legal protection without converting its standards into federal substantive due process.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Preserve claims of actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury or a properly alleged affirmative danger. No factual approval of the city's conduct or assumption that budget choices justified it is needed. A broader new danger-creation rule need not be adopted to decide this omission-based pleading.

# Willy v. Coastal Corp.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before March 3, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any actual effective simulated rule affecting collateral procedural authority.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment sustaining this Rule 11 sanctioning authority.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the collateral sanction without restoring the federal merits judgment or enlarging the sanction. The underlying employment dispute follows the proper jurisdictional disposition.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A source for procedure without assumed merits jurisdiction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Accept the established absence of merits jurisdiction and independently identify authority for the collateral order.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the Rules and collateral-proceeding principles to their proper procedural object. This is an extension of Cooter & Gell's collateral rationale to the presented jurisdictional defect, not permission to assume merits jurisdiction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The court may impose this Rule 11 consequence for litigation conduct before it even though its earlier assertion of subject-matter jurisdiction was erroneous, because the order adjudicates a distinct procedural violation and does not decide the underlying claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A court must process a removed action sufficiently to determine its power, and valid procedural rules govern the parties while that process occurs. Rule 11 has an enacted procedural basis; its limited collateral function does not supply authority over the employment merits. Cooter & Gell identifies the independence of the procedural issue, while Mine Workers supports orderly determination of disputed authority. Catholic Conference does not require the opposite result: compelling merits discovery serves the unauthorized substantive action, whereas this completed sanction addresses misuse of the forum. Willy's correct jurisdictional position is not the sanctioned conduct. Recognizing this distinction prevents forum abuse without making a jurisdictional mistake self-validating.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No hypothetical jurisdiction, no revival of the invalid merits disposition and no broad permission to issue any collateral order. Each order needs its own lawful source, procedural safeguards and genuine independence from adjudication the court lacked power to undertake.

# McCarthy v. Madigan

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before March 4, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate actual effective simulated exhaustion or Bivens rulings; later statutory changes are excluded.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the exhaustion dismissal and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit ordinary proceedings on the damages complaint without making completion of this general grievance process a prerequisite. Resolve no damages amount, merits element or unpresented defense now.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A procedural gate must serve its lawful purpose

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide only the exhaustion ground that prevented adjudication.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply statute-first and purpose-sensitive entering exhaustion doctrine. Reject a categorical circuit rule for this procedure and claim; do not create a universal exception to congressionally mandated exhaustion.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Absent a statutory mandate, this federal prisoner seeking only damages need not exhaust the described BOP grievance procedure, which cannot supply monetary relief and provides insufficient countervailing adjudicative benefit to justify the bar.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress's limited treatment of other prisoner litigation does not authorize importing an unexpressed universal rule here. Carlson supplies an established damages channel for the relevant constitutional setting. The Bureau's opportunity to investigate and improve care is useful, but this claimant requests compensation for completed harm, and the grievance process cannot award it. Its written investigations do not create a formal evidentiary record binding on a later court. The short deadlines can instead foreclose judicial consideration for reasons unrelated to the constitutional claim; the later rejected appeal demonstrates that practical concern without proving the grievance officials acted unlawfully. On this specific balance, administrative convenience does not justify closing the recognized court channel. The government retains ordinary opportunities to develop facts and assert defenses in litigation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** An express statutory requirement controls according to its own terms. The result does not decide exhaustion where prospective relief is requested, the agency can grant the sought compensation, or a materially different administrative scheme performs an adjudicative function. It does not expand Bivens into a new context or treat alleged medical negligence as deliberate indifference.
