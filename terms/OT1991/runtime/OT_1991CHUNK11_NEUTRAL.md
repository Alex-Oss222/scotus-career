# Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife
* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 555 (1992); No. 90-1424.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, authorized for hearing in the simulation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1992-06-12, historical disposition date used only for event ordering, not a certiorari-grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation, not a verified verbatim petition quotation: Whether the conservation organizations have standing to challenge the geographic limitation in the Endangered Species Act consultation regulation and, if so, whether §7(a)(2) reaches federal agency actions abroad.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eighth Circuit, 911 F.2d 117, affirming summary judgment requiring a revised regulation after an earlier reversal of dismissal for lack of standing.
* **Relief Requested:** The Secretary requests reversal; respondents seek preservation of the declaration and order to restore foreign-project consultation.
* **Material Record:** A 1978 joint rule covered foreign actions; the 1986 rule covers the United States and high seas. Members Joyce Kelly and Amy Skilbred had visited foreign project areas, in Egypt and Sri Lanka respectively, and expressed intentions to return. The record includes no fixed return itinerary. Respondents connect endangered-species risks to federally assisted projects and advance ecosystem, animal, professional, and procedural interests. The action agencies financing the projects are not defendants; the Secretary disputes both his power to bind them and whether changed consultation would affect the projects.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Standing is disputed at summary judgment. The earlier appellate ruling on allegations does not conclusively establish facts at this later stage. Aesthetic injury is legally cognizable, but personal exposure, timing, causation, and redressability require separate support. The statutory merits cannot be reached through hypothetical jurisdiction.
* **Relevant Dates:** 1978 initial regulation; 1986 revision; 1988 initial appellate standing ruling; 1989 merits judgment; 1990 appellate affirmance.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires consolidation in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation is identified in the historical opinion.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 1992-06-12, using the pre-term historical baseline unless displaced by an effective simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied. Earlier drafted positions are not Court holdings.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Arkansas v. Oklahoma could affect administrative review only if statutory merits become reachable. No supplied simulated ruling resolves environmental standing; the historical Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation is a distinct 1990 authority.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Article III; associational standing under Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977); Rule 56 requires specific evidentiary support, with reasonable inferences for the nonmovant on each motion.

### Issue 1: Personal injury and procedural standing

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III limits federal judicial power to cases and controversies; ESA §11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), supplies the statutory citizen-suit authorization.
* **Material Other Authority:** Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972), recognizes recreational and aesthetic injury but requires the plaintiff or a member to be among the injured. Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990), requires specific use affected by identified agency action at summary judgment. Whitmore v. Arkansas, 495 U.S. 149 (1990), requires actual or imminent injury. The organizations bear the jurisdictional showing; a special interest or generalized demand for lawful administration is insufficient. Statutory authorization remains distinct from Article III standing.
* **Open Point:** Whether the affidavits establish imminent personal injury or a genuine factual dispute, and what effect the consultation right has on that inquiry.

### Issue 2: Causation, redressability, and conditional statutory merits

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III governs causation and redressability. ESA §7(a)(2), 16 U.S.C. §1536(a)(2), and the 1986 consultation regulation govern the geographic merits if jurisdiction exists.
* **Material Other Authority:** Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976), and Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984), require fairly traceable injury and likely redress, not speculation about independent third parties. Relief need not eradicate every environmental harm, but respondents must connect this defendant and requested order to their own injury. If jurisdiction exists, §1536(a)(2), its international context, the 1986 rule, and Chevron, 467 U.S. 837 (1984), govern the disputed geographic construction; the statutory text and structure are considered before permissible agency construction.
* **Open Point:** Whether the requested rule would bind action agencies or materially change project risks; whether consultation duties cover actions abroad. Neither is predetermined by a procedural label.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Eighth Circuit accepted the members’ interests and the statutory procedural theory, then sustained worldwide consultation. The Secretary emphasizes indefinite future visits, absent action agencies, disputed binding force, and independent foreign-project decisions. Respondents emphasize actual prior visits and intended return, permanent species loss affecting future observation, and consultation as a preventive procedure that need not guarantee a changed ultimate project. A professional interest in a species supplies neither proof of exposure nor exemption from the ordinary jurisdictional requirements. The Solicitor General represents the Secretary; no independent amicus factual finding is assumed.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Failure of personal injury permits reversal and dismissal for want of jurisdiction without deciding geographic coverage. A genuine dispute requiring factual resolution permits vacatur and a jurisdictional remand; it does not justify respondents’ merits summary judgment. If personal injury is established, the Court must independently address causation/redressability and then the statutory merits. Reinstating consultation is a procedural remedy, not a judicial direction to cancel foreign projects. The precise effect of consultation on absent agencies remains contested.

# Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 607 (1992); No. 91-763.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, authorized for hearing in the simulation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1992-06-12, historical disposition date used only for event ordering, not a certiorari-grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation, not a verified verbatim petition quotation: Whether Argentina’s bond rescheduling falls within the FSIA commercial-activity exception for an act abroad causing a direct effect in the United States.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, 941 F.2d 145, affirming denial of Argentina’s motion to dismiss on sovereign-immunity and jurisdictional grounds.
* **Relief Requested:** Argentina and its central bank seek dismissal; the bondholders seek continuation of their contract action in federal court.
* **Material Record:** Argentina issued dollar-denominated Bonods in connection with foreign-exchange obligations. Creditors could select payment in London, Frankfurt, Zurich, or New York. Two Panamanian corporations and a Swiss bank selected New York and rejected Argentina’s unilateral extension of maturity. Earlier interest payments had been made to New York accounts; the challenged scheduled payments were not made. These are contractual payment obligations, although issued for a sovereign currency-stabilization purpose.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The FSIA exception concerns jurisdiction, not whether the bondholders ultimately establish breach or recover a particular amount. Sovereign status and the statutory immunity default must be addressed before merits. Argentina expressly limits its due-process argument to an aid in interpreting the statutory direct-effect requirement; constitutional personal jurisdiction is not independently before the Court. See 504 U.S. 619 n.2.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires consolidation in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation is identified in the historical opinion.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 1992-06-12, using the pre-term historical baseline unless displaced by an effective simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied. Earlier drafted positions are not Court holdings.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier listed disposition is identified as a necessary doctrinal premise; any actually adopted rule altering the framework below requires revalidation.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§1330, 1604, 1605(a)(2), and 1608 separate subject-matter jurisdiction, immunity, and service-based personal jurisdiction. Legal characterization is reviewed independently.

### Issue 1: Commercial nature and domestic effect

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** The FSIA, 28 U.S.C. §§1330, 1603(d), and 1605(a)(2), supplies the jurisdictional grant, commercial-activity definition, and immunity exception.
* **Material Other Authority:** The FSIA defaults to immunity subject to enumerated exceptions. Section 1605(a)(2)’s third clause requires an act outside the United States connected with commercial activity elsewhere and a direct effect here. Section 1603(d) directs examination of the activity’s nature rather than purpose. Verlinden B.V. v. Central Bank of Nigeria, 461 U.S. 480 (1983), recognizes the statutory jurisdictional framework, including suits between foreign parties. International Shoe, 326 U.S. 310 (1945), supplies minimum-contacts principles if constitutional personhood is assumed.
* **Open Point:** Whether sovereign-issued refinancing instruments are commercial in nature and how direct the nonpayment effect must be; statutory text supplies no express substantiality or foreseeability requirement.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The courts below treated issuance and nonpayment of negotiable debt as commercial conduct with a New York performance consequence. Argentina argues that the instruments implement sovereign monetary management and that foreign creditors’ losses do not create a sufficient domestic effect. It also invokes constitutional avoidance to argue that direct effect incorporates minimum contacts, while conceding that constitutional personal jurisdiction is not a separate question presented. The holders respond that private borrowers issue debt of this type and that payment in New York was contractually authorized and selected. The United States supports jurisdiction, distinguishing sovereign purpose from commercial means.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If either commercial character or direct domestic effect fails, dismiss under the FSIA without deciding contract liability. If both are established, affirm denial of dismissal on the FSIA grounds presented and allow merits proceedings. The Court can answer Argentina’s avoidance argument by assuming sovereign constitutional personhood and examining contacts solely to resolve that interpretive objection; the concession forecloses a separate personal-jurisdiction holding. An asset-attachment or execution exception is a separate question.

* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 621 (1992); No. 91-72.

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, authorized for hearing in the simulation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1992-06-12, historical disposition date used only for event ordering, not a certiorari-grant date.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Third Circuit, 922 F.2d 1122, setting aside the FTC’s determination of insufficient state supervision.
* **Relief Requested:** The FTC seeks reversal and restoration of the proper immunity standard; insurers seek recognition that state review mechanisms suffice.
* **Material Record:** Private rating bureaus collectively established title-search and examination rates. State negative-option systems allowed filings to take effect unless disapproved. A power to reject rates existed; its actual exercise and substantive review are the disputed matters.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenged private rate agreements and the claimed immunity must be separated from sovereign action itself. * **Current Through:** Immediately before 1992-06-12, using the pre-term historical baseline unless displaced by an effective simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied. Earlier drafted positions are not Court holdings.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Eastman Kodak concerns antitrust proof in a different setting; any actual earlier simulated rule about evidentiary review requires checking, but its draft cannot establish this exemption. No earlier supplied decision changes Midcal or Patrick.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** FTC Act §5 and judicial review under §5(c), 15 U.S.C. §45(c): supported Commission fact findings are conclusive under the governing substantial-evidence review; immunity’s legal standard receives independent review.

### Issue 1: Active supervision of private restraints

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** The federal antitrust provisions enforced by the FTC govern the challenged private rate restraints, subject to the established state-action exemption.
* **Material Other Authority:** Parker v. Brown, 317 U.S. 341 (1943), recognizes immunity for state sovereign policy. California Retail Liquor Dealers v. Midcal Aluminum, 445 U.S. 97 (1980), requires private restraints to be clearly articulated and affirmatively expressed as state policy AND actively supervised by the State. Patrick v. Burget, 486 U.S. 94 (1988), requires actual state review sufficient to ensure that private conduct implements state policy; judicial review of procedural regularity is insufficient. Southern Motor Carriers, 471 U.S. 48 (1985), permits authorized private rate activity without state compulsion but does not eliminate either prong. The private parties claiming the exemption must establish its application.
* **Open Point:** Whether an available negative-option veto, without demonstrable substantive oversight, satisfies the second prong; how the factual findings differ by State.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Third Circuit considered the States’ supervisory machinery and capacity sufficient. Insurers argue that an opportunity to reject prices and a regulatory scheme can represent a State’s deliberate choice, and that demanding repeated affirmative approvals would interfere with state flexibility. The FTC responds that private price decisions require substantive state supervision, not unused authority; otherwise private horizontal restraints acquire immunity through official inactivity. Clear articulation answers authorization, not whether the particular rates received the required oversight.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Sufficient actual supervision supports immunity; absent supervision defeats it without invalidating the State’s regulatory system. A legal-standard error warrants reversal. Wisconsin and Montana can be resolved on the Commission’s findings; Connecticut and Arizona may require renewed appellate review under the proper standard and statutory respect for agency findings. No particular rate or mandatory paperwork system need be prescribed.

* **Case Name:** Burlington Northern Railroad Co. v. Ford
* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 648 (1992); consolidated Montana venue matters.

* **Material Record:** Ford and Johnson sued under FELA for injuries allegedly suffered while working at Burlington’s premises in Wyoming. The federal question concerns the venue classification as applied to a corporation headquartered outside Montana. No issue of personal jurisdiction or the underlying injury merits is presented.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires consolidation in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation is identified in the historical opinion.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 1992-06-12, using the pre-term historical baseline unless displaced by an effective simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied. Earlier drafted positions are not Court holdings.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier listed disposition is identified as a necessary doctrinal premise; any actually adopted rule altering the framework below requires revalidation.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Fourteenth Amendment equal protection; state construction of Montana venue provisions controls their meaning. Constitutional validity is independently reviewed.

### Issue 1: Ordinary venue classification

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment Equal Protection Clause governs the challenge to Montana’s county-venue classification.
* **Material Other Authority:** Corporations may claim equal protection. State of incorporation is not a suspect classification, and no fundamental right to a preferred county is established. Railway Express Agency v. New York, 336 U.S. 106 (1949), and United States Railroad Retirement Board v. Fritz, 449 U.S. 166 (1980), permit rational line-drawing serving legitimate interests; the challenger bears the burden of establishing irrationality. Power Manufacturing Co. v. Saunders, 274 U.S. 490 (1927), invalidated unequal Arkansas venue treatment on its record; Bain Peanut Co. v. Pinson, 282 U.S. 499 (1931), recognizes latitude in venue arrangements.
* **Open Point:** Whether the distinction here reasonably reflects party convenience and whether Power Manufacturing controls despite materially different headquarters and venue circumstances.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Montana sustained the rule partly by invoking FELA’s plaintiff-protective policies and the availability of federal litigation in Yellowstone County. Burlington argues that its substantial local presence makes the different rule arbitrary and that Power Manufacturing rejects incorporation-based favoritism. The employees defend the State’s authority to privilege a resident corporation’s home county while preserving plaintiff choice against a nonresident whose headquarters are elsewhere. Federal venue availability does not itself establish the validity of unequal state treatment.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm if this classification has a rational relation to lawful venue interests, without approving every rationale below. If Power Manufacturing requires equal treatment on these facts, reverse and remand for a lawful venue ruling. A ruling on out-of-state headquarters need not resolve a foreign corporation actually headquartered in Montana, or any separate Commerce Clause theory.

# United States v. Alvarez-Machain

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Alvarez-Machain
* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 655 (1992); No. 91-712.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, authorized for hearing in the simulation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1992-06-15, historical disposition date used only for event ordering, not a certiorari-grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation, not a verified verbatim petition quotation: Whether the United States–Mexico Extradition Treaty bars prosecution following a United States-sponsored abduction of a Mexican national from Mexico.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, 946 F.2d 1466, affirming dismissal of the indictment and an order of repatriation.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks reinstatement of the indictment; Alvarez-Machain seeks preservation of dismissal and return to Mexico.
* **Material Record:** Alvarez-Machain, a Mexican physician and national, was abducted from Mexico and taken to Texas to face federal charges relating to the kidnapping and murder of a DEA agent and his pilot. The District Court found DEA responsibility for the abduction. Mexico formally protested. The charged conduct is an accusation, not an established finding of guilt. No extradition proceeding supplied the transfer.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The issue is the legal consequence of the manner of transfer under the treaty. Treaty enforceability and interpretation precede trial; no separate adjudication of torture, guilt, damages, or territorial reach of the charged statutes is supplied.
* **Relevant Dates:** Treaty signed May 4, 1978, effective January 25, 1980; abduction April 2, 1990.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires consolidation in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation is identified in the historical opinion.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 1992-06-15, using the pre-term historical baseline unless displaced by an effective simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied. Earlier drafted positions are not Court holdings.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier listed disposition is identified as a necessary doctrinal premise; any actually adopted rule altering the framework below requires revalidation.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Article VI gives treaties status as supreme federal law; treaty interpretation is a judicial question. The challenge does not claim that the court lacks ordinary federal criminal subject-matter jurisdiction over the charged offenses.

### Issue 1: Treaty and forcible transfer

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** The 1978 United States–Mexico extradition treaty defines extraditable offenses and procedures. Article 9 permits a requested State to decline surrender of its own nationals and provides for submission to its prosecuting authorities. It contains no express abduction prohibition.
* **Material Other Authority:** Ker v. Illinois, 119 U.S. 436 (1886), rejected a treaty-based objection where forcible seizure occurred outside an invoked extradition process. Frisbie v. Collins, 342 U.S. 519 (1952), generally rejects loss of trial authority from an unlawful capture. United States v. Rauscher, 119 U.S. 407 (1886), enforces the treaty’s specialty limitation, including implications of the extradition arrangement.
* **Open Point:** Whether a state-sponsored seizure over the territorial sovereign’s objection is distinguishable from Ker and violates the treaty’s structure even without an express prohibition; whether dismissal and return follow.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit applied its Verdugo precedent: governmental responsibility plus Mexico’s protest made the treaty bar effective. The United States argues that the treaty creates a surrender mechanism without making it exclusive; Ker therefore controls, and any independent international wrong is for diplomatic resolution. Alvarez-Machain argues that unilateral state abduction nullifies Article 9’s preserved decision and the treaty’s agreed procedures. Mexico’s supporting submission matters to the claimed sovereign right and objection; it is not conclusive judicial interpretation. General international-law objections alone do not identify this treaty’s remedy.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A nonexclusive-treaty reading permits reversal and trial without endorsing the seizure’s legality. A treaty limitation applicable to this state-sponsored transfer permits affirmance of the dismissal and repatriation order. The Court may distinguish Ker on responsibility, invocation of sovereign treaty rights, and treaty context, but must explain that distinction rather than silently abolish the ordinary capture rule. Questions about broader customary-law remedies can remain open.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1332; Rule 12 dismissal reviewed on the pleaded case. Jurisdiction and abstention are distinct inquiries.

### Issue 1: Domestic relations and exceptional abstention

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III and 28 U.S.C. §1332 govern the federal diversity jurisdiction asserted over these state-law damages claims.
* **Material Other Authority:** Barber v. Barber, 62 U.S. (21 How.) 582 (1859), and De la Rama v. De la Rama, 201 U.S. 303 (1906), contain the domestic-relations limitation associated with divorce and alimony. Article III itself does not exclude every domestic dispute. Younger v. Harris, 401 U.S. 37 (1971), and its eligible civil extensions protect qualifying ongoing state proceedings. Burford v. Sun Oil, 319 U.S. 315 (1943), addresses interference with difficult state policy administration, not every application of state law. Colorado River, 424 U.S. 800 (1976), emphasizes the exceptional character of withholding federal jurisdiction.
* **Open Point:** The statutory foundation and exact boundary of the domestic-relations exception, and whether a damages-only abuse suit falls within it or a recognized abstention category.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The courts below treated the family relationship as sufficient for the exception and alternatively invoked Younger. Ankenbrandt distinguishes adjudicating tort injury from issuing or modifying family-status decrees and points to the absence of pending proceedings. Respondents emphasize state family-law expertise and the risk of duplicating domestic litigation. Those institutional concerns can support a properly triggered doctrine but do not supply the missing pending proceeding or turn damages into custody relief.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A broad domestic-relations exception would support dismissal; a limited decree-based exception leaves this tort action within §1332. Even with jurisdiction, a separately supported abstention ground would require analysis; none can be presumed from the parties’ relationship. Reversal restores adjudication, not a finding of abuse, a transfer of custody, or a federal family-supervision order.

# Morgan v. Illinois

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Morgan v. Illinois
* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 719 (1992); No. 91-5118.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, authorized for hearing in the simulation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1992-06-15, historical disposition date used only for event ordering, not a certiorari-grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation, not a verified verbatim petition quotation: Whether a capital defendant who requests inquiry is entitled to voir dire identifying prospective jurors who would automatically impose death upon conviction.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Illinois Supreme Court, 142 Ill.2d 410, 568 N.E.2d 755, affirming the conviction and death sentence.
* **Relief Requested:** Morgan seeks relief from the death sentence because the trial court refused the requested inquiry; Illinois seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Illinois used the same jury for guilt and sentencing. The prosecution requested questioning about jurors who would invariably oppose death. Morgan requested the converse inquiry about jurors who would invariably impose death. The judge refused, relying on general fairness and law-following questions. Every seated juror was asked about fairness, and most about following legal instructions. The jury convicted and imposed death. No finding establishes that a particular seated juror actually held the automatic-death view.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Morgan requested the inquiry. The challenge concerns adequacy of capital jury selection, not a claim to a particular favorable jury composition. The record’s silence about seated jurors cannot simply cure the denied means of discovering disqualification.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires consolidation in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation is identified in the historical opinion.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 1992-06-15, using the pre-term historical baseline unless displaced by an effective simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied. Earlier drafted positions are not Court holdings.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Stringer v. Black and Sochor v. Florida could affect the operative capital reliability and review framework if actually decided; their current proposed briefs do not replace pre-term mitigation and impartiality law.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Fourteenth Amendment due process and impartial-sentencer requirements; independent review of the constitutional adequacy of the inquiry, distinct from deference to an individual juror credibility finding.

### Issue 1: Impartiality, mitigation, and adequate inquiry

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments protect the required impartial tribunal and fair capital sentencing process; the Eighth Amendment governs the individualized-sentencing requirements. Illinois law directs consideration of aggravating and mitigating factors before death.
* **Material Other Authority:** Witherspoon v. Illinois, 391 U.S. 510 (1968), and Wainwright v. Witt, 469 U.S. 412 (1985), govern death-qualification exclusions; Witt asks whether views prevent or substantially impair performance according to instructions and oath. Lockett’s relevant plurality rule, Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 (1982), and Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 (1989), protect consideration and effect of relevant mitigation. Mu’Min v. Virginia, 500 U.S. 415 (1991), recognizes trial-court latitude while requiring constitutionally adequate voir dire.
* **Open Point:** Whether automatic-death views establish disqualification, whether requested specific inquiry is necessary, and whether general assurances adequately expose that bias.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Illinois held that reverse-Witherspoon questioning was not constitutionally required. The State stresses judicial discretion and the general assurances of impartiality and willingness to follow instructions. Morgan argues that an automatic-death juror cannot consider mitigation in good faith, that generic questions fail to identify the particular disability, and that the ability to challenge for cause is ineffective without inquiry. This does not assert that favoring capital punishment generally disqualifies a juror.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If the inquiry as a whole adequately identifies the disqualifying view, affirm. If automatic-death views are disqualifying and the requested inquiry was not substantially provided, reverse the death-sentence component and require a lawful sentencing process. Guilt need not be disturbed absent a separate basis. The Court must distinguish the risk created by denied inquiry from a factual finding that any particular juror was biased.

# Patterson v. Shumate

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Patterson v. Shumate
* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 753 (1992); No. 91-913.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, authorized for hearing in the simulation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1992-06-15, historical disposition date used only for event ordering, not a certiorari-grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation, not a verified verbatim petition quotation: Whether an ERISA-enforceable antialienation restriction excludes a beneficial trust interest from the bankruptcy estate under §541(c)(2).
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fourth Circuit, 943 F.2d 362, reversing turnover of the debtor’s pension interest.
* **Relief Requested:** The trustee seeks inclusion of the pension interest; Shumate seeks exclusion under §541(c)(2).
* **Material Record:** Shumate participated in his employer’s ERISA-qualified pension plan, which contained the required antialienation clause. His control of the employer prevented the interest from satisfying the state spendthrift-trust theory accepted by the District Court. The federal restriction’s enforceability, rather than a judicial judgment that the debtor is deserving, is material.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Exclusion from the estate under §541 differs from exemption of estate property under §522. The relevant pension qualification and trust features are established premises of this dispute, not a holding that every retirement account is excluded.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires consolidation in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation is identified in the historical opinion.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 1992-06-15, using the pre-term historical baseline unless displaced by an effective simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied. Earlier drafted positions are not Court holdings.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Union Bank v. Wolas and Dewsnup v. Timm may affect the operative bankruptcy interpretation approach if actually adopted. Neither historical same-term holding may be used to predetermine this construction.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Bankruptcy Code §§541(a), 541(c)(1)–(2), 522(d)(10)(E); ERISA §206(d)(1), 29 U.S.C. §1056(d)(1); statutory interpretation reviewed independently.

### Issue 1: Applicable nonbankruptcy law

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** Bankruptcy Code §541(c)(2) preserves a restriction on transfer of a beneficial interest in a trust enforceable under applicable nonbankruptcy law. ERISA §206(d)(1) requires pension antialienation, and §502 authorizes enforcement.
* **Material Other Authority:** Guidry v. Sheet Metal Workers, 493 U.S. 365 (1990), refuses an implied equitable exception to ERISA antialienation for wrongdoing. The proponent of exclusion must identify the trust interest, restriction, and enforceability; the legal phrase’s meaning is not an evidentiary proof question.
* **Open Point:** Whether applicable nonbankruptcy law means only state spendthrift law or includes applicable federal law, and whether the §522 exemption changes that reading.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The District Court restricted §541(c)(2) to state spendthrift law. The Fourth Circuit read its unqualified language to include ERISA. The trustee emphasizes bankruptcy’s broad estate policy, references to spendthrift trusts in legislative history, and possible overlap with §522’s pension exemption. Shumate and the United States answer that Congress elsewhere specified state law when intended and that exclusions and exemptions cover different interests and conditions.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A state-law-only construction requires considering the failed state spendthrift status and inclusion. A federal-law-inclusive construction excludes this qualifying restricted trust interest without requiring a §522 exemption. Affirmance leaves other plan qualification, trust, or statutory-exception disputes to their own records; it does not decide post-distribution tracing.

# Allied-Signal, Inc. v. Director, Division of Taxation

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Allied-Signal, Inc. v. Director, Division of Taxation
* **Citation or Docket:** 504 U.S. 768 (1992); No. 91-615.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, authorized for hearing in the simulation.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 1992-06-15, historical disposition date used only for event ordering, not a certiorari-grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation, not a verified verbatim petition quotation: Whether New Jersey may apportion the gain from Bendix’s ASARCO stock sale and whether the unitary-business limitation should be retained.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** New Jersey Supreme Court, 125 N.J. 20, 592 A.2d 536, sustaining the apportioned assessment.
* **Relief Requested:** Allied-Signal, as successor to Bendix, seeks reversal and tax-refund proceedings excluding the gain; New Jersey seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Bendix held 20.6% of ASARCO and sold the interest in 1981 for a $211.5 million gain. The parties stipulated that the companies were unrelated enterprises whose activities had nothing to do with one another; Bendix’s two directors did not give it control over ASARCO. New Jersey nevertheless treated acquisitions and dispositions as part of Bendix’s operational business. The eventual use of sale proceeds is distinct from the function of the investment while held.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This concerns a nondomiciliary corporation’s investment income, not New Jersey’s power to tax a domiciliary’s worldwide income. The question whether precedent should be retained was expressly presented through reargument.
* **Relevant Dates:** 1981 sale; Supreme Court argument March 4, 1992, and reargument April 22, 1992.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separately listed companion requires consolidation in this module.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist; no case-specific nonparticipation is identified in the historical opinion.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before 1992-06-15, using the pre-term historical baseline unless displaced by an effective simulated decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision has been supplied. Earlier drafted positions are not Court holdings.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Quill, Barker, and the two waste-discrimination matters may affect distinct tax nexus or nondiscrimination propositions if effective simulated holdings exist. They do not themselves answer which investment income belongs in an apportionable unitary base.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Due Process and Commerce Clauses; independent constitutional review of the permissible tax base, with stipulated business facts controlling.

### Issue 1: Unitary base and operational assets

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied holding resolves this issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No adopted simulated test has been supplied; the eligible historical framework follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Due Process and Commerce Clauses limit state attribution of income earned outside the taxing State.
* **Material Other Authority:** Mobil Oil v. Commissioner, 445 U.S. 425 (1980), requires a minimal connection and a rational relation between attributed income and in-state value. ASARCO v. Idaho, 458 U.S. 307 (1982), and F.W. Woolworth v. Taxation Department, 458 U.S. 354 (1982), distinguish discrete investment enterprises from the unitary business. Container Corp., 463 U.S. 159 (1983), considers functional integration, centralized management, and economies of scale, and recognizes operational investment connections. A taxpayer challenging extraterritorial attribution bears the established clear-and-cogent showing; it need not disprove a connection contradicted by accepted stipulations.
* **Open Point:** Whether the gain belongs to an operational asset function despite the absence of an integrated payor/payee enterprise, and whether common ownership alone should replace the unitary limitation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

New Jersey treated acquisitions and divestitures as an operational component of Bendix’s business and urges broader apportionability, including reconsideration of restrictive precedent. Allied-Signal relies on stipulated separation and argues that acquiring an investment to earn money does not make it unitary with every other business. Some amici propose an operational-transaction approach. That alternative may permit working-capital or operational asset income, but does not itself establish this asset’s role.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A demonstrated unitary or operational connection permits apportionment subject to a fair formula. An unrelated investment without that connection must be excluded. Replacing the unitary rule requires an express precedent decision and a defensible territorial limit; broad ownership alone would materially alter the constitutional boundary. Reversal calls for lawful recalculation/refund proceedings, not automatic exemption of all intangible gains.
