# United States v. Ibarra

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Historical law immediately before October 15, 1991; no simulated holding yet exists.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Tenth Circuit judgment dismissing the Government's appeal.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for further appellate proceedings. Do not order admission of the cocaine or prejudge the consent argument.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A timely substantive reconsideration request postpones finality

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve appellate timeliness first; the suppression merits are outside the necessary reach.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Healy* and *Dieter* without adding an abandoned-ground exception.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** This single timely motion sought alteration of the suppression adjudication and made that order nonfinal for appeal purposes until January 3; the January 30 notice was timely.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Government's earlier abandonment may defeat the consent theory without changing what its reconsideration motion requested. *Dieter* already rejects dividing substantive motions according to the kind of error asserted. A rule requiring parties to assess the legal strength of a motion to determine when the appeal clock starts supplies no stable deadline. The statutory thirty days remain thirty days; identifying the final order does not invent equitable tolling. Stone's procedural discipline protects both sides through the same predictable rule, including an unpersuasive government motion.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No decision on successive reconsideration, bad faith, renewed merits waiver, or the search. No change to an appeal deadline or new exception is proposed.

# Mireles v. Waco

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible historical law immediately before October 21, 1991.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's judgment rejecting dismissal on absolute judicial immunity, limited to the alleged separable direction to inflict unnecessary violence.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return for ordinary proceedings on that claim. Preserve immunity for the judicial direction to appear and adjudicate no liability or damages.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Classification of the alleged conduct

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Rule only on the pleaded claim and the function for which immunity is sought.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Forrester* and *Stump* differently from an analysis that classifies the whole encounter as courtroom management. No general misconduct or bad-motive exception is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A plausible allegation that a judge separately directed gratuitous physical violence is an allegation of operational conduct, not a judicial act, even when accompanying an immune attendance order.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The nature of the task controls, not the speaker's title. Issuing an attendance command and directing the infliction of violence are separable in function as well as possible execution. The complaint expressly attributes the unnecessary force to the judge's command, so dismissal cannot assume the officers independently exceeded a lawful direction. The limiting concern is serious: merely alleging an erroneous coercive judicial order cannot erase immunity. Stone therefore preserves protection for warrants, contempt adjudications, attendance orders, and other core decisions even when unlawful. The claim survives only insofar as the evidence establishes the operational direction alleged. This applies the existing nonjudicial-function boundary, rather than relying on Stone's distinct falsification exception, which is not presented.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The pleading does not establish a constitutional violation, causation, punitive culpability, or the officers' defenses. Complete absence of jurisdiction is unnecessary because the surviving conduct is nonjudicial; ordinary judicial attendance authority is not denied.

# Campos v. City of Houston

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Historical law immediately before October 29, 1991 and the user's referral rule.
* **Conditions:** Implement the proposed stay through the pending appellate review channel; any subsequent material order altering Plan 2's status requires revalidation.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Deny the free-standing injunction stopping all City elections; grant the alternative stay insofar as the October 12 order authorizes and compels use of Plan 2 for the City Council election.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Proposed Court-order wording: “The application presented to Justice O'Connor and by her referred to the Court is granted in part. The portion of the District Court's October 12, 1991 order directing use of 9-5-1 Plan 2 is stayed pending further order of this Court. The application is otherwise denied.” Leave the prohibition on malapportioned Plan 1 intact. The lower court may promptly consider a lawful interim plan and necessary schedule changes through its existing jurisdiction; prescribe neither a map nor a sixteen-district structure.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Interim equity cannot bypass the outstanding statutory objection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach entitlement to a limited stay in aid of appellate jurisdiction. Do not decide permanent districting, discriminatory purpose, or the full §2 claim.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply §5, *McDaniel*, *Clark*, and *Upham* to the established legislative origin and objection. No new election test is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Applicants establish sufficient legal and equitable grounds to stay judicial implementation of an expressly objected-to legislative plan when a nonobjected-to alternative exists and the reason for disregarding the objection is scheduling, turnout, and expense.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Plan 2 remains the City's legislative choice; submitting it to a district judge does not transform it into an independently devised judicial map. The Attorney General's objection is established, and the ordinary statutory alternatives were reconsideration, District of Columbia review, or replacement. The local court's view of nonretrogression cannot replace those channels or answer the outstanding purpose issue. *Upham* protects permissible legislative choices while requiring correction of the objectionable portions; the court acknowledged it did not make that correction. *Clark* gives §5's preventive command practical effect before the election takes place. Postponement costs, lower anticipated turnout, and already-cast ballots are substantial harms, but letting objected-to changes determine who exercises public power imposes a statutory electoral injury that later review cannot fully undo. The availability of Plan 3 following a nonobjection defeats the claim that bypassing preclearance is the only lawful administrative possibility. Its implementation may require delay, and plaintiffs may still contest it, but neither fact makes its present legal status identical to Plan 2's. Shared responsibility for haste weighs against applicants; it does not grant the district court substitute preclearance authority. A focused stay preserves lawful remedial choice and avoids the excessive original request to halt every City contest.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding that Plan 2 violates §2 or the Constitution, no conclusion that Plan 3 satisfies all legal requirements, and no automatic rule invalidating every imperfect judicial interim map. Source-bound exceptional interim authority remains, but its conditions are not supplied merely by an election deadline. This proposal neither grants plenary review nor treats the Circuit Justice as sole decider.

# Zatko v. California

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law immediately before November 4, 1991.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Allow Zatko and Martin to proceed in forma pauperis on all 17 grouped pending fee motions; decline to exercise Rule 39.8 fee-denial discretion even assuming a supported finding of current frivolousness.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Process both applicants' grouped filings under ordinary filing and screening rules. Issue no disposition on the underlying petitions and no new prospective filing ban.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Exercise of Rule 39.8 discretion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve only the fee-status issue; the substantive petitions are outside the assigned scope.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing discretionary rule. Neither invalidate Rule 39.8 nor constitutionalize an unrestricted right to free filings.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Even assuming the Court is satisfied that the current filings of both applicants are frivolous, Rule 39.8 permits ordinary fee-waived screening, and Stone would exercise that discretion here.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Zatko's 73 filings and Martin's more than 45 warrant attention but are not enacted mandatory disqualifications. Stone assumes for this discretionary decision that present frivolousness of each applicant's submissions is sufficiently established; the source-access limitation is not his ground for relief. Fee denial adds a financial barrier while the task of assessing the present paper remains. Ordinary screening can dispose of a meritless request, while a demonstrated repetitive mechanism may justify a targeted response under *McDonald* or *Sindram*. Preserving that screening route avoids treating a litigant's history as an instruction to discount a later colorable claim. Stone preserves those precedents, identifies the discretionary choice, and does not claim that wealth is a suspect classification or that court-access interests supply jurisdiction over every petition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different circumstances may support a different exercise of existing discretion. No holding that underlying claims are meritorious, and no preapproved alternative vote, is supplied.

# Hafer v. Melo

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Historical law immediately before November 5, 1991.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any material actual simulated *Mireles* holding; do not treat its draft as law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Third Circuit's judgment on §1983 personal-capacity coverage.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow proceedings on the employees' claims, with their elements and valid defenses unresolved. Award no damages or reinstatement now.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Official conduct and an individual defendant

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve who can be sued, not whether the dismissals violated federal law.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply §1983, *Graham*, and *Scheuer*; clarify *Will* without altering its state-defendant rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A state officer sued individually is a statutory person even when the challenged conduct used official authority. State immunity does not independently bar that personal claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hafer's proposed reading would make the necessary official nexus defeat the remedy it activates. Text distinguishes the actor from the source of authority; capacity identifies whose liability is sought. The appellate court found personal claims, so they cannot be dismissed as suits against Pennsylvania merely because only its Auditor General could fire these employees. Stone preserves *Will* for actual treasury claims, the prospective officer channel for reinstatement, and distinct personal defenses. The existence of those defenses does not invite redesigning qualified immunity here; its governing formulation is not necessary to personhood and is reserved.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No constitutional employment ruling, public compensation channel, waiver, indemnification entitlement, or new immunity rule is proposed.

# Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System v. MCorp Financial, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law immediately before December 3, 1991; no supplied effective simulated decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse in No. 90-913; affirm in No. 90-914.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the jurisdictionally unauthorized interruption of the source-of-strength proceeding; allow both pending proceedings to continue through the statutory channels. Decide no validity or asset-enforcement merits.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The channel controls the present judicial power

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve jurisdiction and reserve delegated regulatory power.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted scheme and confine *Kyne* to its exceptional rationale.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Neither bankruptcy jurisdiction nor *Kyne* authorizes this action to halt these nonfinal proceedings in the face of §1818(i)(1) and meaningful final-order review.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress separately identified temporary-order, final-order, and enforcement stages. This action fits none. Section 362(b)(4) gives regulatory proceedings room to continue; a court cannot make the exception depend on first resolving every contested merits question the review channel postpones. The Board is not another court for §1334(b), and proceeding toward an order does not yet seize estate assets. *Kyne* prevents a statutory right from becoming unreviewable, a defect absent where appellate review can decide authority and application. Stone's insistence that agencies remain within delegation does not create jurisdiction to examine that boundary prematurely. No *Chevron* replacement or new agency-weight framework is necessary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve regulation validity, validity of any final demand, and actual conflict with estate control. This holding protects a review sequence, not agency immunity from law.

# Griffin v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Historical law immediately before December 3, 1991.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the conviction on the presented general-verdict challenge.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No retrial or acquittal on this ground; no advisory instruction governing unrelated prosecutions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Factual inadequacy is not an invalid legal theory

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the due-process and precedent question actually argued; reserve independent Sixth Amendment questions not needed here.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Turner* and distinguish *Yates* and *Stromberg*.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The general verdict does not require reversal merely because proof fails on one lawful conspiracy object when another object supports the conviction under the unchanged beyond-reasonable-doubt requirement.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** *Yates* prevents a jury from applying a legally unavailable route the judge has authorized. Here both alleged objects are legally capable of constituting conspiracy; the defect concerns evidence the jury was charged to evaluate. *Turner* supplies the pertinent distinction. Griffin's timely request made withdrawal of the unsupported theory sensible, but a preferred trial-management practice does not itself supply a constitutional reversal rule. Stone does not presume an unreported factual finding or claim every general verdict is reliable. He asks whether the preserved defect requires setting this one aside under governing law, and the supplied record identifies sufficient IRS-object evidence and no forbidden legal theory. His concern for criminal reliability does not authorize a new rule merely because it might remove uncertainty.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Legal invalidity, failure of proof on all routes, misleading instructions that dilute an element, and properly presented unanimity questions retain their own treatment. No change to *Winship*, *Jackson*, or federal harmless-error law is proposed.
