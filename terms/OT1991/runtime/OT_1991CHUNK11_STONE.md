# Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The eligible baseline in Section I.B.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate against actual earlier effective simulated decisions, particularly the dependencies identified in Section I.B. No approval or prior Stone case-specific position is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit judgment and remand with instructions to dismiss this action for lack of Article III standing on the supplied summary-judgment record.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the merits declaration and mandatory rulemaking relief; dismissal for want of jurisdiction does not adjudicate the legality of foreign-project consultation or preclude a properly supported later action.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Imminent personal injury

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve standing first; do not reach ESA geographic coverage.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the received personal-injury and summary-judgment requirements, without a new documentary prerequisite.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Prior visits plus an indefinite intention to return, without evidence making renewed personal use sufficiently imminent, do not establish standing for these prospective claims.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The members have more than ideological concern: they actually visited relevant places. That proves a personal interest, but not the timing of threatened interference with their use. The record supplies neither a definite plan nor another concrete continuing activity making exposure imminent. Stone does not require purchased tickets or a particular travel document; an established return practice or sufficiently definite undertaking could do the work. He cannot supply those facts from sympathy for species preservation. The same defect defeats nexus theories that merely broaden the class of interested persons. The claimed procedural deprivation also lacks the necessary connection to a sufficiently imminent interference with these members’ own concrete use; it cannot cure that failure by being called procedural. This applies Master §§3 and 17 and its Environment domain without relaxing the plaintiff’s burden.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve disputed redressability, the outer constitutional scope of congressionally created procedural rights, and §7 coverage. A claimant actually using an affected ecosystem presents a materially different record; multiple contributors alone would not defeat that claim.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment denying dismissal on the FSIA grounds presented.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the contract action for ordinary merits proceedings; award neither damages nor execution against sovereign assets in this jurisdictional decision.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: FSIA coverage

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory immunity exception as the necessary threshold.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted nature/purpose distinction and resolve the direct-effect phrase in context.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Issuing the negotiable payment instruments is commercial activity, and failure to make their contractually required New York payment produces a direct United States effect.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A monetary-policy objective does not transform every chosen instrument into an exclusively sovereign act. A private borrower can promise dollar repayment at a creditor-selected financial center. Once New York was validly selected, the missing payment occurred at the agreed performance location; it is not merely a foreign economic loss felt by an observer here. Adding a substantiality requirement would rewrite the exception. Purpose may explain the issuance but cannot replace Congress’s nature inquiry. Assuming sovereign constitutional personhood solely to answer Argentina’s interpretive objection, the purposeful New York payment and financial arrangements satisfy minimum contacts. Constitutional avoidance therefore supplies no reason to narrow direct effect in this case; Stone makes no independent constitutional personal-jurisdiction ruling.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve foreign-sovereign constitutional personhood, ultimate breach defenses, and immunity from attachment. Ordinary regulatory or expropriatory acts are not commercial simply because they affect money.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Third Circuit judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject immunity for the Wisconsin and Montana arrangements on the supported findings; require reconsideration of Connecticut and Arizona with proper deference to Commission fact findings. Leave ensuing enforcement to the statutory proceedings.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Actual state supervision

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the claimed private-conduct exemption, not a general inquiry into the wisdom of state insurance regulation.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Midcal and Patrick, specifying what their existing supervision requirement demands.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A merely available veto does not establish active supervision of privately fixed rates; the immunity claimant must show substantive state examination of the challenged rate-setting practice.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The States may displace competition, but the legal exemption depends on their actually taking responsibility for the resulting restraints. Arithmetic review cannot establish evaluation of rate substance. Unanswered information requests followed by years of effectiveness support the Commission’s finding of inactivity. Stone does not require oral hearings, a uniform form, or rejection of some quota of rates. Written substantive review can suffice; the record must show the function occurred. This is native antitrust-immunity analysis, not constitutional state action under civil-rights law.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve the minimum oversight sufficient in a materially different record, distinct sovereign pricing arrangements, and unpresented insurance-law exemptions.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgments denying these venue transfers.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow both FELA actions to proceed in Yellowstone County; express no view on negligence, damages, or other preserved trial defenses.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Rational venue distinction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved equal-protection issue as applied to Burlington’s established corporate location.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary rational-basis review and distinguish Power Manufacturing.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Montana may give a resident corporation a home-county preference without giving this nonresident corporation, headquartered in Texas, an identical local preference.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The legitimate interest is allocating the parties’ competing convenience, not rewarding local incorporation as an end. A State can rationally conclude that protecting an actual local headquarters has different value from selecting one in-state office of a corporation whose home is elsewhere. Approximation is permitted under rational basis; Burlington does not demonstrate that the mismatch defeats the rule’s relation to that interest. Stone rejects substitution of strict scrutiny or a special corporate-capacity burden. FELA’s merits policy and hypothetical federal venue are not needed to sustain the rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve a foreign corporation with its actual headquarters in Montana, discriminatory commerce burdens under a separately presented claim, and all FELA merits.

# United States v. Alvarez-Machain

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The eligible baseline in Section I.B.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate against actual earlier effective simulated decisions, particularly the dependencies identified in Section I.B. No approval or prior Stone case-specific position is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal of this indictment and the order requiring repatriation to Mexico.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the pre-abduction sovereign position by return; dismissal rests on the unlawful treaty-defeating transfer, not an acquittal or a permanent bar to prosecution following a lawful transfer.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Treaty’s retained sovereign choice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the treaty ground; do not reach a free-standing international-law or constitutional capture exception.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose the narrow extension of Rauscher’s enforceable treaty limitations to a state-sponsored circumvention of this treaty; distinguish Ker.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The United States may not use its agents to seize a Mexican national from Mexico over Mexico’s formal objection and thereby bypass the surrender decision the treaty preserves.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Article 9 reserves a meaningful sovereign choice, including domestic prosecution when surrender is declined. Treating abduction as an alternative governmental means would deprive that reservation of effect at the requesting State’s option. The strongest contrary point is textual silence: a treaty imposing affirmative surrender obligations need not always prohibit every other transfer. Here the inference is limited to the contracting government’s forcible bypass of the reserved decision, not voluntary departure or a transfer Mexico authorizes. Rauscher establishes that enforceable limits can follow from treaty structure; Ker does not require treating all captures as affirmatively authorized by every treaty. Under Master §16, Stone acknowledges the proposed boundary clarification, preserves ordinary Ker–Frisbie treatment outside it, and identifies no protected reliance on a government’s capacity to defeat its treaty counterpart’s retained choice. The treaty supplies the limitation; disapproval of abduction supplies no independent jurisdictional rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve private captures without governmental attribution, consensual transfers, the legality of future extradition, and damages. If Ker is read to cover this materially different treaty circumvention, Stone would narrow it to the stated boundary, openly rather than claiming unchanged application.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit judgment and remand for the damages action to proceed.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reinstate the complaint for ordinary proceedings and defenses; award no damages and modify no domestic decree.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Damages claim and abstention

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach §1332 and each stated dismissal ground, because either could independently sustain the judgment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply §1332 to this damages action. Assume for this disposition that the traditional decree-based exception exists, without deciding the granted foundational question unnecessarily.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Even assuming an exception for divorce, alimony, and custody decrees, it does not exclude this damages action for alleged child abuse; neither Younger nor Burford supports dismissal on this record.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The remedy requested is legally decisive. A family relationship supplies the factual setting, but the court will adjudicate tort conduct and compensation. Barber and De la Rama provide no basis for extending their decree-related limitation to this independent tort claim; Congress’s diversity grant supplies jurisdiction here whether that limited exception ultimately survives or is abolished. This establishes jurisdiction over the actual action without assuming jurisdiction to reach its merits. Younger lacks an ongoing proceeding; Burford lacks a shown interference with a specialized state regime or unresolved status question necessary to the tort. The first granted question can therefore remain open without leaving a possible ground for this dismissal unanswered. Stone does not use family-protection commitments to invent a federal substantive claim.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve whether the decree-based exception should ultimately be retained and its full foundation and reach, future abstention on materially changed facts, and the truth of the allegations.

* **Conditions:** Revalidate against actual earlier effective simulated decisions, particularly the dependencies identified in Section I.B. No approval or prior Stone case-specific position is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Illinois judgment insofar as it sustains Morgan’s death sentence and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Set aside the death sentence; any renewed capital sentencing must use a jury selected through an inquiry sufficient to identify automatic-death jurors. Leave the conviction intact and leave Illinois the option of a lawful noncapital sentence.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Effective identification of capital bias

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved denial of requested inquiry.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend the received impartiality and mitigation principles to the necessary converse of death qualification; do not replace Witt.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** On request, capital voir dire must adequately identify jurors whose automatic-death views prevent or substantially impair lawful consideration of mitigation. The questions asked here did not do so.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A juror can sincerely believe that mandatory death is fair and still be incapable of following a system that permits mercy based on mitigation. General assurances therefore do not answer the particular inquiry. The State’s legitimate interest in excluding jurors incapable of imposing death does not support refusing inquiry into a corresponding inability to consider life. This is a defined procedural consequence of impartiality, not a right to jurors favoring the defense. Courts retain control over wording and follow-up; no fixed questionnaire or proof quantum is added. The failure cannot be answered by presuming favorable responses to a question the court barred, and the strength of guilt evidence does not establish a lawful sentencing tribunal.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No actual bias finding is invented. Reserve unrelated voir dire omissions and collateral-review questions. This holding does not require disqualification for general support of death or a juror’s stated view that some proved circumstances strongly favor it.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Fourth Circuit judgment excluding Shumate’s qualifying pension interest.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny turnover of this excluded interest to the bankruptcy trustee; no exemption determination is necessary for property that never enters the estate.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Federal antialienation law

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory exclusion that determines the requested turnover.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted phrase without inserting a state-law limitation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Applicable nonbankruptcy law includes ERISA, and this plan’s enforceable antialienation restriction satisfies §541(c)(2).
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The text identifies applicable law by its relation to bankruptcy, not by sovereign source. ERISA is federal nonbankruptcy law. Reading state into the phrase would contradict its breadth and the Code’s use of explicit state-law language elsewhere. The trust and enforceability requirements do independent work and prevent all retirement assets from becoming categorically immune. Section 522 can exempt interests not already excluded and does not justify narrowing §541. Guidry confirms that equitable distaste for the beneficiary cannot override the enacted protection. No constitutional or Stone-specific pension entitlement is needed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve plans outside ERISA, interests not held in trust, statutory exceptions to antialienation, and treatment after benefits are distributed.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand the judgment sustaining inclusion of the ASARCO gain.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require exclusion of this gain from the challenged apportionable tax base and further state tax proceedings consistent with that limitation; leave calculation and appropriate refund treatment below.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Retained unitary limitation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach both the presented precedent challenge and its application to the stipulated facts.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Retain the received territorial/unitary limitation and apply it to this investment.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Common ownership and a general objective of earning funds do not establish a unitary or operational connection permitting New Jersey to apportion this gain.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Functional integration, management, and economies of scale identify value a State can fairly share in taxing. The stipulations negate that integration. An operational asset can qualify without a unitary relationship between issuer and holder, but this record does not show that the ASARCO interest served working capital or another identifiable operational function. Spending sale proceeds later in the business is not enough; otherwise every asset would become operational after liquidation. Under Master §16, New Jersey supplies no demonstrated collapse of the territorial rule’s premise or unworkability requiring abandonment. Concrete multistate tax and investment reliance supports continuity. Judicial disagreement over fact-sensitive applications is not proof that the boundary should disappear.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve genuine operational investments and fair-apportionment disputes on a proper tax base. Corporate status neither defeats constitutional tax limits nor creates immunity from state taxation.
