# INS v. Doherty

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before January 15, 1992, as specified in Section I.B.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any actual relevant earlier simulated holdings; no previously approved case position has been supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Second Circuit insofar as it requires reopening for discretionary asylum; vacate its withholding disposition and remand for consideration of the Attorney General's independent statutory-ineligibility ground under the correct distinction between withholding and asylum.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Do not award either protection. The court of appeals must examine the existing administrative exclusion determination under governing review standards; if it cannot sustain that ground, the withholding claim returns for agency adjudication. Destination-selection and detention judgments remain outside this disposition.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Distinct reopening consequences for distinct statutory relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved reopening issue, including its mandatory-versus-discretionary antecedent. Do not decide the undeveloped protection merits.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Abudu's independent grounds and the statutory distinction, without creating a humanitarian exception or changing abuse-of-discretion review.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Attorney General may deny reopening for discretionary asylum on a lawful determination that he would deny that relief, but cannot use that substantive discretion to deny mandatory withholding. A destination-specific withholding claim cannot be rejected simply by importing the separate asylum regulation's explanation requirement. The serious-nonpolitical-crime exclusion requires its own lawful review.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Doherty's tactical choice and serious conduct support refusal of discretionary asylum within §1158. The same considerations are not an extra exception to §1253(h). He had selected Ireland; when the final administrative decision substituted the United Kingdom, protection against that destination acquired a different practical operation. The possibility of that change is not identical to its occurrence, and the agency must apply its actual reopening text rather than a judicially invented requirement to anticipate every administrative reversal. Abudu preserves procedural conditions but expressly differentiates denial of discretionary relief. Stone therefore rejects both automatic entitlement to withholding and an unrestricted procedural veto over it. The extradition determination is relevant history, not an adjudication that the INA exclusion cannot apply.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether the statutory exclusion is established remains for lawful record review. No categorical invalidation of reopening deadlines, no ruling on foreign-policy factors in every asylum case, and no revision of Chevron are necessary. This is a proposed application of eligible law, not adoption of any historical opinion in this matter.

# White v. Illinois

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before January 15, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate relevant effective simulated developments identified in I.B.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment on the limited claim that unavailability was a necessary prerequisite for these accepted hearsay exceptions.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No new trial or evidentiary hearing on this ground. No advisory alteration of state hearsay law.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: No universal production prerequisite for the accepted exceptions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the federal prerequisite question with state-law admissibility fixed. Do not make an unavailability finding from the child's unsuccessful courtroom appearances.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Roberts as qualified by Inadi, extending the latter's rationale to properly admitted spontaneous and medical-treatment statements. This resolves the open exception boundary; it does not replace received confrontation law with Stone's broader preferred approach to testimonial accusation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Constitution does not require a separate production or unavailability showing for these statements merely because the declarant is absent, where their membership in the established exceptions is assumed within the limited review granted.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The preserved contention supplies no reason why trial testimony must precede admission of statements whose pertinent evidentiary characteristic is their earlier setting. Inadi already rejects production as an invariable gateway. The prosecution's admission theory nevertheless remains subject to constitutional reliability review under the governing framework; an exception label cannot make disputed foundational facts true. Here those foundations are the fixed review premise. Coy and Craig require safeguards when the State substitutes a modified method of presenting a trial witness; they do not establish the additional evidentiary prerequisite White seeks. The broader governmental argument that only formal affidavits can implicate confrontation is unnecessary and inconsistent with treating confrontation as a functional protection. Stone does not adopt it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No holding that every police interview is admissible, that childhood always establishes necessity, or that a judge's unsupported reliability judgment replaces confrontation. A case properly disputing the status or constitutional treatment of a formal investigative accusation would require its own record and candid precedent analysis. This narrow decision creates no later testimonial or primary-purpose test and does not decide harmlessness because no error is found on the question resolved.

# Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before January 15, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate actual relevant intervening simulated doctrine.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate both consolidated judgments and remand for reconsideration under Rule 60(b)(5)'s equitable standard.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the decree operative pending lawful modification. The district court determines whether a supported change warrants relief and whether the specific proposed alteration adequately protects the decree's remaining purposes; Stone authorizes no specified number of double cells.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Changed circumstances and continued equitable protection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The refusal to modify presents the standard and the effect of the standard's application, not a new constitutional liability trial.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Clarify Swift in light of its own distinction between accrued rights and future conduct, Rule 60(b)(5), System Federation and United Shoe. Stone proposes a context-specific institutional rule: the movant must establish a material change making continued enforcement inequitable and a modification responsive to that change. That formulation is proposed here, not preexisting simulated law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An institutional consent decree is adjustable on a supported change in law or fact without proof of an unforeseeable catastrophe, but its consent obligations cannot be reduced merely because some lesser protection would be constitutional.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The prospective institutional obligations must be assessed in their own setting rather than by mechanically transferring Swift's fact-specific antitrust formulation. A requirement that every change be unforeseeable would treat risk awareness as a surrender of the court's continuing equitable duty. Actual anticipation and voluntary allocation of that risk nevertheless weigh heavily against relief, and the officials must explain why the undertaken obligation has become inequitable despite their earlier knowledge. Bell alone does not remove a voluntarily accepted single-cell term. Nor can financial pressure excuse unconstitutional conditions. The district court's alternative reliance on single occupancy's centrality does not demonstrate that it assessed the requested accommodation without the restrictive premise; remand permits that assessment without selecting the outcome. The new formulation protects concrete settlement reliance, confines relief to changed circumstances, and preserves the trial judge's experience. It does not overrule Swift's disposition or replace equity with free administrative preference.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether these officials actually meet the standard and whether the proposed design is constitutional remain unresolved factual and equitable questions. Settlement discontent, a new administration, or cheaper lawful alternatives alone do not compel modification.

# Dewsnup v. Timm

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before January 15, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any actual relevant same-term Code holding.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply §§506(a) and (d) consistently and give effect to the established valuation, subject to resolution of any preserved statutory exception or administration issue. Do not invalidate more than the unsupported lien portion or alter personal-discharge rules.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Consistent statutory meaning within §506

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented construction question, not a general policy entitlement to debt relief.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply enacted text and definitions. Distinguish the lien-survival precedents because personal discharge without statutory avoidance is their relevant premise.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 506(d)'s allowed-secured-claim language uses the secured valuation supplied by §506(a); a lien is void to the extent it exceeds that secured claim unless an express statutory exception applies.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Allowance of $120,000 establishes an allowed claim, not that its whole amount remains secured under Congress's valuation language. Reading the adjacent sections together leaves the $39,000 secured component and treats the balance as unsecured for the avoidance provision. The competing reading gives the same phrase materially different work without an express signal. Long and Johnson protect liens from the consequences of personal discharge; neither establishes that a lien is immune from a distinct enacted avoidance provision. Property and transactional reliance are serious, but the Code is the governing source and its explicit exceptions are the administrable boundary. Stone's conclusion changes no Supreme Court holding on this exact provision because none supplies a contrary controlling rule at entry; it interprets the 1978 scheme rather than treating later statutory precedent as already adopted.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No conclusion on reorganization-specific restrictions, wholly unrelated exemptions, or hypothetical future valuation disputes. A fresh start alone supplies no avoidance power, and adverse equity alone would not justify this result if the text read otherwise.

# Wyoming v. Oklahoma

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before January 22, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any subsequently supplied effective simulated rule on state fiscal standing or geographic discrimination.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Exercise original jurisdiction, sustain Wyoming's exception concerning severability, reject Oklahoma's exceptions, and grant Wyoming summary judgment against the Act.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Declare the Act unconstitutional and enjoin its enforcement, retaining jurisdiction for implementation. Do not award tax damages or adjudicate an independently enacted GRDA procurement policy.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Concrete fiscal injury

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Determine jurisdiction before the Commerce Clause merits.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary injury, causation and redressability requirements and original-jurisdiction practice.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A documented reduction in severance taxes caused by the challenged mandatory substitution establishes Wyoming's own injury and supports this sovereign controversy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Wyoming identifies the taxed activity and the law-driven reduction, not merely a hope that its economy would flourish under different policy. An injunction removes the mandated substitution; redressability does not require a guarantee that every buyer returns. The intergovernmental scope and absence of an equivalent single private action support exercising the Court's jurisdiction. No relaxed state-only Article III standard is needed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Generalized future fiscal consequences of unrelated regulation do not suffice under this reasoning.

### Issue 2: Discriminatory purchasing mandate

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statute's validity and necessary severability question after jurisdiction.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the native discrimination rule, with no new balancing test.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Oklahoma cannot compel all covered utilities to substitute a home-state coal quota for interstate purchases on this record; the challenged Act cannot be preserved through a judicial state-utility rewrite.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Origin is the Act's express purchasing criterion. Retaining local revenue is the protectionist advantage the dormant Commerce Clause restricts. Supply reliability is a legitimate concern, but a local quota is not shown necessary when neutral diversification requirements are available. General statutory preservation of state utility regulation does not clearly authorize discrimination. GRDA's ownership raises a distinct participation argument, but the operative enactment regulates an integrated class of public and private generators. Its invalid regulatory program should not be converted into a standalone procurement directive without adequate legislative support. This preserves the participation precedents while resolving the enacted scheme.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No rule against neutral energy regulation or all state purchases; no constitutional decision about a different, independently adopted GRDA policy.

# INS v. Elias-Zacarias

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before January 22, 1992, subject to actual effective Doherty law.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate the reopening standard against the eventual simulated Doherty decision; do not treat either proposed position as approved.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's categorical political-persecution ruling and remand for review of the Board's actual decision under the correct nexus requirement and applicable reopening standard.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require record-specific consideration of actual or imputed political opinion and direct or circumstantial motive evidence. Grant neither asylum nor withholding and make no new administrative factual findings.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Protected opinion requires an individual nexus

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory premise on which the Ninth Circuit displaced the agency; retain the distinction between reviewing reopening and adjudicating an initial application.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the statutory listed-ground requirement; reject both automatic recruitment-based eligibility and an automatic bar to neutrality claims.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Recruitment by a politically motivated group is not necessarily persecution on account of the recruit's political opinion. A refusal may express, or be understood as expressing, a protected opinion, but that connection must be supported by the particular record rather than supplied by a universal presumption.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress protects against persecution for enumerated reasons, not all dangers created by political conflict. The organization's agenda and the applicant's protected ground answer different questions. The testimony about fear of government retaliation can support a practical motive for refusing without conclusively disproving a political belief; the Court should not invent either conclusion. Repeated visits can strengthen the probability of renewed pressure without themselves proving the reason for it. A persecutor's motive may be inferred circumstantially; requiring an explicit declaration would impose an unsupported evidentiary hurdle. The Ninth Circuit's categorical premise nevertheless skips the necessary nexus analysis and therefore cannot sustain its judgment. Remand preserves agency fact assessment and the correct, distinct review of the prima facie reopening determination.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No rule that political neutrality is unprotected, no direct-evidence requirement, and no suggestion that fear must be more likely than not for asylum. The availability of asylum discretion after eligibility and the higher withholding standard remain separate. No general deference doctrine is overruled.

# Presley v. Etowah County Commission

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before January 27, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any actual effective simulated §5 holding before use.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the noncoverage judgments in both No. 90-711 and No. 90-712 and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require §5 preclearance of the Common Fund Resolution and Unit System before their continued implementation on terms the enforcement court lawfully sets. Leave preclearance merits to the designated statutory channels. Do not revisit the unappealed Road Supervision ruling or resolve the remaining §2 or constitutional claims.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Structural removal of district representatives' authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only the two appealed statutory-coverage questions.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Allen's functional coverage principle to substantial structural transfers of established authority attached to elected district office. This resolves an open statutory boundary; it does not overrule an entering Supreme Court holding squarely excluding these transfers.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 5 covers a change that substantially removes a core, independently exercisable function from an elected district representative and relocates that function to an appointed officer or a materially different collective decision structure. The two challenged changes meet that description on this record.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Selection of an official includes selection of the public authority the office carries. Allen's treatment of elective-to-appointive change would be vulnerable to formal evasion if a jurisdiction could retain the elected title while moving the substantial function. Russell transfers road administration to an appointed engineer. Etowah converts the individual representative's established district expenditure authority into pooled majority control. Both alter the institutional connection between a district's vote and exercise of the office's powers; neither is merely an isolated unfavorable vote on an unchanged commission. The statutory purpose and prior functional decisions support this reading, and the Attorney General's position reinforces rather than replaces the textual analysis. The chronological differences caution against finding discriminatory intent here; the statute assigns that inquiry elsewhere.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This is a proposed coverage extension and must be stated narrowly: routine appropriations, individual hiring decisions, and a minority representative losing ordinary policy votes are not covered solely because they affect practical influence. Equal political membership does not guarantee proportional spending or legislative victories. The trigger is a structural change to substantial authority associated with the elected office relative to the statutory benchmark. No proof burden is shifted beyond §5's existing coverage and preclearance sequence, and no discrimination finding is made.

# Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Immediately before January 27, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any actual effective simulated agency-review decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse enforcement of the Board's nonemployee-access order.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Set aside the order insofar as it requires access on these facts; do not authorize interference with employee organizing or lawful public-property advocacy.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Babcock's access exception

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory access rule and its application to the established nondiscriminatory policy.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Babcock and distinguish employee-rights cases. No replacement of Chevron or recognition of a general constitutional right to enter private retail property is necessary.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** General balancing of organizational advantage against a modest property burden cannot replace Babcock's requirement that nonemployee organizers show reasonable offsite communication is ineffective because employees are practically inaccessible.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Employee self-organization is a statutory right; organizer entry is derivative and limited. The Board's expertise matters in determining actual access obstacles, but the existing judicial rule fixes the legal threshold. This workforce lives in the surrounding community. Organizers used public approaches and obtained a substantial number of home contacts; the low card return does not show that communication was unavailable. The record lacks the location-based isolation needed to override the uniformly enforced exclusion rule. Treating a more productive parking-lot method as enough would erase Babcock's separation of employee solicitation from nonemployee entry. Property rights do not categorically defeat §7, but the statutory accommodation is triggered by demonstrated necessity rather than a free-floating judicial or agency preference.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Employees isolated by their living or working location, discriminatory exclusion, and employees' own solicitation remain governed by their distinct rules. No requirement that the union exhaust every imaginable contact method is adopted. Stone's preference for independently examining legal meaning operates within the controlling statutory precedents here and supplies no occasion to overrule the general administrative-deference framework.
