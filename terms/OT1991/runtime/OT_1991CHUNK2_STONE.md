# Estelle v. McGuire

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible law immediately before December 4, 1991.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any material effective simulated *Griffin* rule before the event.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand for consideration of whether the unconstitutional instruction was harmless beyond reasonable doubt.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject evidence-alone and state-law grounds. Do not direct acquittal or immediate unconditional release. If the State fails its harmless-error burden, the habeas court should grant a conditional writ requiring a lawful retrial or release through §2254.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The evidence had a lawful nonidentity use

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply the federal habeas boundary and assess admissibility's constitutional consequence.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Winship*, *Lisenba*, and the narrow fundamental-fairness standard.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Evidence of repeated nonaccidental injuries may establish intentional harm without identifying the abuser; its admission for that purpose does not itself violate due process.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The prosecution still had to prove a criminal killing. McGuire's litigation choice did not remove that burden. The inference to nonaccident is not an inference to this father's identity, however, so legitimate admission does not validate every instruction about use.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve a general constitutional propensity prohibition; no new rule is required on collateral review.

### Issue 2: The instruction risks replacing the required identity finding

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the preserved instructional claim under established elemental-proof law.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Francis*, *Sandstrom*, and whole-charge review, using *Boyde*'s reasonable-likelihood approach to ambiguity.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** McGuire establishes a reasonable likelihood that the jury treated the charge as attributing the earlier serious injuries to him and directing an inference to the charged killing without independently finding the required facts.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The charge grouped known rough handling with grave injuries of unidentified origin under an introduction describing the defendant's other acts. It then described a logical conclusion from earlier offense to present guilt. The word “if” is the warden's strongest answer, but embedded in that sequence it does not clearly return attribution to the jury. The general bad-character prohibition does not necessarily cure the specific suggested identity conclusion. Stone does not find McGuire innocent or deny the sufficiency of circumstantial evidence. He finds a defect in how the jury was directed to use that evidence, under protections already supplied by *Winship* and *Francis*. That ordinary application requires no *Teague* exception for a novel rule. Under *Rose*, the error remains subject to harmless review, and the State bears that separate showing beyond reasonable doubt. The appellate court should decide it on the complete record.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No automatic reversal for ambiguity, no shifted burden on guilt, and no structural-error classification is proposed.

# Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law immediately before December 4, 1991.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any relevant adopted *MCorp* channel rule, preserving statutory differences.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of summary judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit ordinary determination of status and any viable injury claims. No damages or status finding now; apply statutory credit if overlapping recovery arises.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The exclusion and the actual work govern

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the categorical and preclusion defenses, not whether Gizoni is ultimately a seaman.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Wilander* and the LHWCA's enacted crew exclusion.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A listed ship-repair occupation does not foreclose crew status, and unadjudicated benefit receipt does not bar a Jones Act action.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An occupational definition with an explicit exception must be read with that exception intact. Gizoni's platform work, towing participation, and claimed assignment present a legally relevant connection; absence of propulsion or ordinary navigation work does not answer every part of *Wilander*. The factfinder must assess the vessel and the relationship. Administrative priority cannot be imported from a different statute merely because two remedial systems intersect. Congress anticipated movement between these claims and provided credit for overlapping payments. Requiring workers to refuse immediate compensation to preserve a potentially proper claim would add an election rule the enacted structure does not supply.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No presumption that every repair worker is a seaman, no elimination of summary judgment on a conclusive record, and no holding about final adjudications or settlements.

# Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible law immediately before December 4, 1991.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit on both reviewed questions.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the union-constitution claim for proceedings under §301 and permit a jury on the LMRDA legal damages claim. No liability or amount awarded.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Compensation for lost referrals is legal relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the forum's jury obligation before trial.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Terry*.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Wooddell's claim for wages lost from referrals allegedly withheld is compensatory and carries a Seventh Amendment jury right despite accompanying equitable requests.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** He seeks replacement for earnings allegedly lost through retaliation, not return of particular funds held by the union. There is no former job to which this backpay is merely incidental restitution. The asserted interference resembles a personal-injury claim, and the remedy's legal character confirms the jury entitlement.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Equitable relief and statutory limits retain separate treatment; a jury right proves no violation.

### Issue 2: An individual may enforce a qualifying interunion contract

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the categorical plaintiff exclusion, leaving contractual merits open.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply §301's text and *Local 334* with *Evening News*.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 301 reaches a member-beneficiary's claim alleging breach of the international-local constitution described here.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The text selects the contract's parties, not the identity of every permissible plaintiff. Beneficiaries do not change the instrument's interunion character. Uniform interpretation performs the same statutory work when a beneficiary enforces the promise as when a union does. Fear of trivial cases cannot add an organizational-plaintiff restriction Congress omitted.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No federalization of every internal dispute, no holding on standalone local bylaws, and no new cause of action beyond the enacted contract channel.

# Gibson v. Florida Bar

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible law immediately before December 4, 1991.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse insofar as the judgment approves delayed protection and mandatory issue-by-issue objections; preserve the interest correction and reject a categorical objection to Bar-member arbitrators alone.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for prospective relief requiring adequate advance explanation and protection of reasonably disputed sums before use, with a general objection sufficient. Permit a lawful reduction or escrow system; do not award unpreserved retroactive damages.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Timing and scope of an objection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the continuing financing procedure, not the merits of every lobbying position.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Keller*, *Hudson*, *Ellis*, and *Abood* without overruling the integrated-bar or germane-financing baseline.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A known dissenter cannot be required to repeat objections to every position while disputed money remains available for ideological use until each objection arrives.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The rule begins protection after publication and objection, although annual dues have already been collected. Interest compensates a time value but does not undo compelled financing. Budget estimates and a reasonably sufficient protected amount address uncertain future expenditures without requiring exact advance prediction. A general objection states the constitutionally relevant position; the Bar must justify chargeability. Repeated 45-day waivers recreate the monitoring burden rejected in *Abood*. This is the existing native funding doctrine, not a new speech test or a prohibition on the Bar speaking.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Germaneness remains activity-specific; precise accounting and prospective injunction terms belong on remand. No universal opt-in requirement is adopted.

### Issue 2: Impartial review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the asserted facial disqualification based solely on membership.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply *Hudson*'s prompt, impartial-review requirement.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Bar membership alone does not establish that every panel selected through this balanced procedure is constitutionally biased.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Unlike unilateral institutional selection, each side selects a member and neither alone controls the chair. An indirect shared dues interest is not automatically equivalent to a direct stake in a particular award. Actual bias or obstructive appointment delay remains reviewable; the rule's reporting period does not license indefinite delay before constitution.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No approval of an actually biased panel or a procedure that fails in practice.

# Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible law immediately before December 10, 1991; revalidate an effective *Gibson* ruling if materially broader than bar financing.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Second Circuit.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Declare the challenged speech-dependent escrow requirement unconstitutional and remand for appropriate declaratory and injunctive relief against its enforcement. No award to Hill or discharge of victim claims.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A lawful compensation objective does not justify this speech classification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the First Amendment challenge necessary to resolve the Board's demand.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing content-based financial-burden scrutiny; no new capacity test is necessary.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 632-a's selection of crime-related expression for a special escrow obligation fails the governing narrow-tailoring requirement.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The obligation arises because of what the work recounts. It restrains compensation to the source and therefore affects the publisher's ability to obtain expression even without suppressive intent. Victim compensation and recovery of criminal proceeds are substantial and, at their strongest, compelling interests, but they do not explain why other assets escape this particular burden. The law reaches an entire covered contract despite incidental crime discussion and includes admissions never prosecuted. Excluding victimless crimes and allowing limited expenses do not cure that mismatch. Neutral attachment, restitution, and asset recovery demonstrate the distinction between securing compensation and disfavoring a subject. The State has not justified the additional content discrimination.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No right to retain stolen property; no protection from generally applicable judgments; no rule that every regulation affecting paid expression receives this scrutiny. The classification and operation here trigger it.

# Ardestani v. INS

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible law immediately before December 10, 1991.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of this administrative EAJA award.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No fee award under §504 for this proceeding; leave the asylum grant and its factual basis undisturbed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The enacted cross-reference controls coverage

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide coverage before fee merits.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Read EAJA together with the INA and *Marcello*; no overruling or new immunity doctrine.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An administrative deportation proceeding governed by the INA rather than §554 is not an adjudication under §554 within EAJA §504.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A specific statutory cross-reference ordinarily identifies legal governance, not every procedure resembling the referenced statute. Congress made INA procedures exclusive, and *Marcello* resolves their relationship to the APA. Administrative regulations cannot enlarge Congress's fee waiver by making hearings look more alike. EAJA's remedial purpose gives a strong reason for Congress to extend coverage, but does not erase its chosen boundary. The statutory structure resolves the issue before any residual waiver-construction canon is needed; narrow construction must not become a license to disregard otherwise clear coverage. *Hudson* involved proceedings integral to a covered court action and supplies no corresponding predicate here.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No assessment that INS acted reasonably, no restriction on a properly covered judicial EAJA claim, and no decision about any separately presented constitutional claim.

# Union Bank v. Wolas

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Eligible law immediately before December 11, 1991.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the Ninth Circuit to review whether these payments satisfy each statutory element, with the Bank retaining the exception burden.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Debt maturity is not an additional exclusion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve categorical eligibility only.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the amended statutory text and *Ron Pair*.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Payments on long-term debt may qualify under the then-current §547(c)(2).
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress removed an express timing restriction and retained three specific ordinary-course requirements. A court cannot recreate a broader maturity bar because a legislature may have focused on a narrower motivating example. Equal distribution is important, but exceptions necessarily permit some transfers to stand; preserving normal transactions also discourages premature collection. The two policies coexist in the enacted structure. Neither justifies disregarding the words. The bankruptcy court's findings do not require this Court to settle challenges that the appellate court did not address under its categorical rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No presumption that long-term payments are ordinary, no exemption for all interest or commitment fees, and no reduction of the creditor's statutory burden. No precedent is overruled; the circuit construction is rejected.
