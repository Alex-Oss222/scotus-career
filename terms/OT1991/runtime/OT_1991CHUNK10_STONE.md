# Chemical Waste Management, Inc. v. Hunt

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I, subject to actual simulated revalidation.
* **Conditions:** The draft Quill position and hypothetical Wyoming outcome are not precedent.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment sustaining the additional out-of-state charge and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require relief from the discriminatory exaction through the appropriate state remedial proceedings, consistent with McKesson where applicable. Do not prescribe a refund amount or invalidate independent provisions not necessary to this judgment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Equal treatment of equivalent disposal hazards

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the facially discriminatory additional fee and necessary remedy; reserve independent volume limits.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Philadelphia, Hunt, and Maine v. Taylor without changing their burdens.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Alabama has not justified this origin-based additional charge by a relevant difference in risk or the inadequacy of reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The hazards are serious, but the record identifies the waste's origin rather than a different hazard as the classification's operative basis. Every disposed ton uses capacity and creates potential future costs. Alabama can address those mechanisms through evenhanded disposal charges or appropriately drawn capacity and safety rules; transportation burdens can be addressed in terms of actual transportation rather than origin alone. These examples test the State's asserted absence of adequate alternatives; they are not judicial commands to enact particular regulations. Unlike the distinctive parasite and inspection problem established in Maine v. Taylor, Alabama's argument does not explain why equivalent Alabama waste is spared the extra burden. Nor has a corresponding intrastate tax of substantial equivalence been established merely by pointing to residents' general contributions. The State therefore fails the governing justification, without any new demand for perfect empirical proof or a least-imaginable-alternative test.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve neutral base charges, actual risk classifications, lawful capacity restrictions, and other provisions outside the presented challenge. Relief must correct the discrimination without creating an unlimited right to deposit hazardous waste.

# Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill, Inc. v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I, subject to actual simulated revalidation.
* **Conditions:** No same-day proposal is treated as a Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment dismissing the Commerce Clause challenge and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit appropriately tailored declaratory and prospective relief against the county-origin exclusion. Leave separate neutral permitting, safety, capacity, and planning requirements for lawful administration.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A county boundary does not cure origin discrimination

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the challenged import restriction; do not decide an entitlement to unconditional facility operation.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing discrimination branch, including its justification burden, without enlarging the market-participant exception.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Michigan cannot sustain this restriction merely because other Michigan counties share the exclusion, and the asserted planning interests do not justify reserving private disposal capacity by origin on this record.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Interstate suppliers are denied access because their waste originates outside the receiving county. The same disability imposed on some Michigan suppliers does not dissolve that classification. Brimmer and Dean Milk prevent the constitutional boundary from depending on whether the protectionist geographical unit is statewide or smaller. Planning for safe, adequate disposal is legitimate, but origin does not identify a different disposal hazard or capacity cost. Neutral quantity limits, facility standards, and appropriate charges address those mechanisms while preserving a functioning planning system. Respondents have not shown why an origin veto is necessary to that system. These are applications of the existing justification inquiry, not a requirement that counties produce novel scientific studies before every regulation. Because government is regulating this private facility, the public-service character of planning does not make the State a market participant in the transaction.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Preserve lawful public facilities and their distinct market-participant analysis, evenhanded regulation, and any genuinely applicable congressional authorization. Do not require proof of statewide total exclusion or invent a universal duty for a private operator to accept waste.

# Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I, subject to actual simulated revalidation.
* **Conditions:** No preemption result from another draft is assumed effective.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm protection against the threatened enforcement of the identified fare-advertising requirements; reverse the broader portion and remand to narrow the injunction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Tie the injunction to the concrete fare-guideline controversy. Do not enjoin every future consumer-law action touching an airline or issue advisory rules for unthreatened practices.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Apply the enacted relation without making it limitless

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the actual fare requirements and necessary statutory reconciliation. Reserve unrelated practices and constitutional speech claims.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Interpret the express clause within this statutory scheme; use Shaw as an analogy, not as an imported field-preemption regime.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** These binding requirements governing advertised fares, their availability, and mandatory terms relate directly to rates within § 1305(a)(1), even when enforced through a general consumer statute.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The challenged guidelines prescribe how a carrier must present and qualify the price at which it offers transportation. That connection is intrinsic to the actual rule, without assuming that every compliance cost affects fares enough for preemption. Congress's inclusion of standards having the force of law reaches enforcement as well as enactment, so the general statutory vehicle cannot eliminate the particular norm's direct relation. The savings clause retains a real role for remedies outside the expressly displaced category; reading it to preserve all preexisting enforcement regardless of subject would deprive the later preemption language of effect. Conversely, the federal unfair-practices provision does not by itself prove a total bar to state consumer remedies. This analysis proposes no overruling or freestanding economic-effects threshold.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general immunity for fraud, breach of voluntarily undertaken obligations, or unrelated injury. Their actual duties and statutory relationship remain to be examined when presented.

### Issue 2: Match the injunction to the dispute

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the breadth of the entered injunction.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the established equitable requirement of a concrete threatened injury and appropriately bounded relief.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The threatened fare enforcement warrants prospective protection, but unthreatened practices cannot be swept into the decree merely by repeating the statutory words.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The announced fare enforcement and potential repeated sanctions make waiting a materially inadequate route to review. The same record does not establish impending enforcement across the full range of advertising and airline operations covered by the decree. Narrowing preserves the relief justified by the actual controversy and leaves later disputes to their records.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No determination that every future enforcement threat warrants an injunction or that preemption automatically supplies damages.

# Burdick v. Takushi

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I, particularly actual Norman revalidation.
* **Conditions:** No historical or proposed same-term election holding is presumed effective.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand on the electoral-choice claim, rejecting the broader right to an official tally of arbitrary protest messages.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require relief from the complete write-in prohibition as applied within this system, with the decree confined to votes for legally eligible candidates. Remand for a workable prospective remedy; do not dictate new statewide filing, certification, or counting rules or independently invalidate the other access provisions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The complete prohibition requires justification for its actual reach

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach both primary and general-election applications of the ban. Consider the surrounding rules as evidence of burden, without deciding independent challenges to them.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Anderson's calibrated assessment. Do not create an absolute write-in right or treat every election rule as strict scrutiny.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Hawaii's complete ban materially burdens the electoral choice shown here, and its stated interests do not sufficiently justify that ban's full reach within this system.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Fifteen signatures make initial filing accessible, an important fact favoring Hawaii. But access cannot be measured solely at that point: the primary-ticket choice and advancement requirements condition how an alternative reaches the election in which voters choose the officeholder. Burdick faced an actual single-candidate contest and sought to support someone else. This is a substantial electoral burden, even though the Constitution does not guarantee a competitive contest or permit every late candidacy. Anderson therefore requires examining what the complete ban contributes to the stated interests; a presumption based only on the independent validity of other access rules would omit the challenged restriction from the assessment. Sore-loser prevention is legitimate but cannot explain a primary ban, and in the general election it addresses only a subset of excluded candidates. Primary raiding is also legitimate; nothing in this record explains why barring all write-ins, including genuine support for a candidate on the selected primary ticket, is needed to address it. Where state law eliminates a general election for an unopposed primary winner, there is no general-election ballot to write on; that provision does not justify forbidding alternative votes in an election actually held. These fit problems defeat the asserted justification under Anderson's record-specific balance without requiring a new categorical scrutiny tier or legislative proof by scientific study. The remedy must not become an opportunity to select a person legally ineligible under an independently valid qualification rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reject the claimed constitutional entitlement to have votes for fictional figures counted and publicized as political messages. Reserve reasonable write-in procedures, valid candidate qualifications, narrow anti-raiding and sore-loser rules, and other States' materially different systems. No precedent is overruled and no universal write-in mandate is proposed.

# Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I, subject to actual simulated revalidation.
* **Conditions:** No unapproved market or causation proposal is treated as precedent.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the reversal of summary judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow further proceedings on the properly supported claims, with plaintiffs retaining proof of all elements and defenses considered on their evidence. Award no damages or final injunction now.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Actual market constraints govern the inference

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the asserted categorical defense and the evidence supporting the challenged tying elements at summary judgment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Rule 56 and the received tying doctrine, without adopting a presumption that every proprietary aftermarket is a monopoly.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Equipment-market competition does not, by itself, entitle Kodak to judgment against record-supported aftermarket claims; respondents have enough evidence of distinct demand, conditioning, and power to proceed.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Kodak's theory identifies a real potential constraint: prospective buyers can compare equipment systems and punish high lifetime prices. Its conclusive form, however, assumes that buyers can obtain and use the relevant information and readily escape aftermarket terms. Respondents' evidence concerning costly replacement of installed equipment, imperfect service-cost information, and actual price and exclusion changes permits a contrary inference. The separately supplied service and self-servicing exceptions support distinct demand and contested conditioning, rather than a judicial assumption that parts and service necessarily form one product. Matsushita does not compel rejection of a plausible factual mechanism merely because a simpler economic model predicts a different result. That conclusion preserves rational inference review while leaving actual market definition and the strength of the constraint for proof.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding that Kodak has monopoly power, no presumption from patents or corporate size, and no immunity for unsupported allegations. Plaintiffs must establish the remaining tying elements and appropriate injury.

### Issue 2: Exclusion and legitimate explanations remain factual questions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the § 2 basis for the motion; do not merge monopolization and attempted monopolization.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Grinnell and Aspen Skiing to the challenged changes and asserted business reasons.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The power and exclusion evidence creates genuine disputes that the offered justifications do not conclusively eliminate on this record.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Control of compatible parts plus limits on suppliers and independent access can maintain service power if the claimed market and effects are proved. The continuing self-service exception and evidence of independent service practices provide a basis to test the claim that excluding independent organizations is necessary for quality, inventory, or preventing free riding. Stone does not find those reasons pretextual; he concludes that their factual force has not been established so conclusively as to end the action. Success through better equipment remains lawful, and attempt retains its separate intent and probability requirements. The Master's market discipline supports this distinction between actual competitive harm and business status without revising the governing elements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve ultimate markets, liability, antitrust injury, damages, and remedial design. No general duty to deal or compulsory parts-supply decree follows from this judgment.

# United States v. Thompson/Center Arms Co.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I, subject particularly to actual R. L. C. revalidation.
* **Conditions:** No proposed criminal-construction rule is assumed adopted.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the refund judgment on a narrower ground than a universal actual-assembly requirement.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The $200 making tax is not owed for this unassembled dual-use unit. No blanket exemption or injunction governing other kits is granted.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Resolve the package's remaining statutory ambiguity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the making definition and necessary lenity issue; do not decide a constitutional right to possess the regulated configuration.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Use text, defined terms, and statutory structure before applying Crandon's uniform construction. Do not overrule a controlling precedent or create an evidentiary presumption against tax collection.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Act does not clearly classify this genuinely dual-use pistol and conversion package as a short-barreled rifle before that configuration is made; the remaining criminally consequential ambiguity is resolved in the manufacturer's favor.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Government correctly identifies a difficulty in the Federal Circuit's broad rule: “manufacturing” and “otherwise producing” need not wait for the last mechanical connection. Stone therefore does not hold that a complete kit usable only as an NFA firearm is outside the Act. This package, however, contains a lawful long barrel and permits a pistol or long rifle as well as the short-rifle configuration. The rifle definition's shoulder-firing terms and Congress's more explicit parts language in other categories leave uncertainty about when this particular lawful-use collection becomes the defined firearm. The structure supports regulation of real firearm manufacture but does not supply the missing boundary by a general anti-evasion purpose. After those tools, the same classification would expose unapproved making or unregistered possession to criminal consequences. Crandon warrants the narrow reading in the civil action as well, rather than one meaning for taxation and another for punishment. The lawful configurations are supplied facts, not a hypothetical excuse invented to avoid the statute.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual short-rifle assembly, parts usable only for a regulated firearm, sham lawful configurations, and separate offenses remain outside this holding. Do not create a new intent-to-assemble element for every NFA category, alter a criminal proof burden, or require a new storage protocol.

# Sochor v. Florida

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I, subject to actual Stringer, Griffin, and Estelle revalidation.
* **Conditions:** The proposed remedy relies on surviving Clemons and Parker, not an invented earlier simulated capital holding.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment sustaining the death sentence and remand for constitutionally adequate correction of the coldness error.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Florida must conduct adequately supported Chapman review, undertake lawful reweighing if its law permits that function, or provide resentencing. Do not disturb the convictions, prescribe a life sentence, or order a new penalty jury irrespective of an adequate lawful alternative.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Honor the preserved boundary

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decline jurisdiction over the jury-instruction vagueness claim because of the clear, adequate, independent preservation ground. Reach the separate judicial application.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Long and the existing capital-narrowing precedents.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The barred instruction issue cannot support relief here; the judge's heinousness finding is not unconstitutional as applied to conscious-victim strangulation under the relevant Florida narrowing construction.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An alternative merits rejection does not remove the independent procedural basis. No supported challenge to the adequacy or independence of that basis has been established. As to the judge, the record identifies fear, conscious suffering, and strangulation, a consistently recognized application supplying the limiting guidance Walton permits judges to use. Inconsistency alleged in different applications does not establish that this judge operated without a relevant boundary. Stone thus rejects this particular judicial-application challenge without approving the bare words for every jury or every crime.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve other factual applications, the merits of the unpreserved instruction challenge, and any properly supported future challenge to a procedural ground.

### Issue 2: Require correction of the error actually established

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the judge's express reliance on coldness and appellate cure. Reserve whether the jury independently committed an additional coldness error, because that question is unnecessary to the same required remand.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Clemons, Parker, and Chapman without creating a mandatory phrase or automatic-reversal rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The state court's existing explanation does not establish lawful reweighing or that the judge's use of the invalid factor was harmless beyond a reasonable doubt.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Florida's authoritative decision removes heightened premeditation as a supported aggravator, while the sentencing order affirmatively used it. The error is therefore documented, unlike a guess about the advisory jury's undisclosed findings. Three valid factors and no found mitigation may support a powerful harmlessness argument, but counting does not show that attributing an additional kind of culpability made no difference to the weighing. The state paragraph chiefly compares proportionality with other cases and does not explain that contribution; its mixed citations do not make the missing inquiry clear. Stone requires the substantive review received law demands, not recitation of “harmless error” or judicial findings beyond the appellate function. This justifies returning the matter to the state court while leaving it lawful remedial choices. The State retains Chapman's burden; Stone does not silently substitute a later habeas harmlessness standard.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No need to decide the unresolved general-verdict branch, whether every invalid aggravator requires a new jury, or capital punishment's general validity. A properly supported harmlessness finding or authorized reweighing remains available; the present explanation does not establish one.
