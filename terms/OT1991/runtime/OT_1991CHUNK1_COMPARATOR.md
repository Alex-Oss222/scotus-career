# CASE 1 of 10: UNITED STATES v. IBARRA
# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

*Comparison material only. Not simulated law; does not control Stone.*

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Certiorari granted, respondent's in forma pauperis motion granted, judgment of the Tenth Circuit vacated and remanded. Per curiam, no recorded dissent, eight Members participating.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Single per curiam opinion with three footnotes, the second rejecting the tolling characterization and the third reserving bad faith and successive motions.
* **Historical Holding:** Healy established that a motion for rehearing in a criminal case renders an otherwise final district court decision nonfinal until the motion is decided; Dieter rejected an exception for motions not asserting error of law; the Tenth Circuit's further exception for motions resting on previously abandoned grounds is likewise rejected, because a merits inquiry cannot be grafted onto a bright-line jurisdictional inquiry and no reliable way exists to sort meritorious from unmeritorious reconsideration motions in advance.
* **Historical Authority:** Healy, 376 U.S. 75; Dieter, 429 U.S. 6; Pink, 317 U.S. 264.
* **Historical Sources:** 502 U.S. 1; U.S. Reports volume 502.

# CASE 2 of 10: MIRELES v. WACO
# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

*Comparison material only. Not simulated law; does not control Stone.*

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Certiorari granted and judgment of the Ninth Circuit summarily reversed. Per curiam for six Members; Justice Stevens dissenting; Justice Scalia dissenting, joined by Justice Kennedy. Eight Members participating.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Per curiam with two footnotes, the first collecting the recognized limits on judicial immunity and the second setting out the California statutory powers. Stevens dissent on the merits of the judicial-capacity question. Scalia dissent on the propriety of summary disposition, stating that the decision below was at a minimum not clearly in error, that he was unsure which disposition was correct, and that he would have denied the petition.
* **Historical Holding:** Judicial immunity is overcome only by nonjudicial actions or by actions taken in the complete absence of all jurisdiction, and not by allegations of bad faith or malice. A judge's direction to court officers to bring a person in the courthouse before him is a function normally performed by a judge. Although a direction to carry out a judicial order with excessive force is not itself such a function, the relevant inquiry is the particular act's relation to a general function normally performed by a judge, here directing officers to bring counsel in a pending case before the court. The officers' executive character does not convert the direction into executive action. Authorizing and ratifying excessive force was action in excess of authority but, taken in aid of jurisdiction over a pending matter, was not taken in the absence of jurisdiction.
* **Historical Authority:** Stump, 435 U.S. 349; Forrester, 484 U.S. 219; Pierson, 386 U.S. 547; Mitchell, 472 U.S. 511; Bradley, 13 Wall. 335; Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S. 478.
* **Historical Sources:** 502 U.S. 9; U.S. Reports volume 502.

# CASE 3 of 10: CAMPOS v. CITY OF HOUSTON (IN CHAMBERS)
# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

*Comparison material only. Not simulated law; does not control Stone.*

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Application denied by Justice Scalia as Circuit Justice, acting alone. Single-Justice in-chambers disposition; no vote of the Court; not referred.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Short in-chambers opinion, first addressing the requested original order, then the lesser stay relief under the Ripon Society factors.
* **Historical Holding:** There is no basis in law for an original order stopping an entire city election process, as the amicus United States pointed out, and the application must be denied on that ground. On the alternative stay request, the Circuit Justice was doubtful, like the court of appeals, of the district court's authority to issue the order, but found no reason to believe the City acted in bad faith in the sense of seeking to frustrate the purposes of the Voting Rights Act, though it may have engaged in overimaginative lawyering; found that the applicants and the United States shared some responsibility by their delay for the matter being presented and decided in inordinate haste; and treated as determinative the uncertainty whether more good than harm to the public interest would follow from staying the order and making the imminent elections impossible, absentee ballots having already been cast, relying on the judgment of the federal judges on the scene who had declined a stay.
* **Historical Authority:** Republican State Central Committee of Arizona v. Ripon Society Inc., 409 U.S. 1222, 1224 (1972) (Rehnquist, J., in chambers).
* **Historical Sources:** 502 U.S. 1301; U.S. Reports volume 502.

**Later history, comparator only:** The Fifth Circuit subsequently held that the district court had power to grant some form of interim relief but abused its discretion in ordering the election under the objected-to plan, vacated the October 12 order, and remanded, noting that the parties had not asked it to set aside the November 5 election results. That decision postdates the event date and is quarantined here.

# CASE 4 of 10: ZATKO v. CALIFORNIA
# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

*Comparison material only. Not simulated law; does not control Stone.*

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Motions denied. Per curiam for six Members; Justice Stevens dissenting, joined by Justice Blackmun; Justice Thomas took no part.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Per curiam invoking Rule 39.8 for the first time, followed by the Stevens dissent with four footnotes.
* **Historical Holding:** Rule 39.8 is invoked for the first time to deny in forma pauperis status to two petitioners who have repeatedly abused the integrity of the Court's process through frequent frivolous filings, their pattern of repetitious filing having resulted in an extreme abuse of the system. The Court expressly noted that it had not applied the rule to the many other frivolous in forma pauperis petitions to which it might technically apply, denying those in the usual manner, and described its action as a limited step censuring two petitioners who are unique among both in forma pauperis and paying filers. Petitioners were allowed until November 25, 1991 to pay the Rule 38 docketing fee and submit petitions complying with Rule 33, and the Court stated that future similar filings from these petitioners will merit additional measures.
* **Historical Authority:** In re Amendment to Rule 39, 500 U.S. 13 (1991); In re Sindram, 498 U.S. 177 (1991).
* **Historical Sources:** 502 U.S. 16; U.S. Reports volume 502.

# CASE 5 of 10: HAFER v. MELO
# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

*Comparison material only. Not simulated law; does not control Stone.*

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed. Unanimous among the eight participating Members; Justice Thomas took no part.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Single opinion of the Court by Justice O'Connor, joined by all other participating Members. No concurrence or dissent.
* **Historical Holding:** State officials sued in their individual capacities are persons for purposes of § 1983. Will's language about officials acting in their official capacities addressed official-capacity suits, in which the real party in interest is the entity, and cannot be read to bar damages actions against officers personally; Will's Eleventh Amendment discussion does not establish the limits of liability, since that case arose in state court and reached the Amendment only because congressional intent not to override state immunity bore on statutory construction. The contention that acts within an official's authority and necessary to governmental functions are acts of the State incapable of supporting a personal-capacity action was rejected. Concerns that personal liability may hamper the performance of public duties are properly addressed within personal immunity doctrine. The Eleventh Amendment does not bar personal-capacity damages suits, and state officers are not absolutely immune from personal liability under § 1983 solely by virtue of the official nature of their acts.
* **Historical Authority:** Will, 491 U.S. 58; Kentucky v. Graham, 473 U.S. 159; Scheuer v. Rhodes, 416 U.S. 232; Forrester v. White, 484 U.S. 219.
* **Historical Sources:** 502 U.S. 21; U.S. Reports volume 502.

# CASE 6 of 10: BOARD OF GOVERNORS OF THE FEDERAL RESERVE SYSTEM v. MCORP FINANCIAL, INC.
# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

*Comparison material only. Not simulated law; does not control Stone.*

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No. 90-913 reversed; No. 90-914 affirmed. Unanimous among the eight participating Members; Justice Thomas took no part.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Single opinion of the Court by Justice Stevens, joined by all other participating Members. No concurrence or dissent.
* **Historical Holding:** The district court lacked jurisdiction to enjoin either regulatory proceeding. The litigation is controlled by § 1818(i)(1)'s plain, preclusive language, which is not qualified or superseded by the Bankruptcy Code's automatic stay; the Board's planned actions fall squarely within § 362(b)(4), and §§ 362(a)(3) and (a)(6) have no application to ongoing, nonfinal administrative proceedings; reliance on 28 U.S.C. § 1334(b) is misplaced because the Board is not another court and because prosecution of the proceedings before a final order and any enforcement action seems unlikely to impair the Bankruptcy Court's exclusive jurisdiction over estate property. The Court of Appeals erred in reading Leedom v. Kyne to authorize review of the source of strength regulation, because, unlike in Kyne, § 1818(h)(2) expressly provides a meaningful and adequate opportunity for review of the regulation's validity and application if and when the Board finds a violation, and § 1818(i)(1) clearly and directly demonstrates a congressional intent to preclude review.
* **Historical Authority:** 12 U.S.C. § 1818(h)(2), (i)(1); 11 U.S.C. § 362(b)(4); 28 U.S.C. § 1334; Leedom v. Kyne, 358 U.S. 184.
* **Historical Sources:** 502 U.S. 32; U.S. Reports volume 502.

# CASE 7 of 10: GRIFFIN v. UNITED STATES
# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

*Comparison material only. Not simulated law; does not control Stone.*

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed. Seven Members joined the opinion of the Court; one Member concurred in the judgment; Justice Thomas took no part.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Justice Scalia, joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter. Justice Blackmun filed an opinion concurring in the judgment, agreeing that no due process violation was made out but declining to follow the Court's survey of the common law, emphasizing the danger of jury confusion in the multiple-defendant 23-count trial, noting that the Government could have charged the objects in separate counts or agreed to special interrogatories, and commending those techniques for complex conspiracy prosecutions.
* **Historical Holding:** Neither the Due Process Clause of the Fifth Amendment nor the Court's precedents require, in a federal prosecution, that a general guilty verdict on a multiple-object conspiracy be set aside if the evidence is inadequate to support conviction as to one of the objects. Historical practice fails to support the due process claim, the rule applied below having been a settled feature of the common law. The governing precedent is not Yates, which invalidated a general verdict where one possible basis was legally inadequate, but Turner, which upheld a general verdict where one possible basis was supported by inadequate evidence. The line between them makes good sense because jurors are not generally equipped to determine whether a theory of conviction is contrary to law but are well equipped to determine whether it is supported by the facts. Although it would generally be preferable to instruct the jury to disregard an alternative basis lacking adequate evidentiary support, refusal to do so is not an independent basis for reversing an otherwise valid conviction.
* **Historical Authority:** Turner, 396 U.S. 398; Yates, 354 U.S. 298; Stromberg, 283 U.S. 359; Claassen, 142 U.S. 140; Schad, 501 U.S. 624.
* **Historical Sources:** 502 U.S. 46; U.S. Reports volume 502.

# CASE 8 of 10: ESTELLE v. McGUIRE
# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

*Comparison material only. Not simulated law; does not control Stone.*

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed. Six Members joined the opinion of the Court in full; two Members joined only Part I and dissented in part; Justice Thomas took no part.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Chief Justice Rehnquist, joined by Justices White, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter, and joined as to Part I by Justices Stevens and O'Connor. Justice O'Connor filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Stevens, agreeing that the battered child evidence was relevant and probative of causation and intent, but concluding that there was a reasonable likelihood the jury misapplied the prior acts instruction, that the instruction encouraged the jury to assume respondent inflicted the prior injuries and then directed it to conclude that the prior abuser was the murderer, that it may therefore have relieved the State of proving identity beyond a reasonable doubt, and that the instruction was error requiring remand to the Court of Appeals for a determination of harmlessness.
* **Historical Holding:** Neither the admission of the challenged evidence nor the jury instruction as to its use rises to the level of a due process violation. The prior injury evidence, although not linked to respondent himself, was probative on the question of the intent with which the person who caused the injuries acted, demonstrating that the death resulted from an intentional act by someone rather than an accident, and the absence of a claim of accident did not relieve the prosecution of its burden on every element. It was improper for the Court of Appeals to rest on its conclusion that the evidence was incorrectly admitted under state law, since a federal habeas court does not reexamine state-court determinations of state law. The instruction's language forecloses the claim that the jury was directed to find that respondent had committed the prior acts, and the trial court guarded against misuse by forbidding consideration of the evidence to prove bad character or disposition; while the instruction was ambiguous, there is no reasonable likelihood that the jury read it, in the context of the other instructions, to authorize propensity reasoning. Neither a belief that the instruction violated state law nor a belief that the trial judge misinterpreted the state evidence code is a ground for federal habeas relief. The Court expressed no opinion on whether a state law permitting prior crimes evidence to show propensity would violate due process.
* **Historical Authority:** Lewis v. Jeffers, 497 U.S. 764; Lisenba v. California, 314 U.S. 219; Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370; Donnelly v. DeChristoforo, 416 U.S. 637.
* **Historical Sources:** 502 U.S. 62; U.S. Reports volume 502.

# CASE 9 of 10: SOUTHWEST MARINE, INC. v. GIZONI
# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

*Comparison material only. Not simulated law; does not control Stone.*

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed. Unanimous among the eight participating Members; Justice Thomas took no part.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Single opinion of the Court by Justice White, joined by all other participating Members. No concurrence or dissent.
* **Historical Holding:** A maritime worker whose occupation is one of those enumerated in the LHWCA may be a seaman within the meaning of the Jones Act. The LHWCA cannot provide the exclusive remedy for all harbor workers as a matter of law, because it and its exclusionary provision do not apply to a harbor worker who is also a member of a crew of any vessel, a phrase that is a refinement of seaman. The status inquiry, better characterized as a mixed question of law and fact, is fact specific and depends on the vessel's nature and the employee's precise relation to it; a worker need only be doing the ship's work rather than aiding in navigation. The argument that the inquiry may always be resolved as a matter of law where the job fits an enumerated occupation ignores that some maritime workers may be Jones Act seamen performing an enumerated job. The railroad decisions provide no meaningful guidance because the statute contains no comparable exclusion for railroad workers. Congress did not intend to preclude or stay Jones Act suits pending an administrative coverage determination, and the statute contains no unambiguous and comprehensive bar to review comparable to that under the Federal Employees' Compensation Act, nor does it require the jurisdictional limitations the National Labor Relations Act imposes. Receipt of LHWCA benefits does not preclude a later Jones Act suit, because coverage has never been litigated and § 903(e) credits Jones Act amounts against LHWCA liability; for the same reason estoppel arguments fail for want of detrimental reliance. Summary judgment was inappropriate.
* **Historical Authority:** McDermott International, Inc. v. Wilander, 498 U.S. 337; Tipton v. Socony Mobil Oil Co., 375 U.S. 34; Northeast Marine Terminal Co. v. Caputo, 432 U.S. 249; 33 U.S.C. §§ 902(3)(G), 903(e), 905(a), 905(b), 913(d).
* **Historical Sources:** 502 U.S. 81; U.S. Reports volume 502.

# CASE 10 of 10: WOODDELL v. INTERNATIONAL BROTHERHOOD OF ELECTRICAL WORKERS, LOCAL 71
# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

*Comparison material only. Not simulated law; does not control Stone.*

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded. Unanimous among the eight participating Members; Justice Thomas took no part.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Single opinion of the Court by Justice White, joined by all other participating Members. No concurrence or dissent.
* **Historical Holding:** Petitioner was entitled to a jury trial on the LMRDA cause of action. Although he sought injunctive relief as well as damages, the injunctive relief was assertedly incidental to the damages; his claim for lost wages could not be treated as restitutionary incident to reinstatement because the damages sought were pay for jobs to which the union failed to refer him; and an LMRDA action is closely analogous to a personal injury action, a prototypical action at law, so Teamsters v. Terry controls. The subject-matter jurisdiction conferred by § 301(a) extends to suits on union constitutions brought by individual union members, since union constitutions are an important form of contract between labor organizations and petitioner alleged that the IBEW Constitution requires locals to live up to collective bargaining agreements, that the constitution and bylaws bind the local, and that the defendants breached them by discriminating against him in referrals. Section 301 is not limited to suits brought by a party to an interunion contract but extends to individual members who are its beneficiaries, because otherwise identical contract terms might bear different meanings under state and federal law, exerting a disruptive influence on the negotiation and administration of interunion contracts. The contention that this construction is an unwarranted intrusion on state contract law was rejected for want of any indication in the later LMRDA that Congress meant to narrow § 301, and the prediction that federal courts would be inundated with trivial intraunion suits was rejected as unsupported. The Court expressed no view on the merits of the breach-of-contract claims.
* **Historical Authority:** Teamsters v. Terry, 494 U.S. 558; Reed v. United Transportation Union, 488 U.S. 319; Plumbers and Pipefitters, Local 334, 452 U.S. 615; Smith v. Evening News Assn., 371 U.S. 195.
* **Historical Sources:** 502 U.S. 93; U.S. Reports volume 502.
