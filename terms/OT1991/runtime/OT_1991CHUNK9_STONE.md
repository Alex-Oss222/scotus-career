# Foucha v. Louisiana

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The entering law stated in Section I, without assumed simulated holdings.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any material earlier simulated decision before this event.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment sustaining psychiatric confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee rule.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** End confinement on that basis. Remand for implementation without permitting indefinite continuation while the State searches for a new justification; any separate lawful custody requires its own established authority and procedures.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The ended commitment predicate

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved challenge to present custody. The State's concession concerning illness eliminates any need to decide competing psychiatric diagnoses.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Jones, Jackson, and Addington within their distinct spheres. No new general scrutiny tier or dangerousness proof standard is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Louisiana cannot continue this psychiatric commitment solely because an insanity acquittee who is no longer mentally ill cannot establish that he will be harmless.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Jones permits commitment because the verdict supports illness and danger; it does not impose a suspended penal sentence. Once Louisiana abandons the illness predicate, the verdict no longer authorizes psychiatric custody indefinitely. Salerno does not supply the missing authority: this is not temporary pretrial detention within its carefully bounded procedure. The asserted public-safety interest is substantial, but it cannot substitute for the substantive conditions of the channel Louisiana uses. Under the Master’s commitment and source-separation principles, Stone treats medical uncertainty as uncertainty, not as the government's proof. Ordinary civil commitment remains available only if the State can establish its independent requirements under Addington.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve the maximum permissible duration of an otherwise valid commitment, alternative narrowly defined preventive regimes, and the independent equal protection ground because due process supplies complete relief. Stone does not declare Foucha harmless.

# Riggins v. Nevada

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I's eligible law, subject to actual simulated revalidation.
* **Conditions:** No inferred same-term adoption; recheck any material Estelle holding.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for a new trial.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the convictions and dependent sentence. Any renewed involuntary medication must rest on a constitutionally adequate, contemporaneous justification; a new trial is not an order to withhold medically necessary treatment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Justification and trial protection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved objection after consent was withdrawn. Exclude the separately unpreserved Eighth Amendment claim.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Harper's recognized liberty and fair-trial doctrine to a distinct trial setting. Stone proposes the necessary clarification that the State bears justification for continuing treatment over objection: medical appropriateness plus a demonstrated necessity sufficient for the intrusion, considering reasonable less intrusive means. He does not import a later competency-medication test or invent a proof quantum.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An unexplained order continuing antipsychotic medication through trial cannot stand where the State has not established the need for that compelled course and the supported risk to defense participation cannot be found harmless beyond a reasonable doubt.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Medical appropriateness addresses clinical acceptability, not the legal need to override refusal. Several witnesses did not identify loss of competence upon withdrawal, and one questioned the dosage. Stone cannot replace the absent necessity determination with his own medical finding. The State's strongest position, that serious charges deserve a competent trial, is legitimate but leaves unanswered why this treatment was required to obtain one. Demeanor testimony informs jurors only about one possible effect; it cannot reconstruct impaired consultation or attention. Under Chapman the State, rather than Riggins, carries harmlessness. This record does not discharge that burden. The rule uses existing hearings and medical evidence, demands no new documentary ritual, and leaves appropriate safety treatment possible.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve a categorical ban on competency-restoration medication, a general structural-error classification, and the Eighth Amendment sentencing theory. Stone neither declares Riggins incompetent nor assumes every medicated defendant receives an unfair trial.

# Wyatt v. Cole

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I, pending actual same-term revalidation.
* **Conditions:** Recheck Hunter only for relevant adopted immunity propositions.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand the qualified-immunity disposition.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the claim to proceed through its remaining elements and defenses. Award no damages and make no final finding against either defendant.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Limits of private immunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide immunity on the assumed § 1983 predicate while preserving attribution on remand.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Decline the requested extension of Harlow; do not replace public-official qualified immunity in this private-party case.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Private litigants using state replevin machinery do not acquire Harlow immunity from suit merely because their conduct may be attributable to the State.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lugar supplies responsibility for qualifying joint action, not public office. Neither common-law good faith nor reasonable reliance is equivalent to an immunity eliminating factual testing. Cole sought a private commercial advantage; Robbins represented that private claim. Their legitimate reliance concern can be considered only through a legally supported defense, with the conduct, timing, knowledge, and responsible actor actually established. The public-service rationale for protecting discretionary official decisions does not explain their entitlement. Master §§ 3, 4, and Remedies require keeping those questions separate. No precedent is overruled: Lugar reserved the issue, while Harlow concerns eligible officials.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve an independent affirmative good-faith defense and its burden because that question was not adjudicated below. Do not import Stone's preferred official legal-reliance framework into an unresolved private-tort defense, reopen the officials' judgments, or adjudge Robbins a state actor without the needed determination.

# Wade v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I's eligible law, without assumed simulated decisions.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any material earlier sentencing ruling.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the sentence on the supported narrow ground.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No new sentencing proceeding or discovery on this proffer. Do not endorse the lower court's suggestion that unconstitutional refusals are categorically unreviewable.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Motion requirement and a supported constitutional claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the statutory prerequisite and whether Wade supplied a basis for further inquiry.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the motion requirement and ordinary constitutional limits on official discretion. Stone proposes no heightened evidentiary prerequisite requiring defendants to prove hidden discrimination before obtaining evidence necessary to test it.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Substantial assistance alone does not authorize a departure without the required Government motion, and a description of assistance alone supplies no factual basis for constitutional discovery into the refusal. A supported unconstitutional-purpose claim remains reviewable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The enacted trigger is a motion, not the court's own finding that help was valuable. Prosecutorial choice nevertheless remains public action governed by law. Here counsel received an opportunity to identify evidence and offered only the assistance itself. That fact is compatible with lawful disagreement about its value, making an inference of unconstitutional purpose unsupported. The Master’s opposition to circular discovery barriers does not require an inquiry where the defendant supplies no discrimination, retaliation, broken promise, or other actionable factual theory at all. Stone need not choose the exact discovery standard for a materially stronger proffer to resolve this failure under any ordinary one.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve a concrete discovery threshold for an adequately supported claim, remedy for a proven unconstitutional refusal, plea-agreement enforcement, and the independent operation of §§ 3553(e) and 5K1.1. No proof of a promise or forbidden motive is invented.

# Coleman v. Thompson

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I, particularly the unresolved simulated Keeney dependency.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate the governing hearing rule and any effective stay rulings before the application is voted on.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Grant the application to stay execution pending the Court's disposition of the associated review proceeding.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Proposed order: “The application for a stay of execution presented to Justice Stone and by him referred to the Court is granted pending disposition of the associated petition and any further order of the Court.” This is Stone's requested interim order, not an issued Court decision. No release, retrial, or final habeas relief is ordered.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Preserve review of the gateway determination

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only entitlement to interim preservation. The associated habeas review supplies the channel; innocence merits and final petition treatment remain for their proper stage.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing stay and miscarriage-of-justice frameworks; propose no automatic stay for every innocence assertion and no free-standing merits entitlement at this stage.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The supported question about the legal treatment of the combined innocence evidence meets Barefoot's probability-of-review requirement, presents a significant possibility of reversal for a lawful gateway or hearing determination, and carries certain irreparable loss of review if execution proceeds.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The district decision's repeated observation that the jury could resolve credibility against Coleman does not answer whether all evidence gives a fair probability of reasonable doubt under the formulation it invoked, or whether the constitutional-violation gateway has been assessed correctly. Its emphasis on Coleman's having selected the DNA expert does not validate that expert's disputed methodology. Those are identifiable defects in the reasoning, not a judicial finding that the experts criticizing him are correct. Virginia's nonexclusion evidence and contradictory affidavits remain substantial counterevidence. Stone assesses a reasonable probability of the review support Barefoot requires because the question concerns the operative innocence exception and treatment of materially contested evidence, rather than only disagreement about an ordinary credibility finding. The district court's stated reasoning supplies a significant possibility of reversal for application of the proper gateway or hearing rule; the potential relief is that procedural correction, not immediate exoneration. Execution would irreversibly defeat it. The appellate affirmance weighs against intervention but does not answer the identified distinction between possible adverse credibility findings and the governing probability inquiry. These assessments concern the legal stay criteria and do not assign actual votes to other Justices. Delay remains a real public cost, but a stay tied to disposition of review preserves a judicial task rather than granting indefinite custody supervision. Master §§ 3, 17, and Remedies support this record-bound separation of interim relief from exoneration.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve final innocence, automatic hearing entitlement, freestanding innocence relief, and the validity of the preterm default rule. No later testing or eventual historical event enters this vote.

# Burson v. Freeman

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I's eligible sources, subject to actual earlier simulated holdings.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate relevant Simon & Schuster and Norman propositions.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment invalidating the 100-foot campaign boundary and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the federal constitutional basis for relief against that boundary. Do not approve other distances, arbitrary enforcement, or broader bans.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A bounded protection of access to the ballot

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach content discrimination and justification; treat public sidewalks within the boundary as public forums rather than inventing a special forum exemption.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply strict scrutiny and accept Tennessee's showing for this confined election-day restriction. No replacement voting-speech tier is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The 100-foot electioneering restriction, limited to the immediate approach to casting a ballot, is justified by the compelling interest in protecting voters from intimidation and fraudulent control of the voting act and is sufficiently confined to that purpose.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** History matters for the documented mechanism: campaign agents immediately confronting and observing voters at the place they must enter can exercise pressure that a prosecution after voting cannot undo. The same materials explain why a protected approach, rather than protection beginning only inside the door, addresses that problem. The State's existing intimidation laws remain useful but do not provide equivalent prevention when pressure is subtle, difficult to prove, or completed before intervention. Campaign-specific coverage identifies the activity historically tied to interference with choice at this mandatory encounter; it does not discriminate among candidates or viewpoints. The 100-foot line is a legislature's limited implementation of that protection, not a judicially announced universal distance. Freeman's lost opportunity to address voters at their final approach is real, but campaigning remains available beyond that short boundary and throughout the campaign. Stone does not require an experiment exposing voters to coercion to prove each foot indispensable. This is a judgment about supported tailoring under the received rule, consistent with Master §§ 17-18, not an inference that tradition alone defeats liberty.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve larger boundaries, restrictions beyond actual voting periods, rules covering unrelated political speech, and discriminatory enforcement. The State receives no general power to prohibit campaign advocacy merely because an election is occurring.

# United States v. Burke

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** The tax and Title VII provisions governing the recovery, not later versions.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate material earlier simulated interpretive holdings.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit's judgment recognizing the exclusion.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the refund consequence to the existing action and applicable computation. No enlargement of the Title VII settlement.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The statutory right and the remedy's measure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only the tax classification of the identified recovery.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Interpret the existing exclusion and regulation by text, context, and the character of the compensated right. No equality tier or new tax exemption is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Backpay awarded to remedy the personal statutory injury of intentional sex discrimination under this Title VII settlement qualifies as damages on account of personal injury under the then-applicable § 104(a)(2) and tort-type-rights regulation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress's exclusion did not say physical injury or require a full inventory of common-law damages. The regulation directs attention to the kind of right. A right against discriminatory treatment attaches independently of a contract to pay wages, and its invasion has the personal character supported by the discrimination-law analogy in Goodman. Backpay is the measure selected to compensate part of that invasion; a restricted measure does not transform the underlying right into a contract right. Stone does not infer exclusion merely because discrimination is serious or because the taxpayers are sympathetic. He rejects the Government's additional remedial-range prerequisite because neither enacted text nor the regulation supplies it. The administrative line remains the source and object of the payment, distinguishing ordinary unpaid wages from damages resolving a tort-type discrimination claim. Master §§ 4 and 18 require that source-specific explanation rather than automatically carrying a limitations holding into tax law.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve ordinary wage settlements, payments not on account of a tort-type injury, punitive damages, and the treatment of recoveries under amended statutes. No existing Supreme Court holding squarely deciding this tax question is overruled.

# Evans v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I, subject to actual simulated revalidation.
* **Conditions:** No earlier drafted statutory interpretation is treated as law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment on the presented Hobbs Act issue.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No retrial or resentencing. Leave the unchallenged tax component undisturbed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Taking payment for a specific official exchange

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the offense's demand requirement and instruction; do not reopen an unpresented entrapment claim.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the reserved statutory question through text and the common-law legal term, preserving McCormick's exchange limitation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Official-right extortion does not require that the public official initiate the payment, where he knowingly accepts it in exchange for the specific official undertaking required by the governing law. The charge here sufficiently distinguished that transaction from a legitimate campaign contribution.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** “Induced” identifies how property is obtained, but does not itself say who must first suggest the transaction. The official-right language carries a recognized prohibition on taking payment for official duty. Evans's contrary construction protects an official who accepts an explicit corrupt bargain solely because the payer spoke first, a distinction the text and legal usage do not establish. The instruction did not criminalize the disclosed contribution or pending business by themselves; it required an exchange for a specific use of official power. The jury's verdict under that instruction is not recast as a finding that mere official status coerces every donor. Congress's silence alone would not establish the elements, and Stone does not rely on it to resolve an otherwise ambiguous crime. Under the conventional sources the separate-initiation requirement is unsupported, so lenity supplies no additional element.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve mere gratuities, ambiguous expressions of general support, and any case lacking the required undertaking. Do not weaken proof beyond a reasonable doubt or expand the commerce element. McCormick remains applicable rather than overruled.

# Quill Corp. v. North Dakota

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Section I, subject to actual simulated revalidation.
* **Conditions:** No draft commerce position has become governing law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand on the Commerce Clause ground.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject the challenged collection liability on this record. Leave the consumer's use-tax liability and other taxes outside this judgment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Retain the specific collection precedent

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach nexus and the request to reconsider Bellas Hess. Reserve the separate due-process challenge because the commerce ground completely resolves the collection demand.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the specific mail-order collection precedent within Complete Auto rather than announce a new general physical-presence requirement.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The collection duty imposed on Quill is barred by the surviving Commerce Clause rule of Bellas Hess; its limited software interests do not establish the local commercial operation missing here.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Complete Auto ends categorical immunity for interstate business, but does not say that a protected market establishes all four requirements or expressly repudiate this particular collection boundary. Quill's extensive solicitation makes the State's fairness argument substantial; it does not itself identify a conflict that requires overruling the narrower precedent. Under the Master's precedent discipline, computerized calculation reduces one burden without establishing that divergent definitions, administration, and compliance duties have disappeared. The reliance is concrete: interstate sellers organized distribution and previously completed transactions under an express collection rule. Reliance does not make a constitutional error permanent, but neither economic growth nor the availability of a different reasonable nexus rule demonstrates a source-grounded necessity for this Court to replace the boundary now. Congress's power permits coordinated adjustment of collection obligations; that competence supports, but does not independently justify, retaining the precedent. Insignificant licensed ordering software is not a substitute for the in-state market-maintaining activity recognized in Tyler Pipe.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No extension to every tax, no quantitative sales threshold, and no corporate exemption from generally valid law. Reserve due-process reconsideration, including whether purposeful solicitation would satisfy that independently analyzed limitation. No new rule or overruling is proposed.
