# International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 26, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any effective earlier speech ruling and any supplied construction of the regulation; do not assume the cross-case has produced law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment sustaining the solicitation-and-receipt provision, confined to continuous or repetitive solicitation for immediate personal payment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny relief against that limited prohibition. State expressly that appeals for later contributions and independent distribution are outside this rationale; the cross-case controls its own relief.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Apply the more protective standard to a limited transaction rule

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the challenged provision; definitive airport forum classification is unnecessary if the regulation satisfies public-forum standards.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Assume public-forum protection and apply Ward. Read the conjunctive solicitation-and-receipt language, in its continuous-or-repetitive setting, as directed to immediate transactions, not mere discussion of financial support.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The limited, viewpoint-neutral rule reasonably concentrates an interruption-producing activity outside the terminals and survives content-neutral time, place and manner scrutiny.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An immediate exchange requires a traveler to stop, evaluate a demand, handle money and complete the transfer amid moving passengers. Repetition compounds obstruction and pressure on travelers who cannot easily postpone departure. The government has significant operational and anti-fraud interests in that setting. The rule does not select religious or political viewpoints and does not prohibit oral appeals asking a listener to contribute later. Thus advocates can still reach the travelers inside with their message and a request for later support; outside solicitation also permits immediate exchanges with people entering and leaving. Stone does not assume every connecting passenger can conveniently reach the sidewalk: the retained ability to address the interior audience, without immediate payment, is essential to the alternatives analysis. The court below has also protected literature distribution, subject to the separate cross-case; no effective simulated companion decision is presumed. Ward requires a real relation between the burden and the problem, not proof that every alternative would fail. The limited transaction rule has that relation. Treating all solicitation, including nontransactional appeals, as interchangeable would enlarge the burden beyond the justification and is not approved.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general ruling that airports are nonpublic, no categorical exclusion of protected solicitation from speech, and no approval of a literature ban. This uses the native First Amendment test, consistent with Master's speech, institutional-role and scope instructions; it changes no existing precedent.

# United States v. Fordice

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 26, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate Freeman's and Rufo's actual effective holdings if supplied. No earlier proposed position resolves these issues automatically.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand both consolidated judgments for a policy-specific and system-wide assessment under the continuing affirmative duty.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the State to demonstrate dismantlement of its prior dual system. Determine which traceable policies retain segregative effects, whether sound educational reasons justify them, and what practical changes can eliminate those effects. Do not direct particular closures, mergers, numerical quotas or a fixed funding award on this record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Neutral wording does not discharge an uncompleted remedial duty

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The established de jure system triggers affirmative dismantlement; no fresh discriminatory intent finding is necessary simply to examine its continuing machinery.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Brown, Green and Swann to university structures while accounting for voluntary attendance and legitimate differentiated missions.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Good-faith race neutrality is insufficient if policies traceable to the dual system continue to channel students along segregative lines and their effects can practicably be eliminated consistently with sound education.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Free choice operates within the options government constructs. A student may face different admissions thresholds, degree availability and institutional resources inherited from forced separation. Calling the final choice voluntary does not decide whether those state-created constraints persist. Bazemore's voluntary clubs do not require treating a State's integrated system of program and admissions decisions as merely private preference. This specifies the operation of the existing affirmative duty in a new institutional setting rather than imposing a new intent-free discrimination rule on all universities. The State's existing burden of demonstrating dismantlement remains; no heightened fact-proof standard is added.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Current imbalance by itself is insufficient. A university system without the established de jure violation would require its own liability analysis. Master's equality and domination concerns work through the native desegregation duty, not a substitute universal scrutiny tier.

### Issue 2: Remedy the proved structure while preserving lawful educational choice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The incorrect compliance standard affects assessment of the challenged features; remand is necessary before selecting concrete remedies.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Require traceability, current effect, educational justification and practical cure to be considered together. The State must explain continued policies under its existing dismantlement burden; courts must identify findings supporting their remedies.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Reassess differential admissions, unnecessary duplication, inherited mission assignments and continued institutional organization, including their interaction; reassess related funding and other pleaded features insofar as the corrected standard affects them.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An ACT-only standard may exclude applicants differently from an approach considering school performance; mission limits and duplicative programs can reinforce its effects. These are concrete record questions, not findings that every ACT use is invalid. All eight institutions need not be maintained in precisely their inherited roles, but historically black institutions' value and students' interests are relevant to lawful remedial design. Their closure is neither a presumption nor an end in itself. Additional resources may help but cannot alone answer whether unlawful barriers remain.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No right to permanent racial identifiability, no compulsory numerical racial mix, and no unrestricted judicial power to reorganize higher education. The Title VI disposition follows the same established discrimination duty; unrelated statutory theories remain undecided.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Inherent distinctiveness is an alternative route to source identification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the legal instruction given the jury's accepted findings; no need to reopen the whole infringement case.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Interpret the applicable §43(a) consistently with the statute's treatment of source identifiers, without adding a universal secondary-meaning requirement for restaurant trade dress.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Nonfunctional restaurant trade dress that is inherently distinctive may be protected without acquired secondary meaning, provided the remaining infringement requirements, including likely confusion, are met.

Requiring a period of copying before protection would impair source identification and goodwill without a textual basis. A temporary exception would likewise invent a timetable the statute does not supply.

Master property and intellectual-property principles support ordinary source-identification doctrine, not a free-standing priority based on corporate size or moral desert; no Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

# Franklin v. Massachusetts

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 26, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate effective Montana and Lujan holdings. Do not presume any power to issue coercive relief against the President.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the district court's judgment: reach review of the Secretary's completed decision, but reject the constitutional, statutory and arbitrary-and-capricious challenges.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Dissolve the corrective relief. No replacement allocation and no injunction against the President. The latter's APA status and susceptibility to an injunction need not be resolved because the Secretary supplies a review channel and no merits violation remains.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Review the responsible agency's completed census choice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Find concrete representational injury, causation and likely redress through authoritative relief concerning the Secretary's census determination. That conclusion rests on this statutory sequence and the government litigation position, not a general assumption that outsiders obey judicial advice.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Treat the Secretary's completed census decision as final agency action; reject statutory preclusion and committed-discretion objections.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A completed population tabulation determining the operative census data is reviewable under §704 despite the President's subsequent apportionment statement. The counting command supplies law to apply.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 2a directs transmission of numbers ascertained under the Secretary's census. Stone reads that instruction as applying the apportionment formula to the completed count, not conferring a new presidential choice of population figures. General executive supervision does not alter that statutory transmittal duty. The public census report and its other statutory uses further show that the Secretary's substantive decision is complete and drives concrete consequences. Possible corrections do not make every completed administrative decision tentative. The President's role does not erase the independent administrative choice Congress assigned to the Secretary. A corrective legal determination concerning that choice would likely generate a corrected apportionment through the prescribed sequence; the dispute is not an uncertain chain of private economic reactions. Broad discretion over form and content still exists within a duty to count the proper population. This resolves an open application of finality, distinguishing Waterman's presidential foreign-affairs judgment, without overruling controlling precedent. Master agency accountability supports applying the native APA requirements rather than dispensing with them.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general power to review presidential action under the APA or to enjoin presidential discretion. No advisory opinion where likely redress is absent.

### Issue 2: Uphold the supported methodological choice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Having established review, decide both constitutional counting authority and the APA objections advanced against this agency choice.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Permit residence to reflect enduring state ties of temporarily overseas personnel; apply State Farm to the actual explanation and available alternatives.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Inclusion is constitutionally and statutorily permissible, and the comparative choice of home-of-record data is not arbitrary or capricious on this record.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Absence on government assignment does not necessarily end a person's state affiliation. The constitutional text and early practice do not compel exclusion of every absent person. Home of record is imperfect, but legal residence may reflect taxation and last duty station may identify work rather than home. The failed survey is a serious objection, not something to omit: the record nevertheless includes assurances of supplemented information and continuing reasons for counting personnel who regard themselves as temporarily absent residents. Those recorded considerations, rather than a rationale invented in litigation, make the agency's choice reasonably discernible. The statute permits a supported comparative judgment; it does not demand an unattainable perfect residence dataset.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No presumption that any politically convenient allocation is valid, no authorization to count persons without a supportable state connection, and no rule excusing an agency's refusal to reconsider after its only factual premise collapses. Different methodological challenges require their own records.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve declaratory and injunctive relief against that prohibition, while leaving ordinary viewpoint-neutral anti-obstruction, litter and reasonable location rules available for separate assessment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Operational reasonableness requires a relation to the activity excluded

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide distribution; assume nonpublic status for this disposition because the prohibition fails its more deferential test.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply reasonableness in context without importing public-forum strict scrutiny into the assumed nonpublic category.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A terminal-wide prohibition of this protected distribution is unreasonable on the record, given its ordinary compatibility with the public circulation and commercial activity the terminals accommodate.

No categorical entitlement to solicit immediate payments, sell merchandise, obstruct queues or distribute in every operational area. Master speech and scope principles support deciding the concrete burden at the necessary doctrinal level rather than announcing an airport-wide public-forum theory.

# Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Nos. 91-744 and 91-902 — June 29, 1992**
**Version 1.4 — Stone's definitive six-step framework, trimester replacement, authorship and disposition**

Prepared at the user's direction on September 15, 2026 from the user's written instruction of that date, with version 1.3 as the baseline, and revised the same day at the user's further direction to state the framework in holding language and to fix the author; approved for adjudication by those instructions. This version replaces Section II of Stone's earlier Casey supplements. Section I is unchanged: the version 1.1 corrected neutral packet with the version 1.2 source corrections and record clarifications as part E, and the statute as reprinted at 505 U.S. 902–911. The statutory text, the trial findings, the procedural posture and the June 29, 1992 research cutoff are preserved. Every illustration below is hypothetical and is labeled as such; Pennsylvania's provisions are decided on the actual record.

**Source qualification that must accompany every use of the paternal-support disclosure.** Under §3205(a)(2)(iii) the paternal-support information may be omitted in cases of rape. The statute states: "In the case of rape, this information may be omitted." The permission has no police-reporting condition and is distinct from the spousal-sexual-assault exception to spousal notification in §3209, whose incorporated §3128 reporting condition runs 90 days from commission of the offense.

**A. What version 1.4 decides that version 1.3 left open**

Stone adopts the six-step framework stated in part B as his definitive position and proposes it as the rule governing regulation of abortion before viability. Version 1.3 declined to displace Roe's trimester timetable while the substitute remained a proposal. Version 1.4 does both in one decision: it adopts the affirmative protections and displaces the timetable, so that the substitute is operative law at the moment the timetable falls. Stone joins no freestanding elimination of the timetable; his vote for displacement is his vote for the protections that replace it.

Stone reaffirms the liberty Roe protects. Before viability the decision whether to continue a pregnancy belongs to the patient, who need not establish medical necessity or obtain official approval of her reasons. After viability the State may prohibit elective abortion, subject to the required protection of life and health. The State may support childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval of the protected decision does not by itself justify obstructing it, and a reduction in abortions produced by coercion, delay or lost access is not a benefit the State may claim. Roe, 410 U.S. 153–165; Doe, 410 U.S. 192–200; Carey, 431 U.S. 686–689.

Stone qualifies Akron and Thornburgh only insofar as their disclosure reasoning categorically excludes concise, accurate notice of available assistance or alternatives because the information might favor childbirth. Their protections against unjustified delay, coercion, misleading communication and unnecessary medical restriction are retained and applied through the framework. Akron, 462 U.S. 430–450; Thornburgh, 476 U.S. 758–771.

**B. The six-step framework, stated as Stone would hold it**

The constitutional question is whether an identified requirement respects the protected decision and rests on a justification adequate to the interference it actually causes. A court answers that question in six steps, in order.

**Step 1. The duty.** A court first identifies the legal duty the State actually imposes: its statutory terms and authoritative construction, the conduct it changes, its exceptions, and the consequences of noncompliance. A lower court's finding about how the duty operates is evidence of its effect; it is not the duty, and neither is substituted for the other.

**Step 2. The function and the asserted justification.** The court then identifies what the duty does and what the State says it is for, distinguishing medical safety, informed consent, permissible parental involvement, public-health administration, financial accountability and persuasion. The asserted purpose must account for the particular obligation imposed. A requirement whose purpose, shown by objective evidence of its design, context and expected operation, is to obstruct the protected choice is invalid without more; opposition to abortion does not by itself establish that purpose. Truthful information that persuades through voluntary understanding is not an obstacle.

**Step 3. The concrete and cumulative interference.** The challenger bears the burden of showing a concrete addition attributable to the duty: an added appointment, material expense, consequential delay, coercion, privacy exposure, lost provider capacity or clinical harm. The measure is access for the same patients in the same circumstances with and without the challenged duty; where a package of provisions is challenged together, the package is what the comparison removes. The court accounts for the duty's interaction with existing requirements and with actual provider capacity, and counts each consequence once. Poverty, distance and transportation are the circumstances in which the law operates rather than the State's doing, but they explain why an additional state-imposed condition impairs access, and the court must say whether it does. The affected population is defined from the statute's operation and the record, independently of the constitutional conclusion, with evidence-supported subgroups examined on their own; their burdens are not diluted by averaging across everyone who is pregnant. Reliable evidence of predictable effects suffices before enforcement.

Hypothetical illustration: a 24-hour interval operating on a clinic that sees patients one day a week produces a week's added delay for that clinic's patients. The court evaluates that consequence once, as the interval's actual operation in that setting.

**Step 4. The protections, in order.** The following protections overlap and apply in sequence. Once an earlier protection decides the case, a later one is not reached to rescue the requirement.

(a) *Categorical prohibitions.* A requirement that prohibits the protected previability decision, transfers effective control of it to another person, or prevents timely care necessary to preserve life or health is invalid. No showing of a compelling interest saves it. Roe; Doe; Danforth, 428 U.S. 69–75.

(b) *The substantial-obstacle prohibition.* A requirement whose purpose or effect is to seriously obstruct the practical opportunity to obtain timely, clinically appropriate previability care, judged in the circumstances of the people it actually affects, is invalid. Impossibility is not required. That some patients overcome the restriction does not make access constitutionally adequate. A safety label does not change the character of an established obstacle, and the State's preference for childbirth does not justify one.

Hypothetical illustrations: a mandatory second clinic visit adds only expense for some patients while seriously obstructing timely access for an identifiable group whose clinic schedules, transportation, employment or caregiving make a second trip impracticable within the clinical window; on such a record the visit is an obstacle. A narrowly targeted specialist-care requirement for a demonstrated medical risk is a serious burden that may nonetheless stand, if the State establishes the medical need and shows that suitable alternative care is actually and timely available.

(c) *Severe interference.* A requirement that severely interferes with protected access, though it is neither prohibited under (a) nor an obstacle under (b), is invalid unless it serves a compelling permissible interest and is narrowly tailored to that interest. General invocations of safety, administrative convenience or potential life do not make that showing. This rule is a floor beneath the obstacle prohibition, not an exception to it: it never permits a substantial obstacle on a showing of compelling interest. It governs access to abortion and states no rule for other constitutional claims. Carey, 431 U.S. 686–689.

(d) *Meaningful lesser interference.* A requirement that meaningfully but not severely interferes with protected access is invalid unless the State substantiates its permissible purpose and the connection between that purpose and the requirement, and answers any concrete, practicable and substantially less burdensome alternative the challenger identifies. Perfect legislation, professional unanimity and the refutation of every imaginable alternative are not required. Stone proposes this rule and its allocation of the burden of justification as a development of the doctrine, and states its ground: a right that must tolerate every individually bearable obstacle is not protected in operation, and the State that adds a condition to access is the party that knows, and can prove, what the condition accomplishes.

Interference is meaningful when the challenger shows a concrete, nontrivial additional burden attributable to the requirement: an additional appointment, material expense, consequential delay or reduced usable provider capacity. Hypothetical illustrations: a receipt checkbox completed during an existing consultation, with no other demonstrated consequence, is not a meaningful burden; an additional required clinic trip ordinarily is. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly cutoff applies; the court judges the affected patients' actual circumstances from testimony, records, reliable clinical evidence and supported practical inferences. Crossing the threshold triggers review, not invalidation. Falling short of it ends this access inquiry and leaves every independent constitutional objection, including informed consent, clinical judgment, confidentiality and compelled expression, fully available.

**Step 5. Clinical judgment and truthful communication.** A claimed medical benefit is judged on reliable clinical evidence, pertinent professional standards and competent contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement carries substantial evidentiary weight, and a burdensome departure from it requires an explanation; professional associations hold no legislative veto, and unanimity is not required. Necessary treatment remains available on timely, good-faith clinical judgment, assessed clinically as to physical and mental health, without requiring deterioration into catastrophe; preference alone is not an emergency. A duty must be sufficiently definite and carry appropriate culpability rules; a later professional disagreement, without more, is not punishable wrongdoing. Nothing in this step immunizes fraud, malpractice or professional misconduct from lawful enforcement. Clinicians remain free to correct or qualify state information, and no patient must endorse any view to obtain care. Doe; Colautti, 439 U.S. 394–401.

**Step 6. Disposition and remedy.** Relief runs to the established violation. The judgment states separately the duty and the affected group, the proven or reliably predicted interference, the justification and its fit, the invalid requirement or application, and the scope of effective relief with the lawful provisions that remain. Proof of a violation affecting particular patients is distinct from the justification for facial relief, and the scope of any injunction must answer to the challenged duty's contribution. A cumulative violation does not invalidate unrelated provisions. A duty that falls takes its dependent certifications and enforcement with it; independently valid receipt obligations remain. An individual claim does not fail because others can comply, and broader relief requires a demonstrated defect of corresponding scope. Adoption of a new general facial-challenge formula is not a condition of this framework; Stone's qualification of Ohio v. Akron's no-valid-applications formulation is confined to the spousal-notice ruling in part E. Ohio v. Akron, 497 U.S. 514; Brockett, 472 U.S. 501–507.

**C. The trimester timetable is replaced, and the substitute protections are operative**

The trimester timetable no longer determines when the State may regulate in the interest of maternal health or potential life. The framework governs throughout the previability period. Crossing a trimester boundary does not change the governing constitutional standard, and the patient's protected choice does not depend on medical necessity or official approval of her reasons at any previability stage. Gestational age remains relevant where evidence connects a particular stage, procedure, risk and requirement; medically supported regulation remains possible on that showing, and advancing gestation is not by itself permission to obstruct previability choice.

Viability remains the boundary for prohibiting elective abortion. It is an individualized, medically supported determination, not a fixed week written into the Constitution. Timely care necessary for life or health is protected at every stage, on the terms of Step 5.

Precedent displaced: Roe's trimester timetable as the rule fixing when health and potential-life regulation may begin, and the applications of Akron and Thornburgh that rest on that timetable or on the categorical disclosure reasoning qualified in part A. Precedent retained: Roe's protected previability choice, viability boundary and life-and-health safeguard; Doe's protection of clinical judgment; Danforth's rejection of a spousal veto and its permission for ordinary consent and confidential health reporting; Bellotti and Ashcroft on parental involvement with an effective bypass; Akron's holdings against unjustified delay and unnecessary medical restriction, now applied as instances of the framework. Existing injunctions entered under the displaced timetable are not disturbed by this decision alone; they are conformed on remand, provision by provision, under Step 6. The applications to Pennsylvania's provisions are in part E.

**D. Coalition posture and authorship**

Stone presents the complete framework as his proposal and defends it as his operative position. He authorizes no change to its substance to attract a join: the order of the protections in Step 4, the categorical prohibitions, the substantial-obstacle rule, the severe-interference minimum and the proposed lesser-burden rule are what he proposes. He accepts wording accommodations that do not alter substance, and he accepts severable joins: a Justice may join the categorical prohibitions, the substantial-obstacle rule, the severe-interference minimum, the trimester replacement or a provision-specific application without joining the whole. A shared label or a shared disposition is not agreement with his reasoning, and Stone does not count it as such.

Authorship. Stone writes for the Court. He assigns to himself the opinion of the Court on every part in whose judgment coalition he sits: the constitutional foundation and stare decisis; the framework and the trimester replacement, at whatever scope commands a majority; spousal notice and its dependent reporting; and the unanimous and near-unanimous provision rulings. He is the Chief Justice in those coalitions, the framework is his text, and the severable-join structure above lets a Justice who accepts less than the whole join the parts that Justice accepts without another author's mediation. Parts that sustain a requirement over Stone's dissent are assigned by the senior Associate Justice in their coalition. If Stone's authorship of a part would cost that part its governing coalition, that part is assigned instead to the Justice who retains the coalition, and the record states why. No Court-wide result is prescribed.

**E. Stone's disposition of each challenged requirement**

Each disposition is Stone's application of the framework to the actual record.

1. Medical emergency, §3203 and §3205(b): sustained under the appellate construction that compliance cannot create a significant threat to life or health, covering the disputed obstetric conditions, on timely good-faith clinical judgment. The §3205(c) disclosure defense does not substitute for an effective emergency exception. No standalone vagueness claim is decided.

2. Mandatory 24-hour interval, §3205(a)(1)–(2): invalid. The record establishes an additional visit, expense and consequential delay attributable to the interval. Under Step 4(b), on this record the interval seriously obstructs timely access for an identifiable group of patients; independently, under Step 4(d), a general interest in deliberation does not substantiate a universal compulsory reconsideration period after every patient's informed choice, and individualized counseling is a practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative the State has not answered. Akron independently supports this result. The dependent timing certification and enforcement fall with it; the interval is not transferred into parental consent.

3. Required information and its delivery, §3205(a)(1)–(4) and §3208: accurate information material to consent is sustained, as are the concise, nonmisleading notices of available materials, possible assistance and paternal support, with the rape omission permission stated as above and legal support liability not represented as guaranteed payment; materials are supplied only if the patient chooses to review them. The exclusion of qualified personnel from routine disclosures is invalid: it adds an appointment or scheduling burden without demonstrated benefit beyond the physician's retained responsibility for individualized assessment, which is preserved. Written certification is retained only for surviving lawful duties. No materials remand or interim injunction.

4. Parental consent, §3206: the one-parent structure with the confidential, expeditious independent bypass on maturity and informed consent or best interests is sustained, with emergency care not awaiting the process. No attendance-specific vacatur, remand or injunction; the statute does not expressly require an additional parental clinic visit and the disputed construction is unresolved. An established unnecessary attendance requirement remains challengeable. No adult-incapacity claim is decided.

5. Spousal notification, §3209, and §3214(a)(12): the notice requirement and its dependent enforcement are invalid under Step 4(a) and (b). Compelled disclosure gives abusive or coercive spouses effective control over the protected decision, the exceptions leave important threats uncovered, and the absence of a formal consent requirement does not cure effective control. Danforth's protection against a spousal veto extends to a notice requirement shown to produce that result. For this requirement Stone qualifies Ohio v. Akron's no-valid-applications formulation: women who would notify voluntarily do not supply valid restrictive applications defeating relief, and effective obstruction is shown for a large fraction of the independently defined group required to disclose against their wishes; no numerical cutoff is adopted. The notice-given and reason-for-nonnotification fields are invalid; the independently severable confidential marital-status field is preserved.

6. Referring-source identification, §3214(a)(1): the blanket demand is invalid. The specific findings of referral deterrence despite confidentiality establish meaningful interference; indiscriminate identification of referring physicians, agencies and services adds insufficient regulatory value beyond performing-physician identification and targeted inquiry, which are preserved.

7. Automatic public release, §§3207(b) and 3214(f): the challenged release requirements are invalid. Taxpayer accountability is legitimate, and the record supports deterrence and exposure attributable to compelled affiliation and trimester disclosures beyond facilities' existing visibility. The blanket scope is not justified when confidential oversight and public accounting tied to expenditures remain available. Institutional accountability is distinguished from patient-identifying information, which is not at issue. Confidential collection and tailored public accountability are preserved.

8. Other confidential reports, §§3214(a)(8), (10) and (11): sustained. These limited duties record relevant judgments and support lawful oversight without prior approval, without prescribing additional diagnostic tests, and without authorizing punishment for reasonable professional disagreement. Confidentiality and concrete challenges to unlawful enforcement remain.

9. Reassessed narrow outcomes: emergency protection, accurate medical content, optional materials and chosen receipt, basic receipt certification, one-parent consent with bypass, the limited clinical-basis reports and the confidential marital-status field are reassessed under the framework and sustained on the same terms. The distinctions between emergency treatment and the information-omission defense, between optional materials and mandatory notices, and between confidential reports and public institutional disclosures are preserved. No statutory parental attendance requirement and no police-reporting condition on the paternal-support rape permission are invented.

**F. Judgment Stone would enter**

In No. 91-902, the invalidation of spousal notification is affirmed. In No. 91-744, the judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded for implementation and severability consistent with part E: the approval of the mandatory interval, unjustified physician exclusivity, blanket referring-source identification, the challenged automatic public-release requirements and the notice-related reporting fields is reversed; the specified lawful requirements are preserved; the existing injunction is conformed provision by provision. The materials and parental-attendance vacaturs, remands and interim injunctions proposed in version 1.2 are not ordered.

* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 29, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate any effective earlier takings or standing holding. Treat total loss as the reviewed trial finding, subject to issues properly open on remand, rather than supplying a new appraisal.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand the no-compensation judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require consideration of whether background state property or nuisance law already excluded the claimed uses. If it did not and complete economic deprivation is sustained, award just compensation for the actual taking period. The 1990 permit possibility and later permission may limit duration and valuation. Do not automatically reinstate the award premised on a permanent loss.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Later permission cannot erase a ripe earlier deprivation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the 1988-to-amendment deprivation; reserve the effect of permit proceedings on later periods.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply finality to the actual governmental restriction and First English to an already completed period.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The new discretionary permit route does not bar review of the original categorical prohibition that the State defended through a final merits judgment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The stipulated inability to obtain a permit under the original Act makes another application for that period pointless. Lucas pursued the state compensation route, and the state supreme court rejected its merits rather than postponing adjudication. A later change may mitigate or end the loss; it cannot make past permission available retroactively. This preserves meaningful compensation without treating all land-use review as immediately final.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No dispensation from finality for ordinary discretionary permitting or uncertain retained use. Post-amendment development and later losses remain subject to their own record.

### Issue 2: Propose a limited categorical rule for complete economic extinction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The complete-loss finding and the state's purpose-only ground squarely present the rule and its necessary exception.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a categorical compensation rule where regulation removes all economically beneficial use of the relevant property, unless the claimed use was already outside the title under background property or nuisance law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** After the owner establishes complete economic deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate an applicable preexisting ownership limitation that would independently prohibit the claimed use; a newly enacted legislative harm declaration alone does not establish that exception.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** This is a proposed specification and extension of Agins, with a candid limitation on broad readings of the older noxious-use cases, not a claim that this exact rule already governs. Under Master's §16, complete loss is materially different from the partial burdens examined through Penn Central: the owner retains nominal title while the public obtains the entire economic sacrifice. A purpose-only exemption would make compensation turn on whether government describes the same measure as preventing harm or conferring benefit, undermining Pennsylvania Coal's distinction between public authority and taking cost. Existing nuisance and property limits remain because the owner never held the prohibited entitlement. That preserves legitimate reliance on settled ownership law, including genuine restrictions applicable to vulnerable coastal land, while preventing new legislation from defining away its compensation consequence. The State's identified legal exception and its applicability must be proved through ordinary adjudication; no special clear-and-convincing quantum is created. Here the trial finding triggers the proposed category, but the record does not establish the background-law exception, so remand must resolve it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Partial losses remain under native regulatory doctrine, not a percentage shortcut; no new parcel-definition rule or categorical protection of anticipated profits. Public environmental purposes remain legitimate and may justify regulation; compensation is a separate consequence. Changed physical knowledge may affect application of genuine background nuisance law, but a legislative label cannot substitute for that inquiry. Master property and environmental principles support both meaningful compensation and source-grounded ownership limits.

# Espinosa v. Florida

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 29, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate effective Stringer and Sochor holdings, especially treatment of weighting and appellate cure.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the rejection of the sentencing claim and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require valid reweighing or Chapman review that addresses the defective jury recommendation's legal influence; if no lawful cure is available, require a new sentencing proceeding. The guilt judgment remains outside this disposition.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A legally consequential recommendation needs constitutional guidance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved direct-review issue reaches the actual two-stage sentencing process; no collateral default or innocence gateway applies.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing anti-vagueness and weighing principles to both actors where state law makes each actor's assessment consequential.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The judge's correct direct findings do not cure an inadequately narrowed jury instruction when the judge must give great weight to the resulting recommendation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Words such as wicked or evil do not distinguish the aggravated category with the guidance Maynard and Shell require. The jury was authorized to use that category and gave a general recommendation; there is no basis to assume it disregarded the instruction. Florida then required the judge to give its recommendation legal weight. Describing the jury as advisory does not eliminate that statutory and decisional role. The proposed application follows the actual channel by which invalid aggravation can affect punishment, while preserving Walton's permission for genuine judicial sentencing. The presence of other valid aggravators does not itself resolve a weighing error under Clemons. This extends those received principles to the identified split process without replacing them or adopting a new capital-sentencing regime.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The State may allocate sentencing roles differently, but the resulting process must remain constitutionally guided. No speculation about which jurors found which factors, no automatic life sentence, and no adjudication of unrelated aggravators. Master criminal-procedure, punishment and actual-operation instructions support the limited process-and-remedy ruling.

# Benten v. Kessler

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Version:** 1.
* **Approval Status:** Proposed and unapproved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** July 17, 1992.
* **Conditions:** Revalidate the actual effective emergency, exhaustion and agency-review holdings identified above. The imminent deadline is a record fact for this event, not an assumption of a later opportunity for equivalent relief.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Deny the application to vacate the appellate stay on the narrow ground that likely procedural invalidity does not sufficiently establish entitlement to the ordered admission-and-return remedy.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Requested Court order: “The application to vacate the stay presented to Justice Thomas and by him referred to the Court is denied.” This is proposed wording, not an issued order. Leave the appeal and any properly sought relief requiring prompt lawful agency action in their procedural channels; do not issue a new drug-approval order or an unrequested constitutional judgment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Recognize the urgent injury while applying appellate-stay discipline

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The drug's imminent loss of usefulness creates serious irreparable injury for the method sought. The agency's timing problem supports excusing a judicial exhaustion requirement sufficiently to review the procedural claim; it does not dispense with the remedy showing.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the demanding appellate-stay standard and the substantial-likelihood requirement appropriate to effectively final individual relief.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The appellate stay is not shown demonstrably erroneous because the requested substantive return order remains legally doubtful even on a strong procedural claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Court must not discount Benten's supported desire to avoid an invasive procedure or invent an available foreign doctor or clinical-trial placement. Nonetheless, urgency cannot itself show that a court may select the agency's discretionary outcome. The preserved procedural question is reviewable in character, and its importance supports scrutiny; denial rests on the remedial link, not on lack of personal injury or on a refusal to examine the record.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No broad exhaustion command that would invariably defeat time-sensitive procedural claims. No assessment of the unpresented constitutional liberty claim, even if an effective Casey ruling exists.

### Issue 2: Take the agency's 1989 procedural undertaking seriously

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Evaluate procedural likelihood only as required by the application; do not enter a final APA judgment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Treat the binding automatic-detention language and the then-effective §10.40(d) as substantial support for applicants' procedural case.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Applicants have a substantial procedural challenge that cannot be dismissed merely by characterizing the Alert as guidance or citing the narrower rule effective in 1991.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Automatic detention removed the field agent's prior case-by-case latitude. Even if that change is characterized as interpretive or procedural rather than legislative, the regulation then in force expressly required additional process. No qualifying published policy notice or explained good-cause invocation supplies the omitted route on this record. Enforcing those voluntary but binding rules follows Accardi and Nixon and is compatible with Vermont Yankee. The agency's failure to support its decision also figured below, but an independent final §10.70 or arbitrary-and-capricious ruling is unnecessary to the present disposition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Do not hold that every nonbinding enforcement policy needs notice and comment, that the later valid procedural amendment is ineffective prospectively, or that procedural error itself approves RU486.

### Issue 3: A procedural remedy must preserve the decision still assigned to the agency

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Determine whether restoration of the particular district order follows sufficiently from the procedural claim; do not decide general drug safety or eligibility for distribution.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Distinguish restoration of lawful individualized consideration from mandatory admission. Treat Ford Motor's equitable flexibility and §706(1) as authority to make lawful agency action effective, not as a clear warrant to choose a still-discretionary substantive result.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The applicants have not shown a substantial likelihood that the procedural defect entitles them to immediate judicially ordered return for domestic use, even accepting the individual medical findings.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The district court acknowledged that the RPM permits, rather than compels, admission. Setting aside the Alert restores that discretion; it does not turn permission to consider an exception into a right to receive one. The individualized suitability finding and absence of a government safety contest remove an important practical objection, but the statutory approval regime and assigned decisionmaker still matter. The district court's urgency-based equitable rationale is serious and receives attention; it does not clearly establish that judicial selection is authorized where the narrower legal right is a lawful agency decision. That remaining gap permits the appellate court to preserve its review without a demonstrable misapplication of stay standards. Master administrative-law and remedy discipline require source-bound relief even where the personal stakes are immediate.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding that Benten's individual treatment is unsafe, no final endorsement of the Alert, and no denial of possible prompt agency relief. Whether a properly presented constitutional claim independently requires access is expressly undecided.
