# United States v. Ibarra

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Ibarra
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 1 (1991); No. 90-1713.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Review of dismissal of the United States' criminal appeal as untimely. The inventory authorizes hearing this matter; the calendar date is not a certiorari-grant date.
* **Simulated Event Date:** October 15, 1991, the controlling inventory's historical event date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Does a timely government motion to reconsider a suppression order postpone appellate finality when it renews a ground the Government previously abandoned?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Tenth Circuit, 920 F.2d 702 (1990), dismissing the appeal.
* **Relief Requested:** The Government seeks vacatur and appellate consideration of its challenge to suppression.
* **Material Record:** Officers initially searched Ibarra's car with permission, found no cocaine, and impounded the car because neither occupant had a valid driver's license. A second search at the towing lot produced cocaine. Before the district court ruled, the Government expressly abandoned its continuing-consent theory. Suppression issued November 15, 1989; a single reconsideration motion renewing consent was filed December 13; denial followed January 3, 1990; the notice of appeal was filed January 30. No assertion of bad-faith abandonment and renewal is identified in the reported record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Appellate timeliness is the question, not the underlying Fourth Amendment issue. Statutory government-appeal authority exists for suppression orders under 18 U.S.C. §3731. The available account does not identify an independent certification defect.
* **Relevant Dates:** The reconsideration motion fell within thirty days of suppression, and the appeal within thirty days of reconsideration's denial.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist. No case-specific disqualification is supplied; Thomas had not yet taken his judicial oath on this first event date, so the opening roster requires that vacancy to be respected.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before October 15, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None; this is the first listed event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None preceding this matter.

### Issue 1: Reconsideration and appellate finality

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No prior simulated holding exists; the eligible historical baseline supplies law.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No simulated standards record exists. The operative procedural rule comes from the authorities below.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 3731 and then-current Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 4(b) prescribe the Government's thirty-day appeal period. The party invoking appellate jurisdiction must establish a timely appeal.
* **Material Other Authority:** *United States v. Healy*, 376 U.S. 75 (1964), majority, treats a timely criminal rehearing petition as suspending finality until disposition; *United States v. Dieter*, 429 U.S. 6 (1976), per curiam, rejects a law-versus-fact limitation. The full appeal period begins after disposition, rather than only its previously unused portion. *Department of Banking v. Pink*, 317 U.S. 264 (1942), distinguishes substantive reconsideration from a motion that does not seek alteration of adjudicated rights.
* **Open Point:** Whether renewal of an abandoned ground belongs outside the otherwise general reconsideration rule. Bad faith and successive motions present distinct questions not established here. This is legal interpretation reviewed independently, not a scrutiny or evidentiary-proof inquiry.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The majority below treated an expressly abandoned theory differently from an overlooked issue: reconsideration offered little prospect of correcting an inadvertent error and therefore did not justify postponing the appeal. The Government answers that *Healy* and *Dieter* turn on a timely request to alter the adjudication, not its prospects of success. Ibarra can invoke deliberate waiver, finality, and the thirty-day statutory limit; those concerns must distinguish appellate timing from whether the renewed theory deserves consideration on the merits. The dissent below rejected an appeal deadline dependent on evaluating the reconsideration motion's merits.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Applying the general finality rule makes the notice timely and requires vacatur and remand to the Tenth Circuit. Recognizing an abandoned-ground exception sustains dismissal. Neither path decides consent, suppression, guilt, or the permissible effect of the Government's prior abandonment on substantive appellate review. The reproduction's internal October 16 heading is a transcription discrepancy: the official reporter, printed page 1, confirms October 15. The supplied inventory and official date agree.

# Mireles v. Waco

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Mireles v. Waco
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 9 (1991); No. 91-311.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Review of the rejection of absolute judicial immunity at the pleading stage.
* **Simulated Event Date:** October 21, 1991; chronological event date, not a grant date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Is a judge absolutely immune from damages for allegedly directing court officers to bring counsel to his courtroom using excessive force?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, *Waco v. Baltad*, 934 F.2d 214 (1991), reversing dismissal of the claim against Mireles.
* **Relief Requested:** Mireles seeks reversal and restoration of dismissal on judicial-immunity grounds.
* **Material Record:** Waco, a public defender, alleges that Mireles, angry at absent attorneys, directed two officers to bring him from another courtroom using excessive force. Officers allegedly dragged him backwards, insulted him, and unnecessarily slammed him through courtroom doors and gates; Mireles allegedly approved and ratified their actions. These are allegations accepted for the motion, not findings of battery or constitutional liability. Waco seeks compensatory and punitive damages under §1983. A Rule 54(b) judgment separated the judge's dismissal from the claims against officers.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The operative question concerns the judge's function, not a blanket immunity for everyone involved. The Ninth Circuit did not reach absence of jurisdiction. The pleading supplies a pending matter before the judge; no absence of subject-matter jurisdiction is alleged.
* **Participation Matter:** Stone replaces Rehnquist. Thomas was not yet sworn in on October 21; the vacancy must be reflected. No additional participation limitation is supplied.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before October 21, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated decision is supplied. The earlier *Ibarra* submission creates no immunity law.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Ibarra* addresses criminal appellate timing and has no identified doctrinal dependency here.

### Issue 1: Functional absolute judicial immunity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline, absent a supplied simulated adoption.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No simulated provision exists; apply the following functional rule.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983 reaches persons who cause federal-rights deprivations under color of state law. Absolute immunity is a defense distinct from jurisdiction, the cause of action, and the constitutional merits. The official claiming it must establish the protected function.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Bradley v. Fisher*, 80 U.S. 335 (1872), and *Stump v. Sparkman*, 435 U.S. 349 (1978), distinguish excess of jurisdiction from complete absence of jurisdiction. Judicial acts remain immune despite error, malice, or grave procedural irregularity. *Stump* examines the nature of the function and the parties' relationship to the judge; *Forrester v. White*, 484 U.S. 219 (1988), withholds absolute immunity for administrative employment decisions. *Burns v. Reed*, 500 U.S. 478 (1991), uses a function-specific approach. Legal sufficiency is reviewed independently, with well-pleaded facts assumed true.
* **Open Point:** At what level of specificity should the alleged direction to inflict excessive force be classified? A judicial attendance order and a separable operational command may receive different treatment. Malice alone cannot establish separability.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit distinguished a direction to secure counsel's attendance from an express command to use excessive force, treating the latter as nonjudicial. Mireles argues that commanding attendance is an ordinary judicial function and cannot lose immunity whenever its execution is alleged unlawful. Waco's strongest answer is functional, not merely accusatory: an order to inflict unnecessary physical violence does work different from adjudication or attendance, even if communicated alongside a protected order. California Code of Civil Procedure §§128, 177, and 187 provide substantial courtroom-control authority; neither side can treat their generality as resolving every operational direction. The complete-absence-of-jurisdiction exception is an alternative issue, not a substitute for function analysis.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If the alleged instruction is one judicial attendance order implemented improperly, reverse and restore immunity. If the pleading plausibly identifies a separable operational command to commit violence, affirm rejection of absolute immunity while leaving proof, other defenses, liability, and damages for further proceedings. A bare conclusion that force was excessive does not itself establish the latter category. The actual words spoken and the distinction between ordered force and unauthorized officer conduct remain unproved.

# Campos v. City of Houston

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Campos v. City of Houston
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 1301 (1991); application A-301.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Emergency application for an injunction stopping Houston's election process and, alternatively, a stay of a federal district-court order implementing a redistricting plan. Justice O'Connor presents and refers the application to the participating full Court, which decides by majority and issues an order in its name.
* **Simulated Event Date:** October 29, 1991. This schedules the application, not certiorari or plenary merits review.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Is there lawful authority and a sufficient emergency showing to stop the November 5 city-council election or stay the implementing order?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Southern District of Texas, 776 F. Supp. 304 (October 12, 1991), directing use of Plan 2; the district court and Fifth Circuit subsequently declined a stay.
* **Relief Requested:** Applicants seek to stop the entire city election process; a narrower stay of the district court's order is an alternative requiring separate analysis.
* **Material Record:** Houston conceded that the existing nine-district, five-at-large-member plan (Plan 1) was malapportioned. Its June 5 legislative replacement (Plan 2) was submitted under Voting Rights Act §5; the Attorney General objected October 4 because the City had not carried its burden on discriminatory purpose and effect. The City abandoned Plan 2 as a permanent solution but asked the district court to impose it for November 5. On October 11 the City adopted Plan 3; the Attorney General issued a nonobjection October 12 while reserving statutory reexamination. The district court nonetheless selected Plan 2, finding it nonretrogressive, citing timing, turnout, confusion, and an estimated $350,000 saving compared with a separate election. It acknowledged it lacked time to modify Plan 2 to address the objection. Candidate filing had used Plan 2. By this application date absentee voting had begun. Plaintiffs opposed Plan 3 and preferred sixteen single-member districts, a position distinct from whether Plan 2 could lawfully operate. The court prohibited Plan 1 and directed Plan 2's use until successors could be elected under a lawful permanent plan.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** An injunction requires an independent jurisdictional and equitable basis; calling relief a stay does not supply one. Staying the lower order and affirmatively halting an election are different commands. The appellate proceeding supplies a potential Supreme Court review channel under §1254(1), including review before judgment; §1651 permits preservation of that jurisdiction. A city districting action does not require three judges merely under §2284, and no direct three-judge-court appeal is presumed. Applicants have a personal electoral injury, but their preferred plan does not follow from that injury.
* **Relevant Dates:** October 29 application event; November 5, 1991 election.
* **Participation Matter:** O'Connor is the presenting Fifth Circuit Justice by express simulation instruction. Referral makes this a full-Court application. No case-specific disqualification is supplied.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before October 29, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated disposition has been supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Ibarra* and *Mireles* do not supply an identified election-relief rule. Their drafted proposals cannot control this application.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The user's full-Court referral rule controls decisionmaker and order form. The All Writs Act, 28 U.S.C. §1651, supports relief in aid of jurisdiction, not an independent merits jurisdiction. Section 2101(f) must fit the judgment and contemplated review route if invoked.

### Issue 1: Authority and emergency equitable relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline subject to the express referral override.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No simulated standard exists. *Republican State Central Committee v. Ripon Society*, 409 U.S. 1222 (1972), in-chambers persuasive authority, examines likelihood of legal error, threatened injury, responsibility for urgency, and public consequences. An injunction pending review carries a demanding showing of authority and entitlement; the applicant bears that burden. Failure of jurisdiction ends the requested relief; failure of the emergency showing permits denial without resolving ultimate validity.
* **Other Governing Law:** Voting Rights Act §5 prohibits implementation of covered voting changes without prescribed preclearance. A genuinely court-fashioned plan and a jurisdiction's plan embodied in a court order must be distinguished.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Connor v. Johnson*, 402 U.S. 690 (1971), and *McDaniel v. Sanchez*, 452 U.S. 130 (1981), distinguish judicial plans from legislative choices submitted to a court. *Clark v. Roemer*, 500 U.S. 646 (1991), requires enforcement of §5 and recognizes only narrowly supported emergency departures, not a general convenience exception.
* **Open Point:** Plan 2 is demonstrably a legislative choice with an outstanding objection; Plan 3 received a statutory nonobjection. The legal question is whether exceptional equitable authority permits the district court to use Plan 2 despite those facts. Interim necessity does not itself erase §5. *Upham v. Seamon*, 456 U.S. 37 (1982), permits deference to unobjectionable legislative choices while requiring attention to the portions found objectionable; an interim label alone does not supply exemption.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court relied on *Reynolds v. Sims*, *Upham*, and the lower-court interim-plan decisions *Watkins v. Mabus* and *Terrazas v. Clements*. It found the election machinery could not be changed without delay and expense and regarded Plan 2 as the practical interim choice. Applicants object to judicial implementation of a plan the Attorney General has rejected. The City's strongest response is that a court must choose among defective practical options to preserve orderly voting; its own concession of Plan 1's malapportionment makes simple reversion unavailable. The United States contests a free-standing original injunction and its §5 objection to Plan 2 remains a material legal fact. The district court's independent nonretrogression conclusion is not a statutory preclearance determination and does not itself resolve discriminatory purpose. The Attorney General's reservation of reexamination of Plan 3 is not a present objection to it. Applicants' preference for a different council structure does not establish that Plan 3 is unlawful.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Deny the original all-election injunction if no independent channel supports its breadth, while considering a narrower stay in aid of the pending appellate review. Under the applicants' statutory path, stay the portion compelling Plan 2 and permit the lower court to implement a lawful alternative with any necessary schedule adjustment. Under the City's exceptional-equity path, deny a stay upon a supported conclusion that the legally recognized exception permits this temporary use and the public consequences warrant it. Neither course decides the ultimate §2 dilution claim, requires sixteen single-member districts, or resolves Plan 3's merits. The present record establishes practical disruption but does not establish that an election under Plan 2 is the only way to avoid constitutional injury, since postponement and a nonobjected-to plan are alternatives. A stay should leave the separate prohibition on malapportioned Plan 1 intact and reach no unrelated election contests without demonstrated necessity.

[District-court order and findings](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/776/304/2340894/); [Upham](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/456/37/); [Application record, 502 U.S. 1301](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/502/1301/); [Clark](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/500/646/); [McDaniel](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/452/130/); [Connor](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/402/690/). ### Issue 1: Discretionary fee denial for abusive filings

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Eligible historical rules and decisions remain the baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Rule 39.8 permits denial of in forma pauperis status if the Court is satisfied the particular petition or application is frivolous or malicious. Its “may” language confers discretion, not mandatory exclusion. Repetition is probative of abuse but is not the text's sole condition.
* **Other Governing Law:** Supreme Court Rules 33, 38, and 39 regulate form, fees, and indigent filing. The applicant must support eligibility for ordinary fee-waived filing; invocation of abuse authority requires a judicial basis for the rule's stated predicate. No numerical filing threshold or heightened proof quantum is enacted.
* **Material Other Authority:** *In re McDonald*, 489 U.S. 180 (1989), and *In re Sindram*, 498 U.S. 177 (1991), recognize targeted responses to extraordinary-writ abuse while preserving access outside their boundaries. *In re Amendment to Rule 39*, 500 U.S. 13 (1991), supplies the current rule.
* **Open Point:** Whether current submissions, rather than simply their authors' histories, support the prescribed finding and whether fee denial is an appropriate discretionary response. No scrutiny tier resolves ordinary rule administration.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The supported institutional case for fee denial is preservation of scarce judicial resources for litigants with substantial claims. Repeated frivolous requests can impair others' access. The strongest objection is that requiring payment may block the indigent without reducing the work needed to distinguish frivolous claims, while a history-based label risks discounting a later colorable claim. These are competing considerations in discretionary administration; the source does not reproduce developed adversarial briefs or a lower-court rationale, so no concessions are invented.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Deny fee status if the Court finds present frivolousness or malice and determines discretion should be exercised; any fee deadline and form requirement must be stated in the resulting order. Alternatively allow fee-waived filing and consider submissions through ordinary screening. A prospective categorical filing restriction would require its own lawful basis and tailored terms. The unavailable underlying petitions prevent this packet from supplying claim-specific findings or merits dispositions.

# Hafer v. Melo

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Hafer v. Melo
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 21 (1991); No. 90-681.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Review of reinstated individual-capacity claims under 42 U.S.C. §1983.
* **Simulated Event Date:** November 5, 1991, for chronological ordering, not a certiorari grant.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Are state officers “persons” subject to individual-capacity damages under §1983 for acts performed through their official authority?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Third Circuit, 912 F.2d 628 (1990), reversing the relevant dismissal.
* **Relief Requested:** Hafer seeks dismissal of the individual-capacity damages claims; employees seek continued litigation.
* **Material Record:** After becoming Pennsylvania Auditor General, Hafer fired employees. One group alleges termination based on an accusation that they purchased jobs; another alleges termination for Democratic affiliation and support of her opponent. The latter group also seeks reinstatement. The court of appeals construed the damages pleadings as individual-capacity claims. The allegations of wrongdoing are not findings, and the validity of any discharge has not been resolved here.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Capacity in suit, color of law, personal immunity, state immunity, and actual violation are separate. The official-capacity reinstatement component rests on the prospective-relief channel and is not converted into treasury damages. The question concerns §1983 coverage, not the United States Attorney's liability.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas's nonparticipation is recorded without an identified reason; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before November 5, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated holding supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** The earlier *Mireles* matter may affect the scope of functional personal immunity only if an actual simulated holding reaches beyond judicial acts. It cannot be inferred to establish immunity for employment decisions; revalidate any adopted broader rule before simulation.

### Issue 1: Statutory personhood and capacity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Eligible historical baseline; no simulated holding is yet available.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Kentucky v. Graham*, 473 U.S. 159 (1985), distinguishes a personal claim for an officer's actionable conduct from an official-capacity action against the represented entity. Plaintiffs must establish the official's own causation of a federal deprivation under color of law. Personal liability does not require *Monell* policy or custom, but neither is it automatic from the office held.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1983's text reaches every person acting under color of state law. *Will v. Michigan Department of State Police*, 491 U.S. 58 (1989), excludes States and official-capacity damages defendants; its prospective-relief qualification remains.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Scheuer v. Rhodes*, 416 U.S. 232 (1974), permits personal-capacity claims against state executive officers; *Monroe v. Pape*, 365 U.S. 167 (1961), establishes that misuse of official power can be under color of law. *Forrester*, 484 U.S. 219 (1988), distinguishes administrative employment conduct from judicial acts.
* **Open Point:** Whether *Will*'s description of officials acting officially changes those personal-capacity principles. This is a legal question, reviewed independently.

### Issue 2: Immunity and permissible relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Same historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Graham* and *Scheuer* distinguish state immunity from personal defenses. Under *Harlow*, 457 U.S. 800 (1982), an eligible individual officer may assert the then-existing qualified-immunity defense; its application requires a distinct inquiry.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Eleventh Amendment does not automatically bar a judgment payable by an officer personally.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Ex parte Young*, 209 U.S. 123 (1908), permits appropriately directed prospective relief against an ongoing violation; retroactive treasury compensation remains separately constrained.
* **Open Point:** No case-specific qualified-immunity adjudication is necessary to decide personhood. No entitlement to damages or reinstatement follows from coverage alone.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court read *Will* to bar liability for official employment decisions. The Third Circuit instead examined the capacity in which Hafer was sued. Hafer argues that authorized governmental functions are acts of the State and that personal exposure burdens effective administration. Employees answer that her authority supplies the color-of-law nexus, while individual capacity identifies the defendant and source of recovery. Personal immunities address legitimate operational concerns without excluding the very conduct §1983 reaches. The underlying patronage and reputational claims remain unresolved on their own elements.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm and permit individual claims to proceed, subject to proof and defenses. Alternatively extend *Will* to exclude liability for official acts and reverse, explaining its effect on *Graham* and *Scheuer*. Separately preserve the prospective-relief route; neither a damages-personhood holding nor rejection of state immunity decides that claim's merits. The pleadings' capacity construction is taken from the court of appeals; no new formal pleading formula is required.

# Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System v. MCorp Financial, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System v. MCorp Financial, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 32 (1991); Nos. 90-913 and 90-914.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Cross-review of an injunction against pending bank-regulatory proceedings during bankruptcy.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 3, 1991, a merits event calendar date.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: May a district court halt ongoing Board proceedings under bankruptcy jurisdiction or *Leedom v. Kyne*, despite 12 U.S.C. §1818(i)(1)?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, 900 F.2d 852 (1990), permitting the §23A proceeding but directing an injunction against enforcement of the source-of-strength regulation.
* **Relief Requested:** In No. 90-913 the Board seeks reversal of the source-of-strength injunction; in No. 90-914 MCorp seeks protection against both proceedings.
* **Material Record:** The Board charged MCorp with violating its requirement to serve as financial and managerial support to subsidiary banks and separately with causing approximately $63.7 million in unsecured affiliate credit contrary to §23A. MCorp entered bankruptcy in March 1989. A district court halted both proceedings. No final Board order or present enforcement against estate property is at issue in this action. Temporary orders existed, but this suit is not the specified timely challenge to such an order.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Jurisdiction to interrupt administrative proceedings precedes the regulation's validity. Bankruptcy protection does not itself settle the statutory channel. Cross-petitions encompass the two separable components.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate; no reason is assigned. Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 3, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None supplied as effective simulated law.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Earlier *Ibarra* concerns timing within an authorized appeal, not the statutory power to enjoin a regulator. An actual broad jurisdictional ruling would require revalidation; no direct controlling dependency is identified.

### Issue 1: Statutory review channel and bankruptcy

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Eligible historical law; no adopted simulated provision exists.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Express jurisdictional limits govern notwithstanding general grants. MCorp bears the burden of establishing a lawful intervention channel; statutory construction is independently reviewed.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1818(i)(1) bars courts, except as otherwise provided in that section, from affecting issuance or enforcement of notices or orders. Section 1818(c)(2) permits a ten-day district-court challenge to temporary orders; §1818(h)(2) provides appellate review of final orders; §1818(i)(1) permits Board enforcement actions. Bankruptcy §362(a) stays specified proceedings and estate interference, while then-current §362(b)(4) excludes continuation of governmental police or regulatory proceedings. Section 1334(b)'s reference to exclusive jurisdiction of other courts must be read with the Board's administrative character; §1334(d) separately protects estate jurisdiction.
* **Material Other Authority:** Statutes should coexist where their text permits; no implied repeal is inferred merely from bankruptcy's broad remedial purpose.
* **Open Point:** Whether mere continuation of these nonfinal proceedings is interference with estate property under §362(a)(3) or recovery of prepetition claims under §362(a)(6), and whether either provision changes §1818's express allocation. Actual enforcement against property is a separate stage.

### Issue 2: Extraordinary ultra vires review

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Same historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** *Leedom v. Kyne*, 358 U.S. 184 (1958), permitted judicial intervention where the agency violated a specific statutory prohibition and otherwise deprived the claimant of a means to vindicate a congressionally granted right. 
* **Material Other Authority:** *Boire v. Greyhound*, 376 U.S. 473 (1964), confines that exceptional route; ordinary alleged statutory error is insufficient.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1818's final-order review can reach the regulation's validity and application.
* **Open Point:** Whether *Kyne* allows review despite this express preclusion and available later judicial review.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Fifth Circuit accepted general preclusion but used *Kyne* to reach and invalidate the source-of-strength regulation. MCorp argues that bankruptcy courts must protect estate assets and need not await an unauthorized agency demand. The Board argues that regulatory continuation is expressly excepted from the stay and that Congress reserved legal objections for final-order review. MCorp's strongest concern is an eventual forced transfer of estate assets; the Board's limiting answer is that the current action precedes any such enforcement. The Government's institutional position is represented by the Board; no separate amicus theory is invented.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If no interruption channel exists, reverse the source-of-strength component and affirm the §23A component without deciding the regulation's validity. If bankruptcy or *Kyne* supplies jurisdiction, evaluate the particular regulatory challenge and permissible injunction on its own law. A possible future conflict over estate assets does not resolve whether this nonfinal proceeding may continue. Its eventual treatment remains reserved.

# Griffin v. United States

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Griffin v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 502 U.S. 46 (1991); No. 90-6352.
* **October Term:** 1991.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Direct review of a federal conspiracy conviction returned by general verdict.
* **Simulated Event Date:** December 3, 1991.
* **Question Presented:** Record-grounded formulation: Must a general verdict on a multiple-object conspiracy be reversed when one object lacks evidence connecting this defendant and another is sufficiently proved?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Seventh Circuit, *United States v. Beverly*, 913 F.2d 337 (1990), affirming Griffin's conviction.
* **Relief Requested:** Griffin seeks reversal and relief from the conspiracy conviction.
* **Material Record:** A §371 count alleged interference with the IRS's ascertainment of income taxes and the DEA's identification of forfeitable assets. Evidence connected Griffin to the IRS object. Anticipated testimony failed to connect her to the DEA object, a deficiency the Government concedes. The trial court denied severance, a proposed instruction limiting her liability to the IRS object, and requested special interrogatories. The jury could convict on either object and returned a general guilty verdict.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The unsupported alternative is alleged to lack proof, not to criminalize protected conduct or conduct outside §371. The petition referred to both Fifth and Sixth Amendments; developed argument concerns due process and precedent. A free-standing jury-unanimity redesign is not necessary to decide the supplied question.
* **Participation Matter:** Thomas did not participate; Stone replaces Rehnquist.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Immediately before December 3, 1991.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No effective simulated holding supplied.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No earlier listed criminal-verdict holding supplies the rule. *Ibarra* concerns appeal timing. *MCorp* precedes this matter in the controlling index but concerns banking jurisdiction; consult its actual effective holding only if it changes a relevant general principle.

### Issue 1: General verdict and unsupported alternative

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Historical baseline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No simulated standards provision exists. Distinguish invalid legal theories from factual insufficiency and distinguish alternative means of one offense from separate offenses.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 371 requires conspiracy to commit an offense against or defraud the United States and an overt act. The Government must prove every required element beyond reasonable doubt. *In re Winship*, 397 U.S. 358 (1970); *Jackson v. Virginia*, 443 U.S. 307 (1979), asks whether, viewing the evidence favorably to the verdict, a rational factfinder could find the elements beyond reasonable doubt.
* **Material Other Authority:** *Stromberg v. California*, 283 U.S. 359 (1931), rejects a general verdict potentially resting on a constitutionally invalid ground. *Yates v. United States*, 354 U.S. 298 (1957), applies the principle to a legally barred conspiracy object. *Turner v. United States*, 396 U.S. 398, 420 (1970), sustains a verdict where a charged alternative is sufficiently supported despite failure of another factual route. *Schad v. Arizona*, 501 U.S. 624 (1991), includes divided reasoning about alternative means, not a universal authorization to avoid proving offense elements.
* **Open Point:** Whether *Yates* requires the same consequence for factual insufficiency or whether *Turner* governs. A legal instruction or due-process question is independently reviewed; this does not lower the prosecution's trial burden.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Seventh Circuit relied on the sufficiently supported object. Griffin argues that an opaque verdict gives no assurance which route the jury used and that the court should have withdrawn a concededly unsupported theory. The Government distinguishes the jury's ordinary competence to reject missing proof from its inability to correct an instruction declaring unlawful conduct criminal. Griffin's requested limiting instruction identifies a practical method for avoiding uncertainty; whether refusing it requires reversal must still follow the governing constitutional or supervisory rule. The conceded DEA evidentiary gap does not concede insufficiency of the IRS-based conspiracy conviction.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Apply *Turner* and affirm if the IRS object supplies all necessary elements and the defect is solely factual. Extend *Yates* or adopt a source-grounded federal supervisory rule requiring withdrawal and apply the proper reversal standard, explaining any departure. A legally invalid alternative would invoke a different branch. Acquittal is not warranted by the conceded failure of one object where the offense is otherwise sufficiently proved; any new-trial remedy must identify its instructional basis. No presumption that the jury actually selected the IRS object is a factual finding available to this packet.
