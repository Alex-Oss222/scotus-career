# Reynolds v. International Amateur Athletic Federation

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Justice Stevens, acting alone in chambers, granted the stay; there was no full-Court vote in this reported disposition.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Short in-chambers opinion.
* **Historical Holding:** The district court's likelihood-of-success assessment was persuasive, damages could not replace Olympic opportunity, and threats against third parties should not dictate adjudication.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only. The simulation substitutes full-Court referral and decision for the historical single-Justice arrangement.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 1301](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/1301/).

# Sawyer v. Whitley

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed unanimously in the judgment; six Justices joined the controlling rationale.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist wrote for White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Blackmun concurred in the judgment. Stevens, joined by Blackmun and O'Connor, concurred in the judgment on a broader conception of sentencing innocence.
* **Historical Holding:** The prisoner had to show by clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find death eligibility under state law. Additional mitigation alone did not meet that eligibility-focused definition. Sawyer failed; the concurring Justices also found his showing insufficient under their approaches.
* **Historical Authority:** Historical majority rule only; not the simulation's entering gateway.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 333](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/333/).

# R. A. V. v. City of St. Paul

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded unanimously in the judgment, with a five-Justice majority rationale.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Scalia wrote for Rehnquist, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. White, Blackmun and Stevens wrote separately, with differing joins; the separate opinions favored overbreadth or disputed the majority's general treatment of selective regulation.
* **Historical Holding:** The majority assumed the fighting-words narrowing and held the ordinance invalid for content and viewpoint discrimination, identifying limited situations in which distinctions within unprotected categories could survive. It did not decide the overbreadth ground.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only, including its newly stated exceptions.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 377](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/377/).

# Medina v. California

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 7-2 in the judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy's opinion joined by Rehnquist, White, Scalia and Thomas rejected Mathews as the general framework for this criminal procedural rule. O'Connor, joined by Souter, concurred in the judgment. Blackmun, joined by Stevens, dissented.
* **Historical Holding:** Due process permitted placing on the defendant a preponderance burden to prove incompetence; the associated presumption was permissible.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only; no effective simulated rule follows from this historical outcome.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 437](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/437/).

# Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded; different voting groups supported different claim dispositions.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Stevens wrote Parts I-IV for seven Justices, including no preemption under the 1965 Act. Rehnquist, White and O'Connor joined his Parts V-VI claim analysis of the successor Act. Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter would preserve all common-law claims under that Act. Scalia and Thomas favored broader preemption and joined the judgment for the claims the plurality held preempted.
* **Historical Holding:** The 1965 Act did not preempt the damages claims. The successor Act preempted specified warning and warning-neutralization claims but left express warranty and certain fraud/conspiracy claims available. A single numerical vote does not accurately describe every proposition.
* **Historical Authority:** Only the historically supported holdings and opinion portions, for comparison after quarantine.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 504](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/504/).

# Lee v. Weisman

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 5-4.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy wrote for Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. Blackmun and Souter wrote concurrences. Scalia dissented with Rehnquist, White and Thomas.

# Doggett v. United States

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 5-4.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter wrote for White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy. O'Connor dissented; Thomas dissented with Rehnquist and Scalia.
* **Historical Holding:** Extraordinary negligent post-indictment delay violated the Sixth Amendment despite no specific demonstrable prejudice; presumptive prejudice in the Barker balance grew with duration and was neither attenuated by acquiescence nor persuasively rebutted.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 647](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/647/).
