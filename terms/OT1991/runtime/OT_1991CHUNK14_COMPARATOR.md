# International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed as to solicitation, 6-3 in judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist wrote for White, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, treating the terminals as nonpublic fora and sustaining the restriction as reasonable. Kennedy concurred in the judgment through public-forum time, place and manner analysis. Souter, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, dissented from sustaining solicitation restrictions. O'Connor also wrote separately concerning the related literature issue.
* **Historical Holding:** The five-Justice majority classified the terminals as nonpublic fora and upheld the solicitation restriction; the sixth vote rested on a different framework.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only; the cross-case is separately recorded.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 672](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/672/).

# United States v. Fordice

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 8-1 in the dispositive judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** White wrote for Rehnquist, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. O'Connor and Thomas concurred separately. Scalia concurred in part and dissented in part, opposing the remand disposition and aspects of the standard.
* **Historical Holding:** Mississippi had not discharged its duty merely by adopting neutral policies; policies traceable to the dual system with continuing segregative effects had to be eliminated if practicable and consistent with sound educational practices. The Court identified admissions, duplication, mission assignments and continued operation of all institutions for further scrutiny.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only; neither a finding requiring particular campus closures nor an automatic racial-balance command.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 717](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/717/).

# Franklin v. Massachusetts

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed unanimously in judgment, with sharply divided threshold reasoning.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O'Connor's Parts I-II, joined by Rehnquist, White, Scalia and Thomas, held the Secretary's report nonfinal and the President outside the APA. Her standing discussion in Part III had Rehnquist, White and Thomas. Part IV rejecting the constitutional claim had eight votes, all except Scalia. Stevens, joined by Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter, concurred in the judgment, would review the Secretary under the APA, and found the decision lawful. Scalia agreed with the APA disposition but rejected constitutional standing.
* **Historical Holding:** APA review was unavailable under the five-Justice finality and presidential-coverage holding; the constitutional allocation challenge failed. Eight Justices reached the constitutional merits, without eight joining a single written standing rationale.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only; not a unanimous endorsement of one APA or standing theory.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 788](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/788/).

# Lee v. International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 5-4.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** A per curiam referred to O'Connor's, Kennedy's and Souter's opinions in the companion case. O'Connor found the distribution ban unreasonable in a nonpublic forum; Kennedy and Souter used public-forum reasoning, with Blackmun and Stevens joining Souter. Rehnquist dissented with White, Scalia and Thomas.
* **Historical Holding:** The complete literature-distribution prohibition was invalid. The five votes did not share a public-forum classification rationale in this judgment.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only; no invented single five-Justice rationale beyond the judgment's shared result.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 830](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/830/) and the separate opinions at [505 U.S. 672](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/672/).

# Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No. 91-902 affirmed; No. 91-744 affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded for proceedings including severability. Five Justices retained Roe's central previability protection and invalidated spousal notice and associated reporting. All nine accepted the medical-emergency construction; seven sustained parental-consent/bypass and the principal informed-consent and waiting provisions, and eight sustained the other reporting provisions, with differing rationales and some separate distinctions.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter authored the joint opinion. Stevens and Blackmun joined Parts I, II, III, V-A, V-C and VI. Stevens additionally joined Part V-E; Parts IV, V-B and V-D were the trio's opinion. Stevens and Blackmun favored broader invalidation of restrictions. Rehnquist, joined by White, Scalia and Thomas, would overrule Roe and sustain the challenged provisions under a more deferential approach. Scalia also wrote separately with the same three joins. The joint opinion's general undue-burden exposition and its treatment of several upheld provisions did not receive five signatures as a single opinion.
* **Historical Holding:** Roe's central viability protection was retained, the trimester framework abandoned, and the joint opinion formulated the purpose-or-effect substantial-obstacle approach to undue burden. Akron and Thornburgh were overruled to the extent inconsistent with the approved informed-consent approach. Spousal notification and related reporting failed; the other identified provisions were sustained, with component-specific votes and rationales.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only. The fractured opinion structure must be preserved; neither every joint-opinion proposition nor its replacement framework is entering law for this same matter.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 833](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/833/).

# Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 6-3 in judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Scalia wrote for Rehnquist, White, O'Connor and Thomas. Kennedy concurred in the judgment, emphasizing expectations and the particular setting. Blackmun and Stevens dissented; Souter favored dismissal as improvidently granted because of the total-loss record and procedural posture.
* **Historical Holding:** Total deprivation of economically beneficial land use generally requires compensation unless background state property or nuisance principles already prohibited the use. The later permit procedure did not foreclose the pre-amendment claim, and the state-law exception remained for remand.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only, including its categorical formulation and allocation of the background-principles inquiry.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 1003](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/1003/).

# Espinosa v. Florida

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded by per curiam, 6-3 as to the summary disposition.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist and White dissented and would set the case for argument. Scalia dissented on the substantive capital-sentencing theory and would deny review. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas joined the Court's disposition; no individual majority author was identified.
* **Historical Holding:** A vague aggravator considered by a jury whose recommendation receives great weight can infect the judge's sentencing decision even though the judge did not directly use invalid aggravation. The Court remanded the state judgment.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only. The two procedural dissents are distinguished from Scalia's merits disagreement; the historical grant language does not turn this authorized simulation matter into a petition vote.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 1079](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/1079/).

# Benten v. Kessler

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historical interim-application anchor: application denied by the full Court, 7-2, after presentation to Thomas and referral to the Court.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Per curiam. Blackmun noted that he would grant relief. Stevens dissented separately on the constitutional burden associated with the choice of abortion method.
* **Historical Holding:** Applicants had not shown a substantial likelihood of success on the APA/FDA notice-and-comment claim supporting return. The Court expressly reserved the constitutional assertion because it was neither addressed below nor presented in applicants' Supreme Court filings.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only; this was an interim application disposition, not a final general decision approving the drug, adjudicating all agency procedures, or rejecting the constitutional method claim on its merits.
* **Historical Sources:** [505 U.S. 1084](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/1084/).
